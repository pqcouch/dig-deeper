Colossians: Questions for the Logos Research Assistant, Round 3

Purpose: the questions still open across the Colossians studies — the book overview (v0.1.1), the four solo digs (1:15–20 v1.4; 1:24–2:5 v1.3; 2:6–15 v1.3; 2:16–23 v1.0) and claim audit 1 — gathered in one place. They follow logos-research-assistant-guidelines in the Dig Deeper folder. The main aims are (a) to give the three cruxes of 2:16–23 their commentators before a backbone is drafted, (b) to finish the questions rounds 1 and 2 left half-answered, and (c) to fill the “history of interpretation” criterion that claim audit 1 left not checked, before the Finalise pass on the overview. Use: - Ask one question per turn. - Set the scope to a Colossians commentaries collection unless the question says otherwise. - Paste each answer back with its footnotes, under the question as asked.

Date: 27 October 2026

How the List Is Arranged

The questions are grouped by the template they practise, as in the earlier rounds. Within each group the first question matters most. Each question has three parts:

  • Ask: the wording to type.
  • Why: the open point it settles, and where the answer would be used.
  • Watch for: the check from the “Reading the Answer” section of the guidelines that is most likely to matter.

Owned and not owned. Rounds 1 and 2 found these in the library:

  • Commentaries: Dunn (NIGTC), Wright (TNTC), Pao (ZECNT), Beale (BECNT), Brown (Logos Research Commentaries), Osborne, O’Brien’s New Bible Commentary article, Vincent’s Word Studies, Robertson’s Word Pictures.
  • Reference works: Beale and Carson (eds.), Commentary on the NT Use of the OT; Beale and Gladd, Hidden but Now Revealed; Dictionary of Paul and His Letters; EDNT; Trench; Hastings; Blass; the Lexham Bible Dictionary; the Clementine and Stuttgart Vulgates; Brenton.

Not in the library: Moo, Lightfoot, Harris, Hendriksen, BDAG, O’Brien’s WBC. If the Assistant cites any of these, check that it is quoting them at second hand through a work you own, and say which.

Left out on purpose:

  • counts, word chains and “only here” claims, which the corpus has settled;
  • questions closed in rounds 1 and 2 (the Vulgate at 1:15, 1:19, 1:24 and 2:15; πιθανολογία; μή with the future at 2:8; θεότης and θειότης; the 1:20 bracket; the 2:2 apposition; the military image at 2:5; the subject and voice at 2:15);
  • the 2:18 variant (μή), which the NA28 apparatus settles on external evidence;
  • the corpus gap at Rom 16:25–27. The NA28 export has the doxology (double-bracketed). It joins μυστήριον with σιγάω (“kept silent”, not a hiding word), φανερόω (“manifested”) and γνωρίζω (“made known”) (Rom 16:25–26). Claim audit 1’s open check is settled from the export; no Logos question is needed.

1. A Named Commentator’s View

1.1 Colossians 2:23, the last clause

  • Ask: How does Dunn (NIGTC) translate and explain the last clause of Colossians 2:23, “not of any value against the indulgence of the flesh” (οὐκ ἐν τιμῇ τινι πρὸς πλησμονὴν τῆς σαρκός)? What does he take πρός, τιμή and πλησμονή to mean, and what other readings does he mention?
  • Why: this is the hardest clause in the letter and the 2:16–23 dig leaves it open (Open Question 1). The modern versions read πρός as “against” and πλησμονή as “indulgence”; the older Latin shape reads “not in any honour, for the satisfaction of the flesh”, which makes the regimen serve the flesh. Both readings support the dig’s Headline 2 (severity to the body cannot touch the flesh); which one a sermon uses depends on the answer.
  • Watch for: whether “against” for πρός is supported with examples or simply asserted; and whether πλησμονή is given its usual neutral sense (“satisfaction”) or a negative one (“gratification”).
  • Then ask the same question of: Pao (ZECNT), Wright (TNTC) and Brown (LRC).

1.2 Colossians 2:18, “the worship of the angels”

  • Ask: What does Dunn say about “the worship of the angels” (θρησκείᾳ τῶν ἀγγέλων) in Colossians 2:18? Does he take the genitive as the angels being worshipped, or as the worship the angels offer, and what evidence does he give?
  • Why: the dig found that the grammar cannot decide — the LXX has an objective genitive with θρησκεία at Wis 14:27 (“the worship of idols”) and a subjective one at 4 Macc 5:6 (“the religion of the Jews”) (Open Question 2). The answer decides how the rival teaching is described in a sermon (overview, Trap 2).
  • Watch for: a reconstruction (“the Colossian heresy was …”) doing the work instead of the Greek.
  • Then ask: “Which commentators hold each view of ‘the worship of the angels’ in Colossians 2:18, and what is the main argument on each side?” (Brown’s survey is likely to answer this best.)

1.3 Colossians 2:18, “taking his stand on visions he has seen”

  • Ask: What does Dunn say about the meaning of ἃ ἑόρακεν ἐμβατεύων in Colossians 2:18 (“taking his stand on visions he has seen” / “going on in detail about visions”), and does he discuss the inscriptions from the oracle of Apollo at Claros?
  • Why: the dig found two senses in the LXX — entering and occupying land (Josh 19:49, 51; 1 Maccabees) and entering into a subject in detail (2 Macc 2:30), which is the ESV’s sense — and a third, initiatory sense from Claros that is recalled, not checked (Open Question 3). BDAG is not in the library, so a commentator’s report of the lexical evidence is the route.
  • Watch for: the date of the Claros inscriptions (second century AD), and whether the answer says so.
  • Then ask the same question of: Pao and Beale.

1.4 Colossians 2:18 and 2:23, “self-abasement” as fasting

  • Ask: Does Dunn (or Pao) take “self-abasement” (ταπεινοφροσύνη) in Colossians 2:18 and 2:23 as a technical term for fasting, and what evidence is given (for example, the Shepherd of Hermas)?
  • Why: the ESV’s “asceticism” has decided for a practice; the NASB95’s “self-abasement” leaves an attitude (dig, Tool 7 and Tool 8). The noun is absent from the LXX, and at 3:12 the same word is the church’s virtue. If it means “fasting” at 2:18, the 3:12 reversal is a play on a technical word; if not, it is the same virtue counterfeited.
  • Watch for: the date of Hermas (second century) being used as if it were first-century evidence.

1.5 Colossians 2:8 and 2:20, “the elements of the world”

  • Ask: What does Dunn say about “the elements of the world” (τὰ στοιχεῖα τοῦ κόσμου) in Colossians 2:8 and 2:20, and which of the three senses — physical elements, elementary principles, or elemental spirits — does he favour, and why?
  • Why: carried over from rounds 1 and 2 (2:6–15 dig, Open Question 1): only Dunn’s note on 2:15 came back. The ESV’s “elemental spirits” decides the question for the congregation. The 2:16–23 dig adds that “you died … from the elements” (2:20) is a construction found nowhere else in the NT, which fits a power or regime better than a substance.
  • Watch for: evidence for “spirits” that is evidence for spirits associated with the elements (Jubilees; 1 Enoch), not for the word στοιχεῖα used of spirits before the second century.

1.6 Colossians 2:10, ὅς or ὅ

  • Ask: In Colossians 2:10, some early manuscripts (𝔓⁴⁶ B D F G) read ὅ (“which”) instead of ὅς (“who”) before “is the head of all rule and authority”. Which commentators discuss this variant, does anyone defend the neuter, and how do they explain it?
  • Why: carried over from rounds 1 and 2 (2:6–15 dig, Open Question 7); round 2’s answer was about the parallel variant at 1:27. The corpus offers a second explanation of the neuter: the letter’s own formula ὅ ἐστιν (“which is”, 1:24, 27; 3:14), to which a scribe might assimilate.
  • Watch for: an answer about 1:27 again. Ask Dunn first, then Brown.

1.7 Colossians 2:15, “in him” or “in it”

  • Ask: In Colossians 2:15, “triumphing over them in him / in it” (ἐν αὐτῷ), how do Dunn and Brown take ἐν αὐτῷ — in Christ, or in the cross (2:14)?
  • Why: carried over from round 2 (2:6–15 dig, Open Question 3). Round 2 moved the subject of the verse to “slight lean to Christ, true middle”; if Christ is the subject, “in him” becomes awkward and “in it” (the cross) more likely.
  • Watch for: whether the commentator ties the answer to his view of who the subject is.

1.8 Colossians 1:19, the subject of “was pleased”

  • Ask: In Colossians 1:19 (ἐν αὐτῷ εὐδόκησεν πᾶν τὸ πλήρωμα κατοικῆσαι), what do Dunn and Pao take as the subject of εὐδόκησεν (“was pleased”) — “all the fullness”, or God understood — and on what grounds?
  • Why: still open (1:15–20 dig, Open Question 1): slight lean to “the fullness” (ESV), moderate. The corpus has answered Vincent’s argument; BDAG is not in the library.
  • Watch for: an appeal to 2:9, which the dig found does not discriminate (the fullness “dwells” on either construction).

1.9 Colossians 1:20, “to Himself”

  • Ask: In Colossians 1:20, “to reconcile all things to Himself” (εἰς αὐτόν), do Dunn, Pao or Beale discuss whether “Himself” is God or the Son, and do any compare the εἰς αὐτόν of 1:16?
  • Why: evenly balanced in the 1:15–20 dig (Open Question 2). Most commentators read God, but only Col 1:20 has εἰς + accusative with a reconciliation verb; every other NT instance has a dative of the party.
  • Watch for: an answer that assumes the accent (αὑτόν, reflexive) settles it; the early manuscripts are unaccented.

1.10 Colossians 1:24, Brown’s survey

  • Ask: What does Brown (Logos Research Commentaries) say about “filling up what is lacking in Christ’s afflictions” in Colossians 1:24? Which readings does he list, and who holds each?
  • Why: carried over from round 2’s “still open” list. The dig leans to messianic woes with Christ suffering in his body (options 1 + 2), and the absence reading (option 4, Garland) is still live on the idiom (1:24–2:5 dig, Open Question 1). Brown surveys the field.
  • Watch for: Wright filed as a “cross” reading, which round 2 corrected.

2. The State of a Debate

2.1 Colossians 2:16–17 and the weekly Sabbath

  • Ask: In Colossians 2:16, does “a Sabbath day” (σαββάτων, plural) include the weekly Sabbath, or only special sabbaths? What are the main views, who holds each, and what evidence do they give?
  • Why: the dig reads the triad as including the weekly Sabbath, since the same triad does in Hos 2:11 and Ezek 45:17 (moderate–high), but advises against making the sermon a Sabbath debate (overview, Trap 3; dig, Preaching Pitfalls). The preacher should know the state of the question before the pulpit.
  • Watch for: confessional conclusions about the Lord’s Day presented as exegesis of the verse.

2.2 The rival teaching at Colossae

  • Ask: What are the main reconstructions of the teaching opposed in Colossians 2:8–23 (for example, Jewish mysticism, folk religion, a syncretistic mix, Hellenistic philosophy), who holds each, and what is the main evidence for each?
  • Why: the overview and every dig work from the features, not a label (overview, Presenting Situation and Trap 2). A short, sourced map of the reconstructions lets the Finalise pass tag them properly rather than from recall.
  • Watch for: later evidence (the Council of Laodicea; Theodoret; the Michael shrine at Chonae) used as if it were first-century.

2.3 Authorship, place and date

  • Ask: What are the main positions on the authorship of Colossians, who holds each in the commentaries in my library, and where does each place Paul’s imprisonment (Rome, Ephesus or Caesarea)?
  • Why: the overview tags these [S] and lists them as “the first things for a Logos round to test” (Colophon). The Finalise pass needs them sourced, not recalled. No exegetical finding depends on them.
  • Watch for: stylistic arguments presented as settled.

2.4 Colossians 1:15–20 as a “hymn”

  • Ask: Which commentators in my library treat Colossians 1:15–20 as a pre-Pauline hymn that Paul quotes and edits, which treat it as composed for the letter, and what evidence does each side give?
  • Why: the overview tags the “hymn” as a form-critical reconstruction [S] (Structural Arc Map). The Finalise pass should name the holders.
  • Watch for: the claim that Paul “added” words (for example “the church”, 1:18; “through the blood of His cross”, 1:20) resting only on the reconstruction.

2.5 The letter “from Laodicea” (4:16)

  • Ask: What are the main identifications of “the letter from Laodicea” in Colossians 4:16 (a lost letter, Ephesians, Philemon, or the later apocryphal Epistle to the Laodiceans), and who holds each?
  • Why: listed in the overview’s Canonical Position as [S]; to be sourced before Finalise.
  • Watch for: confusion with the fourth-century Latin Epistle to the Laodiceans.

3. Has Anyone Seen This Allusion?

These fill claim audit 1’s “history of interpretation” criterion, which was scored possible, not checked for C1, C4, C7 and C15, and test the new candidates from the 2:16–23 dig (C24–C27).

3.1 Isaiah 29:13–14 behind Colossians 2:22–23 (C24)

  • Ask: Which commentators discuss Isaiah 29:13 behind “the commandments and teachings of men” in Colossians 2:22, and does any of them connect the next verse, Isaiah 29:14 (“I will destroy the wisdom of the wise”), with “an appearance of wisdom” in Colossians 2:23?
  • Why: the citation itself is high (ἔνταλμα with διδασκαλία stands together only at Isa 29:13 in the LXX). The dig’s reading that 2:22 → 2:23 follows Isaiah’s own move from 29:13 to 29:14 is moderate and queued for the audit. Paul quotes Isa 29:14 at 1 Cor 1:19.
  • Watch for: Beale and Carson’s Commentary on the NT Use of the OT — the most likely place; and whether anyone argues the link is shared idiom.

3.2 The umpire pair, 2:18 and 3:15 (C4, C19)

  • Ask: Which commentators connect “let no one disqualify you” (καταβραβευέτω) in Colossians 2:18 with “let the peace of Christ rule” (βραβευέτω) in 3:15, and do any reject the connection as unintended?
  • Why: audited at moderate–high as intended wordplay; history of interpretation was scored “often noted, seldom pressed” from recall [S]. This would confirm or correct that line.
  • Watch for: “rule” glossed from the English without the umpire sense.

3.3 Colossians 3:12–16 as the answer to 2:18–23 (C25)

  • Ask: Do any commentators note that Colossians 3:12–16 takes up the vocabulary of 2:18–23 with the opposite value — “humility” (3:12; cf. 2:18, 23), the “bond” (3:14; cf. “ligaments”, 2:19), “body” (3:15), and “the word of Christ … in all wisdom … teaching” (3:16; cf. “teachings of men … appearance of wisdom”, 2:22–23)?
  • Why: the dig rates the words high and the design moderate; a chance baseline found the word overlap not unusual (it ties with 2:6–13 and 1:26–2:4), so the case rests on reversed sense. History of interpretation would help the audit.
  • Watch for: an answer listing only ταπεινοφροσύνη, which the overview already had.

3.4 “The body is Christ’s” (2:17) and “the body of Christ” (C26)

  • Ask: In Colossians 2:17, “the substance (τὸ σῶμα) belongs to Christ”, do any commentators see a reference to the church as the body of Christ (as in 1:18, 24 and 2:19), or a deliberate play on σῶμα across 2:17, 2:19 and 2:23?
  • Why: the dig’s Headline 2 rests on the three occurrences and the body/flesh contrast of 2:23, which are textual; the deliberate play is moderate–high and queued (C26). Elsewhere in Paul “the body of Christ” always means Christ’s own body or the church (Rom 7:4; 1 Cor 10:16; 12:27; Eph 4:12).
  • Watch for: the reading “let no one judge you … but let the body of Christ (judge)”, which some have proposed; note who holds it.

3.5 Psalm 68:16 behind Colossians 1:19 (C1)

  • Ask: Which commentators connect “in Him all the fullness was pleased to dwell” (Colossians 1:19) with Psalm 68:16 (LXX 67:17), “the mountain which God has desired for His abode” (NASB95), and do any argue against the link?
  • Why: audited at moderate–high on wording and moderate on function; history of interpretation scored possible (“raised in temple readings; a minority report”) from recall.
  • Watch for: Beale (BECNT; CNTUOT) — the most likely holder; check that he cites the LXX wording (εὐδοκέω + κατοικέω).

3.6 Psalm 89:27 and “firstborn” (C7)

  • Ask: Which commentators cite Psalm 89:27 (“I also shall make him My firstborn, the highest of the kings of the earth”) at Colossians 1:15 or 1:18, and do they use it for the sense “rank” rather than “first created”?
  • Why: audited at moderate–high for the point contact (Ps 88:28 LXX, with Rev 1:5); history of interpretation scored possible (“regularly cited”) from recall.
  • Watch for: Psalm 89 used for the whole confession (the audit rated that only moderate).

3.7 The Jesus tradition behind 2:8 and 2:16–22 (C15)

  • Ask: Which commentators connect Colossians 2:8, 2:16 and 2:20–22 with Jesus’ teaching in Mark 7:1–23 and Matthew 15:1–20 (“the tradition of men”; Isaiah 29:13; food entering the stomach), and do they argue for dependence on the Gospel tradition or only a shared theme?
  • Why: reframed by the audit to “shared dominical tradition, moderate; Mark 7 specifically, low–moderate”; history of interpretation not checked.
  • Watch for: the observation that Colossians keeps the LXX order of Isa 29:13 while the Gospels do not — if a commentator notices it, record it.

3.8 Exodus 19:12 behind Colossians 2:21 (C27) — low priority

  • Ask: Do any commentators connect “do not touch” (θίγῃς) in Colossians 2:21 with the Sinai prohibition in Exodus 19:12–13 or with the purity laws of Leviticus 11:8?
  • Why: θιγγάνω occurs once in the LXX, at Exod 19:12, beside ἅπτω — the same pair as Col 2:21. The dig rates it low–uncertain and queues it only so that it is tested rather than preached.
  • Watch for: Hebrews 12:20, which cites the Sinai verse with the same verb; the Assistant may answer about Hebrews instead.

4. Lexicon, Grammar and Text

4.1 ἐθελοθρησκία (“self-made religion”), 2:23

  • Ask: Is the word ἐθελοθρησκία (“self-made religion”, Colossians 2:23) found in any Greek text earlier than Colossians, and do the commentators think Paul coined it or took it from his opponents?
  • Why: the corpus shows it absent from the NT elsewhere and from the LXX; “not known before this verse” is recalled [S] (2:16–23 dig, Open Question 6).
  • Watch for: EDNT and Dunn; an answer that cites only later Christian writers.

4.2 θέλων ἐν, 2:18

  • Ask: In Colossians 2:18, how do the commentators take θέλων ἐν ταπεινοφροσύνῃ — “delighting in” (a Septuagintal idiom), “insisting on”, or adverbially (“wilfully”)?
  • Why: the dig found θέλω + ἐν + dative in the LXX in the sense “delight in” (1 Sam 18:22; 2 Sam 15:26; 1 Kgs 10:9; Ps 146:10) and favours the NASB95’s “delighting in” against the ESV’s “insisting on” (moderate–high). A commentator’s view would confirm or correct.
  • Watch for: the KJV’s “voluntary humility”, which takes θέλων adverbially.

5. Background

5.1 Food, calendar and “philosophy” in Jewish Greek (C28)

  • Ask: Do any commentators on Colossians 2:8 and 2:16–23 compare 4 Maccabees, where Jewish food laws are called both a “religion” (θρησκεία) and a “philosophy” (4 Macc 5:6–11), or the Maccabean martyrs’ refusal to taste forbidden food (2 Macc 6–7)?
  • Why: the dig’s systematic scan of the LXX found this field as the passage’s closest vocabulary partner, and 4 Macc 5:6 is the only LXX verse with both θρησκεία (2:18) and the “philosophy” word of 2:8. It supports a Jewish component in the rival teaching without choosing a reconstruction. Moderate, background, queued.
  • Watch for: Josephus’ description of the Jewish schools as “philosophies” (Antiquities 18.11) — record it if cited.

5.2 Angels in Phrygia, and its date

  • Ask: What evidence do the commentators give for the veneration of angels in Phrygia — for example canon 35 of the Council of Laodicea, Theodoret’s comment on Colossians 2:18, or the shrine of Michael at Chonae — and how much weight do they give it for the first century?
  • Why: the dig reports all three from recall [S] and notes they are centuries later (Historical Background, item 5). The answer should give the exact wording of the canon and of Theodoret, with dates.
  • Watch for: later evidence offered as proof of a first-century angel cult.

Checks to Make Outside the Assistant

These do not need the AI and cost no credits.

  1. The Clementine Vulgate at Colossians 2:18 and 2:23. Open the Vulgate at the verses and paste the Latin. The dig recalls quae non vidit ambulans (2:18) and non in honore aliquo ad saturitatem carnis (2:23) but has not checked them (2:16–23 dig, Open Question 5). They feed question 1.1.
  2. The KJV at Colossians 2:16–23. All KJV wording in the 2:16–23 dig is from recall and tagged [unverified]. A KJV export of Colossians, filed as 12-Colossians-KJV.txt in _texts/logos-exports/02-New-Testament/, would let every quotation be checked in one pass.
  3. Philo, De confusione linguarum 146–147 and De agricultura 51, in Greek, if Philo’s Greek text is in the library: to confirm εἰκών (“image”) and πρωτόγονος (“first-born”) and the standard section numbers (1:15–20 dig, Open Question 7). Round 2’s references looked like Yonge’s chapter numbers.
