## 1. Presenting situation (notes)

Narrator: third-person, retrospective, uses "to this day" repeatedly (1 Kgs 8:8; 9:13; 12:19; 2 Kgs 8:22; 10:27; 17:23, 34, 41) and cites written sources 33×. `[T]` for the retrospection and the source-citations. **Authorship, date and the "Deuteronomistic History" label are `[S]`, not `[T]` — the book names no author.** Audience: the canonical reader who has arrived via Samuel and stands after 25:21. Pressure: **the book's own question, asked at 1 Kgs 9:8–9** — *"Why has the LORD done thus to this land and to this house?"* — and answered at 2 Kgs 17:7–23 and 24:3–4. **Kings is a book that poses its own question in its first movement and spends thirty-nine chapters answering it.**

## 2. Structural arc map (draft, 5 movements)

| Movement | Function |
|---|---|
| 1 Kgs 1–11 | Solomon: throne secured, **temple built** (6–8), terms stated (9:1–9), apostasy, kingdom torn |
| 1 Kgs 12–16 | The division; **Jeroboam's sin defined** (12:26–33) and made the measure for every northern king |
| 1 Kgs 17 – 2 Kgs 13 | **The prophetic centre**: Elijah, Elisha, the house of Ahab, Jehu. The word runs the plot |
| 2 Kgs 14–17 | Israel's decline to exile; **ch. 17 is the book's theological verdict**, and it indicts Judah too (17:19) |
| 2 Kgs 18–25 | Judah alone: Hezekiah, Manasseh, Josiah — and **the temple burned** (25:9) |

**Binding device: the temple as inclusio** (built 1 Kgs 6–8 → burned 2 Kgs 25:9), with the **regnal formulae** as the metronome and the **word-fulfilment notices** as the engine.

## 3. Microscript (draft)

*The LORD gives David's son a throne and a house on stated terms; king after king breaks them, prophet after prophet announces the consequence, and not one word the LORD speaks falls to the ground — until the house is ash, the people are in Babylon, and a son of David is still eating at a foreign king's table.*

## 4. The word-fulfilment spine (the load-bearing thread)

## 5. The temple-treasury thread (new, countable, not yet checked against sources)

The temple is stripped **repeatedly, mostly by Judah's own kings**, long before Babylon: Shishak (1 Kgs 14:26) → Asa buys Aram (15:18) → Jehoash of Israel (2 Kgs 14:14) → Ahaz buys Assyria (16:8) → Hezekiah strips the doors (18:15–16) → Nebuchadnezzar (24:13) → burned (25:9, 13–17). **The vessels Solomon made are itemised as they leave.**

## 6. Early↔late echoes (draft)

- **1 Kgs 8:46–53** (Solomon prays for a people taken captive, that their captors show them compassion) → **2 Kgs 25:27–30** (Jehoiachin shown kindness at the Babylonian table). The book's own prayer, answered in the last four verses.
- **1 Kgs 9:8–9** (why has the LORD done thus?) → **2 Kgs 17:7–23; 24:3–4** (the answer).
- **1 Kgs 11:36 / 15:4; 2 Kgs 8:19** — the "lamp for David" refrain, why Judah outlives Israel.
- **1 Kgs 12:28** (the calves) → **2 Kgs 17:16; 23:15**.
- **1 Kgs 6:1** (480th year, temple begun) → **2 Kgs 25** (temple burned) — the book's chronological frame.
