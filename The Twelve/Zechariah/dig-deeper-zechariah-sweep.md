## Headline Findings

1. **The book is two halves bound by one hope.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Genre, Positional Necessity. Dated night-visions and encouragement (1–8) ground a discouraged rebuilding community; undated apocalyptic-messianic "burdens" (9–14) unveil the coming king and pierced shepherd — held together by the Branch/priest-king, the Spirit, the nations-ingathering, and the Day of the LORD.

2. **The priest-king Branch unites the two offices Israel kept separate.** `[T]`/`[I]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary (*ṣemaḥ*), Quotation/Allusion, Christological Reading. The *Branch* who "shall build the temple… and shall be a priest on his throne, and the counsel of peace shall be between them both" (6:12–13) — with the *high priest* Joshua crowned as the sign — points directly to Christ, priest and king (Heb 7).

3. **Zechariah is the Twelve's Passion-well.** `[T]`/`[S]` Surfaced by: Quotation/Allusion (forward), Christological Reading. The humble king on a donkey (9:9), the thirty pieces of silver thrown to the potter (11:12–13), the pierced one mourned (12:10), and the struck shepherd whose sheep scatter (13:7) form a cluster the NT applies to Jesus' final week — more Passion prophecy than any other book of the Twelve.

4. **God's whole work is done "not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit."** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Biblical Theology, Christological Reading. 4:6 is the pattern of the entire restoration — the temple built, the mountain levelled, the kingdom come — by the Spirit, not human strength.

5. **The book climbs to the universal reign of God.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Move 4, Biblical Theology. From "I have returned to Jerusalem with mercy" (1:16) to "the LORD will be **king over all the earth**… the LORD will be one and his name one" (14:9), with holiness pervading everything down to the horses' bells (14:20–21).

## Book-level Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose
To summon the post-exilic remnant to return to the LORD (1:3) and to lift their eyes beyond the modest present by unveiling God's jealous restoration of Zion, his cleansing of priest and people, his Spirit-empowered work through Zerubbabel, the coming Branch/priest-king, and — in the burdens — the humble king, the betrayed and pierced shepherd, and the Day when the LORD reigns over all. `[T]`/`[I]` Where Haggai pressed the *work*, Zechariah expands the *vision*.

### 2. Context — Positional Necessity within the Twelve
#### Positional Necessity Check
**Preceding movement:** Haggai has re-lit the Davidic hope (Zerubbabel the signet ring) and pressed the rebuilding. **Necessity — why Zechariah here:** the scroll needs the rebuilding generation's *messianic horizon* filled out — Zechariah develops Haggai's signet-ring hope into the **Branch** (3:8; 6:12), and hands the scroll its densest Passion material; Malachi then closes the trio pressing toward the coming Lord (Mal 3:1). `[T]`/`[I]`
**Implication:** preach Zechariah as the scroll's Christological summit — the Branch, the king, the pierced shepherd — grounded in the concrete 520 BC rebuilding but reaching to the cross and the consummation. `[I]`

**General context.** 520–518 BC (dated material) + undated burdens; the post-exilic remnant; two halves (visions/encouragement, then burdens).

### 3. Structure
Two halves (see overview arc map): dated night-visions + Joshua's crowning + fasting-oracles (1–8), then two undated *maśśāʾ* burdens (9–14). The Branch (3:8; 6:12), the Spirit (4:6), the nations (2:11; 8:23; 14:16), and the Day (14) bind the halves. `[T]`

### 4. Linking Words
- **"Return to me, and I will return to you" (1:3)** — the conditional-covenant hinge opening the book. `[T]`
- **"Not… nor… but" (4:6)** — the sharpest antithesis in the book: "not by might, nor by power, **but** by my Spirit." `[T]`
- **"Behold" (3:8; 6:12; 9:9)** — the demonstrative presenting the Branch and the coming king. `[T]`
- **"On that day" (throughout 9–14)** — the eschatological refrain structuring the burdens (esp. ch 12–14). `[T]`

### 9. Tone and Feel
The first half is **numinous and reassuring** — the strange, luminous night visions (horsemen, lampstand, flying scroll), each with an interpreting angel, and the tender "I am exceedingly jealous for Zion" (8:2). The second half swings between **martial grandeur** (the warrior LORD, the besieged and delivered city) and **piercing pathos** — the shepherd sold for thirty pieces of silver, the mourning "as for an only child" (12:10). The book ends in **cosmic triumph and pervasive holiness** (14). Soundtrack: a dream-sequence of glowing visions, rising to a battle-hymn and a coronation, shot through with a single grief-stricken lament (12:10). `[T]`

### 10. Repetition
"**The LORD of hosts**" (~50×, the book's authority-drumbeat); "**Return**" (1:3–6); "**Behold**"; "**on that day**" (esp. 12–14, ~17×); "**the Branch**" (3:8; 6:12); "**shepherd / flock**" (10–11; 13:7); "**Holy to the LORD / holy**" (14:20–21); "**nations / peoples**" joining (2:11; 8:22–23; 14:16). `[T]`

### 12. Genre
A composite: **symbolic vision-reports** with interpreting angel (1:7–6:8), a **sign-act** (the crowning, 6:9–15), **prophetic disputation/instruction** (7–8), and two **apocalyptic-oracle "burdens"** (*maśśāʾ*, 9–11; 12–14). `[T]` Reading rules: (1) the visions are revelatory symbols interpreted *within the text* by the angel — read the interpretation, don't allegorise the props; (2) chs 9–14 are apocalyptic — vivid figurative language for God's sure triumph, not a datable timetable; (3) the messianic texts are prophecy the NT applies to Christ — preach their gospel content. Tool 6 (Narrator's Comment) applies at the sign-act frames (6:9–11); Tool 13 (Copycat) largely **N/A** — the visions have no imitable human hero; the one enacted role (the prophet-shepherd, 11) is a sign, not a model.

## The Eleven Units

### Unit 1 — Zechariah 1:1–6 · Return to me
*"The LORD was very angry with your fathers… Return to me… and I will return to you" (1:2–3). "Do not be like your fathers, to whom the former prophets cried out… but they did not hear" (1:4). "Your fathers, where are they?… But my words… did they not overtake your fathers?" So they repented: "As the LORD of hosts purposed… so has he dealt with us" (1:5–6).*

**Purpose.** Open the book with the covenant summons — learn from the fathers who refused the prophets and were overtaken by God's word. `[T]`
**Quotation/Allusion.** "**Return to me, and I will return to you**" (1:3) — the post-exilic summons shared with **Malachi 3:7** and **Joel 2:12** (Move 2, Twelve-internal; **high**). `[T]` "The **former prophets**" (1:4) names the pre-exilic prophetic witness Zechariah stands on. `[T]`
**Internal echo (Move 4):** *planted* — the "return" (1:3) frames the whole book, cashed out as justice/mercy in 7:9–10 and consummated in the ingathered nations (8:23; 14:16). `[T]`
**So what?** The word of God outlasts the generations who ignore it ("did they not overtake your fathers?"); the summons is to return before the pattern repeats.

### Unit 2 — Zechariah 1:7–2:13 · Night visions 1–3: the LORD's jealous return to Zion
*Vision 1 (1:7–17): a man on a red horse among the myrtles; horsemen who have "patrolled the earth" report it "at rest." The angel: "How long will you have no mercy on Jerusalem?" The LORD: "I am exceedingly jealous for Jerusalem and for Zion… I have returned to Jerusalem with mercy; my house shall be built" (1:14–16). Vision 2 (1:18–21): four horns (the powers that scattered Judah) and four craftsmen to cast them down. Vision 3 (2:1–13): a man with a measuring line; "Jerusalem shall be inhabited as villages without walls… I will be to her a wall of fire all around, and I will be the glory in her midst" (2:4–5); "he who touches you touches the apple of his eye" (2:8); "many nations shall join themselves to the LORD in that day, and shall be my people" (2:11); "Be silent, all flesh, before the LORD" (2:13).*

**Purpose.** Reassure the remnant: the LORD is jealously returning to Zion, will cast down the scattering powers, and will be himself Jerusalem's wall and glory — with the nations joining his people. `[T]`
**Vocabulary.** "**Jealous** for Zion" (*qinʾâ*, 1:14; 8:2) — covenant zeal *for* his people; "**the apple of his eye**" (2:8) — the tender covenant image. `[T]`
**Quotation/Allusion.** *Ezekiel's measuring-and-glory* (Ezek 40–48; 43:1–5) → 2:1–5 — but with a twist: an *unwalled* city protected by God himself, the glory *within*. Move 1. `[S]` *"Many nations shall join" (2:11)* plants the ingathering thread (8:23; 14:16). `[T]`
**Internal echo (Move 4):** *planted* — "I have returned with mercy; my house shall be built" (1:16) opens toward the temple-and-Branch material (3–6) and the universal reign (14:9). `[T]`
**So what?** God's people are secured not by walls but by his presence; and their restoration is aimed, from the start, at the nations.

### Unit 3 — Zechariah 3:1–10 · Joshua cleansed; my servant the Branch
*Joshua the high priest stands before the angel, "and Satan (ha-śāṭān) standing at his right hand to accuse him" (3:1). "The LORD rebuke you, O Satan!… Is not this a brand plucked from the fire?" (3:2). Joshua's filthy garments are removed — "Behold, I have taken your iniquity away from you, and I will clothe you with pure vestments" — and a clean turban set on his head (3:4–5). The charge: walk in my ways (3:7). "Behold, I will bring my servant the Branch (ṣemaḥ)… and I will remove the iniquity of this land in a single day. In that day… you shall invite one another to come under his vine and under his fig tree" (3:8–10); the stone with seven eyes.*

**Purpose.** Cleanse the accused priesthood (and so the people it represents) and announce the coming Branch who will remove iniquity in a single day. `[T]`
**Vocabulary.** "**The Branch**" (*ṣemaḥ*, 3:8) — the Davidic messianic title (Isa 11:1; Jer 23:5; 33:15); "**Satan**" (*ha-śāṭān*, "the accuser") — the adversary rebuked. `[T]`
**Quotation/Allusion.**
**The Branch tradition (Isa 11:1; Jer 23:5; 33:15) → 3:8; 6:12** *(high confidence)* `[T]`
- *Source context (Move 1):* the *ṣemaḥ*/shoot from David's line, the righteous King who reigns in justice (Jer 23:5, "a righteous Branch… he shall reign as king").
- *What it adds:* Zechariah names the coming one "the Branch" and ties him to the *removal of iniquity in a single day* (3:9) — the atonement the priesthood's cleansing foreshadows.
**Internal echo (Move 4):** *planted* — "remove the iniquity of this land in a single day" (3:9) opens toward "a fountain… to cleanse them from sin" (13:1); the Branch (3:8) → 6:12. `[T]`
**Christological cross-report.** The accused priest cleansed by grace ("I have taken your iniquity away," 3:4) and the Branch who removes iniquity in a day is gospel in miniature — the Accuser silenced, the filthy clothed, the atonement promised. `[I]`
**So what?** God answers the Accuser not by denying guilt but by *removing* it ("a brand plucked from the fire") — cleansing given, not earned — and points to the Branch who does it "in a single day."

### Unit 4 — Zechariah 4:1–14 · Not by might, but by my Spirit
*A golden lampstand with seven lamps and two olive trees beside it (4:2–3). The word to Zerubbabel: "Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit, says the LORD of hosts" (4:6). "Who are you, O great mountain? Before Zerubbabel you shall become a plain" (4:7). "The hands of Zerubbabel have laid the foundation of this house; his hands shall also complete it" (4:9). "Whoever has despised the day of small things shall rejoice" (4:10). The two olive trees are "the two anointed ones (sons of oil) who stand by the Lord of the whole earth" (4:14).*

**Purpose.** Assure Zerubbabel that the temple will be finished — not by human power but by God's Spirit — and honour "the day of small things." `[T]`
**Vocabulary.** "**Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit**" (4:6) — the theological centre of the visions; "**the two anointed ones**" (*bĕnê ha-yiṣhār*, "sons of oil," 4:14) — most read as Joshua (priest) and Zerubbabel (governor), the two offices later united in the Branch (6:12–13). *Moderate–high on the referents.* `[T]`/`[S]`
**Internal echo (Move 4):** *develops* — the two anointed ones (4:14) and Zerubbabel's temple-building (4:9) feed the priest-king Branch who "shall build the temple" (6:12); "day of small things" (4:10) answers the discouragement of Haggai 2:3. `[T]`
**So what?** The kingdom advances by the Spirit, not human strength — dependence, not passivity (Zerubbabel still builds); and God does not despise small beginnings.

### Unit 5 — Zechariah 5:1–6:8 · Visions 6–8: the land purged
*Vision 6 (5:1–4): a flying scroll — the curse going out over the land against thieves and false swearers. Vision 7 (5:5–11): a basket (ephah) with a woman named Wickedness inside, sealed with a leaden weight, carried by two winged women to build it a house "in the land of Shinar" (Babylon). Vision 8 (6:1–8): four chariots (red, black, white, dappled horses) — "the four winds/spirits of heaven" patrolling; those going to the north country "have set my Spirit at rest."*

**Purpose.** Complete the vision cycle: sin is purged from the land (curse on wrongdoers; Wickedness personified exiled to Babylon) and God's rule patrols the whole earth. `[T]`
**Vocabulary / imagery.** "**Wickedness**" (*rišʿâ*, 5:8) personified as a woman removed to **Shinar** (Gen 10–11, Babel/Babylon — the archetypal place of rebellion) — evil returned to its source. `[I]` *moderate–high.*
**Internal echo (Move 4):** *develops* — the purging of the land (5–6) sets up the cleansing "fountain" of 13:1; the north-country chariots (6:8) prepare the crowning (6:9, exiles "from Babylon"). `[T]`
**So what?** Restoration is not only rebuilding but *purging* — sin and wickedness removed from the land — before the priest-king is crowned.

### Unit 6 — Zechariah 6:9–15 · The crown and the priest-king Branch
*Take silver and gold from the returned exiles, make a crown, and set it on the head of Joshua the high priest (6:9–11). "Behold, the man whose name is the Branch: for he shall branch out from his place, and he shall build the temple of the LORD. It is he who shall build the temple of the LORD and shall bear royal honor, and shall sit and rule on his throne. And there shall be a priest on his throne, and the counsel of peace shall be between them both" (6:12–13). The crown a memorial in the temple; "those who are far off shall come and help to build" (6:15).*

**Purpose.** The climax of the visions: crown the *priest* Joshua as a sign of the coming Branch who will be *both* king and priest — the two offices united in one person. `[T]`
**Quotation/Allusion.**
**The Branch → priest-king (6:12–13; cf. Ps 110)** *(high confidence — Christological)* `[T]`/`[I]`
- Crowning the *high priest* (not the Davidic governor) with a royal crown, and naming the Branch who "shall be a priest on his throne," fuses the offices Israel kept strictly separate (a king who usurped priesthood was judged — 2 Chr 26, Uzziah). The union anticipates the one who is both (Ps 110:4, "a priest forever"; Heb 7). `[I]`
- *What it adds:* "the counsel of peace shall be between them both" — in the Branch, kingship and priesthood are at peace in one person.
**Internal echo (Move 4):** *resolves* — the Branch (3:8) and the two anointed ones (4:14) converge here in one priest-king; *plants* — "those far off shall come and help to build" (6:15) → the nations (8:23; 14:16). `[T]`
**So what?** The hope is a single person who is both king and priest — the gospel's Christ; preach the union, not merely a temple-building sign.

### Unit 7 — Zechariah 7:1–8:23 · From fasting to feasting; the nations join
*Men ask whether to keep the fast mourning the temple's destruction (7:1–3). The LORD: "When you fasted… was it for me?" (7:5–6). The former prophets called for justice: "Render true judgments, show kindness and mercy… do not oppress the widow, the fatherless, the sojourner, the poor" (7:9–10) — but the fathers refused, made their hearts "diamond-hard," and wrath came (7:11–14). Chapter 8: "I am jealous for Zion… I have returned to Zion… Jerusalem shall be called the faithful city" (8:2–3); old men and women in the streets, boys and girls playing (8:4–5); "Let your hands be strong" (8:9, 13). The fasts shall become "seasons of joy and gladness and cheerful feasts" (8:19). "Many peoples and strong nations shall come to seek the LORD… Ten men from the nations of every tongue shall take hold of the robe of a Jew, saying, 'Let us go with you, for we have heard that God is with you'" (8:20–23).*

**Purpose.** Reframe worship — God wants justice and mercy, not ritual mourning — and promise a restored, joyful Jerusalem to which the nations stream. `[T]`
**Quotation/Allusion.** *The former-prophets ethic (7:9–10) → Amos 5; Hosea 6:6; Micah 6:8* — justice, *ḥesed*, care for the vulnerable; Zechariah re-issues the 8th-century summons. Move 1/OT-to-OT. `[S]`/`[I]`
**Internal echo (Move 4):** *develops the nations thread* — "many nations shall join" (2:11) → "ten men… take hold of the robe of a Jew" (8:23) → the nations keep Booths (14:16); *"God is with you" (8:23)* answers Haggai's "I am with you." `[T]`
**So what?** True worship is justice and mercy, not ritual; and the sign of God's presence draws the nations — "we have heard that God is with you."

### Unit 8 — Zechariah 9:1–17 · Behold, your king comes — humble, on a donkey
*A burden against the nations (Hadrach, Damascus, Tyre, Sidon, Philistia; 9:1–8). Then: "Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Zion!… Behold, your king is coming to you; righteous and having salvation is he, humble and mounted on a donkey, on a colt, the foal of a donkey. I will cut off the chariot… and the battle bow shall be cut off, and he shall speak peace to the nations; his rule shall be from sea to sea… to the ends of the earth" (9:9–10). "As for you also, because of the blood of my covenant with you, I will set your prisoners free from the waterless pit" (9:11); "prisoners of hope" (9:12); the warrior-LORD saves his flock "like the jewels of a crown" (9:16).*

**Purpose.** Announce the coming king — righteous, saving, and humble, riding a donkey, bringing peace to the nations and a worldwide reign. `[T]`
**Quotation/Allusion — the first Passion citation.**
**Zechariah 9:9 → Matthew 21:5; John 12:15** *(high confidence — NT-cited)* `[T]`/`[S]`
- *Source context (Move 1):* the king comes not on a war-horse but a donkey — "humble" (*ʿānî*, lowly/afflicted) and "having salvation" — and *cuts off* the war-horse and battle-bow (9:10); a peaceable, saving king whose rule is universal.
- *Forward:* Jesus deliberately enacts it at the Triumphal Entry (Matt 21:1–7; John 12:14–15 quotes it) — staging his kingship as Zechariah's humble king. `[S]`
- *What it adds:* the king's humility and peaceableness are not incidental — he comes to save, not to conquer by force; "the blood of my covenant" (9:11) already sounds the note of a covenant sealed in blood.
**Internal echo (Move 4):** *develops the Branch* — the king (9:9) is the Branch (3:8; 6:12) in action; *"blood of my covenant" (9:11)* → the struck shepherd (13:7) and the pierced one (12:10). `[T]`
**So what?** God's king comes lowly and saving, disarming war — the Palm Sunday king; preach his humility and his peace, not a triumphalist messiah.

### Unit 9 — Zechariah 10:1–11:17 · The shepherds and the thirty pieces of silver
*Ask rain of the LORD, not idols (10:1–2); "my anger is hot against the shepherds" (10:3); the LORD cares for his flock, Judah, and will strengthen and gather them (10:3–12). Then a doom-song: "Open your doors, O Lebanon" (11:1); the shepherds wail; three shepherds destroyed in a month (11:8). The prophet enacts shepherding "the flock doomed to slaughter," with two staffs, Favor and Union; he breaks Favor (11:10). "If it seems good to you, give me my wages… And they weighed out as my wages **thirty pieces of silver**. Then the LORD said to me, 'Throw it to the potter'—the lordly price at which I was priced by them. So I took the thirty pieces of silver and threw them into the house of the LORD, to the potter" (11:12–13). He breaks Union (11:14). The worthless shepherd raised up (11:15–17).*

**Purpose.** Contrast the LORD's care for his flock with the failure and rejection of the shepherds — and, in the sign-act, dramatise the rejection and paltry valuation of the true Shepherd. `[T]`
**Quotation/Allusion — the betrayal price.**
**Zechariah 11:12–13 → Matthew 27:9–10** *(high confidence — NT-cited; famous attribution crux)* `[T]`/`[S]`
- *Source context (Move 1):* the good shepherd, rejected, asks his wage; "thirty pieces of silver" — the price of a gored slave (Exod 21:32) — is a contemptuous valuation; the LORD directs it "to the potter… in the house of the LORD" (a scornful disposal).
- *Forward:* Judas is paid **thirty pieces of silver** (Matt 26:15) and, remorseful, throws them "into the temple"; the priests buy "the potter's field" (Matt 27:3–10). Matthew quotes the fulfilment but attributes it to "**Jeremiah**" — most likely a conflation with Jeremiah's potter/field/purchase material (Jer 18–19; 32) or citation under the name of the prophetic scroll's head; a noted crux, not an error to hide. `[S]`
- *What it adds:* the rejection and paltry pricing of the true Shepherd is scripted here — the betrayal money, the potter, the temple — a startlingly specific anticipation of the Passion.
**Internal echo (Move 4):** *develops* — the rejected shepherd (11) is the struck shepherd (13:7) and the pierced one (12:10). `[T]`
**So what?** The Shepherd God sends is valued at a slave's price and rejected — the depth of the people's contempt, and the shape of the betrayal; preach the rejected Shepherd who is nonetheless the LORD's own.

### Unit 10 — Zechariah 12:1–13:9 · The pierced one; the fountain; the struck shepherd
*A burden: the LORD makes Jerusalem "a cup of staggering" and "a heavy stone" to the nations, who are wounded gathering against it; the LORD saves and strengthens even "the feeblest… like David" (12:1–9). "And I will pour out on the house of David… a spirit of grace and pleas for mercy, so that, when they look on **me, on him whom they have pierced**, they shall mourn for him, as one mourns for an only child, and weep bitterly… as one weeps over a firstborn" (12:10); the families mourn (12:11–14). "On that day there shall be a **fountain** opened for the house of David… to cleanse them from sin and uncleanness" (13:1); idols and false prophets cut off (13:2–6). "Awake, O sword, against my shepherd, against the man who stands next to me… **Strike the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered**" (13:7); two-thirds cut off, one-third refined — "they will call upon my name, and I will answer them… 'They are my people'; and they will say, 'The LORD is my God'" (13:8–9).*

**Purpose.** Unveil the heart of the burdens: the pierced one mourned, the fountain of cleansing opened, and the shepherd struck so the flock is scattered and refined. `[T]`
**Quotation/Allusion — two Passion citations.**
**Zechariah 12:10 → John 19:37; Revelation 1:7** *(high confidence — NT-cited)* `[T]`/`[S]`
- *Source context (Move 1):* the LORD says "they will look on **me** (*ʾēlay*), on him whom they have pierced" — a startling first-person: God speaks of *being pierced* — and pours out "a spirit of grace" producing deep mourning "as for an only child." (The "me/him" alternation is a textual and referential crux — see Tool 8.)
- *Forward:* John 19:37 cites it of the spear-pierced Christ on the cross; Rev 1:7 of every eye seeing "him whom they pierced" at his return. `[S]`
- *What it adds:* the piercing is bound to *grace and mourning and cleansing* (13:1) — the crucified one looked upon in penitent grief and washed in the fountain his piercing opens.
**Zechariah 13:7 → Matthew 26:31; Mark 14:27** *(high confidence — NT-cited)* `[T]`/`[S]`
- *Source context (Move 1):* the sword against "my shepherd… the man who stands next to me" (a shepherd strikingly identified with the LORD) — struck, the sheep scatter, and a refined remnant remains.
- *Forward:* Jesus quotes it at Gethsemane as the scattering of the disciples at his arrest (Matt 26:31). `[S]`
- *What it adds:* the shepherd is struck *by God's own sword* ("Awake, O sword… against the man who stands next to me") — the divine purpose in the Shepherd's death, and the scattering-then-refining of the flock.
**Internal echo (Move 4):** *resolves* — "remove iniquity in a single day" (3:9) becomes the "fountain… to cleanse" (13:1); the rejected shepherd (11) is pierced (12:10) and struck (13:7). `[T]`
**So what?** The cluster is the gospel in Zechariah: the Shepherd struck by God's sword, the pierced one mourned, the fountain opened for sin — preach the atonement and the grace that produces mourning and cleansing, not just the fulfilment.

### Unit 11 — Zechariah 14:1–21 · The Day when the LORD is king over all
*"A day is coming for the LORD… I will gather all the nations against Jerusalem… the city shall be taken" (14:1–2). "Then the LORD will go out and fight against those nations… On that day his feet shall stand on the **Mount of Olives**… and the Mount of Olives shall be split in two" (14:3–4). "The LORD my God will come, and all the holy ones with him" (14:5). "On that day… at evening time there shall be light" (14:7). "On that day **living waters** shall flow out from Jerusalem, half to the eastern sea and half to the western sea" (14:8). "And the **LORD will be king over all the earth**. On that day the LORD will be one and his name one" (14:9). Jerusalem exalted and secure (14:10–11); plague on the nations that fought (14:12–15); "everyone who survives of all the nations… shall go up year after year to worship the King, the LORD of hosts, and to keep the **Feast of Booths**" (14:16). "On that day there shall be inscribed on the bells of the horses, '**Holy to the LORD**'… every pot in Jerusalem and Judah shall be holy… and there shall no longer be a trader in the house of the LORD" (14:20–21).*

**Purpose.** Bring the whole scroll to its climax: the LORD comes, defeats the nations, becomes king over all the earth, and sanctifies everything — cosmic reign and pervasive holiness. `[T]`
**Quotation/Allusion.**
**Ezekiel 47 (temple river) → 14:8; Day-of-the-LORD tradition → 14:1–9** *(high confidence)* `[T]`/`[S]` — the living waters from Jerusalem rework Ezekiel's temple-river (→ John 7:38; Rev 22:1); "the LORD will be king over all the earth" (14:9) gathers the Twelve's Day-of-the-LORD to its climax (→ Rev 11:15).
**The Mount of Olives (14:4)** — the mount Jesus ascended from and will return to (Acts 1:11–12); the split-mountain imagery read canonically toward the second coming. *Trajectory.* `[I]`
**Internal echo (Move 4):** *climaxes the book* — "I have returned to Jerusalem with mercy" (1:16) reaches "the LORD will be king over all the earth" (14:9); the nations who "join" (2:11) now "keep the Feast of Booths" (14:16). `[T]`
**So what?** History ends in the universal reign of the one LORD and the sanctification of all things — even the horses' bells read "Holy to the LORD." Preach the sure triumph and the all-pervading holiness, held as already/not-yet in Christ; don't reduce it to a datable timetable (Pitfalls).

## Tool 8 — Translations (book level)
- **12:10 — "they will look on **me**, on him whom they have pierced."** The MT reads *ʾēlay* ("to me" — the LORD pierced); a few witnesses/emendations read *ʾēlāyw* ("to him"). John 19:37 quotes a "him"-form. The theological weight — Yahweh speaks of being pierced — is a genuine crux and a striking pointer to the incarnation. **Translation-tradition note**, high significance. `[T]`/`[S]`
- **11:13 — "to the potter" (*yôṣēr*) vs "to the treasury" (*ʾôṣār*).** Some read "treasury" (a small consonantal difference); the MT "potter" is what Matthew follows. Flag. `[S]`
- **9:9 — "humble" (*ʿānî*)** and **"having salvation" (*nôšāʿ*, passive — "saved/endowed with salvation")** are stable and load-bearing; preserve. `[T]`

**Ancient versions check.** The **LXX of 12:10** and the "me/him" question is the notable case (the NT citation form); worth the preacher's awareness. *Moderate–high; recommend NETS and a commentary.* `[S]`

### Textual Variants
The one weighty case is **12:10** ("me"/"him"). **11:13** ("potter"/"treasury") is minor. Otherwise the Hebrew is stable; the chief difficulties are interpretive (the visions; the shepherd allegory of 11; ch 14) rather than text-critical. `[S]`

### Historical and Cultural Background
**Setting.** Post-exilic Yehud, 520–518 BC (dated material), the rebuilding under Darius I — the same context as Haggai (Ezra 5:1). **Zerubbabel** (Davidic governor) and **Joshua** (high priest) are the two leaders behind the "two anointed ones" (4:14) and the crowning (6:11). The undated burdens (9–14) refer to later powers — **ch 9's** sweep down the coast has been linked (by some) to **Alexander's campaign** (332 BC), a datum in the "Second Zechariah" composition debate. **The Mount of Olives** (14:4) is east of Jerusalem, across the Kidron. **Thirty pieces of silver** (11:12) is the compensation for a slave gored by an ox (Exod 21:32) — a deliberate insult. `[S]`

### Original Audience Reception
**Canonical audience:** eleventh in the Twelve, the scroll's Christological summit, completing the post-exilic trio's messianic hope. **The first hearers:** a small, discouraged rebuilding community needing their eyes lifted from a modest temple to God's vast future. `[T]`/`[S]`
**Surprises/comforts:** *reassuring* — God jealous for Zion, himself her wall and glory (2:5); *astonishing* — the Accuser rebuked and the filthy priest re-clothed (3:4); *stirring* — the humble king (9:9); *piercing* — the shepherd sold, struck, and pierced (11–13); *overwhelming* — the LORD king over all, everything holy (14).

**Candidate Fallen Condition Focus.**
| Field | Content |
|-------|---------|
| What they felt | Small, discouraged, and doubtful that God's great promises would ever land on a modest temple and a subject province — despising "the day of small things" (4:10). `[T]` |
| Candidate FCF (shared concern) | The temptation, amid small and discouraging circumstances, to lose sight of God's vast purposes and to doubt that his promised King and kingdom are really coming. |
| Shared / differs | *Shared:* discouragement at the gap between promise and present; despising small beginnings. *Differs:* we stand after the humble King has come, been pierced, and opened the fountain — and we await the Day when he is king over all. |
| Confidence | Anchored `[T]` (4:6, 10; the whole vision-and-burden structure). |

### Biblical-Theological Themes
**1. The Messiah — Branch, King, Shepherd.** The priest-king Branch (3:8; 6:12), the humble king (9:9), the pierced and struck shepherd (11–13) → Christ, in whom all converge (priest, king, and shepherd). `[T]`/`[S]` **2. The Spirit.** "Not by might… but by my Spirit" (4:6) — the kingdom advanced by the Spirit; living waters (14:8 → John 7:38). `[T]`/`[I]` **3. The nations and the universal reign.** From "many nations shall join" (2:11) to "the LORD will be king over all the earth" (14:9) and the nations keeping Booths (14:16) → the ingathering in Christ (Rev 11:15; 21–22). *Confluence — climactic:* the Branch/King/Shepherd, by the Spirit, gathers the nations to the universal reign of the one LORD. `[I]`

### Christological Reading
**Type:** direct prophecy (a dense NT-applied cluster) + typology/trajectory.
- **The priest-king Branch (3:8; 6:12–13)** — Christ, priest and king (Heb 7; Ps 110). *High (trajectory/typology).* `[I]`
- **The humble king (9:9 → Matt 21:5; John 12:15)** — the Triumphal Entry. *High (NT-cited).* `[T]`/`[S]`
- **The thirty pieces of silver (11:12–13 → Matt 27:9–10)** — the betrayal price and the potter's field. *High (NT-cited).* `[T]`/`[S]`
- **The pierced one (12:10 → John 19:37; Rev 1:7)** — the crucified looked upon and mourned. *High (NT-cited).* `[T]`/`[S]`
- **The struck shepherd (13:7 → Matt 26:31)** — Gethsemane's scattering. *High (NT-cited).* `[T]`/`[S]`
- **The fountain for sin (13:1); living waters (14:8); the LORD king over all (14:9)** — cleansing, Spirit, and consummated reign in Christ (John 7:38; Rev 11:15; 22:1). *High (trajectory).* `[I]`

**Moralism check.** Zechariah's dangers are allegorising the visions and mishandling the messianic texts (bare proof, or moralising the fragments). Corrective: the visions serve one message (God's zealous restoration climaxing in the Branch); the Passion texts are *gospel* — the humble saving King, the Shepherd struck *for* the sheep by God's own sword, the pierced one mourned, the fountain opened. Preach Christ the priest-king-shepherd who accomplishes the cleansing. `[I]`
**Confidence:** high across the NT-cited cluster; high on the Branch and the universal reign.

### Difficult / Contested Verses
- **12:10 "me… whom they have pierced."** *Category: doctrinal/textual.* *Handling:* preach the incarnational wonder (Yahweh pierced) and the grace-and-mourning it produces; name the "me/him" crux. `[T]`/`[S]`
- **11:12–13 / Matthew 27:9 "Jeremiah."** *Category: apologetic.* *Handling:* explain the conflation/attribution rather than be ambushed. `[S]`

- **Chapter 11 shepherd allegory (the three shepherds).** *Category: obscure.* *Handling:* the specific referents are debated; preach the clear thrust (the true Shepherd rejected and paltry-priced) and flag the obscurity. `[S]`
- **Composition of 9–14 ("Second Zechariah").** *Category: critical.* *Handling:* note the debate; the canonical book is one, and its messianic reading holds regardless. `[S]`

## Cross-Passage Convergent Findings — Zechariah as one argument, and the Twelve's Christological summit

### A. Threads across Zechariah's own units
1. **The Branch developing into King and Shepherd** (3:8; 6:12 → 9:9 → 11–13): one messianic figure unfolds across the book — priest-king, humble king, rejected/pierced/struck shepherd. `[T]`/`[I]`

Read as a chapter of the scroll, Zechariah is where the whole prophetic hope focuses on the coming, rejected, pierced, and finally reigning Messiah — the material the Gospels reach for most to interpret the cross, and Malachi presses toward as "the Lord [who] will suddenly come to his temple." `[T]`/`[I]`

## Preaching Pitfalls
### Pitfall: Allegorising the night visions
- *Looks like:* decoding every prop of the visions.
- *Wrong because:* each vision makes one interpreted point in a sequence of restoration.
- *Corrective:* read the angel's interpretation; preach the panorama, not a code.

### Pitfall: Preaching the Passion texts as bare proof
- *Looks like:* apologetic ammunition for "the Bible predicted it."
- *Wrong because:* they are gospel — the humble saving king, the shepherd struck for the sheep, the pierced one mourned.
- *Corrective:* preach their content; the fulfilment serves the gospel, not vice versa.

### Pitfall: The Matt 27:9 "Jeremiah" crux left as a gotcha
- *Looks like:* ignoring or being ambushed by the attribution.
- *Wrong because:* it is a known conflation/attribution, explicable.
- *Corrective:* explain it briefly and move to the substance.

### Pitfall: Chapter 14 as an end-times timetable
- *Looks like:* mapping the split mountain and living waters onto a datable programme.
- *Wrong because:* it is apocalyptic vision for God's sure triumph and universal reign.
- *Corrective:* preach the certain reign of the one LORD, already/not-yet in Christ.

### Pitfall: "Not by might" (4:6) as anti-effort
- *Looks like:* a slogan for passivity.
- *Wrong because:* it grounds Zerubbabel's *actual building* in the Spirit's power.
- *Corrective:* preach Spirit-dependent work, not no work.
