Claim Audit: Obadiah

Passages audited: Obadiah 1–21, as carried by book-overview-obadiah-2026-11-04 (v1.1) and dig-deeper-obadiah-1-1to21-2026-11-04 (v1.0) Date: 4 November 2026 Purpose: To test the twenty queued allusion and design claims before any sermon backbone Method: Two blind auditors in turn, working only from the primary texts, then a reproduction of decisive results in the main session. Auditor A took the single contacts and Auditor B the field and design claims Editions: WLC by lemma (Hebrew versification), Swete, Rahlfs, SBLGNT, BHS with apparatus

Verdict in Brief

The sturdy results stand. Most of what the fresh dig added does not. Jeremiah 49, Joel, Micah 7 and the Balaam cluster hold up, along with the verbal link to Jer 38:22. Of the claims the November dig newly proposed, only Mic 7:8–10 kept its rating. Gen 27:41, Deut 32:35, Prov 27:10, Hab 2, the land left undone and Isa 63 all came down, and Prov 27:10 is discarded.

The main cause is one method fault, and it is the proposing run’s own. Those claims rested on “these words stand in one verse only”, with the words drawn from scattered verses of Obadiah. Auditor A’s baseline shows how little that means: 14.2 % of the verses of the Hebrew Bible hold a three-word set from Obadiah’s vocabulary found in no other verse (30 control passages average 8.7 %). A scattered “only” is a candidate, not evidence. What survives has shape: an ordered phrase, a shared construction, or a tight cluster in the source.

Corrected outright: Rom 12:19 quotes the first colon of Deut 32:35, not the “near day” line, so it does not connect to Obad 15 by allusion. It remains good theology against revenge on its own terms. Also: the odds stated for the three-block structure were badly overstated, and “the only plural you in the book” is inexact.

New, and worth keeping (from the auditors, unprompted):

  • Isa 10:17–20 at Obad 17–18. Fire, flame, consume, and פְּלֵיטַת בֵּית־יַעֲקֹב (“the escaped of the house of Jacob”).

  • Ps 137:7–8 at Obad 15. גְּמוּל (“recompense”) with a second-person suffix: Ps 137:8 and Obad 15 only. Test with care, since v. 8 addresses Babylon.
  • Num 20:14 at Obad 10. אָחִיךָ יִשְׂרָאֵל (“your brother Israel”), the nearest frame for אָחִיךָ יַעֲקֹב (“your brother Jacob”).
  • Judg 2:16, 18 and Neh 9:27 at Obad 21. The Judges pattern is the nearer model for “deliverers… to judge”.

  • Ps 38:12 and 2 Sam 18:13 at Obad 11. The relatives of “stood aloof”.

  • Eight prohibitions in three verses at Obad 12–14 are the densest such chain in the WLC, on Auditor A’s floor count. It is unique to Obadiah, and this tells against calling it a wisdom form.

Critical Gaps

  • No history of interpretation. Every such entry is from an auditor’s memory, tagged [S], and moved no verdict. It needs Logos.
  • Direction of dependence stays open throughout. “Shares wording with” is all that any of this establishes.
  • Baselines are lexical. They do not see syntax or genre, and the rival search is lexical too.
  • One translator of the Twelve may account for some Greek agreement within the Twelve. This limits its independent weight (Hab 2:17, Joel).
  • Apparatus findings remain unchecked for manuscript spread: vv. 7, 11, 16, 17, 20, 21.
  • A single audit by the same model family. The auditors were blind to the reports, not to their shared habits.

Report of Auditor A: Single Contacts

Hebrew is the WLC in Hebrew versification unless stated. Greek is Swete unless marked Rahlfs; Swete’s own numbers differ in places (Swete Joel 3 = Hebrew Joel 4; Swete Joel 2:32 = Hebrew Joel 3:5; Swete Jer 45:22 = Hebrew Jer 38:22; Greek Ps 136 = Hebrew Ps 137). Anything from memory is tagged [S] and carries no verdict. Throughout, “shares wording with” means only that; no direction is assumed.

Two baselines that govern every “only”

Positive controls passed first: the search for עֵד (“witness”), קוּם (“rise”) and עָנָה (“testify”) returned Deut 19:16 and Job 16:8, and my own scripts matched the standard search on known words before I trusted them.

Baseline 1: exclusive sets of words. I took Obadiah’s 141 content words (particles, pronouns and the divine name removed) and asked how many verses in the Hebrew Bible hold a set drawn from them that occurs in no other verse outside Obadiah, ignoring where the words stand in Obadiah.

Set Verses with such a set Share of 23,213 30 random 21-verse prophetic or psalm passages: mean (range)
Two words 764 3.3% 567 (115–1,105)
Three words 3,295 14.2% 2,031 (522–4,150)
Four words 3,566 15.4% 1,969 (411–4,248)

So one verse in seven can claim “these three words, only here and in Obadiah”. Spread across Obadiah, an “only” triple or quadruple is nearly worthless. It shows nothing about borrowing unless shape or sense adds to it. Gen 27:41, Ps 7:17, Prov 27:10 and Ps 41:10 all fall inside this large class.

Baseline 2: exclusive two-word phrases. Of Obadiah’s 260 distinct ordered two-word sequences, 33 (12.7%) stand in exactly one other verse. Forty random 21-verse passages average 24.7 (range 6–44). Fifteen of the 33 are Jer 49, which is expected. Others are plainly accidental: וְהָיָה קֹדֶשׁ (“and it will be holy”) with Exod 40:9; לְהַכְרִית אֶת (“to cut off”) with 2 Chr 22:7. A two-word “only” is therefore weak by itself.

A caution on rank. Weighting shared vocabulary by rarity, Jer 49:16 ranks first of 23,192 verses. Third is 1 Sam 17:28, where Eliab rebukes David: אָח (“brother”), זָדוֹן (“arrogance”), לֵבָב (“heart”), יָרַד (“go down”). Nobody would call that a source. Vocabulary density measures vocabulary.

Chapter ranks by the same measure, of 922 chapters of five or more verses: Jer 49, 2nd; Joel 4, 7th; Lam 2, 38th; Gen 27, 63rd; Ezek 35, 80th; Jer 38, 153rd; Joel 3, 216th (2nd on the rare-contact scan, behind only Jer 49; Joel 4 is 14th there); Mic 7, 357th; Hab 2, 388th; Ps 137, 416th; Lam 4, 534th; Deut 32, 555th; Prov 27, 624th; Ps 41, 637th; Prov 24, 658th; Ps 7, 684th; Ps 22, 779th. Joel 4 and Jer 49 alone stand out. Josh 17 and Judg 20 also outrank Joel 4, so even that rank is suggestive only.

A1. Joel 3:5 at Obad 17

Tested. פְּלֵיטָה (“those who escape”) with צִיּוֹן (“Zion”) and הַר (“mount”); ordered phrases; פְּלֵיטָה with שָׂרִיד (“survivor”); rivals for the join of vv. 17–18.

Results. Stated counts hold exactly: 2 Kgs 19:31, Isa 37:32, Joel 3:5, Obad 17. פְּלֵיטָה with צִיּוֹן alone gives the same four. פְּלֵיטָה with שָׂרִיד: Joel 3:5 only. The claim understates the shape. The ordered phrase תִּהְיֶה פְלֵיטָה (“there will be those who escape”) stands in Joel 3:5 and Obad 17 only. Both have בְּהַר־צִיּוֹן (“on Mount Zion”) before it, Joel inserting וּבִירוּשָׁלִַם (“and in Jerusalem”). Kings and Isaiah differ in shape: וּפְלֵיטָה מֵהַר צִיּוֹן (“and those who escape from Mount Zion”), with a different verb. כַּאֲשֶׁר אָמַר יְהוָה (“as the LORD has said”) is unique to Joel 3:5 as a phrase. Greek: Swete Joel 2:32 ἐν τῷ ὄρει Σειὼν καὶ ἐν Ἰερουσαλὴμ ἔσται ἀνασῳζόμενος (“on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem there will be one who escapes”) against Obad 17 ἐν δὲ τῷ ὄρει Σειὼν ἔσται σωτηρία (“on Mount Zion there will be salvation”; Rahlfs ἡ σωτηρία): the frame survives, the noun does not.

Rival not named. Isa 10:17–20. Isa 10:20 is the only other verse with פְּלֵיטָה and יַעֲקֹב (“Jacob”): פְּלֵיטַת בֵּית־יַעֲקֹב (“the escaped of the house of Jacob”). Isa 10:17 reads וְהָיָה אוֹר־יִשְׂרָאֵל לְאֵשׁ וּקְדוֹשׁוֹ לְלֶהָבָה (“the light of Israel will become a fire and his Holy One a flame”), with אָכַל (“consume”). With “flame”, “escaped” and “Jacob” within three verses, only Isa 10:17–20 and Obad 17–18 qualify. Isaiah is a real competitor for the idea but not for the wording of v. 17.

Direction. Joel’s formula marks a prior word without naming it. Obadiah’s simpler line, with Joel expanded by “and in Jerusalem” and the formula, leans towards Joel as secondary, but Isa 37:32 could equally be the “word”, and a shared Zion tradition cannot be excluded.

A2. Joel 4:3, 4, 7, 14, 17, 19

Tested. All stated sets; frames; Greek; leave-one-out on Joel 4’s rank.

Results. Every stated count holds. יַדּוּ גוֹרָל (“cast lots”): Joel 4:3, Obad 11, Nah 3:10. גְּמוּל (“recompense”) with רֹאשׁ (“head”) and שׁוּב (“return”): Joel 4:4, 7 and Obad 15. חָמָס (“violence”) with אֱדוֹם (“Edom”): Joel 4:19 only. זָר (“stranger”) with יְרוּשָׁלִַם (“Jerusalem”): Joel 4:17, Obad 11.

How each one fares. - Lots. Not private to Joel. The closest frame is Nahum’s: וְעַל־נִכְבַּדֶּיהָ יַדּוּ גוֹרָל (“and for her nobles they cast lots”) matches וְעַל־יְרוּשָׁלִַם יַדּוּ גוֹרָל (“and for Jerusalem they cast lots”) in waw, preposition and order, while Joel has וְאֶל־עַמִּי (“and to my people”). Nah 3:10 also has גַּם (“also”) nearby, as Obad 11 does. In Greek, however, ἔβαλον κλήρους (“they cast lots”) joins Joel and Obadiah, with Nahum future βαλοῦσιν (Rahlfs) and also Jon 1:7 and 1 Chr 26. - Recompense on the head. The strongest. Joel’s verb is causative with God as subject, Obadiah’s intransitive, but “recompense” with “head” occurs nowhere else. Greek confirms it: ἀνταπόδομα (“recompense”) with κεφαλή (“head”) stands in Swete Joel 3:4, 7, Obad 15 and Sir 17:23 only. - Violence and Edom. Obadiah has Edom at vv. 1 and 8 and violence at v. 10, so the “pair” is not a same-verse pair there. The better contact is unstated: Joel 4:19 מֵחֲמַס בְּנֵי יְהוּדָה (“for the violence against the sons of Judah”) against Obad 10 מֵחֲמַס אָחִיךָ (“because of violence to your brother”) and Obad 12 לִבְנֵי־יְהוּדָה (“over the sons of Judah”). Both have the same preposition and the same objective genitive. חָמָס with יְהוּדָה (“Judah”) occurs only at Ezek 8:17 and Joel 4:19. The Greek does not preserve it: ἐξ ἀδικιῶν (“from injustices”) against διὰ… ἀσέβειαν (“for impiety”). - Strangers and Jerusalem. Weak. Exclusive pair, but Joel negates (strangers shall no longer pass through) and Obadiah narrates. “Strangers came” has a nearer sentence at Jer 51:51, בָּאוּ זָרִים (“strangers came”) against the sanctuary. - Near day (4:14). כִּי קָרוֹב יוֹם יְהוָה (“for the day of the LORD is near”) stands verbatim in Isa 13:6, Joel 1:15, 4:14, Obad 15 and Zeph 1:7. It is a shared formula and adds nothing.

Missed by the claim. לִשְׁפֹּט אֶת (“to judge”) with nations/Edom as object: Joel 4:12 and Obad 21 (plus Exod 18:13, 1 Kgs 3:9). הַר קָדְשִׁי (“my holy mountain”): Joel 4:17 and Obad 16 among nine verses. קֹדֶשׁ (“holy”) as predicate of the place: Joel 4:17 וְהָיְתָה יְרוּשָׁלִַם קֹדֶשׁ (“and Jerusalem will be holy”) against Obad 17 וְהָיָה קֹדֶשׁ (“and it will be holy”). כִּי יְהוָה דִּבֵּר (“for the LORD has spoken”): Joel 4:8 and Obad 18 among nine verses plain, so idiom but near the limit. The shape of the clause is the same: it closes a dispossession or destruction promise.

Baseline. Joel 4 is 7th of 922 chapters on rarity-weighted overlap. Removing any one of seventeen shared words leaves it between 6th and 12th, so the rank does not hang on one word. Joel 3 ranks 2nd on the rare-contact scan. This is the only claimed partner other than Jer 49 that beats the null at chapter level.

A3. Ps 22:29 at Obad 21

Tested. Ordered phrase; the noun’s whole range; frame; Greek; syntax.

Results. The ordered phrase לַיהוָה הַמְּלוּכָה (“to the LORD the kingdom”) stands in Obad 21 and Ps 22:29 only, plain and skeletal: count holds. מְלוּכָה (“kingship”) is in 23 verses.

Breaking it. 1. Baseline 2 applies: an exclusive two-word phrase is one of 33 in Obadiah. 2. The frame is a formula. לַיהוָה (“to the LORD”) plus a definite noun recurs: הַיְשׁוּעָה (“the salvation”) at Ps 3:9, הַתְּשׁוּעָה (“the victory”) at Prov 21:31, הָאָרֶץ (“the earth”) at Exod 9:29 and Ps 24:1. 3. Syntax differs. Psalm is verbless; Obad 21 is וְהָיְתָה (“and it will be”). The nearest syntax for “kingship will be X’s” is 1 Kgs 2:15, לִי הָיְתָה הַמְּלוּכָה (“the kingship was mine”). 4. Greek diverges in case: Ps 21:29 τοῦ κυρίου ἡ βασιλεία (“the kingdom is the Lord’s”), Obad 21 τῷ κυρίῳ ἡ βασιλεία (“the kingdom [shall be] to the Lord”). Same in Rahlfs for the Psalm. 5. Ps 22 ranks 779th of 922 on overlap; there is no surrounding support.

In its favour. מְלוּכָה (“kingship”) with גּוֹי (“nation”) in one verse is Ps 22:29 only, and “nations” is a key word of Obad 15–16; both are climactic universal statements. Of seven verses joining “kingship” and the LORD, only these two predicate kingship of him. A thematic rival worth noting: Isa 34:12, וְאֵין־שָׁם מְלוּכָה (“and no kingship there”), an oracle about Edom, is the negative of Obadiah’s close. Rev 11:15 is about the same theme but does not quote either.

A4. Ps 41:10 and Jer 38:22 at Obad 7

Tested. Both sets; ordered phrases; Greek; apparatus; rivals. The lemma tagging places אַנְשֵׁי (“men of”) under אִישׁ (“man”) and keeps אֱנוֹשׁ (“man”) separate, so I searched both.

Results. אִישׁ, שָׁלוֹם (“peace”) and לֶחֶם (“bread”): Obad 7 and Ps 41:10 only. Holds. אִישׁ, שָׁלוֹם and יָכֹל (“prevail”): Obad 7 and Jer 38:22 only. Holds. אַנְשֵׁי שְׁלֹמֶךָ (“men at peace with you”): those two only, plain and skeletal, in the same defective spelling.

Jer 38:22 is much tighter than claimed. The frame is the same across five slots: - Jer 38:22: הִסִּיתוּךָ וְיָכְלוּ לְךָ אַנְשֵׁי שְׁלֹמֶךָ (“they have misled you and prevailed over you, the men at peace with you”) - Obad 7: הִשִּׁיאוּךָ יָכְלוּ לְךָ אַנְשֵׁי שְׁלֹמֶךָ (“they have deceived you, they have prevailed over you, the men at peace with you”)

Three consecutive words differ only by a waw; a deception verb with second-person object comes immediately before. There אָמַרְתָּ בִלְבָבְךָ (“you said in your heart”) joins כּוֹכָב (“star”) in the same verse, and Isa 14:15 has תּוּרָד (“you will be brought down”). “Say in heart” with “star” occurs in one verse only: Isa 14:13. The clause is also absent from Jer 49:16, the parallel: it is a distinctive plus in Obadiah and deserves its own note.

A7. Prov 27:10 and the wisdom register, Obad 10–14

Tested. The set; the sense; the phrase אַל־תָּבוֹא (“do not enter”); rivals for “aloof” and for gloating; distribution of אֵיד (“calamity”); prohibition chains.

Results. אָח (“brother”), בּוֹא (“enter”) and אֵיד (“calamity”): Prov 27:10 only. Stronger: אָח with אֵיד alone is Prov 27:10 only. But it is one of the 3,295-class triples, and its high rank (326th) reflects common words.

Breaking it. - Sense. Proverbs: do not go to your brother’s house in the day of your calamity, because a near neighbour is better. Obadiah: do not enter my people’s gate in the day of their calamity. The word “brother” stands in a different verse. The two passages have opposite subjects and opposite motives. No reader needs Proverbs to read Obad 13. - אַל־תָּבוֹא (“do not enter”). Prov 24:17 בִּנְפֹל אוֹיִבְךָ אַל־תִּשְׂמָח (“when your enemy falls, do not rejoice”) does share a prohibition, and Greek μὴ ἐπιχαρῇς (“do not rejoice over”) stands in the second person in Swete only at Prov 24:17 and Obad 12. Prohibitions with אַל (“do not”) and a verb number 676 in my floor count: Psalms 109, Jeremiah 85, Proverbs 84. It is unique to Obadiah. - Prov 24:29 כַּאֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה־לִי כֵּן אֶעֱשֶׂה־לּוֹ (“as he has done to me, so I will do to him”) is forbidden speech, the inverse of Obad 15’s divine talion. A12. Sound plays in Obad 11–13

(a) אֵידָם (“their disaster”) and אֱדוֹם (“Edom”)

Counts verified. אֵיד (“calamity”) is in 22 verses, 24 occurrences. In one verse with אֱדוֹם (“Edom”): none. With עֵשָׂו (“Esau”): Jer 49:8 only (Edom at 49:7). Within three verses of שֵׂעִיר (“Seir”): Ezek 35:5. So it occurs in three Edom settings, with Obad 13’s three uses.

Baseline. Edom oracles make up about 78 verses (0.34%) on my rough count (Isa 34; Jer 49:7–22; Ezek 25:12–14; 35; Amos 1:11–12; Obad; Mal 1:2–5). Three of 22 verses by chance has a probability near 0.00006, so אֵיד (“calamity”) does lean to Edom. But it also leans to oracles against nations generally: Jer 46:21, 48:16, 49:32. And 3 of 6 such settings is not extreme.

Sense and form. The consonants match when the vowel letters are removed: both reduce to א־ד־ם. But the form with final ם is one of three in Obad 13, the other two being אֵידוֹ (“his disaster”). And אֵידָם (“their disaster”) is the commonest form of the word, not a specially chosen one: it stands in 9 of the 22 verses. Ezek 35:5 is therefore thin support by form, though a real one by setting. Better support for the general habit: Gen 25:30 (הָאָדֹם, “the red stuff”, with Edom named), Ezek 35:6 (דָּם, “blood”, four times, an Edom oracle), Isa 63:1. The Greek flattens it with three different words: πόνων (“toils”), ὀλέθρου (“ruin”), ἀπωλείας (“destruction”). No ancient hearer’s evidence for the pun survives there. The BHS apparatus proposes אוֹנוֹ (“his sorrow”) and אָבְדוֹ (“his perishing”) for two of the three from that Greek; these are only proposals, but the triple repetition is not beyond question.

Alternative explanation. The triple אֵיד (“calamity”) may be driven by the refrain בְּיוֹם… (“in the day of…”), and by a word at home in oracle language.

Verdict (a): Uncertain — flag for research. Low–moderate. The resemblance is real and the setting supports it. Design cannot be shown. Say “may be heard as”, not “plays on”.

(b) נָכְרוֹ (“his misfortune”) and נָכְרִים (“foreigners”)

Counts verified. נֵכֶר (“misfortune”) occurs in two verses: Obad 12 and Job 31:3. נָכְרִי (“foreign”) occurs in about 45 verses. In Obadiah the two stand in adjacent verses and never meet elsewhere: a word found only twice in the Hebrew Bible is used immediately after its near-homonym.

Missed. Job 31:3 הֲלֹא־אֵיד לְעַוָּל וְנֵכֶר לְפֹעֲלֵי אָוֶן (“is it not calamity for the unjust, and misfortune for workers of iniquity?”) pairs נֵכֶר (“misfortune”) with אֵיד (“calamity”). This is the same pair as Obad 12–13, and the only other use. So the choice of נֵכֶר may be driven by a word pair and not by sound. That rival must stand beside the sound-play view.

Greek. Both editions read Obad 12 as ἐν ἡμέρᾳ ἀλλοτρίων (“in the day of strangers”), with no pronoun and a different meaning from the Hebrew “misfortune”. V. 11 has ἀλλότριοι (“strangers”) for נָכְרִים (“foreigners”), and ἀλλογενῶν (“foreigners”) for זָרִים (“strangers”). This is a reading: the translator understood the root as “foreign”. It proves the words sounded related to an ancient reader. It does not prove a pun in Hebrew. Greek Job 31:3 has ἀπαλλοτρίωσις (“estrangement”), showing the same instinct elsewhere.

Verdict (b): Confirmed with nuance. Moderate. In the Hebrew this is a play on one root across adjacent verses, supported by an ancient reading. The word pair at Job 31:3 is a live alternative for why this word was chosen. Report the Greek as a reading.

B9. Who drank at Obad 16?

Tested. Plural forms in the book; all 71 verses of שָׁתָה (“drink”) in the Prophets, Lamentations, Psalms and Job, classed by what liquid or wrath word stands within three verses; forms of the “as … so” sentence; Greek and BHS.

Counts and corrections. - כּוֹס (“cup”) is absent from Obadiah; Greek likewise has no ποτήριον (“cup”). - גּוֹרָל (“lot”) with “drink”: Joel 4:3 only. - “Only plural you in the book” is not exact. קוּמוּ (“rise!”, v. 1) is a masculine plural imperative, addressed to the nations. תִּשְׁלַחְנָה (v. 13, “you shall stretch out”) is formally feminine plural, though BHS corrects it. Correct form: “the only plural you in a finite indicative; every address to Edom elsewhere is singular, apart from the form in v. 13 that BHS corrects.”

Pattern. - Every other wrath use has a named cup, wine or wrath within three verses: Isa 51:17, 22; Jer 25:16, 26, 27, 28; 49:12; 51:7; Ezek 23:32, 34; Hab 2:16; Ps 75:9; Job 6:4; 21:20. Fourteen of fourteen. - Absolute “drink” as wrath is well attested (Jer 25:16, 26, 27; Hab 2:16), but only with a cup nearby. Obad 16 is the only wrath use with nothing nearby, and this applies to the undisputed second half (nations drink, stagger, vanish) as much as to the first. - So absence of כּוֹס (“cup”) cannot argue against (b), since the sentence’s own sense needs wrath without a cup anyway. - Revelling use without liquid is almost always paired with אָכַל (“eat”): Isa 21:5, 65:13, Jer 22:15, Zech 7:6. Obad 16 has no “eat”. Amos 4:1, “bring, that we may drink”, is the closest: oppressors on הַר שֹׁמְרוֹן (“Mount Samaria”). Amos 2:8 has wine in God’s house. Joel 4:3 (lots, then wine and drinking), with יַדּוּ גוֹרָל (“cast lots”) found only at Joel 4:3, Obad 11 and Nah 3:10, is the best support for reading (a). - לָעוּ (“they will swallow”/“be rash”): only Obad 16 and Job 6:3. Job 6:4 next says of God’s arrows חֲמָתָם שֹׁתָה רוּחִי (“my spirit drinks their venom”, the word also meaning “wrath”). That small contact points to wrath for the second half.

The sentence. - “As you did, so to you” (v. 15, Lev 24:19, Judg 1:7, Jer 50:15) keeps one party in both limbs, favouring (a) if v. 16 is parallel to v. 15. - But “as … so” with a change of party is also normal: Isa 10:11, Jer 7:14, Ezek 12:11, 24:22. - The grammar helps (b): if “you plural” were Edom and allies, they are mostly the same people as “all the nations” and yet are addressed and then spoken of in the third person. A plural contrasting with “all the nations” fits Judah better. - Against (b): no marker of an address to Judah, in a book that otherwise addresses only Edom and (v. 1) the nations. - Transfer of the cup, in Jer 25:28–29, 49:12, Isa 51:17–23, Lam 4:21–22, always goes from Jerusalem to others. Jer 49:12, in the very oracle Obadiah shares, has that logic: those not sentenced drink, and so will you.

Versions. - The Hebrew plural is firm: the BHS apparatus has no note on שְׁתִיתֶם (“you drank”). - Greek has ἔπιες (“you drank”, singular) in both Swete and Rahlfs, making Edom the drinker. It may simply harmonise with vv. 2–15’s singular; the plural is the harder reading. - Rahlfs adds οἶνον (“wine”) after “all the nations”; Swete lacks “all the nations wine” altogether. That gives Greek reading a carousal colour. - Many manuscripts have סָבִיב (“around”) for תָּמִיד (“continually”), BHS. Compare Zech 12:2. - [S] As I recall, the Targum has “as you rejoiced” over the holy mountain and the Vulgate keeps the plural. Neither moves the verdict. - The early versions show that the verse was already read with Edom as subject, without settling the Hebrew.

Test Result
Counts verified Mostly; “only plural you” needs correction
Baseline Wrath use without a cup is unique, but this affects both readings
Sense (a) needs the verb to change sense between limbs; (b) keeps one sense but needs an unmarked change of addressee
Alternative explanation Wordplay on both senses is possible

Verdict: Uncertain. Lean: slightly to (b), as previously, with low–moderate confidence. The argument that holds is not the absence of “cup” but the plural contrasting with “all the nations”, the single sense of the verb, and Jer 49:12’s logic. Joel 4:3, Amos 2:8 and the Greek keep (a) alive. A preacher should say both are defensible and not build on either.

New candidates

  • Joel 3:5 with Obad 14, 17, 18. The only verse with both פְּלֵיטָה (“escape”) and שָׂרִיד (“survivor”), with “on Mount Zion”, and carrying כַּאֲשֶׁר אָמַר יְהוָה (“as the LORD has said”). That formula is the nearest thing to a marked citation in this field and may bear on direction between Joel and Obadiah; 2 Kgs 19:31 and Isa 37:32 are rival sources. Worth its own audit.
  • Joel 4:19 with Obad 10, 12. חָמָס (“violence”) with Edom: the only such verse, also with “sons of Judah”. A stronger handoff in the Greek order, where Joel precedes Obadiah.
  • Judg 2:16, 18 and Neh 9:27 behind Obad 21. “Deliverers … to judge” as the Judges pattern, a nearer model than Isa 63.
  • Isa 14:13–15 with Obad 3–4. Said in the heart, stars, brought down: a closer model for Obadiah’s pluses against Jeremiah than anything in Jer 49.
  • Job 39:27–28 with Obad 3–4 and Jer 49:16. Make high, eagle, nest, rock, dwell. Ranks fifth for Obadiah (WLC) and sits in the highest windows outside Jer 49. Wisdom’s eagle may be a common stock for both prophets.

  • Amos 4:1 with Obad 16. Oppressors drinking on a mount, without liquid or “eat”: the best parallel for (a) apart from Joel 4:3.
