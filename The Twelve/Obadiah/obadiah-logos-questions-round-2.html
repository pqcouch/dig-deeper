Obadiah: Questions for Logos, Round 2

Purpose: to work the items the finalised overview (v2.0) leaves open, using your Logos library. They come from the overview’s Open Questions, claim audit 2’s critical gaps and new candidates (O33–O40), and the Jeremiah 49 synoptic run. Date: 9 November 2026 Answers go to: obadiah-logos-answers-round-2.md (raw), then an assessment, as in round 1.

Use:

  • Part A costs no AI credits. It asks for look-ups and pastes: an apparatus note, a version at a verse, the Exegetical Guide. Do Part A first; some of it may make a Part B question unnecessary.
  • Part B is for the Research Assistant. Ask one question per turn, of one named work or collection where you can. Keep your Obadiah commentaries collection (Keil–Delitzsch, Block, Baker, Mason, Hadjiev, Bewer, Schnittjer and the rest) as the default scope. Widen the scope only where a question says so.
  • Do not type the “Watch for” notes. They are for you, and would lead the answer.
  • Paste each answer whole, with its footnotes, under the question you asked, and tag it [Logos RA] or [commentary: name].

What the answers can do. Most of Part B fills the “history of interpretation” row of the Hays table, and cannot move a verdict by itself. Questions B1, B2 and B5 are different: they ask for arguments, which can be tested against the text.

Part B: Questions for the Assistant

Ask in this order. B1 to B3 bear on live questions; B7 to B9 are history rows.

B1. Ezekiel 35 and the plural at Obadiah 16 (overview Open Question 2)

  • Scope: your Ezekiel commentaries (from A5), then the Obadiah collection.
  • Ask: How do commentators on Ezekiel 35 explain the plural forms addressed to Mount Seir in 35:9 (“you will know”) and 35:13 (“you magnified yourselves… against me with your mouth”), in an oracle that otherwise addresses Edom in the singular? Do any compare this with the plural “you drank” in Obadiah 16?
  • Why: claim audit 2 counted 31 singular and 5 plural forms to Edom in Ezek 35, which neutralises the argument that Obadiah’s plural marks a new addressee (WLC). No commentator returned in round 1 met it.
  • Watch for: the plural dismissed as a scribal slip with no evidence, or “the inhabitants” supplied without a reason given. Note whether the Greek’s singular is mentioned; the Greek levels both texts.

B2. The force of Obadiah 12–14 (overview Open Question 1)

  • Scope: first any Hebrew grammar you own (Gesenius–Kautzsch–Cowley; Joüon–Muraoka; Waltke–O’Connor; Arnold–Choi; van der Merwe), then the Obadiah collection.
  • Ask (grammar): What do the Hebrew reference grammars say about אַל (“not”) with a jussive referring to an event already past? Do any cite Obadiah 12–14, Jeremiah 20:14 or Job 3:4–7?
  • Ask (commentators), as a follow-up: For Obadiah 12–14, what arguments do commentators give for reading the prohibitions as (a) a warning before the event, (b) an imaginative return to the day, or (c) a prohibition with lasting force on a past act? Which do they hold, with page references?
  • Why: audit 2 found no attested past-tense use with the second person, but third-person jussives aimed at a past day at Jer 20:14 and Job 3:4–7 (WLC). Round 1 named holders for all three readings but gave few of their arguments.
  • Watch for: “the prophetic perfect” used to explain jussives, which it does not. And any answer that takes the English “do not” as the evidence.

B3. How Jeremiah 49:7–22 was put together (synoptic run; overview Open Question 3)

  • Scope: your Jeremiah commentaries (from A5).
  • Ask: How do commentators on Jeremiah explain the repeated material in the Edom oracle: 49:18–21, which recurs at 50:40, 44–46 of Babylon; 49:22, which recurs at 48:40–41 of Moab; and 49:17, which echoes 19:8 of Jerusalem? What do they conclude about how the oracle was composed, and about its relation to Obadiah?
  • Why: the synoptic run found that the lines Jer 49 shares with Obadiah are the ones it does not repeat elsewhere (moderate–high). That bears on direction, but no commentator’s view of it is yet on record.
  • Watch for: direction settled by a date assumption (“Jeremiah is later, so…”). Ask for the argument, not the date.

B4. Does any composite view split Obadiah at verse 9? (synoptic Open Question 2)

  • Scope: Hadjiev, then the Obadiah collection.
  • Ask: Among the reconstructions of Obadiah’s composition that Hadjiev lists, does any treat verses 1–9 (the material shared with Jeremiah 49) as an older oracle, separate from verses 10–14? Who holds that view, and on what grounds?
  • Why: the synoptic run’s historical scenario (an older Edom poem, given a brother afterwards) is low–moderate. It would sit better or worse depending on whether anyone has argued it.
  • Watch for: the usual split at v. 15 reported as if it were a split at v. 9.

B5. Jeremiah 48 and Isaiah 15–16: the analogy behind the lean (queue O32)

  • Scope: your Jeremiah and Isaiah commentaries.
  • Ask: What textual arguments do commentators give for the direction of dependence between Jeremiah 48 and Isaiah 15–16? Do any rely on Isaiah 16:13 (“this is the word the LORD spoke concerning Moab long ago”)?
  • Why: the audited lean (the shared lines came into Jeremiah from outside, moderate) rests partly on Jeremiah’s reuse of Isaiah and Amos in the neighbouring oracles. If the Isaiah–Jeremiah direction is itself argued the other way, the analogy weakens.
  • Watch for: Isaiah 15–16 dated late on critical grounds and the direction then assumed. Record the argument, not the conclusion.

B6. Obadiah 17 against Ezekiel 36:2–5 and Jeremiah 49:2 (overview v1.4)

  • Ask: Which commentators, in deciding between “their possessions” (MT) and “those who dispossessed them” (Murabbaʿat and the versions) at Obadiah 17, cite Jeremiah 49:2 (“Israel will dispossess those who dispossessed him”) or Ezekiel 36:2–5 (the nations’ claim to Israel’s possessions)?
  • Why: audit 2 brought v. 17 down to moderate. Jer 49:2 supports the reading (WLC: the only other place where “possess” governs its own participle), and Ezek 36:2–5 supports the MT.
  • Watch for: the versions counted as several witnesses. They share one interpretive pressure.

B7. Joel 3:5 and Isaiah 37:32 / 2 Kings 19:31 (queue O40; the Joel lean)

  • Scope: your Joel commentaries (including Keil, Bewer, Schnittjer), then Isaiah.
  • Ask: To what earlier word do commentators think “as the LORD has said” in Joel 2:32 (Hebrew 3:5) refers? Do any name Isaiah 37:32 or 2 Kings 19:31 (“out of Jerusalem shall go a remnant, and out of Mount Zion a band of survivors”) as well as, or instead of, Obadiah 17?
  • Why: the Joel lean now rests on the formula and on Joel’s habit of reuse. Isa 37:32 and 2 Kgs 19:31 are the only other texts with escape on Zion, and they also name Jerusalem, as Joel does (WLC).
  • Watch for: the Hebrew and English verse numbers confused (Joel 3:5 = English 2:32).

B8. Numbers 24:17–21 at Obadiah (overview Open Question 6)

  • Ask: Apart from Keil–Delitzsch, which commentators on Obadiah or on Numbers 24 connect Balaam’s oracle (24:17–21: the star, Edom a possession, the survivor destroyed, “set your nest in the rock”) with Obadiah 4, 17–21? Does any reject the link?
  • Why: the two audits differ by one step (moderate–high against moderate). A second holder, or a reasoned rejection, would settle it.
  • Watch for: Num 24:17’s star read messianically and then carried into Obadiah. Obadiah’s star is Edom’s height.

B9. History rows for the untested queue (O33–O39)

Ask these only if credits allow. Each is one question, and an absence changes little.

  • O33, Amos 9:2 at v. 4: Which commentators compare Obadiah 4 (“from there I will bring you down”) with Amos 9:2 (“from there my hand shall take them… from there I will bring them down”)?
  • O34, Isa 29:14 at v. 8: Which commentators compare Obadiah 8 (destroying the wise and understanding) with Isaiah 29:14, and do any connect Obadiah 8 with 1 Corinthians 1:19?
  • O35, Mal 3:19 (Eng 4:1) with Obadiah: Which commentators relate Malachi 4:1 (the arrogant as stubble on the coming day) to Obadiah 3, 15 and 18?
  • O36, Jer 48:28–29 at vv. 3–4: Do commentators on Obadiah 3–4 compare Moab’s pride and rock-dwelling in Jeremiah 48:28–29?
  • O37, Gen 32:9 (Eng 32:8) at vv. 17–18: Does any commentator connect “escape” in Obadiah 17 with Jacob’s “the camp which is left will escape” before Esau in Genesis 32:8 (English numbering)?
  • O39, אֵיד (“calamity”): In TDOT, NIDOTTE or TWOT, if owned, what does the entry on אֵיד say about its use in oracles against Edom (Jer 49:8; Obad 13; Ezek 35:5)?

Critical Note — Where This Round Will Be Weak

  • The library is thin on Jeremiah and Ezekiel, as far as I know. B1, B3 and B5 may return only Keil–Delitzsch. If so, the answer is “one older holder”, not a consensus.
  • Direction will not be settled by commentators. The text gave no further lever, and the commentators are divided three ways. B3 and B5 can show whether anyone has argued from the composition of Jer 49, which is the one argument the synoptic run added; that is all.
  • B2 is the only question that could move the pulpit reading. Even there, the choice between the three readings of vv. 12–14 is exegetical. Logos can supply arguments, not a verdict.
  • Absences mean one search found nothing. They keep low ratings low and say little else.
