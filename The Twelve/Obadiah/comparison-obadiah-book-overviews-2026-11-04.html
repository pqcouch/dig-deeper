Comparison: The Two Obadiah Book Overviews

Documents compared: book-overview-obadiah (v0.1.0, 29 July 2026; “the July overview”) and book-overview-obadiah-2026-11-04 (v1.0; “the November overview”) Date: 4 November 2026 Method: Each disagreement was tested against the WLC, Swete, the Rahlfs export and the BHS apparatus. Counts are from the WLC. Independence: The November overview was written without sight of the July one. Reading the July overview for this comparison ends that independence for later work, so the new dig records it in its declaration.

Verdict

Use the November overview as the working text, but it is not simply the better document. It is far stronger on evidence: it works from the Hebrew, measures its claims and states its limits. It also corrects two things the July overview tags as text. But the July overview has things the November one lacks, including a better talion parallel, a real preaching trap and the clearest gospel line in either document.

The difference is mostly one of method and date. The July overview predates the corpus (built 2 September), the canonical-position pattern and the rarity gate. It was written from the ESV, so it could not have seen most of what the November run found.

Where They Agree

Both overviews, reached separately, agree on these points:

  • Edom is judged and the kingdom is the LORD’s; the charge is violence to a brother.
  • Jeremiah 49 is the dominant parallel, and the direction of dependence is unsettled.
  • The Esau–Jacob frame governs the book, and Amos 9:12 stands behind vv. 17–21.
  • Verses 10–14 are the charge and vv. 15–16 the hinge.
  • יוֹם (“day”) binds the centre of the book.
  • There is no direct messianic prophecy; Christ comes by trajectory and contrast, with Rev 11:15 at v. 21.
  • The two chief traps are moralising about gloating and mapping the text onto modern nations.

Because the two runs were independent, this agreement has some value. It is limited, though: the same kind of reader produced both, so agreement is not proof.

Where They Disagree

Each entry gives the test result. “Stands” means the claim survives it.

v. 16: what is drunk. July: “the cup Edom drank”, rated high. November: “Cup” is not in the text; addressee open. Result: November stands. כּוֹס (“cup”) does not occur in Obadiah. The verb שְׁתִיתֶם (“you drank”) is plural after singular “you”, which July does not notice

The mirror structure. July: “Mirror-imaged”, tagged [T]. November: A proposal, [I]; word counts do not support it. Result: November stands. Verses 10–14 and 15–21 share 3 content words against a chance average of 8.0. A pattern claim is not [T]. The pointed links are real

Joel 3:5 (English 2:32). July: Joel “appears to cite” Obad 17, moderate–high. November: Direction open. Result: November stands. Joel’s כַּאֲשֶׁר אָמַר יְהוָה (“as the LORD has said”) shows a prior word, not whose

Date. July: 587, moderate–high. November: 586-ish, moderate, with Jehoram and later options. Result: Both defensible; November is the more honest. The text names no conqueror. The 586/587 difference is a chronology convention

vv. 12–14: time reference. July: Read as past, not raised. November: Open question. Result: November stands. The grammar is a live dispute and it decides how the sermon is framed

Esau, Malachi, Romans 9. July: Election, moderate–high. November: Warned against as a cipher. Result: Partly each. אָהֵב (“love”) and שָׂנֵא (“hate”) are not in Obadiah, and its charge is conduct. But Mal 1:2–4 is in the Twelve and Rom 9:13 is real. Canonical reflection, not the book’s own argument

Outline. July: 1–4, 5–9, 10–14, 15–16, 17–21. November: 1, 2–9, 10–14, 15–16, 17–18, 19–21. Result: Compatible. July’s 4/5 break has נְאֻם־יְהוָה (“declares the LORD”) at v. 4. November’s 18/19 break has כִּי יְהוָה דִּבֵּר (“for the LORD has spoken”) at v. 18

Series weighting. July: All units HIGH. November: vv. 1–9 Standard, unless Jer 49 is worked. Result: A judgement call. July’s weighting is safer for a series

What the July Overview Has and the November One Lacks

  1. A better talion parallel. July cites Ps 7 and Judg 1:6–7. November offered Lev 24:19 and missed both. Ps 7:17 (English 7:16) has שׁוּב (“return”), רֹאשׁ (“head”), חָמָס (“violence”) and יָרַד (“come down”) in one verse, the only verse in the Hebrew Bible with all four. All four are Obadiah’s words. This is a real miss in the November map.
  2. The trap “ducking the judgment”. Softening or skipping v. 18 is a real failure, and November does not name it.
  3. The gospel line. “The deeds that should return on our own heads returned on his.” It reads the talion of v. 15 through the cross. November has nothing as direct. It is a theological inference and not Obadiah’s own statement, but it belongs in the Christological section, tagged [I].
  4. The trap “marginal minor prophet”. Small, but real for a 21-verse book.
  5. A causal microscript. July’s “Because… the LORD will…” follows the מֵחֲמַס (“because of violence”) of v. 10 better than November’s sequential wording.
  6. The superlative. “The shortest book in the Old Testament” holds: 21 verses in the WLC, against Haggai’s 38.

What the November Overview Has and the July One Lacks

  • Primary text work: Hebrew in its own script, with Rahlfs, Swete and the BHS apparatus. July transliterates a few words and has no Greek.
  • The canonical position block, with checked references. That pattern did not exist in July.
  • The measured Jeremiah result: first of 862 chapters, with all 74 rare pairs in vv. 1–5 and 8 and none in vv. 10–21.
  • New partners: Joel 4 (lots, recompense, violence and Edom), Lam 2:3 and 2:22, Amos 1:6, 9, Amos 5:6, Amos 9:2, Ps 22:29, Ps 41:10, Jer 38:22, Job 39:27 and Isa 15:1.
  • Obadiah’s pluses against Jeremiah: the taunt of v. 3 and the stars of v. 4; also Jacob and Zion, which Jer 49 never mentions.
  • Hebrew and Greek side by side, and a list of ESV and NASB95 divergences. July uses the ESV throughout, so it could not see that its own text had decided things.
  • The internal links by lemma: שָׂרִיד (“survivor”) at vv. 14 and 18, כָּרַת (“cut off”) at vv. 9, 10 and 14, אָבַד (“destroy”) at vv. 8 and 12.
  • Rarity counts, baselines and an audit queue.

Defects Found in the November Overview

Reading the two together exposed these. None has been silently fixed. They await your decision.

  1. Versification. It cites Hebrew numbering without saying so. English readers need Joel 3:5 = 2:32; Joel 4 = 3; Ps 22:29 = 22:28; Ps 41:10 = 41:9; Deut 23:8 = 23:7. This is a defect, and an easy fix.
  2. The talion row should add Ps 7:17 and Judg 1:7 (see above).
  3. Josh 16 was dismissed as incidental, wrongly. Josh 16:10 says Ephraim did not dispossess the Canaanite, using the same verb as Obad 19–20. The new dig takes this up.
  4. Missing trap: ducking the judgement.
  5. Thin checking stage, as its colophon says.

Both overviews missed these, and the new dig found them: Gen 27:41 (Esau “said in his heart” and spoke of killing “Jacob my brother”), Deut 32:35 and Prov 27:10. See the dig.

Recommendation

  1. Treat the November overview as current, and patch it to v1.1 with the five items above and the dig’s additions.
  2. Keep the July overview for the record, since its Christological line and traps should be carried across with credit. Do not delete it. Whether it moves to an archive folder is your decision.
  3. The folder’s index.html still lists the older files only.

Note on This Comparison

The tests were run on the WLC, the BHS export, Rahlfs and Swete. I did not consult any commentary for this comparison. Where I judge “November stands”, the judge is the run that wrote November, checked against the text, so those verdicts deserve the suspicion any self-assessment does. The claim audit, with blind auditors, is the proper test.
