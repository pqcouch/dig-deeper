Warrant tags: `[T]` the text states or shows it · `[I]` an inference from the text · `[S]` supplied by a secondary source, held provisionally. Counts are in the WLC unless another edition is named. "Words in order" means words matched in sequence after the skeletal pass described at Tool 3.

**Scope note.**

- **Three movements.** Obadiah in its own frame; Jeremiah 49:7–22 in its own frame; then the comparison, which is where the depth goes. **Obadiah's frame is supplied by the existing dig** (`dig-deeper-obadiah-1-1to21-2026-11-04` v1.2) and consumed at Phase 0.5, not re-worked. Jeremiah 49:7–22 has no solo dig; the Jeremiah sweep gives it one paragraph, so its frame is worked fresh here.
- **Extent.** Each side is set at its own book's seams, not at the overlap.
  - *Obadiah 1–21*, the whole book. The shared lines all fall in vv. 1–9, but the book's own unit is the book, and vv. 10–21 (the charge and the outcome) are the unique material whose absence from Jeremiah is evidence.
  - *Jeremiah 49:7–22*, from the heading לֶאֱדוֹם ("concerning Edom", 49:7) to the next heading, לְדַמֶּשֶׂק ("concerning Damascus", 49:23). The WLC closes 49:6 and 49:22 with a *setumah* `[T]`. This brings 49:17–22 inside the run; the Jeremiah sweep's extent, 49:7–16, would have left it out, and with it 49:22b, Obad 9's nearest line, and six of the verses that show how the oracle is built.
  - *Rejected:* Obad 1–9 // Jer 49:7–16, the overlap. It would exclude Obadiah's whole charge and half of Jeremiah's oracle, which is precisely the evidence an open case has to weigh.
- **Direction of dependence: open.** Neither text names the other `[T]`. Canonical order puts Jeremiah first (BHS: Jeremiah, then the Twelve); a common compositional argument puts Obadiah first; a third view posits a source behind both `[S]`. Both orders are reported; **no difference below is described as one text "editing", "expanding" or "abbreviating" the other.** What the evidence narrows is stated at the end of Tool 11 and in the Headlines, and is held at moderate.

### The two frames, which is where they differ most

**Obadiah's frame is the rest of Obadiah.** The shared lines open the book and are followed by a verse with no Jeremiah parallel (v. 7, the allies' betrayal), then by the charge: מֵחֲמַס אָחִיךָ יַעֲקֹב ("because of violence to your brother Jacob", v. 10), eight prohibitions on the day of Jerusalem (vv. 11–14), the day of the LORD on all nations (v. 15), the cup on the holy mountain (v. 16), escape on Zion and the house of Jacob as fire (vv. 17–18), the land repossessed (vv. 19–20), and the close: וְהָיְתָה לַיהוָה הַמְּלוּכָה ("and the kingdom will be the LORD's", v. 21) `[T]`. Before it stands Amos, ending on לְמַעַן יִירְשׁוּ אֶת־שְׁאֵרִית אֱדוֹם ("that they may possess the remnant of Edom", Amos 9:12) `[T]`; after it, Jonah.

**Jeremiah's frame is the oracles against the nations.** Before: Ammon (49:1–6), closing וְאַחֲרֵי־כֵן אָשִׁיב אֶת־שְׁבוּת בְּנֵי־עַמּוֹן ("but afterward I will restore the fortunes of the sons of Ammon", 49:6), with a *setumah* `[T]`. After: וּלְדַמֶּשֶׂק ("concerning Damascus", 49:23). Behind it all stands the cup of Jer 25:15–29, which lists אֱדוֹם ("Edom") among the nations made to drink (25:21) `[T]`. And Edom receives no restoration formula, where Egypt (46:26), Moab (48:47), Ammon (49:6) and Elam (49:39) each have a word of "afterward" `[T]`. Its only mercy is 49:11, the orphans kept alive.

**The same lines, then, carry a brother and a kingdom on one side and a cup and a lion on the other.** Neither frame is visible from inside the other book.

## Headline Findings

1. **This is not one text in two books, unlike Hezekiah.** No aligned pair is identical, even for spelling. In the five closest pairs (Obad 1b–5 // Jer 49:9, 14–16), 31 of Jeremiah's 50 words stand in Obadiah in order (WLC, skeletal, words separated at maqqef; *v1.1 — claim audit 2*, correcting 34), and the longest identical run is five words `[S: audit 2]`. Two pairs differ by one or two words; the rest by three or more. Across the whole run the shared wording is about 14 % of Jer 49:7–22 and 12 % of Obadiah. The Hezekiah pair was 70 % identical or nearly so. *Surfaced by:* Structure (the diff), Vocabulary. Any account of the relation must explain a shared poem reworded on both sides, not a copied text. `[T]` *High confidence.*

2. **Jeremiah 49:7–22 is a compilation, and the lines it shares with Obadiah are the ones it does not repeat elsewhere.** Of its sixteen verses, four share Obadiah's block (9, 14–16) and two its themes (7, 10a). Nine repeat material found elsewhere in Jeremiah: 49:8 (Hazor, 49:30; nearer still 46:21), 12 (the cup, 25:28–29), 13 (the oath against the king's house, 22:5), 17 (Jerusalem, 19:8; Babylon, 50:13; also Zeph 2:15), 18 (Babylon, 50:40), 19–21 (Babylon, 50:44–46), 22 (Moab, 48:40–41). Only 49:10b–11 stands alone. Obadiah has none of those nine. *Surfaced by:* Structure, Repetition, Quotation/Allusion. `[T]` *Moderate–high* (*v1.1 — claim audit 2* confirmed the pairings; the stock verses share Obadiah's vocabulary, though not his lines) `[S: audit 2]`.

3. **The direction stays open, but the options narrow.** *v1.1 — claim audit 2 reframed this Headline.* Jer 49's neighbouring oracles each carry a line from Amos 1: Ammon at 49:3 (Amos 1:15) and Damascus at 49:27 (Amos 1:4, 14). Jer 48 shares lines with Isa 15–16, which calls itself the word spoken "long ago" (Isa 16:13), and with Isa 24:17–18 and Num 21:28–29 `[T]`. Jeremiah's oracles against the nations share genuine lines with other books at 11 of 231 verses, against 4 of 189 in Isaiah's (all with Jer 48) and none in Ezekiel's, so the habit is real, not chance `[S: audit 2]`. Read with Headline 2:
   - **the shared lines came into Jeremiah from outside**, and Jeremiah set them among his own repeated lines `[I]`. *Moderate* `[S: audit 2]`;
   - **that they are older than Obadiah, or are Obadiah itself:** *low–moderate*. Between Obadiah first and a common source the text does not decide, and "Obadiah took the lines Jeremiah does not repeat" is less economical than claimed in v1.0 but not excluded.

   *Surfaced by:* Quotation/Allusion, Structure, Historical Background.

4. **Withdrawn in v1.1 (claim audit 2, Discard).** v1.0 claimed that each book owns the shared lines in its own idiom. The claim fails:
   - כּוֹכָבִים ("stars") occurs in Jeremiah (31:35), and v1.0's form count missed the prefixed form;
   - אֲדֹנָי יְהוִה ("Lord GOD") is Ezekiel's and Amos's idiom (217 and 21), not the Twelve's (four uses in the other eleven books);
   - שָׁמַעְנוּ ("we heard") is commonest in Jeremiah (5 of 25), and the Greek of Obadiah reads ἤκουσα ("I heard");
   - across every differing word, unchosen, the two sides do not differ significantly (p ≈ 0.15) `[S: audit 2]`.

   Only עֵת פְּקַדְתִּיו ("the time I punish him", Jer 49:8) stands as Jeremiah's own. **The idiom of the two versions does not show ownership or direction.**

5. **The same lines do different work.** In Obadiah the block is a sentence read before its reason. It is wired into the book: "who will bring me down?" (v. 3) is answered in v. 4, and the question is Obadiah's alone; the deceit of the heart (v. 3) passes to the allies (v. 7); "Mount Esau" (vv. 8–9) returns at vv. 19 and 21. Then the brother is named (v. 10). In Jeremiah the block is one pass of the cup (25:21; 49:12). Edom receives words Jeremiah first spoke of Jerusalem (19:8; 22:5) and later speaks of Babylon (50:40, 44–46), and keeps a sliver of mercy (49:11). There is no charge against Judah, no Jacob and no Zion. Elsewhere in Jeremiah, Edom is Zedekiah's fellow conspirator (27:3) and a refuge for Judeans (40:11), never their betrayer. *Surfaced by:* Context (twice), Move 4 (twice), Narrator's Comment. `[T]` *High confidence.*

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Obadiah.** From the dig, consumed: a whole book on one brother's conduct on his brother's worst day, ending on the LORD's kingdom `[S: dig v1.2]`. The shared block is its opening sentence.

**Jeremiah 49:7–22.** The commission was over nations (Jer 1:10). The Edom oracle shows the cup of ch. 25 reaching a near neighbour after Judah: "those who were not sentenced to drink the cup will certainly drink it, and are you the one who will be completely acquitted?" (49:12), which turns 25:29's argument from Jerusalem to Edom `[T]`. The purpose is to place Edom inside a universal sentence, not to answer a crime. `[I]`

**The purposes are compatible and not identical.** Obadiah's purpose needs the brother; Jeremiah's does not, and does without him.

### 2. Context

#### Phase 0.55 — Canonical position

| Field | Obadiah | Jeremiah |
|---|---|---|
| Section | Nevi'im, Latter: the Twelve, fourth | Nevi'im, Latter: second book |
| Reading implication | Read after Joel 4:19 and Amos 1:11; 9:12 | Read inside the oracles against the nations (46–51), after the cup (25) |

| Neighbours | Amos before, Jonah after (BHS; in the Greek, Joel before) | Ammon before, Damascus after (MT; LXX Jer 30:1–16 Rahlfs, after the Philistines) |

**Canonical and compositional order diverge here, and this run resolves neither by default.** In the Hebrew canon Jeremiah stands before the Twelve. The claim that Obadiah's poem is the older is compositional, and contested `[S]`.

#### Positional Necessity Check — Obadiah

Consumed from the dig `[S: dig v1.2, Tool 2]`. Joel 4:19 has named a violence against Judah, and Amos 9:12 has promised Edom's remnant as a possession, without saying what the violence was or how the possession comes. Obadiah supplies both. **The shared block is necessary to Obadiah as the sentence that the charge (vv. 10–14) then explains.** Without vv. 1–9, v. 10's מֵחֲמַס ("because of violence") would have nothing to be the reason for `[T]`.

#### Positional Necessity Check — Jeremiah 49:7–22

**Preceding movement.** The cup has been ordered round the nations, with Edom in the list (25:21), and Judah first: "I am beginning to work calamity in this city which is called by My name, and should you be completely free from punishment?" (25:29) `[T]`. The oracles of 46–51 carry it out nation by nation. Ammon has just been given its "afterward" (49:6).

**Necessity answer.** 49:7–22 exists here because the cup list named Edom, and 49:12 says so in the words of 25:28–29: שָׁתוֹ תִשְׁתּוּ ("you shall surely drink") and לֹא תִנָּקֶה ("you will not be acquitted") `[T]`. **What the position requires is that Edom drink; it does not require a reason particular to Edom**, and the oracle gives only pride (49:16) and the time of visitation (49:8) `[T]`. `[I]` *Moderate–high.*

**Implication.** The two positional answers are different, and each is unavailable from the other book. Obadiah's block needs a following charge; Jeremiah's needs a preceding cup. **This is the first reason the direction question is hard: the block serves both positions natively.**

**What the diff establishes.**
- **Identical, or identical but for spelling: none.** Two pairs differ by one or two words, one by three to six, the rest by more. The skeletal pass gains only four words over the consonantal (37 against 33), so **spelling is not where these texts differ**; wording is. `[T]`
- **The divergence is concentrated, not spread.** The closest agreement is in the messenger and the "small" line (Obad 1b–2 // 49:14–15) and the grape-gatherers (Obad 5b // 49:9a). The furthest is where Obadiah has lines Jeremiah lacks: the taunt and the stars (vv. 3b–4a), the night-plunderers' interjection (v. 5a). `[T]`
- **Order.** Obadiah's thieves come before the grape-gatherers; Jeremiah's after (Obad 5 // Jer 49:9) `[T]`. The block runs in sequence in Obadiah (1–6, 8–9) and in two clusters in Jeremiah (9–10; 14–16), with 7 and 22 at either end `[T]`. Keil's observation that it is "connected in Obadiah, scattered in Jeremiah" holds `[S: Keil]`. That fits a compilation in Jeremiah, and fits a common source equally.

**Structure of Jer 49:7–22 itself.** The WLC marks *setumot* after 49:11, 19 and 22 `[T; unchecked — apparatus spread]`:
- 7–11: wisdom gone, Dedan, gatherers and thieves, Esau stripped, the orphans;
- 12–19: the cup, the oath, the messenger, "small", pride, desolation, Sodom, the lion;
- 20–22: the plan, the quake, the eagle.

The Obadiah-shared lines fall in the first two units (9–10; 14–16). Both NASB95 and ESV set 49:7–11 and 14–16 as verse and 49:12–13, 17–22 as prose `[S: translators' layout]`. That is, the shared lines and 49:7–11 are verse, and almost all of Jeremiah's repeated material is prose. **The parashot are a reading tradition, not authorial**, so nothing rests on them; the composition argument (Tool 11) stands without them.

**Structure of Obadiah**, from the dig: sentence (1–9), charge (10–14), day (15–21); no paragraph markers in the WLC `[S: dig]`.

### 4. Linking Words

**Obadiah's block is held together by questions and conditionals.** Two of them are Obadiah's alone:
- אִם ("if", vv. 4, 5 ×3);
- הֲלוֹא ("would they not?", v. 5 ×2), where Jeremiah has plain לֹא ("not", 49:9);
- אֵיךְ ("how!", vv. 5, 6);
- the question מִי יוֹרִדֵנִי ("who will bring me down?", v. 3) answered by מִשָּׁם אוֹרִידְךָ ("from there I will bring you down", v. 4).

`[T]` So Obadiah's form is a question and its answer; Jeremiah's is a statement.

**Jeremiah's block is held together by כִּי** ("for, indeed"): 49:8, 10, 12, 13, 15, 16, 19 `[T]`. Its connectors attach the shared lines to the repeated ones. For example, 49:12 opens כִּי־כֹה אָמַר יְהוָה ("for thus says the LORD"), and 49:20 opens לָכֵן ("therefore"), which comes with the Babylon material (50:45) `[T]`.

### 5. Parallels

Both are parallelistic verse in the shared lines. Obadiah's parallelism is tighter in v. 4: אִם…וְאִם… ("though… though…"), the eagle balanced by the stars. Jeremiah's 49:16 has one colon, כִּי־תַגְבִּיהַּ כַּנֶּשֶׁר קִנֶּךָ ("though you make your nest as high as an eagle's") `[T]`. Whether a pair was filled out or cut down is the kind of judgement the open case forbids. Schnittjer calls Obadiah's form "strengthening", Keil "obscure to clear"; these are opposite rules of style and neither is decisive `[S]`.

### 6. Narrator's Comment

**N/A as narration; but the speaker's voice differs.** Jeremiah's version is "I": שָׁמַעְתִּי ("I have heard", 49:14), and the LORD's "I" in חָשַׂפְתִּי, גִּלֵּיתִי ("I have stripped… I have uncovered", 49:10) `[T]`. Obadiah's is "we": שָׁמַעְנוּ ("we have heard") and וְנָקוּמָה ("let us rise"), which places the prophet among those summoned `[T]`. **But the "we" is MT only:** Rahlfs and Swete Obadiah both read Ἀκοὴν ἤκουσα ("I heard a report"), as in Jeremiah, and BHS records it `[T]`. Hold the "we" lightly.

### 7. Vocabulary

**House vocabulary** (Phase 0.5d, point 5). *v1.1 — claim audit 2 discarded v1.0's finding here (see Headline 4); the v1.0 tables are withdrawn.* What survives:
- עֵת פְּקַדְתִּיו ("the time I punish him", 49:8): Jeremiah's own term `[T]`; not in Obadiah.
- חָשַׂפְתִּי ("I have stripped", 49:10): Jer 13:26 and 49:10 only `[T]`, but the root is commoner in the Twelve `[S: audit 2]`, so it shows a Jeremian phrase, not a Jeremian vocabulary.
- יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת ("the LORD of hosts", 49:7): MT only, absent from the Greek `[T]`; also common in the Twelve.
- נִדְמֵיתָה ("you are destroyed", Obad 5) stands in a phrase BHS suspects as a gloss; הַר עֵשָׂו ("Mount Esau") is Obadiah's alone (4 times) `[T]`.
- **The method lesson:** a house-vocabulary claim needs lemma counts and a set of words chosen without regard to the claim. v1.0 had neither, and its pattern disappeared under the unchosen test `[S: audit 2]`.

**Proper nouns.** Obadiah: Edom, Esau, Teman, Mount Esau, then Jacob, Joseph, Judah, Jerusalem, Zion, the Negev, the Shephelah, Philistines, Ephraim, Samaria, Benjamin, Gilead, Canaanites, Zarephath, Sepharad. Jeremiah 49:7–22: Edom, Teman, Dedan, Esau, Bozrah, Sodom, Gomorrah, the Jordan, the Red Sea `[T]`. **Of Israel's names, Jeremiah's oracle has none.** Teman and Bozrah stand together in one verse of the Bible, Amos 1:12; Jeremiah's oracle has both, in different verses (49:7, 20; 49:13, 22), and Obadiah has Teman only `[T]`.

**אָח ("brother") is in both, differently.** Jer 49:10 has וְאֶחָיו ("and his brothers"), Esau's own kin destroyed with him; Obadiah's אָחִיךָ יַעֲקֹב ("your brother Jacob", v. 10) is Edom's victim `[T]`. The NASB95 renders Jer 49:10 "relatives", the ESV "brothers". The Greek reads ὤλοντο διὰ χεῖρα ἀδελφοῦ αὐτοῦ ("they perished by the hand of his brother", Rahlfs Jer 30:4), apparently taking זרעו as "arm" rather than "seed" `[T; I for the reading]`. **This is not Obadiah's brother theme.** It is a different use of the word, and a preacher should not merge them.

### 8. Translations

**NASB95 against ESV**, at points that bear on findings:

| Hebrew | NASB95 | ESV | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| קָטֹן נְתַתִּיךָ (Obad 2 = Jer 49:15, identical) | Obad "I will make"; Jer "I have made" | "I will make" in both | **The NASB95 renders identical Hebrew two ways** |
| וְצִיר ("envoy") | "envoy" in both | Obad "messenger"; Jer "envoy" | **The ESV varies the English for identical Hebrew** |
| נִדְמֵיתָה, נֶחְפְּשׂוּ (Obad 5, 6) | "will be ruined", "will be ransacked" | "have been destroyed", "has been pillaged" | Tense of Obadiah's perfects |
| וְאֶחָיו (Jer 49:10) | "his relatives" | "his brothers" | The ESV keeps the word |

**Pulpit divergence note.** An ESV congregation hears Obad 5–6 as past and Obad 2 as future, and hears Obad 1 and Jer 49:14 as using different words for the same Hebrew word. A NASB95 reader hears Obad 2 and Jer 49:15 differ in tense where the Hebrew is the same. **The English makes the two texts look more and less alike than the Hebrew does**, in different places. Do not argue from the English to a difference between the books.

**Greek.** The two Greek translations are independent: ὀλιγοστόν / μικρόν ("small"), ἠτιμωμένος / εὐκαταφρόνητον ("despised"), περιοχήν / ἀγγέλους (for צִיר), ὑπερηφανία / ἰταμία (for זָדוֹן), κατάξω / καθελῶ ("I will bring down") `[T: Swete, Rahlfs]`. They share one formula, ἀκοὴν ἤκουσα παρὰ κυρίου ("I heard a report from the Lord"). So the Greek gives no evidence that one translator knew the other's book, and its "I heard" at Obad 1 is a reading, not a harmonisation that can be proved.

### 9. Tone and Feel

Obadiah's block is taunting and exclamatory: אֵיךְ ("how!") twice, the rhetorical הֲלוֹא ("would they not?") twice, and Edom's own boast quoted. Jeremiah's is declarative and heavier with formula: the oath, the cup, the lion, the eagle `[T]`. **Obadiah sounds like a challenge to a living enemy; Jeremiah like a verdict entered in a register of nations.** `[I]`

### 10. Repetition

**A negative result, stated plainly:** retention does not separate Jeremiah's own material from borrowed material. His self-reuse runs from 33 % to 96 %, and the Obadiah block sits inside that range; the audit confirms the negative result (moderate–high) `[S: audit 2]`. So the closeness of the wording cannot by itself say who wrote the shared lines `[T]`. What can be said comes from *distribution*, not closeness (Tool 11).

**Within Obadiah**, the dig's Leitwörter: יוֹם ("day", 12 times), the "cut off" and "survivor" mirrors, Mount Esau `[S: dig]`.

### 11. Quotation / Allusion

**The pair itself (Move 1).** The shared material is the run's subject, not a citation by either text.

**The composition evidence, which is where the direction question is narrowed.**

1. **Distribution within Jer 49:7–22.** The lines shared with Obadiah are, but for 49:10b–11, exactly the lines Jeremiah does not repeat elsewhere (Headline 2) `[T]`. If Obadiah drew on Jer 49 as it stands, he took every line of it that is not Jeremiah's stock and none that is, then rewrote them. If Jeremiah drew on an older Edom poem, he set it among lines he uses of Jerusalem, Moab, Hazor and Babylon. Both are possible. The second needs no coincidence `[I]`.
2. **The neighbours.** In the same chapter, Ammon's oracle carries Amos 1:15: כִּי מַלְכָּם בַּגּוֹלָה יֵלֵךְ… וְשָׂרָיו יַחְדָּו ("for Malcam will go into exile… and his princes together", 49:3; also 48:7) `[T]`. Damascus's oracle carries Amos 1:4, 14: וְהִצַּתִּי אֵשׁ בְּחוֹמַת… וְאָכְלָה אַרְמְנוֹת בֶּן־הֲדָד ("and I will kindle a fire in the wall of… and it will devour the citadels of Ben-hadad", 49:27) `[T]`. Amos's heading dates him to Uzziah (Amos 1:1) `[T]`. **So each of the three oracles in Jer 49:1–27 shares material with the Twelve**: two with Amos, one with Obadiah. And Jer 48 shares lines with Isa 15–16, which its own text calls the word spoken מֵאָז ("long ago", Isa 16:13), with Isa 24:17–18, and with Num 21:28–29 `[T]`. The oracles against the nations in Jeremiah absorb other prophets' oracles; this is shown, not supposed. Keil's "Jeremiah's habit of reusing earlier oracles" is borne out in the adjacent chapters `[S: Keil; T for the texts]`.
3. **What this does not show.** The Amos lines are single lines, not a five-verse poem. The direction between Jer 48 and Isa 15–16 is itself argued `[S]`, though Isaiah's own heading claims age. And the idiom of the two versions does not separate them (Tool 7). Obadiah's own v. 7 shares a saying with Jer 38:22: יָכְלוּ לְךָ אַנְשֵׁי שְׁלֹמֶךָ ("the men of your peace prevailed over you") `[T]`. Obad 5 has a gleaning image that Jer 6:9 also has, and Obad 4–5 the height and the plunderers of Jer 51:53 `[T]`. Those who hold Jeremiah first can read all this as Obadiah steeped in Jeremiah `[S: Raabe, Block, through the assessment]`.

**The lean, and its limit** (*v1.1 — claim audit 2*). The shared lines came into Jer 49:7–22 from outside Jeremiah, and Jeremiah set them among his own lines `[I]`: *moderate* `[S: audit 2]`. That they are older than Obadiah, or are Obadiah, is *low–moderate*. **It does not decide between Obadiah first and a common source**, and the text gives no further lever: no heading names the other, and the shared lines are reworded on both sides. Jeremiah first remains possible only in the narrow form that Obadiah took the non-repeated lines and rewrote them. Since that form requires an earlier, shorter Jeremian oracle, it is in practice indistinguishable from a common source.

**A historical scenario, not a finding.** Jeremiah's oracle, unlike Obadiah, has no charge of violence against Judah, and in Jer 27:3 Edom is still Judah's ally against Babylon `[T]`. If Obadiah's charge reflects 586 (overview: moderate), the shared poem could predate it, with Obadiah giving it a brother afterwards `[I]`. *Low–moderate.* It would sit beside the composite views of Obadiah, though none of those reported in the assessment splits the book at v. 9.

**Move 3 (OT to OT).** Behind both stand Isa 14:13–15 (the heart's boast, the height, brought down) and Edom's place in Num 20:14–21 `[S: dig; audit 1]`. Neither adjudicates the pair.

**Move 4 — internal echo, once per book.**

*Obadiah* (consumed from the dig, with the shared lines marked):
- *answers §earlier, Obadiah only:* מִי יוֹרִדֵנִי ("who will bring me down?", v. 3) → מִשָּׁם אוֹרִידְךָ ("from there I will bring you down", v. 4). The question is not in Jeremiah. *High.*
- *replants, Obadiah only:* הִשִּׁיאֶךָ ("has deceived you", v. 3, the heart; shared) → הִשִּׁיאוּךָ ("deceived you", v. 7, the allies; unique). *High.*
- *planted for later:* מֵהַר עֵשָׂו ("from Mount Esau", vv. 8–9, theme-shared) → vv. 19, 21. *High.*
- *planted for later:* בַּגּוֹיִם ("among the nations", vv. 1–2, shared) → כָל־הַגּוֹיִם ("all the nations", vv. 15–16). *Moderate.*
- *planted for later:* "gleanings" left (v. 5, shared) → no survivor (v. 18). *Moderate.*

*Jeremiah 49:7–22:*
- *answers §earlier:* 49:12 → 25:15–29, the cup, with Judah first (25:29) and Edom listed (25:21). *High.*
- *answers §earlier:* 49:13, בִּי נִשְׁבַּעְתִּי ("by myself I have sworn") → 22:5, sworn against the king's house in Jerusalem. *High on the wording.*
- *answers §earlier:* 49:17 → 19:8, every passer-by hissing at Jerusalem's wounds. *High.*
- *answers §earlier:* the deceived heart (49:16) beside Jeremiah's other uses of the same verb, נשׁא in the hiphil (4:10; 29:8; 37:9), where Judah deceives itself about Babylon. *Moderate.*
- *planted for later:* 49:18–21 → 50:40, 44–46, the same lines of Babylon; 49:22 ← 48:40–41, of Moab. *High.*
- *contrast within the series:* Edom receives no "afterward" (cf. 46:26; 48:47; 49:6, 39), only 49:11. *High.*

**The asymmetry.**
- **Obadiah wires the shared lines into a book about Edom and Jacob.** The lines' internal answers are all inside Obadiah, and two of them depend on material Jeremiah lacks (the question of v. 3; the allies of v. 7).
- **Jeremiah wires the same lines into a series about every nation.** Their echoes run back to Jerusalem (19:8; 22:5; 25:29) and forward to Babylon (50:40, 44–46): **Edom is judged in Jerusalem's words and Babylon is judged in Edom's.**
- One book plants Mount Esau and harvests it on Zion. The other uses Edom as a station on the cup's route from Jerusalem to Babylon.

`[T]` for the echoes; `[I]` for the characterisation.

### 12. Genre

Both are oracles against a nation, in verse with prose formulae. Obadiah's is a whole prophetic book with a charge and a restoration; Jeremiah's is one oracle in a collection, without a charge, without a restoration, and with a line of mercy `[T]`. The genre is shared; the book-genre is not.

### 13. Copycat

N/A as narrative. The one conduct named is pride, in both (Obad 3; Jer 49:16) `[T]`. Only Obadiah names a brother's betrayal as the crime.

### 14. Bible Timeline

Jeremiah 49:7 carries no date `[T]`. The date for the series that Keil uses comes from 46:2, a heading for Egypt `[S: assessment]`. Jer 27:3 puts Edom's envoys in Jerusalem under Zedekiah, conspiring with him `[T]`; 40:11 has Judeans in Edom after the fall `[T]`. Obadiah's charge is set after 586 at moderate in the overview `[S: overview v1.3]`. **The two texts need not refer to the same moment**, and the shared poem need not refer to either (Tool 11, scenario).

### 15. Who Am I?

In Obadiah the hearer is Jacob's house, reassured; Edom is addressed in the second person throughout. In Jeremiah the hearer is first the prophet's own audience in Judah, who hear Edom's sentence as part of their own (49:12; 25:29). Then, in 49:11, Edom's widows hear the LORD bid them trust him `[T]`. **Only Jeremiah gives Edom's own vulnerable people a word.** Schnittjer notes that Obadiah lacks it `[S]`.

### 16. So What?

- **Preach each text in its own frame.** Obadiah's sermon is about a brother's betrayal and God's kingdom; Jeremiah's is about the cup that no nation escapes, Judah first, and a word to orphans even in Edom.
- **The overlap teaches something on its own.** A prophetic word about Edom could be heard again in two collections and made to serve each. That is a fact about how Scripture holds prophecy, and it needs no theory of dependence to be preached. `[I]`
- **For a series on Obadiah**, Jer 49 shows what Obadiah did *not* say: no mercy line, no cup round the nations, no lion. It shows what Obadiah *did* say that Jeremiah never does: brother, Jacob, Zion, kingdom.

### Historical Background

Edom's relation to Judah under Zedekiah: an ally in the 594/3 conspiracy (Jer 27:3; the year from 28:1) `[T; S for the year]`; a refuge after 586 (40:11) `[T]`; accused of joining the destruction only in the prophets and poets (Ps 137:7; Lam 4:21–22; Ezek 25:12–14; 35; Obad 10–14) `[T for the texts]`. Jeremiah's book never makes that accusation `[T]`. Bartlett's doubt about Edom's part in 586 and Na'aman's answer are in the overview `[S: overview v1.3]`.

### Biblical Theology

- **The cup.** Jer 25:15–29 → 49:12 (Edom must drink, since Judah did) → Obad 16 (כַּאֲשֶׁר שְׁתִיתֶם, "as you drank", and all the nations) `[T]`. The two books meet here without sharing a word: כּוֹס ("cup") is in Jeremiah and not in Obadiah. Jer 49:12 is the strongest support for the Judah reading of Obad 16 `[S: assessment]`, and this run confirms it is Jeremiah's own cup, from ch. 25 `[T]`.
- **The proud brought down.** Isa 14:13–15 → both texts `[S: audit 1]`.
- **"In that day."** בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא ("in that day"): Obad 8 and Jer 49:22, at the lines that share a theme `[T]`. In Obadiah it grows into יוֹם־יְהוָה ("the day of the LORD", v. 15); in Jeremiah it does not.

### Difficult Verses

- **Jer 49:11 against Obadiah.** A line of mercy for Edom's orphans and widows stands in Jeremiah's oracle and has no counterpart in Obadiah `[T]`. Do not import it into Obadiah, and do not explain it away in Jeremiah.
- **Jer 49:12, who "were not sentenced to drink"?** By 25:29, Judah: the city called by the LORD's name `[T]`. The ESV's "those who did not deserve" is an interpretation of אֲשֶׁר אֵין מִשְׁפָּטָם ("whose sentence was not"), found here only `[T]`.

### Christological Reading

Low key, and kept separate for each book. Obadiah's close is consumed from the dig: saviours on Zion, the kingdom the LORD's `[S: dig]`. Jeremiah's oracle closes its middle unit with מִי כָמוֹנִי… וּמִי־זֶה רֹעֶה אֲשֶׁר יַעֲמֹד לְפָנָי ("who is like me… and who is the shepherd who can stand before me?", 49:19) and וּמִי בָחוּר אֵלֶיהָ אֶפְקֹד ("whoever is chosen I shall appoint over it") `[T]`. That is the LORD's unrivalled appointment of a ruler over the land, the same line used of Babylon (50:44). A reflection on the chosen shepherd is possible from Jeremiah's own shepherd promises (23:4–5) `[I, low]`. Neither text names a deliverer from Edom; Isa 63 stays outside this pair.

### Original Audience

Jeremiah's hearers heard Edom's sentence as a consequence of their own (49:12), in a book where Edom had been their partner (27:3). Obadiah's hearers heard Edom's sentence as justice for what Edom did to them (vv. 10–14). **The same lines would have landed differently.** `[I]`

## Preaching Pitfalls

1. **The harmonised Edom oracle.** Preaching Obadiah with Jeremiah's lion, cup or orphans, or Jer 49 with Obadiah's brother and Zion. **The harmonised text exists nowhere.** *Corrective:* name which book is being preached; use the other only as contrast, and say so.
2. **Narrating dependence from the pulpit.** "Obadiah borrowed this from Jeremiah", or "Jeremiah quoted Obadiah". Neither can be shown. *Corrective:* say, if anything, that the same prophetic word about Edom stands in both books, and each uses it in its own way.
3. **Merging the brothers.** Jer 49:10's "his brothers" (Esau's own kin; NASB95 "relatives") is not Obadiah's "your brother Jacob". *Corrective:* keep the brother theme in Obadiah.
4. **Arguing from English differences.** The ESV's "messenger" and "envoy", and the NASB95's "I will make" and "I have made", are not Hebrew differences. *Corrective:* check the Hebrew before contrasting the books.
5. **Reading Jeremiah's silence as acquittal.** Jeremiah does not charge Edom with betraying Judah; that is not a verdict that Edom was innocent. It is a book with another purpose. *Corrective:* the silence is a fact about Jeremiah's frame, not about 586.
