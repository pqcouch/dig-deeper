## How the List Is Arranged

The questions are grouped by kind, and within each group the first question matters most. Each question has three parts:

- **Ask:** the wording to type.
- **Why:** the open point it settles, and where the answer will be used.
- **Watch for:** the check from the "Reading the Answer" section of the guidelines that is most likely to matter. It is for you, not for the Assistant. Do not type it, because it would lead the answer.

**What the answers can and cannot do.** The history-of-interpretation questions (group 4) cannot move an audit verdict by themselves. They fill one row of the Hays table. An answer of the form "no commentator links …" is a fact about one search, not proof that nobody has made the link. The questions in groups 1–3 can do more, but what counts is the argument given, not the number of commentators who hold a view.

**Numbering.** Commentaries mostly use English verse numbers, so the questions do too. The audit and the overview use Hebrew numbers:

- Joel 2:32 = Hebrew 3:5
- Joel 3 = Hebrew 4
- Psalm 7:16 = Hebrew 7:17
- Psalm 22:28 = Hebrew 22:29
- Psalm 41:9 = Hebrew 41:10
- Deuteronomy 23:7 = Hebrew 23:8

## 1. Before the Round: Date, Occasion and History

**1.1 Date and occasion, one commentator at a time**

- **Ask (once for each owned commentary):** In [commentator]'s commentary on Obadiah, what date and occasion does he conclude for the book? Please quote his conclusion and give his main evidence, with page references.
- **Why:** the overview gives three options: after 586 BC (`[I]`, moderate), the reign of Jehoram, and later. Its holders are only Collins, a footnoted blog, for the early date, and Cook's review of Renkema (about 30 years after the fall, before Nabonidus in 554/3). The overview needs holders from commentaries you own.
- **Watch for:**
  - The date argued from a settled view of the dependence on Jeremiah. That is an assumption, not evidence.
  - 2 Chr 21:16–17 cited for the Jehoram date as if it named Jerusalem or Edom in the raid. It names neither.
  - A summary reporting the case a commentator sets out rather than the one he adopts.

**1.2 Edom's actual part in 586**

- **Ask:** What evidence, biblical and non-biblical, do my commentators give for Edomite involvement in the fall of Jerusalem in 586 BC? Does any of them argue that the evidence is thin or that the charge is exaggerated? Please give page references.
- **Why:** verses 10–14 are the book's charge. The overview ties them to 586 only by inference. If the external evidence is thin, the charge rests on the prophetic texts themselves (Ps 137:7; Lam 4:21–22; Ezek 35), and the preacher should say so.
- **Watch for:**
  - Archaeological claims with no site or publication named.
  - Biblical texts used as both the claim and the proof of it.
  - 1 Esd 4:45, if cited. As far as I recall it blames the Edomites for burning the temple, but I have not checked this. It is late, so record it as evidence of the tradition, not of the event.

**1.3 What became of Edom**

- **Ask:** What do my commentators say about the end of Edom as a state: Nabonidus's campaign, Arab or Nabataean pressure, and the Idumeans? Do any of them connect this with the allies and "those who eat your bread" of Obadiah 7? What ancient evidence do they give?
- **Why:** it bears on the tenses of vv. 5–7 (question 3.2) and on any statement from the pulpit that the oracle "was fulfilled". The dig's only source for Nabonidus is Cook's review.
- **Watch for:** fulfilment asserted with no ancient evidence, and Nabataeans named without a date.

**1.4 Obadiah himself** — *low priority*

- **Ask:** What do my commentators say about who Obadiah was, and do any discuss the tradition that identifies him with Ahab's steward (1 Kings 18)?
- **Why:** the superscription gives only a name. It is useful only to head off a pulpit error.
- **Watch for:** the tradition presented as evidence for an early date.

## 2. Direction of Dependence

The audit left direction open at every point. Each of these questions asks for arguments, not dates.

**2.1 Obadiah 1–9 and Jeremiah 49:7–22**

- **Ask:** What textual arguments do my commentators give for the direction of dependence between Obadiah 1–9 and Jeremiah 49:7–22? Please set out the arguments on each side, who holds each, and the verses they rest on, with page references.
- **Why:** the audit rates the shared material *high* and direction open, with evidence running both ways: the odd imperative שִׂים ("set", v. 4); עָלֶיהָ ("against her", v. 1) beside a masculine Edom; Jer 49:7–22 as a compilation (with Jer 50 and Jer 48:41). Your answers feed queue item O27, the Synoptic Mode run.
- **Watch for:**
  - Direction settled by assuming which book is older.
  - "Most scholars agree". Count the footnotes behind it.
  - The Greek of Jeremiah cited without its different numbering (Edom stands in Greek Jer 29). The audit found that the Greek translators worked separately on the two books.

**2.2 A common source**

- **Ask:** Do any of my commentators argue that Obadiah and Jeremiah 49 draw independently on an earlier oracle against Edom? What is their evidence, and does anyone reject the idea?
- **Why:** this is the third option. The dig's contacts stop at v. 9, with none in vv. 10–21 at the rare-pair level, and the audit found Job 39:27–28 may be common stock behind the eagle lines (O24).
- **Watch for:** a common source proposed only because the direction cannot be settled. That is a way out, not an argument. Ask what in the wording requires it.

**2.3 Obadiah 17 and Joel 2:32**

- **Ask:** Which commentators on Obadiah or Joel discuss the link between Obadiah 17 and Joel 2:32 ("there will be those who escape"), including Joel's "as the LORD has said"? What textual arguments are given for the direction, and does anyone argue that Joel is citing something other than Obadiah?
- **Why:** audit claim A1: *moderate–high*, direction open. The Hebrew phrase תִּהְיֶה פְלֵיטָה ("there will be those who escape") stands only in these two verses (WLC). The July overview said Joel "appears to cite" Obadiah; the November overview leaves the direction open.
- **Watch for:** "Joel quotes Obadiah" stated as settled, with the date of Joel assumed. Joel's date is itself disputed.

**2.4 Obadiah 11–17 and Joel 3**

- **Ask:** Which commentators discuss the links between Obadiah and Joel 3 (casting lots for the people, recompense returning on the head, violence against Judah, Edom, the holiness of Jerusalem)? Do they treat these as dependence, as a shared tradition, or as editing within the Book of the Twelve?
- **Why:** audit claim A2: *moderate–high*, cumulative. Recompense on the head is its strongest point, and Nah 3:10 has the closer frame for the lots.
- **Watch for:** one verse (Joel 3:19) offered as the whole case.

## 3. Cruxes

For the first three, ask for one commentator at a time if the blended answer is thin. Each of these touches the pulpit text.

**3.1 Verses 12–14: past or future?**

- **Ask (once for each owned commentary):** How does [commentator] take the series of prohibitions in Obadiah 12–14 ("do not gloat… do not enter…")? Are they warnings before an event, or are they addressed rhetorically back into a past event? How does he relate them to v. 11, and what grammatical reasons does he give?
- **Why:** the dig leans to the past (*moderate*). The NET note reads them as rhetorical; Collins argues from the same grammar for a real warning and so for the ninth century. The answer decides how the sermon is framed. The audit also found that the chain of eight prohibitions is the densest in the WLC, on its auditor's floor count, and unique to Obadiah.
- **Watch for:**
  - The date deciding the grammar, or the grammar deciding the date. Note which comes first.
  - An English past sense ("you should not have…", which as far as I recall is the KJV's wording) treated as the Hebrew. Both of your texts read "do not".

**3.2 Verses 2 and 5–7: the tenses**

- **Ask:** How do my commentators take the perfect verbs in Obadiah 2 and 5–7: as past events that have already befallen Edom, as prophetic perfects describing what is certain, or otherwise? On what grounds?
- **Why:** this is a pulpit gap. The ESV says "how you have been destroyed", "has been pillaged" and "have driven you"; the NASB95 says "will be ruined", "will be ransacked" and "will send you". Both versions make v. 2 future, though נְתַתִּיךָ ("I have made you") is perfect. The two congregations would hear different events.
- **Watch for:** "prophetic perfect" as a label with no argument. Also see whether the commentator's date (question 1.1) is doing the work.

**3.3 Verse 16: who drank?**

- **Ask (once for each owned commentary):** In Obadiah 16, "as you drank on my holy mountain", whom does [commentator] take "you" to be: Edom, Judah, or someone else? Does he comment on the plural verb after the singular "you" of v. 15? What reasons does he give?
- **Why:** audit claim B9, *Uncertain*: a slight lean to Judah, low–moderate, on the plural and on Jer 49:12. The NET note rejects Judah on the ground that God's people are addressed nowhere else in the book. The Greek ἔπιες ("you drank") is singular.
- **Watch for:**
  - "The cup" read in as though the text had it. The word is absent.
  - The plural passed over in silence. Record that as a gap in the commentator's treatment.
  - Edom's drunken revelling supplied with no text cited.

**3.4 Verse 15: the order and the seam**

- **Ask:** Which of my commentators rearrange or divide Obadiah 15, placing 15b ("as you have done…") with vv. 10–14 and 15a ("the day of the LORD…") with vv. 16–21? What reasons do they give, and does anyone defend the verse as it stands?
- **Why:** this bears on the outline. The audit's three blocks (1–9, 10–14, 15–21, *moderate–high*) cut between vv. 14 and 15, though יוֹם ("day") crosses the cut. Moving 15b would change the block count and the hinge. The overview has this only from Cook's "hinge line".
- **Watch for:** a rearrangement offered with no manuscript support. Note that none is recorded in BHS for this.

**3.5 Verses 19–20: subjects and objects**

- **Ask:** How do my commentators construe Obadiah 19–20: who possesses what? Which words do they treat as glosses, and what do they make of "this host" (הַחֵל־הַזֶּה) in v. 20? Please quote any translation they offer.
- **Why:** the ESV and NASB95 draw different maps. The ESV makes the exiles of v. 20 possess "the land of the Canaanites"; the NASB95 has them "among the Canaanites". BHS marks several phrases in v. 19 as probable glosses and v. 20 as corrupt. The dig's land-left-undone reading (audit B7, low–moderate) leans on the syntax.
- **Watch for:** a confident map resting on a conjecture. Ask which words are the commentator's emendation.

**3.6 Verse 21: "saviours" and "judge"**

- **Ask:** In Obadiah 21, how do my commentators take מוֹשִׁעִים ("saviours" or "deliverers"), against the Greek "men who have been saved", and "from Mount Zion" against "on/to Mount Zion"? Who do they take these figures to be, and does לִשְׁפֹּט mean "to judge" or "to rule" here?
- **Why:** the ESV says "to rule" and the NASB95 "to judge", so the ESV's congregation does not hear the link with Judges. BHS proposes "those saved" and "from the mountain". The audit put the Judges pattern (Judg 2:16, 18; Neh 9:27) nearer than Isa 63.
- **Watch for:** a messianic identification made without argument from the verse. Record who makes it and on what ground.

**3.7 Verse 17: "their possessions" or "their dispossessors"**

- **Ask:** In Obadiah 17, do my commentators read מוֹרָשֵׁיהֶם as "their possessions" or, with the Murabba'at scroll and the versions, as "their dispossessors" (מוֹרִשֵׁיהֶם)? Which do they adopt, and why? How do they take "and it will be holy"?
- **Why:** the MT and the older witnesses differ, and BHS says to read the second. The ESV's "their own possessions" is an interpretation, and neither English version shows the variant. The second reading would sharpen the reversal `[I]`.
- **Watch for:** "the versions" with none named. Check any claim against the BHS apparatus; I can do this when you bring the answer back.

**3.8 Verse 7: the bread and the trap**

- **Ask:** In Obadiah 7, how do my commentators treat "your bread" (לַחְמְךָ), given that some propose "those fighting you" (לֹחֲמֶיךָ)? Who do they take "there is no understanding in him" to refer to, and do they treat it as original or as a gloss?
- **Why:** the audit's Ps 41:9 link (A4b, low–moderate) rests entirely on the MT's "your bread". The ESV turns the aside into direct address ("you have no understanding").
- **Watch for:** Ps 41:9 used to defend the MT and the MT used to defend Ps 41:9. Name it if it happens.

**3.9 Verse 1: "we" and "her"** — *low priority*

- **Ask:** In Obadiah 1, how do my commentators explain "we have heard" (Jeremiah 49:14 has "I have heard") and "against her" after a masculine Edom?
- **Why:** both features are also evidence for question 2.1.
- **Watch for:** the feminine explained as "the land" with no parallel given.

**3.10 Sepharad** — *low priority*

- **Ask:** Where do my commentators locate Sepharad (Obadiah 20), and what ancient evidence do they give for it? Do any discuss the Greek Ephratha (Εφραθα)?
- **Why:** סְפָרַד ("Sepharad") stands only here in the Hebrew Bible. The dig gives Sardis as "often proposed", marked `[S, uncertain — verify]`. Nothing should rest on it.
- **Watch for:** a Lydian inscription mentioned without a reference. Also the Spanish identification, which belongs to later tradition, as far as I recall (I have not checked this); record it as reception.

## 4. History of Interpretation for Claim Audit 1

The audit's history rows came from its auditors' memory, tagged `[S]`. These questions replace that memory with sources. Each asks who proposes the link, whether anyone calls it idiom, and, where it matters, the direction. Ask 4.1–4.6 first.

**4.1 Numbers 24:17–21 (audit B3)**

- **Ask:** Which commentators connect Obadiah with Balaam's oracle in Numbers 24:17–21 (a star from Jacob, Edom a possession, the survivor destroyed by one from Jacob, the nest set in the rock)? What wording do they point to, and does anyone reject the link?
- **Why:** *moderate–high*. The old connection of Num 24:17–18 with Edom is known; reading Obad 4 and 18 from it is, as far as the auditor could recall, not standard. Your answer would test that.
- **Watch for:** Num 24:18 alone cited as "Edom's fate", without any point about Obadiah's wording. Record it, but it does not meet the criterion.

**4.2 Micah 7:8–10 (audit A8)**

- **Ask:** Which commentators on Obadiah or Micah discuss a link between Obadiah 10–12 and Micah 7:8–10 ("do not rejoice over me", "shame will cover her", "my eyes will look on her")? Do they treat it as dependence, as shared lament language, or otherwise?
- **Why:** *moderate–high*. Shame as subject of "cover" with the enemy as object stands only at these two places (WLC).
- **Watch for:** the rejoicing motif alone offered as the link. It is common, and it is the shame clause that carries the claim.

**4.3 Jeremiah 38:22 and Psalm 41:9 at Obadiah 7 (audit A4)**

- **Ask:** Which commentators discuss the link between Obadiah 7 and Jeremiah 38:22 ("the men at peace with you… prevailed over you")? Do any treat the words as a proverb or a stock saying? Which direction do they argue? Do any also cite Psalm 41:9?
- **Why:** Jer 38:22 is the audit's only clean Confirmed, *moderate–high*, with direction open and possibly a proverb. Ps 41:9 stands apart, low–moderate.
- **Watch for:** John 13:18 brought in through Ps 41:9. It is not evidence about Obadiah.

**4.4 Isaiah 14:13–15 at verses 3–4 (queue O21)**

- **Ask:** Which commentators compare Obadiah 3–4 with Isaiah 14:13–15 ("you said in your heart… above the stars… brought down")? Do they call this dependence, or a shared taunt tradition? Do any cite Job 39:27–28 or Jeremiah 49:16 alongside it?
- **Why:** the audit thought this the best model for Obadiah's pluses over Jer 49. It is in the overview at *moderate* and not yet audited. It also bears on O24.
- **Watch for:** a mythological background (Canaanite, Babylonian) offered instead. Record it as `[S]`, but it is a different claim.

**4.5 Amos 9:12 and the place of Obadiah in the Twelve (audit B2)**

- **Ask:** Which commentators explain the placing of Obadiah straight after Amos by Amos 9:12 ("that they may possess the remnant of Edom")? What wording do they point to? Does anyone argue against reading it as editorial design, and do any discuss the different order in the Greek?
- **Why:** *moderate* as contacts, *low–moderate* as design. Twelve-level studies may not be in your library, so a thin answer here means "not found in your library", not "not found".
- **Watch for:**
  - A theory of the editing of the Twelve presented as fact.
  - Rabbinic or later explanations (Obadiah as an Edomite, for instance) presented as evidence for the placement.

**4.6 Isaiah 10:17–20 at verses 17–18 (queue O22)**

- **Ask:** Which commentators connect Obadiah 17–18 (fire, flame, stubble, those who escape of the house of Jacob) with Isaiah 10:17–20? Which other texts do they name? Does anyone call the imagery stock language?
- **Why:** it is in the overview at *moderate*, found by the audit, and not yet tested. Isa 10:20 has פְּלֵיטַת בֵּית־יַעֲקֹב ("the escaped of the house of Jacob"). Other fire or stubble texts a commentator may name instead include Exod 15:7, Mal 4:1 (Hebrew 3:19) and Zech 12:6.
- **Watch for:** a list of "fire" texts with no shared wording named.

**4.7 Malachi 1:2–4 and the Edom frame of the Twelve (audit B6)**

- **Ask:** Which commentators relate Obadiah's brother charge (v. 10) to Malachi 1:2–4 (Jacob and Esau; Edom laid waste)? Do any argue that Edom forms a frame or a running thread across the Book of the Twelve, and does anyone reject the idea?
- **Why:** Obad 10 with Mal 1:2–4 is *moderate–high*; "frame" is *low*. The July overview used Malachi and Rom 9 for election, and the November overview warned against Esau as a cipher. The comparison called it canonical reflection, not the book's own argument.
- **Watch for:** Rom 9:13 read back into Obadiah, where אָהֵב ("love") and שָׂנֵא ("hate") do not occur and the charge is conduct.

**4.8 Psalm 137:7–8 at verse 15 (queue O23)**

- **Ask:** Do any commentators connect Obadiah 15 ("your dealings will return on your own head") with Psalm 137:7–8 (the sons of Edom on the day of Jerusalem; recompense, גְּמוּל, on daughter Babylon)? Do they treat this as a shared tradition about 586?
- **Why:** untested. גְּמוּל ("recompense") with a second-person suffix stands only in Ps 137:8 and Obad 15 (WLC), but Ps 137:8 addresses Babylon, not Edom.
- **Watch for:** Ps 137:7 alone. It belongs to question 1.2, not to this link.

**4.9 The talion parallels (audit A10, B5)**

- **Ask:** In discussing "as you have done, it will be done to you" (Obadiah 15), which texts do my commentators cite as parallels: Leviticus 24:19, Judges 1:7, Psalm 7:16, Ezekiel 35:15, Joel 3:4, 7, or others? Does anyone argue that the mirror between vv. 10–14 and vv. 15–21 is a deliberate design?
- **Why:** the principle is *high* as stated; the lexical design, *low–moderate*. Ps 7:16 is *low–moderate*.
- **Watch for:** "mirror" asserted with no pairs of words. On the counts, vv. 10–14 and 15–21 share 3 content words against a chance average of 8.0.

**4.10 Lamentations (audit A11)**

- **Ask:** Which commentators connect Obadiah with Lamentations 2:3, 2:22 and 4:21–22 (fire in Jacob, no survivor or escapee on the day of the LORD's anger, daughter of Edom)? Which do they treat as closest? Does anyone read Obadiah 18 as reversing Lamentations 2:3?
- **Why:** *moderate* overall; the reversal is *low–moderate*, and the text marks no reversal. The auditor rated the history row strongest for Lam 4:21–22, but from memory.
- **Watch for:** Lam 2:22 offered as dependence. Survivor and fugitive are a common pair, so ask what more than the pair is shared.

**4.11 Isaiah 63 and the Judges pattern at verse 21 (audit B8)**

- **Ask:** Which commentators read Obadiah 21 with Isaiah 63:1–6 (the one coming from Edom), and which with the deliverers of Judges or Nehemiah 9:27? Does anyone connect Obadiah 21 with Revelation 11:15 or Revelation 19?
- **Why:** Isa 63 to Rev 19 is *high*; Obad 21 to Isa 63 is *low–moderate*. The overview's Christological section uses these with care, and an ownerless link should not reach the pulpit.
- **Watch for:** a typological chain with no step argued. Record each step separately.

**4.12 Sound plays (audit A12)**

- **Ask:** Do any commentators note a play on sound or sense between "their disaster" (אֵידָם, Obadiah 13) and "Edom" (אֱדוֹם), or between "his misfortune" (נָכְרוֹ, v. 12) and "foreigners" (נָכְרִים, v. 11)?
- **Why:** the first is *Uncertain* ("may be heard as"), the second *moderate*. A commentator noticing them meets the criterion. Their absence means little.
- **Watch for:** a play asserted from an English transliteration.

**4.13 Psalm 22:28 at verse 21 (audit A3)** — *low priority*

- **Ask:** Do any commentators connect "the kingdom will be the LORD's" (Obadiah 21) with Psalm 22:28 ("the kingdom is the LORD's")? Do they treat it as a shared acclamation?
- **Why:** *low–moderate*: an exact shared acclamation, not a demonstrated allusion.

**4.14 Habakkuk 2 (audit A9)** — *low priority*

- **Ask:** Do any commentators compare Obadiah 3–4, 10 with Habakkuk 2:9 and 2:17 (the nest set on high; the violence of Lebanon)?
- **Why:** *low–moderate* as a cluster; 2:17 alone *moderate*; 2:16 dropped.

**4.15 The land left undone (audit B7)** — *low priority*

**4.16 Genesis 27:41 and Numbers 20:14 (audit A5)** — *low priority*

**4.17 Deuteronomy 32:35 (audit A6)** — *low priority*

- **Why:** *low–moderate*, and Jer 18:17 has v. 13's exact form.

**4.18 The wisdom register (audit A7b)** — *low priority*

**4.19 Zechariah 12:2 and Amos 4:1 at verse 16 (queue O25)** — *low priority*

## 6. Lexicons: Open the Entry and Paste It

These are faster done by hand than through the Assistant, and they use no credits. Paste the whole entry. If you would rather use the Assistant, scope to *My Library* and name the lexicon.
