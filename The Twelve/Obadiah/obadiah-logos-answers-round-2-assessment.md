## Summary

- **Two new witnesses, and they matter more than the Assistant's answers.**
  - **The Murabbaʿat scroll (Mur 88)** reads מורישיהם at v. 17, with the yod, which can only be the participle "those who dispossessed them". At v. 16 it has תמיד ("continually"), not סביב ("around"), and the plural שתיתם ("you drank"). At v. 20 it has the MT's החל הזה ("this host") `[T: Mur 88, as Penner transcribes it]`.
  - **The Targum** reads v. 17 as "the possessions of the peoples who had held them". That is the MT's noun, "possessions", glossed. At v. 16 it has "as you *rejoiced* over the blow on my holy mountain", which turns the drinking into Edom's rejoicing `[T: Targum, CAL]`.
- **v. 17 stays at moderate, with the evidence now split.** The only Hebrew witness besides the MT, and the Greek, Vulgate and Peshitta, read "dispossessors". The Targum and the MT read "possessions".
- **v. 16: the lean to Judah is thinner.** The Targum and Robinson read it of Edom; the plural argument for Judah was already neutralised by audit 2. The rating does not move on history, but the lean now rests almost wholly on the cup texts. See §4.
- **"Around" at v. 16 is closed.** No early witness has it: Mur 88, the Targum (תְּדִירָא, "continually") and the Greek all read against it.
- **The composite scenario has a holder.** Assis treats vv. 1–9 as a pre-exilic oracle on Edom's pride, supplemented after 587 with vv. 10–14, 15b `[S: Assis, through Hadjiev 42–43]`. The synoptic run's scenario was ownerless; now it is one named scholar's reconstruction.
- **Jer 48 // Isa 15–16 shows the same three options as Jer 49 // Obadiah.** Barnes uses Isa 16:13 for a common older source; Lalleman denies direct dependence; Lange and Kidner have Jeremiah reusing Isaiah. **Either way the Moab material came into Jeremiah from outside**, so the analogy behind the audited lean holds.
- **History rows filled:**
  - the Joel quotation (Hubbard, Baker, Allen);
  - Num 24 (Robbins, 1846, besides Delitzsch);
  - Amos 9:2 (Lange);
  - Mal 3:19 with v. 18 (Keil);
  - Gen 32:8 at v. 17 (Baker);
  - Jer 48:28 at v. 3 (Fausset);
  - Grotius's Isa 37:32 at v. 17, through Fausset.

  Absences: the Ezek 35 plurals, the composition of Jer 49 as an argument for direction, Isa 29:14 at v. 8, and the grammar of a past-referring prohibition.

- **49:16:** a cf Ob 3.4 · b–b l השׁיאַתְךָ · c sic L, mlt Mss Edd הַּ—.

**Reading them.**
- **Note d** is an editor's proposal, with no witness cited. Its wording is as the export gave it, so the placement of the letter is the only open point, and nothing rests on it `[T: BHS]`. The synoptic report's Open Question 5 can be closed.
- **Note b at 49:10** confirms the synoptic report: the Syriac (and the Targum, in brackets) read Obadiah's root `[T: BHS]`.
- **Note b at 49:14** shows the Greek of Jeremiah reading "he sent", active, where the MT has the passive participle. Greek Obadiah has the active too (ἐξαπέστειλεν). So both Greek translations agree against the MT of both books here `[T: BHS; Swete]`.
- **BHS's own "cf Ob" notes** stand at 49:9 (Ob 5, from the export; Patrick's paste began at 49:10), 49:10 (Ob 6), 14 (Ob 1), 15 (Ob 2) and 16 (Ob 3–4), and nowhere else in 49:7–22 `[T: BHS]`. That is the synoptic run's close block exactly. BHS draws no line to Obadiah at the two theme-only pairs (49:7 // Obad 8; 49:22 // Obad 9). At 49:9 BHS also notes that the Greek reads "they will lay their hand" for the MT's "they destroyed" `[T: BHS]`.

### 2.2 Mur 88 at Obad 16, 17 and 20 (A2)

| Verse | Mur 88 (Penner) | Against the MT | Bearing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | שתיתם ("you [pl.] drank")… ת֯מ֯יד ("continually") | Agrees | "Around" has no early Hebrew support; the plural is old |
| 17 | את מורי̇שיהם̇ (the yod marked uncertain) | Plene yod | **Only the participle "those who dispossessed them" fits**; the noun מוֹרָשׁ ("possession") has no yod |
| 20 | ה֯ח֯ל ה̇זה֯ ("this host")… ב֯ס֯פ֯ר֯ד̇ ("in Sepharad") | Agrees | The crux is in the oldest Hebrew too; no help for Halah |

**Two cautions.**
- **The decisive yod at v. 17 carries Penner's mark of an uncertain letter.** It is a probable reading, not a certain one `[T: Penner's marks]`.
- **The scroll is one manuscript**, from the early second century AD by the usual dating `[S]`. It is a Hebrew witness older than the MT's codices, not a proof of the original.

The *Lexham Textual Notes* entry Patrick pasted at v. 17 again says the Greek, Syriac and Vulgate read "their possessions". They read the opposite. This is the error round 1 recorded `[T]`.

### 2.3 The Targum at Obad 16, 17 and 20 (A3)

Glosses are mine `[I]`, and the Aramaic is as CAL prints it.
- **v. 16:** אֲרֵי כְמָא דַחדִיתוּן עַל מַחַת טוּרָא דְקוּדשִי ("for as you [pl.] *rejoiced* over the blow on my holy mountain"); then כַס פֹורעָנוּתְהֹון תְדִירָא ("the cup of their punishment continually").
  - The Targum keeps the plural.
  - It replaces "drank" with "rejoiced", which makes the subject Edom, rejoicing as v. 12 forbade.
  - It supplies the cup that the Hebrew lacks, and it keeps "continually".
- **v. 17:** שֵיזָבָא ("deliverance"), an abstract noun, as at v. 17 in the Greek and Syriac; then קַדִישִין ("holy ones"), people rather than place. Then נִכסֵי עַמְמַיָא דַהֲוֹו מַחסְנִין לְהֹון ("the possessions of the peoples who had been holding them"). **The noun is the MT's "possessions"**, and the gloss names the peoples who held them, a gloss that leans towards the other reading's sense.
- **v. 20:** עַמָא הָדֵין ("this people") for הַחֵל הַזֶּה ("this host"), and בִספָמיָא ("in Aspamia", Spain) for Sepharad `[T]`. The Targum is the source of the Spain reading noted in round 1.

**Weight.** The Targum is an interpretive translation, and its "rejoiced" at v. 16 is an interpretation, not a variant `[I]`. It is still the oldest Jewish reading of the verse that we now hold, and it reads Edom.

### 2.4 The Greek at Obad 16 (A2)

Rahlfs: πίονται πάντα τὰ ἔθνη οἶνον, πίονται ("all the nations will drink *wine*, they will drink"). Swete lacks πάντα τὰ ἔθνη οἶνον, πίονται, which leaves one πίονται ("they will drink") `[T]`. Swete prints Vaticanus. The omission falls between two identical words, which is the classic shape of an accidental skip (homoeoteleuton) `[I]`. So Swete's shorter text is likely a slip in one manuscript, and Rahlfs's "wine" stands as the Greek. Where Rahlfs's οἶνον ("wine") stands, the MT has תָּמִיד ("continually"). The overview's Greek table should carry "wine" with Rahlfs, as Auditor A noted.

### 2.5 No Peshitta, no other Vulgate (A4)

The Peshitta at vv. 16, 17, 21 remains the peshitta.eu text, edition unnamed, and the Vulgate the Clementine at v. 17. Both stay tagged as such.

## 3. Part B: The Assistant

### B1. The Ezek 35 plurals and Obad 16

**Absence on the question.** No commentary addresses the plurals at Ezek 35:9 or 35:13, or compares them with Obad 16 `[S: none found]`. The Assistant's explanation, that the plural reaches past the mountain to its inhabitants, is its own `[RA]`. Thompson (Cornerstone, p. 212) speaks only of the mountain's personification. The observation therefore stays audit 2's: 31 singular, 5 plural, in two places (WLC).

**A by-product.** Robinson (Biblical Expositor 2:316) reads the v. 16 cup as "festive celebration over Jerusalem's fall" turned into the cup of fury. That makes him a holder for the Edom reading `[S: Robinson, as summarised]`.

### B2. The force of vv. 12–14

- **Grammar: absence.** No grammar in the library discusses אַל ("not") with a jussive referring to a past event. Davidson (*Hebrew Syntax*, p. 89) cites Obad 12–14 only for אַל kept through a run of prohibitions "in impassioned language" `[S: Davidson]`. Audit 2's "no attested past-tense use with the second person" stands unchallenged.
- **Commentators:**
  - **Hadjiev (p. 45):** "most interpreters" take it as a rhetorical device for past actions. That is Hadjiev's own count, not the Assistant's `[S: Hadjiev]`.
  - **Baker (NIVAC, p. 181):** the prophet puts himself back in the day and calls "STOP!" — reading (b) `[S: Baker]`.
  - **Patterson–Hill (p. 228)** set out all three readings and quote Keil for (c). They also quote Finley: "in the final analysis very little difference may exist between the two interpretations" `[S: Patterson–Hill; Finley through them]`.
- **Effect.** The past referent stands at moderate–high. The force stays open, but **reading (b), an imaginative return to the day, is the majority reading on Hadjiev's report**. Finley's remark is the useful pastoral point: for a sermon, (b) and (c) come to nearly the same thing. A majority is not an argument, and the overview should say "most commentators" with Hadjiev as its source, not "the text shows".

### B3. The composition of Jer 49:7–22

**Absence on the argument.** No Jeremiah commentary in the library discusses the doublets (49:18–21 = 50:40, 44–46; 49:22 = 48:40–41; 49:17 ≈ 19:8) as evidence for how the oracle was made or for its relation to Obadiah. Kaiser–Rata (p. 526) give the Obadiah parallels and say no theory of priority is conclusive `[S]`.

Robinson (Biblical Expositor 2:310) takes Obadiah to quote an earlier utterance, from the way Obadiah introduces it ("we have heard a report") `[S: Robinson]`. That makes him a holder for an older oracle behind Obadiah, close to the common-source view.

**The synoptic run's distribution argument therefore has no holder in this library.** It remains a finding from the text, rated by audit 2: moderate–high for the compilation, moderate for "from outside Jeremiah".

### B4. A composite view split at v. 9

**Found: Assis** `[S: through Hadjiev, pp. 42–43]`:
- vv. 1–9 are a late pre-exilic prophecy against Edom's arrogance;
- vv. 10–14 and 15b were added after 587, accusing Edom over Jerusalem's destruction;
- vv. 15a and 16–21 are later still, about Edomite occupation of Judean land.

His grounds, as reported: the change of accusation, from pride to crimes against Judah.

**Bearing.** This is the synoptic run's scenario, an older Edom poem given a brother afterwards, held by a named scholar.
- *It strengthens the scenario's standing, not its rating* (low–moderate). It still rests on a change of subject, which a single author can also make.
- *It fits the audited result* that the shared lines came into Jeremiah from outside: a pre-exilic vv. 1–9 could be what both books drew on.
- *Assis is not owned* and reaches us second-hand; the Assistant's paraphrase of his grounds is partly its own `[RA]`.

The overview's composition paragraph should add him to Hadjiev's list.

### B5. Jer 48 // Isa 15–16

The holders split three ways:

| Holder | View | Use of Isa 16:13 |
|---|---|---|
| Barnes (Jeremiah, pp. 258–59; Isaiah, p. 285) | Jeremiah expands Isaiah; or both draw on an ancient prophecy | **Yes**: Isa 16:13 shows Isaiah using an older oracle, which Jeremiah may have used too |
| Lalleman (TOTC, p. 286) | Resemblance need not mean dependence; Num 21:27–30, older, shaped both | No |
| Lange (Jeremiah, p. 373) | From 48:29 Jeremiah makes "constant, more or less free use of older utterances", Isaiah especially (48:29–38) | Notes Isaiah reproduces an older oracle |
| Kidner (NBC, p. 644); Feinberg (EBC, p. 661) | Isaiah quoted and expanded; affinities | No |

**Bearing on O31/O32.** Every holder has the Moab material coming into Jeremiah from outside: from Isaiah, from Isaiah's source, or from Numbers. None has Isaiah drawing on Jeremiah `[S]`. **The analogy behind the audited lean holds**, and it holds in the same three forms the Obadiah question takes: borrowing, a common older source, or shared tradition. Lange's "free use of older utterances" is the Moab chapter's version of Keil's "Jeremiah's habit". Rating unchanged: from outside Jeremiah, moderate.

### B6. Obad 17, Jer 49:2 and Ezek 36:2–5

The HCSB and CSB study Bibles (Parsons) cite Jer 49:2 for the conquest motif of vv. 17–20, and Ezek 36:2, 5 for its setting. They count "possess by dispossessing" five times in vv. 17–20, once of the enemies who dispossessed Israel, which means they read "dispossessors" at v. 17 `[S: Parsons]`. They do not argue the textual choice. Study-Bible tier; one Hays row. v. 17 stays at moderate, on the witnesses of §2.

### B7. "As the LORD has said" in Joel

- **Holders for Joel quoting Obad 17:** Hubbard (TOTC, p. 77, adding that both may draw on an oral tradition); Baker (NIVAC, p. 26; NBC, p. 812); Allen (NBC, p. 789) `[S]`.
- **Isa 37:32 / 2 Kgs 19:31:** no one names them for Joel. **But Grotius, through Fausset (4:568), reads Obad 17 itself of the deliverance from Sennacherib in Isa 37:32** `[S: Grotius, through Fausset]`. So the Isaiah text has an old holder at Obad 17, not at Joel.
- **The Assistant's reason** (Isaiah historical, Obadiah eschatological) is its own `[RA]`.
- **Effect:**
  - Joel later stays at moderate, now with modern holders.
  - O40 is reframed: Isa 37:32 / 2 Kgs 19:31 stand beside Obad 17 as the other escape-on-Zion texts (Grotius), not as Joel's referent.
  - Hubbard's oral tradition is the common-source option in Joel's case too.

**A corpus check on O39.** אֵיד ("calamity") occurs 24 times in 22 verses (WLC). Jeremiah's uses are 18:17 and four in the oracles against the nations: 46:21 (Egypt), 48:16 (Moab), 49:8 (Edom), 49:32 (Kedar). The rest are in Deut 32:35, 2 Sam 22:19 / Ps 18:19, Obad 13, Ezek 35:5, and nine in Job and Proverbs. **It is the word of Jeremiah's oracles against the nations and of wisdom, not an Edom word**; Edom has three of the 22 verses `[T]`. TWOT's "twenty-two occurrences" counts verses. **O39 should be reframed:** אֵיד is not Edom-oracle vocabulary, and the claim should be dropped.

## 6. Critical Assessment

- **The two texts are the real result of this round.** The Research Assistant mostly reported absences, which is what the question sheet predicted. Mur 88 and the Targum change what the v. 17 and v. 16 entries can say, and they came from the free look-ups.
- **Mur 88's yod is a single, marked-uncertain letter in a damaged scroll.** It is good evidence and should be stated as probable, not certain. If the overview leaned harder on v. 17 because of it, the next audit would be right to ask for the plate or another edition.
- **The Targum cuts both ways and should not be recruited to one side.** Its "possessions of the peoples who had held them" keeps the MT's noun while giving the other reading's sense. Its "rejoiced" at v. 16 is interpretation, not text. It is evidence of how the verses were read, not of what they said.
- **"Most interpreters" (Hadjiev) is a headcount.** The overview should report it as such and leave the choice of reading to the preacher, as it does now.
- **Assis is reported through one study guide.** His argument from the change of subject is the weakest kind for composition, since one writer can turn from pride to crime, as Obadiah's own v. 10 does. His view gives the scenario a name, not a proof.
- **The Assistant drifted.** It carried B6's terms into B8 and offered unfootnoted explanations in B1 and B7. Those sentences are marked `[RA]` above and have been given no weight.
- **The library limits this whole line of enquiry.** On Jeremiah and Ezekiel it is pastoral and older-critical; for Obadiah it has Block, Baker, Hadjiev and Keil, which round 1 used fully. A round 3 would add little without new books. On present holdings, the Obadiah work is as far as Logos can take it.
