# Claim Audit 2: Obadiah

**Claims audited:** 18. Auditor A took 11 single contacts and textual claims (A1–A11); Auditor B took 7 claims on design, grammar and direction (B1–B7). The 18 cover the queue (O21–O26, O28–O32), the two upgrades in overview v1.3 (Obad 12–14 past referent; Obad 17 "dispossessors"), the Joel 3:5 lean, and three findings of the Jeremiah 49 synoptic run.
**Date:** 9 November 2026
**Purpose:** Trigger 2 fired when overview v1.3 put two unaudited claims at moderate–high. The audit also clears the queue and tests the synoptic run's results before they enter the overview.
**Method:** Two blind auditors, run one after the other (standing rule of 16 October 2026). Neither saw the reports that made the claims, the Logos assessment, or the other auditor's report. Both had the WLC with lemma index, Swete, the SBLGNT, Rahlfs and BHS for the relevant books, and the Obadiah NASB95, ESV, Vulgate and Peshitta exports. Both were required to quote every occurrence of an "only" or "verbatim" claim, to run a positive control before any nil result, and to run chance baselines for field and design claims. Fourteen of their decisive results were re-checked in the main session.
**Editions:** WLC for all Hebrew counts; Swete for Greek observation, Rahlfs and BHS for citation.

---

## Verdict in Brief

- **One of the two v1.3 upgrades holds.** Obad 12–14 refer to past events: *moderate–high*, confirmed. Restate the grammar point as "no attested case of a past-tense sense", not "the grammar does not allow it". Obad 17's "those who dispossessed them" **comes down to moderate**. The scroll and all the versions support it, and Jer 49:2 has the same construction. But the versions count as one strand, and Ezek 36:2–5 supports the MT's "their possessions".
- **The Ezekiel 35 test of the v. 16 plural holds, and is stronger than claimed.** Ezek 35 addresses Edom 31 times in the singular and 5 in the plural (35:9; 35:13). A single plural therefore need not mark a new addressee. The Judah reading of v. 16 stays at a slight lean, *low–moderate*, and now rests chiefly on the cup tradition (Jer 49:12; Lam 4:21; Isa 51:17–23).
- **The Jeremiah 49 synoptic run is partly confirmed and partly withdrawn.**
  - *Confirmed (moderate–high):* the compilation finding; the negative result on retention, with corrected figures.
  - *Withdrawn:* the house-vocabulary finding. "Stars" occurs in Jeremiah (31:35); "Lord GOD" belongs to Ezekiel and Amos, not to the Twelve; and an unchosen word set shows no significant difference between the two sides.
  - *Reframed:* the "older poem" lean. That the shared lines came into Jeremiah from outside is *moderate*; that they are older than Obadiah, or are Obadiah itself, is *low–moderate*.
- **The Joel lean stays at moderate**, on Joel's citation formula and Joel's habit of reuse. Drop the "longer is later" argument. Isa 37:32 / 2 Kgs 19:31 may lie behind Joel too.
- **Single contacts mostly hold at moderate or below.** Isa 14:13–15 (moderate) and Job 39:27–28 (moderate) are not one pool: Job supplies the wording and Isa 14 the hubris. Isa 10:20 has the only other "escaped of the house of Jacob", but in Isa 10:17 the fire is God. Ps 137:8 falls to low–moderate at v. 15, where Joel 4:4, 7 are the real partner. Ezek 35:5's "form" claim is withdrawn; the exact form is Jer 18:17. Zech 12:2 and the "around" variant are low.

## Critical Gaps

- **The synoptic run counted forms, not lemmas, for house vocabulary, and chose its words.** One form count missed a prefixed word (Jer 31:35). The Twelve was treated as one idiom when "Lord GOD" is Amos's and Ezekiel's. Auditor B's unchosen-word baseline (p ≈ 0.15) is the right test, and it was not run. **The rule:** a house-vocabulary claim needs lemma counts and an unchosen baseline before it is written.
- **v. 17 was raised on the versions alone.** The versions share one interpretive pressure, and the MT has its own support in Ezek 36:2–5. A textual upgrade above moderate needs a reason beyond agreement of the versions.
- **The Num 24:17–21 rating differs between audits.** Audit 1 raised Num 24:17–21 to moderate–high. Auditor A, blind to that verdict, found it independently as a new candidate and rates it moderate, though its window ranks in the top 0.5 per cent. The two audits agree on the contact and differ by one step on the rating. Keep audit 1's verdict, note the difference, and leave the rating for Finalise.
- **Not tested:** the Targum; the Vulgate and Peshitta beyond v. 17 (one web text, edition unrecorded); history of interpretation (only from the auditors' memory, tagged `[S]`, which moved no verdict).

## Report of Auditor A: Single Contacts and Text


**Scope:** claims A1–A11 (single contacts and text: wording, rarity, rivals, Greek and versions).
**Editions:** Hebrew observation from the WLC (lemma-indexed, Hebrew versification); Greek observation from Swete; Rahlfs LXX, the BHS text and apparatus, the Clementine Vulgate (Obad 17 only) and a web Peshitta text (Obad 16 opening, 17, 21; edition unrecorded) from the Logos exports; NT from the SBLGNT. Hebrew counts are by lemma unless stated as a surface phrase. Anything from memory is tagged [S] and carries no verdict.

**A note on method used throughout.** Besides the stated counts, I ran two kinds of chance baseline. (i) An independence estimate: how many verses (or five-verse windows) would be expected to contain a given combination of words if those words were scattered at random through the 23,213 verses of the WLC. (ii) An empirical window baseline: every five-verse window in the Hebrew Bible outside Obadiah (19,486 windows) was scored by the number of distinct, moderately rare words (occurring in fewer than 400 verses; proper names set aside) that it shares with Obadiah. Under that baseline, 25 per cent of all windows share three or more such words, 7 per cent share four or more, 1.6 per cent share five or more, and 0.4 per cent share six or more. A window sharing three is therefore ordinary. I also ran the corpus's own verse-against-verse scan, which ranks chapters by rare shared vocabulary.

### A1. Isa 14:13–15 at Obad 3–4

**What I tested.** The rarity of כּוֹכָב ("star") with לֵב / לֵבָב ("heart") in one verse; the "say in the heart" idiom; the descent verb; rival sources for the two lines Obadiah has and Jer 49:16 lacks — אֹמֵר בְּלִבּוֹ מִי יוֹרִדֵנִי אָרֶץ ("who says in his heart, who will bring me down to the ground?", v. 3b) and וְאִם־בֵּין כּוֹכָבִים שִׂים קִנֶּךָ ("and if among the stars your nest is set", v. 4a).

**Results.**
- כּוֹכָב ("star") with לֵב ("heart", short form) in one verse: no verse at all (WLC). כּוֹכָב with לֵבָב ("heart", long form) in one verse: Isa 14:13 only. The claim therefore holds only if the two spellings of "heart" are pooled, which is legitimate (they are one word in two forms), but it should say so. Positive control: Obad 3–4 is returned when the window is widened to two verses.
- Widened to within two verses, "star" with "heart" (either form) also occurs at Jer 31:33–35, Ezek 32:7–9, Ps 147:3–4 and Joel 2:10–12 — none of which has the hubris-and-descent frame.
- "Say in the heart" (אָמַר ("say") directly followed by "in/to the heart", either form): 21 verses (Gen 17:17; Deut 7:17; 8:17; 9:4; 18:21; Isa 14:13; 47:8; 47:10; 49:21; Jer 5:24; 13:22; Hos 7:2; Obad 3; Zeph 1:12; 2:15; Ps 4:5; 10:6, 11, 13; 35:25; 74:8). It is idiom; Isa 47:8, 10 and Zeph 2:15 use it of arrogant nations too.
- "Star" + "say" + יָרַד ("go down / bring down") within two verses: Gen 26:2, Exod 32:13, Isa 14:13 and Obad 3 (WLC). Only Isa 14 and Obadiah have the hubris sense. "Star" + "heart" + "go down" within two verses: Isa 14:13 (long form) and Obad 4 (short form) only. The independence estimate for "star" + "say in the heart" + "go down" falling within one five-verse window is 0.06 windows in the whole Hebrew Bible; one such window outside Obadiah (Isa 14:11–15) is observed. That beats the null, though hubris oracles cluster these words, so independence overstates rarity.
- **Rival for v. 4's syntax.** מִשָּׁם ("from there") with a causative form of "bring down" in one verse: Jer 49:16, Amos 9:2 and Obad 4 only (WLC). Amos 9:2 reads אִם־יַחְתְּרוּ בִשְׁאוֹל מִשָּׁם יָדִי תִקָּחֵם וְאִם־יַעֲלוּ הַשָּׁמַיִם מִשָּׁם אוֹרִידֵם ("if they dig into Sheol, from there my hand will take them; and if they climb to the heavens, from there I will bring them down"). Obadiah's double אִם… וְאִם ("if… and if…") followed by מִשָּׁם אוֹרִידְךָ ("from there I will bring you down") is exactly Amos's frame; Jer 49:16 has כִּי ("though") and no second limb. In Greek the match is verbatim: ἐκεῖθεν κατάξω ("from there I will bring down") occurs in Swete at Amos 9:2 and Obad 4 only; Greek Jer 29:17 (= Heb 49:16) has ἐκεῖθεν καθελῶ σε ("from there I will pull you down").
- **Rival for שִׂים קִנֶּךָ ("set your nest").** "Set" with "nest" in one verse: Num 24:21, Obad 4 and Hab 2:9 only. Num 24:21 has the same anomalous form שִׂים ("set") with קִנֶּךָ ("your nest") and with סֶלַע ("rock"): וְשִׂים בַּסֶּלַע קִנֶּךָ ("and set in the rock your nest"). BHS on Obad 4 notes the form שִׂים is suspect (read "you set", with the versions, or delete); Num 24:21 shows the same form in the same phrase.
- Ezek 28:2, 8 (heart lifted up; "they will bring you down to the pit") and Ezek 31:10–18 (height, "between the clouds", heart lifted up, brought down to Sheol) share the hubris-and-descent theme. Ezek 31 actually shares more rare vocabulary with Obadiah than Isa 14 does: the window Ezek 31:11–15 shares ten of the rare words (fewer than 20 of 19,486 windows do as well), Isa 14:11–15 only three (ordinary; a quarter of all windows do as well). The corpus scan places Ezek 31 fifth of 923 chapters and Isa 14 at rank 510 with no rare-vocabulary verse contacts at all. Isa 14's claim rests on a combination of common words, not on rare ones. Job 20:6 and Ps 139:8 are the general "if he climbs to the heavens" idiom without stars, speech in the heart or descent by God.
- Greek: Isa 14:13 has εἶπας τῇ διανοίᾳ σου ("you said in your mind") and ἀστέρων ("stars"); Obad 3–4 has λέγων ἐν καρδίᾳ αὐτοῦ ("saying in his heart") and ἄστρων ("stars"). The Greek surface contact is weaker than the Hebrew.

**Corrections to stated counts.** "Star with heart in one verse: Isa 14:13 only" holds only with the long form of "heart" counted; with the short form there is no verse. State that the two forms are pooled.

**Verdict: Confirmed with nuance — recommended rating moderate.** Isa 14:12–15 is the only passage that combines speech in the heart, the stars and a forced descent, and that combination beats a chance baseline, so it remains the best single model for the *content* of Obadiah's extra lines. The extra lines are best described as drawing on a shared hubris-and-descent repertoire in which Isa 14, Amos 9:2 and Num 24:21 each contribute something.

**Missed.** Amos 9:2 (syntax), Num 24:21 (exact phrase), Ezek 31 (lexically denser than Isa 14).

### A2. Isa 10:17–20 at Obad 17–18

**What I tested.** The combination of fire, flame, consuming and "the escaped of the house of Jacob"; rivals for "a people as fire"; and the sense of the fire in each place.

**Results.**
- פְּלֵיטָה ("escape, escaped remnant") with יַעֲקֹב ("Jacob") in one verse: Isa 10:20 and Obad 17 only (WLC); with בֵּית ("house") as well, the same two. Independence estimate: 0.38 verses expected; one observed outside Obadiah. Modestly better than chance. Positive control: Obad 17 returned.
- אֵשׁ ("fire") with לֶהָבָה ("flame") and אָכַל ("consume") in one verse: nine verses — Num 21:28; Isa 5:24; Isa 10:17; Jer 48:45; Ezek 21:3; Joel 1:19; Joel 2:3; Obad 18; Lam 2:3. This is stock imagery.
- "Fire" with "flame" and קַשׁ ("stubble") in one verse: Isa 5:24, Isa 47:14 and Obad 18 only; within two verses, Joel 2:3–5 and Ps 83:15 as well. Isa 10:17 has thorns and briars (שִׁית, שָׁמִיר), not stubble. Fire with "stubble" and "consume" in one verse: Isa 5:24, Isa 33:11, Joel 2:5, Obad 18.
- "Fire" + "flame" + "escape" within three verses: Isa 4:5, Isa 10:17, Joel 2:3, Joel 2:5, Obad 18. Joel 2:3 has all three in one verse: אֵשׁ… לֶהָבָה… וְגַם־פְּלֵיטָה לֹא־הָיְתָה לּוֹ ("fire… flame… and there was no escape from it").
- **Sense.** In Isa 10:17 the fire is God: אוֹר־יִשְׂרָאֵל לְאֵשׁ וּקְדוֹשׁוֹ לְלֶהָבָה ("the light of Israel will become a fire and his Holy One a flame") burns Assyria. In Obad 18 the fire is the people: בֵית־יַעֲקֹב אֵשׁ וּבֵית יוֹסֵף לֶהָבָה ("the house of Jacob a fire and the house of Joseph a flame"). The claim's wording "a people as fire" does not fit Isa 10:17.
- **Rivals for "a people as fire".** Zech 12:6: the clans of Judah "like a pot of fire among wood and a torch of fire among sheaves, and they will consume all the peoples around". Greek Zech 12:6 even has ἐν καλάμῃ ("among stubble"), matching Greek Obad 18 εἰς καλάμην ("for stubble"). Amos 5:6: פֶּן־יִצְלַח כָּאֵשׁ בֵּית יוֹסֵף וְאָכְלָה ("lest he break out like fire against the house of Joseph and it consume") — "house of Joseph" with "fire" in one verse: Amos 5:6 and Obad 18 only (WLC). In Amos the fire is against Joseph; in Obadiah Joseph is the flame.
- **Num 24:19** (see New candidates): שָׂרִיד ("survivor") with "Jacob" within one verse: Num 24:19 and Obad 18 only. Num 24:18–19 also makes Edom a יְרֵשָׁה ("possession").
- Window baseline: Isa 10:17–21 shares four rare words with Obadiah; about 11 per cent of all windows share four or more — modest.

**Corrections.** None to counts (none were stated); the description "a people as fire" is inaccurate for Isa 10:17.

### A10. The text of Obad 17

**What I tested.** What the consonants allow; the spelling of the causative participle of "possess"; the Murabbaʿat reading as BHS reports it; Greek in both editions; the Vulgate and Peshitta (both present in the exports for v. 17); internal and external evidence for each sense.

**Results.**
- **BHS.** Apparatus at v. 17: "l מוֹרִשֵׁיהֶם cf Mur מוריש׳ et Vrs" — read "those who dispossessed them", compare the Murabbaʿat scroll, which spells the word with a yod after the ר, and the versions. The BHS text prints מֹורָֽשֵׁיהֶם ("their possessions") with the note letter.
- **The consonants.** MT מורשיהם can be pointed either as the noun מוֹרָשׁ ("possession") plural with "their", or as the causative participle of יָרַשׁ ("possess / dispossess") with "their", written without the yod. The causative participle elsewhere is spelled with the yod every time (Deut 9:4, 9:5, 18:12; 1 Sam 2:7: מוֹרִישׁ ("dispossessing")), but other causative forms of the same verb are written without it (Num 14:12, Num 14:24, Josh 13:12, Zech 9:4, Job 20:15), so a yod-less participle is possible. The noun מוֹרָשׁ ("possession") is rare: Isa 14:23, Obad 17, Job 17:11 only. Murabbaʿat's yod is the one consonantal witness that fixes the participle; it is an orthographic difference, but a real one.
- **Greek.** Rahlfs and Swete agree: κατακληρονομήσουσιν ὁ οἶκος Ιακωβ τοὺς κατακληρονομήσαντας αὐτούς ("the house of Jacob will inherit those who inherited them"). The Greek phrase occurs nowhere else in Swete. Note that it renders a participle with a personal object meaning "those who took possession of them" — which corresponds to the simple participle sense ("possessors") rather than strictly to the causative ("dispossessors"); the Greek cannot distinguish the two.
- **Vulgate (export, v. 17):** *et possidebit domus Jacob eos qui se possederant* ("and the house of Jacob will possess those who had possessed them"). Confirmed.
- **Peshitta (web text of unknown edition, v. 17):** ܘܢܺܐܪܬܽܘܢ ܕܒܶܝܬ ܝܰܥܩܽܘܒ ܠܰܐܝܠܶܝܢ ܕܺܝܪܶܬܘ ܐܶܢܽܘܢ ("and those of the house of Jacob will inherit those who inherited them"). Confirmed, with the edition caveat.
- **What the versions show and do not show.** All three read a participle with a personal object. None requires a consonantal text different from the MT; each could be a reading of the unpointed MT consonants. They therefore attest a reading tradition (possibly not independent: the Vulgate and Peshitta are known to consult the Greek at points [S]), not a different Hebrew text. Only Murabbaʿat is consonantal evidence, and it agrees with them.
- **Internal and parallel evidence, for the participle.** The reciprocity of v. 15 ("as you have done, it will be done to you") favours "possess those who dispossessed them". Jer 49:2 (Ammon oracle, in the same chapter as the Edom oracle) has the exact idea: וְיָרַשׁ יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶת־יֹרְשָׁיו ("and Israel will dispossess those who dispossessed him") — the only verse in the WLC where "possess" governs a participle of the same verb with a personal suffix. Isa 14:2 has the same reciprocity with another verb ("they will take captive their captors").
- **For the MT noun.** Ezek 36:2–5 (against Edom) twice uses the related noun מוֹרָשָׁה ("possession") for Israel's land, which Edom seized "with joy of heart": the Hebrew can be read as Jacob taking back the possessions Edom took. Obad 19–20 lists territories as the objects of "possess", which suits "their possessions"; but v. 19 also has a people as object (אֶת־פְּלִשְׁתִּים ("the Philistines")), so personal objects are in the passage too.

**Corrections.** The claim said the Vulgate and Peshitta are not in the corpus; both readings are present in the Logos exports (Vulgate v. 17; Peshitta, a web text, vv. 16–17, 21) and match what was stated.

| Design-claim check | Result |
|---|---|
| Counts verified | Mur reading and versions as stated; Greek identical in Rahlfs and Swete |
| Baseline | Not applicable (text-critical); the participle-with-personal-object idiom is attested once elsewhere (Jer 49:2) |
| Sense | Both readings make sense; the participle fits v. 15's reciprocity and Jer 49:2; the noun fits vv. 19–20 and Ezek 36:2–5 |
| Alternative explanation | Versions as interpretation of ambiguous MT consonants |

**Verdict: Confirmed with nuance — recommended rating moderate (lowered from moderate–high).** The participle reading has one consonantal witness (Murabbaʿat's yod), unanimous versional support, a reciprocity motive inside Obadiah and an exact idiom in Jer 49:2, so it is at least as likely as the MT. But the versions are one strand (a reading of the same consonants, probably not independent), and Ezek 36:2–5 gives the MT noun a coherent Edom-specific sense, so "likelier" should be held at moderate rather than moderate–high.

### A11. פְּלֵיטָה at Obad 17 as an abstract noun

**What I tested.** The sense of פְּלֵיטָה ("escape / escaped remnant") with הָיָה ("be") and with Zion elsewhere, its parallel terms, and its Greek, Latin and Syriac renderings.

**Results.**
- פְּלֵיטָה occurs in 28 verses (WLC). The exact construction תִּהְיֶה פְלֵיטָה ("there will be escape/escapees") occurs at Joel 3:5 and Obad 17 in the positive; negated at 2 Sam 15:14 (לֹא־תִהְיֶה־לָּנוּ פְלֵיטָה ("there will be no escape for us")) and Dan 11:42 ("will not be for escape"); Joel 2:3 and Jer 50:29 also have the negated form with "be". In the negated uses the sense is clearly abstract ("escape").
- With Zion: 2 Kgs 19:31 and Isa 37:32 וּפְלֵיטָה מֵהַר צִיּוֹן ("and an escaped band from Mount Zion") with תֵּצֵא ("will go out") and parallel שְׁאֵרִית ("remnant") — concrete. Joel 3:5 כִּי בְּהַר־צִיּוֹן וּבִירוּשָׁלַםִ תִּהְיֶה פְלֵיטָה… וּבַשְּׂרִידִים ("for on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem there will be escape… and among the survivors") — the parallel "survivors" makes it collective-concrete or at least people-centred.
- Inside Obadiah: v. 14 has פְּלִיטָיו ("his fugitives") with שְׂרִידָיו ("his survivors") — a concrete pair; v. 18 has שָׂרִיד ("survivor") for Esau. Verse 17's פְּלֵיטָה stands between them, as in Joel 3:5.
- **Greek.** Obad 17 σωτηρία ("deliverance") in both editions (Rahlfs with the article). Greek renderings of the same Hebrew noun elsewhere: σωτηρία at 2 Sam 15:14 (abstract), Jer 25:35 (Greek 32:21, abstract, parallel φυγή ("flight")) and Ezra 9:8 (where the Hebrew is concrete: "to leave us a remnant" rendered "to leave us for deliverance"); concrete participles in the Zion passages — ἀνασῳζόμενος ("one escaping") at 2 Kgs 19:31 and Joel 2:32 (Greek numbering of Joel 3:5), οἱ σῳζόμενοι ("those being saved") at Isa 37:32, οἱ σωθέντες τοῦ Ἰακώβ ("the saved of Jacob") at Isa 10:20. Within Greek Obadiah the translator uses the concrete participle for פָּלִיט at v. 14 (τοὺς ἀνασῳζομένους ("those escaping")) and σωτηρία at v. 17, so he did distinguish them. But Ezra 9:8 shows σωτηρία can render a concrete remnant.
- **Vulgate** *salvatio* ("salvation") and **Peshitta** ܡܫܰܘܙܒܽܘܬܳܐ ("deliverance") at v. 17 are also abstract nouns.

The noun should be glossed "deliverance / an escaped remnant" rather than forced into one sense.

### B1. Obad 12–14: past referent

#### What I tested
(1) The count and forms of the prohibitions. (2) Every אַל ("not") followed directly by a verb in the WLC, to see whether the construction ever has a past or counterfactual sense. (3) The run of verb forms in vv. 10–16. (4) Words that tie v. 11 to vv. 12–14 in the text itself.

#### Results
**The count holds.** There are eight אַל prohibitions: three in v. 12 (וְאַל־תֵּרֶא "do not look", וְאַל־תִּשְׂמַח "do not rejoice", וְאַל־תַּגְדֵּל פִּיךָ "do not make your mouth great"), three in v. 13 (אַל־תָּבוֹא "do not enter", אַל־תֵּרֶא "do not look", וְאַל־תִּשְׁלַחְנָה "do not stretch out"), and two in v. 14 (וְאַל־תַּעֲמֹד "do not stand", וְאַל־תַּסְגֵּר "do not hand over"). Four of them have a form that can only be jussive: the short forms תֵּרֶא ("look") twice, תַּגְדֵּל ("make great") and תַּסְגֵּר ("hand over"). The form תִּשְׁלַחְנָה in v. 13 is tagged second feminine plural and does not fit. BHS reads it as וְאַל־תִּשְׁלַח יָד ("do not stretch out a hand"). So the series holds one more number anomaly besides the plural in v. 16.

**The construction.** The WLC has 676 places where אַל stands directly before a verb. Of these, 480 are second person, 130 third person and 13 first person. I read the 44 verses in which the construction stands with יוֹם ("day"), together with the known places where scholars have suspected a past sense. No second-person case has a counterfactual meaning ("you should not have…"). The second-person cases fall into three groups:
- **Directives about the present or future.** These are the normal case.
- **Directives quoted inside a report of the past.** Gen 42:22 has הֲלוֹא אָמַרְתִּי אֲלֵיכֶם לֵאמֹר אַל־תֶּחֶטְאוּ בַיֶּלֶד ("Did I not say to you, 'Do not sin against the boy'?"). Lam 3:57 has אָמַרְתָּ אַל־תִּירָא ("you said, 'Do not fear'"). The prohibition keeps its forward-looking force. Only the frame is past.
- **Prohibitions whose force lasts, about a past act.** Gen 45:5 has אַל־תֵּעָצְבוּ… כִּי־מְכַרְתֶּם אֹתִי ("do not be grieved… that you sold me"). 2 Sam 19:20 has אַל־יַחֲשָׁב־לִי אֲדֹנִי עָוֺן וְאַל־תִּזְכֹּר אֵת אֲשֶׁר הֶעֱוָה עַבְדְּךָ בַּיּוֹם ("let my lord not count it against me… do not remember what your servant did wrong on the day…").

The **third-person** jussive *is* aimed at a past day in two curse passages:
- Jer 20:14: יוֹם אֲשֶׁר־יְלָדַתְנִי אִמִּי אַל־יְהִי בָרוּךְ ("the day my mother bore me, let it not be blessed").
- Job 3:4, 6, 7: אַל־יִדְרְשֵׁהוּ ("let God not seek it"), אַל־יִחַדְּ ("let it not rejoice"), אַל־יָבֹא ("let it not come"), אַל־תָּבֹא רְנָנָה בוֹ ("let no joyful cry come into it"), all about the night of Job's birth.

This is the nearest thing in the Hebrew Bible to an "imaginative return to the day". The speaker places himself at a day already past and pronounces on it in the jussive.

**Verb forms in vv. 10–16 (WLC tagging).**
- v. 10: imperfect תְּכַסְּךָ ("will cover you") and weqatal וְנִכְרַתָּ ("you will be cut off"). Both future.
- v. 11: infinitive with "day" (בְּיוֹם עֲמָדְךָ, "on the day you stood"), infinitive שְׁבוֹת ("carrying off"), and perfects בָּאוּ ("entered") and יַדּוּ ("cast"). Past.
- vv. 12–14: eight jussives.
- v. 15: perfect עָשִׂיתָ ("you have done"), past; imperfects יֵעָשֶׂה ("will be done") and יָשׁוּב ("will return"), future.
- v. 16: perfect שְׁתִיתֶם ("you drank"), past; then imperfect and weqatal, future.

So the passage is **framed by a past deed** (v. 11, v. 15a, v. 16a) **and its future requital** (v. 10, vv. 15b–16b). The prohibitions sit inside the frame.

**Lexical links between v. 11 and vv. 12–14** (WLC lemmas):
- חַיִל ("wealth"): v. 11 חֵילוֹ and v. 13 בְחֵילוֹ.
- גַּם־אַתָּה ("you too"): v. 11 and v. 13.
- שַׁעַר ("gate"): v. 11 (ketiv שערו) and v. 13 בְשַׁעַר.
- עָמַד ("stand"): v. 11 עֲמָדְךָ and v. 14 תַּעֲמֹד.
- the נכר group ("foreign/misfortune"): v. 11 נָכְרִים and v. 12 נָכְרוֹ.
- יוֹם ("day"): 10 times in vv. 11–14 (2, 4, 3, 1).

The prohibitions replay the scene of v. 11 item by item. Elsewhere in Obadiah "day" points forward: v. 8 בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא ("on that day") and v. 15 יוֹם־יְהוָה ("the day of the LORD").

**Greek.** Swete and Rahlfs both render the prohibitions as μή / μηδέ with the aorist subjunctive (μὴ ἐπίδῃς, "do not look on", and so on). This is the ordinary Greek prohibition, so the Greek does not settle the matter.

**English.** NASB95 and ESV have "Do not gloat…". NIV84 has "You should not look down… You should not march…". To an English ear that modal can sound like reproach for something past ("you ought not to have"). The Hebrew does not mean that, and a congregation hearing NIV84 should be told so. That is a note to the preacher, not a verdict on the translation.

#### Corrections to the claim
None to the count. One addition: v. 13 holds a second number anomaly (תִּשְׁלַחְנָה), usually emended.

#### Verdict
**Confirmed with nuance — moderate–high.** "Past referent" does not outrun the evidence. The perfects of v. 11, the "as you have done" of v. 15 and the five lexical links between v. 11 and the prohibitions all point back to one day already past. "The grammar does not allow 'you should not have'" holds as a statement about meaning: no case of אַל with a counterfactual sense turned up. But "the grammar does not allow" is stronger than a survey of 44 verses plus the known candidates can prove. "No attested case" is the safer wording. The three readings of the *force* remain open. Jer 20:14 and Job 3 give the "imaginative return" reading a grammatical precedent in the third person. Gen 45:5 and 2 Sam 19:20 do the same for "lasting force on a past act".

**What the claim missed.** The lexical links between v. 11 and vv. 12–14, which are the strongest evidence for a past referent; the anomaly in v. 13; and the NIV84 wording.

### B2. Obad 16: who drank — and Ezek 35:13 as a test of the plural argument

#### What I tested
(1) Every second-person form in Obadiah and in Ezek 35:1–15, sorted by number. (2) The Greek of both passages. (3) Other oracles against nations for a single plural break. (4) The cup-drinking texts, for the sense of שָׁתָה ("drink").

#### Results
**Obadiah (WLC tags).** There are 40 second-person forms. Of these, 37 are singular, including the pausal לָךְ forms in vv. 5 and 15. Three are plural:
- v. 1 קוּמוּ ("arise!"), addressed to the nations, not to Edom;
- v. 13 תִּשְׁלַחְנָה, the anomaly noted under B1;
- v. 16 שְׁתִיתֶם ("you [pl] drank").

So שְׁתִיתֶם is the only clear plural verb with Edom as its possible addressee. The claim holds, provided v. 13 is read with BHS.

**Ezek 35:1–15.** There are 40 second-person forms. Four are addressed to the prophet: שִׂים פָּנֶיךָ ("set your face") and הִנָּבֵא ("prophesy") in 35:2, and וְאָמַרְתָּ ("and say") in 35:3. That leaves 36 addressed to Mount Seir/Edom:
- 31 singular;
- 5 plural, in **two** places:
  - 35:9 וִידַעְתֶּם כִּי־אֲנִי יְהוָה ("and you [pl] will know that I am the LORD");
  - 35:13 וַתַּגְדִּילוּ עָלַי בְּפִיכֶם וְהַעְתַּרְתֶּם עָלַי דִּבְרֵיכֶם ("you [pl] magnified yourselves against me with your mouth and multiplied your words against me"), four plural forms.

The addressee in 35:13 is plainly Edom. It continues 35:12 אָמַרְתָּ ("you [sg] said") and the quoted "they are given to us to devour". So the claim's fact is right, but "an oracle otherwise singular" is not quite accurate. Ezek 35 breaks into the plural twice: once in the recognition formula and once in 35:13.

**The Greek levels both.** Swete and Rahlfs Ezek 35:13 read καὶ ἐμεγαλορημόνησας ἐπ᾽ ἐμὲ τῷ στόματί σου ("you [sg] spoke boastfully against me with your [sg] mouth"). The Greek is singular and shorter. Swete Ezek 35:9 has γνώσῃ ("you [sg] will know"). Swete and Rahlfs Obad 16 both have ἔπιες ("you [sg] drank"). The Greek translators smoothed away the plural in both books. The singular of the Greek Obadiah is therefore not independent evidence for an Edom reading. It is the same levelling seen in Ezekiel.

**Other single-addressee oracles with a plural break** (a WLC survey of the oracles against nations in Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel and the Twelve; chapters with eight or more singular second-person forms and only a few plurals, read for addressee):
- Ezek 25:3 שִׁמְעוּ ("hear [pl]") and 25:5 וִידַעְתֶּם ("you [pl] will know"), to Ammon, in an address otherwise to Ammon in the singular (25:3 אָמְרֵךְ "your [sg] saying", 25:6–7 singular).
- Nah 1:9 מַה־תְּחַשְּׁבוּן אֶל־יְהוָה ("what do you [pl] plot against the LORD?"). The addressee is disputed [S].
- The other hits (Isa 14:21; Ezek 32:20; Nah 2:9–10; Zech 9:12) are imperatives to a different party.

So the same addressee changing number once is attested, chiefly in Ezekiel.

**The sense of "drink".** כּוֹס ("cup") with שָׁתָה ("drink") in one verse occurs in 10 verses (WLC). Three of them bear directly on v. 16:
- Jer 49:12, in the Edom oracle itself: "those who were not sentenced to drink the cup will drink it, and you [sg, Edom]… will surely drink".
- Isa 51:17, 22: Jerusalem "has drunk the cup of his wrath", and the cup passes to her tormentors.
- Lam 4:21, to "daughter Edom": גַּם־עָלַיִךְ תַּעֲבָר־כּוֹס ("to you also the cup will pass").

The pattern "the cup passes from Judah to the nations, Edom included" belongs to this very tradition. It fits the reading that Judah drank on the holy mountain and the nations, Edom among them, will now drink. Against it is the plain continuity with v. 15. The כַּאֲשֶׁר ("as") clause of v. 15, כַּאֲשֶׁר עָשִׂיתָ ("as you [sg] have done"), is resumed by כִּי כַּאֲשֶׁר שְׁתִיתֶם ("for as you drank"), which suggests the same addressee. And Edom's "drinking" on Zion would then be its triumphal carousing.

#### Corrections to the claim
Ezek 35 has five plural forms in two verses (35:9, 35:13), not one isolated plural. Both are levelled to the singular in the Greek.

|---|---|---|---|

#### Verdict

The lean towards Judah must rest on the cup tradition (Isa 51; Lam 4:21; Jer 49:12), not on grammar. It is offset by the continuity of the כַּאֲשֶׁר clauses of vv. 15–16. **Keep the reading at low–moderate.** Do not raise it: the plural has been neutralised as evidence and the cup tradition only matches in theme.

### B4. The shared Edom poem is older than Jer 49:7–22 as compiled

#### (a) The named contacts
- **Jer 49:3 ~ Amos 1:15.** Jer: כִּי מַלְכָּם בַּגּוֹלָה יֵלֵךְ כֹּהֲנָיו וְשָׂרָיו יַחְדָּיו ("for their king [or Milcom] will go into exile, his priests and his princes together"). Amos: וְהָלַךְ מַלְכָּם בַּגּוֹלָה הוּא וְשָׂרָיו יַחְדָּו ("their king will go into exile, he and his princes together"). The clause is shared, with an added "his priests" and a different verb form. As a share of Jer 49:3's 20 words only 20 % is retained, because the rest of the verse is unrelated. As a share of Amos 1:15, 50 %. Confirmed as a shared clause.
- **Jer 49:27 ~ Amos 1:4 and 1:14.** Jer: וְהִצַּתִּי אֵשׁ בְּחוֹמַת דַּמָּשֶׂק וְאָכְלָה אַרְמְנוֹת בֶּן־הֲדָד ("I will kindle a fire in the wall of Damascus, and it shall devour the citadels of Ben-hadad"). The first half equals Amos 1:14 (וְהִצַּתִּי אֵשׁ בְּחוֹמַת רַבָּה, "I will kindle a fire in the wall of Rabbah"). The second half equals Amos 1:4 (וְאָכְלָה אַרְמְנוֹת בֶּן־הֲדָד, "and it shall devour the citadels of Ben-hadad"). 5 of 8 words retained against each. Confirmed: one Jeremiah line combines two Amos lines.
- **Jer 48 ~ Isa 15–16.** 14 of Jer 48's 47 verses keep four or more in-order words from Isa 15–16. The strongest are 48:5 (≈ Isa 15:5), 48:29–30 (≈ 16:6), 48:31–33 (≈ 16:7–10), 48:34 (≈ 15:4–6), 48:36 (≈ 16:11) and 48:37 (≈ 15:2–3). Isa 16:13 reads זֶה הַדָּבָר אֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר יְהוָה אֶל־מוֹאָב מֵאָז ("this is the word the LORD spoke to Moab long ago"). Confirmed.
- **Jer 48:43–44 ~ Isa 24:17–18.** פַּחַד וָפַחַת וָפָח עָלֶיךָ יוֹשֵׁב ("terror, pit and snare are upon you, inhabitant of…") is identical in both, with "Moab" in Jeremiah for "the earth" in Isaiah. In 48:44 the flight-and-fall sequence is identical in wording for most of the verse. Confirmed. Isa 24 is not an oracle against a nation, and Jeremiah has narrowed it to Moab.
- **Jer 48:45–46 ~ Num 21:28–29.** The fire from Heshbon and the flame from Sihon, and אוֹי־לְךָ מוֹאָב… עַם־כְּמוֹשׁ ("woe to you, Moab… people of Chemosh"). Confirmed.

#### (b) Are such contacts unusual? Chance baseline
**Definition.** A verse "shares a line" with another verse when the two have **five or more lemmas in the same order** after the most frequent lemmas are set aside (the roughly 70 words occurring more than about 1,000 times, such as "LORD", "say", "not", the prepositions and the article). I calibrated this on known pairs. It finds Isa 2:4 ~ Mic 4:3 (15), Jer 49:14 ~ Obad 1 (7), Jer 49:16 ~ Obad 3 (7), Jer 48:44 ~ Isa 24:18 (7) and Jer 49:27 ~ Amos 1:4 and 1:14 (5 each). It misses short or very common-word contacts: Jer 49:3 ~ Amos 1:15, Jer 49:9 ~ Obad 5 and Isa 13:6 ~ Joel 1:15 all score 3. So the counts below are floors.

**Results** (verses sharing a line with the Twelve, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel or Numbers, excluding the verse's own book; WLC):

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Ezek 25–32, 35 (oracles against the nations) | 212 | 5 | 2.4 % | **none**: stock idiom (אָדָם וּבְהֵמָה "man and beast", ≈ Jer 7:20; תְּהוֹם רַבָּה "the great deep", ≈ Amos 7:4) |

The same measure gives other comparisons:
- With the Twelve only: Jer 46–51 has 4 verses (1.7 %); Isa 13–23 has 0; Ezek 25–32, 35 has 3, all idiom.
- The non-oracle parts of the same books (with Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel and the Twelve as partners, Numbers not counted): Jer 1–45 1.0 %, Isa 1–12 and 24–66 0.9 %, Ezek outside the oracles 1.2 %. These were not sense-checked, so they are upper bounds on genuine sharing.

A looser threshold of four in-order lemmas gives the same ranking: Jeremiah's oracles 10.8 %, Isaiah's 6.9 %, Ezekiel's 11.8 % (Ezekiel inflated by date and formula lines), against 3–7 % for the non-oracle chapters.

**Plain-number null.** Prophetic oracles against the nations share lines with other books at about one to two verses in a hundred, and on reading Ezekiel's share none. Jeremiah's share genuine lines at about **five in a hundred**: roughly two to five times the other collections, and higher than Jeremiah's own other chapters. In that collection the borrowing gathers in **Jer 48–49**. So Jeremiah's oracles against the nations really do take in other prophets' material, beyond what chance or genre alone would predict.

#### (c) Does the inference follow?
**The habit is shown; the age of the Edom poem is inferred.** If Jeremiah's oracles against the nations take in older material (Isa 15–16 calls itself an "old" word; Num 21 is an old song), then an Edom block with no doublets anywhere in Jeremiah is most simply read as one more piece taken in. Three things limit this:
1. **The other direction is not uneconomical.** On an "Obadiah-later" account, a later writer choosing the four lines of Jer 49:7–22 that are *not* Jeremiah's stock is behaving sensibly. He takes what is particular to Edom and leaves what is general: the Babylon doublets of 50:40, 44–46, the curse-list, the woman in labour. Such selection would leave exactly the pattern B3 found. The pattern fits both accounts. It is better explained by absorption only because absorption is a demonstrated habit of Jer 46–51.
2. **Direction inside Jeremiah's doublets is itself open.** 49:19–21 ≈ 50:44–46 and 49:18 ≈ 50:40. Nothing measured here says whether the Edom oracle or the Babylon oracle had these lines first.
3. **Obad 7 ≈ Jer 38:22** ("the men of your peace") puts a Jeremianic phrase in Obadiah outside the shared block. This fits Obadiah knowing Jeremiah's wording, or both drawing on common idiom. It slightly weakens a clean "Obadiah is the source" picture.

#### Verdict
**(a) and (b) confirmed; (c) needs reframing — moderate for "Jeremiah's Edom oracle takes in an Edom poem from outside Jeremiah", low–moderate for "that poem is older than Obadiah, or is Obadiah".** Jeremiah's oracles absorb other prophets at a rate that beats the null, so the Edom block is likely to have come from outside Jeremiah. That makes it older than Jer 49:7–22 *as compiled*, which is all the claim states. But the inference says nothing about whether the external poem was Obadiah, a source behind both, or a text that Obadiah later reshaped. Leaving that open, as the claim does, is correct.

### B7. Joel 3:5 and Obad 17: Joel is the later text

#### What I tested
(1) The formula כַּאֲשֶׁר אָמַר יְהוָה ("as the LORD has said") and its relatives, and what they refer back to. (2) Whether Joel itself supplies an earlier word to which the formula could refer. (3) Rival earlier words with פְּלֵיטָה ("escape") on Zion. (4) The other Joel–Obadiah contacts. (5) Whether "longer is later" holds. (6) Greek and NT.

#### Results
**The formula.** As an ordered phrase, כַּאֲשֶׁר אָמַר יְהוָה occurs **only at Joel 3:5** (plain and skeletal). The wider phrase כַּאֲשֶׁר אָמַר ("as he said") occurs at Gen 21:1, Gen 41:54, Gen 43:17, Exod 17:10, Num 23:30, Josh 11:9, 2 Kgs 8:19 and Joel 3:5. Every case outside Joel points back to a word spoken earlier in the story:
- Gen 21:1 → the promise of Gen 17–18;
- Gen 41:54 → Joseph in 41:30;
- Gen 43:17 → 43:16;
- Exod 17:10 → 17:9;
- Num 23:30 → 23:29;
- Josh 11:9 → 11:6;
- 2 Kgs 8:19 → the promise to David.

The commoner כַּאֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר יְהוָה ("as the LORD spoke", 28 verses) behaves the same way. So the formula does mark a *prior* word.

**Nothing in Joel supplies it.** The only other פְּלֵיטָה in Joel is **Joel 2:3** וְגַם־פְּלֵיטָה לֹא־הָיְתָה לּוֹ ("and there was no escape from it"), its opposite. No earlier word in Joel promises escape on Zion. The referent lies outside Joel.

**Rival earlier words.** פְּלֵיטָה with צִיּוֹן ("Zion") in one verse: **2 Kgs 19:31, Isa 37:32, Joel 3:5, Obad 17 only**. Of these, the Isaiah–Kings line has *both* Jerusalem and Mount Zion, as Joel does: כִּי מִירוּשָׁלַםִ תֵּצֵא שְׁאֵרִית וּפְלֵיטָה מֵהַר צִיּוֹן ("for out of Jerusalem a remnant shall go forth, and escape from Mount Zion"). Obadiah has Mount Zion only. As an ordered phrase, תִּהְיֶה פְלֵיטָה ("there will be escape") is found only in Joel 3:5 and Obad 17. So Joel's line has two possible antecedents:
- Obadiah, for "on Mount Zion there will be escape";
- Isaiah 37 / 2 Kings 19, an explicit word of the LORD through Isaiah, for "Zion and Jerusalem".

Joel's "and in Jerusalem" therefore need not be an expansion of Obadiah. It can be Joel combining the two.

**The other Joel–Obadiah contacts** (ordered phrases, WLC):
- **יַדּוּ גוֹרָל** ("they cast lots"): **Joel 4:3, Obad 11, Nah 3:10 only**.
- **גְּמֻל + רֹאשׁ** ("recompense on the head"): Obad 15 גְּמֻלְךָ יָשׁוּב בְּרֹאשֶׁךָ ("your dealings will return on your head"); Joel 4:4, 7 גְּמֻלְכֶם בְּרֹאשְׁכֶם ("your recompense on your heads").
- **מֵחֲמַס** ("because of violence") with Edom: Obad 10 מֵחֲמַס אָחִיךָ יַעֲקֹב ("because of the violence to your brother Jacob"); Joel 4:19 וֶאֱדוֹם… מֵחֲמַס בְּנֵי יְהוּדָה ("and Edom… because of the violence to the sons of Judah"). The phrase occurs in 4 or 5 verses in all (2 Sam 22:3; Ezek 12:19; Joel 4:19; Obad 10; Ps 72:14 by skeleton).
- **קֹדֶשׁ and זָרִים**: Joel 4:17 וְהָיְתָה יְרוּשָׁלַםִ קֹדֶשׁ וְזָרִים לֹא־יַעַבְרוּ־בָהּ עוֹד ("Jerusalem shall be holy, and strangers shall not pass through her again"). This brings together Obad 17 וְהָיָה קֹדֶשׁ ("and it shall be holy") and Obad 11 זָרִים ("strangers"), and reverses the latter.
- **כִּי קָרוֹב יוֹם יְהוָה** ("for the day of the LORD is near"): Isa 13:6, Joel 1:15, Joel 4:14, Obad 15, Zeph 1:7. A shared prophetic phrase.
- **כִּי יְהוָה דִּבֵּר** ("for the LORD has spoken"): 9 to 11 verses. Idiom; no weight.

Joel elsewhere reuses other prophets' lines verbatim:
- Joel 4:16 = Amos 1:2 מִצִּיּוֹן יִשְׁאַג וּמִירוּשָׁלַםִ יִתֵּן קוֹלוֹ ("he roars from Zion and utters his voice from Jerusalem"), ordered phrase, these two only;
- Joel 1:15 ≈ Isa 13:6 (by skeleton, these two only);
- Joel 4:10 reverses the swords-to-ploughshares line of Isa 2:4 (and Mic 4:3).

So Joel is a demonstrated reuser of prophetic lines. That raises the prior that it is the later party with Obadiah too.

**"Longer is later" does not hold as a rule.** Joel 3:5 has 20 words to Obad 17's 11, but most of Joel's extra is a different clause. In known synoptic pairs length runs both ways. 2 Kgs 18:13–20:19 has 1,380 words against Isa 36–39's 1,237 (leaving out Hezekiah's psalm), yet Kings is widely, though not universally, taken as the source [S]. 2 Sam 22 and Ps 18 are almost equal (366 and 373). *Lectio brevior* is a rule for scribal copying, not for literary reuse.

**Anything pointing the other way.** The day of the LORD runs through Joel (1:15; 2:1; 2:11; 3:4; 4:14). In Obadiah it occurs once (v. 15). One could argue that Obadiah took up a theme that is structural in Joel. The phrase is shared with Isa 13:6 and Zeph 1:7, though, so it belongs to neither book. Obadiah's lines are shorter, and on a "condensing" account Obadiah could be the later one. Nothing in the text excludes this.

**Greek and NT.** Swete Joel 2:32 reads καθότι εἶπεν κύριος ("just as the Lord said") and καὶ εὐαγγελιζόμενοι οὓς Κύριος προσκέκληται ("and those bringing good news, whom the Lord has called"). The Greek reads the Hebrew "and among the survivors" (וּבַשְּׂרִידִים) as "those who bring good news" (מְבַשְּׂרִים). Joel's opening clause is quoted at Acts 2:21 and Rom 10:13, and its closing words are echoed at Acts 2:39 (ὅσους ἂν προσκαλέσηται κύριος, "as many as the Lord will call"). The NT never touches the "as the LORD has said" clause.

#### Hays table (Joel 3:5 ← an earlier word)
| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Possible | Depends on dating, which the texts alone do not settle |
| Volume | Strong | תִּהְיֶה פְלֵיטָה ("there will be escape") only in Joel 3:5 and Obad 17; the marker formula unique |
| Recurrence | Strong | Joel 4:3, 4, 7, 17, 19 repeat Obadiah's wording (lots, recompense on the head, violence with Edom, holiness and strangers) |

#### Verdict
**Confirmed with nuance — moderate for "Joel is the later text", with the longer-is-later argument dropped.** The formula does refer back, and Joel has no earlier word it could refer to. Joel also demonstrably reuses prophetic lines, and its chapter 4 gathers Obadiah's wording in five or six places, including a three-verse phrase (יַדּוּ גוֹרָל). Those two facts, not length, carry the lean. Add the rival antecedent Isa 37:32 / 2 Kgs 19:31, which matches Joel's "Zion *and* Jerusalem" better than Obadiah does. That makes it likely Joel is drawing on more than one earlier word.

### New candidates
