# Claim Audit: Obadiah

**Passages audited:** Obadiah 1–21, as carried by `book-overview-obadiah-2026-11-04` (v1.1) and `dig-deeper-obadiah-1-1to21-2026-11-04` (v1.0)
**Date:** 4 November 2026
**Purpose:** To test the twenty queued allusion and design claims before any sermon backbone
**Method:** Two blind auditors in turn, working only from the primary texts, then a reproduction of decisive results in the main session. Auditor A took the single contacts and Auditor B the field and design claims
**Editions:** WLC by lemma (Hebrew versification), Swete, Rahlfs, SBLGNT, BHS with apparatus

---

## Verdict in Brief

**The sturdy results stand. Most of what the fresh dig added does not.** Jeremiah 49, Joel, Micah 7 and the Balaam cluster hold up, along with the verbal link to Jer 38:22. Of the claims the November dig newly proposed, only Mic 7:8–10 kept its rating. Gen 27:41, Deut 32:35, Prov 27:10, Hab 2, the land left undone and Isa 63 all came down, and Prov 27:10 is discarded.

**The main cause is one method fault, and it is the proposing run's own.** Those claims rested on "these words stand in one verse only", with the words drawn from scattered verses of Obadiah. Auditor A's baseline shows how little that means: **14.2 % of the verses of the Hebrew Bible hold a three-word set from Obadiah's vocabulary found in no other verse** (30 control passages average 8.7 %). A scattered "only" is a candidate, not evidence. What survives has shape: an ordered phrase, a shared construction, or a tight cluster in the source.

**Corrected outright:** Rom 12:19 quotes the first colon of Deut 32:35, not the "near day" line, so it does not connect to Obad 15 by allusion. It remains good theology against revenge on its own terms. Also: the odds stated for the three-block structure were badly overstated, and "the only plural you in the book" is inexact.

**New, and worth keeping** (from the auditors, unprompted):

## Critical Gaps

- **Baselines are lexical.** They do not see syntax or genre, and the rival search is lexical too.
- **One translator of the Twelve** may account for some Greek agreement within the Twelve. This limits its independent weight (Hab 2:17, Joel).
- **Apparatus findings remain unchecked** for manuscript spread: vv. 7, 11, 16, 17, 20, 21.
- **A single audit by the same model family.** The auditors were blind to the reports, not to their shared habits.

## Report of Auditor A: Single Contacts

Hebrew is the WLC in Hebrew versification unless stated. Greek is Swete unless marked Rahlfs; Swete's own numbers differ in places (Swete Joel 3 = Hebrew Joel 4; Swete Joel 2:32 = Hebrew Joel 3:5; Swete Jer 45:22 = Hebrew Jer 38:22; Greek Ps 136 = Hebrew Ps 137). Anything from memory is tagged [S] and carries no verdict. Throughout, "shares wording with" means only that; no direction is assumed.

### Two baselines that govern every "only"

Positive controls passed first: the search for עֵד ("witness"), קוּם ("rise") and עָנָה ("testify") returned Deut 19:16 and Job 16:8, and my own scripts matched the standard search on known words before I trusted them.

**Baseline 1: exclusive sets of words.** I took Obadiah's 141 content words (particles, pronouns and the divine name removed) and asked how many verses in the Hebrew Bible hold a set drawn from them that occurs in no other verse outside Obadiah, ignoring where the words stand in Obadiah.

| Set | Verses with such a set | Share of 23,213 | 30 random 21-verse prophetic or psalm passages: mean (range) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two words | 764 | 3.3% | 567 (115–1,105) |
| Three words | 3,295 | 14.2% | 2,031 (522–4,150) |
| Four words | 3,566 | 15.4% | 1,969 (411–4,248) |

So one verse in seven can claim "these three words, only here and in Obadiah". Spread across Obadiah, an "only" triple or quadruple is nearly worthless. It shows nothing about borrowing unless shape or sense adds to it. Gen 27:41, Ps 7:17, Prov 27:10 and Ps 41:10 all fall inside this large class.

**Baseline 2: exclusive two-word phrases.** Of Obadiah's 260 distinct ordered two-word sequences, 33 (12.7%) stand in exactly one other verse. Forty random 21-verse passages average 24.7 (range 6–44). Fifteen of the 33 are Jer 49, which is expected. Others are plainly accidental: וְהָיָה קֹדֶשׁ ("and it will be holy") with Exod 40:9; לְהַכְרִית אֶת ("to cut off") with 2 Chr 22:7. A two-word "only" is therefore weak by itself.

**A caution on rank.** Weighting shared vocabulary by rarity, Jer 49:16 ranks first of 23,192 verses. Third is 1 Sam 17:28, where Eliab rebukes David: אָח ("brother"), זָדוֹן ("arrogance"), לֵבָב ("heart"), יָרַד ("go down"). Nobody would call that a source. Vocabulary density measures vocabulary.

Chapter ranks by the same measure, of 922 chapters of five or more verses: Jer 49, 2nd; Joel 4, 7th; Lam 2, 38th; Gen 27, 63rd; Ezek 35, 80th; Jer 38, 153rd; Joel 3, 216th (2nd on the rare-contact scan, behind only Jer 49; Joel 4 is 14th there); Mic 7, 357th; Hab 2, 388th; Ps 137, 416th; Lam 4, 534th; Deut 32, 555th; Prov 27, 624th; Ps 41, 637th; Prov 24, 658th; Ps 7, 684th; Ps 22, 779th. Joel 4 and Jer 49 alone stand out. Josh 17 and Judg 20 also outrank Joel 4, so even that rank is suggestive only.


### A1. Joel 3:5 at Obad 17

**Tested.** פְּלֵיטָה ("those who escape") with צִיּוֹן ("Zion") and הַר ("mount"); ordered phrases; פְּלֵיטָה with שָׂרִיד ("survivor"); rivals for the join of vv. 17–18.

**Results.** Stated counts hold exactly: 2 Kgs 19:31, Isa 37:32, Joel 3:5, Obad 17. פְּלֵיטָה with צִיּוֹן alone gives the same four. פְּלֵיטָה with שָׂרִיד: Joel 3:5 only. The claim understates the shape. The ordered phrase תִּהְיֶה פְלֵיטָה ("there will be those who escape") stands in Joel 3:5 and Obad 17 only. Both have בְּהַר־צִיּוֹן ("on Mount Zion") before it, Joel inserting וּבִירוּשָׁלִַם ("and in Jerusalem"). Kings and Isaiah differ in shape: וּפְלֵיטָה מֵהַר צִיּוֹן ("and those who escape from Mount Zion"), with a different verb. כַּאֲשֶׁר אָמַר יְהוָה ("as the LORD has said") is unique to Joel 3:5 as a phrase. Greek: Swete Joel 2:32 ἐν τῷ ὄρει Σειὼν καὶ ἐν Ἰερουσαλὴμ ἔσται ἀνασῳζόμενος ("on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem there will be one who escapes") against Obad 17 ἐν δὲ τῷ ὄρει Σειὼν ἔσται σωτηρία ("on Mount Zion there will be salvation"; Rahlfs ἡ σωτηρία): the frame survives, the noun does not.

**Rival not named.** Isa 10:17–20. Isa 10:20 is the only other verse with פְּלֵיטָה and יַעֲקֹב ("Jacob"): פְּלֵיטַת בֵּית־יַעֲקֹב ("the escaped of the house of Jacob"). Isa 10:17 reads וְהָיָה אוֹר־יִשְׂרָאֵל לְאֵשׁ וּקְדוֹשׁוֹ לְלֶהָבָה ("the light of Israel will become a fire and his Holy One a flame"), with אָכַל ("consume"). With "flame", "escaped" and "Jacob" within three verses, only Isa 10:17–20 and Obad 17–18 qualify. Isaiah is a real competitor for the idea but not for the wording of v. 17.

**Direction.** Joel's formula marks a prior word without naming it. Obadiah's simpler line, with Joel expanded by "and in Jerusalem" and the formula, leans towards Joel as secondary, but Isa 37:32 could equally be the "word", and a shared Zion tradition cannot be excluded.

**Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. Moderate–high.** Joel 3:5 is the closest wording and the phrase is exclusive. It survives Baseline 2 because it is reinforced by the Zion frame, by שָׂרִיד ("survivor") at Obad 18, and by Joel 4. Only "shares wording" is established. Joel's formula is evidence of a citation in Joel, not of Obadiah as its source.


### A2. Joel 4:3, 4, 7, 14, 17, 19

**Tested.** All stated sets; frames; Greek; leave-one-out on Joel 4's rank.

**Results.** Every stated count holds. יַדּוּ גוֹרָל ("cast lots"): Joel 4:3, Obad 11, Nah 3:10. גְּמוּל ("recompense") with רֹאשׁ ("head") and שׁוּב ("return"): Joel 4:4, 7 and Obad 15. חָמָס ("violence") with אֱדוֹם ("Edom"): Joel 4:19 only. זָר ("stranger") with יְרוּשָׁלִַם ("Jerusalem"): Joel 4:17, Obad 11.

**How each one fares.**
- *Lots.* Not private to Joel. The closest frame is Nahum's: וְעַל־נִכְבַּדֶּיהָ יַדּוּ גוֹרָל ("and for her nobles they cast lots") matches וְעַל־יְרוּשָׁלִַם יַדּוּ גוֹרָל ("and for Jerusalem they cast lots") in waw, preposition and order, while Joel has וְאֶל־עַמִּי ("and to my people"). Nah 3:10 also has גַּם ("also") nearby, as Obad 11 does. In Greek, however, ἔβαλον κλήρους ("they cast lots") joins Joel and Obadiah, with Nahum future βαλοῦσιν (Rahlfs) and also Jon 1:7 and 1 Chr 26.
- *Recompense on the head.* The strongest. Joel's verb is causative with God as subject, Obadiah's intransitive, but "recompense" with "head" occurs nowhere else. Greek confirms it: ἀνταπόδομα ("recompense") with κεφαλή ("head") stands in Swete Joel 3:4, 7, Obad 15 and Sir 17:23 only.
- *Violence and Edom.* Obadiah has Edom at vv. 1 and 8 and violence at v. 10, so the "pair" is not a same-verse pair there. The better contact is unstated: Joel 4:19 מֵחֲמַס בְּנֵי יְהוּדָה ("for the violence against the sons of Judah") against Obad 10 מֵחֲמַס אָחִיךָ ("because of violence to your brother") and Obad 12 לִבְנֵי־יְהוּדָה ("over the sons of Judah"). Both have the same preposition and the same objective genitive. חָמָס with יְהוּדָה ("Judah") occurs only at Ezek 8:17 and Joel 4:19. The Greek does not preserve it: ἐξ ἀδικιῶν ("from injustices") against διὰ… ἀσέβειαν ("for impiety").
- *Strangers and Jerusalem.* Weak. Exclusive pair, but Joel negates (strangers shall no longer pass through) and Obadiah narrates. "Strangers came" has a nearer sentence at Jer 51:51, בָּאוּ זָרִים ("strangers came") against the sanctuary.
- *Near day (4:14).* כִּי קָרוֹב יוֹם יְהוָה ("for the day of the LORD is near") stands verbatim in Isa 13:6, Joel 1:15, 4:14, Obad 15 and Zeph 1:7. It is a shared formula and adds nothing.

**Missed by the claim.** לִשְׁפֹּט אֶת ("to judge") with nations/Edom as object: Joel 4:12 and Obad 21 (plus Exod 18:13, 1 Kgs 3:9). הַר קָדְשִׁי ("my holy mountain"): Joel 4:17 and Obad 16 among nine verses. קֹדֶשׁ ("holy") as predicate of the place: Joel 4:17 וְהָיְתָה יְרוּשָׁלִַם קֹדֶשׁ ("and Jerusalem will be holy") against Obad 17 וְהָיָה קֹדֶשׁ ("and it will be holy"). כִּי יְהוָה דִּבֵּר ("for the LORD has spoken"): Joel 4:8 and Obad 18 among nine verses plain, so idiom but near the limit. The shape of the clause is the same: it closes a dispossession or destruction promise.

**Baseline.** Joel 4 is 7th of 922 chapters on rarity-weighted overlap. Removing any one of seventeen shared words leaves it between 6th and 12th, so the rank does not hang on one word. Joel 3 ranks 2nd on the rare-contact scan. This is the only claimed partner other than Jer 49 that beats the null at chapter level.

### A3. Ps 22:29 at Obad 21

**Tested.** Ordered phrase; the noun's whole range; frame; Greek; syntax.

**Results.** The ordered phrase לַיהוָה הַמְּלוּכָה ("to the LORD the kingdom") stands in Obad 21 and Ps 22:29 only, plain and skeletal: count holds. מְלוּכָה ("kingship") is in 23 verses.

**Breaking it.**
1. Baseline 2 applies: an exclusive two-word phrase is one of 33 in Obadiah.
2. The frame is a formula. לַיהוָה ("to the LORD") plus a definite noun recurs: הַיְשׁוּעָה ("the salvation") at Ps 3:9, הַתְּשׁוּעָה ("the victory") at Prov 21:31, הָאָרֶץ ("the earth") at Exod 9:29 and Ps 24:1.
3. Syntax differs. Psalm is verbless; Obad 21 is וְהָיְתָה ("and it will be"). The nearest syntax for "kingship will be X's" is 1 Kgs 2:15, לִי הָיְתָה הַמְּלוּכָה ("the kingship was mine").
4. Greek diverges in case: Ps 21:29 τοῦ κυρίου ἡ βασιλεία ("the kingdom is the Lord's"), Obad 21 τῷ κυρίῳ ἡ βασιλεία ("the kingdom [shall be] to the Lord"). Same in Rahlfs for the Psalm.
5. Ps 22 ranks 779th of 922 on overlap; there is no surrounding support.

**In its favour.** מְלוּכָה ("kingship") with גּוֹי ("nation") in one verse is Ps 22:29 only, and "nations" is a key word of Obad 15–16; both are climactic universal statements. Of seven verses joining "kingship" and the LORD, only these two predicate kingship of him. A thematic rival worth noting: Isa 34:12, וְאֵין־שָׁם מְלוּכָה ("and no kingship there"), an oracle about Edom, is the negative of Obadiah's close. Rev 11:15 is about the same theme but does not quote either.

### A4. Ps 41:10 and Jer 38:22 at Obad 7

**Tested.** Both sets; ordered phrases; Greek; apparatus; rivals. The lemma tagging places אַנְשֵׁי ("men of") under אִישׁ ("man") and keeps אֱנוֹשׁ ("man") separate, so I searched both.

**Results.** אִישׁ, שָׁלוֹם ("peace") and לֶחֶם ("bread"): Obad 7 and Ps 41:10 only. Holds. אִישׁ, שָׁלוֹם and יָכֹל ("prevail"): Obad 7 and Jer 38:22 only. Holds. אַנְשֵׁי שְׁלֹמֶךָ ("men at peace with you"): those two only, plain and skeletal, in the same defective spelling.

**Jer 38:22 is much tighter than claimed.** The frame is the same across five slots:
- Jer 38:22: הִסִּיתוּךָ וְיָכְלוּ לְךָ אַנְשֵׁי שְׁלֹמֶךָ ("they have misled you and prevailed over you, the men at peace with you")
- Obad 7: הִשִּׁיאוּךָ יָכְלוּ לְךָ אַנְשֵׁי שְׁלֹמֶךָ ("they have deceived you, they have prevailed over you, the men at peace with you")

Three consecutive words differ only by a waw; a deception verb with second-person object comes immediately before. Greek agrees, and this is decisive against the Psalm: ἄνδρες εἰρηνικοί ("men of peace") stands in Obad 7 and Swete Jer 45:22 only (Rahlfs the same at Jer), with δύναμαι ("be able") in both: Ἠπάτησάν σε καὶ δυνήσονταί σοι ἄνδρες εἰρηνικοί σου ("they deceived you and will prevail over you, your men of peace") against ἠδυνάσθησαν πρὸς σὲ ἄνδρες εἰρηνικοί σου ("your men of peace prevailed against you"). Jer 38:22 ranks 87th of 23,192 verses on overlap with Obadiah. יָכֹל ("prevail") with לְ and second-person object also occurs at Jer 1:19 and 15:20, so the idiom is Jeremianic in distribution. One rival nearby: Jer 20:10 כֹּל אֱנוֹשׁ שְׁלוֹמִי ("every man of my peace") with נוּכְלָה לוֹ ("we shall prevail over him"), a third voice in the same idiom.

**Direction.** Jer 38:22 is a saying sung by women within a narrative; that favours a song or proverb already in circulation [S], which Obadiah could share rather than take from Jeremiah. The other direction is possible.

**Ps 41:10 is weaker than claimed.** The shared bread word rests on the MT alone. לַחְמְךָ ("your bread") has no equivalent in Swete or Rahlfs Obad 7, and the BHS apparatus proposes לֹחֲמֶיךָ ("those fighting you"), citing versional evidence, with ἐπολέμησάν σε ("they fought you") in one Greek witness, and suspects a dropped verb. Ps 40:10 Greek ὁ ἄνθρωπος τῆς εἰρήνης μου… ὁ ἐσθίων ἄρτους μου ("the man of my peace… who eats my bread") shares no phrase with Greek Obad 7 at all. English "They who eat your bread" (NASB95) supplies the participle that links to the Psalm; the Hebrew has no אֹכֵל ("eating"). The Psalm remains relevant as a parallel motif: the MT's bread line makes sense read that way. Two further rivals for broken alliance: Ps 55:21 שָׁלַח יָדָיו בִּשְׁלֹמָיו חִלֵּל בְּרִיתוֹ ("he put out his hands against those at peace with him, he violated his covenant"), which joins שָׁלוֹם ("peace") and בְּרִית ("covenant") as Obad 7 does; and Jer 9:7, peace in the mouth and an ambush inside.

### A5. Gen 27:41 at Obad 3, 10, 15

**Tested.** All three hooks separately, the triple, Greek, and rivals.

**Results.** אָמַר ("say"), לֵב ("heart") and עֵשָׂו ("Esau") in one verse: Gen 27:41 only; count holds. But Obad 3 has no עֵשָׂו ("Esau"), and the triple falls in the 14% class of Baseline 1.

**Hook 1, "said in his heart".** Idiom. "Say" with בְּ ("in") plus "heart" within three words occurs in at least 30 verses, including Deut 7:17, 8:17, 9:4, Isa 14:13, 47:8, 47:10, Jer 5:24, Zeph 1:12, 2:15, Ps 10:6, 11, 13, 14:1. Obadiah's form is a participle in a taunt on a proud place; its true relatives are הָאֹמְרָה בִּלְבָבָהּ ("who says in her heart") at Isa 47:8 and Zeph 2:15, and above all Isa 14:13. There אָמַרְתָּ בִלְבָבְךָ ("you said in your heart") joins כּוֹכָב ("star") in the same verse, and Isa 14:15 has תּוּרָד ("you will be brought down"). "Say in heart" with "star" occurs in one verse only: Isa 14:13. Obad 3–4 has all three elements within two verses. Isa 14 outcompetes Gen 27:41 on this hook. The clause is also absent from Jer 49:16, the parallel: it is a distinctive plus in Obadiah and deserves its own note.

**Hook 2, "your brother Jacob".** אָחִיךָ יַעֲקֹב ("your brother Jacob") is unique to Obad 10. יַעֲקֹב אָחִי ("Jacob my brother") is meaningful only at Gen 27:41 (the other matches, Gen 29:4 and 36:6, are false positives). But אָח ("brother") with יַעֲקֹב ("Jacob") occurs in 25 verses, 23 of them in Genesis. The nearest frame is not Gen 27:41 but Num 20:14, spoken to Edom's king: אָחִיךָ יִשְׂרָאֵל ("your brother Israel"). Also Deut 23:8 (the Edomite is אָחִיךָ, "your brother"), Amos 1:11 and Mal 1:2. The "brother" framing belongs to the Edom tradition as a whole.

**Hook 3, near days.** Cannot bear it. יִקְרְבוּ ("let them draw near") is a verb, plural subject, days of mourning for Isaac, hoped for by Esau. קָרוֹב יוֹם ("the day is near") is an adjective, singular, the day of the LORD, and it is a formula found verbatim at Isa 13:6, Joel 1:15, 4:14, Zeph 1:7. Verb with "days" is itself idiom: Gen 47:29, Deut 31:14, 1 Kgs 2:1, Ezek 12:23. Obad 15 needs no Genesis source.

**Greek.** The translator breaks hook 1: Gen 27:41 ἐν τῇ διανοίᾳ αὐτοῦ ("in his mind") against Obad 3 ἐν καρδίᾳ αὐτοῦ ("in his heart"). Greek Obad 3's nearest wording is the Psalter's εἶπεν γὰρ ἐν καρδίᾳ αὐτοῦ ("for he said in his heart", Ps 9:27, 34; also 13:1, 52:2). Hook 3 differs too: ἐγγισάτωσαν αἱ ἡμέραι ("let the days approach") against ἐγγὺς ἡμέρα ("the day is near").

All three hooks have closer or equal parallels elsewhere. A reader may well hear Esau's murderous resolve behind Obad 10, but the wording does not require this verse.


### A6. Deut 32:35 at Obad 13, 15

**Tested.** All sets, phrases, the Greek in both editions, the NT.

**Results.** קָרוֹב ("near"), יוֹם ("day") and אֵיד ("calamity") in one verse: Deut 32:35, Prov 27:10. In sense: Deut 32:35 only. Holds. Within a window of two verses: Deut 32:35, Obad 13–15, Prov 27:10. So "near day of calamity" as a composite is rare.

**Breaking it.** The composite rests on two independent items with their own wide distribution.
1. *Day of calamity.* יוֹם ("day") with אֵיד ("calamity") is in 8 verses. Obad 13's exact form בְּיוֹם אֵידָם ("in the day of their calamity") is shared with one verse only, and it is **Jer 18:17**, not Deuteronomy, which has כִּי קָרוֹב יוֹם אֵידָם without the preposition. Jer 46:21 has כִּי יוֹם אֵידָם בָּא עֲלֵיהֶם ("for the day of their calamity has come upon them"). Jer 49:8, in the Edom oracle itself, has אֵיד עֵשָׂו ("the calamity of Esau"). Ezek 35:5, also to Edom, has בְּעֵת אֵידָם ("in the time of their calamity"). Jer 48:16 has קָרוֹב אֵיד־מוֹאָב ("the calamity of Moab is near"), which is "near" with "calamity" in an oracle against a nation, and is closer in setting than Deuteronomy.
2. *Near day.* קָרוֹב ("near") with יוֹם ("day") occurs in 16 verses, 11 in the relevant sense; five verbatim with Obad 15. Over the ten-verse limit: idiom.

**Greek.** Rahlfs Deut 32:35 ὅτι ἐγγὺς ἡμέρα ἀπωλείας αὐτῶν ("for the day of their destruction is near") is as stated. Swete prints ἀπωλίας αὐτοῖς ("destruction for them"), with αὐτῶν ("their") in Odes 2:35; my first search missed these for the spelling, and a control caught it. But:
- ἡμέρα ἀπωλείας αὐτῶν ("day of their destruction") is also Jer 18:17, both editions, so Deuteronomy has no monopoly.
- At Obad 12 it renders בְּיוֹם אָבְדָם ("in the day of their perishing"), a different Hebrew word from אֵיד ("calamity"). The translator renders אֵיד ("calamity") in v. 13 with πόνων ("toils"), ὀλέθρου ("ruin") and ἀπωλείας ("destruction"), so no phrase is being carried.
- ἐγγὺς ἡμέρα ("the day is near") is in 9 Swete verses, including Jer 31:16 ἐγγὺς ἡμέρα Μωάβ ("the day of Moab is near").
- The recompense word has a better home. The Hebrew of Deut 32:35 has נָקָם ("vengeance") and שִׁלֵּם ("repayment"), neither in Obadiah; גְּמוּל ("recompense") belongs to Joel. In Greek, ἀνταποδώσω ("I will repay") at Deut 32:35 is outdone by Swete Joel 3:4, 7 ἀνταποδώσω τὸ ἀνταπόδομα ὑμῶν εἰς κεφαλὰς ὑμῶν ("I will repay your recompense on your heads"), and τὸ ἀνταπόδομά σου ("your recompense") stands only in Obad 15 and Greek Ps 136:8.

**Rom 12:19.** The claim misdescribes it. Paul's Ἐμοὶ ἐκδίκησις, ἐγὼ ἀνταποδώσω ("vengeance is mine, I will repay") takes the verse's *first* colon, not the "near day" line. Heb 10:30 has the same wording. It is closer to the Hebrew לִי נָקָם וְשִׁלֵּם ("vengeance and repayment are mine") than to the Greek ἐν ἡμέρᾳ ἐκδικήσεως ἀνταποδώσω ("in the day of vengeance I will repay"). Nothing in the NT quotes the near-day line; the nearest thing, Rom 13:12 ἡ ἡμέρα ἤγγικεν ("the day has drawn near"), is not marked as Deuteronomy. That Paul's quotation bears on how Obad 15 is used has no textual basis.

**Verdict: Needs reframing. Low–moderate,** not moderate to high. Deut 32:35 is a real parallel and a plausible part of the background. But Obadiah's two phrases are explained by the common day-of-calamity idiom (with Jer 18:17 the exact match) and the common near-day formula. **The Romans point should be discarded.**


### A7. Prov 27:10 and the wisdom register, Obad 10–14

**Tested.** The set; the sense; the phrase אַל־תָּבוֹא ("do not enter"); rivals for "aloof" and for gloating; distribution of אֵיד ("calamity"); prohibition chains.

**Results.** אָח ("brother"), בּוֹא ("enter") and אֵיד ("calamity"): Prov 27:10 only. Stronger: אָח with אֵיד alone is Prov 27:10 only. But it is one of the 3,295-class triples, and its high rank (326th) reflects common words.

**Breaking it.**
- *Sense.* Proverbs: do not go to your brother's house in the day of *your* calamity, because a near neighbour is better. Obadiah: do not enter *my people's* gate in the day of *their* calamity. The word "brother" stands in a different verse. The two passages have opposite subjects and opposite motives. No reader needs Proverbs to read Obad 13.
- *אַל־תָּבוֹא ("do not enter").* Plain: Jer 16:5, Obad 13, Prov 27:10. It is an ordinary prohibition.
- *מִנֶּגֶד ("aloof") against מֵאָח רָחוֹק ("than a brother far away").* No shared word. "Aloof" is the English bridge. מִנֶּגֶד ("aloof") with עָמַד ("stand") has real relatives: Ps 38:12 מִנֶּגֶד נִגְעִי יַעֲמֹדוּ ("they stand aloof from my plague") with מֵרָחֹק עָמָדוּ ("they stand far off"), 2 Kgs 2:7, and 2 Sam 18:13 וְאַתָּה תִּתְיַצֵּב מִנֶּגֶד ("and you would have stood aloof"), a second-person accusation, like Obad 11's. Ps 38:12 is much closer in sense.

**Wisdom register: real evidence, overstated claim.**
- For: אֵיד ("calamity") occurs in 22 verses, 12 of them in Job and Proverbs (55%), though those books hold 8.5% of the verses. Prov 24:17 בִּנְפֹל אוֹיִבְךָ אַל־תִּשְׂמָח ("when your enemy falls, do not rejoice") does share a prohibition, and Greek μὴ ἐπιχαρῇς ("do not rejoice over") stands in the second person in Swete only at Prov 24:17 and Obad 12. A closer rival than the claim names is Prov 17:5 שָׂמֵחַ לְאֵיד לֹא יִנָּקֶה ("one who rejoices at calamity will not go unpunished"): the only verse joining rejoicing and אֵיד ("calamity"), found through the adjective rather than the verb (a verb search returns nothing). Obad 8 also names חֲכָמִים ("wise men") and תְּבוּנָה ("understanding").
- Against: אַל־תִּשְׂמַח ("do not rejoice") occurs in five verses, and only one is wisdom: Isa 14:29, Hos 9:1, Mic 7:8, Obad 12, Prov 24:17. The other ten uses of אֵיד outside wisdom are concentrated in Jeremiah's oracles (46:21, 48:16, 49:8, 49:32). Prohibitions with אַל ("do not") and a verb number 676 in my floor count: Psalms 109, Jeremiah 85, Proverbs 84. Eight in three verses at Obad 12–14 is the densest chain in the Hebrew Bible: the only other three-verse windows reaching five are Ezek 2:6 (a prophet's call) and Eccl 7:16. The chain has no parallel in Proverbs and cannot be labelled a wisdom form on density. It is unique to Obadiah.
- Prov 24:29 כַּאֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה־לִי כֵּן אֶעֱשֶׂה־לּוֹ ("as he has done to me, so I will do to him") is forbidden speech, the inverse of Obad 15's divine talion. Lev 24:19 is closer (see A10).

**Design check.** Counts verified: yes. Baseline: Prov 27:10 falls inside the class of exclusive triples; אֵיד's lean to wisdom beats the null. Sense: Prov 27:10 fails; Prov 17:5 and 24:17 hold. Alternative explanation: אֵיד ("calamity") is at home in both wisdom and oracle against nations.

**Verdict: Prov 27:10 Discard as allusion. Low. Wisdom register: Needs reframing. Low–moderate.** State it as: Obad 12–13 uses a word and a censure at home in wisdom (Prov 17:5, 24:17), alongside prophetic usage (Mic 7:8, Jer 49:8), without claiming a wisdom form.


### A8. Mic 7:8–10 at Obad 10, 12

**Tested.** The set; subject of the verb; synonym rivals; windows; Greek.

**Results.** כָּסָה ("cover") with בּוּשָׁה ("shame"): Ezek 7:18, Mic 7:10, Obad 10. Holds, but the count overstates the rivals. In Ezek 7:18 the thing covering them is פַּלָּצוּת ("horror"); בּוּשָׁה ("shame") stands in a separate clause. So shame as *subject* of "cover" with the enemy as object occurs **only** at Mic 7:10 וּתְכַסֶּהָ בוּשָׁה ("and shame will cover her") and Obad 10 תְּכַסְּךָ בוּשָׁה ("shame will cover you"). בּוּשָׁה ("shame") itself is in four verses only.

With rejoicing added, a two-verse window gives Mic 7:8–10 and Obad 10–12 only; three verses add Ps 89:43–46. Further unclaimed contacts: Mic 7:8 אַל־תִּשְׂמְחִי… לִי ("do not rejoice… over me") has the same preposition as Obad 12 לִבְנֵי־יְהוּדָה ("over the sons of Judah"); Mic 7:10 עֵינַי תִּרְאֶינָּה בָּהּ ("my eyes will look on her") answers Obad 12–13 אַל־תֵּרֶא ("do not gloat"); and Mic 7:14 names גִּלְעָד ("Gilead"), as Obad 19 does.

**Rivals.** Synonyms abound: כְלִמָּה ("disgrace") covers at Jer 3:25, 51:51, Ps 69:8; בֹּשֶׁת ("shame", another noun) at Ps 44:16; Ps 35:26 joins rejoicers at disaster with shame clothing them; Ps 89:46 has shame put on him. All describe the *speaker* or use other verbs, or use the other noun. Ps 35:26 is the best rival, and אַל־תִּשְׂמַח ("do not rejoice") is also at Isa 14:29 and Hos 9:1.

**Greek.** The Hebrew contact is not preserved: Mic 7:10 περιβαλεῖται αἰσχύνην ("she will put on shame") against Obad 10 καλύψει σε αἰσχύνη ("shame will cover you"). The noun survives, the verb does not. Mic 7:8 μὴ ἐπίχαιρε ("do not rejoice over") shares its root with Obad 12.

**Sense.** The same: a gloating enemy, a reversal. Micah's enemy is unnamed; Obadiah's is Edom. Which way any borrowing goes is not shown.

### A9. Hab 2:9, 16, 17 at Obad 3–4, 10, 16

**Results.** מָרוֹם ("height") with קֵן ("nest"): Jer 49:16, Hab 2:9. Holds. שְׁתֵה גַם־אַתָּה ("drink, you too"): Hab 2:16 only. חָמָס ("violence") with כָּסָה ("cover"): seven verses, Isa 59:6, Obad 10, Jonah 3:8, Hab 2:17, Mal 2:16, Prov 10:6, 10:11. Holds.

**2:9 the nest.** Weak against rivals. The primary parallel is Jer 49:16. Beyond it, Job 39:27–28 has נֶשֶׁר ("eagle"), the verb גָּבַהּ ("make high"), קֵן ("nest"), סֶלַע ("rock") and שָׁכַן ("dwell"). "Nest, rock, dwell" within a verse's reach gives only Jer 49:16, Obad 3–4 and Job 39:27–28. Num 24:21 has וְשִׂים בַּסֶּלַע קִנֶּךָ ("and set your nest in the rock"). Hab 2:9 does add שׂוּם ("set"), which Jer 49:16 lacks; but שׂוּם with קֵן is also Num 24:21. In Greek, θῇς… νοσσιάν σου ("you set your nest") stands only at Num 24:21 and Obad 4 (Rahlfs Numbers confirms), while Hab 2:9 has τοῦ τάξαι… νοσσιὰν αὐτοῦ ("to set his nest"). Hab 2:9 is a motif parallel. It shares the sense of safety from harm in a high place.

**2:16 the cup.** Discard. The phrase is not in Obadiah. גַם־אַתָּה ("you too") is in 15 verses plain, and at Obad 11 and 13 it has nothing to do with drinking; "drink" is at v. 16. שָׁתָה ("drink") with כּוֹס ("cup") is ten verses, among them Jer 49:12, in the Edom oracle, which is the obvious neighbour; Lam 4:21 sets a cup before Edom directly.

**2:17 violence that covers.** This one holds. Sense-filtering the seven: Jonah 3:8 is sackcloth; Isa 59:6 is covering oneself with works; Prov 10:6, 11 have the mouth hiding violence; Mal 2:16 has violence covering a garment. Only Hab 2:17 חֲמַס לְבָנוֹן יְכַסֶּךָּ ("the violence against Lebanon will overwhelm you") and Obad 10 מֵחֲמַס אָחִיךָ… תְּכַסְּךָ ("because of violence to your brother… will cover you") have: violence, objective genitive, the verb "cover" with second-person object, and a named victim. Obadiah interposes בּוּשָׁה ("shame") as subject. **The Greek is striking:** καλύψει σε ("will cover you") is at Obad 10 and Hab 2:17 only (a compound at Ezek 26:10), each with ἀσέβεια ("impiety"): ἀσέβεια τοῦ Λιβάνου καλύψει σε ("the impiety of Lebanon will cover you"; same in Rahlfs) against τὴν ἀσέβειαν ἀδελφοῦ σου Ἰακὼβ καλύψει σε αἰσχύνη ("for the impiety to your brother Jacob, shame will cover you"). A single translator of the Twelve may explain this [S], so it is modest independent support.

### A10. Ps 7:17 at Obad 4, 10, 15

**Results.** שׁוּב ("return"), רֹאשׁ ("head"), חָמָס ("violence") and יָרַד ("descend"): Ps 7:17 only. Holds; indeed "return, head, violence" alone gives Ps 7:17 only, and חָמָס with יָרַד gives Ps 7:17 only. Still, it belongs to the Baseline 1 class, with the hooks scattered across vv. 4, 10 and 15.

**Breaking it.**
- *יָרַד ("descend").* Different stem, subject and sense: in Ps 7:17 the wicked person's violence comes down on his crown; in Obad 3–4 the LORD brings Edom down (causative). Greek differs too: καταβήσεται ("will come down") against κατάξω ("I will bring down"). Remove this hook and the "quadruple" collapses.
- *שׁוּב ("return") with בְּרֹאשׁ ("on the head").* A common idiom: Judg 9:57, 1 Sam 25:39, 1 Kgs 2:32, 33, 44, Joel 4:4, 7, Ps 7:17, Esth 9:25 (with עַל, "on"), Neh 3:36 (with אֶל, "to"), 2 Chr 6:23. One point in the Psalm's favour: Ps 7:17 יָשׁוּב עֲמָלוֹ בְרֹאשׁוֹ ("his mischief will return on his head") shares the intransitive verb, the same imperfect form and the preposition with Obad 15 גְּמֻלְךָ יָשׁוּב בְּרֹאשֶׁךָ ("your recompense will return on your head"). 1 Kgs 2:33 is intransitive with the same preposition but in another tense, Esth 9:25 has עַל ("on"), and Joel has the noun with a causative verb, so the Psalm alone has this exact syntax.
- *Rivals.* The first half of Obad 15, כַּאֲשֶׁר עָשִׂיתָ יֵעָשֶׂה לָּךְ ("as you have done, it will be done to you"), has its near-formal twin in **Lev 24:19** כַּאֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה כֵּן יֵעָשֶׂה לּוֹ ("as he has done, so it will be done to him"), with the same verb in the same passive stem. Jer 50:15 כַּאֲשֶׁר עָשְׂתָה עֲשׂוּ־לָהּ ("as she has done, do to her") and 50:29 are imperative, directed to Babylon. Judg 1:7 is confession in the first person. **Ezek 35:15** is to Edom: כְּשִׂמְחָתְךָ… כֵּן אֶעֱשֶׂה־לָּךְ ("as you rejoiced… so I will do to you"), with Ezek 35:11 too. Ps 94:23 has only the causative verb with עַל ("on"); 1 Kgs 2:33 is about blood. So the verse is not Ps 7:17 alone: it is law (Lev 24), plus oracle (Ezek 35), plus idiom.

### A12. Sound plays in Obad 11–13

#### (a) אֵידָם ("their disaster") and אֱדוֹם ("Edom")

**Counts verified.** אֵיד ("calamity") is in 22 verses, 24 occurrences. In one verse with אֱדוֹם ("Edom"): none. With עֵשָׂו ("Esau"): Jer 49:8 only (Edom at 49:7). Within three verses of שֵׂעִיר ("Seir"): Ezek 35:5. So it occurs in three Edom settings, with Obad 13's three uses.

**Baseline.** Edom oracles make up about 78 verses (0.34%) on my rough count (Isa 34; Jer 49:7–22; Ezek 25:12–14; 35; Amos 1:11–12; Obad; Mal 1:2–5). Three of 22 verses by chance has a probability near 0.00006, so אֵיד ("calamity") does lean to Edom. But it also leans to oracles against nations generally: Jer 46:21, 48:16, 49:32. And 3 of 6 such settings is not extreme.

**Sense and form.** The consonants match when the vowel letters are removed: both reduce to א־ד־ם. But the form with final ם is one of three in Obad 13, the other two being אֵידוֹ ("his disaster"). And אֵידָם ("their disaster") is the commonest form of the word, not a specially chosen one: it stands in 9 of the 22 verses. Ezek 35:5 is therefore thin support by form, though a real one by setting. Better support for the general habit: Gen 25:30 (הָאָדֹם, "the red stuff", with Edom named), Ezek 35:6 (דָּם, "blood", four times, an Edom oracle), Isa 63:1. The Greek flattens it with three different words: πόνων ("toils"), ὀλέθρου ("ruin"), ἀπωλείας ("destruction"). No ancient hearer's evidence for the pun survives there. The BHS apparatus proposes אוֹנוֹ ("his sorrow") and אָבְדוֹ ("his perishing") for two of the three from that Greek; these are only proposals, but the triple repetition is not beyond question.

**Alternative explanation.** The triple אֵיד ("calamity") may be driven by the refrain בְּיוֹם… ("in the day of…"), and by a word at home in oracle language.

**Verdict (a): Uncertain — flag for research. Low–moderate.** The resemblance is real and the setting supports it. Design cannot be shown. Say "may be heard as", not "plays on".

#### (b) נָכְרוֹ ("his misfortune") and נָכְרִים ("foreigners")

**Counts verified.** נֵכֶר ("misfortune") occurs in two verses: Obad 12 and Job 31:3. נָכְרִי ("foreign") occurs in about 45 verses. In Obadiah the two stand in adjacent verses and never meet elsewhere: a word found only twice in the Hebrew Bible is used immediately after its near-homonym.

**Missed.** Job 31:3 הֲלֹא־אֵיד לְעַוָּל וְנֵכֶר לְפֹעֲלֵי אָוֶן ("is it not calamity for the unjust, and misfortune for workers of iniquity?") pairs נֵכֶר ("misfortune") with אֵיד ("calamity"). This is the same pair as Obad 12–13, and the only other use. So the choice of נֵכֶר may be driven by a word pair and not by sound. That rival must stand beside the sound-play view.

**Greek.** Both editions read Obad 12 as ἐν ἡμέρᾳ ἀλλοτρίων ("in the day of strangers"), with no pronoun and a different meaning from the Hebrew "misfortune". V. 11 has ἀλλότριοι ("strangers") for נָכְרִים ("foreigners"), and ἀλλογενῶν ("foreigners") for זָרִים ("strangers"). This is a reading: the translator understood the root as "foreign". It proves the words sounded related to an ancient reader. It does not prove a pun in Hebrew. Greek Job 31:3 has ἀπαλλοτρίωσις ("estrangement"), showing the same instinct elsewhere.

**Verdict (b): Confirmed with nuance. Moderate.** In the Hebrew this is a play on one root across adjacent verses, supported by an ancient reading. The word pair at Job 31:3 is a live alternative for why this word was chosen. Report the Greek as a reading.

### B1. Jeremiah 49 and Obadiah

**Tested.** Chapter ranking at four rarity ceilings; attachment of each pair; subsets of Obadiah; exact phrases; two-pass diff; Greek of both; a null for how often any short prophetic passage has such a partner.

**Counts reproduced.** All hold.
- Jer 49 ranks first of 862 chapters of ten verses or more: 74 pairs in 39 verses, 1.897 per verse. Ezek 31 is second at 0.278.
- The 74 pairs attach to Obad 1 (13), 2 (4), 3 (26), 4 (13), 5 (17) and 8 (1), and to none in vv. 10–21.
- On the Jeremiah side they fall at 49:7, 9, 14, 15, 16 and, once, 49:20.
- The first place is stable. With the ceiling at three, five, ten or twenty verses, Jer 49 stays first (1.64, 1.90, 2.13, 2.51 against second places of 0.19, 0.28, 0.41, 0.67).
- For Obad 10–14 alone Jer 49 falls to 483rd of 862; for 15–21 to 467th; for 10–21 to 506th.
- מִי יוֹרִדֵנִי אָרֶץ ("Who will bring me down to earth?"): Obad 3 only, plain and skeletal. בֵּין כּוֹכָבִים ("among the stars"): Obad 4 only, plain and skeletal. Control: the same search for מִשָּׁם אוֹרִידְךָ ("from there I will bring you down") returned Jer 49:16 and Obad 4.
- יַעֲקֹב ("Jacob", 319 verses) and צִיּוֹן ("Zion", 153 verses) are absent from Jer 49. Control: Edom is found at 49:7, 17, 20, 22, and Israel at 49:1, 2.
- Order holds: Obad 1, 2, 3–4 = 49:14, 15, 16; Obad 5, 6 = 49:9, 10; Obad 8 = 49:7. This is a clean inversion of three blocks.

**Corrections and nuance.**
- "None to vv. 10–21" is true of rare pairs only. Single words do cross: אֵיד ("calamity"), Jer 49:8 and Obad 13; אָח ("brother"), Jer 49:10 and Obad 10, 12; שָׁתָה ("drink"), Jer 49:12 and Obad 16. These matter for B5 and B9.
- "Obadiah alone has the taunt" holds for the wording, not the form. Jer 49:4 gives Ammon מִי יָבוֹא אֵלָי ("Who will come to me?"), and Jer 21:13 has "Who will come down against us?". The closer rival for Obad 3–4's pluses is Isa 14:13, 15: "you said in your heart", "above the stars", "you will be brought down". It is the only verse with כּוֹכָב ("star") and לֵבָב ("heart"), with the descent two verses on.

**Diff, pass one (consonantal).**
- Obad 1–4 (50 words) against Jer 49:14–16 (38 words): 24 words in order.
- There are three runs of four or more words: מֵאֵת יְהוָה וְצִיר בַּגּוֹיִם ("from the LORD, and an envoy among the nations"), הִנֵּה קָטֹן נְתַתִּיךָ בַּגּוֹיִם בָּזוּי ("behold, small I have made you among the nations, despised") and קִנֶּךָ מִשָּׁם אוֹרִידְךָ נְאֻם יְהוָה ("your nest, from there I will bring you down, declares the LORD").
- Obad 5 against Jer 49:9: four words in order, plus the thieves clause transposed.
- Obad 6 against Jer 49:10: only עֵשָׂו ("Esau") is identical. נֶחְפְּשׂוּ ("are searched out") and חָשַׂפְתִּי ("I have stripped bare") have the same three consonants in a different order. מַצְפֻּנָיו ("his treasures", a word found only here) stands for מִסְתָּרָיו ("his hiding places").
- Obad 8 against Jer 49:7: two shared roots, in different syntax.

**Diff, pass two (waw, yod and final he deleted).**
- Obad 1–4 rises only from 24 to 25 words; Obad 5 from four to six.
- Spelling fullness cuts both ways. Jeremiah is the fuller in "sent" and "gleanings", Obadiah in "leave". Orthography gives no direction.
- What separates the two texts is wording, not spelling: this is composition or reworking, not copying.

**Evidence on direction.**

*Pointing to Obadiah as the reworked form:*
1. In Jer 49:16 "your nest" is the object of "make high". In Obad 4 "make high" has lost its object, and the words "and if among the stars set" (which BHS notes are absent from Jer 49:16) carry שִׂים ("set"), a passive participle that BHS would emend or delete. That pointed form with "your nest" occurs only at Num 24:21 and Obad 4. The plus looks built from other wording (see B3).
2. עָלֶיהָ ("against her", Obad 1) is the only third feminine singular suffix in the book. In Jeremiah, feminine בָּצְרָה ("Bozrah") stands in the preceding verse, 49:13.
3. "If plunderers by night, how you are destroyed" (Obad 5) interrupts its verse; BHS asks whether to transpose it or delete it as a gloss from Jer 49:9.

*Pointing to Jeremiah as the reworked form:*
1. Jer 49:7–22 is demonstrably a compilation. Jer 49:19 shares 24 of 27 words with 50:44, 49:20 shares 20 of 24 with 50:45, 49:18 shares 12 of 15 with 50:40, and 49:22 shares 8 of its 17 with 48:41.
2. Jeremiah's plus תִּפְלַצְתְּךָ ("your horror") occurs nowhere else.
3. Obadiah's first-person plural (שָׁמַעְנוּ "we heard", וְנָקוּמָה "let us rise") holds together; so does Jeremiah's singular, however.
4. Obadiah's rhetorical הֲלוֹא ("would they not?") makes a clearer argument than Jeremiah's flat לֹא ("not"), which BHS proposes to correct.

*Neutral:* a block inversion can be made in either direction. Both books also reuse material elsewhere (for Obadiah's side, see Joel 3:5 under New candidates).

**Greek.**
- The translators worked separately. For צִיר ("envoy") Obadiah has περιοχήν ("an enclosing", an odd rendering), Jeremiah ἀγγέλους ("messengers"). For קָטֹן ("small"), ὀλιγοστόν ("least") against μικρόν ("small"). For בָּזוּי ("despised"), ἠτιμωμένος ("dishonoured") against εὐκαταφρόνητον ("contemptible"). For זָדוֹן ("arrogance"), ὑπερηφανία ("pride") against ἰταμία ("recklessness"). For "bring down", κατάξω ("I will bring down") against καθελῶ ("I will pull down").
- The shorter Greek Jeremiah still has all the overlap, and lacks the taunt and the stars. The overlap is therefore not a late addition to the longer Hebrew Jeremiah, and the pluses were already distinct.
- Greek Obadiah's ἤκουσα ("I heard") against the Hebrew "we heard" agrees with Jeremiah. BHS records this. It may be harmonisation or a different Vorlage; it cannot decide the matter.

**Baseline.** I took 120 arbitrary 21-verse stretches of the Latter Prophets and ranked partner chapters outside each stretch's own book. The median top density is 0.33; only 8% reach 1.0. Every stretch at or above 1.0 belongs to a known literary parallel: Isa 37 with 2 Kgs 19, Jer 52 with 2 Kgs 25, Isa 2 with Mic 4, Jer 48 with Isa 16, and Jeremiah's own Edom oracle with Obadiah. So the density places Obad–Jer 49 in the class of real parallels, not of shared vocabulary.

### B2. Amos 9:12 as a designed handoff

**Tested.** Each stated contact, with expected counts; by-book rates with intervals; arbitrary 73-verse stretches as a null; all 66 book pairings in the Twelve; order reshuffling; closing-chapter to opening-chapter seams; the Greek order.

**Counts reproduced.** All hold.
- יָרַשׁ ("possess") with אֱדוֹם ("Edom") in one verse: Amos 9:12 only.
- הִסְגִּיר ("hand over") with גָּלוּת ("exiles"): Amos 1:6, 9 only, both also naming Edom.
- אֵשׁ ("fire") with יוֹסֵף ("Joseph"): Amos 5:6, Obad 18.
- מִשָּׁם אוֹרִידֵם ("from there I will bring them down"): Amos 9:2 only.
- By-book rates: Joel first (8 pairs, 73 verses, 10.96 per 100); Jeremiah second (109 in 1,364, 7.99); Amos eleventh (4 in 146, 2.74); Jonah sixteenth (1 in 48, 2.08).

**Corrections and sense.**
- "Only" flatters the Amos 9:12 pair. "Possess" (204 verses) and "Edom" (93 verses) would be expected together in 0.82 verses by chance. One is what chance gives.
- Obadiah never puts the two words together: Obad 19 has "possess" with עֵשָׂו ("Esau"), which also occurs at Deut 2:12, 22 and Josh 24:4. The cognate noun in Num 24:18, יְרֵשָׁה ("possession") with Edom, is an equal rival, and it is part of the B3 cluster. No scan can see this link, since it is not a pair within any verse of Obadiah.
- Amos 1:6, 9: in Amos other nations hand exiles over *to* Edom; in Obad 14 Edom does the handing over, and the object is שְׂרִידָיו ("his survivors").
- Amos 1:11 is not unique for brother with Edom: Gen 32:4, Num 20:14, Amos 1:9, 1:11. Num 20:14's אָחִיךָ יִשְׂרָאֵל ("your brother Israel") is the nearest in form to Obad 10's אָחִיךָ יַעֲקֹב ("your brother Jacob").
- Fire with Joseph (expected 2.88, since Joseph fills Genesis) proves little as a pair. "House of Joseph" with fire and אָכַל ("consume") is sharper: Amos 5:6 and Obad 18 only. The sense is an inversion: fire against Joseph in Amos, Joseph as flame in Obadiah. This is the best Amos link.
- Amos 9:2's closest relative is Jer 49:16 and Obad 4, which share "from there I will bring you down" verbatim. Obad 6's חפשׂ ("search"), however, differs from Jeremiah's word and is at Amos 9:3, in that very passage. That small datum favours contact.

**Chance baselines.**
- *Adjacency.* I normalised each pair's shared rare pairs by the product of the two books' lengths. The eleven adjacent pairs have a mean rank of 34.7 of 66, against 33.5 by chance; 60% of 20,000 random orders of the Twelve do as well or better. Adjacent books share no more than non-adjacent ones. Amos–Obadiah ranks 46th of 66.
- *Seams.* Comparing each book's last chapter to each other book's first, the adjacent seams average 63.6th of 132 (chance 66.5). Amos 9 into Obadiah is high at 6th, but Joel 4 into Obadiah is 2nd and Nah 3 into Obadiah is 4th. Obadiah's shortness also lifts it in many pairings.
- *Rarer words.* By lemmas in 50 verses or fewer, normalised, Amos is seventh of eleven partners; Joel first.

**Is Joel the densest partner, and do short books distort it?**
- Joel holds first place, with limits. The 95% intervals overlap heavily: Joel 4.7–21.6 per 100; Nahum 1.3–18.7; Malachi 1.1–15.9; Habakkuk 1.1–15.7; Amos 0.7–7.0. Short books are distorted: Nahum, Malachi and Habakkuk reach third to fifth on three pairs each.
- Against a real null Joel still stands out. Arbitrary 73-verse stretches of Isaiah, Jeremiah (without ch. 49) and Ezekiel have a median of 3 pairs, and only 0.8% reach 8.
- But some of Joel's eight are weak. One involves only the negative particle; the two from Joel 3:5 count one verse twice; "stubble" with "fire" is idiom. About five independent contacts remain (lots at 4:3; repayment on the head at 4:4, 7; Zion with escape at 3:5; go up and judge at 4:12; strangers and Jerusalem at 4:17), giving a rate near 6.8.
- Leave-one-out: dropping Joel 3:5 leaves 8.2 per 100, still first.
- Jeremiah's second place is wholly Jer 49. Without it, 39 pairs and 2.94 per 100 put Jeremiah about ninth.

**Missed.** Joel 4:19 is the only verse with חָמָס ("violence") and "Edom", and it adds "the sons of Judah". Compare Obad 10 ("violence") and Obad 12 ("sons of Judah"). In Swete's order Joel stands directly before Obadiah; in the Hebrew order Amos does. So two successive books close on Edom, and a handoff theory that chooses Amos depends on the order assumed.

| Recurrence | Possible: Amos has six of the Twelve's Edom verses |
| Thematic coherence | Strong |

**Verdict: Needs reframing. Rating: low–moderate as a designed handoff; moderate as a set of contacts.** The order is a fact and Amos's closing does name Edom, but adjacency has no general lexical effect in the Twelve, the key pair is at chance level, and Joel's closing has at least as strong a claim. Say "Amos ends with Edom and Obadiah is next; a reader will connect them", not "Obadiah unfolds that clause".


### B3. The Num 24:17–21 cluster

**Tested.** Phrases and counts; 19,492 five-verse windows across the canon, scored on Obadiah's rarer words; leave-one-out on the triple; the pointed form; the Greek.

**Counts reproduced.** All hold.
- כּוֹכָב מִיַּעֲקֹב ("a star from Jacob") and אֱדוֹם יְרֵשָׁה ("Edom a possession"): one verse each, plain and skeletal.
- שִׂים בַּסֶּלַע קִנֶּךָ ("set your nest in the rock"): Num 24:21, found by the skeletal pass only (the source spells the conjunction differently).
- שָׂרִיד ("survivor") with יַעֲקֹב ("Jacob"): Num 24:19, Obad 18 only (expected 0.38).
- Scan rank: 66th of 922 at a five-verse floor; 58th of 862 at the ten-verse floor.

**The chapter scan understates it.** The scan works verse against verse and divides by chapter length, so it cannot see a tight cluster spread over five verses.
- Scored by window on Obadiah's rarer words (in 150 verses or fewer), Num 24:17–21 ranks 13th of 19,492. Only overlapping windows of Jer 49 (ten) and Job 39 (two) stand above it. It is the third independent place in the canon after Jeremiah's Edom oracle and Job's eagle.
- Without the proper names Edom, Esau, Jacob and Seir, it ranks 18th.
- Star, nest and survivor in one five-verse window: Num 24:17–21 only.
- Leave-one-out, any two of the three within five verses: nest with survivor, only there; star with survivor, only there; star with nest, only there and in Obadiah itself. No one word carries it.
- Independence would predict about 0.002 such windows for this triple, though that triple was chosen after seeing the texts.

**Sharpest datum.** The pointed form שִׂים ("set") with "your nest" occurs only at Num 24:21 and Obad 4. This is the grammatical oddity BHS would emend, and Jer 49:16 does not have it. Hab 2:9 has "set" and "nest" (with an infinitive) and מָרוֹם ("height", cf. Obad 3), so "set a nest" is shared by three verses, but the form is shared by two.

**Greek (Swete) strengthens it.**
- Num 24:17 ἄστρον ἐξ Ἰακώβ ("a star from Jacob") with Obad 4 ἄστρων ("stars").
- Num 24:18 reads Ἠσαύ ("Esau") for Seir, alongside Ἐδώμ ("Edom") and κληρονομία ("inheritance"); Obadiah's Greek has Esau with κατακληρονομήσουσιν ("they shall inherit", v. 19).
- Num 24:21 ἐὰν θῇς ἐν πέτρᾳ τὴν νοσσιάν σου ("if you set your nest in the rock") with Obad 4 ἐὰν … θῇς νοσσιάν σου ("if … you set your nest"). Only these two verses have "if", "set" and "nest". The Greek of Jer 29:17 has the nest, but neither "if" nor "set".
- Num 24:19 ἀπολεῖ σωζόμενον ("will destroy the one being saved"); Obad 8 ἀπολῶ ("I will destroy"); Obad 14 ἀνασῳζομένους ("those escaping").

**Sense.** The transfers are real but bold. The star in Numbers is a ruler from Jacob; in Obadiah stars are Edom's false height. The nest belongs to the Kenite, not Edom, and Num 24:21–22 itself threatens that nest-builder. שָׂרִיד ("survivor") in Num 24:19 is from a city; in Obad 18 from the house of Esau. The hiphil of אָבַד ("destroy") is ordinary (26 occurrences), and on its own would not count. Num 24:19's וְיֵרְדְּ ("and he shall rule") looks like Obad 3–4's "bring down" but is another root: a sound-play at most.

Direction is unmarked, and part of the rock-and-nest wording is already in Jer 49:16, so the case rests on the star, the form "set" and the survivor. Say "shares wording with", not "quotes".


### B4. The three-block structure

**Tested.** Reproduced the score; ranked cuts raw and by standard deviations from each cut's own shuffle; varied the word threshold; the same test on 600 arbitrary 21-verse stretches of the Latter Prophets, and on every whole chapter of 15–30 verses in the Twelve; the boundary claim against all boundaries.

**Counts reproduced.** All hold.
- Shared words: 4 (1–9 with 10–14), 4 (1–9 with 15–21), 3 (10–14 with 15–21), total 11.
- 20,000 shuffles: mean 30.3; lowest found 15; none at 11.
- Of 91 cuts (each block at least three verses) this one is second lowest; the lowest is 1–3 | 4–6 | 7–21 at 9, which is merely the effect of two tiny blocks.
- No content word crosses 14|15 within three verses either side.

**Corrections.**
- By the fair measure the result is better than stated: distance from each cut's own shuffle ranks 1–9 | 10–14 | 15–21 **first** of 91 (about 5 standard deviations). Neighbours 1–9 | 10–15 and 1–7 | 8–14 follow.
- The result depends somewhat on the filter: with the threshold at 300 or 800 it stays second; at 3,000, or with no filter, it falls to tenth and eighth.
- "Content words" still include אַתָּה ("you") and עַד ("until").

**Does verse-shuffling overstate cohesion? Yes, greatly.** Shuffling destroys the local clustering every text has, so the null is far too easy.
- Among arbitrary 21-verse stretches of the Latter Prophets, each given its own best balanced cut (blocks of five or more), 49% reach better than 1 in 200 by the same shuffle, and 38% are never matched in 200 shuffles.
- "None in 20,000" therefore tells us little.
- The proper comparison is the ratio of observed to expected. Obadiah's is 0.36. Against arbitrary stretches' best cuts, this sits at the 5th percentile (share at or below: 5%).
- Same result for whole chapters of 15–30 verses in the Twelve: of 39, only Amos 2 (0.32, oracles against nations) goes lower. Joel 4 is 0.44. Isa 34 has an even lower raw score on unconstrained cuts.

So the honest odds are about 1 in 20, not less than 1 in 20,000: a real but ordinary-sized signal of oracular composition.

**The 14|15 boundary.**
- Zero crossing within three verses is not rare: 311 of 3,785 such boundaries (8.2%) in the Latter Prophets and Obadiah, and 71 of about 715 in the Twelve.
- The result is partly made by the filter. יוֹם ("day", 1,930 verses) is excluded as too common, yet it is the book's hinge: vv. 8, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, with v. 14 בְּיוֹם צָרָה ("in the day of distress") meeting v. 15 יוֹם־יְהוָה ("the day of the LORD"). Allow that one word and the boundary is crossed by the key term.
- v. 15 therefore works as a join rather than a clean break.
- 9|10 is also soft: the Greek, Syriac and Latin join מִקָּטֶל ("by slaughter") to v. 10 (BHS: perhaps rightly), as Swete shows with διὰ τὴν σφαγήν ("because of the slaughter").
- Verse-level testing cannot address the view [S] that v. 15a and v. 15b belong to different units.
- WLC has no paragraph marks inside Obadiah, so the Masoretic layout neither supports nor opposes.

**Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. Rating: moderate–high.** The division is the best available by vocabulary and fits the content. The stated odds and the "no word crosses" claim should be dropped or restated, since יוֹם ("day") crosses and joins the second and third blocks.


### B5. The talion mirror

**Tested.** All crossing vocabulary; a null for how much any 21-verse prophetic stretch recurs across a same-position block; sense of each link.

**Counts reproduced.**
- כָּרַת ("cut off") vv. 9, 10, 14; אָבַד ("destroy") vv. 8, 12; שָׂרִיד ("survivor") vv. 14, 18; פָּלִיט ("fugitive") v. 14; פְּלֵיטָה ("escape") v. 17.
- 10–14 with 15–21: Jerusalem, survivor, Jacob: three.
- The stated chance mean holds: 8.02 in 20,000 shuffles, with three or fewer in 1.6% of them.

**Do the pointed links beat chance? By count, no.**
- Only seven content words of vv. 10–14 recur elsewhere in the book: survivor, destroy, cut off, Jacob, Jerusalem, שָׁלַח ("send"), and אַתָּה ("you"). A random five verses of Obadiah share 14.2 on average. Only 0.6% do as poorly as this.
- Among words in 400 verses or fewer, four cross; in Latter Prophets 21-verse stretches the median is seven and 88% have four or more.
- So the links are not surplus. They are nearly all the crossing vocabulary the block has. Calling them "pointed" is a claim about sense only.
- Four chosen families recurring together occurs in no other Latter Prophets stretch (of 1,004 windows, none has even three), but the families were picked from Obadiah, so this is not independent evidence.

**By sense they are unequal.**

| Link | Crime | Sentence | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| כָּרַת ("cut off") | v. 14, Edom cuts off Judah's fugitives (active) | vv. 9, 10, Edom is cut off (passive) | Real mirror |
| שָׂרִיד ("survivor") | v. 14, Edom hands over his survivors | v. 18, no survivor for Esau | Real mirror; best of the four. "Hand over" with "survivor": Obad 14 only |
| פָּלִיט / פְּלֵיטָה ("fugitive" / "escape") | v. 14 | v. 17, escape on Zion | Reversal in the victim's favour, not talion on Edom; different lemmas |
| אָבַד ("destroy") | v. 12, Judah's day of ruin: Edom rejoices at it | v. 8, the LORD destroys Edom's wise | Weak: different subject, object and act |

The filter also hides real echoes: בּוֹא ("come", too common for the filter), thieves "come" to Edom (v. 5) and Edom "comes" in at the gate (vv. 11, 13); שָׁלַח ("send"), allies send Edom away (v. 7) and Edom reaches out (v. 13).

**The claim does not need counts, because the text says it.**
- כַּאֲשֶׁר עָשִׂיתָ יֵעָשֶׂה לָּךְ ("as you have done, it will be done to you", v. 15) is the book's own statement, and v. 16 repeats the "as … so" form.
- Comparable wording: Lev 24:19, Judg 1:7, Jer 50:15, 29.
- גְּמֻלְךָ יָשׁוּב בְּרֹאשֶׁךָ ("your deed will return on your head") has its nearest partner at Joel 4:4, 7.

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Counts verified | Exact |
| Baseline | Fails: fewer crossings than chance and than typical oracles |
| Sense | Two real mirrors, one reversal, one weak |
| Alternative explanation | Standard judgement vocabulary repeats in any such oracle; but two links are specific |

**Verdict: Needs reframing. Rating: high that talion is the stated principle; low–moderate as lexical design.** Base it on v. 15a and illustrate it with cut off and survivor. Do not call פְּלֵיטָה ("escape") talion or lean on אָבַד ("destroy"), and do not speak of a whole-book mirror.


### B6. Edom as frame of the Twelve

### B8. Isa 63:1–5 and Rev 19 as a trajectory from Obad 21

**Tested.** Phrases; expected counts; all shared vocabulary of Isa 63:1–6 and Obadiah; rivals for each link; Greek of Obadiah, Isaiah, Revelation; Edom in the NT.

**Counts reproduced.** מִי־זֶה בָּא מֵאֱדוֹם ("Who is this who comes from Edom") and רַב לְהוֹשִׁיעַ ("mighty to save"): Isa 63:1, plain and skeletal. יָשַׁע ("save") with "Edom": 2 Sam 8:14, Isa 63:1, 1 Chr 18:13. Isa 63:3 לְבַדִּי ("alone").

**Corrections.**
- "Save" (198 verses) and Edom (93) would meet in 0.79 verses by chance; three is little more. In 2 Sam 8:14 and its parallel, the LORD saves David after he garrisons Edom: an unnamed David precedent as apt as anything in Obadiah.
- יָשַׁע ("save") with עֵשָׂו ("Esau"): Obad 21 only.

**Obad 21 and Isa 63.**
- Shared content vocabulary of Isa 63:1–6 and Obadiah: Edom and "save" are the only specific items; the rest (who, this, speak, none, heart, go down, with) is common.
- Scan rank of Isa 63 against Obadiah: 127th of 862, one pair.
- Isa 63's own neighbours are elsewhere: בָּצְרָה ("Bozrah") at Isa 34:6, Jer 49:13, 22, Amos 1:12, and not in Obadiah; יוֹם נָקָם ("day of vengeance") at Isa 34:8, 61:2, 63:4; Isa 63:5 nearly repeats Isa 59:16.
- And the sense opposes. Obad 21 has plural deliverers going up; Isa 63:3, 5 denies any helper: "no one with me", "none to help". A trajectory would have to be a correction.

**Obad 21's own relatives.**
- מוֹשִׁיעִים ("deliverers") as a plural: Neh 9:27 and Obad 21 only (Obad 21 spelled without yod, BHS would repoint).
- "Save" with שָׁפַט ("judge") leads to Judg 2:16, 18, then Isa 33:22, Ezek 34:22, Hos 13:10, Ps 72:4, 109:31 among eight. The obvious model for "deliverers … to judge" is the Judges pattern, unnoted by the claim.
- לַיהוָה הַמְּלוּכָה ("to the LORD the kingship"): Obad 21 and Ps 22:29 only, Hebrew and Swete alike.

**Greek.**
- Obad 21 does not have deliverers: Swete ἀνασῳζόμενοι ("those who escape"), Rahlfs ἄνδρες σεσῳσμένοι ("men who have been saved"), which BHS retroverts. It also has ἐξ ὄρους ("from the mountain") and ἐκδικῆσαι ("to avenge"). In Greek the first link does not exist.
- Isa 63:1–3 has ἱμάτια ("garments"), ληνός ("winepress"), πατητός/κατεπάτησα ("trodden"/"I trod"), θυμός ("wrath"), αἷμα ("blood"). Rev 19:13, 15 has ἱμάτιον ("garment"), αἷμα ("blood"), πατεῖ ("treads"), ληνόν ("winepress"), θυμοῦ ("wrath"). Rivals have only part: Joel 3:13 (Greek numbering; πατεῖτε "tread", ληνός "winepress") and Lam 1:15. Of the verses with ληνός ("winepress") in Swete, only Isa 63:2–3 has all five. βεβαμμένον ("dipped") is Revelation's own word. ληνός in the NT: Matt 21:33, Rev 14:19, 20, 19:15. Isa 63 to Rev 19 is firm.
- But Edom drops out: Rev 19 names Babylon, Ἰδουμαία ("Idumea") appears only at Mark 3:8 in the NT, and Ἠσαύ ("Esau") only at Rom 9:13, Heb 11:20, 12:16. Rev 11:15 Ἐγένετο ἡ βασιλεία ("the kingdom has become") is the nearest NT echo of Obad 21's kingship, not Rev 19.

**Verdict: Needs reframing. Rating: high for Isa 63 to Rev 19; low–moderate for the first link; moderate at most for the whole as a thematic canonical reading.** Edom judged connects them, not wording. Offer it as biblical-theological reflection, with the contrast stated (many deliverers against the one who treads alone), and keep the claim's own caveat. It must not be presented as Obad 21's own direction.


### B9. Who drank at Obad 16?

**Tested.** Plural forms in the book; all 71 verses of שָׁתָה ("drink") in the Prophets, Lamentations, Psalms and Job, classed by what liquid or wrath word stands within three verses; forms of the "as … so" sentence; Greek and BHS.

**Counts and corrections.**
- כּוֹס ("cup") is absent from Obadiah; Greek likewise has no ποτήριον ("cup").
- גּוֹרָל ("lot") with "drink": Joel 4:3 only.
- "Only plural you in the book" is **not exact**. קוּמוּ ("rise!", v. 1) is a masculine plural imperative, addressed to the nations. תִּשְׁלַחְנָה (v. 13, "you shall stretch out") is formally feminine plural, though BHS corrects it. Correct form: "the only plural you in a finite indicative; every address to Edom elsewhere is singular, apart from the form in v. 13 that BHS corrects."

**Pattern.**
- Every other wrath use has a named cup, wine or wrath within three verses: Isa 51:17, 22; Jer 25:16, 26, 27, 28; 49:12; 51:7; Ezek 23:32, 34; Hab 2:16; Ps 75:9; Job 6:4; 21:20. Fourteen of fourteen.
- Absolute "drink" as wrath is well attested (Jer 25:16, 26, 27; Hab 2:16), but only with a cup nearby. Obad 16 is the only wrath use with nothing nearby, and this applies to the undisputed second half (nations drink, stagger, vanish) as much as to the first.
- So absence of כּוֹס ("cup") cannot argue against (b), since the sentence's own sense needs wrath without a cup anyway.
- Revelling use without liquid is almost always paired with אָכַל ("eat"): Isa 21:5, 65:13, Jer 22:15, Zech 7:6. Obad 16 has no "eat". Amos 4:1, "bring, that we may drink", is the closest: oppressors on הַר שֹׁמְרוֹן ("Mount Samaria"). Amos 2:8 has wine in God's house. Joel 4:3 (lots, then wine and drinking), with יַדּוּ גוֹרָל ("cast lots") found only at Joel 4:3, Obad 11 and Nah 3:10, is the best support for reading (a).
- לָעוּ ("they will swallow"/"be rash"): only Obad 16 and Job 6:3. Job 6:4 next says of God's arrows חֲמָתָם שֹׁתָה רוּחִי ("my spirit drinks their venom", the word also meaning "wrath"). That small contact points to wrath for the second half.

**The sentence.**
- "As you did, so to you" (v. 15, Lev 24:19, Judg 1:7, Jer 50:15) keeps one party in both limbs, favouring (a) if v. 16 is parallel to v. 15.
- But "as … so" with a change of party is also normal: Isa 10:11, Jer 7:14, Ezek 12:11, 24:22.
- The grammar helps (b): if "you plural" were Edom and allies, they are mostly the same people as "all the nations" and yet are addressed and then spoken of in the third person. A plural contrasting with "all the nations" fits Judah better.
- Against (b): no marker of an address to Judah, in a book that otherwise addresses only Edom and (v. 1) the nations.
- Transfer of the cup, in Jer 25:28–29, 49:12, Isa 51:17–23, Lam 4:21–22, always goes from Jerusalem to others. Jer 49:12, in the very oracle Obadiah shares, has that logic: those not sentenced drink, and so will you.

**Versions.**
- The Hebrew plural is firm: the BHS apparatus has no note on שְׁתִיתֶם ("you drank").
- Greek has ἔπιες ("you drank", singular) in both Swete and Rahlfs, making Edom the drinker. It may simply harmonise with vv. 2–15's singular; the plural is the harder reading.
- Rahlfs adds οἶνον ("wine") after "all the nations"; Swete lacks "all the nations wine" altogether. That gives Greek reading a carousal colour.
- Many manuscripts have סָבִיב ("around") for תָּמִיד ("continually"), BHS. Compare Zech 12:2.
- [S] As I recall, the Targum has "as you rejoiced" over the holy mountain and the Vulgate keeps the plural. Neither moves the verdict.
- The early versions show that the verse was already read with Edom as subject, without settling the Hebrew.

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Counts verified | Mostly; "only plural you" needs correction |
| Baseline | Wrath use without a cup is unique, but this affects both readings |
| Sense | (a) needs the verb to change sense between limbs; (b) keeps one sense but needs an unmarked change of addressee |
| Alternative explanation | Wordplay on both senses is possible |

**Verdict: Uncertain. Lean: slightly to (b), as previously, with low–moderate confidence.** The argument that holds is not the absence of "cup" but the plural contrasting with "all the nations", the single sense of the verb, and Jer 49:12's logic. Joel 4:3, Amos 2:8 and the Greek keep (a) alive. A preacher should say both are defensible and not build on either.

### New candidates

- **Joel 3:5 with Obad 14, 17, 18.** The only verse with both פְּלֵיטָה ("escape") and שָׂרִיד ("survivor"), with "on Mount Zion", and carrying כַּאֲשֶׁר אָמַר יְהוָה ("as the LORD has said"). That formula is the nearest thing to a marked citation in this field and may bear on direction between Joel and Obadiah; 2 Kgs 19:31 and Isa 37:32 are rival sources. Worth its own audit.
- **Joel 4:19 with Obad 10, 12.** חָמָס ("violence") with Edom: the only such verse, also with "sons of Judah". A stronger handoff in the Greek order, where Joel precedes Obadiah.
- **Judg 2:16, 18 and Neh 9:27 behind Obad 21.** "Deliverers … to judge" as the Judges pattern, a nearer model than Isa 63.
- **Isa 14:13–15 with Obad 3–4.** Said in the heart, stars, brought down: a closer model for Obadiah's pluses against Jeremiah than anything in Jer 49.
- **Job 39:27–28 with Obad 3–4 and Jer 49:16.** Make high, eagle, nest, rock, dwell. Ranks fifth for Obadiah (WLC) and sits in the highest windows outside Jer 49. Wisdom's eagle may be a common stock for both prophets.
- **Num 20:14 with Obad 10.** "Your brother Israel" and "your brother Jacob": unique forms of one address to Edom.
- **Zech 12:2 with Obad 16.** A reeling bowl for all the peoples *around*, set in Jerusalem, joined to the manuscripts' סָבִיב ("around") at Obad 16. It suits reading (b).
- **Amos 4:1 with Obad 16.** Oppressors drinking on a mount, without liquid or "eat": the best parallel for (a) apart from Joel 4:3.
- **Greek Mal 4:1 with Obad 11, 18.** Foreigners as stubble: a Greek-only link that the Hebrew reading misses.
