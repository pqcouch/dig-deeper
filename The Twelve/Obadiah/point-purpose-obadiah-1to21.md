## The Scope Decision

**One sermon on Obadiah 1–21.** This is the overview's ⭐ HIGH single unit. Twenty-one verses make one oracle in three movements: the sentence (vv. 1–9), the charge (vv. 10–14) and the day (vv. 15–21). Its force comes from holding the three together. The sentence is passed before the reason is given, and the reason is answered by the outcome `[S: overview v2.1]`. A series would let a congregation hear vv. 1–9 as a sermon on pride, which is the book's first trap.

**What one sermon has to carry.** The full arc, including v. 18. What it can leave light: the place-names of vv. 19–20 (though not their verb; see Movement 4), the Jeremiah question, and the crux at v. 20.

**Governing versions used.** The dig's Christological Reading rates "one Saviour from Edom" (Isa 63) at moderate to high. Its v1.1 section and the overview v2.1 bring that down to low–moderate, a reflection only, with no commentator linking it to Obad 21. **This plan builds from the lower rating.** On v. 16 it uses the dig's v1.3 rating and the overview's v2.1 note: a thin lean to Judah, not something to preach from. On Joel 3:5 it uses audit 2's direction (B7): a slight lean to Joel as the later text, moderate.

## 1. Fallen Condition Focus

**When those who should have stood with us join in on our worst day, and nothing seems to happen to them, we doubt that God has seen. We are tempted either to despair or to take justice into our own hands. And we forget that we have stood aloof ourselves, and enjoyed another's fall.**

- **Anchor in the text:**
  - מֵחֲמַס אָחִיךָ יַעֲקֹב ("because of violence to your brother Jacob", v. 10);
  - eight prohibitions over eight day-phrases (vv. 12–14);
  - כַּאֲשֶׁר עָשִׂיתָ יֵעָשֶׂה לָּךְ ("as you have done, it will be done to you", v. 15) `[T]`.
- **Them then.** The book's readers, most likely Judah after Jerusalem's fall in 586 BC (moderate, the leading reading). The date is contested: the reign of Jehoram and a later setting are also live, and Edom's part in 586 is itself disputed `[S: overview v2.1]`. As the book presents it, the brother nation stood by, joined in, cut off the fugitives and handed over the survivors (vv. 11–14). The readers' question is whether the LORD has seen it, and whether the day of Jerusalem is the last word.
- **Shared:** family betrayal; the unpunished wrong; the temptation to settle it ourselves.
- **Different:** we are not a dispossessed nation, and we stand this side of the cross.
- **The second half of the FCF is the text's too.** Edom was a brother who stood מִנֶּגֶד ("aloof", v. 11), and the charge rises through rejoicing and boasting. Most hearers have been the bystander, and some have been worse `[S: dig, candidate FCF]`.

## 2. Teaching Point

**The LORD has seen every step of what was done to his people on their worst day. On his day, which is near for all nations, he will bring it back on the doer's head. And he keeps an escape on Mount Zion, where the kingdom will be his.**

- **Subject:** what does God do when a brother joins in on his brother's worst day?
- **Complement:** he sees it all, he brings it home on his day, and he keeps an escape for his people under his own reign.

**Its warrant in the dig:**
- *Headline 1:* the charge is a brother's conduct on his brother's worst day, and it escalates; high.
- *Headline 2:* talion is the book's stated principle; high on the principle.
- *Headline 4:* the book ends on who reigns, and it is not Jacob; high.
- *Convergent Findings 1, 2, 8 and 10:* brother as the moral centre; vv. 10–14 as the reason; the kingdom as destination; talion belongs to God alone.

**Christ in the Teaching Point.** Obadiah's hope is theocentric: no מֶלֶךְ ("king") and no Davidic figure stands in the book `[T]`. So the Teaching Point is kept in the book's own terms, and the sermon carries it to Christ by two lines the canon itself draws:
- **The escape on Zion.** Joel 3:5 (EVV 2:32) and Obad 17 share the ordered phrase תִּהְיֶה פְלֵיטָה ("there will be escape"); no other verse has it `[T]`. Joel's verse opens with כֹּל אֲשֶׁר־יִקְרָא ("whoever calls") on the name of the LORD, and the New Testament quotes that half-verse at Acts 2:21 and Rom 10:13 `[T]`. The line from Obadiah through Joel to the apostles is `[I]`.
- **The kingdom.** "The kingdom will be the LORD's" (v. 21) becomes "the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ" (Rev 11:15) `[S: Jones, Bridger, Patterson–Hill]`. High on the trajectory; it is not a quotation.

A one-line version for the pulpit:

> *The LORD saw it all; his day will bring it home; and on his mountain there is escape, under his King.*

---

## 3. Purpose Statement

**To assure God's people that he has seen every step of the wrong done to them and will bring it home on his day, so that, leaving justice with the King and repenting of every Edom-like pleasure in another's fall, they take refuge on the mountain where there is escape, which is open even to Edom's children.**

The one purpose is assurance that leads to refuge. The turn on the bystander is part of the repentance that refuge requires, not a second aim. It comes in under v. 15, where "all the nations" includes the hearer.

| Hearer | How the one purpose lands |
|---|---|
| The wounded (betrayed by family or by a church) | Assurance that God saw in detail, *without* being hurried to forgive and forget; and release from carrying the vengeance themselves (v. 15 is God's act; Prov 24:17–18, 29; Rom 12:19) |
| The comfortable bystander | Recognition, as part of the same refuge. Standing aloof is the first step of the eight, and gloating is the crime charged (v. 12); then repentance and escape |
| The believer | Confidence that the day is near and the kingdom has an owner, and so freedom from both despair and revenge. Sent: the door is open even to Edom's children, so believers carry the word to those who wronged them |
| The unbeliever | Height is no protection (vv. 3–4); the day comes on *all* nations (v. 15); but there is escape on Zion (v. 17) for whoever calls on the name of the Lord (Joel 3:5; Rom 10:13) |

**Gospel motive.** The sermon does not end on "don't gloat". It ends on the announcement of v. 21 and the King it points to, and on the escape that God, not the hearer, provides.

## 4. Candidate Big Question

> **When the people who should have stood with you joined in on your worst day — has God seen it, and what will he do?**

A sharper second form, if you want the turn on the hearer from the start:

> *Who will bring them down? — and which side of that day have you stood on?*

Both are candidates. The final wording is yours. The first is closer to the FCF and to the one Purpose. The second borrows Edom's own question, מִי יוֹרִדֵנִי אָרֶץ ("who will bring me down to earth?", v. 3), and lets the sermon answer it; but it puts the subordinate turn at the front.

## 5. Seven-Second Sermon

| | Them then | Us now |
|---|---|---|
| **Question?** | Jerusalem has fallen, and the brother nation stood by, joined in, cut off the fugitives and now holds the land. Has the LORD seen it, and is the day of our ruin the last word? | When those who should have stood with us joined in on our worst day, and nothing seems to happen to them, has God seen it, and what will he do? |
| **Answer!** | The LORD has seen every step. His day is near on all the nations, and it will bring every deed back on the doer's head. On Mount Zion there will be escape, and the kingdom will be the LORD's. | *(the same)* |
| **So what…** | Do not despair, and do not take up Edom's sword: the day is the LORD's. Look to Zion, where escape is kept and the kingdom is his. | Leave justice with the King who has come. The "them then" column follows the leading reading of the date (586, moderate). If you preach the date as open, say "the book's first readers" and keep the rest.

## 6. Proposed Structure

Four movements, following the book's own three blocks. The last block is split at v. 19, as the ESV's own headings split it (vv. 10, 15, 19).

| Movement | Verses | Title | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–9 | **"Who will bring me down?"** | The verdict before the reason: the self-secure brought down |
| 2 | 10–14 | **On your brother's day** | The charge, step by step: God saw it all; and we have stood there too |
| 3 | 15–18 | **As you have done** | The day near for all: justice is the LORD's, and there is escape on Zion for whoever calls |
| 4 | 19–21 | **The kingdom shall be the LORD's** | The end of the story: possession reversed, God's reign, the King who has come, the door open even to Edom |

**Movement 1 (vv. 1–9): "Who will bring me down?"**
- Edom sits high in the rock and asks מִי יוֹרִדֵנִי ("who will bring me down?"), and the LORD answers at once: מִשָּׁם אוֹרִידְךָ ("from there I will bring you down", vv. 3–4) `[T]`. The question is Obadiah's alone; Jer 49 has the sentence without it `[S: synoptic run]`.
- The thieves leave something and the grape-gatherers leave gleanings, but Edom will be searched out, betrayed by its own allies, its wise men gone (vv. 5–9).
- **Leave the hearer asking why.** The verdict comes before the reason, and pride is the condition, not the charge.
- *Hinge:* v. 10 opens with מֵחֲמַס ("because of violence"). Do not answer the "why" before v. 10.

**Movement 2 (vv. 10–14): On your brother's day.**
- The reason: violence to "your brother Jacob" (v. 10). אָח ("brother") stands only at vv. 10 and 12, at the head of the charge `[T]`.
- **"Brother" is a Torah word before it is a moral one.** Israel was told to treat Edom as a brother: "Thus says your brother Israel" (Num 20:14); the sons of Esau are "your brothers", and their land is not to be taken (Deut 2:4–8); "you shall not abhor an Edomite, for he is your brother" (Deut 23:8, EVV 23:7) `[T]`. Amos had already charged Edom with pursuing "his brother with the sword" (Amos 1:11) `[T]`. Edom has done to Jacob what Israel was commanded never to do to Edom. One sentence is enough.
- **Walk the eight steps:** stood aloof → like one of them → gloated → rejoiced → boasted → entered → looted → stood at the crossroads to cut off fugitives and hand over survivors `[T]`.
- **God's eye is in the grammar.** Eight times "in the day of…" (vv. 12–14): nothing in that day escaped his notice. That is the comfort to the wounded.
- **The turn on the hearer:** standing aloof is the first step, and gloating the second `[S: dig, Pitfalls]`. Keep it brief here; it is landed in Movement 3, under "all the nations".
- *The force of "do not":* see Keep Honest 3. The ESV prints "do not"; the events are past by v. 11 (moderate–high). Most commentators hear the prophet putting himself back into the day and crying "Stop!" `[S: Hadjiev, Baker]`. For a sermon, that reading and Keil's lasting prohibition come to nearly the same thing `[S: Finley, through Patterson–Hill]`.

**Movement 3 (vv. 15–18): As you have done.**
- **v. 15 is the hinge.** "The day of the LORD is near upon *all* the nations" (v. 15a) opens the wider horizon; "as you have done, it will be done to you" (v. 15b, singular) closes the case against Edom. Edom's eight "days" are answered by one Day. That lets the sermon turn from Edom to all the nations, and so to us, without a jump. (The verse's halves are a known seam; Block and Baker keep it as it stands `[S: overview v2.1]`.)
- **Talion is the LORD's act** (v. 15), stated in words that Prov 24:29 forbids on a human tongue. Gloating at Edom's fall would be doing what Edom did `[T; S: dig]`. This is where the bystander turn lands.
- **Do not duck v. 18:** fire, flame, stubble, no survivor, "for the LORD has spoken". Without it the comfort to the wounded is empty. Its word שָׂרִיד ("survivor") is the one Edom handed over at v. 14 `[T]`.
- **Against it stands v. 17:** "in Mount Zion there shall be those who escape". The same root as Edom's victims, the פְּלִיטָיו ("his fugitives", v. 14), whom Edom hunted, now found on Zion `[T]`.
- **The gospel turn**, along the canon's own line:
  - The escape on Zion is not a closed door. Joel 3:5 (EVV 2:32) has the same phrase, תִּהְיֶה פְלֵיטָה ("there will be escape"), and these are the only two verses that have it `[T]`. Joel adds כַּאֲשֶׁר אָמַר יְהוָה ("as the LORD has said"), and audit 2 leans, moderately, to Joel as the later text, recalling a word already spoken `[S: audit 2, B7]`. Joel opens the verse with כֹּל אֲשֶׁר־יִקְרָא ("whoever calls") on the name of the LORD. The dig calls this "a major control on how vv. 17–21 are preached" `[I, moderate; S: dig]`.
  - Peter preaches that half-verse at Pentecost (Acts 2:21), and Paul writes it after "there is no distinction between Jew and Greek" (Rom 10:12–13), with the Lord Jesus as the name called on `[T]`.
  - So, in support: "your deeds shall return on your own head" (v. 15) is the law every hearer stands under; for those in Christ, what should return on us returned on him (Isa 53:6; 2 Cor 5:21) `[I, moderate; S: Bridger, BST 66–68]`.
  - *Optional, as a reflection only:* Hebrews warns against being like Esau (Heb 12:16–17), tells believers they "have come to Mount Zion" (12:22), and ends on "the sprinkled blood that speaks a better word than the blood of Abel" (12:24), the first brother's blood that cried out from the ground (Gen 4:10). It answers the wounded hearer's cry for justice. Hebrews does not cite Obadiah; the dig lists Heb 12:16–17 under "Brothers and the land" at moderate `[S: dig]`, and vv. 22–24 are a further reflection `[I, low–moderate]`.
- *Hinge:* v. 17's "escape" is what connects judgement to good news. Hold it.

**Movement 4 (vv. 19–21): The kingdom shall be the LORD's.**
- **Possession reversed.** Keep the place-names light: v. 20 is textually damaged `[T: BHS]`, and the map is no modern border. But give the verb one clear sentence: יָרַשׁ ("possess") stands four times in vv. 17, 19 (twice) and 20, with the noun מוֹרָשֵׁיהֶם ("their possessions") in v. 17 `[T: WLC lemma index]`. Those who were dispossessed possess, and Mount Esau is possessed. The Greek of v. 17 already reads "those who dispossessed them" (κατακληρονομήσαντας, Rahlfs), as the Murabbaʿat scroll probably does.
- **Saviours going up to Zion "to rule Mount Esau"** (v. 21; see the pulpit-text note on "judge").
- **The last line is the point:** וְהָיְתָה לַיהוָה הַמְּלוּכָה ("and the kingdom shall be the LORD's"). The book ends on who reigns, and it is not Jacob `[T]`.
- **To Christ, in order.** First the announcement: the King has come, and "the kingdom of God is at hand" (Mark 1:15); he reigns by way of the cross preached in Movement 3. Then the end: "the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ" (Rev 11:15).
- **Even Edom.** James quotes Amos 9:12, the verse that ends the book before Obadiah in the Hebrew order, in a Greek that reads "that the remnant of *mankind* may seek the Lord" where the Hebrew has "that they may possess the remnant of *Edom*" (Acts 15:17) `[T for the texts; I for the connection]`. (Idumea also appears among the crowds coming to Jesus, Mark 3:8: a passing aside, no more.)
- **Land the Purpose Statement:**
  - leave justice with the King;
  - come to the mountain of escape, calling on his name;
  - and go: the door is open even to the children of the brother who stood aloof, so carry the word to your own Edoms.

## 7. Keep Honest

1. **Not a sermon on pride.**
   - The besetting misreading is vv. 2–4 as "pride goes before a fall". Pride is the condition; the charge is violence to a brother (v. 10).
   - Hold the "why" until v. 10, and do not stop at v. 11's standing aloof either: walk all eight steps `[S: dig, Pitfalls 1–2]`.
2. **Neither duck the judgement nor relish it.**
   - v. 18 must be read and owned; without it the comfort is empty.
   - But in a congregation with real grievances, "they'll get what's coming" said with satisfaction is Edom's crime (v. 12). The justice of v. 15 is the LORD's.
   - Say both, in that order `[S: dig, Pitfalls 3–4]`.
3. **Say what is written, and decide openly where the text is contested.**
   - vv. 12–14 read "do not", though the events are past. Say which way you take it.
   - v. 16 has no כּוֹס ("cup"), so say "drink". Who drank (Edom, or Judah) is genuinely uncertain, with a thin lean to Judah. **Build nothing on it**; one sentence is enough `[S: audit 2; Logos round 2]`.
   - v. 17's "their own possessions" (ESV) is probably not the best text (see the pulpit-text note).
4. **No map and no cipher.**
   - Edom is not a modern people, vv. 19–20 are not a modern border, and Esau is not "the flesh".
   - Herod's descent is not the New Testament's argument, and Rom 9 is not the key: אָהֵב ("love") and שָׂנֵא ("hate") are absent from the book, whose charge is conduct.
   - Go to Christ by the escape (v. 17 → Joel 3:5 → Acts 2:21; Rom 10:13) and the kingdom (v. 21 → Mark 1:15; Rev 11:15), not by the Isaiah 63 chain. Obad 21 to Isa 63 is low–moderate, a reflection that no commentator draws `[S: audit 1; overview v2.1]`.
5. **Say which text, and how strong the link.**
   - **Acts 15:17 follows the Greek of Amos 9:12**, not the Hebrew. Do not say that Amos's Hebrew promises the nations will seek the Lord. Say that the apostles read the Greek of the verse just before Obadiah and found in it the Gentiles coming in.
   - **Do not claim the Twelve was arranged to make these links.** Amos 9:12 as a designed handoff to Obadiah is low–moderate; as a contact it is moderate `[S: overview v2.1]`. Joel 3:5's direction is a moderate lean, not a fact.
   - **Jonah, the next book, is a thematic guard only.** No lexical handoff was found `[S: overview v2.1]`. If you use it ("the nations are judged here, and a nation is spared in Jonah, to a prophet's anger"), present it as the reader's next page, not as Obadiah's design.

## Anti-Moralism Check

- **"Be like X" or "trust the One X points to"?** The payoff is not "don't gloat". It is that the LORD has seen, will act, keeps an escape for whoever calls, and reigns. The imperatives (stop gloating; stand with your brother; leave vengeance; carry the word) come as fruit of that, and as repentance for having been Edom. **Pass.**
- **Is God the subject?** In the Teaching Point, yes: he sees, brings home, keeps and reigns. Christ comes in as the name called on for escape and as the King of v. 21's kingdom. **Pass.**
- **Is the good news grounded in saving work?** Yes. The escape of v. 17 is carried, by Joel 3:5 and its apostolic use, to the gospel Peter preached at Pentecost, the death and resurrection of the Lord on whom men call; the Bridger line on the returning deeds supports it. **Pass.**
- **Independent review.** This version incorporates an independent two-framework review (PT and Sailhamer), so the checks above are no longer only self-checks.

## Pulpit-Text Note (ESV)

Where the ESV and the NASB95 (or the Hebrew) diverge on a point the plan rests on:

| Verse | ESV | NASB95 / Hebrew | What the congregation will hear, and what to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5–6 | "how you **have been** destroyed… How Esau **has been** pillaged" | "will be ruined… will be ransacked" | Edom's fall as already done. Fine for Movement 1, if you say once that the prophet sees it as done |
| 7 | "**you** have no understanding" | אֵין תְּבוּנָה בּוֹ ("there is no understanding in him") | A small shift to second person. Only matters if you link it to v. 8's "understanding" |
| 12–14 | "do not…" ×8 | "do not…" ×8 | **Agree.** The tension with v. 11's past tense is in the Hebrew too (Keep Honest 3) |
| 16 | "as **you** have drunk" | שְׁתִיתֶם ("you [plural] drank") | The plural cannot be heard in English; nothing in the plan depends on it |
| 17 | "possess **their own** possessions" | מוֹרָשֵׁיהֶם ("their possessions"); likely text "those who dispossessed them" | "Own" is the ESV's addition. The oldest Hebrew manuscript, the Murabbaʿat scroll, probably reads "those who dispossessed them" (moderate), and the Greek agrees. Do not build on "own"; Movement 4's sentence on יָרַשׁ ("possess") works with either reading |
| 21 | "to **rule** Mount Esau" | לִשְׁפֹּט ("to judge") | The Judges echo is inaudible. Say "to judge — the ESV has 'rule'" if you want the deliverers of Judges in view; the plan does not need it |

**No divergence bears on the Teaching Point's hinges.** "Your brother" (vv. 10, 12), "as you have done, it shall be done to you" (v. 15), "those who escape" (v. 17) and "the kingdom shall be the LORD's" (v. 21) are all clear in the ESV. The ESV's Joel 2:32 also reads "there shall be those who escape", so the v. 17 → Joel link is audible in the pew Bible, though Joel's verse number differs from the Hebrew (Joel 3:5). **The ESV's section headings** (at vv. 1, 10, 15, 19) sit at this plan's movement breaks, which will help those following in the pew Bible.

**The Greek of v. 21, for reference only.** Where the Hebrew has מוֹשִׁעִים ("saviours") going up *on* Mount Zion, the Greek has men *saved* going up *from* Mount Zion: ἄνδρες σεσῳσμένοι ("men who have been saved", Rahlfs) or ἀνασωζόμενοι ("those being saved", Swete). Not a pulpit point.

**For another pulpit (NIV84):**
- vv. 12–14 read "You should not look down on your brother… nor rejoice… nor boast…". That decides the past sense in English, and it removes the "Stop!" of the Hebrew's prohibitions. Movement 2 would need a sentence on what is written.
- v. 17 has "deliverance" (the abstract noun, nearer the Hebrew than the ESV's "those who escape") and "its inheritance".
- v. 21 has "Deliverers… to govern", with "on [or from] Mount Zion" in a footnote.

**Not taken from the review as first written.** The review's first draft said Joel "supplies" Obadiah's vocabulary and that the Twelve's seams were deliberate design. The dig and audit 2 had already rated these more cautiously: Joel 3:5 leans later than Obad 17 (moderate), Joel 4 is a cumulative contact (moderate–high) with no direction claimed, Amos 9:12 as design is low–moderate, and Jonah is a thematic neighbour only. This version follows the audits. The route to Acts 2:21 and Rom 10:13 stands whichever way the dependence runs.

**What v1.0 had missed.** The dig called Joel 3:5's "whoever calls" "a major control on how vv. 17–21 are preached" (moderate), but v1.0 did not carry it into the plan.
