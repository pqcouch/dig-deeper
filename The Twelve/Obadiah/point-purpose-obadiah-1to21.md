# Point and Purpose: Obadiah 1–21

**Scope:** one sermon on the whole book
**Pulpit text:** ESV (your own church). Other versions can be adapted where needed; see the pulpit-text note.
**Study text:** NASB95
**Date:** 9 November 2026
**Built from:** `dig-deeper-obadiah-1-1to21-2026-11-04` v1.3 (Headline Findings, So What, candidate FCF, Christological Reading, Preaching Pitfalls, read through its governing *What Changed* sections v1.1–v1.3); `book-overview-obadiah-2026-11-04` v2.1; claim audits 1 and 2; Logos rounds 1 and 2; the Jeremiah 49 synoptic run v1.1.

## The Scope Decision

**One sermon on Obadiah 1–21.** This is the overview's ⭐ HIGH single unit. Twenty-one verses make one oracle in three movements: the sentence (vv. 1–9), the charge (vv. 10–14) and the day (vv. 15–21). Its force comes from holding the three together. The sentence is passed before the reason is given, and the reason is answered by the outcome `[S: overview v2.1]`. A series would let a congregation hear vv. 1–9 as a sermon on pride, which is the book's first trap.

**What one sermon has to carry.** The full arc, including v. 18. What it can leave light: the land list of vv. 19–20, the Jeremiah question, and the crux at v. 20.

**Governing versions used.** The dig's Christological Reading rates "one Saviour from Edom" (Isa 63) at moderate to high. Its v1.1 section and the overview v2.1 bring that down to low–moderate, a reflection only, with no commentator linking it to Obad 21. **This plan builds from the lower rating.** On v. 16 it uses the dig's v1.3 rating and the overview's v2.1 note: a thin lean to Judah, not something to preach from.

## 1. Fallen Condition Focus

**When those who should have stood with us join in on our worst day, and nothing seems to happen to them, we doubt that God has seen. We are tempted either to despair or to take justice into our own hands. And we forget that we have stood aloof ourselves, and enjoyed another's fall.**

- **Anchor in the text:**
  - מֵחֲמַס אָחִיךָ יַעֲקֹב ("because of violence to your brother Jacob", v. 10);
  - eight prohibitions over eight day-phrases (vv. 12–14);
  - כַּאֲשֶׁר עָשִׂיתָ יֵעָשֶׂה לָּךְ ("as you have done, it will be done to you", v. 15) `[T]`.
- **Shared:** family betrayal; the unpunished wrong; the temptation to settle it ourselves.
- **Different:** we are not a dispossessed nation, and we stand this side of the cross.
- **The second half of the FCF is the text's too.** Edom was a brother who stood מִנֶּגֶד ("aloof", v. 11), and the charge rises through rejoicing and boasting. Most hearers have been the bystander, and some have been worse `[S: dig, candidate FCF]`.

---

## 2. Teaching Point

**The LORD has seen every step of what was done to his people on their worst day. On his day, which is near for all nations, he will bring it back on the doer's head. And he keeps an escape on Mount Zion, where the kingdom will be his.**

- **Subject:** what does God do when a brother joins in on his brother's worst day?
- **Complement:** he sees it all, he brings it home on his day, and he keeps an escape for his people under his own reign.

**Its warrant in the dig:**
- *Headline 1:* the charge is a brother's conduct on his brother's worst day, and it escalates; high.
- *Headline 2:* talion is the book's stated principle; high on the principle.
- *Headline 4:* the book ends on who reigns, and it is not Jacob; high.
- *Convergent Findings 1, 2, 8 and 10:* brother as the moral centre; vv. 10–14 as the reason; the kingdom as destination; talion belongs to God alone.

**Christ in the Teaching Point.** Obadiah's hope is theocentric: no מֶלֶךְ ("king") and no Davidic figure stands in the book `[T]`. So the Teaching Point is kept in the book's own terms, and the sermon carries it to Christ along the line the canon itself draws. "The kingdom will be the LORD's" (v. 21) becomes "the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ" (Rev 11:15) `[S: Jones, Bridger, Patterson–Hill]`. That line is high on the trajectory; it is not a quotation. A one-line version for the pulpit:

> *The LORD saw it all; his day will bring it home; and on his mountain there is escape, under his King.*

---

## 3. Purpose Statement

**To assure those who have been betrayed that God has seen every step and will act, so that they can leave justice with him rather than take it. To uncover in every hearer the Edom who stands aloof, rejoices at another's fall and profits by it, and to send them to the mountain where there is escape. And to lift their eyes to the kingdom that is the LORD's and is given to Christ, into which even Edom's children are called.**

| Hearer | What the sermon seeks |
|---|---|
| The wounded (betrayed by family or by a church) | Assurance that God saw in detail, *without* being hurried to forgive and forget; and release from carrying the vengeance themselves (v. 15 is God's act; Prov 24:17–18, 29; Rom 12:19) |
| The comfortable bystander | Recognition. Standing aloof is the first step of the eight, and gloating is the crime charged (v. 12); then repentance |
| The believer | Confidence that the day is near and the kingdom has an owner, and so freedom from both despair and revenge |
| The unbeliever | Height is no protection (vv. 3–4); the day comes on *all* nations (v. 15); but there is escape on Zion (v. 17), and Idumeans came to Jesus (Mark 3:8) |

**Gospel motive.** The sermon does not end on "don't gloat". It ends on the announcement of v. 21 and the King it points to, and on the escape that God, not the hearer, provides.

---

## 4. Candidate Big Question

> **When the people who should have stood with you joined in on your worst day — has God seen it, and what will he do?**

A sharper second form, if you want the turn on the hearer from the start:

> *Who will bring them down? — and which side of that day have you stood on?*

Both are candidates. The final wording is yours. The first is closer to the FCF. The second borrows Edom's own question, מִי יוֹרִדֵנִי אָרֶץ ("who will bring me down to earth?", v. 3), and lets the sermon answer it.

## 5. Proposed Structure

Four movements, following the book's own three blocks. The last block is split at v. 19, as the ESV's own headings split it (vv. 10, 15, 19).

| Movement | Verses | Title | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–9 | **"Who will bring me down?"** | The verdict before the reason: the self-secure brought down |
| 2 | 10–14 | **On your brother's day** | The charge, step by step: God saw it all; and we have stood there too |
| 3 | 15–18 | **As you have done** | The day near for all: justice is the LORD's, and there is escape on Zion |
| 4 | 19–21 | **The kingdom shall be the LORD's** | The end of the story: God's reign, given to Christ, open even to Edom |

**Movement 1 (vv. 1–9): "Who will bring me down?"**
- Edom sits high in the rock and asks מִי יוֹרִדֵנִי ("who will bring me down?"), and the LORD answers at once: מִשָּׁם אוֹרִידְךָ ("from there I will bring you down", vv. 3–4) `[T]`. The question is Obadiah's alone; Jer 49 has the sentence without it `[S: synoptic run]`.
- The thieves leave something and the grape-gatherers leave gleanings, but Edom will be searched out, betrayed by its own allies, its wise men gone (vv. 5–9).
- **Leave the hearer asking why.** The verdict comes before the reason, and pride is the condition, not the charge.
- *Hinge:* v. 10 opens with מֵחֲמַס ("because of violence"). Do not answer the "why" before v. 10.

**Movement 2 (vv. 10–14): On your brother's day.**
- The reason: violence to "your brother Jacob" (v. 10). אָח ("brother") stands only at vv. 10 and 12, at the head of the charge `[T]`.
- **Walk the eight steps:** stood aloof → like one of them → gloated → rejoiced → boasted → entered → looted → stood at the crossroads to cut off fugitives and hand over survivors `[T]`.
- **God's eye is in the grammar.** Eight times "in the day of…" (vv. 12–14): nothing in that day escaped his notice. That is the comfort to the wounded.
- **The turn on the hearer:** standing aloof is the first step, and gloating the second `[S: dig, Pitfalls]`.
- *The force of "do not":* see Keep Honest 3. The ESV prints "do not"; the events are past by v. 11 (moderate–high). Most commentators hear the prophet putting himself back into the day and crying "Stop!" `[S: Hadjiev, Baker]`. For a sermon, that reading and Keil's lasting prohibition come to nearly the same thing `[S: Finley, through Patterson–Hill]`.

**Movement 3 (vv. 15–18): As you have done.**
- "The day of the LORD is near upon *all* the nations" (v. 15). Edom is judged as one of the nations, and so are we.
- **Talion is the LORD's act** (v. 15), stated in words that Prov 24:29 forbids on a human tongue. Gloating at Edom's fall would be doing what Edom did `[T; S: dig]`.
- **Do not duck v. 18:** fire, flame, stubble, no survivor, "for the LORD has spoken". Without it the comfort to the wounded is empty. Its word שָׂרִיד ("survivor") is the one Edom handed over at v. 14 `[T]`.
- **Against it stands v. 17:** "in Mount Zion there shall be those who escape". The same root as Edom's victims, the פְּלִיטָיו ("his fugitives", v. 14), whom Edom hunted, now found on Zion `[T]`.
- **The gospel turn**, offered as a theological reading, not as Obadiah's statement:
  - "your deeds shall return on your own head" (v. 15) is the law every hearer stands under;
  - for those in Christ, what should return on us returned on him (Isa 53:6; 2 Cor 5:21) `[I, moderate; S: Bridger, BST 66–68]`;
  - the escape on the holy mountain is where that is found.
- *Hinge:* v. 17's "escape" is what connects judgement to good news. Hold it.

**Movement 4 (vv. 19–21): The kingdom shall be the LORD's.**
- **Land restored, south to north,** and saviours going up to Zion "to rule Mount Esau" (vv. 19–21). Keep it light: v. 20 is textually damaged `[T: BHS]`, and the map is no modern border.
- **The last line is the point:** וְהָיְתָה לַיהוָה הַמְּלוּכָה ("and the kingdom shall be the LORD's"). The book ends on who reigns, and it is not Jacob `[T]`.
- **To Christ:** the kingdom becomes "the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ" (Rev 11:15). Edom's own story turns too:
  - Idumea appears once in the New Testament, among the crowds coming to Jesus (Mark 3:8);
  - James reads Amos 9:12, the verse Obadiah unfolds, as the nations seeking the Lord (Acts 15:17) `[T for the texts; I for the connection]`.
- **Land the Purpose Statement:**
  - leave justice with the King;
  - come to the mountain of escape;
  - and know that the door is open even to the children of the brother who stood aloof.

## 6. Keep Honest

1. **Not a sermon on pride.**
   - The besetting misreading is vv. 2–4 as "pride goes before a fall". Pride is the condition; the charge is violence to a brother (v. 10).
   - Hold the "why" until v. 10, and do not stop at v. 11's standing aloof either: walk all eight steps `[S: dig, Pitfalls 1–2]`.
2. **Neither duck the judgement nor relish it.**
   - v. 18 must be read and owned; without it the comfort is empty.
   - But in a congregation with real grievances, "they'll get what's coming" said with satisfaction is Edom's crime (v. 12). The justice of v. 15 is the LORD's.
   - Say both, in that order `[S: dig, Pitfalls 3–4]`.
3. **Say what is written, and decide openly where the text is contested.**
   - vv. 12–14 read "do not", though the events are past. Say which way you take it.
   - v. 16 has no כּוֹס ("cup"), so say "drink". Who drank (Edom, or Judah) is genuinely uncertain, with a thin lean to Judah. **Build nothing on it**; one sentence is enough `[S: audit 2; Logos round 2]`.
   - v. 17's "their own possessions" (ESV) is probably not the best text (see the pulpit-text note).
4. **No map and no cipher.**
   - Edom is not a modern people, vv. 19–20 are not a modern border, and Esau is not "the flesh".
   - Herod's descent is not the New Testament's argument, and Rom 9 is not the key: אָהֵב ("love") and שָׂנֵא ("hate") are absent from the book, whose charge is conduct.
   - Go to Christ by the kingdom (v. 21 → Rev 11:15), not by the Isaiah 63 chain. Obad 21 to Isa 63 is low–moderate, a reflection that no commentator draws `[S: audit 1; overview v2.1]`.

## Anti-Moralism Check

- **"Be like X" or "trust the One X points to"?** The payoff is not "don't gloat". It is that the LORD has seen, will act, keeps an escape, and reigns. The imperatives (stop gloating; stand with your brother; leave vengeance) come as fruit of that, and as repentance for having been Edom. **Pass.**
- **Is God the subject?** In the Teaching Point, yes: he sees, brings home, keeps and reigns. Christ comes in as the King of v. 21's kingdom and as the one in whom the returning deeds are borne. **Pass**, with the Bridger line flagged as a theological reading.
- **Is the good news grounded in saving work?** Yes, through the escape on Zion (v. 17), carried to Christ in Movement 3 and the kingdom in Movement 4. It is not merely information about God's justice. **Pass.**

## Pulpit-Text Note (ESV)

Where the ESV and the NASB95 (or the Hebrew) diverge on a point the plan rests on:

| Verse | ESV | NASB95 / Hebrew | What the congregation will hear, and what to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5–6 | "how you **have been** destroyed… How Esau **has been** pillaged" | "will be ruined… will be ransacked" | Edom's fall as already done. Fine for Movement 1, if you say once that the prophet sees it as done |
| 7 | "**you** have no understanding" | אֵין תְּבוּנָה בּוֹ ("there is no understanding in him") | A small shift to second person. Only matters if you link it to v. 8's "understanding" |
| 12–14 | "do not…" ×8 | "do not…" ×8 | **Agree.** The tension with v. 11's past tense is in the Hebrew too (Keep Honest 3) |
| 16 | "as **you** have drunk" | שְׁתִיתֶם ("you [plural] drank") | The plural cannot be heard in English; nothing in the plan depends on it |
| 17 | "possess **their own** possessions" | מוֹרָשֵׁיהֶם ("their possessions"); likely text "those who dispossessed them" | "Own" is the ESV's addition. The oldest Hebrew manuscript, the Murabbaʿat scroll, probably reads "those who dispossessed them" (moderate). Do not build on "own" |
| 21 | "to **rule** Mount Esau" | לִשְׁפֹּט ("to judge") | The Judges echo is inaudible. Say "to judge — the ESV has 'rule'" if you want the deliverers of Judges in view; the plan does not need it |

**No divergence bears on the Teaching Point's hinges.** "Your brother" (vv. 10, 12), "as you have done, it shall be done to you" (v. 15), "those who escape" (v. 17) and "the kingdom shall be the LORD's" (v. 21) are all clear in the ESV. **The ESV's section headings** (at vv. 1, 10, 15, 19) sit at this plan's movement breaks, which will help those following in the pew Bible.

**For another pulpit (NIV84):**
- vv. 12–14 read "You should not look down on your brother… nor rejoice… nor boast…". That decides the past sense in English, and it removes the "Stop!" of the Hebrew's prohibitions. Movement 2 would need a sentence on what is written.
- v. 17 has "deliverance" (the abstract noun, nearer the Hebrew than the ESV's "those who escape") and "its inheritance".
- v. 21 has "Deliverers… to govern", with "on [or from] Mount Zion" in a footnote.

## Handing On

The candidate Big Question and the four movements are ready for the one-sentence sermon and the manuscript. Three decisions are yours before writing:
- which force of vv. 12–14 you will take (the plan assumes the prophet's "Stop!" in the day);
- whether to say one sentence on v. 16's "who drank", or let "drink" stand;
- how far to take the Bridger line on the returning deeds in Movement 3. It is a theological inference, well grounded, but not Obadiah's statement.
