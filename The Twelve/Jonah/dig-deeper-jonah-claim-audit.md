# Claim Audit: Jonah

**Documents audited:** `book-overview-jonah.md` (v1.0.0) and `jonah-dig-deeper-macro-synthesis.md`, together with the four solo runs and the whole-book sweep
**Passages behind the claims:** Jonah 1:1–4:11 (complete coverage)
**Date:** 6 August 2026
**Purpose:** **Test the Jonah documents before the series is preached.** All of this material was produced in **July 2026, before the `/logos-research` skill was installed** — so the overview, the sweep, the four digs and the macro-synthesis have **never had a checking stage against the user's own library.** Several claims were explicitly flagged in those documents as "verify before preaching". This audit supplies the missing pass and tests the load-bearing claims besides.

**Method note.** As with the Exodus audit, **confirmation by the originating run was not accepted as evidence.** The whole of Jonah (48 verses) plus Joel 2:12–14 and Nahum 1:2–3 were re-verified against a public-domain text (KJV) in this session. **Three of the four Logos targets were the documents' own flagged items**; the fourth was a claim nobody had thought to question, and it is the one that moved.

---

## Phase 0.6 — Positional Necessity, applied at book level

**Question 1 — what does the preceding canonical movement require that Jonah provides?** Within the Twelve, Jonah follows Obadiah — an oracle *against* Edom — and precedes Micah and Nahum. **The sequence sets a book about mercy to Nineveh immediately after a book about judgment on a neighbour, and immediately before the book that finally destroys Nineveh.**

**Question 2 — why does the book end where it ends?** On an unanswered question (4:11), with no narratorial comment past it. **That ending is the strongest control on every claim below**: any reading that resolves the book has un-written it.

**What this surfaces about the documents.** The overview and synthesis both honour the closing question well — argument 6 is built on it. **The positional check raises a different risk, and the audit tests it: the documents lean heavily on the Twelve-level framing** (Joel's creed, Nahum's bracket), which is `[S]`-dependent and was never checked. **Claims 2 and 3 below are where the exposure is.**

### Claim 2: ⭐ Jonah 4:2 cites the creed "in the expanded form the Book of the Twelve had already given it (Joel 2:13)"

**Source:** Macro-argument 2; overview intertextual map, rows for Exod 34:6–7 and Joel 2:13–14. Current confidence: **strong** on the creed; **moderate–high** on the Joel form.
**Claim:** Jonah draws the expanded creed *from* Joel — i.e. **Joel is prior and Jonah is the borrower.**

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | **Strong** | Both are in the Twelve |
| Volume | **Strong** | Verified: Joel 2:13 and Jonah 4:2 share *"gracious… merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, and repenteth him of the evil"* almost verbatim, **including the relenting clause Exodus lacks**; and Jonah 3:9 ("Who can tell if God will turn and repent") matches Joel 2:14 ("Who knoweth if he will return and repent") |
| Recurrence | **Strong** | Two adjacent Joel verses matched by two Jonah verses |
| Thematic coherence | **Strong** | |
| History of interpretation | **Strong — and it contradicts the direction claimed** | See below |
| Satisfaction | **Strong** | |

**Verdict: ⭐ Needs reframing — the direction of dependence is backwards.**

**Reasoning.** **The verbal data is exactly as the documents describe it, and I re-verified all four verses.** What is wrong is the direction. **Schnittjer argues that the dependence runs *from Jonah to Joel*** — Exodus is the source, **Jonah the intermediate text that modifies it**, and Joel dependent on Jonah's version. The decisive evidence: **Joel lacks the phrase "from his fierce anger", which Jonah preserves from Exodus** — indicating Jonah drew directly on Exodus while Joel drew on Jonah (*Old Testament Use of Old Testament*, Zondervan Academic, 2021, 375–77).

**This does not weaken the argument — it strengthens Jonah.** On the documents' version, Jonah is quoting a formula the Twelve had already coined. On the evidence, **Jonah is the book that coined it**, and the expanded creed is Jonah's own theological contribution — which makes 4:2 not a citation of a tradition but the prophet's own reformulation, thrown back at God as a grievance. Commentators note the two books deploy the same alteration differently: **Joel to celebrate mercy toward Judah, Jonah to protest mercy toward Nineveh** (Claassens, OTL *Jonah*, 2024, 17).

**One caution.** Relative dating in the Twelve is contested territory, and Schnittjer's is an argued position rather than a consensus. **The safe formulation is the one the text supports without a dating judgement: Jonah 4:2 and Joel 2:13 share an expanded creed that Exodus does not have, and the shared clause is the interpretive key to both** — with Schnittjer's direction noted as the better-argued option.

**Action required:** Reverse the direction in the overview's intertextual map (two rows), macro-argument 2, and the synthesis's summary table. **Upgrade the Joel link from *moderate–high* to *strong*** — the verbal match is now verified, not recalled.

### Claim 3: Nahum 1:3 holds the creed's judgment half over the same city

**Source:** Overview intertextual map (flagged *"moderate–high (canonical reflection; verify)"*); macro-argument 2. Current confidence: **moderate–high, explicitly flagged for verification.**

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | **Strong** | Verified: Nahum 1:3 has *"slow to anger"* (Exod 34:6) **and** *"will not at all acquit the wicked"* (Exod 34:7) |
| Recurrence | **Strong** | The creed is the Twelve's most re-used text |
| Thematic coherence | **Strong** | Same city, opposite verdict |
| History of interpretation | **Strong** | Now sourced — see below |
| Satisfaction | **Strong** | Explains why the canon puts two Nineveh books in one collection |

**Verdict: Confirmed — and upgraded from *moderate–high* to *strong*.**

**Reasoning.** The commentators state it directly: **Nahum "overturns the emphasis in Joel, Jonah, and Micah on God's mercy from Exodus 34:6, instead focusing on the negative aspect of God exacting vengeance from Exodus 34:7"** (Yates, *Themelios* 41:2, 259); and Nahum's context "highlights the divine justice that Jonah implicitly questions, while clarifying that God's earlier clemency was simply patient grace" (Youngblood, ZECOT *Jonah*, 165–66). **The flagged "verify" is now discharged.**

**And the audit adds a text-verified sharpening the documents did not have.** Comparing the three creed-citations word by word: Exodus, Joel and Jonah all read *"abounding in steadfast love"* (*rab-ḥesed*). **Nahum 1:3 replaces that clause with "great in power" (*ûgĕdol-kōaḥ*)** and then adds the judgment half. **Nahum does not merely cite a different half of the creed — he keeps its shape and substitutes power for steadfast love.** That is a far more preachable observation than "the Twelve distributes the two halves", and it is `[T]`, verified here against both texts. *(The commentators support the emphasis-shift; the specific lexical substitution is this audit's own observation.)*

**Action required:** Upgrade to **strong**; add the *ḥesed* → *kōaḥ* substitution to the overview's Nahum row and to macro-argument 2. Remove the "verify" flag.

### Claim 4: ⭐ "That we may not perish" — the pagan hope God finally owns

**Source:** Macro-argument 4; overview echo table (rated **high**). Current claim: the "not perish" hope travels from the captain (1:6) to the king (3:9) and is **finally owned by God himself at 4:11.**

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | **Strong — but not where the documents put it** | See below |
| Recurrence | **Strong** | |
| Thematic coherence | **Strong** | |
| Satisfaction | **Strong — and stronger once corrected** | |

**Verdict: ⭐ Confirmed with nuance — and the correction materially improves the argument.**

**Reasoning.** The verb *ʾābad* ("perish") was traced through the whole book in this session. **The documents have both under-counted it and mis-placed its climax.**

- **1:6** — the captain: *"if so be that God will think upon us, that we perish not"*. ✓ (cited)
- **1:14** — the sailors, praying to the LORD: *"let us not perish for this man's life"*. **Not cited in either document.**
- **3:9** — the king: *"Who can tell… that we perish not?"* ✓ (cited)
- **4:10** — the LORD, of the plant: *"which came up in a night, and **perished** in a night"*. **Not cited in either document.**
- **4:11** — **contains no *ʾābad* at all.** The documents' climax is an inference, correctly tagged `[T]`/`[I]` in the synthesis but presented as **high** in the overview's echo table.

**So the verbal chain is 1:6 → 1:14 → 3:9 → 4:10 — four occurrences across three pericopes, complete without any inference.** And its real climax is sharper than the one claimed: **the word three pagan mouths use to plead for their lives is finally used of a plant** — the thing Jonah pitied. God's closing question (4:11) then draws the conclusion: *you cared that a plant perished; should I not care about 120,000 people?*

**A second verified detail the documents missed.** 4:10 and 4:11 share the verb *ḥûs*: *"**Thou hast had pity** on the gourd… and should not **I spare** Nineveh?"* **The same verb, Jonah's object against God's.** That pairing is the closing argument's hinge and belongs in the echo table.

**Action required:** Rewrite the argument's evidence line (add 1:14 and 4:10; remove 4:11 from the *verbal* chain and state it as the conclusion drawn from 4:10). **Retitle** from "the pagan hope God finally owns" to something like "**the word that travels from pagan lips to a withered plant**". Add the *ḥûs* pairing. Downgrade the overview echo-table row from **high** to **high on the chain, inference on 4:11** — or simply restate it correctly, which is better.

### Claim 9: The Hosea 6:2 "third day" resonance behind 1:17

**Source:** Overview intertextual map and synthesis candidate list — **rated *uncertain*, "verify before use".**

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | **Strong** | Twelve neighbour |
| Volume | **Possible** | A numeral plus a pattern, not a phrase |
| Recurrence | **Strong** | |
| Thematic coherence | **Strong** | Both concern restoration after judgment on the third day |
| History of interpretation | **Strong** | Now sourced |
| Satisfaction | **Strong** | Supplies an OT anchor for the sign of Jonah |

**Verdict: ⭐ Promote — from *uncertain* to *moderate–high*.**

**Reasoning.** The checking stage found this **positively supported, not merely permitted**: Jonah's punishment, prayer and deliverance after "three days and three nights" **alludes to Hosea 6:1–2 and applies that pattern to Jonah individually** (Chen, *Wonders from Your Law*, IVP Academic, 2024, 228–29). And the phrase carries more than chronology: *"three days and three nights" in the belly of the fish denoted death* — with the words "Jonah died" conspicuously absent, creating the expectation that death is not the end (Nixon, BST *Jonah*, 135).

**Why this matters for the series.** The sign of Jonah has always rested on Matthew 12:40 alone. **It now has an Old Testament anchor**: the death-and-third-day-raising pattern is already in the Twelve, and Jonah is written against it. **This is the audit's largest single gain for preaching.**

**Action required:** Upgrade in the overview's intertextual map and promote from the synthesis's candidate list into macro-argument 7's evidence.

**The headline: the Jonah documents are in good shape, and better than the Exodus ones were at the equivalent stage.** Six of the seven macro-arguments stand as written. **Nothing was discarded, and two claims got *stronger*** — the Nahum bracket (now sourced) and the Hosea 6:2 echo (promoted out of the candidate list).

**Two things moved, and both are worth the preacher's attention:**

1. **The Joel dependence runs the other way.** Jonah is not borrowing the Twelve's expanded creed; on the best-argued account, **Jonah coined it.** That makes 4:2 a sharper verse, not a weaker one.
2. **The "not perish" chain was under-counted and its climax mis-placed.** The documents cite two occurrences and infer a third; **there are four, and the fourth is a plant.**

**A methodological note, and it matches Exodus exactly.** Both corrections are of the same type: **a verbal chain extended by inference into a verse that does not contain the word.** The Exodus audit found the identical failure in the descent-spine (*šākan* claimed at 40:34, where it does not occur). **That is now a named failure mode of this project's book-level work** — and the cheapest guard against it is to re-verify every claimed chain against the text before it reaches an overview.
