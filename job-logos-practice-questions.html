Job: Practice Questions for the Logos Research Assistant

Purpose: these are the open questions from the Job work, written for the Research Assistant. They are drawn from: - the book overview (v0.1.1) and the sweep; - the six digs so far: 1:1–3:1, 3, 4–5, 9–10, 13–14, and the 4:1–14:22 unit dig; - the two claim audits; - the plan’s list of Logos checks; - the assessment of the first Job answers (2 October).

They follow logos-research-assistant-guidelines in the Dig Deeper folder and are laid out like the Matthew list.

Use: ask one question per turn. Set the scope to a Job commentaries collection unless the question says otherwise. Paste each answer back with its footnotes, under the question as asked. If you do not own the work a question names, ask it without the name, as you did for Matthew, and say so when you paste the answer.

Date: 3 October 2026

How the List Is Arranged

The questions are grouped by the template they practise. Within each group, the first question matters most to the work.

Each question has three parts:

  • Ask: the wording to type.
  • Why: the open point it settles, and where the answer would be used.
  • Watch for: the check from the “Reading the Answer” section of the guidelines that is most likely to matter.

Three kinds of question are left out on purpose:

  • Questions about counts, word chains or manuscript witnesses. The corpus answers those.
  • The prologue’s cross-reference patterns (Genesis 22, Zechariah 3, the plagues, “his day”). These wait with the prologue audit you deferred. When that audit is run, one question on each pattern will be added.
  • Questions already answered in the first Job round, apart from one asked again with a named tier (2.3):
    • whether the friends address God;
    • the direction of Jer 20 and Prov 8;
    • Isa 40:12.

What the Corpus Has Already Settled

Some of the plan’s “Logos checks” have been answered from the exports in _texts/logos-exports/ this week. The questions below build on these answers rather than repeat them.

  • Job 19:25–27.
    • The BHS apparatus has no note on 19:25 or 19:26.
    • At 19:27 it records only that one manuscript lacks a phrase.
    • Rahlfs, like Swete, has a different text: “for I know that everlasting is he who is about to deliver me; on earth may he raise up my skin” (ἀέναός ἐστιν ὁ ἐκλύειν με μέλλων … ἀναστήσαι τὸ δέρμα μου).
    • So the crux is one of interpretation, not of the Hebrew text, and the questions on it (1.1) go to the commentaries.
  • Job 42:6.
    • BHS has no note.
    • Rahlfs and Swete supply an object: “therefore I despised myself and melted away, and I count myself earth and ashes” (ἐφαύλισα ἐμαυτὸν).
    • Again, the question (1.2) is for the commentaries.
  • Job 9:33.
    • BHS records לוּ (“would that”) for לֹא (“there is no”) in some manuscripts, the Greek and the Syriac.
    • Swete has εἴθε ἦν ὁ μεσίτης ἡμῶν (“would that there were our mediator”).
    • The NIV84 follows לוּ.
    • Only the commentary check remains (1.3).
  • Job 13:15. BHS records that many manuscripts and the versions read the qere לוֹ (“in him”). The ketiv is לֹא (“not”).
  • The “Rahlfs asterisks” on the prologue’s Greek pluses (1:5, 1:16, 1:21, 2:8, 2:9, 2:11).
    • In chapters 1–2, the Rahlfs export prints an asterisk at one place only, 2:1b (“to present himself before the LORD”). That is Hebrew wording which the Old Greek lacked and the hexaplaric text supplied.
    • None of the six pluses is asterisked, and none could be, because an asterisk marks the opposite case.
    • The real question is whether Origen obelised the pluses. Obeli are not printed in Rahlfs; they are recorded in the Göttingen apparatus (4.6).
  • שָׂהֵד (“witness”, 16:19) occurs nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible (WLC, lemma).
  • גאל (“redeem”) in Job occurs only at 3:5 and 19:25 (WLC, lemma; the index files both under the same lemma).

1. A Named Commentator’s View

1.1 Job 19:25–26, “my Redeemer” and “from my flesh”

  • Ask: What does Clines (WBC) say about who the גֹּאֵל (“Redeemer”) of Job 19:25 is, with page references?
  • Then ask: What does Clines say about whether וּמִבְּשָׂרִי (“yet from my flesh”, 19:26) means seeing God in the body or apart from it?
  • Then ask both of: Hartley (NICOT), Delitzsch (Keil–Delitzsch) and Andersen (TOTC).
  • Why: 19:1–29 is a HIGH solo dig for Sermon 4, and the plan puts these checks first. The text and the versions are settled (see above). What is open is the identity of the Redeemer (God, a heavenly figure, or the witness of 16:19) and the force of the preposition מִן (“from”).
  • Watch for:
    • reasoning from the English capital R in “Redeemer”, or from Handel;
    • whether the preposition is argued from other uses of מִן (“from”) or only asserted.

1.2 Job 42:6, “I retract” and “dust and ashes”

  • Ask: What does Clines (WBC) say about the object of אֶמְאַס (“I despise / retract”) in Job 42:6?
  • Then ask: What does he say about the meaning of נִחַמְתִּי עַל (“I repent / am comforted concerning”) with עָפָר וָאֵפֶר (“dust and ashes”)?
  • Then ask both of: Hartley (NICOT) and Delitzsch.
  • Why: 42:6 is the crux of Sermon 8’s HIGH dig. BHS has no note, and the Greek supplies “myself” as the object.
  • Watch for: reasoning from the English “repent in dust and ashes”. Check whether the answer argues from other uses of נחם עַל (for example Exod 32:12, 14; Jer 18:8, 10).

1.3 Job 9:33, “there is no arbiter”

  • Ask: What does Clines (WBC) say about reading לֹא (“there is no”) or לוּ (“would that”) in Job 9:33, and which does he follow?
  • Then ask of: Hartley (NICOT).
  • Why: this is the plan’s outstanding commentary check. The Sermon 3 backbone uses 9:33 as Job’s longing for a mediator, which holds under either reading. The answer decides how to say it in the pulpit.
  • Watch for: whether the manuscript evidence is weighed, or only the sense.

1.4 Job 13:15, “Though he slay me”

  • Ask: What does Hartley (NICOT) say about the ketiv לֹא and the qere לוֹ in Job 13:15, and which does he follow?
  • Then ask of: Clines (WBC) and Delitzsch.
  • Why: the ESV and NIV84 follow the qere (“I will hope in him”). The ketiv gives “I have no hope”. The Sermon 3 backbone tells the preacher to read the whole verse, and this decides what to say about it.
  • Watch for: reasoning from the KJV’s “yet will I trust in him”.

1.5 Job 7:20, the tiqqun sopherim

  • Ask: What does Clines (WBC) say about the scribal correction at Job 7:20 (“a burden to myself” or “to you”), and does he think the correction is real or a later theory?
  • Why: this is an open question in the unit dig. The ESV and the Greek read “to you”; the NASB95 reads “to myself”.
  • Watch for: answers that treat the tradition of scribal corrections as a single settled fact. Some lists of the corrections are later than others.

2. The State of a Debate

2.1 Job 16:19–21, the witness in heaven

  • Ask: What are the main views on who the “witness in heaven” (עֵד, שָׂהֵד) of Job 16:19 is, who holds each, and what evidence do they give?
  • Why: 16:1–17:16 is the other HIGH solo dig for Sermon 4. The mediator trajectory runs from 9:33 through 16:19–21 and 19:25 to 33:23. In Job, מֵלִיץ (“interpreter, mediator”) occurs only at 16:20 and 33:23 (WLC, lemma).
  • Watch for:
    • answers that settle the question by Christian theology alone, without the text;
    • whether 16:20 is read as “my friends scorn me” or “my interpreter is my friend”.

2.2 Job 42:7, “of me” or “to me”

  • Ask: What are the main explanations of “you have not spoken of me what is right” in Job 42:7, and does anyone argue that אֵלַי means “to me”?
  • Why: this is the sweep’s open question 6. The introductory backbone tells the preacher to supply “to me”, on the ground that the same verse twice uses the phrase that way.
  • Watch for:
    • reasoning from the English “of me”;
    • whether the answer cites other places where דבר אֶל means “speak concerning” (for example 1 Sam 3:12; Jer 40:16).

2.3 Job 28, who speaks

  • Ask: Which major commentaries (WBC, NICOT, OTL, TOTC) discuss who speaks Job 28, what view does each take, and what evidence from the text do they give?
  • Why: the first round’s answer had no citations and treated the NASB95 heading as the text. The third-cycle claim audit must come before Sermon 5, and it needs the state of the question.
  • Watch for: any section heading treated as evidence; and the 27:1 = 29:1 formula, which the claim audit treats as structural.

2.4 Elihu’s place in the book

  • Ask: What are the main views on the place of Elihu’s speeches (Job 32–37) in the book, who holds each, and what evidence do they give?
  • Why: Sermon 7 asks Elihu’s status as an explicit question. The sweep left it open: a delay, a preparation for God’s speeches, or both.
  • Watch for: a date or composition theory (a later addition) doing the work of an argument from the text.

2.5 The Accuser in Job 1–2

  • Ask: What are the main views on the identity of “the satan” (הַשָּׂטָן, with the article) in Job 1–2, who holds each, and what evidence do they give?
  • Why: this is open question 7 in the 1:1–3:1 dig. It bears on Sermon 1 and the introductory sermon, both still to be preached.
  • Watch for:
    • whether the article is argued from the Hebrew;
    • whether Zech 3:1–2 and 1 Chr 21:1 are compared;
    • reading the New Testament back into the text without saying so.

2.6 Job 14:16–17, hope or surveillance

  • Ask: What are the main readings of Job 14:16–17: does it continue the hope of 14:13–15, or return to complaint? Who holds each, and on what grounds?
  • Why: this is open question 2 in the 13–14 dig, and it touches movement 4 of the Sermon 3 backbone. The כִּי־עַתָּה (“for now”) pattern in Job favours hope. The Greek and the NASB95 read surveillance.
  • Watch for: answers that argue from the tense of an English version.

3. Has Anyone Seen This Allusion?

Each of these fills in the “history of interpretation” criterion, which the audits recorded as not checked.

3.1 Job 3:5 and 19:25, גאל (“claim” / “Redeemer”)

  • Ask: Do any commentators connect “let darkness … claim it” (גאל) in Job 3:5 with “my Redeemer” (גֹּאֲלִי) in Job 19:25? How do they take the verb at 3:5: “redeem, claim”, or “defile”?
  • Why: queue #8 is armed before the 19:1–29 dig. The WLC files 3:5 under “redeem”, and these are the only two occurrences in Job. The link depends on that.
  • Watch for: the homonym גאל II (“defile”) argued from the Hebrew, not from the English “stain” (KJV) or “claim”.

3.2 Job 42:11 and Ezekiel 14:22–23

  • Ask: Which commentators discuss a link between Job 42:11 and Ezekiel 14:22–23, what wording do they point to, and what textual arguments do they give for the direction of dependence?
  • Why: queue #21 is armed before the 42:7–17 dig. In the first round the Assistant settled the direction by assuming Ezekiel was older.
  • Watch for: a date assumption in place of an argument.

3.3 Job 13:19, 28 and Isaiah 50:8–9

  • Ask: Which commentators discuss a link between Job 13:19, 28 and Isaiah 50:8–9, what wording do they point to, and does anyone reject it?
  • Why: claim audit 2 rated it moderate–high. The Sermon 3 backbone uses it as colour, through Rom 8:33–34.

3.4 Job 4:12–16 and the Sinai–Horeb revelation texts

  • Ask: Do any commentators compare Eliphaz’s night vision in Job 4:12–16 with Numbers 12:8, Deuteronomy 4:12 or 1 Kings 19:12, and what do they conclude?
  • Why: claim audit 2 confirmed it: high on the words, moderate–high on the design. Each pair is exclusive in one verse.
  • Watch for: whether anyone takes the vision as a parody of genuine revelation, or as genuine. Both are live.

3.5 Job 13:24 and Psalm 44:24 [Heb 44:25]

  • Ask: Which commentators note that “Why do you hide your face?” (Job 13:24) matches Psalm 44:24, and do any discuss Job’s use of the Psalter’s laments more widely?
  • Why: claim audit 2 rated it high on the words. Ps 39 (at 10:20–21) and Ps 44 are Job’s two strongest psalm sources.

3.6 James 5:11 and Greek Job (scope: James commentaries)

  • Ask: What evidence do commentators on James 5:11 give that James knew the Greek text of Job, or the Testament of Job, when he speaks of “the steadfastness (ὑπομονή) of Job”?
  • Why: this is open question 7 in the unit dig. Greek Job uses ὑπομονή only at 14:19, where God destroys it. The backbone keeps this as colour until it is checked.
  • Watch for: whether the Testament of Job is dated, and how.

3.7 Job 6:15–23 and Jeremiah 15:18–21

  • Ask: Do any commentators compare Job’s “deceitful brook” (Job 6:15–20) and “Redeem me from the hand of the tyrants” (6:23) with Jeremiah 15:18–21?
  • Why: queue #50. Rated moderate; low priority.

4. Lexicon, Grammar and Text

4.1 שָׂהֵד (“witness”, Job 16:19)

  • Ask: What does HALOT give for שָׂהֵד in Job 16:19, and what cognates does it cite?
  • Why: the word occurs only here (WLC). Its Aramaic cognate stands at Gen 31:47 (שָׂהֲדוּתָא, “witness”). Sermon 4 needs the gloss settled.

4.2 The preposition in Job 4:17

  • Ask: How do the standard Hebrew grammars (GKC, Joüon–Muraoka, Waltke–O’Connor) classify the preposition מִן in “Can mankind be just before (מֵ) God?” (Job 4:17)?
  • Why: this is open question 5 in the 4–5 dig.
    • The ESV and NASB95 read “before”, which carries the “in the right before God” chain (4:17 → 9:2 → 13:18) used in the Sermon 3 backbone.
    • The NIV84 reads “more righteous than God”, which breaks it.
  • Watch for: answers from a single English version.

4.3 חֲלִיפָה (“change, renewal”, Job 14:14)

  • Ask: What does HALOT give for חֲלִיפָה in Job 14:14 (“until my change comes”), and does it relate the word to 10:17?
  • Why: this is open question 10 in the 13–14 dig. It bears on the military image of צָבָא (“hard service”) at 7:1, 10:17 and 14:14.

4.4 לַמָּס (Job 6:14)

  • Ask: What does HALOT give for לַמָּס in Job 6:14, and how does it construe the verse?
  • Why: this is open question 1 in the unit dig. The ESV makes the friend who withholds kindness forsake the fear of God; the NASB95 makes the despairing man the one at risk. The Sermon 3 backbone notes the difference.

4.5 מַסָּה (Job 9:23)

  • Ask: What does HALOT give for לְמַסַּת in Job 9:23: “trial” (מַסָּה I) or “despair” (from מסס)?
  • Why: this is open question 3 in the 9–10 dig. If it means “trial”, God mocks at the testing of the innocent, which is the prologue’s situation.

4.6 The Göttingen Iob (only if you own Ziegler’s edition)

  • Ask: What does the Göttingen apparatus record at Job 1:21 and 2:9 about Origen’s signs (obelus or asterisk)?
  • Then ask: What does it record at 4:6 for κακία / ἀκακία?
  • Why: this is the real form of the plan’s “Rahlfs asterisks” check (see above).
  • Watch for: confusion of the asterisk (Hebrew material the Old Greek lacked) with the obelus (Greek material the Hebrew lacks).

5. Background

5.1 “Skin for skin” (Job 2:4)

  • Ask: What is known of the expression “skin for skin” (עוֹר בְּעַד־עוֹר) in Job 2:4 — its origin and its meaning — and what evidence is given?
  • Why: this is open question 5 in the 1:1–3:1 dig. It bears on Sermon 1.
  • Watch for: uncited sentences; this is a proverb about which little is known.

5.2 Teman and Eliphaz the Temanite

  • Ask: What was Teman’s reputation for wisdom (Jeremiah 49:7; Obadiah 8–9), and do commentators connect it with Eliphaz the Temanite?
  • Then ask: Do any connect the caravans of Tema (Job 6:19) with Eliphaz?
  • Why: queue #40, and open question 5 in the unit dig (“commentary check; otherwise drop”).
  • Watch for: Teman (Edom) and Tema (Arabia) treated as the same place without argument.

5.3 “Why did the knees receive me?” (Job 3:12)

  • Ask: What custom lies behind “Why did the knees receive me?” in Job 3:12 — the father’s acknowledgement, the mother’s, or the midwife’s — and what evidence do commentators give (Genesis 30:3; 50:23)?
  • Why: this is open question 7 in the Job 3 dig. It bears on Sermon 2.

6. Checks to Make Outside the Assistant

The guidelines say the Assistant usually cannot answer these. They are listed so that the plan’s Logos list is complete.

  • The spread of the paragraph markers in the manuscripts. These are the petuḥot after 1:5, 1:22, 2:10, 3:1, 3:26, 5:27, 8:22, 10:22, 11:20, 14:22, 28:28, 31:40, 32:1 and 42:6, and the absence of one at 27:23.
    • Where to look: read BHS on screen at each place, and an Aleppo-based edition if you own one.
    • Priority: low. No finding rests on a marker alone.
  • The Exegetical Guide’s Textual Variants section at 14:3 (“me” or “him”) and 13:28 (“rot” or “wineskin”). It uses no AI credits. Low priority.

  2. 2.1 (the witness in heaven) and 4.1 (שָׂהֵד), for the 16:1–17:16 dig.
  3. 3.1 (גאל at 3:5 and 19:25). This is armed before the 19 dig.
  4. 1.3 (9:33) and 4.2 (4:17). These close the two points in the Sermon 3 backbone that wait on a check.
  5. 1.4 (13:15).
