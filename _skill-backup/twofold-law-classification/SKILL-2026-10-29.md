# Twofold Law Classification

Apply the **twofold distinction of the commandments** to a body of Old Testament law (or a NT passage handling it), commandment by commandment, and report what the scheme shows **and where it strains**.

> **Status: adopted on trial (29 October 2026).** The scheme is Patrick's own tentative thesis (`Torah/tripartite-law-paper.md` §6) and the method rests on **one run** — the Book of the Covenant (`Torah/Exodus/twofold-distinction-exodus-20-22to23-33`). Treat it as working method, not a settled grid. **Withdrawal / revision test:** if two or three corpora each produce a large share of laws classed *both* or *neither* (more than about a quarter), or if the Sabbath problem recurs unresolved in every corpus, say so in the report and recommend revisiting the scheme rather than forcing the classification.

**This skill exists to test the scheme as much as to apply it.** A run that only confirms the scheme has not done its job. The closing section, *Where the Scheme Strains*, is required.

## The scheme in one paragraph

Two kinds of commandment within one covenant: **kind 1**, commandments expressing the created moral order, binding on humanity as such and attested before and outside Sinai; **kind 2**, commandments constituting Israel as a separated and priestly people (food, purity, cult, calendar, covenant signs), characteristically — not necessarily — marked by the language of separation and of perpetuity in Israel's generations. **The judicial law is the polity's application and sanction of both kinds**; law that God alone enforces is **covenant law under divine sanction**, outside the judicial law. The apostles carry the law into the church by analogy ("general equity").

## Procedure

### 1. List the commandments
Take them from the Hebrew (WLC in `_texts/hebrew-wlc/`), not from an English version, dividing where the text divides (a new case, a new prohibition). Give references in English versification with the Hebrew in brackets where they differ.

### 2. For each, record four parts
| Part | Question |
|---|---|
| **Norm** | What does it require or forbid? |
| **Ground** | What reason does the text give? |
| **Form** | In what shape is it given to Israel (cult, calendar, land, polity)? |
| **Sanction** | Who enforces it, and how? |

### 3. Classify by the norm — never by the motive
A motive drawn from Israel's history ("for you were sojourners in Egypt") does **not** make a norm kind 2. Where norm and form fall in different kinds, the law is **Both**; say which part is which.

### 4. Name the test that carries each classification
**Kind 1:** **1a** before Sinai (stated or assumed as a norm); **1b** outsiders held to it (nations judged; a non-Israelite setting — Job counts by its setting in Uz, not by any date); **1c** creation ground; **1d** apostolic reading (the NT binds Gentile believers by it, or condemns Gentiles for it).
**Kind 2:** **2a** content (cult, calendar, food, purity, sign); **2b** separation or holiness stated as ground; **2c** perpetuity (*ḥuqqat ʿôlām*, 'a perpetual statute'; "throughout your generations"; covenant sign); **2d** the Torah elsewhere draws the line at the foreigner (e.g. Deut 14:21; 23:20 [Heb 23:21]).
Where only 1d or only 2a/2d carries a classification, **say so**. Mark *uncertain* where no test applies cleanly.

### 5. Mark the judicial role
**A** application (case, measure, intent, **procedure** — judges, oath, evidence) · **S** sanction by the community (death, restitution, ransom, compensation, **release**) · **D** sanction by God alone (not judicial) · **—** none in this passage.

### 6. Check the vocabulary markers mechanically
Before classifying, search the passage by lemma for the kind-2 markers — בָּדַל ('separate'), חֹק / חֻקָּה ('statute'), דּוֹר ('generation'), עוֹלָם ('for ever') — with `_texts/tools/find.py`, and report the result in words. Their absence is a finding (the Book of the Covenant has none), not a reason to drop kind 2.

### 7. Verify every evidence text
Every text cited as a test is read in the corpus (WLC / Swete / SBLGNT) in the run, not cited from memory. Texts cited from standard knowledge without being opened are **listed by name** in the declaration and flagged for checking. Any "only" or count claim is a lemma search with a positive control, edition named.

### 8. Count, then report proportions
Count the classes from the finished table (mechanically, not by eye). Report proportions; say the counts depend on how laws are divided.

### 9. Read the table for patterns
At minimum ask: (a) **which kinds the passage actually sanctions** — do not assume it sanctions both; (b) **where the kinds cluster** in the passage's structure; (c) which capital laws coincide with the nations' judgement (Lev 18:24–28; Deut 18:12); (d) which laws the NT quotes, and how it handles their sanction.

### 10. Where the Scheme Strains (required)
At least three specific pressure points from this passage: laws that are *both* or *neither*; classifications resting only on 1d or 2a/2d; motive/norm divergences; divine-sanction law; the Sabbath (record which grounding each Sabbath text gives — creation, sign, or rest of the dependent); any place the Torah's own line wavers (e.g. Lev 17:15 against Deut 14:21). Propose any refinement to the method. **If a refinement is proposed, offer to fold it into `Torah/twofold-distinction-of-the-commandments.md`** (draft the edit first; Patrick decides).

## Pitfalls

- Classifying the motive instead of the norm.
- Treating absence of separation/perpetuity vocabulary as absence of kind 2.
- Calling divine-sanction law "judicial".
- Assuming every corpus sanctions both kinds.
- Citing a pre-Sinai text that is narrative as if it stated a norm (Gen 9:22–25).
- Letting the Sabbath be settled by the scheme rather than reported as divided.
- Writing a report that only confirms the scheme.
