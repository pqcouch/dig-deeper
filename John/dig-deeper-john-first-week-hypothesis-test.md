# Hypothesis Test: Does John's Opening Week End on an Eighth Day at Cana?

**Hypothesis (yours).** John 1:1–2:12 is built as a new-creation week. "In the beginning" (1:1) echoes Genesis 1:1; Genesis 1:1–2 is *dayless* and so is John 1:1–3; the Light of 1:4–5 therefore marks day one, as Genesis 1:3 does; John the Baptist appears on day two. The three "next day" markers carry the count to 1:43, a seventh day passes unnarrated — the Sabbath, and the day Christ lay in the tomb — and the wedding at Cana falls on the eighth day, which is also "the third day" of 2:1, the day of resurrection. The feast alludes to Isaiah 25:6. In your later refinement the eighth day is better named **the first day of the second week**, which is John's own number at 20:1.

**Primary texts.** John from the SBLGNT with the MorphGNT lemma index (observation). Genesis from the WLC (Hebrew) and Swete (Greek), with the caution recorded below. Isaiah, Hosea and Exodus from Swete. The NA28 apparatus in Logos for every reading that bears weight.

**Study text.** NASB95 (1995 edition), for English reference only.

**Pulpit text.** None declared. No sermon is in view; an essay is intended.

**Consumes.** Nothing yet. `John/book-overview-john.md` and the John digs in this folder have not been revised against these findings, and no edit to them is proposed here.

**Mode.** Hypothesis test, in progress. The text work was done first and secondary literature opened only afterwards.

**Date.** 14 November 2026. Draft v0.1 — a record of one morning's work, not a finished report.

> **Status.** Two of the eight claims have been tested to a conclusion, three are strengthened, one has failed a control, and two are untouched. Nothing here has been through a claim audit. The one apparently original finding (the σκοτία frame, below) must not enter the book overview above *moderate*, or any sermon, until a blind auditor has tested it.

## The Hypothesis, Decomposed

A compound hypothesis is tested claim by claim, because some limbs can hold while others fail. Falsifiers were set before any counting.

| | Claim | Falsified if |
|---|---|---|
| **H1** | John 1:1 echoes Genesis 1:1 | (consensus; not seriously at risk) |
| **H2** | John 1:19–2:11 is a deliberately numbered day sequence | John's temporal connectives work the same way elsewhere with no counting function |
| **H3** | The count begins in the prologue: 1:4–5 = day one, 1:6–8 = day two | the prologue carries no marker of the kind governing 1:29ff, and the assignment rests on the Genesis parallel alone |
| **H4** | A seventh day passes unnarrated | no counting convention produces a gap |
| **H5** | Cana is the eighth day | eight requires an assumption nothing else in John supports |
| **H6** | "The third day" and the eighth day coincide by design, pointing to resurrection | 2:1 can only be counted from 1:43 under a convention yielding six or seven |
| **H7** | The feast alludes to Isaiah 25:6 | no contact beyond "wine" and "feast", and rivals explain it as well or better |
| **H8** | The opening forms an inclusio with John 19–20 | the Genesis links at the Gospel's close are absent or generic |

## The Arithmetic, and Where the Weight Falls

| Day | Text | Marker |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1:4–5 | none — supplied by the Genesis parallel |
| 2 | 1:6–8 | none — supplied by the Baptist's appearance |
| 3 | 1:19–28 | none (reckoned a day retrospectively, from 1:29) |
| 4 | 1:29–34 | τῇ ἐπαύριον ("on the next day") |
| 5 | 1:35–42 | τῇ ἐπαύριον |
| 6 | 1:43–51 | τῇ ἐπαύριον |
| 7 | — | silent |
| 8 | 2:1–11 | τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῇ τρίτῃ ("on the third day") |

Counted inclusively from day six, "the third day" lands on day eight and the silent seventh **falls out of the arithmetic** rather than being imposed on it. That is a genuine strength: the Sabbath is observed, not inserted.

Every other route was tested and none reaches eight:

| Route | Result |
|---|---|
| Count from 1:19, no prologue days | Cana = day 6 or 7 |
| Count from 1:19, with the πρωΐ variant at 1:41 splitting a day | Cana = day 7 |
| Prologue supplies one day | Cana = day 7 |
| **Prologue supplies two days** | **Cana = day 8, day 7 silent** |
| Prologue supplies two days *and* 1:41 splits | Cana = day 9 |

**The whole count therefore rests on H3.** Nothing else moves the total.

## What the Corpus Confirmed

**Genesis 1:1–2 is dayless, and demonstrably so.** The day formula first appears at 1:5: וַיְהִי־עֶרֶב וַיְהִי־בֹקֶר י֥וֹם אֶחָד ("and there was evening and there was morning, day one"). The series then runs שֵׁנִי, שְׁלִישִׁי, רְבִיעִי, חֲמִישִׁי ("second, third, fourth, fifth"), with הַשִּׁשִּׁי ("the sixth") and the seventh taking the article. Swete matches: ἡμέρα μία ("day one"), then ordinals. **This premise survives the Genesis 1:1 syntax debate** — on every construal of בְּרֵאשִׁית ("in the beginning"), 1:1–2 carries no day formula.

**John 1:1–5 does not narrate.** ἦν ("was", imperfect) four times, then φαίνει ("shines", present) at 1:5 — a timeless present. The aorists at 1:3 (ἐγένετο, "came into being") are summary, not sequence. The first aorist that puts an event on the ground is Ἐγένετο ἄνθρωπος ("there came a man") at 1:6. **This is the grammatical objection to H3, and it stands.**

**One limb of that objection falls away.** 1:19 carries no temporal marker either; it is reckoned a day only retrospectively, from τῇ ἐπαύριον at 1:29. Unmarked days are therefore not foreign to the scheme. What the prologue days lack is not a marker but that retrospective one.

**τῇ ἐπαύριον is concentrated.** Five occurrences in John — 1:29, 1:35, 1:43, 6:22, 12:12. Three of five fall within fifteen verses; the two outside are single links with nothing to count. Clustering supports H2.

**An ἀρχή inclusio brackets 1:1–2:11.** ἐν ἀρχῇ ("in the beginning") at 1:1 and 1:2; ἀρχὴν τῶν σημείων ("beginning of the signs") at 2:11; then 2:12 moves off with Μετὰ τοῦτο ("after this"). Supports H2.

**A σκοτία frame runs from 1:5 to 20:1.** σκοτία ("darkness") stands in six verses — 1:5, 6:17, 8:12, 12:35, 12:46, 20:1 — with σκότος used once only, at 3:19. The outer two are the prologue and the resurrection morning.

**John 20:1 carries three words of Genesis's first day.** Τῇ δὲ μιᾷ τῶν σαββάτων … ἔρχεται πρωῒ σκοτίας ἔτι οὔσης ("on the first day of the week … comes early, while it was still dark"): μιᾷ ("one", cardinal, as Genesis 1:5 ἡμέρα μία), πρωΐ ("early/morning", as Genesis 1:5 ἐγένετο πρωί), and darkness. This is the strongest support found for H8, and it vindicates the refinement from "eighth day" to "first day of the second week" — the number is John's own.

**Genesis 2:7 καὶ ἐνεφύσησεν ("and he breathed into") = John 20:22 ἐνεφύσησεν.** Identical form. Not exclusive: the same form stands at 3 Reigns 17:21 (Elijah over the dead child), Job 4:21 and Tobit(S) 11:11, with the stem also at Ezekiel 37:9, Wisdom 15:11, Sirach 43:4, Nahum 2:2, Ezekiel 21:31 and 1 Esdras 9:55. Genesis 2:7 is the only creation instance.

**Hosea 6:2 (Swete) shares John's exact phrase.** ὑγιάσει ἡμᾶς μετὰ δύο ἡμέρας· **ἐν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῇ τρίτῃ** καὶ ἀναστησόμεθα, καὶ ζησόμεθα ἐνώπιον αὐτοῦ ("he will heal us after two days; on the third day we shall also rise, and we shall live before him"), against John 2:1 Καὶ **τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῇ τρίτῃ** γάμος ἐγένετο. Verbatim, and Hosea's third day is explicitly resurrection and life.

**Exodus 19:11 and 19:16 (Swete) share it too**, and 19:16 shares the verb as well: Ἐγένετο δὲ τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῇ τρίτῃ ("and it came to pass on the third day"). Syntactically Exodus is the closer match; semantically Hosea is.

## What the Corpus Corrected

Four claims proposed in conversation did not survive the texts, and are recorded here so they are not repeated:

- **κῆπος is not a Genesis echo.** LXX Genesis 2:8 has παράδεισον ("paradise, garden"), and κῆπος occurs **nowhere** in the book. John 19:41's garden is a conceptual link only.
- **τετέλεσται is weaker than claimed.** Genesis 2:1–2 has συνετελέσθησαν / συνετέλεσεν — the same root as John 19:30 τετέλεσται ("it is finished"), but a different compound.
- **Isaiah 25:8 will not carry resurrection in the Greek.** Swete reads κατέπιεν ὁ θάνατος ἰσχύσας — *death* does the swallowing. The Hebrew, and Paul at 1 Corinthians 15:54, have it the other way.
- **Isaiah 25:6 is the weakest limb of the hypothesis.** Swete: ἐπὶ τὸ ὄρος τοῦτο πίονται εὐφροσύνην, πίονται οἶνον ("on this mountain they shall drink gladness, they shall drink wine"). Shared with John 2: οἶνος ("wine") and an eschatological feast. Not shared: the wedding, the mountain, the nations. One common noun is not an allusion.

**Redirection worth weighing.** Hosea may serve the hypothesis better than Isaiah 25 for both halves of the intuition: Hosea 6:2 gives the third day as resurrection in John's exact words, and Hosea 2 gives the marriage and the new wine. One book, both elements.

## Corpus Fault: Swete's Genesis

**Swete is not a safe observation layer for Genesis 1–2.** Vaticanus lacks Genesis 1–46, so the text is reconstructed, and the lacunae are visible: Genesis 1:23, 1:31 and the whole of 2:1–2 print with brackets. More seriously for this hypothesis, Swete's Genesis 2:2 reads συνετέλεσεν … **τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῇ ἕκτῃ** ("he finished on the sixth day") where the WLC has בַּיּוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי ("on the seventh day").

## The Arbitrary-Pairing Control

**Why.** The content argument for H3 is that John 1:4–5 answers Genesis day one because light answers light. The risk is that such correspondences can be manufactured for almost any pairing. The control measures whether they can.

**Method.** Each candidate Johannine day-unit was reduced to its content lemmas (SBLGNT index; nouns, verbs and adjectives, minus the standard stop set) using the folder's own `_texts/tools/contacts.py` matcher, and each stem matched against the words of each Genesis day in Swete. Score = distinct content lemmas of the John unit contacting that Genesis day. All 720 one-to-one alignments of six John units to six Genesis days were then enumerated — an exact test, not a simulation.

|  | G1 light | G2 firmament | G3 land | G4 lights | G5 swarms | G6 beasts/man | G7 rest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| J1 1:4–5 | **1** | 0 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 0 |
| J2 1:6–8 | 2 | **1** | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| J3 1:19–28 | 1 | 1 | **1** | 2 | 2 | 0 | 1 |
| J4 1:29–34 | 1 | 2 | 3 | **3** | 3 | 2 | 5 |
| J5 1:35–42 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 5 | **3** | 4 | 3 |
| J6 1:43–51 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 2 | **3** | 3 |

**Results.**

- **Generosity: 33 of 36 cells (92%) carry at least one contact.** A link is available for almost any pairing.
- **All contacts:** hypothesis diagonal 12; 720 alignments mean 11.50, median 12, max 16, min 7. **p = 0.524.** Exactly average.
- **Distinctive contacts only** (lemmas in ≤ 2 of the 7 days, stripping out θεός and the like): diagonal 4, mean 2.83, max 7. **p = 0.300.** Better than average, not significantly.

Four of the six diagonal cells are carried by θεός ("God"), ἡμέρα ("day") and καλέω ("he called") — words spread across the whole of Genesis 1 and worth nothing as evidence.

**What survives.** φῶς ("light") stands in **1 of the 7 Genesis days** — day one only. The light contact is genuinely distinctive, and is the strongest content link in the matrix.

**What complicates it.** φῶς links Genesis day one to **both** J1 (1:4–5) and J2 (1:6–8), because 1:7–8 says three times of the Baptist that he came περὶ τοῦ φωτός ("concerning the light") and οὐκ ἦν ἐκεῖνος τὸ φῶς ("he was not the light"). **The Gospel's light vocabulary runs straight across the seam the scheme needs.** On the lexical evidence 1:4–8 is one unit about the light, not a first day followed by a second.

Second complication: John 1:5's verb φαίνει ("shines") matches not day one but **day four** — Genesis 1:15, 17 use φαίνειν of the luminaries. John 1:4–5 therefore carries light vocabulary from Genesis days one *and* four.

**Two caveats on the instrument.** σκοτία registers in 0 of 7 days — not a finding about Genesis but about words: Swete has σκότος, John has σκοτία, and the matcher treats them as the two different nouns they are. And the test sees shared vocabulary only; it cannot see theme, structure or syntax, and its counts are floors.

**Verdict.** The content argument cannot support a mapped week. It was never the main argument — the case for the two prologue days is positional — but it can no longer be used to reinforce it.

### Free checks

**Jones, *OT Quotations and Allusions in the NT*.**

| Entry | NT texts listed |
|---|---|
| Hosea 6:2 | Luke 24:46; 1 Corinthians 15:4 |
| Exodus 19:16 | Revelation 4:5; 11:19 |
| Exodus 19:16–19 | Revelation 8:5; 16:18 |
| Exodus 19:16–22 | Hebrews 12:18–19 |
| John 2:1 | *no entry* |

Two consequences. **Hosea 6:2 is the recognised scriptural source of the New Testament's "third day" resurrection formula** — it is what "according to the Scriptures" means at 1 Corinthians 15:4. And **the Exodus 19 rival weakens**: its recognised New Testament reception runs entirely through the Sinai theophany imagery, not the day-count.

**Jobes, *John through Old Testament Eyes*, at John 2:1.** She reaches Hosea 6:2 independently — not as direct allusion but through the Jewish tradition that the dead rise after three days, routed via Esther 5:1 and the Midrash comparing Abraham, Jacob and Jonah. She then **dissolves the count rather than solving it**: walking 1:19, 1:29, 1:35, 1:43 she concludes that strict chronology puts the wedding on the *fifth* day, and takes "the third day" as symbolic — a day of God's deliverance, quoting Hays on the Gospel's indifference to linear time.

**Her arithmetic is loose, and that is the opening.** Reckoned inclusively from 1:43, τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῇ τρίτῃ gives day six; exclusively, day seven. Neither is five. The mismatch she uses to drive the phrase into pure symbolism is smaller than she states.

### Credit questions (Study Assistant; 29% → 30% of credits)

**Q1 — has anyone begun the count in the prologue? No.** Nothing found. The consensus begins at 1:19. Fee and Stuart (owned) give the reason: the prologue's Baptist references at 1:6–8 and 1:15 **anticipate** the narrative beginning at 1:19 — the proleptic reading, which is the standard objection to H3.

**Q2 — how is a seventh day obtained?** Per Weinrich, *John 1:1–7:1*, Concordia Commentary, 230–231, by two routes only:

- **Boismard** grounds it in the πρωΐ variant at 1:41. Weinrich judges the variant too weak to carry a symbolic scheme; **our NA28 check shows why — no Greek witness at all.** That route is closed.
- **Carson** declines the variant and reaches seven narratively: the two disciples spend day three (1:35–39) with Jesus, so Peter's introduction falls on day four, 1:43 is day five, and "the third day" lands on day seven. Weinrich objects that this inserts a day with no textual indication.

**Carson reckons "the third day" inclusively from the last named day** — the same convention as this hypothesis. Applied here, 1:43 is day six and Cana is day eight. **But Weinrich's charge against Carson applies here with double force: Carson inserts one day without textual indication; this hypothesis inserts two.**

**Q3 — are the structural observations known?**

- **The ἀρχή inclusio: yes, in part, and in a book you own.** Wilkin, *Grace New Testament Commentary*, 370, notes that John writes ἀρχή rather than πρῶτος at 2:11 and takes it as an inclusio with the prologue. (Carson separately notes a different, geographic inclusio at 2:1/2:11, Cana of Galilee.)
- **The σκοτία frame: nothing found.** No source paired σκοτία at 1:5 with 20:1, and none connected 20:1's τῇ μιᾷ and πρωΐ to the Genesis 1:5 formula. **This appears to be original, and is therefore unaudited.**

## Where the Hypothesis Stands

| | Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | 1:1 echoes Genesis 1:1 | **Holds** (consensus; ἐν ἀρχῇ verbatim with Swete Genesis 1:1) |
| H2 | A deliberate numbered sequence | **Strengthened** — ἀρχή bracket; τῇ ἐπαύριον concentrated 3 of 5 in fifteen verses |
| H3 | The count begins in the prologue | **Unsupported, not refuted.** Unattested in the literature; grammar puts the narrative boundary at 1:6; the light vocabulary spans the seam; the content argument failed its control |
| H4 | A silent seventh day | **Follows from H3.** Falls out of the arithmetic if H3 holds |
| H5 | Cana is the eighth day | **Follows from H3.** No other route reaches eight |
| H6 | Third day = eighth day, pointing to resurrection | **Strengthened.** Textually secure; Hosea 6:2 verbatim and recognised as the NT's third-day source; the same inclusive reckoning Carson uses. Not isolated from the Exodus 19 phrase |
| H7 | Isaiah 25:6 | **Weak.** One common noun; rivals unexplored; Hosea 2 a better candidate |
| H8 | Inclusio with John 19–20 | **Strengthened, and the strongest limb.** σκοτία frame; 20:1's three day-one words; ἐνεφύσησεν exact |

**The honest summary: the frame is in considerably better shape than the count.** The case that John wrote his Gospel with Genesis 1–2 open at both ends is strong and partly original. The case that the opening days number eight rests entirely on H3, which has survived every test without passing any.
