# Point & Purpose: John 6:1–71

**Passage:** John 6:1–71 (the feeding of the five thousand, the walking on the sea, the Bread of Life discourse, and the sifting)
**Source exegesis:** *Dig Deeper: John 6:1–71*
**Scope:** One sermon on the whole chapter.
**Date:** 22 July 2026

---

## Fallen Condition Focus

We hunger for a life and a security that perishing things cannot give — and we are tempted to want Jesus's *benefits* more than we want Jesus *himself*. The crowd that chased the loaves, the disciples who found the teaching too hard, and every reader who has followed Christ for what he provides share one condition: a hunger that only he can satisfy, and a heart easily satisfied with less than him.

## Teaching Point

Jesus himself — his flesh given for the world — is the true bread from heaven; whoever comes to him and feeds on him by faith will never hunger and will be raised on the last day.

*(Subject: what is the food that gives eternal life? Complement: Jesus himself, given in death and received by faith.)*

## Purpose Statement

To move hearers from seeking Jesus for what he provides to feeding on Christ himself by faith — assuring the wavering that the Father loses none who come to him, warning the half-hearted before a hard saying sifts them, and inviting the hungry to come to Christ and never hunger again.

## Big Question

What are you really hungry for — and will you have Christ himself, or only what he can hand you?

## Proposed Structure

A Christ-centred, four-movement shape, tracking the chapter's own structure (two signs, discourse, sifting) and the report's Headline Findings.

**1. The bread we chase (vv.1–15, 22–26).**
The crowd, the sign, and the hunger that misreads it — "you seek me… because you ate your fill of the loaves." Jesus performs the sign and then flees the crown it provokes. This is the Fallen Condition on display: a following built on appetite, not on faith in the person.

**2. The bread God gives (vv.27–51).**
Not the manna Moses gave but the true bread the Father *gives* (present tense) — Jesus, come down from heaven and given "for the life of the world." The one "work of God" is to believe in him whom he sent (v.29); the bread is received simply by coming and believing. This is the heart of the Teaching Point, and the ground of assurance: "whoever comes to me I will never cast out… I will lose nothing… I will raise it up on the last day."

**3. The bread that divides (vv.52–66).**
The hard saying, the Spirit who gives life (v.63), and the sifting. Bread-seeking faith cannot survive a costly word and walks away. The preacher should let this land as hard as it is; the comfort of movement 2 is only felt against this severity.

**4. The bread worth staying for (vv.67–71).**
"Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life." Peter's confession is the intended response — not loyalty by willpower but clinging to the one who alone has the words of life. The movement ends where the sermon aims: resting on the Father's pledge, and staying when others leave.

## Keeping Honest

Drawn from the report's Preaching Pitfalls:

- The hero is **Christ the bread**, not the boy with the loaves; resist "bring your little to Jesus" moralism.
- Let **v.63 govern vv.51–58**: the eating is Spirit-wrought faith-union with the crucified Christ, not a debate about the elements of the Lord's Supper.
- Hold **the Father's drawing (vv.44, 65) and the open invitation (vv.35, 37) together**: sovereign grace is the ground of assurance, not an excuse for passivity.
- Do not preach the chapter cheerfully **past the defection** (vv.60–66); the sifting is the climax.
