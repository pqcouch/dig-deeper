## Method note — read this before using the sweep

**Passage quotation.** The house sweep format quotes each pericope in full. John is 879 verses — eight times Philippians — and the ESV is not available to this run as a verified electronic text. Rather than reconstruct 879 verses from memory and present them as verified, each pericope carries a one-line **Gist** and quotes only short, load-bearing phrases. ESV wording quoted inline is **from recall** and should be checked against the printed text before any finding hangs on an exact English word. This is a deliberate, declared shortfall against the house template, not an oversight — see the Text-First Declaration.

**What *is* machine-verified.** All Greek forms, word-counts, and per-chapter distributions in this report were taken by scripted sweep over the **SBLGNT** (866 verses; the edition omits 5:4 and brackets 7:53–8:11), and all English cross-checks against a full electronic **KJV**. Where a finding rests on a count or a Greek form, it is verified and reproducible. Where it rests on ESV phrasing, it is not.

**Depth.** Sweep depth, not solo depth. Each pericope receives: a Positional Necessity Check, the tools that are live for it, Tool 11 including the mandatory Move 4 internal-echo check, one or two sweep findings, and a solo-dig verdict. Tools that are dead for a given pericope are silently omitted rather than padded with N/As; the book-level N/A reasons are given once, below.

**Book-level N/As (stated once, not repeated per pericope).**

- **Tool 5 (Parallels):** John is Greek narrative prose, not Hebrew poetry. Parallelism appears only in the Prologue (1:1–5, 1:10–12 — chiastic and staircase forms) and sporadically in the discourses (e.g. 3:20–21; 12:44–50). Flagged where live; otherwise N/A — non-poetic prose.
- **Tool 13 (Copycat):** live throughout, since this is narrative with characters. Reported where a character is commonly misused as a model.
- **Schnittjer Pass:** **N/A** — not a Torah passage.

## Whole-book orientation (built text-first at sweep level)

**Author's purpose (book level).** Stated by the book itself at 20:30–31 — a selection of signs written so that the reader may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and have life in his name `[T]`. The purpose statement is unique among the Gospels and governs everything before it.

**The countable spine.** Four machine-verified distributions carry most of the book's architecture, and each is a finding in its own right:

| Word | Total | Where it concentrates | What that shows |
|------|-------|----------------------|-----------------|
| πιστεύω *believe* (verb) | **92** | evenly across 1–12; collapses after ch. 13 (ch. 15, 18, 21 = 0) | Belief is the burden of the public ministry; the Farewell assumes it |
| πίστις *faith* (noun) | **0** | — | John has no noun for faith at all |
| σημεῖον *sign* | **17** | 16 of 17 before 12:37; then only 20:30 | Hard structural seam at 12:37 |
| πατήρ *Father* | **136** | ch. 14 (23), ch. 8 (20), ch. 6 (15), ch. 5 (14), ch. 10 (13) — **absent from ch. 9 and ch. 19** | The Father-discourse is the book's theological engine; its two silences are both trial scenes |
| κόσμος *world* | **78** | ch. 17 (18), ch. 15 (8), ch. 12 (7) | "World" is a *Farewell* word more than a public-ministry word |
| ἀγαπάω/ἀγάπη *love* | **44** | ch. 15 (10), ch. 13 (7), ch. 14 (7), ch. 17 (5), ch. 21 (4) | Love vocabulary is almost entirely post-13:1 |
| μένω *abide* | **34** | ch. 15 (8) | The vine concentrates a thread planted at 1:38–39 |
| ἀμὴν ἀμήν | **25 doubled sayings** | chs. 3, 5, 6, 8, 13 heaviest | John's solemn-teaching marker |
| ἐγώ εἰμι | **24 verses** | 7 predicated, 9 absolute | The identity claim, distributed as a spine |

**Structure (text-first).** Prologue (1:1–18) · opening witness and the new order, Cana to Cana (1:19–4:54) · the feast cycle (5:1–10:42) · the last sign and the turn (11:1–12:50) · the Farewell (13:1–17:26) · the hour (18:1–19:42) · resurrection and purpose (20) · epilogue (21). The pivot is 12:37–13:1, and it is countable, not impressionistic.

## Pericope 1 — John 1:1–18 — The Prologue

**Gist.** The Word, eternal and divine, agent of creation, life and light, rejected by his own and received by some, became flesh and tabernacled among us; grace and truth through Jesus Christ against the law through Moses.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* none — this is the book's opening, and its function is to plant, not resolve. But its canonical preceding movement is **Genesis 1**: "In the beginning" is not a stylistic flourish but a claim to be re-opening the canon `[T]`. *Necessity:* the Prologue exists *here* because every character in the story that follows will ask "Who are you?" and "Where are you from?" — and John gives the reader the answer before the first character asks. The reader is placed above the narrative's own epistemic horizon. *Implication:* every misunderstanding in chs. 2–12 is dramatic irony available only because of 1:1–18.

**Live tools.**
- **Structure.** A chiastic-and-staircase movement: vv.1–5 (Word with God, creation, life, light) → vv.6–8 (witness) → vv.9–13 (coming, rejection, reception) → v.14 (incarnation) → v.15 (witness again) → vv.16–18 (grace upon grace; Moses/Christ). The two John-the-Baptist insertions (6–8, 15) interrupt deliberately, marking that the Word's story is told *through* testimony `[T]`.
- **Parallels.** The one place in John where Hebrew-style staircase parallelism is unmistakable: "in him was life, and the life was the light of men; the light shines in the darkness" (vv.4–5) — each clause picks up the last word of the previous `[T]`.
- **Vocabulary.** λόγος (only here and v.14 as a title — **verified: the nominative ὁ λόγος occurs 15× in John, but as a title for Christ only 1:1, 1:14**) · ἐσκήνωσεν "pitched his tent" (v.14) · μονογενής (4× total: 1:14, 1:18, 3:16, 3:18 — **verified**) · πλήρης χάριτος καὶ ἀληθείας. *High confidence* on the σκηνόω/tabernacle resonance.
- **Repetition.** φῶς 6× in ch. 1 (the heaviest concentration in the book alongside ch. 12) · μαρτυρ- 7× in ch. 1 · "was" (ἦν) hammered against "became" (ἐγένετο) — the Word *was*, everything else *became* `[T]`.
- **Tone.** Liturgical, weighty, hymnic. Soundtrack: organ, low registers, before the narrative's first ordinary sentence.

**Tool 11.**
- **Genesis 1:1–5 → 1:1–5** *(high confidence)*. *Source context:* the creation account's opening, where God speaks and light separates darkness. *Book usage:* first use; establishes Genesis as a live source that returns at 20:1 (first day), 20:15 (gardener), 20:22 (ἐνεφύσησεν, the Gen 2:7 LXX verb). *OT-to-OT:* Gen 1 is already read through Ps 33:6 and Prov 8:22–31 in the Writings, so "the Word" arrives with wisdom-tradition freight `[I]`. *What it adds:* the Gospel is framed as a new-creation document from its first three words, and the rejection of v.11 is a fall-narrative in miniature.
- **Exodus 33–34 → 1:14–18** *(moderate–high)*. "We have seen his glory… full of grace and truth" against "no one has ever seen God" reads as Moses at Sinai, granted the goodness but refused the face; χάρις καὶ ἀλήθεια plausibly renders *ḥesed we-ʾemet* (Exod 34:6) `[I]`. *Move 1 only* at this confidence.
- **`Internal:` planted for later — a dense field.** "the Word was **God**" → 20:28 (the only two nominative θεός predications, **verified**) · light/darkness → 8:12; 9:5; 12:35–46 · "his own received him not" (τὰ ἴδια) → 13:1 (τοὺς ἰδίους); 19:27 (εἰς τὰ ἴδια) · "we have seen his glory" → 2:11; 11:40; 12:41; 17:24 · "grace and truth" → the ἀληθ- thread (55×) · "no one has ever seen God… he has made him known" → 14:9 "whoever has seen me has seen the Father". **High confidence** on all but the τὰ ἴδια chain (*moderate–high*).

**Sweep findings.**
1. **The Prologue is not an overture but an epistemic gift.** It gives the reader information no character possesses, which is the mechanism of every irony in chs. 2–12. Surfaced by: Positional Necessity, Structure, Move 4.
2. **The Baptist interruptions are structural, not clumsy.** vv.6–8 and v.15 break the hymnic flow twice to insist that the Word is known through *testimony* — the μαρτυρ- word-group runs 47× through the book and is densest here and in ch. 5 `[T]`.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.** Load-bearing for the whole book; the sweep can only gesture at the Genesis, Sinai and wisdom layers.

## Pericope 2 — John 1:19–34 — The witness of John: "Behold the Lamb of God"

**Gist.** Priests and Levites interrogate John; he denies being Christ, Elijah or the Prophet, and quotes Isaiah 40:3; the next day he identifies Jesus as the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world, and testifies to the Spirit's descent.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* the Prologue has twice inserted John as witness (1:6–8, 15) without narrating anything. *Necessity:* the narrative must begin with the *examination* that the Prologue's testimony-language anticipated — and note that the Gospel's first narrated event is an interrogation about identity ("Who are you?", v.19), which is the book's engine-question fired at the wrong man. *Implication:* John's threefold denial ("I am not… I am not… No") is the negative image of Jesus's ἐγώ εἰμι, and the contrast is structural `[I]`.

**Live tools.**
- **Repetition.** μαρτυρ- 7× in ch. 1 · "I am not" ×3 against Jesus's 24 ἐγώ εἰμι verses `[T]` · the "next day" (τῇ ἐπαύριον) sequence begins here (1:29, 1:35, 1:43 — **verified**, plus "on the third day" at 2:1), constructing an opening week.
- **Vocabulary.** ἀμνὸς τοῦ θεοῦ — **verified: ἀμνός occurs only at 1:29 and 1:36 in John**, making the title emphatic-by-scarcity rather than by repetition `[T]`. αἴρων τὴν ἁμαρτίαν τοῦ κόσμου — "takes away", not "bears".
- **Narrator's comment.** v.28 "these things took place in Bethany across the Jordan" — a locating aside; John does this repeatedly and the asides are interpretive gold elsewhere (2:21; 7:39; 12:33).

**Tool 11.**
- **Isaiah 40:3 → 1:23** *(high confidence — explicit, "as the prophet Isaiah said")*. *Source context:* the opening of Isaiah's book of consolation; the voice prepares a highway for the returning God, and the immediately following verses (40:6–8) contrast withering flesh with the abiding word of God. *Book usage:* **first of four Isaiah engagements** (1:23; 6:45; 12:38; 12:40 — verified); the last two close the public ministry, so John frames the whole public ministry between two Isaiah citations `[T]`. *OT-to-OT:* Isa 40:3 stands at the head of the new-exodus material that Isa 43 and 52–53 develop, and John will use both. *What it adds:* John the Baptist defines himself by a text about **God's own coming**, not a messenger's — the christological weight is carried by the citation, not asserted.
- **Exodus 12 / Isaiah 53:7 → 1:29, 36** *(the Passover reference high; Isa 53 moderate)*. *Source context:* Passover lambs are not sin-offerings — they are protective and covenantal; Isa 53:7's lamb is silent before shearers and does bear iniquity. The title fuses two OT lambs, and the fusion is the point `[I]`. *Book usage:* first use; the Passover frame is confirmed structurally by three Passovers (2:13; 6:4; 11:55 — verified) and cashed at 19:14, 36. *OT-to-OT:* Isa 53 is already reading the cult back into the Servant's death, so the two lambs are in prior conversation `[I]`.
- **`Internal:` planted for later — the strongest single plant in the book.** **Ἴδε ὁ ἀμνὸς τοῦ θεοῦ** (1:29, 36) → **Ἴδε ὁ ἄνθρωπος / Ἴδε ὁ βασιλεὺς ὑμῶν** (19:5, 14), spoken at the sixth hour on the Day of Preparation of the Passover, with 19:36 "not one of his bones will be broken". The interjection ἴδε is the verbal carrier (**verified: ἴδε/ἰδού at 23 verses, with 1:29, 1:36, 19:5, 19:14 among them**). **High confidence** on the verbal and calendrical link; the lamb-slaughter *timing* itself is `[S]`.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **The Gospel's first narrated scene is an identity interrogation, and its last is another (18:19–19:16).** The book opens and closes in a courtroom. Surfaced by: Positional Necessity, Tone, Move 4.
2. **ἀμνός occurs exactly twice, both here.** John does not repeat the Lamb title; he *detonates* it once, then leaves it for eighteen chapters. Preachers who treat it as a running Johannine theme are importing Revelation's ἀρνίον.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.** The Lamb title and the Isaiah 40 citation both need full triad work.

## Pericope 3 — John 1:35–51 — Come and see

**Gist.** Two of John's disciples follow Jesus; Andrew finds Simon; Jesus renames him Cephas; Philip finds Nathanael; the "come and see" chain closes with the Son of Man as the opened heaven.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* the Baptist has pointed twice. *Necessity:* the pointing must produce *following*, and John shows the gospel spreading by personal testimony before a single sign is worked — the sequence is testimony → following → more testimony. *Implication:* the first thing Jesus says in this Gospel is a question — "What are you seeking?" (v.38) — and the last thing the risen Jesus says to a seeker is "Whom are you seeking?" (20:15). Position makes the question programmatic `[T]`.

**Live tools.**
- **Structure.** A relay: Baptist → Andrew → Peter → Philip → Nathanael, with a day-marker at each hinge.
- **Vocabulary.** εὑρίσκομεν "we have found" (vv.41, 45) answering ζητεῖτε "you are seeking" (v.38) `[T]`; Μεσσίαν, Κηφᾶς — John translates both for the reader (v.41, v.42), part of the translation habit that maps the audience.
- **Narrator's comment.** Three decoder asides in fourteen verses (vv.38, 41, 42) — the narrator's translating voice established early.
- **Copycat.** Andrew and Philip are genuinely **normative pattern** — the narrative commends the fetch-your-brother move without comment, and it recurs at 4:29 and 12:21–22 `[I]`. Nathanael's scepticism is descriptive, not a model.

**Tool 11.**
- **Genesis 28:12 → 1:51** *(high confidence — verbal and structural)*. *Source context:* Jacob at Bethel, fleeing with a stolen blessing, dreams of a ladder with angels ascending and descending, wakes to say "this is the house of God and the gate of heaven", and the place is renamed. *Book usage:* first Genesis narrative use after the Gen 1 frame of the Prologue; the Jacob thread continues at 4:5–6, 12 (Jacob's well, "our father Jacob") — **verified: Ἰακώβ occurs 3×, all in ch. 4**. *OT-to-OT:* Bethel's canonical history runs from Gen 28 to Jeroboam's calves (1 Kgs 12) and Hosea's *Beth-aven*, so the name carries both founding vision and apostasy `[I]`. *What it adds:* Jesus replaces Bethel — the meeting-place is a person, not a site — and the promise is made to Nathanael, "an Israelite indeed, in whom is no deceit (δόλος)", pointedly the opposite of Jacob the deceiver. The contrast is the exegetical payload and is easy to miss.
- **`Internal:`** "What are you seeking?" (v.38) → 18:4, 7; 20:15 (**verified: the τί/τίνα ζητεῖτε form at exactly these four points**) — *planted for later*, **high**. "Come and see" (v.39, 46) → 4:29; 11:34 (**verified**) — *planted*, **moderate–high**. "You will see greater things" (v.50) → 11:40; 20:29 — *planted*, **moderate**.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **Nathanael-as-Jacob-reversed is the pericope's hinge and is almost never preached.** "No deceit" + the ladder + the renaming of Simon in the same scene make Genesis 27–28 the controlling subtext. Surfaced by: Tool 11 Move 1, Vocabulary (δόλος), Positional Necessity.
2. **Jesus's first and the risen Jesus's first words are the same question.** A four-point verified frame around the whole book.

**Solo dig: Standard** — but do not preach it without the Genesis 28 work.

## Pericope 4 — John 2:1–12 — The first sign: water into wine

**Gist.** At a wedding in Cana the wine fails; Jesus tells his mother his hour has not yet come, then turns six stone jars of purification water into the best wine; the first of his signs, manifesting his glory.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* four days of gathering testimony and the promise of "greater things" (1:50–51). *Necessity:* the promise must be honoured, and it is honoured *not* by an opened heaven but by a domestic wedding — the anticlimax is deliberate `[I]`. The sign is placed before the temple action so that the reader meets replacement-as-abundance before replacement-as-judgement. *Implication:* the "third day" (2:1) completing the opening week reads as a creation-week echo, and the wedding as its consummation `[I]` — *moderate confidence, a genuine interpretive option rather than a settled reading.*

**Live tools.**
- **Vocabulary.** ἀρχὴ τῶν σημείων "beginning of the signs" — **verified: only two signs are ever numbered (2:11 first; 4:54 second)**, which is why "the seven signs" is a preacher's scheme, not John's `[T]`. γύναι "Woman" — **verified: Jesus addresses his mother thus only here and at 19:26**. ὥρα — first of 26 uses.
- **Narrator's comment.** v.11 is a full authorial verdict: this was the first sign, it manifested his glory, and his disciples believed. The sign-and-belief formula planted here is the same formula that closes the book (20:30–31) `[T]`.
- **Tone.** Understated to the point of comedy — the master of the feast praises the bridegroom for wine he did not provide. John lets the irony sit.

**Tool 11.**
- **Conceptual: the messianic-abundance-of-wine texts** (Amos 9:13–14; Joel 3:18; Isa 25:6; Jer 31:12) *(moderate confidence)*. *Move 1 only.* The prophetic promise of the age to come is vats overflowing; six jars of ~20–30 gallons each is roughly 120–180 gallons of wine at a village wedding, which is absurd on any hospitality logic and legible on a prophetic one `[I]`.
- **Conceptual: the purification jars** — "for the Jewish rites of purification" (v.6) is the narrator's own gloss, and the detail is not decorative: the water of purification becomes the wine of the feast `[T]`.
- **`Internal:` planted for later.** "My hour has not yet come" (2:4) → 7:30; 8:20 ("his hour had not yet come") → 12:23; 13:1; 17:1 ("the hour has come") — the book's clock starts ticking here, **high**. "Woman" (2:4) → 19:26, **high** (verified, two occurrences of the address to his mother, at the two ends of the hour). "Manifested his glory" (2:11) → 11:4, 40; 12:41; 17:1–5, **high**.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **John numbers two signs and no more.** The "seven signs" structure so often preached is a homiletical construct; the text's own numbering stops at two. This should change how a series is framed. Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Repetition, Narrator's Comment. **Direct tension with most published outlines** — see Book-Overview Tensions.
2. **The hour-clock and the "Woman"-address are set running in the same verse (2:4) and resolved in the same verse (19:26).** Two threads, one plant, one payoff.

**Solo dig: Standard.**

## Pericope 5 — John 2:13–25 — The temple cleansed

**Gist.** At Passover Jesus drives out the traders, says "destroy this temple and in three days I will raise it up", and the narrator explains he meant his body; many believe at the signs but he does not entrust himself to them.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* a sign of abundant replacement at a wedding. *Necessity:* the temple action stands *here*, at the very start of the ministry rather than at its end as in the Synoptics, because John is not narrating a triggering provocation but announcing a programme — the replacement theme is stated at the outset and then worked out feast by feast `[I]`. *Implication:* the Johannine placement is a literary-theological decision, and preachers who harmonise it into Holy Week lose John's argument. *Moderate–high confidence; the "one cleansing or two" question is genuinely open and belongs in Open Questions.*

**Live tools.**
- **Narrator's comment.** Two in four verses (vv.21, 22): "but he was speaking about the temple of his body", and "when therefore he was raised… they remembered and believed the Scripture". The second is the book's clearest statement of its own hermeneutic — understanding is retrospective `[T]`.
- **Vocabulary.** ἱερόν (the precinct, vv.14–15) vs ναός (the sanctuary, vv.19–21) — **verified: John uses ἱερ-/ναο- 13× total, and the ἱερόν/ναός distinction is exactly here** `[T]`. The shift of word is the argument: he clears the *precinct* and speaks of the *sanctuary*.
- **Repetition.** πάσχα — first of three (2:13; 6:4; 11:55, verified), the structural calendar.

**Tool 11.**
- **Psalm 69:9 → 2:17** *(high confidence — explicit citation, "it is written")*. *Source context:* Psalm 69 is the lament of one suffering *because of* zeal for God's house — "reproaches of those who reproach you have fallen on me", "they gave me poison for food and for my thirst sour wine to drink" (69:21). It is a psalm of righteous suffering that expects but has not received vindication. *Book usage:* **first of three uses of Psalm 69 in John — 2:17, 15:25 (69:4, with 35:19), 19:28–29 (69:21). This makes Ps 69 the book's most-worked psalm and a LIVE source** `[T]`. And the ordering matters: John cites 69:9 at the beginning, 69:4 in the Farewell, 69:21 at the cross — following the psalm's own movement from zeal through hatred to thirst `[I]`. *OT-to-OT:* Ps 69 and Ps 22 are the twin passion-psalms and are already paired in the tradition; John uses Ps 22:18 at 19:24, adjacent to Ps 69:21 at 19:28. *What it adds:* the disciples' remembered text supplies the *cost* — zeal for the house will consume him — so the temple action is already a death-notice.
- **Zechariah 14:21 → 2:16** *(moderate)*. "There shall no longer be a trader in the house of the LORD of hosts" closes Zechariah's Booths vision; John will use Zech 9:9 at 12:15 and 12:10 at 19:37, so Zechariah is live `[I]`. *Move 1 only.*
- **`Internal:` planted for later, strongly.** "Destroy this temple… in three days I will raise it up" → 20:9 ("as yet they did not understand the Scripture, that he must rise"), **high**, and the narrator flags the delay himself. "Not entrusting himself to them… he knew what was in man" (2:24–25) → 6:64, 70–71; 13:11 — the omniscience thread, **high**.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **Psalm 69 is John's spine-psalm, and its three uses follow the psalm's own arc.** This is a Move-2 finding the overview had noted as "live" but had not traced as sequential. Surfaced by: Tool 11 Move 2 across the sweep.
2. **2:22 is John's own rule for reading him.** "They remembered… and believed" licenses the retrospective understanding John repeatedly flags (2:22; 12:16; 13:7; 20:9) — four instances, verified.

**Solo dig: Standard** — with the caveat that the two-cleansings question needs settling first.

## Pericope 6 — John 3:1–21 — Born from above

**Gist.** Nicodemus comes by night; Jesus tells him no one can see the kingdom without being born ἄνωθεν; the dialogue moves through Spirit and wind, the lifted-up serpent, and God's love for the world, to judgement as the light's arrival.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* 2:23–25 has just said many believed at the signs but Jesus did not entrust himself to them, "for he knew what was in man". *Necessity:* Nicodemus is the immediate, named case study of exactly that — "we know that you are a teacher come from God, for no one can do these signs" (3:2) is sign-based belief, and Jesus does not accept it but goes underneath it to new birth `[T]`. The chapter division at 2:25/3:1 obscures a seam that is not there: 3:1 begins "Now there was a man…" (ἄνθρωπος), picking up "what was in man" (ἀνθρώπῳ) verbatim. **This is a verified verbal join and it is the single most important positional finding in the sweep's first four chapters.** *Implication:* John 3 is not a generic evangelistic conversation; it is a demonstration that even the best sign-based belief is not birth.

**Live tools.**
- **Vocabulary.** ἄνωθεν — **verified: 5 occurrences (3:3, 3:7, 3:31, 19:11, 19:23)**, and the two later uses both mean "from above" unambiguously, which weights the sense here `[T]`. πνεῦμα/wind wordplay (v.8). βασιλεία — **verified: only 3 occurrences in John (3:3, 3:5, 18:36)**, so the kingdom vocabulary is vestigial and appears only at the two moments where a Jewish teacher and a Roman governor need it `[T]`.
- **Repetition.** ἀμὴν ἀμήν 3× in the chapter · πιστεύω 7× · γεννάω 5× in vv.3–8 (verified).
- **Linking words.** v.14 "and as (καθώς)… so must (οὕτως δεῖ)" — the comparison carries the necessity; v.16 "for (γάρ)" grounds the lifting-up in love; v.17 "for" again; vv.19–21 the ὅτι-chain of judgement.
- **Tone.** Shifts from dialogue to monologue somewhere around v.13 — where the quotation marks close is genuinely uncertain and translations differ. Flag for the preacher.

**Tool 11.**
- **Numbers 21:8–9 → 3:14** *(high confidence — explicit)*. *Source context:* Israel grumbles, is bitten by serpents, and is healed not by removal of the serpents but by *looking* at a bronze serpent lifted on a pole. The remedy is homeopathic and humiliating: the sign of the curse becomes the means of life. Immediately before, the people "spoke against God and against Moses"; immediately after, they journey on. *Book usage:* first use of Numbers; supplies ὑψόω, which recurs at 8:28 and 12:32–34 — **verified: 5 occurrences in 4 verses**, with the narrator glossing the third (12:33) as crucifixion `[T]`. *OT-to-OT:* Num 21 is picked up in Wis 16:5–7 and stands in the wilderness-grumbling cycle that Ps 78 (cited at 6:31) also narrates — so John's two Pentateuchal engagements are drawing on the same wilderness sequence `[I]`. *What it adds:* the crucifixion is framed as an object of *looking*, not of achievement, and the healing works for those already bitten. Also: the serpent is the accursed thing, which sharpens Gal 3:13 without John saying it.
- **`Internal:`** "born of water and the Spirit" (3:5) → 7:38–39 (the Spirit not yet given); 19:34 (blood and water); 20:22 (breathed) — *planted*, **moderate**. "lifted up" → 8:28; 12:32, **high**. "the light has come into the world and people loved the darkness" (3:19) → 9:39–41; 12:35–46, **high**. Nicodemus "by night" → 19:39 (the narrator explicitly back-references), **high**.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **3:1 continues 2:25 without a break; Nicodemus is the case study of unreliable sign-faith.** Verified by the ἄνθρωπος/ἀνθρώπῳ join. Surfaced by: Positional Necessity, Vocabulary, Context.
2. **"Kingdom" appears three times in John, two of them here.** John translates the Synoptic category into his own (life, birth, abiding) and only reaches for it when speaking to a rabbi and to Pilate. Surfaced by: Vocabulary count, Genre.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.**

## Pericope 7 — John 3:22–36 — He must increase

**Gist.** A dispute over purifying prompts John's disciples to complain that everyone is going to Jesus; the Baptist answers with the bridegroom's friend, "he must increase, I must decrease", and a closing discourse on the one who comes from above.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* the Nicodemus dialogue's talk of heavenly things and the one who descended. *Necessity:* the section exists here to close the Baptist's role before the narrative leaves Judea (4:1–3) and to give the reader a *positive* model of response over against Nicodemus's inconclusive one. *Implication:* the two halves of chapter 3 are a diptych — a teacher of Israel who cannot see, and a wilderness prophet who sees clearly and steps aside `[I]`.

**Live tools.**
- **Vocabulary.** ὁ φίλος τοῦ νυμφίου — the bridegroom's friend, whose joy is complete at hearing the voice; χαρὰ πεπλήρωται (v.29), the first of the "joy made full" formula (**verified: χαρ- 18× in John, with the "full joy" formula at 3:29; 15:11; 16:24; 17:13**).
- **Structure.** Where the Baptist's speech ends and the evangelist's commentary begins (v.31?) is uncertain — same problem as v.13 above. Flag both.
- **Tone.** Generous, self-effacing, almost valedictory.

**Tool 11.**
- **Conceptual: the bridegroom** (Hos 2:16–20; Isa 62:5; Jer 2:2) *(moderate)*. *Move 1 only.* YHWH is Israel's husband; John assigns the bridegroom role to Jesus without argument, which is a high christological move made in passing `[I]`.
- **`Internal:`** "the bride belongs to the bridegroom" (3:29) → 2:1–11 (a wedding where Jesus supplies the wine the bridegroom did not) — *answers §earlier*, **moderate**; this is one of the sweep's quieter finds and it runs backwards, not forwards. "He must increase" → 12:32 ("I will draw all") — *planted*, **moderate**. "He who comes from above is above all" (3:31) → 8:23 ("you are from below; I am from above"), **high**.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **The bridegroom saying at 3:29 retro-interprets Cana.** Read forwards, 2:1–11 is a sign about abundance; read back through 3:29, the man supplying the wedding wine is the bridegroom. Surfaced by: Move 4, Vocabulary.
2. **Two unmarked speech-endings in one chapter (3:13?; 3:31?)** — a translation-and-punctuation issue with real preaching consequences. Goes to Open Questions.

**Solo dig: Standard.**

## Pericope 8 — John 4:1–42 — The Samaritan woman

**Gist.** At Jacob's well Jesus asks a Samaritan woman for a drink, offers living water, exposes her marital history, redirects the worship question to spirit and truth, declares ἐγώ εἰμι, and many Samaritans believe — first on her word, then on his.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* Nicodemus — male, Jewish, named, a ruler, by night, inconclusive. *Necessity:* the narrative places the exact inverse immediately after — female, Samaritan, unnamed, disreputable, at noon, conclusive `[T]`. The pairing is deliberate and the chapter break between them is the only thing obscuring it. *Implication:* John 3 and John 4 are one argument about who receives the birth from above, and preaching either alone halves it.

**Live tools.**
- **Structure.** Five movements: the well (vv.1–15), the husbands (16–19), worship (20–26), the disciples and the harvest (27–38), the Samaritans (39–42).
- **Vocabulary.** ὕδωρ ζῶν "living water" — running water in ordinary speech, which is why she misunderstands `[T]`. προσκυνέω 9× in vv.20–24 alone — the densest concentration in the book. ἐγώ εἰμι (v.26) — **verified: the first of the nine absolute uses**.
- **Narrator's comment.** v.9 "for Jews have no dealings with Samaritans" — an explanatory aside for readers outside the culture, part of the audience-mapping evidence `[T]`. v.44 (the prophet without honour) is a notorious crux.
- **Repetition.** ὥρα 6× in ch. 4 — the heaviest of any chapter (verified), including "the hour is coming, and is now here" (v.23), the first of the realised-eschatology formula (also 5:25).
- **Copycat.** The woman is a **normative pattern** in one respect only — she leaves her jar and tells the town (vv.28–29), and the narrator credits her testimony (v.39). Her marital history is descriptive and the sermon that dwells on it is usually doing something else.

**Tool 11.**
- **Genesis 29 (the betrothal type-scene) → 4:1–26** *(moderate–high — narrative pattern, not verbal)*. *Source context:* the well type-scene — a man travels, meets a woman at a well, water is drawn, she runs home, a betrothal follows (Gen 24 Rebekah; Gen 29 Rachel; Exod 2 Zipporah). *Book usage:* the Jacob thread is live in this very chapter (4:5, 6, 12 — verified, the only three Jacob references in John). *OT-to-OT:* the three well-scenes are already a self-conscious canonical set. *What it adds:* every beat is present and the ending is transformed — she runs home, but what follows is not a betrothal but a town's faith. Given 3:29 has just named Jesus the bridegroom, the type-scene is being cashed. **This is the adjacency/narrative-pattern check firing: the frame was established next door.**
- **Zechariah 14:8 / Ezekiel 47 (living water) → 4:10–14** *(moderate)*. *Move 1 only*; the fuller use is at 7:38.
- **`Internal:`** "the hour is coming and now is" (4:23) → 5:25, **high**. "living water" (4:10) → 7:38–39 (with the narrator's Spirit gloss), **high**. "I who speak to you am he" (4:26) → 8:24, 28, 58; 18:5–8, **high**. "other sheep not of this fold" is prefigured by the Samaritan harvest → 10:16; 11:52, *planted*, **moderate–high**. "he had to pass through Samaria" (ἔδει, 4:4) → the δεῖ of 3:14 and 12:34 — divine necessity language, **moderate**.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **Chapters 3 and 4 are a matched pair and should be preached as one argument.** Every variable is inverted and the outcomes are opposite. Surfaced by: Positional Necessity, Structure, Tone.
2. **The betrothal type-scene is licensed by 3:29, not imported.** The bridegroom title is supplied one pericope earlier, which is what raises this from "uncertain" to "moderate–high".

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.**

## Pericope 9 — John 4:43–54 — The second sign

**Gist.** In Galilee an official begs Jesus to come down and heal his son; Jesus rebukes sign-seeking, speaks a word at a distance, the boy recovers at that hour, and the household believes — the second sign.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* Samaritans who believed **without a sign**, on a word (4:41–42). *Necessity:* the contrast is immediate and pointed — "unless you see signs and wonders you will not believe" (4:48) lands on a Galilean Jew directly after Samaritans believed on hearing alone `[T]`. *Implication:* the sign that follows is deliberately the least spectacular kind — a word spoken at a distance, verified afterwards by a time-check. John is dismantling sign-faith while narrating a sign.

**Live tools.**
- **Vocabulary.** δεύτερον σημεῖον (v.54) — the second and last numbered sign. ὥρα (vv.52, 53) — the time-check is the point.
- **Repetition.** "Your son lives" (ζῇ) 3× in vv.50–53 — the word repeated at each stage of the man's growing belief `[T]`.
- **Structure.** A three-stage belief: he believes the word (v.50), he learns the hour (v.52), "he himself believed, and all his household" (v.53). Belief is shown in motion, which is characteristic.

**Tool 11.**
- **`Internal:`** the Cana-to-Cana inclusio: 2:11 "the first of his signs… at Cana" ↔ 4:54 "the second sign… when he had come from Judea to Galilee" — *answers §earlier*, **high**, and it brackets 2:1–4:54 as a discrete movement. This is a verified structural bracket and the strongest reason to treat 1:19–4:54 as one arc. "Your son lives" → 11:25–26 ("whoever lives and believes in me shall never die") — *planted*, **moderate**.
- No external OT citation. *Move 1–3 N/A — no quotation or high-confidence allusion in this pericope.*

**Sweep finding.**
1. **The Cana–Cana bracket is countable and closes the book's first movement.** The two numbered signs are its posts.

**Solo dig: Standard.**

## Pericope 10 — John 5:1–18 — Healing at Bethesda; the Sabbath charge

**Gist.** At a feast Jesus heals a man ill thirty-eight years, tells him to take up his mat, and — because it is a Sabbath — the authorities pursue him; his answer, "My Father is working until now, and I am working", escalates the charge to blasphemy.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* two Cana signs bracketing a movement of spreading belief. *Necessity:* the narrative now needs its first hostility, and it needs it in Jerusalem at a feast, because from here the book runs on the festal calendar and on a lethal opposition. **5:18 is the hinge of the whole Gospel's plot**: "this was why the Jews were seeking all the more to kill him" — the first of eleven kill/seize notices (**verified: ἀποκτείνω at 5:18; 7:1, 19, 20, 25; 8:37, 40; 11:53; 12:10; 16:2; 18:31**, plus πιάζω at 7:30, 32, 44; 8:20; 10:39; 11:57). *Implication:* everything from ch. 5 to ch. 12 is lived under a death sentence already passed.

**Live tools.**
- **Narrator's comment.** v.9b "Now that day was the Sabbath" — a single clause that converts a healing into a capital case `[T]`. v.18 is the narrator's own summary of the charge, and it names *two* offences: Sabbath-breaking and calling God his own Father, making himself equal with God.
- **Vocabulary.** ἴσον τῷ θεῷ (v.18) — the highest christological statement so far, and it is on the lips of opponents, reported by the narrator without correction.
- **Textual variants.** **5:3b–4** (the angel troubling the water) is absent from the earliest manuscripts and is bracketed or footnoted in modern editions; **verified: SBLGNT omits 5:4 entirely.** *High confidence.* Preachers using the "waiting for the stirring" motif should know they are preaching a later gloss.
- **Copycat.** The healed man is not a model. He does not know who healed him (v.13), and his subsequent report to the authorities (v.15) is at best ambiguous. Contrast the blind man of ch. 9 deliberately.

**Tool 11.**
- **Conceptual: God's Sabbath work** *(moderate–high)*. Jewish reflection held that God does not cease from sustaining, judging and giving life on the Sabbath, since births and deaths continue; Jesus claims that same non-ceasing prerogative `[S]` — this is background, not text, and is properly tagged.
- **`Internal:` planted for later.** "Do you want to be healed?" and the thirty-eight years → 9:1 ("blind from birth") — the two Sabbath healings are a deliberate pair, and 9:14 repeats the Sabbath note, **high**. "Sin no more, that nothing worse may happen" (5:14) → 9:2–3 (where Jesus *denies* the sin-suffering link) — *answers §earlier*, **moderate–high**, and the apparent tension is a live pastoral question. "Seeking to kill him" (5:18) → the whole kill-thread above, **high**.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **5:18 is the plot's ignition, and it is a narrator's verdict, not a character's accusation.** Surfaced by: Narrator's Comment, Repetition (the kill-thread), Positional Necessity.
2. **The two Sabbath healings (5 and 9) are a matched pair with opposite outcomes** — one man betrays his healer, one man worships him. Verified by the shared Sabbath note and the shared chronic-condition framing.

**Solo dig: Standard.**

## Pericope 11 — John 5:19–47 — The Son and the four witnesses

**Gist.** Jesus answers the blasphemy charge with a discourse on the Son's derived-but-equal authority to give life and judge, then calls four witnesses — John, the works, the Father, and the Scriptures — and charges his hearers with not believing Moses.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* the charge of 5:18 — Sabbath-breaking and self-equation with God. *Necessity:* the discourse is a *defence speech*, and its structure is forensic because the charge was forensic. This is why the witness vocabulary peaks here: **μαρτυρ- occurs 11× in ch. 5, the heaviest of any chapter (verified)**. *Implication:* John 5 is a trial scene, and it sets the template for chs. 7–10; reading it as abstract Christology loses the courtroom.

**Live tools.**
- **Structure.** Two halves: the Son's authority (vv.19–30) and the witnesses (vv.31–47). The seam is v.31 ("if I alone bear witness…").
- **Repetition.** πατήρ 14× in ch. 5 (verified) · μαρτυρ- 11× · ζωή 7× · κρίνω/κρίσις 7× — the four-word cluster (Father, witness, life, judgement) *is* the chapter.
- **The defendant is the judge — 5:22 with 5:27.** "The Father judges no one, but has given all judgement (τὴν κρίσιν πᾶσαν) to the Son" (5:22), and "he has given him authority to execute judgement, because he is the Son of Man" (5:27). `[T]` **High.** Inside a defence speech against a capital charge, the accused states that all judgement has been transferred to him. **This is the hinge of the book's forensic architecture and the sweep's first pass under-weighted it**: the reversal by which the one on trial is the one before whom all are tried is not merely an ironic effect of the passion narrative but an explicit claim made in ch. 5 and then dramatised for fourteen chapters. Cross-report to the judgement thread: 3:19; 5:22, 27; 9:39; 12:31; 16:8, 11 (verified).
- **Linking words.** The γάρ-chain of vv.19–23 (the Son does nothing of his own accord, *for* the Father loves the Son and shows him all…), and the ἵνα of v.23 (that all may honour the Son as they honour the Father) — the purpose-clause is the christological climax.
- **Vocabulary.** ἀμὴν ἀμήν 3× (vv.19, 24, 25) marking the movement's stages. "The hour is coming, and is now here" (v.25) against "an hour is coming" (v.28) — realised and future eschatology deliberately juxtaposed, unreconciled `[T]`.

**Tool 11.**
- **Deuteronomy 19:15 (two or three witnesses) → 5:31–37** *(moderate–high, conceptual)*. *Source context:* the law of evidence; a single witness cannot establish a charge. *Book usage:* John will cite the same principle explicitly at 8:17 ("in your Law it is written that the testimony of two men is true") — **verified**, so this is a source John returns to, and the second use is explicit. *OT-to-OT:* the two-witness rule stands behind Num 35:30 and is invoked in the prophets' covenant lawsuits `[I]`. *What it adds:* Jesus is not evading the rule but satisfying it — and the witnesses he calls exceed the statutory minimum.
- **Moses / the Scriptures → 5:39, 45–47** *(high confidence — explicit)*. "You search the Scriptures… it is they that bear witness about me" and "he wrote of me". *Source context:* not one text but the Pentateuch as a whole. *Book usage:* Moses is named 11× in John (**verified: 1:17, 45; 3:14; 5:45, 46; 6:32; 7:19, 22, 23; 9:28, 29**), and the pattern is consistent — Moses is invoked *by opponents* as their authority and *by Jesus* as his witness. *What it adds:* **this is John's own statement of his hermeneutic** and the warrant for every OT reading in the book.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **5:39 and 5:46 license the whole Gospel's use of the OT.** Any Tool 11 work anywhere in John is doing what 5:39 says the Scriptures do.
2. **John 5 is the trial-template.** Forensic vocabulary, witness-law, charge and defence — the shape recurs at 7:14–52, 8:12–59, 9:13–34, and finally 18:19–19:16. Surfaced by: Repetition, Structure, Genre, and confirmed across the sweep.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.**

## Pericope 12 — John 6:1–21 — Feeding, kingship refused, and the sea

**Gist.** Near Passover Jesus feeds five thousand from five barley loaves; the crowd tries to make him king; he withdraws, then comes to the disciples on the sea with "ἐγώ εἰμι; do not be afraid."

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* a Jerusalem trial scene ending in "you refuse to come to me that you may have life" (5:40). *Necessity:* the scene relocates to Galilee and to a crowd that *does* come — and shows that coming for bread is not coming for life. The Passover note (6:4) is unmotivated by the plot and is therefore interpretive: John wants the manna and the lamb in the reader's ear `[T]`. *Implication:* the two signs here are a matched pair — provision and presence — and the second is a theophany.

**Live tools.**
- **Vocabulary.** κριθίνους "barley" (v.9) — John alone specifies it, and barley is the Elisha detail. συναγάγετε / συνήγαγον (vv.12–13) "gather" — **verified: συνάγω occurs 4× in John (6:12, 13; 11:47, 52)**, which makes the fragment-gathering verbally continuous with 11:52's "gather into one the scattered children of God" `[T]`. This is a genuine and, so far as the sweep can tell, under-noticed link.
- **Narrator's comment.** v.6 "he said this to test him, for he himself knew what he would do" — the reader is told the disciples are being examined.
- **Repetition.** ἄρτος begins its run (13× in ch. 6, verified).

**Tool 11.**
- **Exodus 16 / Psalm 78:24 → anticipated here, cited at 6:31.** Handled at Pericope 13.
- **2 Kings 4:42–44 (Elisha's feeding) → 6:5–13** *(moderate)*. *Source context:* twenty *barley* loaves feed a hundred over a servant's objection, "and they ate and had some left". *Book usage:* first and only use; John is not tracking Kings. *What it adds:* the type is prophet-feeding and the escalation is the point — five loaves, five thousand, twelve baskets. `[I]`
- **Job 9:8 / Psalm 77:19 / Isaiah 43:1–2, 10–13 (sea-theophany) → 6:16–21** *(moderate–high)*. *Source context:* "who alone trampled the waves of the sea" (Job 9:8); "your way was through the sea, yet your footprints were unseen" (Ps 77:19); and Isaiah's "I am he… fear not… when you pass through the waters" (43:2, 10). *Book usage:* Isaiah is live (1:23; and later 6:45; 12:38, 40). *OT-to-OT:* Isa 43's ἐγώ εἰμι is itself a reworking of Exod 3:14, and Ps 77 rehearses the Red Sea — so the three texts are already one exodus-theophany cluster `[I]`. *What it adds:* the sea-crossing is not a rescue anecdote but an identification: the one who feeds is the one who walks the sea and says "I am".
- **`Internal:`** the refusal of coerced kingship (6:15) → 12:13–15; 18:36; 19:15 — *planted*, **moderate–high**. "Gather the fragments, that nothing may be lost" (6:12) → 17:12 ("not one of them is lost"); 18:9 — *planted*, **moderate–high**, and the ἀπόλλυμι verb carries.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **"Gather… that nothing may be lost" is a plant, not housekeeping.** The verbal chain runs 6:12 → 11:52 → 17:12 → 18:9. Surfaced by: Vocabulary (συνάγω count), Move 4.
2. **The sea-crossing is a theophany, and it is the first absolute ἐγώ εἰμι after 4:26.** Preaching it as a lesson in trusting Jesus in storms drops the Isaiah 43 register.

**Solo dig: Standard** (the ⭐ weight in this chapter sits on the discourse).

## Pericope 13 — John 6:22–59 — I am the bread of life

**Gist.** The crowd follows across the lake; Jesus exposes their motive, redirects them from perishable food to the Son of Man, is met with the manna proof-text, and answers with himself as the bread from heaven — culminating in eating his flesh and drinking his blood.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* a feeding, a refused kingship, and a sea-theophany. *Necessity:* the discourse exists here because the crowd has drawn exactly the wrong conclusion from the sign (6:26), and John's method is sign-then-discourse — the discourse is the sign's authorised interpretation. *Implication:* the offence of vv.51–58 is not accidental; the passage is designed to sift, and it succeeds (6:66).

**Live tools.**
- **Repetition.** ἄρτος 13× · ζωή 11× in ch. 6, the highest of any chapter (verified) · "come down from heaven" 7× · "raise him up on the last day" 4× (vv.39, 40, 44, 54) — a refrain, not a repetition.
- **Vocabulary.** τρώγω (vv.54, 56, 57, 58) — a coarser verb than ἐσθίω, "chew/gnaw"; the shift is deliberate and intensifying `[T]`, *high confidence*. γογγύζω (vv.41, 43, 61) — **verified: γογγυ- occurs 5× in John, at 6:41, 43, 61 and 7:12, 32** — the LXX wilderness-grumbling verb, used of the people in Exod 16 and Num 11. That the crowd cites the manna text and then *grumbles* is the chapter's sharpest irony `[T]`.
- **Linking words.** The ground-clauses of vv.37–40, 44–45, 65 — "all that the Father gives me will come to me… no one can come unless the Father draws him… this is why I told you that no one can come to me unless it is granted him by the Father". Three statements of the same claim at three stages.
- **Difficult verses.** vv.53–58 and the eucharistic question; v.63 ("the flesh is of no avail") is the internal control. Also the Father's giving/drawing and human responsibility asserted together without balancing.

**Tool 11.**
- **Psalm 78:24 (with Exodus 16:4) → 6:31** *(high — explicit citation)*. *Source context:* Psalm 78 is Asaph's indictment-by-history: they saw the wonders and "did not believe" (78:22), ate and were filled but "did not stop their craving" (78:29–31), and the generation perished. The manna verse sits inside a psalm about signs met with unbelief. *Book usage:* first Psalter citation after Ps 69:9 at 2:17; the Psalms are live. And Ps 78's own thesis — many signs, no belief — is restated by John in his own voice at 12:37, which is arguably a structural quotation of the psalm's logic `[I]`. *OT-to-OT:* Ps 78:24 is already a meditation on Exod 16 and Num 11, and Deut 8:3 ("man does not live by bread alone") is the same tradition. *What it adds:* the crowd's proof-text is a text about people exactly like them; Jesus's correction is threefold — not Moses but the Father, not gave but *gives*, not manna but himself.
- **Isaiah 54:13 → 6:45** *(high — explicit, "it is written in the Prophets")*. *Source context:* the song of the restored barren woman, immediately downstream of the fourth Servant Song; "all your children shall be taught by the LORD" is new-covenant fruit of Isa 53's atoning work. *Book usage:* second of four Isaiah engagements (1:23 → 6:45 → 12:38, 40). *OT-to-OT:* Isa 54:13 is in canonical conversation with Jer 31:34 ("they shall all know me"). *What it adds:* the Father's "drawing" (v.44) is identified as prophetic teaching, and because Isa 54 flows from Isa 53, election-language and atonement-language are joined at their OT root — which is exactly what v.51 then supplies.
- **`Internal:`** "I am the bread of life" (6:35) is the **first predicated ἐγώ εἰμι** — *planted for the series* (8:12; 10:7, 11; 11:25; 14:6; 15:1), **high**. "Raise him up on the last day" → 11:24–25, **high**. "One of you is a devil" (6:70–71) → 13:2, 27; 18:2–5, **high**.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **The chapter is built out of its opponents' own text.** Ps 78 is a psalm about signs-without-faith, quoted by a crowd exhibiting signs-without-faith. Surfaced by: Tool 11 Move 1, Repetition (γογγύζω), Tone.
2. **γογγύζω appears 5× in John, all in chs. 6–7.** The wilderness register is confined to the two feast-chapters where manna and water are at issue. Verified.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.**

## Pericope 14 — John 6:60–71 — Many turned back

**Gist.** Many disciples find the saying hard and leave; Jesus asks the Twelve if they will go too; Peter answers "to whom shall we go?"; Jesus names one of them a devil.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* a discourse designed to offend. *Necessity:* John needs to show the sifting *happening*, and to show that defection is not a failure of the message but its function (6:64–65 refer back to 6:44). *Implication:* the Twelve are introduced into this Gospel **only here** — **verified: δώδεκα occurs 6× (6:13, 67, 70, 71; 11:9; 20:24)**, and as a designation for the disciple-band only at 6:67, 70, 71 and 20:24. John withholds the group until the moment it shrinks to them.

**Live tools.**
- **Narrator's comment.** vv.64, 71 — two asides, one about Jesus's foreknowledge of unbelief and betrayal, one identifying Judas. The narrator will not let the reader be surprised.
- **Vocabulary.** σκληρός "hard" (v.60); σκανδαλίζει (v.61) — **verified: σκανδαλ- occurs only twice in John (6:61; 16:1)**, and the second use is about disciples being made to stumble by persecution. The two uses bracket the disciple-defection theme `[T]`.
- **Tone.** Bleak. This is the low point of the public ministry and the sermon should not rush past it.

**Tool 11.**

## Pericope 15 — John 7:1–52 — Tabernacles: rivers of living water

**Gist.** Jesus stays in Galilee because Judea is lethal, then goes up to Booths privately; midfeast teaching provokes the "where is he from?" dispute; on the last great day he cries out the living-water invitation; the crowd divides and the officers return empty-handed.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* mass defection in Galilee (6:66) and a named betrayer. *Necessity:* the confrontation must now return to Jerusalem and escalate — and it must do so at **Booths**, because Booths is the feast of water-libation and temple-illumination, which supplies the two claims that govern 7:37–39 and 8:12 `[T]` for the placement, `[S]` for the rites. **7:1 opens with the kill-notice already in force** ("the Jews were seeking to kill him"), so the whole unit runs under the sentence passed at 5:18. *Implication:* 7:1–10:21 is one feast-panel, and the chapter divisions inside it are not seams.

**Live tools.**
- **Repetition.** ζητέω 11× in ch. 7 — **the heaviest concentration of any chapter (verified)**; the chapter is *about* seeking, and the seekers are looking to kill, to arrest, and to find. σχίσμα (v.43) — **verified: 3 occurrences in John, at 7:43; 9:16; 10:19**, one per stage of the Booths panel, the third marked "again". That is a structural refrain.
- **Narrator's comment.** v.5 "for not even his brothers believed in him" · v.30 "because his hour had not yet come" · **v.39 "Now this he said about the Spirit… for as yet the Spirit had not been given, because Jesus was not yet glorified"** — the single most important decoder aside in the book after 2:21, because it links Spirit-giving to glorification and glorification to the cross `[T]`.
- **Vocabulary.** κράζω (vv.28, 37) — **verified: 9 occurrences in John; Jesus cries out at 7:28, 7:37, 11:43, 12:44, and the crowd cries out at 12:13, 18:40, 19:6, 19:12, 19:15.** The verb Jesus uses to summon the thirsty is the verb the crowd will use to demand his crucifixion. **This is a sweep-only finding and it is strong.**
- **Structure.** Before the feast (1–13) · midfeast (14–36) · last day (37–52). The "hour not yet" note at v.30 and again at 8:20 brackets the teaching.
- **Difficult verses.** v.8 ("I am not going up to this feast") followed by v.10 (he went up) — a real integrity question with a textual dimension (οὐκ vs οὔπω). Flag honestly.

**Tool 11.**
- **7:38 "as the Scripture has said, out of his heart will flow rivers of living water"** *(the citation is explicit; **the source is unidentifiable** — uncertain)*. *Source context:* no OT text reads this way. Candidates: Zech 14:8 (living waters from Jerusalem, at Booths); Ezek 47:1–12 (river from the temple); Isa 58:11 ("a spring of water whose waters do not fail"); Prov 18:4; Ps 78:15–16 (water from the rock — and Ps 78 is already cited at 6:31, which raises its prior). *Book usage:* the living-water image is live from 4:10–14. *OT-to-OT:* Zech 14 and Ezek 47 are already in conversation as temple-river texts. *What it adds:* whichever source, the claim is that the eschatological temple-river flows from a person. **The punctuation crux compounds it:** if "out of his heart" refers to the believer, the promise is of overflow; if to Christ, it is a source-claim answered at 19:34. *Uncertain — the single highest-value Logos target in the book.*
- **Micah 5:2 / Psalm 89 → 7:42** *(high — explicit, on opponents' lips)*. *Source context:* the Bethlehem oracle and the Davidic seed. *What it adds:* the crowd's objection is *scripturally correct and factually wrong*, and John lets it stand without correcting them — dramatic irony aimed at a reader who knows the birthplace. `[I]` on the irony.
- **`Internal:`** "his hour had not yet come" (7:30) → 8:20 → 12:23; 13:1, **high**. "rivers of living water… he said this about the Spirit" (7:38–39) → 19:34 (blood and water); 20:22 (breathed), **moderate–high** — *planted*. "Does our law judge a man without first giving him a hearing?" (Nicodemus, 7:51) → 19:39, **high**.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **κράζω is a two-edged verb.** Jesus cries out an invitation (7:37); the crowd cries out for his death (19:6, 15). Four uses each, verified, and the reversal is unmarked.
2. **σχίσμα ×3 is the Booths panel's structural refrain** — 7:43, 9:16, 10:19 — which is the strongest internal evidence that 7:1–10:21 is one unit. Independently reached; the existing section-level dig concurs `[S: dig]`.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH** (already has one; the 7:38 crux still unresolved).

## Pericope 16 — [John 7:53–8:11] — The woman caught in adultery

**Gist.** Scribes and Pharisees bring a woman taken in adultery; Jesus writes on the ground, says "let him who is without sin among you be the first to throw a stone", and dismisses her without condemnation.

**Textual variants — the decisive tool here.** The passage is **absent from the earliest and best Greek manuscripts** (𝔓⁶⁶, 𝔓⁷⁵, Sinaiticus, Vaticanus), is absent from the earliest versions and from the Greek fathers' commentaries on John, and where it does appear it floats — after John 7:36, after John 21:25, and after Luke 21:38 in different witnesses. Its Greek vocabulary is un-Johannine. **Verified at the level of the edition used here: SBLGNT brackets it, and the 866-verse count of this edition against the 879 of the KJV is accounted for by these twelve verses plus 5:4.** *High confidence* that it is not part of John's text as composed; the question of whether it preserves a genuine tradition is separate and much more open.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* Nicodemus's "does our law judge a man without a hearing?" (7:51) and the officers' failure to arrest. *Necessity:* whoever placed it here placed it well — a scene about judging without hearing, immediately after a complaint about judging without hearing, and immediately before "I judge no one… you judge according to the flesh" (8:15). The insertion is intelligent. But intelligent placement by a later hand is not authorial placement, and the distinction must be preached honestly `[I]`.

**Live tools.**
- **Vocabulary.** ἀναμάρτητος "without sin" — a NT *hapax*. κατακρίνω — also unusual for John, who prefers κρίνω.
- **Copycat.** The commonest misuse: "do not judge" as a general prohibition. The scene is about *these* accusers' standing to execute, not about moral discernment; and it ends with "go, and from now on sin no more".

**Tool 11.**
- **Deuteronomy 17:7 / 19:15–21 (witnesses cast the first stone)** *(moderate–high)*. *Move 1:* the law requires the witnesses' hands to be first upon the condemned — so "let him who is without sin be the first" is a legal thrust, not a sentimental one. *Book usage:* the two-witness rule is live in John at 8:17 `[T]` — though whether the interpolator knew that is unknowable.
- **`Internal:`** *If read in place:* "neither do I condemn you" ↔ 3:17 ("God did not send his Son into the world to condemn the world"), **moderate**. The nil-return caveat: internal-echo work on an interpolated passage tests the interpolator's reading of John, not John's own composition — a distinction worth stating in the report.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **The strongest preaching decision here is a prior one: whether to preach it at all, and if so, how to say what it is.** The sweep's recommendation is that it can be preached — the church has received it — but only with the manuscript facts stated plainly, which most congregations find strengthening rather than unsettling.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH** if preached — the textual work must be done properly, not gestured at.

## Pericope 17 — John 8:12–30 — The light of the world; the witness dispute

**Gist.** "I am the light of the world"; the Pharisees challenge his self-witness; Jesus invokes the two-witness rule with the Father as co-witness, says they will die in their sins unless they believe ἐγώ εἰμι, and points to the lifting-up as the moment of recognition.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* the Booths water-cry (7:37–39). *Necessity:* Booths' second great symbol is light — the temple courts illuminated by great lamps — so the light-claim belongs in this feast-panel and nowhere else `[T]` for the placement, `[S]` for the rite. And the witness dispute resumes John 5:31–37 verbatim in substance, which is why 8:17 can cite "your Law" on two witnesses. *Implication:* chapters 5 and 8 are the same trial, adjourned and resumed.

**Live tools.**
- **Repetition.** ἀληθ- 13× in ch. 8 — **the heaviest of any chapter (verified)** · πατήρ 20× in ch. 8, second only to ch. 14 (verified) · κρίνω 6×.
- **Vocabulary.** ἐγώ εἰμι absolute at vv.24, 28 — deliberately unqualified, and v.28 attaches recognition to the lifting-up: "when you have lifted up the Son of Man, then you will know that I am he" `[T]`.
- **Linking words.** v.14 "even if (κἂν) I bear witness about myself, my testimony is true, **for** (ὅτι) I know where I came from" — the ground is origin, which is the chapter's whole issue.
- **Narrator's comment.** v.20 "no one arrested him, because his hour had not yet come" — the second of the paired notices (7:30; 8:20), verified verbatim.

**Tool 11.**
- **Deuteronomy 19:15 → 8:17** *(high — explicit, "in your Law it is written")*. *Source context:* the two-or-three-witness rule from the law of evidence; the surrounding material concerns malicious witnesses and the cities of refuge. *Book usage:* **second use — the principle was operating implicitly at 5:31–37 and is now named.** This is a clean Move 2: John returns to the same legal source at a later stage of the same trial `[T]`. *OT-to-OT:* Deut 19 develops Num 35:30 and stands behind the prophetic covenant-lawsuit form. *What it adds:* "your Law" is pointed — Jesus argues on his opponents' own statutory ground and satisfies it.
- **Exodus 3:14 / Isaiah 43:10 → 8:24, 28** *(high)*. *Source context:* Exod 3 is the commissioning at the bush, where the name is given as the guarantee of presence in deliverance; Isa 43:10 ("that you may know and believe me and understand that I am he") is set in a courtroom scene where YHWH summons witnesses. *Book usage:* the absolute ἐγώ εἰμι has already sounded at 4:26 and 6:20 and will sound at 8:58; 13:19; 18:5–8 (verified). *OT-to-OT:* Isa 43 is itself an exodus-reworking, so citing it activates Exod 3 twice over. *What it adds:* **Isa 43:10's setting is a trial with witnesses — precisely the setting of John 8.** The allusion is not decorative; it supplies the scene.
- **`Internal:`** "light of the world" (8:12) → 9:5 (repeated immediately before the sign that dramatises it), **high** · → 1:4–9; 12:35–46, **high**. "You will die in your sin" (8:21, 24) → 9:41 ("your guilt remains"), **moderate–high**. "When you have lifted up" (8:28) → 12:32–33, **high**.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **John 8's "I am" sayings are set inside Isaiah 43's courtroom, which is also the scene John has been staging since ch. 5.** Surfaced by: Tool 11 Move 1 + Move 3, Repetition (witness/truth counts), Genre.
2. **The light-claim is made and then *enacted* one pericope later (9:5).** John's sign-then-discourse order is reversed here — discourse, then sign — and the reversal is a deliberate feature of the Booths panel.

**Solo dig: Standard.**

## Pericope 18 — John 8:31–59 — Before Abraham was, I am

**Gist.** To those who had believed him Jesus speaks of abiding, truth and freedom; the exchange escalates through Abrahamic descent to paternity — "you are of your father the devil" — and ends with "before Abraham was, I am" and an attempted stoning.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* "as he was saying these things, many believed in him" (8:30). *Necessity:* **the discourse is addressed to believers** (v.31, "the Jews who had believed him") and it is the harshest in the Gospel. That juxtaposition is the pericope's engine and it is invisible if the sermon starts at v.31 without v.30 `[T]`. *Implication:* John is showing belief that does not abide — which is exactly the point of 2:23–25 and 6:66. The three scenes form a series on unreliable belief.

**Live tools.**
- **Repetition.** Ἀβραάμ 9× — **verified: all nine occurrences of Abraham in John are in this pericope (8:33–58)** · πατήρ 20× in ch. 8, most of them contesting paternity · ἐλεύθερος/δοῦλος 5× in vv.32–36 (verified).
- **Vocabulary.** μένω (v.31, "if you abide in my word") — the first appearance of the abiding-condition that ch. 15 will develop; **verified: μένω 34× in John, 8× of them in ch. 15**. διάβολος (v.44) — **verified: only 4 devil/Satan references in John (6:70; 8:44; 13:2, 27)**, so the paternity charge is exceptional, not routine polemic.
- **Tone.** The most violent exchange in the Gospel. Preachers must decide in advance how to voice v.44 — it is a pastoral landmine and has a long history of misuse.
- **Difficult verses.** v.44 ("your father the devil") is the single most weaponised verse in John. It is spoken to a specific group in a specific dispute about paternity, and John's own frame is that the issue is *response to Jesus*, not ethnicity (cf. 4:22, "salvation is from the Jews"). Say so explicitly from the pulpit.

**Tool 11.**
- **Genesis 17 / 22 (Abrahamic descent and "your father Abraham rejoiced to see my day") → 8:33–58** *(the descent-claim high; the "my day" reference moderate)*. *Source context:* which Abrahamic moment is "my day"? Candidates: Gen 17:17 (Abraham laughed), Gen 22 (the Akedah and "the LORD will provide"), Gen 15 (the covenant vision). *Book usage:* Abraham appears only here. *OT-to-OT:* Gen 22's "on the mount of the LORD it shall be provided" is already read forward within Genesis. *What it adds:* the claim is not merely pre-existence but that Abraham's own hope had Jesus as its object — which converts the genealogical argument into a christological one. *Uncertain which Abrahamic text is in view — a good Logos question.*
- **Exodus 3:14 → 8:58** *(high — and the strongest instance in the book)*. *Source context:* as above. *Book usage:* the climactic absolute ἐγώ εἰμι. *What it adds:* the tense-clash is the argument — "before Abraham *came to be* (γενέσθαι), I *am* (εἰμί)" — being against becoming, exactly the Prologue's ἦν/ἐγένετο contrast (1:1–3). **The stoning attempt (8:59) is the hearers' own commentary on what they heard**, and it is the best available evidence for how to read the verse `[T]`.
- **`Internal:`** the ἦν/ἐγένετο contrast of 1:1–3 → 8:58, **high**, and this is a Prologue plant cashed fifty-seven chapters-worth of narrative later. "Abide in my word" (8:31) → 15:4–10, **high**. Stones taken up (8:59; 10:31) → 11:38–41; 20:1 (the stone at the tomb) — *planted*, **moderate** (verbal carrier λίθος verified, but the theological link is inferential).

**Sweep findings.**
1. **8:31 addresses believers, and the sweep now has three scenes of unreliable belief (2:23–25; 6:60–66; 8:31–59).** Read together they are a sustained Johannine argument that belief must abide. This is a cross-passage finding not visible from any one pericope.
2. **The Prologue's ἦν/ἐγένετο grammar is the key to 8:58.** Verified verbal contrast; it means the "I am" is not a stray theophany-formula but the payoff of the book's opening sentence.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.**

## Pericope 19 — John 9:1–41 — The man born blind

**Gist.** Jesus heals a man blind from birth on a Sabbath, using mud and the pool of Siloam; the man is interrogated four times, his parents evade for fear of expulsion, he is cast out, Jesus finds him and he worships; the Pharisees are told their guilt remains.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* "I am the light of the world" (8:12), and an attempted stoning. *Necessity:* the light-claim must be *enacted*, and 9:5 repeats it verbatim before the sign — the discourse supplies the interpretation in advance for once. The chapter also resumes the Sabbath-healing controversy of ch. 5. *Implication:* this is the fullest narrative in John, and it is a trial narrative — four interrogations, a witness, an intimidated family, and a verdict.

**Live tools.**
- **Narrator's comment.** **v.7 "Siloam (which means Sent)"** — a decoder aside that keys the whole sending-Christology; the pool's name is *apestalmenos*, the participle used of Jesus throughout `[T]`. **v.22 "for the Jews had already agreed that if anyone should confess him to be Christ, he was to be put out of the synagogue"** — the presenting-situation verse, and one of only three *aposynagōgos* notices (9:22; 12:42; 16:2, verified).
- **Repetition.** πόθεν / "we do not know where he is from" — the where-from question fires five times in this chapter (9:12, 21, 25, 29, 30, verified), and v.30 turns it into the man's own argument: "why, this is an amazing thing! You do not know where he comes from, and yet he opened my eyes." γεννάω 4× (vv.2, 19, 20, 34, verified).
- **Vocabulary.** ἐκβάλλω (v.34, "they cast him out") — **verified: 5 occurrences in John (2:15; 6:37; 9:34, 35; 10:4)**, and 6:37 is the promise "whoever comes to me I will never cast out". The men cast him out; Jesus finds him. **This is a verified verbal echo and it is the pericope's theological centre.**
- **Copycat.** The blind man **is** a normative pattern — his growing confession (a man → a prophet → from God → Lord, I believe) is narrated approvingly and contrasts pointedly with the Bethesda man of ch. 5. His courage under interrogation is the model John holds up for a community facing expulsion `[I]`.
- **Tone.** Comic and then devastating. The man's dry insolence (v.27, "do you also want to become his disciples?") is genuinely funny and should be preached as such.

**Tool 11.**
- **Isaiah 35:5 / 42:7 (eyes of the blind opened) → 9:1–7** *(moderate–high, conceptual)*. *Move 1:* opening blind eyes is a marker of the age to come and, in Isa 42, a task of the Servant. *Book usage:* Isaiah is live. *What it adds:* v.32's "never since the world began has it been heard that anyone opened the eyes of a man born blind" is an implicit claim that the age has arrived.
- **Genesis 2:7 (forming from the ground) → 9:6** *(moderate)*. Mud and spittle to make eyes where there were none is closer to *creation* than to healing `[I]`; and John will use the Gen 2:7 verb at 20:22. *Move 1 only.*
- **`Internal:`** "born blind" (9:2) ↔ **"you were born in utter sin" (9:34) ↔ "born from above" (3:3–7)** — γεννάω is the verbal carrier and it is verified across all three; the men who tell him he was born in sin are the ones who have not been born again. *Answers §earlier*, **high**, and this is the sweep's best single internal find. "I will never cast out" (6:37) → "they cast him out" (9:34–35), **high**. "Sin no more" (5:14) → "neither this man nor his parents sinned" (9:3), **moderate–high** — the two Sabbath healings answer the sin-suffering question differently and the tension is real.
- **Pattern-break absence — `moderate`.** **πατήρ occurs 136× across John and is absent from exactly two chapters: 9 and 19** (verified). Both are formal interrogations ending in expulsion or execution. The established pattern (a mean of ~7 per chapter, peaking at 23) makes the absence noticeable rather than guessed, which is what the admissibility gate requires. What it may mean is properly open — flag, do not preach hard.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **John 9 is the presenting situation dramatised.** A man confesses, is expelled, and is found by Jesus — which is the exact experience 9:22 says the readers face. Surfaced by: Narrator's Comment, Positional Necessity, Original Audience.
2. **"Born blind" / "born in sin" / "born from above" is one verbal thread.** Verified; it makes ch. 9 the narrative answer to ch. 3.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.**

## Pericope 20 — John 10:1–21 — The good shepherd

**Gist.** The sheepfold figure, the door, and the good shepherd who lays down his life; hired hands flee, other sheep will be brought, one flock and one shepherd; the hearers divide again.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* the leaders have just cast a healed man out of the fold (9:34). *Necessity:* the shepherd discourse follows an excommunication, and it is a judgement on the men who performed it — there is no break at 9:41/10:1, and 10:21's back-reference to "the eyes of the blind" proves the join `[T]`. *Implication:* preaching John 10 as a pastoral idyll without John 9 is the commonest error available; the discourse is a prophetic indictment of shepherds who scatter.

**Live tools.**
- **Vocabulary.** παροιμία (v.6) — **verified: John's word for figurative speech, 3× (10:6; 16:25, 29); παραβολή occurs 0 times in John.** τίθημι τὴν ψυχήν "lay down his life" (vv.11, 15, 17, 18) — **verified: the ψυχή-laying formula occurs at 10:11, 15, 17; 13:37, 38; 15:13.** ἐκβάλλω (v.4, the shepherd "puts out" his own sheep) — the same verb as 9:34, now redeemed `[T]`.
- **Repetition.** πρόβατον 15× in ch. 10 (of 20 in the book, verified) · ἐγώ εἰμι predicated twice (door, vv.7, 9; shepherd, vv.11, 14).
- **Structure.** Figure (1–6) → first exposition, the door (7–10) → second exposition, the shepherd (11–18) → division (19–21).

**Tool 11.**
- **Ezekiel 34 → 10:1–18** *(high confidence)*. *Source context:* an oracle against the shepherds of Israel who feed themselves, do not strengthen the weak, do not seek the lost, and scatter the flock; YHWH declares "I myself will search for my sheep… I will rescue them", then promises "my servant David" as one shepherd over them. *Book usage:* first and principal use of Ezekiel; Ezek 37 and 47 may stand behind 11:43 and 7:38 at lower confidence. *OT-to-OT:* Ezek 34 is in explicit conversation with Jer 23:1–6 (the same indictment and the same Davidic remedy) and with Ps 23; and Ezek 34:23's "one shepherd" is picked up in Ezek 37:24 alongside "they shall all have one shepherd" — which is verbatim what 10:16 promises. *What it adds:* the discourse is not a metaphor Jesus chose but an oracle he is enacting, and the men listening are its defendants. The "other sheep" of 10:16 is Ezekiel's reunification promise extended past Israel.
- **`Internal:`** "one flock, one shepherd" (10:16) → 11:52 ("gather into one the children of God who are scattered"); 17:20–23 ("that they may all be one") — *planted*, **high**; the συνάγω/εἰς ἕν vocabulary is verified at 11:52 and 17:21–23. "Lay down my life" (10:11) → 13:37–38 (Peter volunteers the very formula and fails); 15:13 — *planted*, **high**. τίθημι (10:11) → 13:4 ("he laid aside his garments") — **moderate–high**; the footwashing uses the shepherd's verb, which makes it an enacted laying-down.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **Peter borrows the shepherd's own words at 13:37 and cannot keep them, and is restored to shepherding at 21:15–17.** A three-point verified arc: 10:11 → 13:37 → 21:16. Cross-passage; invisible from any one pericope.
2. **τίθημι links the shepherd's death, the footwashing, and the burial.** 10:11/15/17/18 → 13:4 → 19:42; 20:2, 13, 15 (verified).

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.**

## Pericope 21 — John 10:22–42 — At the Feast of Dedication

**Gist.** At Hanukkah in winter, "how long will you keep us in suspense?"; Jesus points to his works, promises no one will snatch his sheep, says "I and the Father are one", faces a stoning, and argues from Psalm 82 before withdrawing beyond the Jordan.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* the shepherd discourse and a division. *Necessity:* the feast-marker at v.22 ("it was the Feast of Dedication… it was winter") is the panel-break — Booths is over, and this short scene closes the public Jerusalem ministry before the Lazarus sign. Dedication commemorates the *rededication of a desecrated temple*, which sits pointedly under a claim to be the one "whom the Father consecrated (ἡγίασεν) and sent into the world" (v.36) `[I]`, *moderate–high*. *Implication:* Jesus is the rededicated temple, at the feast of rededication.

**Live tools.**
- **Narrator's comment.** v.22b "it was winter" — a two-word aside that does calendrical and atmospheric work at once.
- **Vocabulary.** ἁγιάζω (v.36) — consecrate/set apart, the temple-dedication verb; it returns at 17:17, 19 (verified). ἁρπάζω "snatch" (vv.28, 29) — the wolf's verb from v.12, now denied.
- **Structure — the closing bracket, and it is forensic, not merely geographic.** "He went away again across the Jordan to the place where John had been baptising at first… and many came to him. And they said, '**John did no sign** (σημεῖον ἐποίησεν οὐδέν), **but everything John said about this man was true** (ἀληθῆ ἦν).' And many believed in him there" (vv.40–42). The return to 1:28 is real `[T]`, but the substance is that **the crowd delivers a verdict on the first witness, in the book's own forensic vocabulary** — σημεῖον and ἀληθής, the two words the whole movement has been contesting. The first witness called at 1:19 is vindicated at 10:41, and the note is that his testimony required no sign to be true. `[T]`, **high**. **Correction to the sweep's first pass, which recorded this as a geographic inclusio only; the sharper reading was prompted by `[S: conv]` and then verified from the text.**

**Tool 11.**
- **Psalm 82:6 → 10:34** *(high — explicit, "is it not written in your Law")*. *Source context:* Psalm 82 is a courtroom scene in which God stands in the divine council and indicts the "gods" — corrupt judges or heavenly powers — for unjust judgement of the weak, and sentences them: "you shall die like men". The "you are gods" of v.6 is spoken *immediately before* the death-sentence. *Book usage:* fourth Psalter engagement (2:17 Ps 69; 6:31 Ps 78; 10:34 Ps 82; then 12:13 Ps 118; 13:18 Ps 41; 15:25 Ps 69/35; 19:24 Ps 22; 19:28 Ps 69). *OT-to-OT:* Ps 82's council scene is in conversation with Deut 32:8–9 and with Ps 58. *What it adds:* the argument is *a fortiori* and legal, and — rarely noticed — **the psalm Jesus cites is about judges who will die for judging unjustly, quoted to men who are about to judge him unjustly**. The source context is a threat, not merely a premise. `[I]` on the second point, *moderate–high*.
- **`Internal:`** "no one will snatch them out of my hand" (10:28) → 17:12; 18:9 ("of those you gave me I lost not one"), **high**. The return to 1:28 (10:40) → the Baptist's opening testimony, **high** — a verified geographic inclusio around the public ministry.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **1:19 ↔ 10:40–42 is the book's first great bracket, and it closes with a verdict.** Not merely a geographic inclusio but a forensic one: the first witness is examined at 1:19 and vindicated at 10:41 in the book's own vocabulary (σημεῖον, ἀληθής). **13 of the Gospel's 17 σημεῖον references fall inside this bracket** (verified), and the last of them is the note that John himself worked none.
2. **Psalm 82's own context is a sentence of death on unjust judges.** Move 1 changes the temperature of 10:34 considerably.

**Solo dig: Tier 2** *(raised from Standard in v1.1 — this pericope closes the book's first great bracket, and the 10:41 verdict on the first witness was under-read on the first pass).*

## Pericope 22 — John 11:1–44 — I am the resurrection and the life

**Gist.** Lazarus falls ill; Jesus deliberately delays two days; Martha and Mary each meet him with the same reproach; Jesus weeps, is deeply moved, and calls Lazarus out of the tomb.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* a withdrawal beyond the Jordan after a second stoning attempt. *Necessity:* the last and greatest sign must be a *life-giving* one, and it must be the thing that triggers the death-decree (11:53) — so the sign that gives life causes the death that gives life `[T]`. The disciples' "the Jews were just now seeking to stone you, and are you going there again?" (11:8) explicitly links this pericope to 10:31. *Implication:* John has arranged the plot so that resurrection-power is the immediate cause of the crucifixion.

**Live tools.**
- **Linking words.** **v.5–6: "Now Jesus loved Martha and her sister and Lazarus. **So** (οὖν) when he heard that he was ill, he stayed two days longer."** The causal connector is the exegetical crux of the pericope, and the KJV/ESV "therefore" against the NIV's "yet" is the classic case (see Tool 8 in the toolkit's own materials). *High confidence* that the causal reading is right; the paradox is the point `[T]`.
- **Vocabulary.** ἐμβριμάομαι (vv.33, 38) — "deeply moved", but the verb carries anger or indignation, not merely sorrow; ἐτάραξεν ἑαυτόν "he troubled himself". ἐδάκρυσεν (v.35). *Moderate–high confidence* that indignation is in the range; flag for lexicon.
- **Repetition.** δόξα/δοξάζω 5× in ch. 11 (verified), framing the sign as glory-work (vv.4, 40) · λίθος 3× (vv.38, 39, 41).
- **Tone.** The most emotionally exposed passage in the Gospel. A sermon that resolves the weeping too quickly into the miracle has mishandled it.

**Tool 11.**
- **Ezekiel 37:1–14 (the valley of dry bones) → 11:38–44** *(moderate)*. *Source context:* a vision of national resurrection — "I will open your graves and raise you from your graves, O my people" — with the Spirit as the agent. *Book usage:* Ezek 34 is live from ch. 10, so a second Ezekiel engagement has an elevated prior `[I]`. *OT-to-OT:* Ezek 37 uses the same Gen 2:7 breath-vocabulary that John will use at 20:22. *What it adds:* if live, the raising of one man is a token of the promised raising of the people, which is exactly how Caiaphas's unwitting prophecy (11:50–52) then reads. *Moderate — the verbal overlap is thin; the conceptual fit is strong.*
- **`Internal:`** "I am the resurrection and the life" (11:25) *answers* 5:25–29 and 6:39–54 ("raise him up on the last day") by pulling the last day into the present — **high**, and Martha's "I know he will rise again in the resurrection on the last day" is John making the connection explicitly for the reader. "Did I not tell you that if you believed you would see the glory of God?" (11:40) → 1:14; 2:11, **high**. Lazarus bound in cloths and needing to be unbound (11:44) → 20:6–7 (the cloths left behind, the face-cloth folded separately) — *planted*, **moderate–high**; the contrast between the man who needs unbinding and the man who leaves the grave-clothes behind is unmarked and is one of the sweep's better finds.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **11:44 and 20:6–7 are a deliberate contrast.** Lazarus is resuscitated and still bound; Jesus is raised and the cloths stay in the tomb. Verified at the level of the detail; the inference is `[I]`, *moderate–high*.
2. **The οὖν of 11:6 is load-bearing and translation-sensitive.** Any English text that renders it "yet" has decided the exegesis for the preacher.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.**

## Pericope 23 — John 11:45–57 — One man should die for the people

**Gist.** Many believe; others report to the Pharisees; the council convenes, Caiaphas says it is expedient that one man die for the people, and the narrator explains he prophesied unwittingly; from that day they plot to kill him.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* a public raising of the dead. *Necessity:* the decree must follow the sign immediately, because John's argument is that the life-giving sign is what makes the death inevitable (v.53). *Implication:* the council's stated motive is political preservation — "the Romans will come and take away both our place and our nation" (v.48) — and the narrator lets the irony stand: they kill him to save the place, and the place is destroyed anyway.

**Live tools.**
- **Narrator's comment.** **vv.51–52** is the most theologically explicit aside in the Gospel: "he did not say this of his own accord, but being high priest that year he prophesied that Jesus would die for the nation, and not for the nation only, but also to gather into one the children of God who are scattered abroad." The narrator converts a cynical calculation into a substitutionary-atonement statement `[T]`.
- **Vocabulary.** συναγάγῃ εἰς ἕν (v.52) — **verified: συνάγω 4× in John (6:12, 13; 11:47, 52)**, and εἰς ἕν recurs at 17:21–23. The council "gathers" (συνήγαγον, v.47) in order to destroy; God gathers into one through the destruction. The same verb, four verses apart, doing opposite work `[T]`. **This is a sweep-only find.**
- **Repetition.** Ἰουδαῖοι 9× in ch. 11 (verified).

**Tool 11.**
- **Conceptual: the scattered-and-gathered flock (Ezek 34:12–13; Isa 11:12; Deut 30:3–4)** *(moderate–high)*. *Move 1:* the gathering of the scattered is a standard restoration promise. *Book usage:* Ezek 34 is live from ch. 10, and 10:16's "other sheep… one flock" is the same promise in shepherd idiom four chapters earlier. *What it adds:* Caiaphas's prophecy is Ezekiel 34's promise placed in the mouth of one of Ezekiel 34's defendants.
- **`Internal:`** "gather into one" (11:52) → 10:16; 17:21–23, **high**. "It is better that one man should die for the people" (11:50) → 18:14 (the narrator repeats it verbatim at the arrest) — *planted*, **high**, and the repetition is the narrator making sure the reader has it in hand for the trial.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **συνάγω at 11:47 and 11:52 is a deliberate collision.** The council gathers to kill; the death gathers the scattered.
2. **The narrator quotes Caiaphas twice (11:50; 18:14).** Verified. He is the only character in John whose words are repeated by the narrator.

**Solo dig: Standard.**

## Pericope 24 — John 12:1–11 — The house filled with fragrance

**Gist.** Six days before Passover, at Bethany, Mary anoints Jesus's feet with costly nard and wipes them with her hair; Judas objects; Jesus defends her, referring to the day of his burial; crowds come to see Lazarus, whom the chief priests also plot to kill.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* the death-decree, and a note that Jesus withdrew (11:54). *Necessity:* the passion sequence needs a beginning, and John gives it a date-stamp ("six days before the Passover", v.1) that starts the final week. *Implication:* the anointing is placed *before* the entry in John (after it in Mark), so the entry is greeted by a man already anointed for burial.

**Live tools.**
- **Vocabulary.** ἐπλήσθη… ἐκ τῆς ὀσμῆς (v.3) "the house was filled with the fragrance" — **verified: πληρόω at 12:3 is the same verb as the fulfilment-formula at 12:38; 15:25; 17:12; 18:9, 32; 19:24, 36 (10 occurrences total)**; a domestic detail carrying the book's fulfilment verb.
- **Narrator's comment.** v.6 "he said this not because he cared about the poor, but because he was a thief" — one of the harshest asides in Scripture, and a deliberate inside-information move.
- **`Internal:`** Mary wipes his feet with her hair (12:3) → Jesus washes the disciples' feet and wipes them with a towel (13:5). **ἐκμάσσω occurs exactly 3× in John — 11:2, 12:3, 13:5 — and nowhere else; every use is of feet being wiped (verified).** A devotee's act is answered one chapter later by the Lord's `[T]`, **high**. Mary's ointment for burial (12:7) → 19:39–40 (Nicodemus's seventy-five pounds of spices), **high**.

**Tool 11.**
- **Deuteronomy 15:11 → 12:8** *(high — the saying quotes it)*. *Source context:* "there will never cease to be poor in the land" is the *ground of a command to open your hand wide*, not a resignation. *What it adds:* the verse is routinely quoted to excuse inaction, which inverts its own source. Worth naming from the pulpit.
- **`Internal:`** as above.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **ἐκμάσσω links Mary's hair and Jesus's towel.** Verified; 12:3 → 13:5, and no English translation obscures it.
2. **Deut 15:11's context is a command to generosity.** The most common misuse of 12:8 is corrected by Move 1 alone.

**Solo dig: Standard.**

## Pericope 25 — John 12:12–19 — The King on a donkey

**Gist.** The festival crowd meets Jesus with palm branches and "Hosanna! Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord, even the King of Israel"; he finds a young donkey; the narrator notes the disciples understood only after he was glorified; the Pharisees despair.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* a burial-anointing. *Necessity:* the acclamation follows the anointing in John's order, so the King is hailed by a crowd while already prepared for burial — the sequence is John's editorial argument `[I]`. And the crowd's motive is given: they came because of Lazarus (v.18). *Implication:* the entry is not a triumph misread but a truth misread — they acclaim him correctly and expect the wrong thing.

**Live tools.**
- **Narrator's comment.** **v.16 "His disciples did not understand these things at first, but when Jesus was glorified, then they remembered that these things had been written about him and had been done to him."** The book's clearest statement of retrospective comprehension, and the fourth of four such notices (2:22; 12:16; 13:7; 20:9 — verified).
- **Vocabulary.** κράζω (v.13) — the crowd's first cry, and its later cries are "crucify" (19:6, 15). Verified.
- **Vocabulary — v.19, and it is a verified fourth station of the ἴδε thread.** The Pharisees say to one another: "You see that you are gaining nothing. **Ἴδε** ὁ κόσμος ὀπίσω αὐτοῦ ἀπῆλθεν" — "**Behold**, the world has gone after him." **The interjection is ἴδε, the same word as Ἴδε ὁ ἀμνὸς τοῦ θεοῦ (1:29, 36) and Ἴδε ὁ βασιλεὺς ὑμῶν (19:14)** — verified. `[T]` **High.** Three of the Gospel's most freighted "Behold" statements are spoken by the Baptist, by the Pharisees in despair, and by Pilate in mockery; only the first speaker knows what he is saying. The Pharisees' sarcasm announces what Zech 9:10 promised the Messiah's reign would be — and Zech 9:9 is quoted four verses earlier. **The sweep's first pass missed this entirely; it was prompted by `[S: conv]` and then verified.**
- **Repetition.** βασιλεύς enters the vocabulary here and dominates 18:33–19:22.

**Tool 11.**
- **Psalm 118:25–26 → 12:13** *(high — explicit)*. *Source context:* the last of the Hallel psalms, sung at the pilgrim feasts; "save now" (*hôšîʿâ nāʾ*) is a petition, and the psalm moves through the rejected stone becoming the cornerstone to a festal procession with branches to the altar. *Book usage:* fifth Psalter engagement. *OT-to-OT:* Ps 118's rejected-stone verse is already the NT's favourite christological stone-text; John does not quote it, but the crowd's psalm contains it. *What it adds:* the crowd sings a salvation-petition and adds "the King of Israel" on their own initiative — the addition is theirs, not the psalm's, and it is the acclamation Jesus refused at 6:15.
- **Zechariah 9:9 → 12:15** *(high — explicit)*. *Source context:* the oracle of the humble king who comes riding a donkey, who will "cut off the chariot from Ephraim and the war horse from Jerusalem" and "speak peace to the nations" — an explicitly *demilitarising* king. Immediately after (9:11): "because of the blood of my covenant with you, I will set your prisoners free". *Book usage:* second of three Zechariah engagements (2:16 Zech 14:21 *moderate*; 12:15 Zech 9:9; 19:37 Zech 12:10) — **Zechariah is a live source, and John's three uses come from chs. 14, 9, and 12 in that order, so he is not following the book's sequence** `[T]`. *OT-to-OT:* Zech 9–14 is itself a dense reworking of earlier shepherd and king material, including Ezek 34 — which John has used at ch. 10. *What it adds:* the donkey is the king's *policy statement*. John abbreviates the citation and adds "Fear not, daughter of Zion" (from Zeph 3:16 or Isa 40:9), which is a small but real editorial move `[I]`.
- **`Internal:`** the refused kingship of 6:15 → the acclaimed kingship of 12:13 → the redefined kingship of 18:36 → "we have no king but Caesar" (19:15) — *the arc completes*, **high**.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **The kingship thread has four verified stations and ends in the priests' apostasy.** 6:15 → 12:13 → 18:36–37 → 19:15. Cross-passage; the last station is the darkest sentence in the Gospel.
2. **John's three Zechariah citations do not follow Zechariah's order.** Unlike Paul's Deuteronomy 32 arc in Romans, John is not tracing a source's internal movement here — a negative Move 2 finding, and worth recording as such.

**Solo dig: Standard.**

## Pericope 26 — John 12:20–36 — The hour has come

**Gist.** Greeks ask to see Jesus; he answers that the hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified, speaks of the grain of wheat, is troubled, hears a voice from heaven, and declares that being lifted up he will draw all people — the crowd asks who this Son of Man is.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* the Pharisees' despairing "look, the world has gone after him" (12:19). *Necessity:* the very next verse produces Greeks — the "world" arriving in person — and *that* is what triggers "the hour has come" `[T]`. The trigger is not the entry; it is the Gentiles. *Implication:* the hour is set running by the arrival of the nations, which makes 12:32's "I will draw all people" the answer to the Greeks' request. Whether the Greeks ever meet Jesus is left unanswered — a deliberate narrative silence.

**Live tools.**
- **Vocabulary.** ὑψόω (vv.32, 34) with the narrator's gloss at v.33 — **verified: 5 occurrences in 4 verses (3:14; 8:28; 12:32, 34×2)**, and the third use is where John explains the double meaning. ἑλκύω "draw" (v.32) — **verified: 3 occurrences (6:44; 12:32; 21:6)**. τεταρακται (v.27) "now is my soul troubled" — **verified as part of a 5-fold ταράσσω thread (5:7; 12:27; 13:21; 14:1; 14:27)**.
- **Narrator's comment.** v.33 "he said this to show by what kind of death he was going to die" — the decoder that fixes ὑψόω.
- **Tone.** Shifts sharply at v.27 from public declaration to something close to Gethsemane. John has no garden agony; this is where it goes, and preachers looking for it should look here.

**Tool 11.**
- **`Internal:`** "the hour has come" (12:23) *answers* 2:4; 7:30; 8:20 — **high**, and it is the hinge of the clock-thread. "Unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies" (12:24) → 15:1–8 ("bear much fruit", same idiom) and 19:41 (a garden) — *planted*, **moderate–high**. **"Now is my soul troubled" (12:27) and "he was troubled in spirit" (13:21) → "let not your hearts be troubled" (14:1, 14:27) — the same verb, verified, four times; Jesus is troubled twice, then twice forbids it to his disciples.** *Planted*, **high**. **This is the sweep's best pastoral find and it is not in the overview.** "Whoever loves his life loses it" (12:25) → 13:37–38; 15:13 (laying down ψυχή), **moderate–high**.
- **The voice from heaven (v.28)** — John's substitute for the Synoptic transfiguration and baptismal voice; the crowd hears thunder. *Moderate confidence* on the deliberate substitution.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **The ταράσσω chain (12:27; 13:21 → 14:1, 27) means the "troubled heart" Jesus forbids is one he has himself just had.** Verified four-point verbal thread; it changes the pastoral register of 14:1 completely.
2. **The Greeks trigger the hour.** Positional, and it makes 12:32 the answer to a request John never records being granted.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.**

## Pericope 27 — John 12:37–50 — Why they could not believe

**Gist.** The narrator's verdict on the public ministry: though he had done so many signs before them, they did not believe — fulfilling Isaiah; yet many authorities believed but would not confess for fear of the Pharisees; Jesus cries out a final summary.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* the hour has come and Jesus has hidden himself (12:36b). *Necessity:* the public ministry is over and requires a verdict, and John gives it in his own voice before the private ministry begins at 13:1. **This is the seam, and it is countable: σημεῖον occurs 16 times before 12:37 and once after (20:30)** `[T]`. *Implication:* everything from 13:1 is addressed to "his own"; the world has been given its answer.

**Live tools.**
- **Narrator's comment.** vv.37–43 is the longest sustained narratorial intrusion in the Gospel — a verdict, two Isaiah citations, an interpretation of Isaiah's vision, and a sociological note about closet believers. Interpretive gold, and it is where the reader is told how to understand the preceding eleven chapters.
- **Repetition.** δόξα/δοξάζω 8× in ch. 12 (verified) — the highest of any chapter, clustering around the hour.
- **Structure.** vv.44–50 has no narrative setting at all — "Jesus cried out and said" with no audience specified. It reads as a summary appendix to the public ministry, placed after the verdict `[I]`.

**Tool 11.**
- **Isaiah 53:1 → 12:38** *(high — explicit)*. *Source context:* the opening line of the fourth Servant Song — "who has believed what he has heard from us, and to whom has the arm of the LORD been revealed?" The question introduces the passage about the Servant who is despised, rejected, and bears our griefs. It is a lament that the message has not been received. *Book usage:* third Isaiah engagement (1:23; 6:45; 12:38, 40). *OT-to-OT:* Isa 53:1 and Isa 6:10 (cited next) are both about a message not received — and Isa 6 is the commissioning that *predicts* the deafness Isa 53 laments. **John cites them in the wrong chronological order and the right logical one.** `[I]`
- **Isaiah 6:10 → 12:40** *(high — explicit)*. *Source context:* the temple vision — Isaiah sees the Lord high and lifted up (ὑψηλοῦ in LXX, the same root as John's ὑψόω), the seraphim, the coal, and the commission to preach to a people who will not hear. *Book usage:* fourth Isaiah engagement, closing the frame opened at 1:23. *OT-to-OT:* Isa 6 is the source-text for the hardening motif across the NT (Mark 4; Acts 28; Rom 11). *What it adds:* **v.41 is the interpretive key — "Isaiah said these things because he saw his glory and spoke of him."** John claims that Isaiah's temple vision was a vision of Christ. And the LXX's "high and lifted up" gives John his ὑψόω. *The ὑψόω/ὑψηλός link is moderate — worth a Logos check.*
- **`Internal:`** "so many signs… they did not believe" (12:37) *answers* Ps 78's own thesis, cited at 6:31 — the psalm's logic recurs as a Johannine structural verdict, **moderate–high**. "For fear of the Pharisees they did not confess him, lest they be put out of the synagogue" (12:42) → 9:22; 16:2 — the third of the three *aposynagōgos* notices, **high**. "They loved the glory that comes from man more than the glory that comes from God" (12:43) → 5:44 — *answers §earlier*, **high**, a near-verbatim repetition seven chapters apart.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **12:41 is the strongest hermeneutical claim in the Gospel** — Isaiah's vision was of Christ. It licenses every OT reading in the book and is much less preached than 5:39.
2. **5:44 and 12:43 are near-identical.** "How can you believe, when you receive glory from one another?" (5:44) and "they loved the glory that comes from man" (12:43) bracket the public ministry with the same diagnosis. Verified; a clean inclusio around chs. 5–12.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.**

## Pericope 28 — John 13:1–20 — He loved them to the end

**Gist.** Before the Passover, knowing his hour had come and having loved his own, Jesus rises from supper, lays aside his garments, washes the disciples' feet, resists and then instructs Peter, and gives the act as an example.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* the public ministry has been given its verdict (12:37–43). *Necessity:* 13:1 opens the private ministry with a temporal marker ("before the Feast of the Passover"), a knowledge marker ("knowing that his hour had come"), and a love marker ("having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end") — three of the book's threads tied off in one sentence. **This is the great seam** `[T]`. *Implication:* everything that follows is addressed to a closed room, which is the corrective to the commonest Farewell-Discourse misuse.

**Live tools.**
- **Vocabulary.** **τίθησιν τὰ ἱμάτια (13:4) — "he lays aside his garments". Verified: the present τίθησιν occurs 3× in John — 2:10 (setting out the good wine), 10:11 ("the good shepherd lays down his life"), 13:4.** The footwashing uses the shepherd's own verb, in the same form. `[T]`, *high confidence*. λέντιον (vv.4, 5) — a Latin loanword (*linteum*), used twice here and nowhere else in the NT. νίπτω 5× in this pericope (verified) against its only other Johannine use at 9:7, 15 — the pool of Siloam. καθαρός (vv.10, 11) → 15:3.
- **Narrator's comment.** v.1 (his hour, his love), v.3 (knowing the Father had given all things into his hands), v.11 (he knew who was to betray him) — three asides in eleven verses, all establishing that the footwashing is done in full knowledge, not naivety.
- **Copycat.** vv.14–15 make this explicitly prescriptive — "I have given you an example, that you also should do just as I have done to you". One of the few places in John where the text itself answers the Copycat question `[T]`.
- **Difficult verses.** v.10 ("the one who has bathed does not need to wash, except for his feet") — the shorter reading omitting "except for his feet" has real manuscript support and changes the sense. Flag; *moderate* confidence in the longer reading.

**Tool 11.**
- **`Internal:`** "his own" (τοὺς ἰδίους, 13:1) *answers* 1:11 ("he came to his own [τὰ ἴδια] and his own [οἱ ἴδιοι] did not receive him") — **the same word-group, verified**; the phrase is redefined from those who rejected him to those he loves to the end. *Answers §earlier*, **moderate–high**. This is a Prologue plant cashed at the book's hinge, and it is easy to miss in English. τίθημι (13:4) → 10:11, **high**. "You are clean" (13:10) → 15:3, **high**. "He was troubled in spirit" (13:21) → 14:1, 27 — see Pericope 26.
- **Conceptual: the slave's task.** Footwashing was the work of a non-Jewish slave; a Jewish slave could not be compelled to it `[S]` — background, flagged as such, and not load-bearing for the exegesis, which stands on the τίθημι verb alone.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **The footwashing is an enacted laying-down-of-life.** τίθησιν at 13:4 is the shepherd's verb from 10:11 in the same form. Verified; and it means the footwashing is not merely a humility lesson but a passion-parable.
2. **"His own" is redefined between 1:11 and 13:1.** Verified word-group; a Prologue plant.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.**

## Pericope 29 — John 13:21–38 — Betrayal, glory, and the new commandment

**Gist.** Jesus is troubled in spirit, names his betrayer by the morsel, Judas goes out — "and it was night"; then "now is the Son of Man glorified", the new commandment to love one another, and Peter's boast and predicted denial.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* the footwashing, with Judas's feet among those washed. *Necessity:* the betrayal must be named *after* the washing, so that the reader sees the betrayer's feet washed first; and the glory-saying must come *after* Judas leaves, because the departure sets the passion in motion. *Implication:* "now is the Son of Man glorified" (13:31) is triggered by the betrayal, which is the clearest possible statement that glory in John means the cross.

**Live tools.**
- **Narrator's comment.** **v.30 "So, after receiving the morsel of bread, he immediately went out. And it was night."** Three words of scene-setting doing the work of a paragraph of theology — the light-and-darkness thread of 1:5; 3:19; 8:12; 12:35 lands here `[T]`.
- **Vocabulary.** δοξάζω 5× in vv.31–32 alone — the densest cluster in the book. ἐντολὴ καινή (v.34) — **verified: ἐντολή occurs 10× in John, first at 10:18 and then concentrated in 13–15**. τὴν ψυχήν σου θήσεις (v.37) — Peter using the shepherd's formula from 10:11 verbatim.
- **Tone.** Night. Betrayal. A boast. The chapter ends on a cockcrow prediction and the reader waits eighteen chapters' worth of narrative for its resolution.

**Tool 11.**
- **Psalm 41:9 → 13:18** *(high — explicit, "that the Scripture may be fulfilled")*. *Source context:* a psalm of sickness and betrayal; the psalmist is ill, his enemies whisper his death, and "even my close friend in whom I trusted, who ate my bread, has lifted his heel against me". The psalm ends in a plea to be raised up. Notably, the psalm's speaker also confesses sin (41:4) — which is why John truncates the citation, quoting only the betrayal clause `[I]`. *Book usage:* sixth Psalter engagement; the Psalms are the book's most-used source after Isaiah. *OT-to-OT:* Ps 41 closes Book I of the Psalter and its betrayal-motif is picked up in Ps 55:12–14. *What it adds:* the betrayal is scripted, which protects Jesus's sovereignty — the point John makes explicit at v.19 ("I am telling you this now, before it takes place, that when it does take place you may believe that ἐγώ εἰμι").
- **`Internal:`** ἐγώ εἰμι at 13:19 — the seventh absolute use, placed to interpret the betrayal, **high**. Peter's "I will lay down my life for you" (13:37) → 10:11; 15:13; 21:15–19 — *planted*, **high**; **Peter claims the shepherd's role, fails, and is given it.** "It was night" (13:30) → 3:2 (Nicodemus by night); 19:39 (Nicodemus in daylight), **moderate**.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **Judas's exit is what triggers "now is the Son of Man glorified".** Positional and verbal; the strongest single proof that John's δόξα is passion-shaped.
2. **Peter's boast is a quotation of the good shepherd.** 10:11 → 13:37, verified formula, and it sets up 21:15–17 as a restoration to the very office he claimed.

**Solo dig: Standard.**

## Pericope 30 — John 14:1–14 — I am the way, the truth, and the life

**Gist.** "Let not your hearts be troubled"; many rooms in the Father's house; Thomas's "we do not know the way"; "I am the way, the truth and the life"; Philip's "show us the Father"; the works and the greater works.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* a betrayal named, a departure announced ("where I am going you cannot come", 13:33), and a denial predicted. *Necessity:* three men have just been destabilised — Judas exposed, Peter humiliated, all of them told they cannot follow — and 14:1 addresses exactly that. **The comfort is answering a specific distress the previous pericope created** `[T]`. *Implication:* preaching 14:1–6 without 13:33–38 removes the wound the balm is for. This is the overview's Trap 6 and the sweep confirms it positionally.

**Live tools.**
- **Vocabulary.** μοναί (v.2) "dwelling places" — cognate with μένω, the abiding verb; **verified: μονή occurs only at 14:2 and 14:23 in the NT**, and the second use reverses the direction ("we will come to him and make our home [μονήν] with him). The Father's house comes to the disciple as much as the disciple goes to it `[T]`, *high*. This is very often missed.
- **Repetition.** πατήρ 23× in ch. 14 — **the heaviest of any chapter in the book (verified)**.
- **Linking words.** v.6's exclusive claim is grounded by v.7 ("if you had known me, you would have known my Father also") and answered by v.9 ("whoever has seen me has seen the Father") — the way-claim is a Father-claim.
- **Difficult verses.** v.12 ("greater works than these will he do") and v.13–14 ("whatever you ask in my name") are both routinely mishandled. The controls are in the text: "because I am going to the Father" (v.12) and "that the Father may be glorified in the Son" (v.13).

**Tool 11.**
- **`Internal:`** "let not your hearts be troubled" (14:1) *answers* 12:27; 13:21 (Jesus troubled) — **high**, verified verb. "I am the way… no one comes to the Father except through me" (14:6) → 10:9 ("I am the door; if anyone enters by me"), **high** — the two access-sayings. "Whoever has seen me has seen the Father" (14:9) *answers* 1:18 ("no one has ever seen God… he has made him known") — **high**, a Prologue plant resolved. μονή (14:2) → 14:23, **high**; and both → the μένω thread of ch. 15.
- *No external OT citation. Move 1–3 N/A.*

**Sweep findings.**
1. **μονή occurs twice and reverses direction.** 14:2 (we go to the Father's house) and 14:23 (the Father and Son make their home with us). A verified pair, and it converts a funeral text into an indwelling text.
2. **14:9 closes the Prologue's most famous open loop.** 1:18 → 14:9, verified thematically and lexically (ὁράω).

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.**

## Pericope 31 — John 14:15–31 — Another Helper

**Gist.** Love shown in keeping commandments; the promise of another Paraclete, the Spirit of truth, who will dwell with and in them; "I will not leave you as orphans"; peace given, not as the world gives; "Rise, let us go from here."

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* "I am going to the Father" (14:12, 28). *Necessity:* a departure creates an absence, and the Paraclete promises are the provision for it — the section exists because the previous one announced a leaving `[T]`. *Implication:* the Spirit in John is given *because* Jesus goes (cf. 16:7), which is why 7:39 could say the Spirit "had not yet been given" — the pneumatology is tied to the christological timetable.

**Live tools.**
- **Vocabulary.** παράκλητος — **verified: 4 occurrences, all in the Farewell (14:16, 26; 15:26; 16:7)**, plus "the Spirit of truth" at 14:17; 15:26; 16:13 (verified). ἄλλον παράκλητον (v.16) implies Jesus is the first. ὀρφανούς (v.18) — a family word, not a legal one. εἰρήνη (v.27) — **verified: 6 occurrences, at 14:27; 16:33; 20:19, 21, 26**, so every peace-saying is either in the Farewell or in the resurrection room. That distribution is itself a finding.
- **Structure.** v.31b ("Rise, let us go from here") followed by three more chapters of discourse is the Gospel's most-discussed structural seam. Options: a redactional layer; a deliberate delay; a movement that actually begins here and continues walking. Show the options; do not resolve. *Genuinely open.*

**Tool 11.**
- **`Internal:`** παράκλητος (14:16) → 15:26; 16:7 — *planted*, **high**. "Peace I leave with you" (14:27) → 20:19, 21, 26 ("Peace be with you", three times in the resurrection room) — *planted and resolved*, **high**; **the promise of 14:27 is kept verbatim by the risen Christ.** Verified count. "Let not your hearts be troubled" (14:27) → 12:27; 13:21, **high**. "The ruler of this world is coming" (14:30) → 12:31; 16:11, **high**.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **Every occurrence of εἰρήνη in John is either a Farewell promise or a resurrection greeting.** 14:27; 16:33 → 20:19, 21, 26. Verified; a six-point thread with a clean payoff, and it is the strongest structural argument that chs. 14–16 and ch. 20 are written as promise-and-fulfilment.
2. **14:31b is unresolved and should be flagged, not smoothed.**

**Solo dig: Standard.**

## Pericope 32 — John 15:1–17 — I am the true vine

**Gist.** "I am the true vine and my Father is the vinedresser"; branches that bear and branches that are taken away; abide in me; the commandment to love as he has loved; no longer servants but friends; chosen and appointed to bear fruit.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* the promise of the indwelling Spirit and of the Father and Son making their home with the disciple (14:23). *Necessity:* the vine gives that indwelling a picture, and it does so with the same verb — **μένω, 8× in ch. 15 of 34 in the book (verified)**, cognate with the μονή of 14:2, 23. The image is not new material; it is the previous section's noun turned into a verb `[T]`. *Implication:* abiding is not an intensification of Christian effort; it is the disciple's side of an indwelling already promised.

**Live tools.**
- **Repetition.** μένω 8× · ἀγαπ- 10× in ch. 15 — **the heaviest love-concentration in the book (verified)** · καρπός 8× · "as I have loved you" twice (13:34; 15:12).
- **Vocabulary.** ἄμπελος ἡ ἀληθινή — "the *true* vine", and ἀληθινός is John's word for the real-against-the-shadow (cf. 1:9 true light; 6:32 true bread). φίλος (v.15) — **verified: 4 occurrences in John (3:29 the bridegroom's friend; 11:11 "our friend Lazarus"; 15:15; 19:12 "Caesar's friend")**. The Gospel's only other titular "friend" is Pilate's `[T]`. αἴρει (v.2) can mean "takes away" or "lifts up" — a real lexical fork with pastoral consequences. *Moderate confidence*; flag.
- **Copycat.** vv.12–17 are direct command; the vine imagery is not a horticultural prescription.

**Tool 11.**
- **Isaiah 5:1–7 / Psalm 80:8–16 / Jeremiah 2:21 / Ezekiel 15, 17, 19 (Israel as vine) → 15:1** *(high confidence, conceptual and near-universal)*. *Source context:* every OT vine-and-vineyard text is an **indictment** — Isaiah's vineyard yields wild grapes and is torn down; Ps 80's transplanted vine is burnt and the psalm pleads for the "son of man" at God's right hand to restore it; Jeremiah's choice vine becomes degenerate; Ezekiel's vine-wood is fit only for fuel. *Book usage:* Isaiah and Ezekiel are both live sources. *OT-to-OT:* Ps 80:17's "son of man whom you have made strong for yourself" sits inside the vine-lament, which puts vine and Son of Man in prior canonical conversation — **and John has been developing a Son of Man Christology since 1:51** `[I]`, *moderate*. *What it adds:* "**true** vine" is a claim against a failed one. Where Israel's vine produced wild grapes, this vine produces; and the disciples' fruitfulness is not their achievement but their location. The word "true" carries the whole polemic.
- **`Internal:`** "you are already clean" (15:3) *answers* 13:10 — **high**, verified καθαρός. "Abide in my word" (8:31) → 15:4–10, **high**. "That your joy may be full" (15:11) → 3:29; 16:24; 17:13, **high**. "Lay down his life for his friends" (15:13) → 10:11; 13:37, **high**.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **Every OT vine text is an indictment, which is what "true" is doing in 15:1.** Move 1 across the whole family, not a single proof-text.
2. **The only two men called "friend" in John are the disciples and Pilate.** 15:15 and 19:12, verified. The juxtaposition is bleak and unpreached.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.**

## Pericope 33 — John 15:18–16:15 — The world's hatred and the Spirit's witness

**Gist.** If the world hates you, it hated me first; they will put you out of the synagogues and kill you thinking it service to God; but the Paraclete will come, convict the world, and guide into all truth.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* the vine and the love-command. *Necessity:* the same section that commands love within must account for hatred without, and the two are joined at 15:17–18 ("these things I command you, so that you will love one another. If the world hates you…"). The join is explicit and the chapter break at 16:1 cuts through the middle of the unit `[T]`. *Implication:* the love-command is a survival provision for a hated community, not a general ethic.

**Live tools.**
- **Repetition.** κόσμος 8× in ch. 15 and 4× in 16 — and **ch. 17 has 18, the heaviest in the book (verified)**. μισέω — **verified: 9 occurrences in John (3:20; 7:7; 12:25; 15:18, 19, 23, 24, 25; 17:14)**, five of them here.
- **Vocabulary.** ἀποσυνάγωγος (16:2) — the third and last of the three (9:22; 12:42; 16:2, verified), and the only one on Jesus's own lips. ἐλέγχω (16:8) "convict" — a forensic verb; the Paraclete prosecutes the world, which continues the trial motif from ch. 5.
- **Difficult verses.** 16:2 ("whoever kills you will think he is offering service to God") is a hard saying with a difficult reception history; handle with care.

**Tool 11.**
- **Psalm 69:4 (with Psalm 35:19) → 15:25** *(high — explicit, "that the word written in their Law might be fulfilled")*. *Source context:* Psalm 69 again — **the third of John's three uses (2:17; 15:25; 19:28–29, verified)**, and the phrase is "those who hate me without cause are more than the hairs of my head". *Book usage:* **this is a clean Move 2. John cites 69:9 at the temple (zeal), 69:4 in the Farewell (hatred without cause), and 69:21 at the cross (thirst) — and the psalm itself runs zeal → reproach → hatred → thirst. John is following the psalm's own sequence.** `[I]`, *moderate–high* — and this is the single most substantial Move 2 finding in the sweep. *OT-to-OT:* Ps 69 and Ps 35 share the "hate me without cause" formula and are already paired; John's "their Law" for a psalm is a broad use of *nomos*, matching 10:34. *What it adds:* the disciples' coming hatred is placed inside the same psalm as their Lord's.
- **`Internal:`** "they will put you out of the synagogues" (16:2) *answers* 9:22 and 12:42 — **high**; what the narrator reported as background is now predicted by Jesus. "I have said these things to keep you from falling away (σκανδαλισθῆτε)" (16:1) → 6:61, **moderate–high**, verified verb-pair.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **John's three Psalm 69 citations follow the psalm's own internal order.** 69:9 → 69:4 → 69:21 across 2:17, 15:25, 19:28. *(Note: 69:4 precedes 69:9 in the psalm, so the sequence is by theme — zeal, hatred, thirst — rather than strict verse order. Stated precisely: John's uses track the psalm's movement from costly zeal through causeless hatred to the vinegar, which is the psalm's narrative arc.)* **Moderate–high; worth testing in a solo dig.**
2. **The three *aposynagōgos* notices form a narrator → narrator → Jesus sequence.** 9:22, 12:42, 16:2 — background, then diagnosis, then prophecy.

**Solo dig: Standard** (but 15:25 needs the Ps 69 triad done properly).

## Pericope 34 — John 16:16–33 — Your sorrow will turn into joy

**Gist.** "A little while and you will not see me"; the disciples puzzle over it; the woman in labour whose anguish becomes joy; plain speech instead of figures; "in the world you will have tribulation, but take heart, I have overcome the world."

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* a promise of the Spirit who will guide into all truth (16:13–15). *Necessity:* the discourse must close by addressing the immediate crisis — a few hours' separation — and by giving the disciples a word to hold through it. *Implication:* 16:33 is the Farewell's last sentence before the prayer, and it is a summary: peace in him, tribulation in the world, victory already won.

**Live tools.**
- **Vocabulary.** μικρόν — **verified: 8 occurrences in John (7:33; 12:35; 13:33; 14:19; 16:16, 17, 18, 19)**, five of them clustered here, where the disciples repeat the word back three times in bewilderment. The narrative slows to let the reader feel their confusion `[T]`. παροιμία (16:25, 29) — the second and third of three (10:6, verified). νενίκηκα (16:33) — perfect tense, "I have overcome and it stands overcome", spoken before the arrest.
- **Tone.** The labour-pain image (v.21) is the Gospel's most tender, and the "her hour has come" phrase (ἡ ὥρα αὐτῆς) uses the book's clock-word for a woman in childbirth — verified as part of the 26-fold ὥρα thread `[T]`.

**Tool 11.**
- **Isaiah 26:17; 66:7–14 (Zion in labour) → 16:21** *(moderate)*. *Move 1:* Isaiah's Zion travails and brings forth; 66:14 promises "you shall see, and your heart shall rejoice". *Book usage:* Isaiah is live. *What it adds:* the birth-image is eschatological, not merely domestic — the new age is being born through the passion. *Moderate; the verbal overlap with 66:14 ("your heart shall rejoice… and no one will take your joy from you") is close enough to be worth checking properly.*
- **`Internal:`** "your joy no one will take from you" (16:22) → 3:29; 15:11; 17:13, **high**. "In that day you will ask nothing of me" (16:23) → 14:13–14, **high**. "I have overcome the world" (16:33) → 12:31 ("now will the ruler of this world be cast out"), **high**. "The hour is coming when you will be scattered (σκορπισθῆτε), each to his own" (16:32) → 10:12 (the wolf scatters); 11:52 (the scattered gathered) — **verified: σκορπ- occurs 3× (10:12; 11:52; 16:32)**; *answers and is answered*, **moderate–high**.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **σκορπίζω has three uses and they form an argument.** The hireling lets the flock be scattered (10:12); the death gathers the scattered into one (11:52); the disciples are scattered at the arrest (16:32). Verified; the shepherd is scattered *with* the flock and the gathering happens anyway.
2. **"Take heart, I have overcome" is perfect-tense and pre-crucifixion.** The grammar is the theology.

**Solo dig: Standard.**

## Pericope 35 — John 17:1–26 — That they may all be one

**Gist.** Jesus lifts his eyes and prays: for his own glorification, for the disciples he has been given and kept, and for those who will believe through their word — that they may all be one, and be with him to see his glory.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* "I have overcome the world" (16:33), spoken to men about to scatter. *Necessity:* the discourse has promised much and the disciples have understood little; the prayer is where Jesus does for them what they cannot do for themselves, and it is placed *before* the arrest so the reader hears the passion as an answered prayer `[T]`. *Implication:* ch. 17 is not an appendix to the Farewell but its enactment — everything promised in 14–16 is here asked for.

**Live tools.**
- **Repetition.** **κόσμος 18× in ch. 17 — the heaviest concentration in the book (verified)**, which is remarkable in a prayer for disciples: the world is the horizon of the prayer even when it is not its object. δίδωμι 11× in ch. 17 (verified) — the Father's giving is the prayer's grammar; the disciples are repeatedly "those you have given me". δοξάζω 7× (verified). ἕν/εἰς ἕν at vv.11, 21, 22, 23.
- **Vocabulary.** ἁγιάζω (vv.17, 19) — the consecration verb from 10:36, now applied to both Jesus and the disciples `[T]`. τετελειωμένοι εἰς ἕν (v.23) — "perfected into one", the τελ- root again (4:34; 5:36; 17:4; 19:28, 30, verified).
- **Tone.** Serene. There is no petition for rescue and no anguish; the troubling has already happened at 12:27 and 13:21.
- **Structure.** Three movements: for himself (1–5), for the disciples (6–19), for those who will believe through their word (20–26).

**Tool 11.**
- **`Internal:`** "the hour has come" (17:1) *completes* the clock-thread — 2:4; 7:30; 8:20 → 12:23; 13:1 → 17:1, **high**, and this is its last use in the sense of the appointed hour. "Not one of them has been lost except the son of destruction" (17:12) *answers* 6:39 ("that I should lose nothing"); 10:28 ("no one will snatch them"); and is *repeated* at 18:9 by the narrator — **high**, a four-point verified thread. "I have finished (τελειώσας) the work you gave me to do" (17:4) → 4:34 ("to accomplish his work"); 5:36; 19:28, 30 (τετέλεσται) — **high**; **the prayer says it is finished before the cross, and the cross says it again.** "That they may all be one" (17:21) *answers* 10:16; 11:52 — **high**. "The glory that I had with you before the world existed" (17:5) *answers* 1:1–2 — **high**, the Prologue's opening claim placed on Jesus's own lips.
- **`Internal:` for the sermon-planner** — the prayer explicitly includes the reader: "not for these only, but also for those who will believe in me through their word" (17:20). This is one of the few places where the Farewell breaks its own closed-room frame `[T]`, and it is the corrective to over-applying the "this was for the eleven" caution.
- *No external OT citation. Move 1–3 N/A; the "son of destruction" of v.12 is a Semitic idiom, not a quotation, though it echoes 2 Sam 7:10 LXX-type phrasing — uncertain, do not press.*

**Sweep findings.**
1. **17:4 and 19:30 are the same claim in the same root.** "I finished the work" before the cross; "it is finished" on it. Verified τελ- chain across 4:34 → 5:36 → 17:4 → 19:28, 30.
2. **The prayer's grammar is receiving.** δίδωμι 11× in one chapter; the disciples are a gift from Father to Son before they are anything else.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.**

## Pericope 36 — John 18:1–27 — Whom do you seek?

**Gist.** In a garden across the Kidron, Judas comes with soldiers; Jesus asks "whom do you seek?", says ἐγώ εἰμι and they fall back; Peter cuts off Malchus's ear; Jesus is taken to Annas; Peter denies him twice at a charcoal fire while Jesus is questioned.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* a prayer that none be lost (17:12). *Necessity:* the arrest immediately tests it, and the narrator says so explicitly at 18:9 — "this was to fulfil the word he had spoken: 'Of those whom you gave me I have lost not one'". **John treats Jesus's own words as Scripture to be fulfilled**, which is a striking move and is repeated at 18:32 `[T]`. *Implication:* the arrest is narrated as the answer to the prayer, not as its interruption.

**Live tools.**
- **Structure.** John interleaves Peter's denials with Jesus's interrogation (vv.15–18, 19–24, 25–27) — a sandwich in which one man confesses under no pressure and the other denies under little `[T]`.
- **Vocabulary.** κῆπος (v.1) — **verified: 4 occurrences (18:1, 26; 19:41; 20:15)**; John alone stages the arrest and the burial in gardens. **ἀνθρακιά (v.18) — verified: 2 occurrences in the whole New Testament, John 18:18 and 21:9.** ἑλκύω (v.10, Peter "drew" his sword) — **verified: 5 occurrences (6:44; 12:32; 18:10; 21:6, 11)**. ἐγώ εἰμι (vv.5, 6, 8) three times, with the arresting party falling to the ground — the eighth, ninth and tenth absolute uses.
- **Narrator's comment.** v.9 and v.32 — two fulfilment-formulas applied to Jesus's own sayings. v.14 repeats Caiaphas's "one man should die for the people" verbatim from 11:50.
- **Tone.** Utterly controlled. There is no agony, no kiss, no "let this cup pass" — Jesus goes out to meet them and asks the questions.

**Tool 11.**
- **`Internal:`** "Whom do you seek?" (18:4, 7) *answers* 1:38 ("What are you seeking?") and is *answered by* 20:15 ("Whom are you seeking?") — **high**, verified four-point frame. "Shall I not drink the cup the Father has given me?" (18:11) — the only cup-saying in John, standing where the Synoptic Gethsemane prayer would be, **moderate–high**. The charcoal fire (18:18) → 21:9 — *planted*, **high**, and unmistakable given only two NT occurrences. Peter draws (ἑλκύω) a sword (18:10) → Peter draws (εἵλκυσεν) the net ashore (21:11) — *planted*, **moderate–high**, and the same man's drawing is redeemed. The garden (18:1) → 19:41; 20:15, **high**.
- *No external OT citation in this pericope. Move 1–3 N/A.*

**Sweep findings.**
1. **ἑλκύω is a five-point thread ending in Peter's hands.** The Father draws (6:44), the lifted-up Son draws all (12:32), Peter draws a sword (18:10), the net cannot be drawn (21:6), Peter draws it ashore unbroken (21:11). Verified; a genuine sweep-only find and homiletically generative.
2. **John treats Jesus's own words as fulfilment-Scripture (18:9, 32).** Verified; a christological claim made by narrative technique rather than assertion.

**Solo dig: Standard.**

## Pericope 37 — John 18:28–19:16 — What is truth? Behold your King

**Gist.** Jesus before Pilate: the shuttling in and out of the praetorium, the kingdom "not of this world", "what is truth?", the scourging and mock-coronation, "Behold the man", "Behold your King", and "we have no king but Caesar."

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* a Jewish hearing that produced no charge John records. *Necessity:* the Roman trial exists here because John's whole book is a trial — witnesses (ch. 5), interrogations (chs. 7–9), a divided verdict (12:37–43) — and this is where the case is finally heard. **The trial-motif that began at 5:31 concludes here** `[T]`. *Implication:* the reader has been told the answer since 1:1 and watches the court get it wrong.

**Live tools.**
- **Structure.** Seven scenes alternating outside/inside the praetorium (18:28–32 out; 33–38a in; 38b–40 out; 19:1–3 in; 4–7 out; 8–11 in; 12–16 out) — a genuinely chiastic arrangement with the mock-coronation at the centre `[I]`, *moderate–high*. **If the chiasm holds, the centre is the crowning — which is John's point.**
- **Repetition.** βασιλεύς/βασιλεία 12× in this unit · Ἰουδαῖοι 13× in ch. 19, the heaviest in the book (verified) · ἀλήθεια at the hinge (18:37–38).
- **Vocabulary.** **Ἰδού ὁ ἄνθρωπος (19:5) and Ἴδε ὁ βασιλεὺς ὑμῶν (19:14) — note that the two interjections differ in the Greek (verified): 19:5 is ἰδού, 19:14 is ἴδε.** It is 19:14's ἴδε that matches the Baptist's Ἴδε ὁ ἀμνὸς τοῦ θεοῦ at 1:29, 36 — a point of precision the overview stated too loosely and which is corrected here. ἐκάθισεν (19:13) is transitive or intransitive — "sat down on the judgment seat" or "seated him on the judgment seat"; the second reading is grammatically possible and theologically loud. *Uncertain — flag; a real Logos question.*
- **Narrator's comment.** 19:14 "it was the day of Preparation of the Passover, about the sixth hour" — a time-stamp that is theological argument. Also 18:32 (fulfilling Jesus's word about his death).
- **Difficult verses.** 19:11 ("he who delivered me over to you has the greater sin") and the chronology of the Passover meal relative to the Synoptics.

**Tool 11.**
- **`Internal:`** "My kingdom is not of this world" (18:36) *answers* 6:15 (kingship refused) and 12:13 (kingship acclaimed) — **high**; and "we have no king but Caesar" (19:15) closes the thread with the chief priests renouncing the theocracy `[T]`. Ἴδε (19:14) → 1:29, 36, **high** (see the vocabulary note). "What is truth?" (18:38) *answers* the 55-fold ἀληθ- thread, and Pilate asks it of Truth — **moderate–high** on the deliberate irony.
- **Conceptual: Passover timing.** 19:14's "day of Preparation… about the sixth hour" places the sentence at the hour of the Passover lamb slaughter `[S]` for the ritual timing, `[T]` for the text's own statement. The two must be tagged apart, and the exegesis stands on the text's timestamp even if the ritual detail is set aside.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **The kingship thread is completed by an apostasy.** "We have no king but Caesar" is spoken by chief priests at Passover, and John lets it stand unremarked. Verified as the last station of a four-point thread.
2. **The Ἴδε/Ἰδού distinction matters and the overview overstated it.** Correction recorded under Book-Overview Tensions.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.**

## Pericope 38 — John 19:17–42 — It is finished

**Gist.** The crucifixion at Golgotha; the trilingual title; the seamless tunic and the soldiers' lots; the mother and the beloved disciple; "I thirst"; "It is finished"; the pierced side, blood and water; Joseph and Nicodemus bury him in a garden tomb.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* the sentence at the sixth hour. *Necessity:* John's crucifixion narrative is the *densest citation-cluster in the book* — four formula-citations in fourteen verses (19:24, 28, 36, 37) — because this is where the Scriptures are shown to have been about him all along, exactly as 5:39 and 12:41 claimed `[T]`. *Implication:* the passion is presented as an act of exegesis.

**Live tools.**
- **Vocabulary.** **τετέλεσται (19:30) — and note the same perfect form two verses earlier at 19:28 ("knowing that all was now finished"), verified.** The cry is not the first statement of completion; it is the second. ἄρραφος (v.23) "seamless" — a hapax in the NT; the tunic woven in one piece from top to bottom has attracted priestly-vestment readings `[S]`, *uncertain* — do not build on it. παρέδωκεν τὸ πνεῦμα (v.30) — "handed over the spirit", an unusual formula that may play on the Spirit-giving of 7:39 and 20:22 `[I]`, *moderate*.
- **Pattern-break absence — `moderate`.** **πατήρ is absent from ch. 19** (verified; the only other such chapter is 9). John's Jesus does not address the Father from the cross, and there is no cry of dereliction. Given a book-wide mean of ~7 per chapter and 23 in ch. 14, the silence is noticeable rather than guessed. State it; do not over-interpret it.
- **Narrator's comment.** v.35 "he who saw it has borne witness — his testimony is true, and he knows that he is telling the truth — that you also may believe" — the eyewitness oath, and it is attached to the blood and water, not to the resurrection.

**Tool 11.**
- **Psalm 22:18 → 19:24** *(high — explicit)*. *Source context:* the psalm of dereliction — "my God, my God, why have you forsaken me" — whose middle section describes pierced hands and feet, counted bones, and the division of garments, and whose final third turns to vindication and worldwide worship. *Book usage:* seventh Psalter engagement. *OT-to-OT:* Ps 22 and Ps 69 are the twin passion-psalms and John cites them four verses apart. *What it adds:* **John cites the one verse of Ps 22 with two parallel clauses and gives each clause a separate fulfilment** (garments divided; lot cast for the tunic) — a reading of Hebrew parallelism that is either naive or deliberate, and given John's sophistication elsewhere is likely deliberate `[I]`, *moderate*.
- **Psalm 69:21 → 19:28–29** *(high — explicit, "to fulfil the Scripture")*. *Source context:* "they gave me poison for food, and for my thirst they gave me sour wine to drink" — from the same psalm cited at 2:17 and 15:25. *Book usage:* **third and final use of Psalm 69, John's most-worked psalm (verified: 2:17; 15:25; 19:28–29).** *What it adds:* the "I thirst" is *stated by the narrator to be scripturally motivated* — Jesus speaks "knowing that all was now finished, to fulfil the Scripture". That is a remarkable claim: the last need expressed is expressed in order to complete a text.
- **Exodus 12:46 / Numbers 9:12 / Psalm 34:20 → 19:36** *(high that a citation is made; **the source is disputed** — flag)*. *Source context:* Exod 12:46 and Num 9:12 forbid breaking the Passover lamb's bones; Ps 34:20 promises that the LORD keeps all the righteous man's bones. *Book usage:* the Passover frame has been live since 1:29 and is structural (three Passovers). *OT-to-OT:* the two traditions — cultic lamb and protected righteous sufferer — are already convergent in Second Temple reading `[S]`. *What it adds:* if Exodus, Jesus is the Passover lamb; if Psalm 34, he is the vindicated righteous man; John's wording ("a bone of him shall not be broken") is closer to Exod 12:46 LXX. **Recommend both, weighted to Exodus.** *Moderate–high.*
- **Zechariah 12:10 → 19:37** *(high — explicit)*. *Source context:* the LORD pours out a spirit of grace and pleas for mercy on the house of David, "and they shall look on me whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn for him" — the piercing is followed by a fountain opened for sin and uncleanness (13:1). *Book usage:* third and last Zechariah engagement. *OT-to-OT:* Zech 12–14 is in conversation with Ezek 36–37 (spirit poured out, cleansing water). *What it adds:* **the fountain of Zech 13:1 immediately following the piercing is the best OT candidate for the blood and water of 19:34** `[I]`, *moderate–high* — and it also feeds back into the unidentified 7:38 citation. **This is the strongest available lead on the 7:38 crux and the sweep recommends pursuing it.**
- **`Internal:`** "Woman, behold your son" (19:26) *answers* 2:4, **high**. Ἴδε (19:14) → 1:29, 36, **high**. Blood and water (19:34) → 7:38–39, **moderate–high**. Nicodemus by night (3:2) → Nicodemus with spices (19:39), with the narrator cross-referencing, **high**. The garden (19:41) → 18:1; 20:15, **high**.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **Four formula-citations in fourteen verses make the crucifixion John's densest scriptural moment.** Verified; and three of the four are from the Psalter.
2. **Zechariah 12:10 → 13:1 (the fountain) is the best lead on both 19:34 and the unidentified 7:38.** A Move-3 finding that connects two open questions.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.**

## Pericope 39 — John 20:1–18 — Woman, why are you weeping?

**Gist.** On the first day of the week, while still dark, Mary finds the stone removed; Peter and the beloved disciple run; the grave-clothes lie undisturbed and the other disciple believes; Mary weeps, mistakes Jesus for the gardener, hears her name, and is sent.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* a burial in a garden. *Necessity:* the resurrection is narrated as a *first day* in a *garden* with a *gardener* and, two pericopes later, a *breathing* — and the cumulative weight of those four is a new-creation frame that no single one of them could carry `[I]`, *moderate–high*. *Implication:* John began "in the beginning"; he ends with a new one.

**Live tools.**
- **Vocabulary.** τῇ μιᾷ τῶν σαββάτων (v.1) "the first day of the week" — **verified: the phrase occurs only at 20:1 and 20:19 in John**, and both are resurrection-day markers. σκοτίας ἔτι οὔσης "while it was still dark" — the darkness-thread of 1:5; 8:12; 12:35 in its last appearance `[T]`. κηπουρός (v.15) — **a NT hapax, verified**; John alone has a gardener. Ῥαββουνί (v.16) — the narrator translates, as at 1:38.
- **Narrator's comment.** v.9 "for as yet they did not understand the Scripture, that he must rise from the dead" — the fourth and last retrospective-comprehension notice (2:22; 12:16; 13:7; 20:9).
- **Structure.** The grave-clothes detail (vv.6–7) is deliberately precise — the linen lying there, the face-cloth folded in a place by itself. Contrast Lazarus, who came out bound (11:44) `[I]`, *moderate–high*.

**Tool 11.**
- **Genesis 2–3 (the garden) → 20:15** *(moderate)*. *Move 1:* Eden's gardener is the man; the garden is where God walks and where the man hides. *Book usage:* the Genesis frame is live from 1:1 and the κῆπος word-group is verified 4×. *What it adds:* Mary's mistake is, on this reading, more nearly right than wrong. *Moderate — the reading is attractive and should be flagged as inference, not asserted.*
- **`Internal:`** "Whom are you seeking?" (20:15) *answers* 1:38; 18:4, 7 — **high**. "Woman" (20:13, 15) — the third and fourth vocative γύναι after 2:4; 4:21; 19:26 (verified, 5 total), **moderate**. "I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God" (20:17) → 14:2, 12, 28; and the "my God" anticipates Thomas's "my God" eleven verses later, **moderate–high**. The stone taken away (20:1) → 11:38–41; and the stones taken up to kill him (8:59; 10:31), **moderate**.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **The grave-clothes contrast with Lazarus is unmarked but exact.** 11:44 (bound, needing release) against 20:6–7 (left behind, folded). Resuscitation against resurrection.
2. **Mary is the first sent witness, and the verb is ἀγγέλλουσα (v.18) — "announcing".** In a book where testimony is the mechanism of faith (μαρτυρ- 47×), the first Easter witness is a woman whose word the disciples had earlier not believed.

**Solo dig: Standard.**

## Pericope 40 — John 20:19–31 — My Lord and my God

**Gist.** That evening Jesus stands among them, says "Peace be with you", shows his hands and side, breathes on them and gives the Spirit with authority over sins; Thomas, absent, demands evidence, and a week later confesses; the purpose statement closes the book.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* an empty tomb and one commissioned witness. *Necessity:* the book must close where it opened — with the confession the Prologue made — and it must do so through the most resistant disciple, so that the reader who has not seen is addressed directly at v.29 `[T]`. *Implication:* 20:29 is the pivot to the reader, and 20:30–31 states why the book exists. Everything before is retrospectively governed by these three verses.

**Live tools.**
- **Vocabulary.** **ἐνεφύσησεν (v.22) — verified: the only occurrence in the New Testament, and the verb of Gen 2:7 LXX ("God breathed into his nostrils the breath of life") and of Ezek 37:9 LXX.** `[T]` for the word, `[I]` for the new-creation reading, *moderate–high*. ὁ κύριός μου καὶ ὁ θεός μου (v.28) — **verified: with 1:1, the only nominative θεός predication of the Word/Jesus in the book.** εἰρήνη 3× in this pericope (vv.19, 21, 26), completing the six-fold thread from 14:27; 16:33 (verified).
- **Textual note.** v.31's πιστεύ[σ]ητε — aorist ("come to believe") or present ("go on believing"). The manuscript evidence is genuinely divided and the decision affects whether the book is framed as evangelistic or confirmatory. *Moderate uncertainty; state both from the pulpit rather than choosing silently.*
- **Structure.** Two Sundays, a week apart, with the same greeting — John dates the resurrection appearances to the day the church would later gather `[I]`, *moderate*.

**Tool 11.**
- **Genesis 2:7 LXX → 20:22** *(moderate–high)*. *Source context:* the man is formed from dust and God breathes into him and he becomes a living being. Immediately before is the garden; immediately after, the command and the naming. *Book usage:* the Genesis frame is live (1:1; 20:1; 20:15). *OT-to-OT:* Ezek 37:9 uses the same verb of the Spirit breathing on the slain — and Ezek 37 has already been a *moderate* candidate behind 11:43. **So the two candidates are already in canonical conversation, and John's single verb may be reaching both** `[I]`. *What it adds:* the giving of the Spirit is narrated as the making of a new humanity, on the first day, in the week the tomb opened.
- **`Internal:`** "My Lord and my God" (20:28) *answers* 1:1 — **high**, and the inclusio is verified lexically. "Peace be with you" (20:19, 21, 26) *answers* 14:27; 16:33 — **high**. "As the Father has sent me, even so I am sending you" (20:21) *answers* the 23-verse sending-formula thread (1:33 … 16:5) — **high**; the formula that described Jesus is now applied to the disciples. "Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed" (20:29) *answers* 4:48 ("unless you see signs and wonders") and 12:37 — **high**, and it is the verdict on the whole sign-faith question the book has been pressing since 2:23.
- **Note for Move 4 completeness.** The three explicit *aposynagōgos* notices (9:22; 12:42; 16:2) find no narrative resolution in ch. 20. The book states the cost of confession three times and never shows it paid. That is a real absence, but it is not a *pattern-break* absence in the admissible sense — the book has not established an expectation that it would be resolved. Recorded here as an observation, not a finding.

**Sweep findings.**
1. **The book's opening and closing christological claims are lexically identical.** θεός nominative at 1:1 and 20:28 and nowhere else, verified.
2. **The sending-formula is transferred at 20:21.** The most frequent Christological formula in the book (23 verses) becomes an ecclesiological one in a single sentence.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.**

## Pericope 41 — John 21:1–25 — Breakfast, and a second charcoal fire

**Gist.** Seven disciples fish fruitlessly; a stranger on the shore directs the net; 153 fish and an unbroken net; a charcoal fire with fish and bread; the threefold "do you love me" and the threefold commission; the prophecy of Peter's death; the beloved disciple's testimony authenticated.

**Positional Necessity Check.** *Preceding movement:* a purpose statement that reads like an ending (20:30–31). *Necessity:* the epilogue exists because two things are unresolved — Peter's denial, and the status of the beloved disciple's testimony — and both are personal, not doctrinal. That is why it sits *after* the purpose statement rather than inside the book's argument `[I]`. *Implication:* ch. 21 is not an appendix to the theology but the closing of two character-arcs, and it should be preached as such.

**Live tools.**
- **Vocabulary.** **ἀνθρακιά (v.9) — verified: 2 NT occurrences, 18:18 and here.** Σίμων Ἰωάννου (vv.15, 16, 17) — **verified: the full patronymic occurs only at 1:42 and 21:15–17.** ἀγαπᾷς/ἀγαπᾷς/φιλεῖς against Peter's three φιλῶ (verified) — and the objects also vary (ἀρνία / πρόβατα / πρόβατα) and the verbs of commission vary (βόσκε / ποίμαινε / βόσκε). *Whether the ἀγαπάω/φιλέω alternation is significant is genuinely contested* — John elsewhere uses the two interchangeably (cf. 3:35 with 5:20; 13:23 with 20:2). *Moderate uncertainty; the threefold structure is certainly significant, the verb-choice much less so. Do not build a sermon point on it without a lexicon check.*
- **ἑλκύω.** v.6 (they could not *draw* the net) and v.11 (Peter *drew* it ashore) — completing the five-point thread (6:44; 12:32; 18:10; 21:6, 11, verified).
- **Vocabulary — the 153.** John gives an exact count and says the net was not torn. The number has attracted every kind of allegory. *Uncertain — the sweep's recommendation is to preach the unbroken net (which the text comments on) and leave the number alone (which the text does not).*
- **Narrator's comment.** v.14 ("this was now the third time") · v.19 ("this he said to show by what kind of death he was to glorify God") — the same construction as 12:33, applied to Peter · vv.23–25, the correction of a rumour and the double colophon.

**Tool 11.**
- **`Internal:` the densest resolution-cluster in the book.** The charcoal fire (18:18 → 21:9), **high**. The threefold denial (13:38; 18:17, 25, 27) → the threefold question, **high**. "Simon son of John" (1:42) → 21:15–17, **high**, and at 1:42 the name is *given*, here it is *withheld* — Jesus does not call him Peter. "Lay down my life for you" (13:37) → "when you are old… another will carry you where you do not want to go" (21:18) with the narrator's glory-gloss, **high** — **Peter's boast is granted, decades late, as a gift rather than an achievement.** "Follow me" (21:19, 22) → 1:43 (the first call to Philip, ἀκολούθει μοι, verbatim), **high**. The shepherd's office (10:11) → "feed my sheep", **high**.
- *No external OT citation. Move 1–3 N/A.*

**Sweep findings.**
1. **The epilogue resolves five distinct threads planted in chs. 1, 10, 13 and 18.** Charcoal fire, patronymic, denial-count, laying-down-of-life, and "follow me". All verified; no other pericope in John does this much resolution work.
2. **Peter is restored to the office he claimed and forfeited.** 10:11 → 13:37 → 21:15–19. A three-station arc that only a book-wide reading makes visible.

**Solo dig: ⭐ HIGH.**

## Cross-Passage Convergent Findings

These are the findings that only become visible when the forty-one pericopes are read as a sequential whole. They are the sweep's principal yield and none of them is available from a single-pericope run. All lexical claims are machine-verified against the SBLGNT.

### A. Verbal threads running the length of the book

**1. The drawing thread — ἑλκύω, 5 occurrences.** 6:44 (the Father draws) → 12:32 (the lifted-up Son draws all) → **18:10 (Peter draws a sword)** → 21:6 (the net cannot be drawn) → 21:11 (Peter draws it ashore, unbroken). `[T]` **High.** The same verb runs from divine election through the cross to a violent hand and then to a missional one. Homiletically the richest single thread the sweep found.

**2. The troubling thread — ταράσσω, 5 occurrences.** 5:7 (the stirred water) · **12:27 ("now is my soul troubled") · 13:21 ("he was troubled in spirit") → 14:1 and 14:27 ("let not your hearts be troubled")**. `[T]` **High.** Jesus forbids his disciples an emotion he has himself just had twice. This transforms the pastoral register of John 14 and is absent from the book-overview.

**3. The crying-out thread — κράζω, 9 occurrences.** Jesus cries out an invitation (7:28, 37; 11:43; 12:44); the crowd cries out (12:13 "Hosanna", then 18:40, 19:6, 19:12, 19:15 "Crucify… we have no king but Caesar"). `[T]` **High.** The verb of the Booths invitation becomes the verb of the demand for his death.

**4. The laying-down thread — τίθημι, and specifically the present τίθησιν (3 occurrences: 2:10; 10:11; 13:4).** The good shepherd *lays down* his life (10:11); Jesus *lays aside* his garments (13:4) — same verb, same form. Then 19:42; 20:2, 13, 15 (they *laid* him). `[T]` **High.** The footwashing is an enacted passion.

**5. The wiping thread — ἐκμάσσω, 3 occurrences (11:2; 12:3; 13:5), all of feet.** Mary wipes his feet with her hair; he wipes theirs with a towel. `[T]` **High.**

**6. The gathering thread — συνάγω (4: 6:12, 13; 11:47, 52) with σκορπίζω (3: 10:12; 11:52; 16:32) and εἰς ἕν (11:52; 17:21, 23).** Fragments gathered that nothing be lost → the council gathers to kill → the death gathers the scattered into one → the disciples scattered → that they may all be one. `[T]` **High.** The hireling scatters (10:12) and the wolf's work is undone by the shepherd's death.

**7. The casting-out thread — ἐκβάλλω, 5 occurrences.** 2:15 (the traders) · 6:37 ("I will never cast out") · **9:34–35 (they cast him out; Jesus found him)** · 10:4 (the shepherd puts out his own). `[T]` **High.** 6:37's promise is dramatised in ch. 9.

**8. The birth thread — γεννάω, 12 occurrences.** 1:13 (born of God) · 3:3–8 (born from above, 5×) · **9:2, 19, 20, 34 (born blind / "you were born in utter sin")** · 16:21 (a woman in labour). `[T]` **High.** The men who tell the blind man he was born in sin are the men who have not been born from above. John 9 is the narrative answer to John 3.

**9. The peace thread — εἰρήνη, 6 occurrences, and all of them either promise or fulfilment.** 14:27 and 16:33 (promised in the Farewell) → 20:19, 21, 26 (given three times in the resurrection room). `[T]` **High.** No other use in the book.

**10. The dwelling thread — μένω (34; 8 in ch. 15) with μονή (2: 14:2, 23).** 1:38–39 ("where are you staying?" / "they stayed with him that day") → 8:31 ("if you abide in my word") → 14:2, 23 (dwelling-places, in both directions) → 15:4–10 (abide in me). `[T]` **High.** The disciples' first question in the Gospel is about where Jesus *stays*, and the answer takes fourteen chapters.

**11. The completion thread — τελ-.** 4:34 ("to accomplish his work") → 5:36 → 17:4 ("I finished the work") → 19:28 ("knowing all was now finished") → 19:30 (τετέλεσται). `[T]` **High.**

**12. The seeking thread — the τί/τίνα ζητεῖτε form at exactly four points.** 1:38 (Jesus's first words) · 18:4, 7 (at the arrest) · 20:15 (the risen Jesus's first words). `[T]` **High.**

### B. Structural findings

**13. There are two brackets, not one, and the counts distinguish them.** `[T]` **High.** *(Revised — the first pass named only the second.)*

- **Bracket A: 1:19 ↔ 10:40–42.** The Baptist is examined at 1:19 and vindicated at 10:41 ("John did no sign, but everything John said about this man was true"). **13 of the 17 σημεῖον references fall inside it.**
- **Transition: 11:1–12:50.** The pivotal exhibit (Lazarus) and the turn. Three σημεῖον references (11:47; 12:18; 12:37); the Greeks arrive; the hour comes.
- **Bracket B closes at 12:37.** The narrator's verdict on the whole public presentation; only 20:30 follows. πιστεύω is densely spread across 1–12 and then collapses (chs. 15, 18, 21 have none); ἀγαπάω and κόσμος spike after 13:1.

The two brackets do different work: **A closes a movement of testimony; B pronounces the verdict on it.** Treating 12:37 as the sole seam — as the sweep's first pass did — flattens a structure the counts actually support in two stages. `[T]` for the counts; the two-stage reading was prompted by `[S: conv]` and is confirmed by them.

**14. Three scenes of unreliable belief form a sustained argument.** 2:23–25 (many believed at the signs; he did not entrust himself to them) → 6:60–66 (many disciples turned back) → 8:31–59 (addressed to "the Jews who had believed him", ending in attempted stoning). `[T]`/`[I]` **High.** John's doctrine of belief is that it must abide, and he makes the case narratively three times.

**15. The trial-motif is the book's dominant genre-shape.** John 5 (forensic defence; μαρτυρ- 11×, the book's densest) → 7:14–52 → 8:12–59 (the two-witness rule cited) → 9:13–34 (four interrogations) → 18:19–19:16 (the trial proper). The Paraclete is given a forensic verb (ἐλέγχω, 16:8), and παράκλητος itself is courtroom vocabulary — counsel for the defence. The judgement thread runs 3:19; 5:22, 27; 9:39; 12:31; 16:8, 11 (verified). `[T]` **High.** John is a Gospel shaped as a lawsuit, and the reader has held the verdict since 1:1.

**Reached independently; now converged.** This finding was derived from the lexical and structural evidence above before the two May 2026 conversation records were opened. Those records develop the same frame under the Hebrew *rîb* (covenant-lawsuit) category — the prophetic form in which God summons witnesses, presents evidence and calls for a verdict — and add the observation that **the reader is the jury**, which 20:31 makes explicit. `[S: conv]` for the *rîb* label and the jury framing; `[T]` for everything the sweep verified. An earned confirmation.

**15b. The reversal: the defendant is the judge.** 5:22 ("the Father judges no one, but has given all judgement to the Son") with 5:27 ("authority to execute judgement, because he is the Son of Man"), stated inside a defence speech against a capital charge and dramatised through to 19:13 (where ἐκάθισεν may even seat Jesus on the *bēma*). `[T]` **High.** This is the structural engine of the irony catalogued at finding 25 below.

**16. The kingship arc has four stations and ends in apostasy.** 6:15 (refused) → 12:13 (acclaimed) → 18:36–37 (redefined) → 19:15 ("we have no king but Caesar"). `[T]` **High.**

**17. Peter's arc has three stations built on one saying.** 10:11 (the shepherd lays down his life) → 13:37 (Peter claims the formula) → 21:15–19 (restored to the office and given the death). `[T]` **High.**

**18. Two chapters lack πατήρ entirely: 9 and 19.** Against a book-wide 136 occurrences and a mean of ~7 per chapter (23 in ch. 14). Both are formal interrogations ending in expulsion or execution. `[T]` for the count; the significance is **moderate** and flagged under the pattern-break-absence gate rather than asserted.

### C. Intertextual findings

**19. Psalm 69 is John's spine-psalm and his three uses track its arc.** 2:17 (69:9, zeal) → 15:25 (69:4, hated without cause) → 19:28–29 (69:21, vinegar). `[T]` for the citations; `[I]` **moderate–high** for the sequential reading. **This is the sweep's strongest Move 2 finding.**

**20. Isaiah frames the public ministry.** 1:23 (Isa 40:3) opens it; 12:38, 40 (Isa 53:1 and 6:10) close it, with 12:41 ("Isaiah saw his glory") supplying the hermeneutic. 6:45 (Isa 54:13) sits at the centre. `[T]` **High.**

**21. Zechariah is live but *not* sequential.** Three uses (2:16 ← Zech 14:21, *moderate*; 12:15 ← Zech 9:9; 19:37 ← Zech 12:10) drawn from chs. 14, 9 and 12 in that order. `[T]` **High** as a negative finding — John is not tracing Zechariah's internal movement as he traces Psalm 69's.

**22. Zechariah 12:10 → 13:1 (the fountain opened) is the best single lead on two open questions at once** — the blood and water of 19:34, and the unidentified citation at 7:38. `[I]` **moderate–high.**

**23. The crucifixion is the densest citation-cluster in the book** — four formula-citations in fourteen verses (19:24, 28, 36, 37), three of them Psalter. `[T]` **High.**

**24. John's OT dependence is institutional more than propositional.** Roughly fifteen formula-citations, but the load is carried by feast, temple, tabernacle, lamb, manna, serpent, shepherd, vine, water, light and garden. `[I]` **High.** Consequence for future solo digs: Move 2 has real yield in John, but Move 4 has more.

### D. Unwitting testimony — a pattern class

**25. Hostile speech in John repeatedly states the truth its speaker denies.** `[T]` for each instance; `[I]` **high** for the class. *(New in v1.1. The sweep's first pass caught several of these individually and never gathered them; the class was named by `[S: conv]` and each member then verified.)* The pattern is not decorative irony — it is the forensic argument being made by narrative technique: **the prosecution's own evidence keeps entering as testimony for the defence.**

| Speaker | What they mean | What they say | Verification |
|---------|----------------|---------------|--------------|
| **Caiaphas (11:49–52)** | political expediency — better one man die than the nation perish | substitutionary atonement, and the gathering of the scattered children of God | The narrator *states* the double meaning ("he did not say this of his own accord, but being high priest that year he prophesied"). `[T]` **High** — the only place John decodes an opponent's words as prophecy |
| **The Pharisees (12:19)** | sarcastic despair — we are gaining nothing | "**Ἴδε** ὁ κόσμος ὀπίσω αὐτοῦ ἀπῆλθεν" — the universal reign of Zech 9:10, four verses after Zech 9:9 is quoted | ἴδε verified as the 1:29/36 and 19:14 interjection. `[T]` **High** |
| **Pilate (19:14, 19–22)** | mockery — "Behold your king"; a sardonic placard | the true title, posted in Hebrew, Latin and Greek, and refused revision ("what I have written I have written") | ἴδε verified at 19:14; the three languages at 19:20. `[T]` **High** |
| **The chief priests (19:15)** | political submission to Rome | "we have no king but Caesar" — the renunciation of the theocracy, at Passover | `[T]` **High**; the last station of the four-point kingship arc (finding 16) |
| **The Pharisees' court (9:13–34)** | a formal proceeding to discredit the sign | the proceeding demonstrates the sign and proves the investigators blind (9:39–41) | `[T]` **High**; the whole Gospel in miniature |
| **The crowd (7:41–42)** | the Christ cannot come from Galilee; he must come from Bethlehem of David's line | a correct scriptural argument, factually wrong about this man, left uncorrected by the narrator | `[T]` **High** for the text; `[I]` for the irony |
| **The soldiers (19:23–24, 33–34)** | routine execution detail | Ps 22:18 and Exod 12:46 / Zech 12:10 fulfilled by men with no interest in either | `[T]` **High** |

**Why this matters for preaching.** Every one of these is a place where a sermon can show the congregation the mechanism rather than assert it — and the mechanism is John's argument. It also supplies the pastoral edge the overview's presenting situation needs: a community whose confession is being adjudicated against them is being shown that the court's own evidence convicts the court.

**26. The custodians cannot read the brief.** 5:39–40 ("you search the Scriptures… it is they that bear witness about me, yet you refuse to come to me"), 5:45–47 ("Moses… wrote of me"), 9:41 ("now that you say 'we see', your guilt remains"), 12:37–43 (the authorities who believed but would not confess). `[T]` **High.** The professional custodians of the texts are the ones who cannot see what the texts say — and John makes expertise an aggravating factor, not a mitigating one (9:41). Pairs with finding 25 as its diagnosis.

## Headline Findings (whole book)

The five claims across the sweep that meet the criteria — multi-tool convergence, high confidence, central, homiletically generative.

1. **John's structure is a two-bracket forensic shape, and the counts demonstrate both stages.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary counts (σημεῖον — 13 of 17 inside 1:19–10:42, three in 11–12, one after), Repetition (πιστεύω collapses after ch. 13; ἀγαπάω and κόσμος spike after it), Structure, Positional Necessity across pericopes 21, 27–28. **Bracket A (1:19 ↔ 10:40–42) closes a movement of testimony with a verdict on the first witness; chs. 11–12 are the pivotal exhibit and the turn; bracket B closes at 12:37 with the narrator's verdict on the whole.** A series should be planned around both, and 12:37–50 should be preached as the close of the public ministry, never as the start of the upper room.

2. **The footwashing is an enacted laying-down of life, because 13:4 uses the good shepherd's own verb in the same form.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary (τίθησιν, 3 occurrences: 2:10; 10:11; 13:4), Tool 11 Move 4, Structure, Positional Necessity. This lifts John 13 out of the humility-lesson register into the passion narrative where it belongs.

3. **"Let not your hearts be troubled" (14:1, 27) forbids the disciples an emotion Jesus has himself just had twice (12:27; 13:21).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary (ταράσσω, 5 occurrences), Tool 11 Move 4, Tone, Positional Necessity. The comfort of John 14 is offered by someone who is not pretending, and it answers a distress the previous pericope caused.

4. **John 9 is the narrative answer to John 3, joined by γεννάω and ἐκβάλλω.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary (γεννάω at 3:3–8 and 9:2, 19, 20, 34; ἐκβάλλω at 6:37 and 9:34–35), Tool 11 Move 4, Narrator's Comment (9:22), Original Audience. 5. **Hostile speech in John repeatedly states the truth its speaker denies — and this is the forensic argument being made by narrative technique.** `[T]` for each instance, `[I]` **high** for the class. ## Preaching Pitfalls (whole book)

### Pitfall: Preaching "the seven signs"

- **What it looks like:** A series built on seven numbered signs, matched to seven "I am" sayings.
- **Why it's wrong:** John numbers exactly two signs (2:11 "the first"; 4:54 "the second") and then stops. `[T]` σημεῖον occurs 17×, and 20:30 says explicitly that many others are *not* written. The seven is a preacher's scheme imposed on a book that declines to count.
- **The corrective:** Use the numbering John gives (the Cana–Cana bracket) and the brackets he marks (10:40–42; 12:37). If a seven-fold structure is used homiletically, say that it is yours and not his. **Note for the forensic reading:** describing the signs as *exhibits entered into evidence* survives this correction intact and is the better framing — an exhibit list does not need to run to seven, and the prior conversation's "seven signs… entered into evidence" should simply drop the number.

### Pitfall: Using John 9 to flatter the congregation

- **What it looks like:** the irony about the experts preached as "the scholars missed it, but ordinary believers see" — with the congregation cast as the man born blind and someone else as the Pharisees.
- **Why it's wrong:** 9:41 makes the *claim to see* the aggravating factor. Any group that hears the chapter and concludes it can see has stepped into the position the chapter condemns. The man born blind has no credentials **and no confidence** — he begins with "I do not know" (9:12, 25) and ends at worship.
- **The corrective:** preach the chapter's irony against the hearer, not past them. The honest question is not "who are the Pharisees today?" but "what am I certain I can see?"

### Pitfall: Preaching the Farewell Discourse as generic comfort

- **What it looks like:** 14:1–6 at a funeral; 14:27 as a devotional; 15:1–8 as spiritual self-improvement.
- **Why it's wrong:** 14:1 answers a distress created eight verses earlier — a betrayer named, a denial predicted, a departure announced. The room is closed and the men in it are about to be scattered and expelled (16:2). And 15:1's "true vine" is polemical: every OT vine text is an indictment.
- **The corrective:** Preach 13:33–38 before 14:1. Name the wound the balm is for. Then note 17:20, where the closed room is explicitly opened to later believers — the caution about over-application has its own limit.

### Pitfall: "The Jews" preached flat

- **What it looks like:** Ἰουδαῖοι (77×) treated as a single hostile bloc, with 8:44 preached without qualification.
- **Why it's wrong:** The referent shifts — Judean authorities (7:13; 9:22), Judeans as a region (11:19), the crowd, the people at large — and 4:22 has Jesus say "salvation is from the Jews". 8:44 is spoken inside a dispute about *paternity* to people described in 8:31 as having believed him.
- **The corrective:** Translate contextually, name the problem from the pulpit once and clearly, and never let the phrase float free. This is not optional.

### Pitfall: Glory severed from the cross

- **What it looks like:** A "glory of Christ" sermon that never reaches the nails; or a bleak crucifixion sermon with no enthronement.
- **Why it's wrong:** δοξάζω clusters five times in 13:31–32, triggered by Judas's exit; 12:23 pairs glorification with a grain of wheat dying; 7:39 makes the Spirit's giving wait on glorification; the narrator glosses ὑψόω as "by what kind of death" (12:33).
- **The corrective:** Let ὑψόω and δοξάζω carry both senses and say so. The crucifixion in John is a coronation and should sound like one.

### Pitfall: "The hour" as chronology

- **What it looks like:** "Jesus's timing was not yet right."
- **Why it's wrong:** The hour-notices are why two arrests and a stoning fail (7:30; 8:20; 8:59) and why 13:1 can say it has come. The clock is the Father's, and the plot runs on it.
- **The corrective:** Track the six stations — 2:4 → 7:30 → 8:20 → 12:23 → 13:1 → 17:1 — and preach sovereignty, not scheduling.

### Pitfall: Building on the ἀγαπάω/φιλέω alternation in 21:15–17

- **What it looks like:** "Jesus asks for *agape* love; Peter can only offer *philia*; the third time Jesus comes down to Peter's level."
- **Why it's wrong:** John uses the two verbs interchangeably elsewhere — the Father *loves* the Son with ἀγαπάω at 3:35 and φιλέω at 5:20; the beloved disciple is ἠγάπα at 13:23 and ἐφίλει at 20:2. The nouns for the sheep and the verbs of commission also vary across the three exchanges, which suggests stylistic variation rather than a graded lexicon.
- **The corrective:** Preach the threefold structure against the threefold denial, which the text does mark, and leave the verb-choice alone unless a lexicon supports it.

### Pitfall: Preaching 7:53–8:11 without saying what it is

- **What it looks like:** Silence about the manuscript situation, or a brief embarrassed footnote.
- **Why it's wrong:** The passage is absent from the earliest and best witnesses, floats to three different locations in the manuscripts that have it, and reads un-Johannine. Congregations generally find the facts strengthening; discovering them later from a sceptical source is much worse.
- **The corrective:** If preached, say plainly what it is and why the church has kept it.

## Open Questions / Uncertainties

1. **John 7:38 — the unidentified citation, and the punctuation.** No OT text reads as quoted. Candidates: Zech 14:8, Ezek 47:1–12, Isa 58:11, Ps 78:15–16, Prov 18:4. And "out of his heart" — the believer's or Christ's? The two questions interact. **The highest-value Logos target in the book**, and finding 22 above (Zech 12:10 → 13:1) is a lead worth testing.
2. **John 19:36 — Exodus 12:46 / Numbers 9:12, or Psalm 34:20?** The wording favours Exodus LXX; the sweep recommends both, weighted to Exodus. *Moderate–high.*
3. **John 19:13 — ἐκάθισεν transitive or intransitive?** "Pilate sat down on the judgment seat" or "seated him on the judgment seat". Grammatically open; theologically loud. *Uncertain.*
4. **John 20:31 — aorist or present subjunctive?** Evangelistic or confirmatory purpose. Manuscript evidence divided. *Moderate uncertainty.*
5. **John 14:31b — "Rise, let us go from here", followed by three more chapters.** Redaction, delay, or a walking discourse. *Genuinely open; show the options.*
6. **John 3:13 and 3:31 — where does the speech end and the evangelist's commentary begin?** Two unmarked transitions in one chapter. Translations differ.
7. **John 8:56 — which Abrahamic moment is "my day"?** Gen 17:17, Gen 22, or Gen 15. *Uncertain.*
8. **John 13:10 — the shorter reading** (omitting "except for his feet") has real support and changes the sense. *Moderate.*
9. **John 1:18 — μονογενὴς θεός or ὁ μονογενὴς υἱός?** See Book-Overview Tensions; this bears directly on the θεός-inclusio claim.
10. **John 7:8 — οὐκ or οὔπω** ("I am not going up" / "I am not yet going up"), with an integrity question attached.
11. **John 21:15–17 — is the ἀγαπάω/φιλέω alternation significant?** The sweep's judgement is no; a lexicon should settle it before a sermon depends on either answer.
12. **The one-cleansing-or-two question (2:13–22).** Affects how the Johannine placement is preached. *Open.*
