Claim Audit: John — the “First Week” Hypothesis Candidates

Source audited: the six unaudited candidates raised by John/dig-deeper-john-first-week-hypothesis-test.md (Draft v0.1, 14 November 2026). None had been tested; five were proposed by the run itself. Passages engaged: John 1:1–2:12; 19:30–20:31; with Genesis 1–2, Exodus 19, Hosea 2 and 6, Isaiah 25, 54, 62. Date: 14 November 2026 Mode: Claim Audit (dig-deeper Phase 0.5b) Purpose: Settle the six candidates before any of them reaches book-overview-john, a sermon, or the essay the hypothesis test is written towards.

Method. Two independent auditors, run one after the other, each blind to the proposing document. Auditor A took the four single contacts (C3–C6): wording, rarity, rival sources, Greek. Auditor B took the two design claims (C1–C2) and was required to build a chance baseline for each. Neither opened a commentary or a web source; both were given the corpus and the house search rules. Their decisive counts are reproduced in the main session.

Headline. One claim survives with nuance; five need reframing. The claim the proposing run rated strongest — the σκοτία frame with its Genesis day-one vocabulary at 20:1 — failed comprehensively, and failed on evidence the proposing run never gathered. The claim it rated weakest on structure — the ἀρχή link — turned out to be the only one with a decisive statistical footing, though it is misdescribed.

Claims Audited

Claim 1: σκοτία frames the Gospel at 1:5 and 20:1, and 20:1 echoes the Genesis day-one formula

Source: hypothesis test v0.1, listed as the run’s one apparently original finding and as the strongest support for the inclusio with John 19–20. Claim: σκοτία (“darkness”) at 1:5 and 20:1 brackets the Gospel; and τῇ μιᾷ τῶν σαββάτων (“on the first day of the week”) with πρωΐ (“early”) at 20:1 echoes Genesis 1:5, ἡμέρα μία (“day one”) and ἐγένετο πρωί (“there was morning”).

Hays criteria:

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability Strong Greek Genesis was available; John 1:1 shows him engaging Genesis 1:1
Volume Weak One shared cardinal, in a different case and construction; no ἡμέρα at 20:1; the darkness lexeme differs
Recurrence Weak John does recur to Genesis, but 20:1 shows no independent Genesis contact
Thematic Coherence Possible New creation on the first day is coherent Johannine theology — supplied by the reader, not by 20:1’s wording
Historical Plausibility Strong Nothing makes the intention impossible; the question is whether it is signalled
History of Interpretation Not assessed Text-first audit; would need secondary literature
Satisfaction Weak The cross-Gospel idiom control explains the wording without the allusion

Verdict: Needs reframing — both halves fail as stated.

Reasoning. σκοτία stands in six verses of John (eight tokens): 1:5 twice, 6:17, 8:12, 12:35 twice, 12:46, 20:1; σκότος occurs once, at 3:19. Five tokens fall inside the proposed frame and three at its edges — the opposite shape from a bracket. The chance baseline is decisive: among content lemmas of comparable frequency, about seven would be expected to occur in both the prologue and chapter 20 by chance alone, so finding one was guaranteed. Three do: πρῶτος (“first”, thirteen occurrences, twice in chapter 20), σκοτία, and ὀπίσω (“after, behind”). No one proposes a πρῶτος frame. The result is also unstable: widening the opening zone from the prologue to the whole of chapter 1 raises the count from three to nine.

Three further objections stand independently of the statistics. 20:1 is not a terminus — it is verse 811 of 866, with fifty-five verses after it; it opens the resurrection narrative rather than closing the book. The senses differ: 1:5’s ἡ σκοτία αὐτὸ οὐ κατέλαβεν (“the darkness did not overcome it”) is cosmic and figurative, while 20:1’s σκοτίας ἔτι οὔσης (“darkness still being”) is a genitive-absolute time-of-day note whose nearest Johannine relative is 6:17, σκοτία ἤδη ἐγεγόνει (“darkness had already come on”). And nothing at 20:1 marks it as a resumption of 1:5 — no shared collocate, no φῶς (“light”).

The Genesis half fails separately and more sharply. All four Gospels use the same idiom for Easter morning, and Luke 24:1 and John 20:1 open with the identical five words, τῇ δὲ μιᾷ τῶν σαββάτων; Mark 16:2 has τῇ μιᾷ τῶν σαββάτων and Matthew 28:1 εἰς μίαν σαββάτων. John’s wording is shared early-Christian idiom. Genesis has ἡμέρα μία — nominative, with the noun ἡμέρα; John has a dative governed by σάββατα with no ἡμέρα present at all. The only shared element is the commonest numeral in Greek. πρωΐ carries no weight either: it stands thirteen times in the four Gospels, twice more in John itself (18:28; 21:4), and in Genesis 1 it occurs six times, once in each day’s refrain — so it points to the six-day formula, not to day one. The one element genuinely distinctive to day one is ἡμέρα μία, and that is precisely what John lacks. Finally, Genesis 1’s darkness word is σκότος, not σκοτία: σκοτία occurs nowhere in Genesis, and across the whole of Swete it appears in only three books — Job, Micah and Isaiah, once each. It is essentially not a Septuagintal word.

What survives. σκοτία is a genuine Johannine Leitwort running from 1:5 to 20:1 — a thread, not a frame. John’s Genesis engagement is real but located at 1:1 (ἐν ἀρχῇ) and 20:22 (ἐνεφύσησεν), not at 20:1.

Action required: remove the claim from the hypothesis test’s H8 evidence and from its list of original findings. The Leitwort may be stated at moderate; the frame and the Genesis echo may not be stated at all.

Claim 2: an ἀρχή inclusio brackets John 1:1–2:11

Source: hypothesis test v0.1, offered as support for H2 (a deliberate numbered sequence) and as partly attested by Wilkin. Claim: ἐν ἀρχῇ (“in the beginning”) at 1:1–2 and ἀρχὴν τῶν σημείων (“beginning of the signs”) at 2:11 form an inclusio bracketing 1:1–2:11 as a unit.

Hays criteria:

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability (of the device) Strong Inclusio is pervasive, and John demonstrably uses verbatim repetition structurally
Volume Possible One lemma, two cases, two senses — but with a decisive baseline behind it
Recurrence Weak as a bracket ἀρχή recurs five more times in John; ἐν ἀρχῇ never again after 1:2
Thematic Coherence Possible “Beginning” of the Word and “first” of the signs cohere conceptually; 2:11’s sense is ordinal
Historical Plausibility Possible John can do inclusio; whether he intended this one against the 1:18/1:19 seam is the live question
History of Interpretation Not assessed Wilkin notes the link; the audit did not consult him
Satisfaction Weak The Cana bracket and the signs frame each explain more text with better formal credentials

Verdict: Needs reframing — the link is real and non-random; the device is misdescribed.

Reasoning. ἀρχή stands eight times in John: 1:1, 1:2, 2:11, 6:64, 8:25, 8:44, 15:27, 16:4. ἐν ἀρχῇ occurs only at 1:1 and 1:2. The baseline is the strongest number in this audit and it favours the claim: apart from the article, καί, οὗτος and ἐν, ἀρχή is the only lemma shared between 1:1–2 and 2:11, against a chance expectation of about 0.03 — roughly thirty times chance, with no competitor at any frequency. Whatever else is wrong here, it is not coincidence.

What is wrong is the description. The two uses differ in sense and syntax: 1:1–2 has an absolute temporal dative, 2:11 a predicate accusative with a partitive genitive, ordinal rather than absolute. Five of the eight tokens fall outside the proposed frame. And the span overrides the clearest seam in the book: 1:18 closes the prologue and 1:19 opens a new unit with a formal heading — Καὶ αὕτη ἐστὶν ἡ μαρτυρία τοῦ Ἰωάννου (“And this is the witness of John”).

Two rivals explain the data better. ἐν Κανὰ τῆς Γαλιλαίας (“in Cana of Galilee”) stands verbatim at 2:1 and 2:11 and nowhere else in John — 4:46 has εἰς τὴν Κανὰ τῆς Γαλιλαίας and 21:2 ἀπὸ Κανὰ τῆς Γαλιλαίας. Four words, same sense, same syntax, enclosing a self-contained pericope with a clear exit at 2:12. That is the real bracket at 2:11, and it brackets 2:1–11. Separately, σημεῖον (“sign”) occurs seventeen times in John, first at 2:11 and last at 20:30, both with ἐποίησεν … ὁ Ἰησοῦς, and 20:30 sits immediately before the Gospel’s stated purpose at 20:31. On that reading ἀρχὴν τῶν σημείων opens a frame rather than closing one.

Defensible restatement: ἀρχή at 2:11 deliberately recalls 1:1–2 as a verbal resumption, without constituting an inclusio that makes 1:1–2:11 a unit.

Action required: restate as a verbal resumption at moderate. Do not use it as evidence that 1:1–2:11 is a bounded composition.

Claim 3: τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῇ τρίτῃ at John 2:1 alludes to Hosea 6:2

Source: hypothesis test v0.1, H6; reported there as a verbatim phrase match and treated as the strongest support for the resurrection reading of 2:1. Claim: John 2:1’s “third day” alludes to Hosea 6:2, where the third day is a day of resurrection.

Hays criteria:

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability Strong Hosea was within reach
Volume Weak The phrase stands in twenty-five verses of Swete; it is the stock rendering of the Hebrew formula, itself in twenty-five verses of the WLC
Recurrence Weak No explicit Hosea citation found elsewhere in John
Thematic Coherence Strong Supplied by John himself: 2:19 ἐν τρισὶν ἡμέραις ἐγερῶ αὐτόν (“in three days I will raise it up”), and 2:22
Historical Plausibility Possible 1 Corinthians 15:4’s κατὰ τὰς γραφάς (“according to the Scriptures”) shows some scriptural warrant was current, but names no text
History of Interpretation Not assessed Jobes reaches Hosea 6:2 through the Jewish three-days tradition; the audit did not consult her
Satisfaction Possible It explains the dating, but so would several rivals

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning. The phrase cannot carry an allusion. τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῇ τρίτῃ stands in twenty-five verses of Swete — Genesis 22:4; 34:25; 40:20; 42:18; Exodus 19:16; Leviticus 7:8; 19:7; Numbers 7:24; 19:12; 19:19; 29:20; 31:19; Judges 20:30; 1 Reigns 30:1; 2 Reigns 1:2; 3 Reigns 3:18; 12:12; 12:24; 4 Reigns 20:5; 20:8; 2 Chronicles 10:12; 29:17; Esther 5:1; Hosea 6:2; 1 Maccabees 11:18 — and the Hebrew formula behind it in twenty-five verses of the WLC. Hosea 6:2 is in fact marginally further from John’s form than fourteen of those, since Hosea has ἐν before the phrase and John has none.

The word-order evidence runs against the claim. The New Testament’s resurrection formula is the other order, τῇ τρίτῃ ἡμέρᾳ (Matthew 16:21; 17:23; 20:19; Luke 9:22; 24:7, 46; Acts 10:40). The NA28 apparatus shows B, Θ and f¹³ moving John 2:1 towards that formula — so the resurrection-shaped reading at 2:1 is the secondary one. That argues against reading resurrection off the phrase, and for reading it off the chapter.

What survives, and it is still worth having. John 2 itself binds the third day to resurrection at 2:19–22. And Hosea 6:2 remains the best candidate for the scriptural third-day resurrection that 1 Corinthians 15:4 appeals to without naming. Reframe as a thematic resonance licensed by John 2:19–22, not a verbal allusion.

Action required: the hypothesis test’s H6 must drop “verbatim” as evidence of allusion and rest on 2:19–22 instead. The historical-plausibility limb goes to research.

Claim 4: Exodus 19:11, 16 is a rival or second layer behind the same phrase

Source: hypothesis test v0.1; raised as the main rival to Hosea 6:2. Claim: the Sinai theophany on the third day stands behind, or alongside, John 2:1.

Hays criteria:

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability Strong The best-known third-day text in the Torah
Volume Weak Same twenty-five-verse problem; the marginally closer bare-dative form is shared with fourteen other verses
Recurrence Strong John names Moses and the law at 1:17, so Sinai is in his field a few verses earlier
Thematic Coherence Weak at Cana No mountain, cloud, fire, trumpet, trembling or law in 2:1–11. The one hook is δόξα at 2:11, supplied by 1:14–17
Historical Plausibility Strong A first-century reader would hear “third day” against Sinai readily
History of Interpretation Not assessed —
Satisfaction Weak It does not explain a wedding, water or wine

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning. As worded the claim fails, because the phrase is stock idiom and cannot support layering, and nothing in Cana’s own vocabulary points to Sinai. What is defensible is more modest and belongs elsewhere: John 1:14–17 has already put Moses, law and δόξα (“glory”) in play, so a Sinai colouring attaches to the sequence of John 1–2 and to δόξα at 2:11, not to the date formula at 2:1.

Action required: reframe at the level of 1:14–17, or drop.

Claim 5: John 20:22 ἐνεφύσησεν alludes to Genesis 2:7

Source: hypothesis test v0.1, H8. Claim: John’s “he breathed” echoes the creation of man.

Hays criteria:

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability Strong John 1:1 shows Genesis demonstrably in hand
Volume Strong, with a caveat ἐμφυσάω is a hapax in the New Testament, and John has exactly the Genesis form — but one word is all there is
Recurrence Strong John’s Genesis engagement is established at 1:1
Thematic Coherence Strong Life-giving breath conferring πνεῦμα on a new community reads as new creation
Historical Plausibility Strong Genesis 2:7 was the standard creation-of-man text
History of Interpretation Not assessed Expected to be a commonplace
Satisfaction Strong It explains an otherwise inexplicable choice of a rare verb

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance — the only survivor of the six.

Reasoning. ἐμφυσάω occurs once in the whole New Testament, at John 20:22, in exactly the form Genesis 2:7 uses; the Hebrew root נפח (“to breathe”) stands in only twelve verses of the WLC, of which Genesis 2:7 and Ezekiel 37:9 are the only two where breathing gives life.

Two nuances the claim as worded suppressed. John’s ἐνεφύσησεν stands absolute — no object and no prepositional phrase; “he breathed on them” is a translator’s supply, where Genesis has ἐνεφύσησεν εἰς τὸ πρόσωπον αὐτοῦ (“breathed into his face”). So the verbal tie is a single word, not a phrase. And Ezekiel 37:9 is a co-candidate, not a weaker rival: the same verb, the same Hebrew root, and it supplies πνεῦμα (“spirit, breath”) and the raising of the dead — both of which John 20:22 has and Genesis 2:7 lacks, Genesis having πνοή (“breath”).

Action required: restate as “Genesis 2:7, with Ezekiel 37:9 as a second and possibly conflated voice”. May be carried at moderate–high.

Claim 6: Cana draws on Hosea 2 rather than, or better than, Isaiah 25:6

Source: hypothesis test v0.1, H7, proposed as a redirection away from Isaiah 25:6. Claim: Hosea 2 (marriage and new wine) is a better background for the Cana feast than Isaiah 25:6 (the mountain feast with wine).

Hays criteria:

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability Strong Both available; no discriminator
Volume Weak Each shares only οἶνος (“wine”) with John 2; neither has γάμος (“wedding”) or νυμφίος (“bridegroom”)
Recurrence Weak No Hosea citation found elsewhere in John
Thematic Coherence Possible, split Hosea 2 wins on marriage; Isaiah 25:6 wins on feast-and-wine and adds death’s defeat at 25:8
Historical Plausibility Possible Both were available as restoration texts
History of Interpretation Not assessed —
Satisfaction Weak Hosea 2 explains neither a wedding feast, nor a bridegroom, nor water-to-wine, nor quality

Verdict: Needs reframing — the disjunction cannot be sustained.

Reasoning. Neither candidate contains γάμος at all — a nil return with a positive control passed, the word standing elsewhere in Swete at Genesis 29:22, Esther 1:5, 2:18, 9:22, Tobit, Wisdom and Maccabees. The claim also mis-describes its own evidence: Hosea 2’s “new wine” is תִּירוֹשׁ in the Hebrew, which the Greek flattens to plain οἶνος, so a Greek-level allusion to new wine in Hosea 2 is not available; and John’s qualifier is καλός (“good”), not “new”.

A text the claim never considered beats both. Isaiah 62:5 has νυμφίος ἐπὶ νύμφῃ (“a bridegroom over a bride”) — John’s own vocabulary, at 2:9 and again at 3:29. On John’s wording Isaiah 62:4–5 is a stronger marriage source than Hosea 2, whose Greek offers only ἀνήρ, γυνή and μνηστεύομαι.

Reframe as a convergence of restoration motifs — marriage from the Hosea and Isaiah 62 stream, feast-wine from the Isaiah 25 stream — rather than a contest one text wins.

Action required: H7 should be rewritten accordingly, and Isaiah 62:4–5 queued for its own audit.

New claims raised by the audit

  2. Isaiah 62:4–5 → John 2:9 / 3:29 — νυμφίος and νύμφη, John’s own bridegroom vocabulary. Suggested: moderate.

Method findings worth keeping

  • πρῶτος as a control word. It occurs thirteen times in John, including the prologue and twice in chapter 20. If a proposed “frame” in John would equally support a πρῶτος frame, it has not cleared chance. Adopt as a standing control.
  • Apparatus sigla defeat phrase searches. The SBLGNT and Swete files print apparatus sigla inside words and phrases, in the Unicode block U+2E00–U+2E1F. An ordered phrase search that does not strip that block returns a false nil: in this audit it silently dropped John 2:1 and 1 Corinthians 15:4 from a search for their own phrase. This belongs in the corpus README beside the accent-stripping rule. (Applied to _texts/README.md on 14 November 2026, with two further rules — see below.)
  • A corpus gap — since closed. At the time of the audit the John NA28 export was not a continuous running text and could not be addressed by verse. Patrick re-exported it the same afternoon, and it has been verified: all 21 chapters, each ending on its Nestle-Aland verse, no verse missing, 5:4 rightly absent and 7:53–8:11 present in double brackets; 1:41 reads πρῶτον and 2:1 τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῇ τρίτῃ. Two parsing conventions were established in the course of checking and are now in the corpus README: in the NA28 exports a chapter number is followed by an ordinary space and a verse number by a no-break space; and John 6 is the one chapter whose numeral sits alone on its own line, so a parser that requires number, space, Greek letter loses it and folds chapter 6 into chapter 5. No verdict in this audit rested on the faulty file — the load-bearing readings were taken from the NA28 apparatus directly.

Addendum — the σημεῖον envelope baselined (14 November 2026)

Candidate J10 was raised by Auditor B while testing C1–C2, and was put to a third independent blind auditor the same afternoon with a chance baseline required. Verdict: Needs reframing. The data were confirmed and the structural inference failed.

Confirmed. σημεῖον (“sign”) stands seventeen times in John, first at 2:11 and last at 20:30 in both the SBLGNT and the NA28; both outer verses carry ἐποίησεν (“he did”) with ὁ Ἰησοῦς as subject, and both mention μαθητής (“disciple”), which the proposal did not notice and which slightly strengthens the overlap. 20:30 does stand immediately before the purpose statement, and the thematic link between 2:11’s ἐπίστευσαν (“they believed”) and 20:31’s πιστεύητε (“that you may believe”) is real.

Why the structural inference fails, on four independent grounds.

  1. The envelope shape is unremarkable. Among a pool of 100 lemmas of comparable frequency in John, the expected number showing first-occurrence in chapters 1–3 and last occurrence in chapter 20 or 21 is 39; the observed number is 39. σημεῖον is one of them, with γυνή (“woman”), πρόβατον (“sheep”), Γαλιλαία and φέρω (“carry”). The bare shape has no discriminating power.
  2. The collocate is cheap, and not even parallel. Ἰησοῦς stands in 26% of John’s verses and ποιέω in 11%. And the contiguous phrase ἐποίησεν ὁ Ἰησοῦς stands at 4:54, 9:14, 20:30 and 21:25 — but not at 2:11, where the verb and subject are split by the object (ἐποίησεν ἀρχὴν τῶν σημείων ὁ Ἰησοῦς). On the proposal’s own criterion, 20:30’s closest formal partner is 4:54.
  3. The standing πρῶτος control is not cleared. πρῶτος has its first occurrence in the prologue (1:15), its last in chapter 20, twice in chapter 20 — and the cognate verb ἔρχομαι at both ends (ἐρχόμενος 1:15; ἦλθεν 20:4; ἐλθών 20:8), with both ends closing on πιστεύω. That is the same shape of evidence with a better verbal tie, for a word nobody thinks frames anything.
  4. The far member belongs to chapter 21. βιβλίον (“book”) occurs exactly twice in John — 20:30 and 21:25, and nowhere else; it is the only lemma confined to those two verses. 21:25 is the fuller form of the same formula, dropping σημεῖα for the unrestricted ἃ ἐποίησεν ὁ Ἰησοῦς. The vocabulary that makes 20:30 sound like a colophon binds it forward to 21:25, not back to 2:11. Add that σημεῖον is absent from chapters 13–19 and from chapter 21 altogether — the occurrences run 2:11 to 12:37, then nothing for seven chapters. An eight-chapter gap before the far member is a colophon’s shape, not an envelope’s.

The manufactured-uniqueness finding, which generalises beyond this claim. σημεῖον does pass one narrow test uniquely: of the 39 lemmas with the envelope shape, only it has both end verses containing ποιέω and Ἰησοῦς. But that test was specified after inspecting σημεῖον’s endpoints. Building, for each of the 39, the analogous test drawn from its own two end verses, twelve of the thirty-nine pass only themselves — μαρτυρία, Σίμων, γραφή, δύο and πρῶτος among them. Apparent improbability in a frame claim is routinely an artefact of the specification. This belongs in the method notes for the whole project, not only for John.

The reframing the text supports. 20:30–31 is a colophon to the signs material, looking back over a series narrated 2:11–12:37 and counted at 2:11 (ἀρχήν), 4:54 (δεύτερον) and 12:37 (τοσαῦτα). It is the first of two colophons, the wider being 21:24–25. 2:11 opens the series; it does not open a frame closed at 20:30.

Three candidates raised by this baseline, all queued and all untested: the μαρτυρία / μαρτυρέω frame 1:7 → 21:24 (noun and cognate verb at both ends, in the same construction, and it includes chapter 21); the ἐφανέρωσεν / ordinal thread 2:11 → 4:54 → 21:1, 14; and Patrick’s resurrection as the eighth sign, whose textual basis is John’s own categorisation at 2:18–19, where the demand τί σημεῖον δεικνύεις ἡμῖν (“what sign do you show us?”) is answered with the raising of the temple of his body.

A methodological point recorded from Patrick, 14 November 2026: absence of an ordinal is not absence of a series. A reader already counting needs no label on every member — the signs are commonly counted as seven although John numbers only the first two. The principle is sound and bears directly on J14. It should be noted, though, that “seven signs” is a scholarly construct rather than John’s count, and that the traditional list varies, so the principle raises rather than lowers the burden of establishing where a series begins and ends.

Standing result after three design claims. σκοτία, ἀρχή and σημεῖον were each proposed as structural frames in John and each failed a chance baseline — the first because chance guarantees such a word exists, the second because the statistic was real but the device misdescribed, the third because the uniqueness was manufactured by its own specification. In this Gospel, frames built on salient vocabulary are easy to find and very hard to establish.

Text-First Declaration

  • Both auditors worked from the primary texts, blind to the proposing document, and opened no commentary or web source. Where a verdict genuinely needed secondary literature — the first-century currency of a third-day-resurrection reading of Hosea 6:2, and the history of interpretation on all six claims — it was routed to research rather than guessed.
  • Editions. Counts in John and the Gospels from the SBLGNT by lemma; Greek Old Testament counts from Swete, with every stem hit read for sense before counting; Hebrew counts from the WLC by lemma. Load-bearing readings cited from the NA28 apparatus and from the Rahlfs export, not from Swete’s Genesis, which is lacunose in chapters 1–2.
