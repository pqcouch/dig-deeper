# Point & Purpose: John 7:1–10:21 (the Tabernacles cycle)

**Passage:** John 7:1–10:21 — the Feast of Booths unit (living water, the light of the world, the great "I AM," the man born blind, the Good Shepherd)
**Source exegesis:** *Dig Deeper: John 7:1–10:21*
**Scope:** A short series (the material is ~130 verses and is bracketed by the feast, stitched by the water → light → sight arc; it does not sit comfortably as one sermon).
**Date:** 22 July 2026

---

## Series Fallen Condition Focus

We are prone to trust heritage, respectability, or partial sight — and so to miss, even resist, the Christ who alone gives living water, true light, and a shepherd's care. Like the festival crowd who knew "where he came from," and the leaders who cast out the man given his sight, we are tempted to let what we already have (pedigree, position, a little seeing) harden us against the one standing in front of us.

## Series Teaching Point

At the feast of water and light, Jesus reveals himself as the divine "I AM" — the living water, the light of the world, and the good shepherd who lays down his life — so that the thirsty drink, the blind see, and the outcast are gathered, while those who trust their own pedigree and sight are exposed and hardened.

## Series Purpose Statement

To bring hearers to come to the I AM for living water and true light, to hear the shepherd's voice and follow even at the cost of exclusion — and to warn the religiously confident against a hardness that hears the highest claims and reaches for stones.

## Big Question

When the true light and the living water stand in front of you, will your pedigree and your partial sight let you come — or will they harden you against him?

## The Sermons

Each movement carries its own Teaching Point and preaching aim. The *pericope adulterae* (7:53–8:11) is bracketed out as text-critically separate.

**1. The living water (7:1–52).**
*Teaching Point:* on the last, great day of the feast Jesus offers the Spirit-river to anyone who thirsts, while the hour of his glory stays in the Father's hands.
*Aim:* come to Christ and drink; stop judging him by appearances and origins.
*(Exegesis: the standalone* Dig Deeper: John 7:1–52 *report.)*

**2. The light and the great "I AM" (8:12–59).**
*Teaching Point:* Jesus is the light of the world and the eternal I AM — "before Abraham was, I am" — whose word alone frees from slavery to sin; religious pedigree saves no one.
*Aim:* abide in his word and be freed; beware a hardened unbelief that hears the highest claim and reaches for stones.
*(Handle 8:44 with the pastoral care the report flags — an intramural prophetic rebuke on moral likeness, not a statement about the Jewish people.)*

**3. The light that opens blind eyes (9:1–41).**
*Teaching Point:* the light of the world gives sight to a man born blind and is worshipped by the outcast, while the "seeing" go blind — sight is his gift, received in worship, not earned.
*Aim:* receive sight from Christ and confess him at any cost; beware the blindness of the confident.

**4. The Good Shepherd (10:1–21).**
*Teaching Point:* over against the shepherds who cast out the sheep, Jesus is the good shepherd who knows his own, lays down his life of his own accord, and gathers one flock.
*Aim:* hear his voice and follow; rest in the shepherd who dies and seeks the outcast — and, for those who lead, gather rather than scatter.

**Alternative five-part split.** If you would prefer four sermons of more even length, 8:12–59 divides naturally at v.31: a "light and witness" sermon (8:12–30) and a "freedom and true sonship" sermon (8:31–59), giving five sermons across the series.

## Keeping Honest

Series-wide, from the report's Preaching Pitfalls:

- **Preach across the chapter breaks.** Chapter 9 cashes out chapter 8's light-claim (the light gives sight), and chapter 10 answers chapter 9's excommunication (the shepherd gathers the cast-out). Slicing at the chapter numbers loses the argument.
- **Restore the condition on "the truth will set you free" (8:31–32).** The freedom is release from slavery to sin, and it is promised to those who *abide in his word* — not a slogan for free inquiry or self-expression.
- **Never let the Good Shepherd become a leadership seminar.** Jesus is *the* shepherd; the hearers are sheep who hear his voice. Under-shepherd application is secondary and derivative.
- **Guard 9:3 pastorally.** Jesus rejects the retributive "who sinned?"; "that the works of God might be displayed" names redemptive purpose, not a cold instrumentalising of the man's lifelong blindness.
