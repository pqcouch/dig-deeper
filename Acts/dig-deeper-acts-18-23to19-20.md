## The Passage

> **18:23** After spending some time there, he departed and went from one place to the next through the region of Galatia and Phrygia, strengthening all the disciples.
> **24** Now a Jew named Apollos, a native of Alexandria, came to Ephesus. He was an eloquent man, competent in the Scriptures. **25** He had been instructed in the way of the Lord. And being fervent in spirit, he spoke and taught accurately the things concerning Jesus, though he knew only the baptism of John. **26** He began to speak boldly in the synagogue, but when Priscilla and Aquila heard him, they took him aside and explained to him the way of God more accurately. **27** And when he wished to cross to Achaia, the brothers encouraged him and wrote to the disciples to welcome him. When he arrived, he greatly helped those who through grace had believed, **28** for he powerfully refuted the Jews in public, showing by the Scriptures that the Christ was Jesus.
> **19:1** And it happened that while Apollos was at Corinth, Paul passed through the inland country and came to Ephesus. There he found some disciples. **2** And he said to them, "Did you receive the Holy Spirit when you believed?" And they said, "No, we have not even heard that there is a Holy Spirit." **3** And he said, "Into what then were you baptized?" They said, "Into John's baptism." **4** And Paul said, "John baptized with the baptism of repentance, telling the people to believe in the one who was to come after him, that is, Jesus." **5** On hearing this, they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus. **6** And when Paul had laid his hands on them, the Holy Spirit came on them, and they began speaking in tongues and prophesying. **7** There were about twelve men in all.
> **8** And he entered the synagogue and for three months spoke boldly, reasoning and persuading them about the kingdom of God. **9** But when some became stubborn and continued in unbelief, speaking evil of the Way before the congregation, he withdrew from them and took the disciples with him, reasoning daily in the hall of Tyrannus. **10** This continued for two years, so that all the residents of Asia heard the word of the Lord, both Jews and Greeks.
> **11** And God was doing extraordinary miracles by the hands of Paul, **12** so that even handkerchiefs or aprons that had touched his skin were carried away to the sick, and their diseases left them and the evil spirits came out of them. **13** Then some of the itinerant Jewish exorcists undertook to invoke the name of the Lord Jesus over those who had evil spirits, saying, "I adjure you by the Jesus whom Paul proclaims." **14** Seven sons of a Jewish high priest named Sceva were doing this. **15** But the evil spirit answered them, "Jesus I know, and Paul I recognize, but who are you?" **16** And the man in whom was the evil spirit leaped on them, mastered all of them and overpowered them, so that they fled out of that house naked and wounded. **17** And this became known to all the residents of Ephesus, both Jews and Greeks. And fear fell upon them all, and the name of the Lord Jesus was extolled. **18** Also many of those who were now believers came, confessing and divulging their practices. **19** And a number of those who had practiced magic arts brought their books together and burned them in the sight of all. And they counted the value of them and found it came to fifty thousand pieces of silver. **20** So the word of the Lord continued to increase and prevail mightily.

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims that emerged across multiple tools:

1. **The passage's spine is the supremacy of the word of the Lord over every rival — deficient discipleship, fraudulent power, and costly idolatry — climaxing in the growth-seam of 19:20.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Author's Purpose, Structure (the three-stage progression deficient→fraudulent→renounced), Repetition (the Way, the name, baptism), and the summary seam itself (19:20, matching 6:7 and 12:24). This is the passage's centre of gravity and the reason the episodes are gathered here.

2. **19:10 is the deliberate reversal of the Spirit's prohibition at 16:6 — "all Asia heard the word of the Lord" answers "forbidden… to speak the word in Asia."** `[T]` for the verbal correspondence (both name Asia and "the word"); `[I]` for the reversal being intentional. Surfaced by: Positional Necessity Check, Tool 11 Move 4 (internal echo), Tool 2 (context). Confirmed independently by Pao. What was closed in God's timing is now flung wide — the same Spirit governs the No and the later Yes.

3. **A single Greek verb links the defeat of Sceva's sons to the triumph of the word: the demoniac "overpowered" (ἴσχυσεν, 19:16) the exorcists; the word "prevailed" (ἴσχυεν, 19:20).** `[T]` for the shared verb; `[I]` for intended catchword. Surfaced by: Repetition, Vocabulary/Original Languages, Tone. The counterfeit users of the name are overpowered; the word that carries the name overpowers. The irony is the argument.

4. **The Ephesian "disciples" (19:1–7) are a frontier transition, not a paradigm for a two-stage Spirit — Luke marks them as deficient ("only… John's baptism," "not even heard there is a Holy Spirit").** `[T]` Surfaced by: Context, Copycat, Tool 11 Move 4 (the Pentecost/Samaria/Cornelius/Ephesus frontier sequence), Difficult Verses. The oddity is the point of the episode, not a template to generalise.

5. **19:20's "mightily" (κατὰ κράτος) is military-conquest language, colouring the whole seam as the victorious advance of the word through opposition.** `[T]` for the phrase; `[S: Pao]`/`[I]` for the military register. Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Tone, Original Languages (κράτος elsewhere in Luke only at Luke 1:51; the phrase's sole LXX occurrence at Judg. 4:13 is a battle). The seam is not bland church-growth optimism; it is a conquest report.

## Phase 0.6 — Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement:** Three threads converge on Ephesus. (a) At 18:19–21 Paul made a brief first visit to the Ephesian synagogue and left promising, "I will return to you if God wills," leaving Priscilla and Aquila behind. (b) Far earlier, at 16:6, "the Spirit… forbade them to speak the word in Asia," diverting the mission to Macedonia. (c) The Corinth episode just before (18:1–17) closed with the Lord's night-vision assurance, "I have many in this city… no one will attack you to harm you" (18:9–10) — a template of protected, fruitful, extended urban ministry.

**Necessity answer:** This unit exists *here* because the preceding movement has set up exactly it. The promised return (18:21) is kept (19:1); the deferred Asian mission (16:6) is finally granted and completed ("all… Asia heard the word of the Lord," 19:10); and the Corinthian pattern of long, protected, region-saturating ministry (18:9–11, "a year and six months") is now surpassed at Ephesus ("two years," 19:10). The pericope is the payoff of a prohibition, a promise, and a paradigm — not a fresh start.

**Implication for this passage:** Read against 16:6, 19:10 is not a travel statistic but a theological resolution: *the Spirit who once closed Asia now fills it.* The same divine will (θέλημα) that redirected the mission now directs it home. This reframes the whole unit as God's sovereign timing vindicated — which is precisely the assurance Luke is writing to give (Luke 1:4). Preached without 16:6 in view, 19:10 loses its deepest note.

**Revisit after Tool 11:** With no OT citation to track (Move 2 nil-return), the positional weight rests on the *internal* echoes — the 16:6 reversal and the frontier-Spirit sequence (Move 4). Both confirm rather than qualify the answer above: the passage's necessity is intra-Acts, not intertextual.

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book-level:** Luke writes to give Theophilus and a mixed church *certainty* (Luke 1:4) that the Gentile movement is the God-authorised continuation of Israel's story, and that "the word" advances unstoppably through opposition under God's sovereign plan (the six growth-seams; the recurring *dei*/*boulē*).

**Passage-level:** This unit makes that case in the magic-capital of the empire. Luke gathers episodes that each test the *adequacy and power of a person's relation to Jesus*, and shows the word of the Lord triumphing over every rival: over **deficient but corrigible** discipleship (Apollos knowing "only… John's baptism"; the twelve who had "not even heard" of the Spirit), over **fraudulent** power (Sceva's sons wielding the name as a formula), and over **costly idolatry** (the burned magic books). The cumulative verdict is 19:20: "the word of the Lord continued to increase and prevail mightily." Passage serves book: the Ephesian panel is one more demonstration that the word cannot be stopped, co-opted, or out-powered — and that the Spirit-marked inclusion of these frontier believers is genuine.

---

### 2. Context

**Immediately before (18:18–22):** Paul's brief first touch on Ephesus, the vow at Cenchreae, the deposit of Priscilla and Aquila, the promise to return "if God wills," and the return to Antioch — closing the second journey. 18:23 opens the third journey with the travel-and-strengthening note.

**Immediately after (19:21–41):** Paul "resolved in the Spirit" to go to Jerusalem and then Rome ("I must also see Rome," 19:21) — the Rome trajectory is planted the moment the Asian mission is declared complete — followed by the riot of Demetrius and the silversmiths (19:23–41), the economic backlash of the very idol-toppling power 19:11–20 has displayed.

**Section/unit:** The whole runs 18:23–19:41 (Ephesus, third journey), of which our pericope is the first two-thirds, sealed by the growth-seam at 19:20. Chapter and paragraph divisions obscure that 18:24–28 (Apollos) and 19:1–7 (the twelve disciples) are *paired panels* on the same problem — an incomplete grasp of Jesus tied to "the baptism of John" — deliberately set side by side before Paul's main ministry begins.

**Positional Necessity Check** *(see Phase 0.6 above).*

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|--------|---------|---------|
| 18:23 | Travel hinge | Third journey begins; "strengthening all the disciples" — the pastoral keynote |
| 18:24–28 | Apollos at Ephesus/Achaia | A powerful teacher with a deficient baptism, completed by Priscilla and Aquila |
| 19:1–7 | The twelve "disciples" | A deficient discipleship completed by the Spirit through Paul |
| 19:8–10 | The two-year Ephesian ministry | Synagogue → hall of Tyrannus; "all Asia heard the word" (the seam's setup) |
| 19:11–16 | Extraordinary power vs. Sceva's sons | The name is not a magic formula; the counterfeit is overpowered |
| 19:17–20 | Fear, confession, book-burning, seam | The name extolled; costly renunciation; "the word… prevailed mightily" |

**Device:** linear narrative with a **thematic through-line** (adequacy/power of relation to Jesus) and a **summary bookend-seam** at 19:20 matching the book's other "the word grew" seams. The two opening panels (Apollos; the twelve) are a **near-doublet** — same deficiency (John's baptism), different remedy (human instruction; the Spirit). The progression is deliberate: **corrigible deficiency → exposed fraud → costly repentance**, each stage raising the stakes of what it means to be truly aligned with the name.

---

### 4. Linking Words

Narrative, so connectors are sparse; three carry weight.

- **19:9** "**But** (δέ) when some became stubborn…" — the adversative pivot from synagogue to hall of Tyrannus; the withdrawal is a response to hardening, the same Jew-then-wider pattern that recurs across Acts.
- **19:10** "…**so that** (ὥστε) all the residents of Asia heard the word of the Lord." — *result* clause. The two years in the hall of Tyrannus *result in* Asia-wide reach; ὥστε makes 19:10 the consequence of Paul's daily reasoning, not a separate claim.
- **19:20** "**So** (οὕτως) the word of the Lord continued to increase and prevail mightily." — the summary-adverb draws the seam's conclusion *from the immediately preceding scene*: it is the fear, confession, and book-burning of 19:17–19 that show *how* (οὕτως, "in this way") the word prevailed. The seam is earned by the episode, not tacked on. `[T]`

### 6. Narrator's Comment

Luke's authorial hand shows at several interpretive asides — the tool's "editorial verdict" and "inside information" types:

- **18:25** "though he knew **only** the baptism of John" — the narrator flags Apollos's deficiency before the reader could infer it; decisive for reading both panels.
- **18:27** "those who **through grace** had believed" — a theological verdict slipped into a travel note: the Achaian believers' faith is God's gift, not Apollos's achievement.
- **19:7** "There were **about twelve** men in all." — a narrator's count, not action; Luke wants the number noticed (see Tool 7).
- **19:11** "God was doing **extraordinary** (lit. *not the ordinary*) miracles" — the narrator explicitly marks these as *un*usual, guarding against normalising the handkerchiefs (see Pitfalls).
- **19:17** "the name of the Lord Jesus was **extolled**" and **19:20** the seam — the narrator names the outcome the events are for. These asides are the interpretive spine; the miracles and farce are *for* the magnifying of the name and the growth of the word.

### 7. Vocabulary

**ἡ ὁδός — "the Way" (18:25, 26; 19:9; cf. 19:23)** `[T]`
The passage rings the changes on ὁδός: Apollos knows "the way of **the Lord**" (18:25); Priscilla and Aquila expound "the way of **God**" more accurately (18:26); the hardened "speak evil of **the Way**" (19:9). By 19:9 ὁδός has become the movement's self-designation (cf. 9:2; 19:23; 24:14). The near-synonymous genitives ("of the Lord"/"of God") frame Apollos's completion as a deepening within one road, not a change of road. *High confidence.*

**τὸ ὄνομα τοῦ κυρίου Ἰησοῦ — "the name of the Lord Jesus" (19:5, 13, 17)** `[T]`
The organising term of 19:11–20. Baptism is *into* the name (19:5); the exorcists invoke *over* the name (19:13); the name is *extolled* (19:17). The name is the locus of saving power (cf. 4:12) — and precisely therefore not a formula available to outsiders. *High confidence.*

**κατὰ κράτος — "mightily" (19:20)** `[T]` for the phrase; register `[S: Pao]`/`[I]`
An adverbial phrase, "with strength / by force." κράτος appears in Luke–Acts only here and at Luke 1:51 ("he has shown **strength** with his arm"), a martial line in the Magnificat. The phrase κατὰ κράτος is standard Hellenistic *military* idiom ("by main force," of storming a position) and its only LXX occurrence, Judg. 4:13, is a battle muster. The seam thus reads as a conquest bulletin. *Moderate–high confidence on the military colouring; see Original Languages and Open Questions.*

**περίεργα — "magic arts" (19:19)** `[T]`
Literally "things overdone/meddlesome"; a recognised euphemism for magic (the "Ephesian letters," *Ephesia grammata*, were famous magical formulae). The word quietly indicts the practice as officious dabbling in the illicit. *High/moderate confidence.*

**Proper-noun inventory:** Galatia, Phrygia (18:23); Apollos, Alexandria, Ephesus (18:24); John [the Baptist] (18:25); Priscilla, Aquila (18:26); Achaia (18:27); Corinth (19:1); Asia (19:10); Tyrannus (19:9); Sceva (19:14). Numbers: "about twelve" (19:7); "three months" (19:8); "two years" (19:10); "seven sons" (19:14); "fifty thousand pieces of silver" (19:19). Most are routine itinerary/setting. Candidates for canonical/thematic freight — **twelve**, **Alexandria**, **Sceva "high priest"**, **fifty thousand** — are worked at Tool 11 and Historical Background.

**Paronomasia / catchword — ἰσχύω (19:16, 20):** the demoniac "**overpowered** (ἴσχυσεν) them" (19:16); the word "increase and **prevail** (ἴσχυεν)" (19:20). Same verb, four verses apart, framing the unit's close — cross-reported to Repetition and Tool 11 Move 4. *Moderate confidence on deliberate design; the aorist/imperfect pairing and the intervening material are consistent with intent but not decisive — flag for commentary.*

### 8. Translations

**19:2 — "Did you receive the Holy Spirit when you believed?"** The participle is aorist πιστεύσαντες. ESV/NASB "**when** you believed"; KJV "**since** ye believed." KJV's "since" implies a *temporal gap* between believing and Spirit-reception — the grammatical hook of two-stage-Spirit theology. ESV/NASB treat the aorist participle as coincident/antecedent with the main verb ("receive"), i.e., not implying a settled interval. The Greek does not by itself demand a gap; the *narrative* supplies the anomaly (these men lacked even knowledge of the Spirit). Prefer ESV/NASB; see Difficult Verses and Original Languages. `[T]`

**19:20 — "the word of the Lord."** ESV/NASB "of the **Lord**" (τοῦ κυρίου); KJV "of **God**" (τοῦ θεοῦ, reflecting the TR). The earliest witnesses read "of the Lord"; a minor variant with no doctrinal weight (see Textual Variants). ESV "**prevail mightily**" / NASB "**growing mightily and prevailing**" / KJV "So **mightily grew** the word of God, and prevailed" — all preserve the κατὰ κράτος adverb; NIV "grew in power and prevailed" slightly abstracts the martial force.

**Ancient versions check:** The exegetically weighty rendering where the moderns agree is κατὰ κράτος at 19:20 ("mightily"). This is a lexical/idiomatic matter rather than a translation-tradition split; the relevant control is the LXX's own usage of the phrase (Judg. 4:13, a military muster), which *supports* the martial reading and is cross-reported to Vocabulary/Original Languages rather than flagged as an ancient-vs-modern split. No `**Translation-tradition split:**` identified in this passage. *Moderate confidence on the LXX datum; verify against NETS/BDAG.*

### 9. Tone and Feel

**Dominant register: confident, advancing, culminating in awe.** The unit moves from brisk itinerary (18:23) through teaching and debate to a crescendo of the numinous: "**fear fell upon them all**" (19:17). The seam (19:20) is a trumpet-line.

Within it, a flash of **dark comedy**: the naked, wounded rout of Sceva's sons (19:16) is deliberately undignified — the would-be masters of spirits sprinting out of the house stripped and bleeding. Luke lets the counterfeit be ridiculous; the preacher should let the congregation feel the deflation before the reverence of 19:17 lands.

The book-burning (19:19) carries a costly, decisive weight — the accountancy detail ("fifty thousand pieces of silver") is there to make the reader feel the *price* of the break with the old powers.

**Soundtrack:** a steady advancing march that breaks into a brass fanfare at "the name of the Lord Jesus was extolled" — with a comic stumble in the middle where the exorcists flee.

### 10. Repetition

A dense, deliberately woven passage. Repeated features:

- **ὁδός "the Way / way"** — 18:25 (of the Lord), 18:26 (of God), 19:9 (the Way); cf. 19:23. The road-word threads the two panels to Paul's ministry.
- **βαπτίζω / βάπτισμα "baptize/baptism"** — 18:25 ("baptism of John"); 19:3 (×2: "into what… baptized"; "into John's baptism"); 19:4 ("John baptized… baptism of repentance"); 19:5 ("baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus"). The catchword carries the passage's argument about deficient vs. complete initiation.
- **ἀκριβῶς / ἀκριβέστερον "accurately / more accurately"** — 18:25 (Apollos "taught **accurately**"); 18:26 (expounded "**more accurately**"). The comparative is the hinge: even accurate teaching can be bettered.
- **πνεῦμα "spirit/Spirit"** — Apollos "fervent in **spirit**" (18:25); "**Holy Spirit**" (19:2 ×2, 6); "**evil spirit**" (19:15, 16). One word spanning zeal, the Spirit of God, and the demonic — the field over which the name rules.
- **τὸ ὄνομα "the name"** — 19:5, 13, 17 (see Vocabulary).
- **"both Jews and Greeks" / "all Asia"** — 19:10, 17 — the universal reach doubled.
- **ἰσχύω "overpower / prevail"** — 19:16, 20 (see Vocabulary; Tool 11 Move 4). `[T]`
- **"the word of the Lord"** — 19:10, 20 — bracketing the ministry and its outcome; the growing subject of the seam.

The cumulative drumbeat — Way, baptism, Spirit, name, word — *is* the theology: everything in Ephesus is measured against a full, Spirit-sealed, name-of-Jesus relation to the word of the Lord.

### 11. Quotation/Allusion

*Book-level live-source note (Move 2 prep):* Acts is saturated with the LXX, and the eschatological-Spirit texts (Joel 2:28–32 at 2:17–21; Isa 32:15 behind 1:8) are confirmed live sources for the book's frontier-Spirit scenes. **But this pericope contains no direct OT quotation and no high-confidence verbal OT allusion.** Independent candidate-finding from the passage's own distinctive vocabulary (Way, name, baptism, κατὰ κράτος) surfaces no quoted OT phrase; κατὰ κράτος echoes LXX *idiom* (Judg. 4:13) but is not a citation. This is itself a finding: **a major growth-seam (19:20) carries no OT citation** — evidence against the strong form of Pao's claim that OT quotations mark every Lukan turning point (already qualified in prior work; see Text-First Declaration).

**Moves 1–3:** *Not applicable — no external OT citation in the passage.*

**Move 4 — Internal echo check** *(required; this is where the passage's intertextual weight lies)*:

- **`Internal:` 16:6 → 19:10 — *answers §earlier* — high confidence.** "The Spirit… forbade them to speak the word in **Asia**" (16:6) is answered by "all the residents of **Asia** heard **the word** of the Lord" (19:10). Both name Asia and "the word"; the reversal is the passage's positional key (Phase 0.6). *What it adds:* the Asian mission is not a fresh initiative but the granting, in God's timing, of a mission earlier withheld — the same Spirit over the No and the Yes. Confirmed by Pao. `[T]` correspondence; `[I]`/`[S: Pao]` intentional reversal.

- **`Internal:` the frontier-Spirit sequence — Pentecost (2) / Samaria (8) / Cornelius (10) / Ephesus (19) — *answers §earlier* — moderate–high.** The laying on of Paul's hands (19:6) puts Paul in the apostolic role played at Samaria (8:17); the Spirit's descent marks a new frontier constituency (Gentile-territory "disciples") as full members. *What it adds:* Ephesus is the *last* of Luke's frontier-Spirit scenes; its oddity (deficient disciples completed) belongs to a salvation-historical series, not a repeatable order of salvation. Confirmed and sharpened by Pao (Paul takes the Jerusalem apostles' role; the point is continuity of the one mission). `[I]`/`[S: Pao]`.

- **`Internal:` the magician-confrontation pattern — Simon Magus (8:18–24) / Elymas (13:6–11) / Sceva's sons (19:13–16) — *answers §earlier* — moderate.** A recurring Acts scene: the gospel meets a manipulator of power and exposes it. Here the twist is that the counterfeiters invoke *the true name* and are still overpowered — sharpening the point that the name is not a technique. Pao independently links 19:13–16 back to Simon (8:18–23). `[I]`/`[S: Pao]`.

- **`Internal:` κατὰ κράτος / Luke 1:51 — *answers §earlier* — moderate.** The only other Lukan κράτος is the Magnificat's "he has shown **strength** (κράτος) with his arm, he has scattered the proud" (Luke 1:51). The seam's "mightily" may deliberately close the Ephesian conquest with the Magnificat's language of God's arm scattering the mighty — the proud powers of Ephesus among them. `[I]`; flag for verification.

- **`Internal:` planted for later — 19:21 ("I must also see Rome") and the 19:23–41 riot.** The completed Asian mission immediately plants the Rome trajectory (the book's *dei*) and triggers the idol-economy backlash. `Internal: [planted — watch for resolution]`. `[T]`.

*Addressee-differentiation:* the book does not open by naming distinct parties whose individual resolution this passage supplies; N/A. Opening material (Acts 1–2, the 1:8 programme and Pentecost) checked — the Spirit-and-witness programme is being *executed* here at its Asian frontier, not resolved.

### 12. Genre

Historical narrative (Hellenistic historiography with Septuagintal texture), containing a brief embedded dialogue (19:2–4) and two "power-encounter" episode-types (19:11–16; the implied idol-conflict). Reading rules: these events *happened* and are theologically loaded (use Narrator's Comment and Copycat); the miracles are "signs" pointing to the name, not techniques; the two-panel doublet (Apollos / the twelve) is Luke's selective arrangement carrying the thesis, not a chronicle. Brief — genre is not contested here.

### 13. Copycat

Walking the actors:

- **Apollos (18:24–28):** *normative pattern* in his teachability — a gifted man accepts private correction and grows; *descriptive* in the specifics (not re-baptised — Luke implies no deficiency in an already-believing preacher of the word). The underlying virtue (humble corrigibility) is imitable; "get re-instructed by a tentmaking couple" is not a rule.
- **Priscilla and Aquila (18:26):** *normative pattern* — gentle, private, accurate correction ("took him aside"). Imitable in kind.
- **The twelve disciples (19:1–7):** *unique/transitional event.* A salvation-historical frontier moment (John's-baptism believers brought into the Pentecost reality), **not** a template for a normal two-stage Christian experience. We are not them: post-Pentecost, the Spirit is given to all who believe and are baptised in Jesus' name.
- **The itinerant Jewish exorcists / Sceva's sons (19:13–16):** *negative example / warning* — invoking the name without relationship. Explicitly cautionary.
- **The Ephesian believers burning their books (19:18–19):** *normative underlying virtue* — decisive, costly, public renunciation of former powers as the fruit of repentance; the specifics (which objects, what value) are descriptive. Imitable as *thoroughness of repentance*, not as a programme of book-burning.
- **Paul (18:23; 19:8–10):** *instrument, not hero.* His daily reasoning models persevering proclamation, but the agent of the miracles is "**God**… by the hands of Paul" (19:11), and the subject that grows is "**the word**" (19:20).

**Manner-of-the-act check (19:6, 11–12):** the Spirit comes *and* the healings occur by divine initiative, "God… doing" (19:11); the posture implied is *receiving what God gives through the word and the name*, not mastering a technique — the exact opposite of the exorcists' attempted method.

---

### 14. Bible Timeline

This side of the cross and Pentecost, on the Church-age frontier where "we are." Two timeline notes matter. (1) The **John's-baptism believers** (Apollos; the twelve) are living artefacts of the *overlap* between the old era (John, the last prophet of the promise) and the new (the poured-out Spirit) — a hinge that has now closed: after Pentecost, John's baptism is insufficient, because the One to whom John pointed has come and given the Spirit. (2) The **frontier-Spirit scenes** are foundational and unrepeatable in their salvation-historical function (Jerusalem, Samaria, the Gentiles, the far Asian disciples) — the reader today receives the *result* (the Spirit given to all believers) not the *method* (apostolic frontier ratification). Reading either as a present template collapses the timeline.

---

### 15. Who Am I?

NT narrative, so identification is by function, not by inserting oneself as hero:

- **The Ephesian believers** (who hear the word, fear, confess, and renounce) are the *recipients* whose situation maps most closely to ours — those to whom the word comes with power in a world of rival spiritualities.
- **Apollos and the twelve** illustrate the *incompletely-instructed believer* — a recognisable "us" in need of fuller grounding, not a Christ-type.
- **Sceva's sons** are a *warning* — the religious outsider who treats the name as leverage.
- **Paul** is neither hero-to-copy nor Christ-type but *instrument*; the true protagonist is the **exalted Christ** whose name is extolled and whose word prevails. The "Moses-is-me"/"Paul-is-me" temptation (do Paul's miracles, wield Paul's power) is precisely what the Sceva episode forbids.

### 16. So What?

**Author's intended response:** confidence that the word of the Lord — carrying the name of Jesus and sealed by the Spirit — is supreme over deficient religion, counterfeit power, and costly idolatry, and therefore advances unstoppably; and, consequently, wholehearted alignment with that word rather than with any rival power.

**Worldview:** the risen Christ reigns; his word is not one force among many but the conquering word ("mightily," 19:20); spiritual power is not a technique to be mastered but a Lord to be submitted to (baptism *into the name*). The Christian lives in a contested world where the powers are real (evil spirits, magic) but already outmatched.

**Behaviour — stop/start, with motive:** *Stop* treating faith as a formula or clinging to rival "powers"; the Ephesians' book-burning models decisive, costly renunciation — motivated not by legalism but by the name of the Lord Jesus being magnified (19:17). *Start* trusting the sufficiency of Christ's name and the Spirit given in him (against the anxiety "is my grasp of Jesus enough?"), and persevering in proclamation confident of the word's advance through opposition.

**Four audiences:**
- *For me:* Where am I trusting technique, experience, or a rival "power" rather than the Lord whose name saves? What would my costly, public book-burning be?
- *For a Christian friend:* the believer anxious that their faith is "deficient" — like Apollos, like the twelve — needs the assurance that Christ completes what is lacking, and marks his people with his Spirit.
- *For the church:* a congregation in a culture of counterfeit spiritualities can take confidence that the name cannot be co-opted and the word will prevail; and can practise thorough, corporate repentance.
- *For an unbeliever:* the name of Jesus is not a lucky charm to invoke (Sceva) but a Lord to be met; the word that toppled Ephesus's magic still confronts every rival allegiance.

**Prayer:** Lord Jesus, whose name is above every name, let your word increase and prevail mightily in us and through us; burn out of us every rival power we have clung to, complete what is lacking in our knowledge of you by your Spirit, and give us confidence that your word conquers still.

## Extensions

### Original Language Observations

**κατὰ κράτος (19:20)** — adverbial "with strength / by main force." κράτος occurs in Luke–Acts only here and Luke 1:51 (Magnificat). The phrase is standard Hellenistic *military* idiom and its sole LXX instance (Judg. 4:13) is a battle muster; hence the seam reads as a conquest report. *Moderate–high confidence on the martial register; verify BDAG / Barrett / Bruce.* `[T]` phrase; `[S: Pao]`/`[I]` register.

**ἴσχυσεν (19:16) / ἴσχυεν (19:20)** — same verb ἰσχύω ("be strong, prevail, overpower"). 19:16 aorist, of the demoniac overpowering the exorcists; 19:20 imperfect, of the word prevailing. The repetition across the unit's close is a plausible deliberate catchword (the counterfeit is overpowered; the word overpowers). *Moderate confidence; flag.*

**πιστεύσαντες (19:2)** — aorist participle, "having believed / when you believed." Aorist participles are typically coincident or antecedent to the main verb; the participle does *not* lexically encode a temporal gap. The KJV "since ye believed" over-reads a settled interval. *High confidence that the grammar does not require a gap; the anomaly is narrative, not grammatical. Verify Wallace, Greek Grammar Beyond the Basics.* `[T]`

**τοῦ κυρίου (19:20) — syntactical position.** The clause is οὕτως κατὰ κράτος τοῦ κυρίου ὁ λόγος ηὔξανεν καὶ ἴσχυεν. τοῦ κυρίου may construe with ὁ λόγος ("the word **of the Lord** grew… mightily," most translations) or, given its immediate juxtaposition, with κράτος ("by the **might of the Lord** the word grew…"). The sense-difference is modest but real (whose might?). *Unresolved — see Open Questions; this was flagged in prior project work and remains open.* `[T]` for the ambiguity.

**Recommended verification:** BDAG on κατὰ κράτος and περίεργα; Wallace on the aorist participle at 19:2; Barrett/Bruce (ICC/NICNT) on the 19:20 syntax and the ἰσχύω catchword.

### Textual Variants

**19:9 — "the hall of Tyrannus."** The Western text (Codex Bezae, D) adds "**from the fifth hour to the tenth**" (≈ 11 a.m.–4 p.m.), the midday/siesta hours when a lecture hall would be free. A plausible early tradition and a vivid historical detail, but not in the earliest Alexandrian witnesses; modern translations omit it. *Preaching implication:* usable as colour ("Paul may have taught in the hall's off-hours") **if flagged** as a Western addition, not read as original text. *Moderate confidence.*

**19:20 — "of the Lord" (τοῦ κυρίου) vs "of God" (τοῦ θεοῦ).** Earliest witnesses read "of the Lord"; the TR/KJV "of God" is secondary. No doctrinal weight. *High confidence; not worth pulpit mention.*

Otherwise no significant variants affect the preaching of this passage.

### Historical and Cultural Background

**Setting.**
- **Time:** mid-50s AD, Paul's third journey; roughly two-and-a-quarter years in Ephesus (19:8, 10; cf. 20:31 "three years").
- **Location:** **Ephesus**, capital of the Roman province of Asia, a major port and commercial hub, home of the temple of Artemis (one of the seven wonders) and a theatre seating c. 24,000 — and famous as a centre of magic (the *Ephesia grammata*, "Ephesian letters," were renowned magical formulae).
- **Political/religious mood:** a wealthy, cultured, cultically saturated city where the Artemis cult and the magic trade were economically significant (the 19:23–41 riot follows for exactly this reason).

**What the original audience knew, that shapes the reading:**
- **Alexandria** (Apollos's origin) was the great centre of Hellenistic Judaism, home of the LXX and of Philo's allegorical learning — so "eloquent/learned man, competent in the Scriptures" evokes a formidable Diaspora-Jewish education.
- **John's-baptism believers** existing at Ephesus c. 25 years after John shows how far, and how incompletely, the Baptist's renewal movement had radiated through the Diaspora synagogues — Apollos is the same phenomenon.
- **Itinerant Jewish exorcists** were widely respected precisely because Jews were thought to have access to the powerful divine Name; invoking a new potent name ("the Jesus whom Paul proclaims") is exactly what such practitioners would try. The audience would find the *attempt* unsurprising and the *backfire* devastating.
- **"A Jewish high priest named Sceva":** no high priest of that name is known; the grand title is most likely self-styled or a charlatan's advertisement — an irony the informed reader catches. *Flag as historically uncertain.*
- **"Fifty thousand pieces of silver"** (likely drachmas, each ≈ a day's wage) is an enormous sum — the book-burning is a staggering economic sacrifice, not a token gesture.
- **The hall of Tyrannus** was a lecture hall/school; Paul's daily public reasoning there fits the conventions of a travelling teacher renting or borrowing a venue.

**What this changes:** in the magic-capital of Asia, the burned books and the routed exorcists are not incidental colour but a *public verdict*: the name of Jesus out-powers the very speciality of the city. That is why "fear fell upon all" and why Luke can seal the panel with a conquest-word (19:20).

*Confidence:* Ephesus's magic reputation and the drachma valuation are well attested (*moderate–high*); the Sceva-title irony and the Western "fifth-to-tenth hour" are *moderate/uncertain* — verify with Keener's *IVP Bible Background Commentary* or the *Dictionary of Paul and His Letters*.

### Original Audience Reception

**Canonical audience.** NT canonical reader, post-Pentecost, holding Luke's Gospel (esp. the Magnificat's "strength/κράτος," Luke 1:51; John's baptism, Luke 3) and the earlier Acts frontier-Spirit scenes. They read Ephesus as the far edge of the 1:8 programme reaching Asia.

**The first hearers.** Theophilus and a mixed Jewish-Gentile church seeking *assurance* (Luke 1:4). Their pressing concern: is this movement genuine, God-authorised, and safe to belong to, given synagogue opposition and a pagan-magical environment?

**Where the passage fits their world.**
- They knew Ephesus's magical reputation; the public defeat of magic would land as a decisive demonstration, not a quaint anecdote.
- Jewish hearers would register "**about twelve** men" as an evocation of Israel's twelve (cf. 1:15–26) — the frontier believers constituted as full members of the restored people (so Pao).
- They knew respected Jewish exorcists; the Sceva farce would be both funny and reassuring — the name cannot be co-opted.

**Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances.**
- *Shocking:* a possessed man physically routing seven exorcists, stripping and wounding them (19:16) — the counterfeit publicly humiliated.
- *Surprising:* believers already ("through grace had believed") yet needing to be brought into the Spirit and re-baptised — that a real grasp of Jesus could still be incomplete.
- *Comforting:* the name of Jesus is supreme over the powers; the word prevails despite hardened opposition (19:9).
- *Disturbing/searching:* the call to renounce lucrative, cherished "powers" at real cost (19:19) — and the exposure of religion-as-technique.

**What we bring that they didn't:** individualism (reading 19:1–7 as a private "second blessing" recipe rather than a corporate frontier moment); a disenchanted worldview that treats "magic" and "evil spirits" as pre-modern décor rather than the real rival powers the text names; and a therapeutic instinct that asks "what Spirit-experience can I get?" rather than "is the name of the Lord being magnified?"

### Biblical-Theological Themes

**1. The Word of God that grows (Acts's own spine).** `[T]` *high confidence.*
- *Where it sits:* 19:20 is the fifth of the six growth-seams (6:7; 9:31; 12:24; 16:5; 19:20; 28:31), and one of the three "the word grew" forms (6:7; 12:24; 19:20 — so Pao).
- *Before:* the word has grown through persecution (8:4), across the Gentile threshold (12:24), through Europe (16:5).
- *After:* the final seam, Rome, "without hindrance" (28:31).
- *Payoff:* the passage is not "Paul's Ephesian success" but one more panel in Luke's argument that *the word* is the growing, conquering subject — sealed here with a conquest-verb (κατὰ κράτος).

**2. The Holy Spirit and the reconstitution of God's people.** `[I]` *moderate–high.*
- *Where it sits:* Ephesus is the last frontier-Spirit scene (Pentecost/Samaria/Cornelius/Ephesus); the "about twelve" mark the frontier believers as full members of the restored Israel.
- *Before:* Joel 2 / Isa 32:15 promise (poured out at 2:17; each frontier since).
- *After:* the Spirit indwelling all believers as the norm (the epistles).
- *Payoff:* guards against reading 19:1–7 as a private ordo salutis; the theme is *inclusion and continuity*, not a technique for Spirit-reception.

**3. The Name — salvation and power in no other.** `[T]` *high.*
- *Where it sits:* the name of the Lord Jesus is the locus of power (19:5, 13, 17), continuous with 4:12 ("no other name").
- *Payoff:* the name is not a formula (Sceva) but a Lord submitted to (baptism into the name) — the passage's ethical and doctrinal centre.

**Confluence:** these three are in **climactic convergence** at 19:20 — *the word* (theme 1) *carrying the name* (theme 3) *and sealed by the Spirit* (theme 2) prevails mightily. The seam names the single reality all three describe; that convergence is the preaching gold.

### Christological Reading

**Type of connection:** NT passage; the exalted Christ is named and active — this is *present Christ-reality*, not OT typology. No typological claim is being pressed.

**How the passage centres on Christ:** "the Lord" (κύριος) throughout is Jesus; "the word of the Lord" is the word of/about the risen Christ; the name extolled (19:17) is his. The passage is a demonstration of the reign of the ascended Christ, who acts by his Spirit through his witnesses (the book's programmatic claim, 1:1–2, 8) — healing "by the hands of Paul" but as *God's* doing (19:11), and marking frontier believers with *his* Spirit.

**Moralism check:**
- *The "be like X" temptation:* "be a bold missionary like Paul"; "do power-encounters like Paul"; "burn your books like the Ephesians." Each displaces Christ as agent.
- *The gospel grounding:* the agent is the exalted Christ whose name saves and whose word conquers; human actors are instruments and recipients.
- *Christ as hero, not example:* keep 19:17 the climax — *the name* is extolled, not Paul; keep 19:20's subject *the word*, not the church's technique.

*Confidence: high* that the passage is Christ-as-reigning-Lord rather than typological.

### Difficult / Contested Verses

**19:2 — "Did you receive the Holy Spirit when you believed?"**
- *Category:* doctrinal (Pentecostal/charismatic second-blessing; subsequence of Spirit-baptism).
- *The difficulty:* the verse is a classic proof-text for a Spirit-reception distinct from and subsequent to conversion.
- *Rhetorical function in context:* Paul's diagnostic question exposes a *deficiency* (these men had "only… John's baptism," had "not even heard" of the Spirit). The verse functions to identify an anomaly to be corrected, not to establish a normal two-stage pattern.
- *Interpretive options:* (a) a normative subsequence of Spirit-baptism (Pentecostal); (b) a unique frontier/transitional case of pre-Pentecost "disciples" being brought into the new-covenant reality (majority Reformed/evangelical).
- *Honest exegesis:* the grammar (aorist πιστεύσαντες) does not demand a temporal gap; the *narrative* marks these men as sub-Christian in knowledge — option (b) fits Luke's frontier-Spirit design. *Moderate–high confidence.*
- *Suggested handling:* address briefly and clearly — name the frontier-transition reading and why the passage is not a template; don't let it hijack the sermon.

**19:1–7 — apparent re-baptism.**
- *Category:* doctrinal (baptism). The one NT case where people are (re-)baptised — from John's baptism into "the name of the Lord Jesus."
- *Honest exegesis:* the move is John-era → Christ-era, a redemptive-historical transition, not a warrant for re-baptising Christians. *Moderate–high.*
- *Handling:* acknowledge in passing; resist building a general re-baptism doctrine on it.

**19:6 — tongues and prophecy at Spirit-reception.**
- *Category:* doctrinal (cessationism/continuationism). *Handling:* note as a frontier-authentication sign (as at Pentecost/Cornelius); don't make it either a required badge of Spirit-reception or a proof that such signs have ceased.

**19:11–12 — healing handkerchiefs/aprons.**
- *Category:* pastoral/weaponised (relic veneration; prosperity "anointed cloths").
- *Honest exegesis:* Luke explicitly calls these "**extraordinary** [not ordinary] miracles" (19:11) — flagging them as *unusual*, tied to the apostolic frontier, not normative technique. *High confidence on the narrator's flag.*
- *Handling:* name Luke's own qualifier; do not license contemporary relic/cloth practices.

**19:19 — book-burning.**
- *Category:* pastoral/ethical (misused as a mandate for censorship/biblioclasm).
- *Honest exegesis:* the point is *costly repentant renunciation of the occult by its own practitioners*, not a programme against literature. *Handling:* preach the thoroughness of repentance, not book-bonfires.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: 19:2 as a second-blessing / two-stage-Spirit template
- **What it looks like:** "Like these disciples, you can believe and only later receive the Spirit — have you had your second blessing?"
- **Why it's wrong:** Luke marks these men as *deficient* (John's baptism only; ignorant of the Spirit); the aorist participle does not encode a gap; this is a frontier-transition, one of a salvation-historical series (Pentecost/Samaria/Cornelius/Ephesus), not an ordo salutis.
- **The corrective:** preach the *completion* of an incomplete grasp of Jesus and the marking of frontier believers as full members — not a technique for getting more Spirit.

### Pitfall: 19:11–12 as a method (relics, anointed cloths, prosperity power)
- **What it looks like:** "Send off your prayer-cloth for your healing, as they did with Paul's handkerchiefs."
- **Why it's wrong:** Luke himself calls these "**extraordinary** [not ordinary] miracles" — his narrator's flag marks them as *un*usual and apostolic-frontier, not a repeatable programme.
- **The corrective:** honour Luke's qualifier; the point is God's authenticating power magnifying the name (19:17), not a transferable technique.

### Pitfall: making Paul (or the exorcism) the model for spiritual-warfare technique
- **What it looks like:** a how-to for casting out demons in Jesus' name, drawn from 19:13–16.
- **Why it's wrong:** the passage is precisely a warning against name-as-formula; the counterfeiters *use the true name* and are still routed. The agent is God (19:11); the point is submission to the Lord, not mastery of a method.
- **The corrective:** preach the supremacy and non-manipulability of the name; the Sceva farce is the anti-model.

### Pitfall: 19:20 as generic church-growth optimism
- **What it looks like:** "God will grow our church too" — detached from the specific conquest that produces the seam.
- **Why it's wrong:** οὕτως ("so, in this way") ties the growth to the *particular* triumph over the powers (19:17–19); κατὰ κράτος frames it as conquest through opposition, not smooth expansion.
- **The corrective:** ground the seam in the costly, contested victory of the word over rival powers — advance *through* opposition, not around it.

### Pitfall: 19:19 as a mandate for censorship / book-burning
- **What it looks like:** using the bonfire to justify purging libraries or policing reading.
- **Why it's wrong:** it is the *practitioners themselves* renouncing *their own occult materials* at great personal cost — repentance, not cultural policing.
- **The corrective:** preach thorough, costly renunciation of one's own rival allegiances.

1. **19:20 syntax of τοῦ κυρίου.** Does it construe with ὁ λόγος ("the word **of the Lord** grew… mightily," most translations) or with κράτος ("by the **might of the Lord** the word grew…")? The sense-difference is modest but real. *Unresolved — flagged in prior project work; verify against Barrett (ICC), Bruce (NICNT), and the NA28 apparatus.*

3. **κατὰ κράτος military register and the Luke 1:51 echo.** The martial idiom (Judg. 4:13 LXX) and the sole other Lukan κράτος (Magnificat) support a conquest reading, but the deliberate Magnificat allusion is an inference. *Moderate; verify BDAG and a Lukan-intertextuality treatment.*
