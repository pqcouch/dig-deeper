Claim Audit: Acts (3)

Passages audited: Acts 6:8–7:60 (A20–A26, A28, A29, A31); 7:42–50 with 15:13–18 (A27); 10:1–33 with Luke 1 (A30); 27:34 (A32); 17:24 (A31). Date: 25 November 2026 Purpose: The thirteen candidates raised by claim audit 2 (A20–A32). Most are the “better claims next door” that audit 2 offered in place of claims it reframed. They are audited here before they enter the 6:8–7:60 dig’s v1.1 patch or the overview. Method:

  • Two blind auditors in turn. Auditor A took the eight single contacts; auditor B the five design and field claims, each with chance baselines.
  • Neither could see audit 2, the Logos round, the digs, the overview or the queue.
  • The main session reproduced the decisive results. Shape claims were reproduced by ordered phrase. Logos round 3: The library answers gathered this morning are brought in at the end of each verdict as history of interpretation. The auditors did not see them.

Positional frame (Phase 0.6)

The frame is that of audit 2. Stephen’s speech answers the charge of 6:13–14 (“this holy place and the Law”) before the gospel leaves Jerusalem. James’ speech at 15:13–21 settles the Gentile question. Every claim below concerns how the speech handles land, place, house and dwelling, or how Luke shapes reported revelation. A claim earns its place only if it bears on those questions.

Claims Audited

A20: Exodus 2:22 / 18:3 behind Acts 7:6

Claim: Acts 7:6 replaces Gen 15:13’s ἐν γῇ οὐκ ἰδίᾳ with πάροικον ἐν γῇ ἀλλοτρίᾳ. πάροικος + γῇ ἀλλοτρίᾳ stands only at Exod 2:22 and 18:3 in the Greek Old Testament and only at Acts 7:6 in the New. Stephen returns to that moment at 7:29.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability Strong Exodus is quoted throughout the speech
Volume Strong The pair is exclusive to Exod 2:22 and 18:3 in Swete and in Rahlfs 1979; the Hebrew (WLC) shows the same pair at the same two verses only
Recurrence Strong 7:29 blends Exod 2:15 (ἐν γῇ Μαδιάμ), 2:22 (πάροικος) and 18:3 (two sons)
Thematic coherence Strong Sojourning without land is the speech’s opening theme
Historical plausibility Strong —
History of interpretation Weak Logos round 3 found no commentator making the link; Childs (OTL) 35 is adjacent only
Satisfaction Strong —

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning: The New Testament “only” holds for the noun but not the family. Heb 11:9 has παρῴκησεν εἰς γῆν … ὡς ἀλλοτρίαν (reproduced). Stephen’s habit of splicing in wording from other texts in 7:5–7 (Deut 2:5 at 7:5; Exod 3:12 at 7:7) makes a third splice at 7:6 probable. Moderate–high, unchanged. It is a finding from the text that scholarship has apparently not recorded.

Book-overview action required: Enter it at moderate–high, with the note that the history of interpretation is not found.

A21: Ps 132 and Isa 66:1 as a three-noun Hebrew contact

Claim: מָקוֹם, מְנוּחָה and הֲדֹם רַגְלַיִם stand within one chapter only in Ps 132 and Isa 66 (WLC).

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning: The data hold (reproduced in audit 2). The rarity argument does not.

  • The two rare nouns, rest and footstool, also stand together in 1 Chr 28. Only the commonest of the three, מָקוֹם (379 verses), excludes it.
  • Baseline (auditor A, three settings): Isa 66 has between 4 and 14 partner chapters joined to it by an exclusive three-noun set of comparable rarity. Ps 132 is its weakest such partner at every setting.
  • The contact matters because Acts 7:46 quotes Ps 132:5, not because it is rare.
  • In Greek the cleaner datum is τόπος + κατάπαυσις + πούς, which stand together only in Ps 131 and Isa 66 (Swete).

Moderate, down from moderate–high.

Hays: availability strong; volume possible; recurrence strong; coherence strong; history not found; satisfaction possible.

Book-overview action required: Use the Greek datum, not the Hebrew rarity.

A22: 1 Chronicles 28:2 as the target of Isaiah 66:1

Claim: Rest + footstool, and rest + house + build, stand in one verse only at Isa 66:1 and 1 Chr 28:2 (WLC). 1 Chr 28:2 is David’s “house of rest”.

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning:

  • The Hebrew verse-level data are exact. Widen the window, though, and Ps 132:7–8 and 1 Chr 22:8–9 come in.
  • In Greek the contact vanishes. 1 Chr 28:2 has οἶκον ἀναπαύσεως and στάσιν ποδῶν where Isa 66:1 has κατάπαυσις and ὑποπόδιον (Swete, reproduced; Rahlfs 1979 agrees).
  • Acts 7:46–50 shows no sign of knowing 1 Chr 28.
  • “Target” reverses the likely direction of dependence, since Chronicles is the later book.

Low–moderate. Logos round 3 found no history.

Book-overview action required: Do not enter it.

A23: 1 Kings 8:27 (= 2 Chr 6:18) as the nearer partner of 7:48

Claim: 1 Kgs 8:27 (“will God indeed dwell…?”) is the nearer partner of 7:48 than Ps 132:14. 1 Kgs 8:14–30 has the same four-step sequence as Acts 7:46–50: David’s wish, Solomon’s building, the dwelling question, transcendence. Logos round 3 raised this to moderate–high on Walton’s report of Sylva.

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning:

  • Wording. 1 Kgs 8:27 shares only κατοικέω with Acts 7:48.
  • Ps 131:14 is closer. κατοικέω stands with the noun κατάπαυσις in Swete only at Ps 131:14. Acts 7:48–49 sets exactly those two words side by side. (Deut 12:10 has κατοικέω with the verb καταπαύω, which is not the same contact; reproduced.)
  • Isa 57:15 (ὁ ὕψιστος … κατοικῶν) shares two of 7:48’s three content words. ὕψιστος with κατοικ- stands in Swete at Isa 57:15 (and once in the Psalms of Solomon); reproduced.
  • The sequence. The order David, then Solomon, then the dwelling question, then transcendence is largely fixed by the history itself, so its recurrence is weak evidence of dependence.
  • 2 Chr 6 carries the sequence better than 1 Kgs 8. At 6:41–42 Solomon’s prayer also quotes Ps 132:8–10, using κατάπαυσις.

Reconciling with Logos round 3. Walton’s report of Sylva is history of interpretation for a sequence parallel, and it stands as such. But the corpus shows that the sequence is what the story dictates. Its fullest Old Testament form is 2 Chr 6, which joins the dedication prayer to Ps 132. The verbal partner of 7:48 is Ps 131:14 (Greek), with Isa 57:15.

The rise to moderate–high is withdrawn. As stated, 1 Kgs 8:27 as the nearer partner is low–moderate. The reframed claim is moderate: Acts 7:46–50 against the Solomonic dedication tradition (1 Kgs 8 // 2 Chr 6, with 2 Chr 6:41–42 quoting Ps 132).

Book-overview action required: Enter the reframed claim at moderate [S: Walton, citing Sylva], naming Ps 131:14 and Isa 57:15 as the verbal partners.

A24: Deuteronomy 33:3 at Acts 7:38

Claim: Greek Deut 33:3 ἐδέξατο ἀπὸ τῶν λόγων αὐτοῦ sits beside 7:38’s ἐδέξατο λόγια, next to the angels of 33:2.

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning: δέχομαι + λόγος is a common idiom: nine Swete verses and nine in the New Testament, four of them in Luke–Acts. What carries the claim is that Deut 33:2 is the only Swete verse with angels at Sinai (audit 2). Auditor A adds a better partner for one phrase. Deut 18:16 (“the day of the assembly” at Horeb) follows the Deut 18:15 quotation at Acts 7:37, so it is the natural source of ἐν τῇ ἐκκλησίᾳ at 7:38. Moderate, unchanged.

Book-overview action required: Add Deut 18:16 beside Deut 33:2–3 at 7:37–38.

A28: Luke 12:14 echoing Exodus 2:14

Claim: Jesus’ τίς με κατέστησεν κριτὴν ἢ μεριστὴν ἐφ᾽ ὑμᾶς; echoes Exod 2:14 (quoted at Acts 7:27, 35).

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning:

  • The “who appointed you/me judge?” question is rare in the Greek Old Testament: τίς σε κατέστησεν stands only at Exod 2:14 (Swete, reproduced).
  • 2 Sam 15:4 is a close rival: Absalom’s τίς με καταστήσει κριτήν has the first person and κριτήν, but a future verb.
  • The NA28 apparatus records that A, K, N, Q, W, Γ, Δ, Θ, Ψ and the Byzantine text read δικαστὴν ἢ μεριστήν at Luke 12:14. The scribes heard Exod 2:14.
  • Framing. Jesus asks the question of himself in order to decline the role. It is not thrown at him, as it was at Moses.

Moderate, unchanged.

Book-overview action required: None (it belongs to Luke). Note it for any Luke work.

A31: χειροποίητος + κατοικέω (Acts 7:48; 17:24)

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning:

  • The pair stands in the New Testament only at Acts 7:48 and 17:24 (SBLGNT, reproduced), and never together in the Greek Old Testament.
  • In Swete χειροποίητος (fifteen verses, reproduced) is always used of idols or cult objects. At Isa 16:12 it is used of Moab’s sanctuary, rendering מִקְדָּשׁ.
  • 17:24–25 is best explained as Luke reusing 7:48 together with Isa 42:5. διδοὺς πνοήν stands only at Isa 42:5 in Swete (reproduced).

Data moderate–high (up from moderate). The explanation of 17:24 is moderate.

Book-overview action required: Enter the 7:48 / 17:24 pair at moderate–high, with Isa 16:12 and Isa 42:5.

A32: Daniel (Theodotion) 3:94 at Acts 27:34

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning:

  • 27:34 is closest to Luke 21:18, inside the Old Testament oath formula (1 Sam 14:45; 2 Sam 14:11; 1 Kgs 1:52; the Byzantine text reads πεσεῖται).
  • Dan Th 3:94 shares only “the hair of the head”, which stands elsewhere too, and the Old Greek lacks even that.
  • Scanned against every Swete chapter, Dan 3 is not a stable partner of Acts 28:1–6 (median rank 35).

Low, down from low–moderate. Do not use it.

A25: Luke’s Joel order (“wonders and signs”)

Claim: At 2:19 Luke adds σημεῖα to Joel, setting “wonders … signs”. He keeps that order for Jesus, the apostles, Stephen and Moses, against the Septuagint’s “signs and wonders”. Audit 2 rated this moderate–high.

Baseline (auditor B, reproduced in part).

  • The order is rare. Outside Acts the New Testament has “signs and wonders” seven times out of seven. Swete has “signs and wonders” in 29 verses and “wonders and signs” once, at Wis 10:16, which is about Moses (reproduced). The Hebrew puts “signs” first in all 18 cases.
  • The sequence in Acts is not distinctive. Five of the six “wonders and signs” verses in the Greek Bible are in Acts 2–7. But in verse order Acts runs W W W S S W W S S (2:19/22/43; 4:30; 5:12; 6:8; 7:36; 14:3; 15:12), which interleaves rather than switching once. The chance that all five “wonders and signs” fall at or before 7:36 is 0.167; the chance of four runs or fewer is 0.262.
  • No variant. The NA28 apparatus records no change of order at any of the nine places.

Corrections.

  • The apostles are given both orders: 2:43 “wonders and signs”, 5:12 “signs and wonders”. The order does not follow who works the signs.
  • The four “signs and wonders” verses form a chain that starts with the church’s prayer: 4:30 → 5:12 → 14:3 → 15:12.
  • Audit 2 is corrected. Its statement that the wonders-first order “belongs to Acts 2–7 and stops there” is true of where the verses fall, but it implied a single switch that the text does not show.

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning: That Luke uses an unusual order is certain ([T]). That he means something by it is unproven. The best reframing has two strands: Joel’s order at Pentecost, with Moses precedented by Wis 10:16; and the prayer’s “signs and wonders” at 4:30, answered in its own words at 5:12, 14:3 and 15:12. Moderate, down from moderate–high.

Logos round 3: Longenecker (source and redaction), Rengstorf (typology) and Remus (no significance). History is present and divided.

Book-overview action required: Enter “Luke’s use of the rare order ‘wonders and signs’ (2:19–7:36), with the prayer chain 4:30 → 15:12” at moderate. This replaces audit 2’s moderate–high entry.

A26: τόπος as Stephen’s temple word

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning:

  • Count. The speech itself has τόπος three times (7:7, 33, 49). The five occurrences counted in the claim include 6:13–14.
  • Two are quotations. 7:33 comes word for word from the Septuagint (Exod 3:5) and 7:49 from Isa 66:1. The change at 7:7 may render the Greek ὧδε of Gen 15:14.
  • Baseline. Of 32 nouns of similar frequency in Acts, 72% cluster five or more times inside some 68-verse stretch, and τόπος ranks 21st of the 23 that do. Twelve such nouns occur three or more times in the speech; τόπος is not among the seven that occur four or more times.
  • Not a gap of its own. ἱερόν is absent from 5:42 through to 19:27, so its absence here is part of a thirteen-chapter gap.
  • οἶκος is the word the speech uses of the temple itself (7:47, 49; and 7:46 in NA28). The auditor’s count of five (SBLGNT) or six (NA28) occurrences of οἶκος in the speech includes three that are not temple uses (7:10, 20, 42); corrected 25 November.

Low–moderate. This also removes the replacement that audit 2 proposed for A8.

Book-overview action required: Neither the temple-word absence nor the τόπος thread enters. If a temple-word row is wanted, it is οἶκος of the temple at 7:47 and 7:49 (with 7:46 in NA28’s reading). The auditor’s count of five or six οἶκος includes 7:10, 20 and 42, which are not temple uses; corrected 25 November.

A27: The tent sequence of 7:43–47 answered at 15:16

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning:

  • Block test. At every rarity setting, 7:42–50 shares only σκηνή with James’ speech. It ties for first place with 55–93 other passages at the strict settings and ranks 17th to 107th at the others.
  • The introductions. The two καθὼς γέγραπται introductions are not identical: “the prophets” comes before the formula at 15:15 and after it at 7:42. Their landing on the two Amos citations would be about 1 in 300–435 by chance. But the plural “prophets” names the scroll of the Twelve (compare Habakkuk at 13:40), which explains it.
  • The decisive datum. At 15:16 Luke writes ἀνοικοδομήσω … καὶ ἀνορθώσω αὐτήν. Amos 9:11 (Swete) has ἀναστήσω and ἀνοικοδομήσω, but not ἀνορθόω (reproduced). In Swete, ἀνορθόω belongs to Nathan’s oracle about David’s throne and house: 2 Sam 7:13, 16; 1 Chr 17:12, 14, 24; 22:10. That is six of its nineteen verses (reproduced).
  • The Hebrew. סֻכָּה is never a sanctuary word, and Amos 9:11’s breaches and ruins are political.
  • So Luke’s own wording points “the tent of David” towards the dynasty (Glenny’s reading, reported by Tabb), not the sanctuary (Bauckham’s).

As stated, low–moderate. Reframed as a shared Davidic background (2 Sam 7; Ps 131), moderate.

Consequence for A0. Audit 2 restated the Amos pair as “the tent sequence of 7:43–47 answered at 15:16”. That restatement now fails. The Amos pair falls back to low–moderate as a design. What it still has is a scroll-level fact: Luke cites the Twelve twice by the same formula.

Book-overview action required:

  • Do not enter the tent-sequence row.
  • Restate A0 as “two citations from the Twelve introduced by the same formula”, low–moderate.
  • Enter ἀνορθώσω → Nathan’s oracle (new claim A42) at moderate–high (Rahlfs 1979 and Swete’s apparatus checked; see the addendum).

A29: The heart-cut response series (2:37; 5:33; 7:54)

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning:

  • Corrections. 7:54 has Ἀκούοντες (present), not ἀκούσαντες. 5:33 has no καρδία (reproduced).
  • Baseline across Acts’ 23 speech endings:
    • a participle of ἀκούω introduces the reaction in 9 (11 if ἤκουον is counted);
    • a visceral reaction appears in 4 (7 on a broad count);
    • καρδία appears in 2.
  • The “having heard” opening is therefore common.
  • What is distinctive is the rare verbs. διαπρίομαι stands in the New Testament only at 5:33 and 7:54, both of the Sanhedrin. κατανύσσομαι stands only at 2:37.
  • “Hearing, then pierced” is already a Septuagint idiom (Gen 34:7; Ps 108:16; Dan Th 10:9).

Moderate, unchanged, resting on the rare verbs.

Book-overview action required: Optional row at moderate.

A30: A Lukan angelophany idiom

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning:

  • Scoring. Only 5 of the 13 angelophanies in Luke–Acts show the pattern (4–7 of 8 features). The other eight score 0–2.
  • Baseline. Old Testament angelophanies in Swete score as high (Gen 16, 21; Judg 6, 13; Dan 8–10; Tob 5).
  • εἰσακούω does stand in Luke–Acts only at Luke 1:13 and Acts 10:31 (reproduced). But the wording differs: δέησις in Luke, προσευχή in Acts, and no μὴ φοβοῦ in Acts 10.
  • Closer models. Dan 10:12 (Old Greek: Μὴ φοβοῦ, Δανιήλ … εἰσηκούσθη) and Sir 51:11 are closer to Luke 1:13. Cornelius’ prayer, alms and memorial “before God” match Tob 12:8, 12 and Tob 3:16 (Sinaiticus text) more closely than Luke 1.

Low–moderate. A16 (27:23–25 cast in the Luke 1 idiom, moderate–high in audit 2) stands on its own exclusive data. It is not a case of a general “Lukan idiom”.

Book-overview action required: Do not enter a general angelophany-idiom row. Keep A16 as a specific Luke 1 ↔︎ 27:23–25 row.

Method notes

  • A design claim proposed by an audit must be baselined before it is rated above moderate, including when a later Logos round finds history for it. A23 shows why: the history (Sylva’s sequence) was real, but the corpus showed that the sequence is dictated by the story.
  • Corpus notes from auditor A.
    • There is no Rahlfs Theodotion file for Daniel in the snapshot, although the README says one was added; Daniel-LXX is the Old Greek.
    • The Swete Kings files are labelled 1Ki.

  • Pattern hygiene. One hand-typed search pattern carried a Latin letter among the Greek and returned a silent zero; it was caught by its positive control.

Addendum: Amos 9:11 in Rahlfs 1979 and in Swete’s apparatus (A27, A42)

Checked in Logos the same day, through the Exegetical Guide at Amos 9:11.

  • Rahlfs 1979 (Septuagint with Logos Morphology) reads: ἀναστήσω τὴν σκηνὴν Δαυιδ τὴν πεπτωκυῖαν καὶ ἀνοικοδομήσω τὰ πεπτωκότα αὐτῆς καὶ τὰ κατεσκαμμένα αὐτῆς ἀναστήσω καὶ ἀνοικοδομήσω αὐτὴν καθὼς αἱ ἡμέραι τοῦ αἰῶνος. This is the same as Swete, and it has no ἀνορθόω.
  • Swete’s apparatus records only three variants at 9:11, none touching the verbs:
    • καταπεπτ- for πεπτωκυῖαν (Q, apparently first hand);
    • αὐτῶν for the first αὐτῆς (Q margin);
    • κατεστραμμένα for κατεσκαμμένα (A, corrector b; Q, first hand).
    No Greek witness it collates reads ἀνορθώσω.

So ἀνορθώσω at Acts 15:16 is not in the Greek of Amos in either edition, or in the manuscripts Swete reports. It is Luke’s wording, or that of his source, and its only Septuagint home is Nathan’s oracle and its parallels.
