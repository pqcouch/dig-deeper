Claim Audit: Acts (2)

Passages audited: Acts 6:8–7:60 (A1–A12); 7:42–43 with 15:16–18 (A0); 27:1–28:18 (A13–A19) Date: 25 November 2026 Purpose: The whole Acts claim-audit queue, which had been overdue since 19 November. Twenty claims (A0–A19) come from the overview sweep, the 6:8–7:60 dig and the 27:1–28:18 dig. Four of the five Headline Findings of the 6:8–7:60 dig, and the first three of the 27:1–28:18 dig, rest on them. Method:

  • Auditors. Two blind auditors worked one after the other. Auditor A took the nine single contacts (wording, rarity, rivals, Greek and Hebrew). Auditor B took the eleven design and field claims (block tests, chance baselines, rank stability).
  • Blindness. Neither auditor could open the digs, the overview, the sweep, the queue, the earlier chapter-15 audit, or the other auditor’s work.
  • Corpus. SBLGNT and NA28 for the New Testament; Swete and Rahlfs 1979 for the Greek Old Testament where exported; WLC and BHS for the Hebrew.
  • Reproduction. The main session reproduced the decisive results. Shape claims (“only”, “identical”) were reproduced by ordered phrase, not by lemma.
  • History of interpretation. Not assessed in this audit. It is the business of the Logos round that follows, and the questions are listed at the end. Editions: Every count names its edition. Swete has no lemma index, so Swete counts are accent-stripped surface counts and should be read as floors.

Positional frame (Phase 0.6)

6:8–7:60.

  • What the preceding movement set up: the church’s growth is threatened from inside (6:1–7). Its leaders are appointed (6:3). A charge is laid: “this holy place and the Law” (6:13–14).
  • Why the passage stands here: to answer that charge before the gospel leaves Jerusalem (8:1–4).
  • The claims audited here (A1–A12) all concern how the speech answers the charge from Israel’s own history: land, place, prophets, rejected deliverers, angels. That is the right frame in which to judge them. A claim that does not bear on the charge is decorative, however true its words.

7:42–43 with 15:16–18 (A0). The Amos pair spans the Hellenist crisis and the Jerusalem council. The positional question is whether 15:16 answers 7:43–47, the tent and house sequence of Stephen’s speech. Auditor B’s reframing turns on this.

27:1–28:18.

  • What the preceding movement set up: the Lord’s “must” (23:11), the appeal (25:12) and the chain of verdicts (23:29; 25:25; 26:31–32).
  • Why the passage stands here: to carry the witness through the sea and to let Paul sum up his case (28:17–18).
  • The claims audited (A13–A19) concern how the voyage and the summary echo earlier Luke–Acts. They are judged against that frame.

Claims Audited

A0: The Amos pair as design (7:42–43; 15:16–18)

Source: Overview v2.2–2.5, rated moderate [S: sweep]; the oldest item in the queue. Claim:

  • Acts cites Amos twice, and both citations follow the Greek against the Hebrew.
  • Both stand at structural hinges of the book.
  • They are linked by σκηνή (“tent”), which stands in Acts only at 7:43, 7:44 and 15:16 (SBLGNT).
  • The design is the “tent of Moloch” against the “tent of David” rebuilt.
Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability Strong Both are cited texts
Volume Possible One shared noun. The consonants of “booth/tent” (סכת) stand at both Amos 5:26 and 9:11 in the WLC, so the link is latent in the Hebrew too
Recurrence Strong The same prophet is cited twice. Both citations, and only these two in Acts, are introduced by καθὼς γέγραπται + “the prophets” (7:42; 15:15; SBLGNT, ordered phrase)
Thematic coherence Strong Tent and house is the subject of 7:43–50
Historical plausibility Strong —
History of interpretation Not assessed Logos round
Satisfaction Possible Strong only when restated (below)

Baselines (auditor B, reproduced in part).

  • The σκηνή distribution is at chance. Of 176 Acts lemmas that occur exactly three times, 36% fall in only two chapters, and 4.5% fall in two chapters 7–9 apart. σκηνή is one of eight such lemmas.
  • The citation pair is rare. Of about fifty pairs of different citations from one Old Testament book, only the Amos pair shares a noun as rare as this (three Acts verses or fewer). That puts it first at both settings tested, at about 2% and 4%.
  • “Greek against Hebrew” does not distinguish the pair: the Habakkuk citation at 13:41 does the same.

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning: The bare tent word is chance-level. What is not chance-level is the pairing of the two citations (shared rare noun, shared and exclusive introductory formula) and the sequence in Stephen’s speech:

  1. Moloch’s tent (7:43);
  2. the tent of witness (7:44);
  3. David’s request for a σκήνωμα (7:46);
  4. Solomon’s house (7:47).

David’s tent rebuilt (15:16) answers that sequence, not a two-point antithesis. Moderate.

Book-overview action required: Restate the Echo Table row as “the tent sequence of 7:43–47 answered at 15:16” and keep it at moderate. Drop “follows the Greek” as a distinguishing mark. Add the καθὼς γέγραπται formula as the second link.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability Strong Deuteronomy is a live source in Acts 7
Volume Strong οὐδὲ βῆμα ποδός with a negated δίδωμι in both verses. The three-word string is once in Swete (Deut 2:5), once in Rahlfs 1979, and once in the New Testament (Acts 7:5, SBLGNT and NA28). The Hebrew מִדְרַךְ is a hapax
Recurrence Possible Deuteronomy is cited elsewhere in the speech (7:37)
Thematic coherence Strong Land promised and not given
Historical plausibility Strong —
History of interpretation Not assessed Logos round
Satisfaction Possible The “Edom” overtone is interpretation; the rest of 7:5 comes from Gen 17:8 / 48:4

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning: The contact is tighter than the claim stated, because the negated δίδωμι is shared as well. The wording is high. The “Edom’s land” reading is an overtone the phrase permits, not a point the speech makes, and 7:5 is a splice with Gen 17:8 / 48:4. Moderate–high overall.

Book-overview action required: Enter it in the intertextual map at moderate–high, wording high, noting the splice.

A3: Exodus 3:12 spliced onto Genesis 15:13–14 at 7:7

Source: 6:8–7:60 dig; splice and substitution data, intention moderate. Claim: 7:7’s καὶ λατρεύσουσίν μοι ἐν τῷ τόπῳ τούτῳ takes Exod 3:12 and turns “this mountain” into “this place”.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability Strong —
Volume Strong The splice is adapted, not verbatim: λατρεύσουσίν μοι against λατρεύσετε τῷ θεῷ. “Mountain” stands in Swete, Rahlfs 1979 and the WLC, and no Old Testament text reads “serve … in this place”
Recurrence Strong Exod 3 is quoted at 7:30–34
Thematic coherence Strong The charge of 6:13
Historical plausibility Strong —
History of interpretation Not assessed Logos round
Satisfaction Strong —

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning: Two things narrow the “ambiguity”. First, Exod 3:5 already calls the mountain ὁ τόπος, and Acts 7:33 quotes it. Second, the Greek of Gen 15:14 adds ὧδε (“here”), which the Hebrew lacks. So “this place” folds Horeb, the land and (pressed by 6:13–14) the temple into one word, using texts Stephen actually uses. The same verb, עָבַד, in Gen 15:14 and Exod 3:12 is the Hebrew hinge of the splice. Splice and substitution high; the reading moderate.

Book-overview action required: Add Exod 3:5 and the Greek ὧδε of Gen 15:14 to the row.

A6: The 2:22 triad given to Stephen at 6:8

Source: 6:8–7:60 dig (Headline 4); distribution high, design moderate–high. Claim: δύναμις + τέρας + σημεῖον stand together in two Acts verses, 2:22 (Jesus) and 6:8 (Stephen), and wonders come before signs at 6:8 and 7:36.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability Strong —
Volume Possible A stock triad. At 6:8 δύναμις is Stephen’s endowment (“full of grace and power”), not a deed
Recurrence Strong The word order recurs (below)
Thematic coherence Strong —
Historical plausibility Strong —
History of interpretation Not assessed Logos round
Satisfaction Strong for the order —

Baseline (reproduced).

  • The order. In Acts, the adjacent phrase “wonders and signs” stands at 2:22, 2:43, 6:8 and 7:36; “signs and wonders” stands at 4:30, 5:12, 14:3 and 15:12 (SBLGNT, ordered phrase). The wonders-first order belongs to Acts 2–7 and stops there.
  • The Greek Old Testament. In Swete the adjacent phrase is “signs and wonders” eleven times to one (my reproduction). Auditor B’s looser count gives 29 to 2. Moses’ miracles always come signs first, for example Deut 34:11.
  • Luke’s Joel. Luke sets the order at 2:19 by adding σημεῖα to Joel.

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning: The triad as design is low–moderate: it is a formula, and at 6:8 one member is a quality. The real signal is the order. Luke’s Joel at 2:19 sets “wonders … signs”, and he keeps it for Jesus (2:22), the apostles (2:43), Stephen (6:8) and Moses (7:36), against the Septuagint’s near-invariable order. Moderate–high for the order; moderate overall.

Book-overview action required: Replace “the triad given to Stephen” with “Luke’s Joel order (2:19) carried through Jesus, the apostles, Stephen and Moses”. Moderate–high.

A12: The διαπρίομαι contrast (5:33; 7:54)

Source: 6:8–7:60 dig; data high, reading moderate–high.

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning: διαπρίομαι stands in two New Testament verses, Acts 5:33 and 7:54, both of the same council (SBLGNT, reproduced). The rarity is less striking than it looks: of 79 New Testament two-occurrence words found only in Acts, 17 fall in chapters three or fewer apart. But the contrast is structural. At 5:33 Gamaliel stays the council’s hand; at 7:54 no one does. The contrast becomes a series with 2:37 (κατενύγησαν τὴν καρδίαν): repentance, restrained fury, murder. Data high; the 5:33 / 7:54 contrast moderate–high; the Gamaliel-to-Saul overlay (22:3; 7:58) moderate. Textual note: at 5:33 the majority text, with א and D, reads ἐβουλεύοντο for ἐβούλοντο.

Book-overview action required: Optional row, “heart-cut response series 2:37 / 5:33 / 7:54”, moderate.

Volume Possible–Strong See corrections
Recurrence Strong Jesus–Paul trial parallels run through Luke–Acts
Thematic coherence Strong —
Historical plausibility Strong —
History of interpretation Not assessed Logos priority 1

Corrections (reproduced).

  • “A verb of wishing + ἀπολῦσαι in two verses” holds only in strict adjacency. βούλομαι — the verb Paul uses at 28:18 — stands with ἀπολύω at Mark 15:15, John 18:39 and Matt 1:19 as well as 28:18. θέλω with ἀπολύω stands in the Pilate scenes of all four Gospels (Matt 27:15, 17, 21; Mark 15:9; Luke 23:20).
  • Luke 23:22 has αἴτιον, not αἰτία. The verbatim partner of μηδεμίαν αἰτίαν θανάτου is Acts 13:28.
  • θάνατος with ἀπολύω in one verse stands only at Luke 23:22 and Acts 28:18 (SBLGNT, reproduced). This supports the claim.

Ranking (auditor B). As single-verse partners of 28:18, Acts 13:28 ranks first on rarity-weighting and Luke 23:22 sixteenth. As two-verse blocks, Acts 25 leads at every setting, and Luke 23:13–14 is the best Gospel block (with ἀνακρίνω, as at 28:18).

Reasoning: That Paul’s summary is shaped as a Jesus-like trial is moderate–high. The parallel is pervasive in Luke–Acts, and the formula’s verbatim partner is Acts 13:28, of Jesus. That Luke 23:20–22 specifically is the source is moderate: Luke 23:13–14 and 23:22 are both candidates, and the release motif belongs to the whole passion tradition.

  • Add 28:18 to the verdict list as Paul’s retrospective.

A15: The 15:1 formula at 27:31

Source: 27:1–28:18 dig, Headline 3; low-to-moderate.

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance (the data); the contrast unproven.

Reasoning: ἐὰν μή … οὐ δύνασθε σωθῆναι is New Testament-exclusive to Acts 15:1 and 27:31 (SBLGNT, reproduced). But the word order differs:

  • 15:1: Ἐὰν μὴ περιτμηθῆτε … οὐ δύνασθε σωθῆναι

The dig’s “formula” was found by a search that allowed either order. Two-verse exclusive pairs are common: there are fourteen within Acts 6–7 alone. And the voyage’s own salvation register explains σωθῆναι without a back-reference. Low–moderate, unchanged; possible, not shown.

Method notes

  • Corpus trap — candidate README amendment. The SBLGNT index files ἱερόν under ἱερός. It belongs beside the δεῖ / δέω note.
  • Corpus coverage (corrected 25 November 2026). Rahlfs 1979, BHS and BHS-apparatus exports exist for the whole Old Testament, including the Twelve, which are in a sub-folder of the Latter Prophets (04-The-Twelve). The snapshot made for this audit’s auditors went one folder too shallow and missed them. That is why both auditors, and the first version of this note, treated Amos, Joel, Jonah and Habakkuk as covered only by Swete and the WLC. The readings concerned have since been checked against the Rahlfs 1979 export of Amos and in Logos (claim audit 3, addendum), and none changes.
  • Ordered-phrase searches must keep the order. The 27:1–28:18 dig’s “formula” at 15:1 / 27:31 was found by a search that accepted either order. The rule in the claim-audit format already requires order for shape words; the dig gate should require it too.
  • False friends. A shared phrase must be tested for shared sense before it is called a contact (μετὰ σοῦ; εὔχομαι).
