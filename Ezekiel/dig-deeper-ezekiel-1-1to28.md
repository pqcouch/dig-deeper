## The Passage

¹ In the thirtieth year, in the fourth month, on the fifth day of the month, as I was among the exiles by the Chebar canal, the heavens were opened, and I saw visions of God. ² On the fifth day of the month (it was the fifth year of the exile of King Jehoiachin), ³ the word of the LORD came to Ezekiel the priest, the son of Buzi, in the land of the Chaldeans by the Chebar canal, and the hand of the LORD was upon him there.

⁴ As I looked, behold, a stormy wind came out of the north, and a great cloud, with brightness around it, and fire flashing forth continually, and in the midst of the fire, as it were gleaming metal. ⁵ And from the midst of it came the likeness of four living creatures. And this was their appearance: they had a human likeness, ⁶ but each had four faces, and each of them had four wings. ⁷ Their legs were straight, and the soles of their feet were like the sole of a calf's foot. And they sparkled like burnished bronze. ⁸ Under their wings on their four sides they had human hands. And the four had their faces and their wings thus: ⁹ their wings touched one another. Each one of them went straight forward, without turning as they went. ¹⁰ As for the likeness of their faces, each had a human face. The four had the face of a lion on the right side, the four had the face of an ox on the left side, and the four had the face of an eagle. ¹¹ Such were their faces. And their wings were spread out above. Each creature had two wings, each of which touched the wing of another, while two covered their bodies. ¹² And each went straight forward. Wherever the spirit would go, they went, without turning as they went. ¹³ As for the likeness of the living creatures, their appearance was like burning coals of fire, like the appearance of torches moving to and fro among the living creatures. And the fire was bright, and out of the fire went forth lightning. ¹⁴ And the living creatures darted to and fro, like the appearance of a flash of lightning.

¹⁵ Now as I looked at the living creatures, I saw a wheel on the earth beside the living creatures, one for each of the four of them. ¹⁶ As for the appearance of the wheels and their construction: their appearance was like the gleaming of beryl. And the four had the same likeness, their appearance and construction being as it were a wheel within a wheel. ¹⁷ When they went, they went in any of their four directions without turning as they went. ¹⁸ And their rims were tall and awesome, and the rims of all four were full of eyes all around. ¹⁹ And when the living creatures went, the wheels went beside them; and when the living creatures rose from the earth, the wheels rose. ²⁰ Wherever the spirit wanted to go, they went, and the wheels rose along with them, for the spirit of the living creatures was in the wheels. ²¹ When those went, these went; and when those stood, these stood; and when those rose from the earth, the wheels rose along with them, for the spirit of the living creatures was in the wheels.

²² Over the heads of the living creatures there was the likeness of an expanse, shining like awe-inspiring crystal, spread out above their heads. ²³ And under the expanse their wings were stretched out straight, one toward another. And each creature had two wings covering its body. ²⁴ And when they went, I heard the sound of their wings like the sound of many waters, like the sound of the Almighty, a sound of tumult like the sound of an army. When they stood still, they let down their wings. ²⁵ And there came a voice from above the expanse over their heads. When they stood still, they let down their wings.

²⁶ And above the expanse over their heads there was the likeness of a throne, in appearance like sapphire; and seated above the likeness of a throne was a likeness with a human appearance. ²⁷ And upward from what had the appearance of his waist I saw as it were gleaming metal, like the appearance of fire enclosed all around. And downward from what had the appearance of his waist I saw as it were the appearance of fire, and there was brightness around him. ²⁸ Like the appearance of the bow that is in the cloud on the day of rain, so was the appearance of the brightness all around. Such was the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the LORD. And when I saw it, I fell on my face, and I heard the voice of one speaking.

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims that emerged across multiple tools:

1. **The vision's controlling point is a *mobile* throne: the glory is enthroned on a wheeled chariot that goes "wherever the spirit would go" (1:12, 20) — the LORD is free, not bound to the Jerusalem temple.** — Surfaced by: Structure (the wheels and their omnidirectional motion), Repetition ("wherever the spirit would go, they went", 1:12//20; "without turning", 1:9, 12, 17), Vocabulary (*ophanim*; *ruach* as animating spirit), Move 4 (the throne departs at 10–11, returns at 43). This single datum makes the whole book possible. *High.*

2. **Ezekiel never claims to see God; the relentless "likeness / appearance / like" language guards the transcendence — he sees "the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the LORD" (1:28), three removes from direct sight.** — Surfaced by: Vocabulary (*demûṯ*, *marʾeh*, *ke-*), Repetition (dozens of hedging terms), Tone (reverent groping at the edge of language). *High.*

3. **The vision is a Sinai-and-temple theophany relocated to unclean Babylon: the sapphire throne (1:26) echoes Sinai's "pavement of sapphire" (Exod 24:10), the fire-and-cloud echoes the glory that filled tabernacle and temple (Exod 40:34–35; 1 Kgs 8) — God's glory appears *in exile*.** — Surfaced by: Quotation/Allusion (Triad), Historical background (the Chebar, an unclean land), Positional Necessity Check, Vocabulary. *High.*

4. **A figure of human form is enthroned as the glory (1:26), and the whole throne is haloed by the covenant bow of mercy (1:28) — a judgment-throne encircled by mercy, on a trajectory the NT completes when the four living creatures reappear around the throne of the Lamb (Rev 4:6–8).** — Surfaced by: Christological reading, Quotation/Allusion (Gen 9 bow; Rev 4 reuse), Tone, Vocabulary (*ʾādām* enthroned, over against *ben-ʾādām* addressed at 2:1). *High on the Rev 4 link; the enthroned-figure-as-Christophany reading is moderate — see Christological Reading.*

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book level** (from the overview, not re-derived): Ezekiel exists to vindicate the holiness of the LORD in the fall of Jerusalem and then, for the sake of his name, to restore — so that all "know that I am the LORD." The glory departing and returning is its spine.

**Passage level:** ch. 1 has a double purpose the tools converge on. First, it **authorises the prophet** — a genuine sight of the enthroned glory stands behind every word he will speak; the call-vision credentials the messenger (cf. Isa 6; 1 Kgs 22:19). `[T]` Second, it **reorients the exiles' theology of presence** — before a syllable of judgment, the reader is overwhelmed with a glory that is enthroned, sovereign, *and mobile*, present by a Babylonian canal. The chapter is not information about angelology; it is *doing* something — flattening the reader into the dust (1:28) so that everything downstream is heard from the ground. `[T]/[I]` The passage serves the book's purpose by planting the glory that the book's whole architecture will move (depart, 8–11; return, 43).

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement:** This is the book's opening — nothing precedes it *within* the book. But the Positional Necessity Check still fires on the **canonical horizon** (the passage's real "preceding movement"): the reader of the Nevi'im arrives knowing the glory filled the tabernacle (Exod 40:34–35) and Solomon's temple (1 Kgs 8:10–11), and knowing the exile has happened (597). That canonical movement has raised an acute, unspoken question: *if the LORD's glory dwelt in Zion and Zion's king is now deported, has the LORD been defeated, or left behind in a ruined land?* `[I]`

**Necessity answer:** ch. 1 exists *here*, first, because the entire book is an answer to that question and cannot begin until the throne is introduced as **enthroned and mobile**. Only a throne that is shown to travel (ch. 1) can later be shown to *leave* the temple (8–11) and *return* (43). Position is argument: the vision is the theological foundation the book is built on, and 43:3 explicitly reaches back to "the vision that I had seen… by the Chebar canal." Remove ch. 1 and the glory-departure of 8–11 has no enthroned subject and the glory-return of 43 has nothing to return. `[T]`

**Implication:** the vision is not a stand-alone spectacle; it is the book's first move in a single sustained action. Preach it as the opening of an arc, holding 8–11 and 43 in view.

**General context.** Immediately after (2:1–3:27) comes the commission — "son of man, stand on your feet" (2:1) — so the throne-vision flows directly into the sending of the prophet. Structurally, Ezekiel's commissioning (2:1–3:15) is framed by glory-visions (ch. 1 and the second glory-appearance in the valley, 3:22–23) — a "sandwich" that makes the point his words come from the enthroned Lord of glory. The chapter is frequently read as a curiosity divorced from this function (see Pitfalls); the context corrects it — the vision is a *call*.

### 3. Structure

Stripping the verse numbers, the chapter builds **upward** in five stages, the eye led from the storm at the edge to the enthroned figure at the summit:

| Verses | Section | Function |
|--------|---------|----------|
| 1:1–3 | Superscription | Date, place, prophet; the frame (note the voice-shift, below) |
| 1:4–14 | The four living creatures | The storm-cloud opens; the throne-bearers described |
| 1:15–21 | The wheels | The chariot's mobility — "the spirit… in the wheels" |
| 1:22–25 | The expanse and the sound | The crystal platform; the roar like many waters; a voice |
| 1:26–28 | The throne and the glory | The sapphire throne, the human-form figure, the bow; prostration |

**Device: a deliberate bottom-to-top ascent that delays the climax.** `[T]` The description withholds the throne until the very end, moving storm → creatures → wheels → expanse → throne → the One enthroned → the encircling bow, so the reader arrives at the human figure and the glory only at 1:26–28, and the natural response (falling on the face) closes the unit. The **fourfold** patterning (four creatures, four faces, four wings, four wheels) gives the vision a structure of totality/universality — the throne faces all four directions at once. The whole is bracketed by fire (1:4, 27) and by the "likeness/appearance" register.

### 4. Linking Words

Largely **N/A for logical argument** — this is descriptive vision, not reasoned discourse, so the connective tissue is **spatial and sequential**, not causal. Worth noting rather than tracing exhaustively: the piled *waw* ("and… and… and") of 1:4–14 enacts the overwhelming accumulation (the syntax itself is part of the tone); the spatial markers do the structural work ("Under their wings…", 1:8; "Over the heads…", 1:22; "And above the expanse…", 1:26), climbing the vision; and the one load-bearing temporal-consecutive clause closes it — "*And when* I saw it, I fell on my face" (1:28), the vision issuing in worship. `[T]`

### 5. Parallels

Not classic bicolon parallelism (this is elevated vision-prose), but two poetic patterns carry weight. First, a **climactic/staircase** tricolon in the sound-description (1:24): "like the sound of many waters / like the sound of the Almighty / … like the sound of an army" — three similes mounting to convey the overwhelming roar. `[T]` Second, a **structural parallelism of number** — the relentless fourfoldness (creatures/faces/wings/wheels) functions as a merism of totality (the four directions, the whole created order represented in the throne-bearers: man, lion, ox, eagle — the pinnacle of persons, wild beasts, domestic beasts, and birds). `[I], moderate` — the four-living-things-as-creation-summary reading is a reasonable inference, not stated by the text; flag.

### 6. Narrator's Comment

Not third-person narrative, so the tool mostly **N/A** — but two authorial features function like narrator's comment and are interpretive gold. (a) The **voice-shift** in the superscription: 1:1 is first person ("*I* saw"), 1:2–3 abruptly third person ("the word of the LORD came to Ezekiel the priest… *the hand of the LORD was upon him*"), then 1:4 resumes first person. The third-person frame reads like an editorial certification of the vision — dating it, naming the prophet and his priestly lineage, locating it — an authenticating aside around a first-person report. `[T]` (b) The seer's own **decoder-label** at 1:28, "Such was the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the LORD," tells the reader how to construe everything that preceded — the equivalent of a narrator's "this is what it was." `[T]`

### 7. Vocabulary

**Proper-noun inventory (Tool 7c).** Names/places/numbers present: *the thirtieth year*, *fourth month / fifth day*, *the exile of King Jehoiachin* (→ fifth year), *the Chebar canal*, *the land of the Chaldeans*, *Ezekiel*, *Buzi*, *the LORD*; the recurring number *four*. Triage: Chaldeans/Chebar = setting (one line: Babylonia, a canal near Nippur — freight handled under Historical background). Two carry canonical freight and get the full check:

- **"the fifth year of the exile of King Jehoiachin" (1:2).** The vision is dated not by Nebuchadnezzar's regnal year (the imperial calendar) but by *the exile of the legitimate Davidic king*. Ezekiel keeps **covenant time** in Babylon — reckoning history by the deposed son of David, not the reigning pagan emperor. A quiet but real theological datum: the Davidic line still sets the clock. *Moderate–high; cross-reported to Historical background.* `[T]/[I]`
- **"Ezekiel" (*yeḥezqēʾl*, "God strengthens/hardens").** The name is apt for the commission that immediately follows — "I have made your face hard… like emery harder than flint" (3:8–9). A name-wordplay in prospect. *Moderate.* `[I]`
- The number **four** — not merely a count but, with the fourfold faces/wings/wheels and the four directions, a marker of **totality/universality** (cf. "the four winds", 37:9; "four corners of the land", 7:2). *Moderate.* `[I]`

**Big/loaded terms:**

- **glory** (*kāvôd*, 1:28) — the "weight," the visible manifestation of God's presence; the governing noun of the vision and the book. *High.*
- **likeness / appearance** (*demûṯ / marʾeh*) and the particle **"like/as" (*ke-*)** — the analogical hedging vocabulary, saturating the chapter (see Repetition). The vision is described almost entirely in the language of resemblance, never identity. *High.*
- **the living creatures** (*ḥayyôṯ*, 1:5) — un-named here; the book itself identifies them as **cherubim** (*kᵉrûvîm*) at 10:15, 20. Reading ch. 1 with ch. 10, these are the throne-guardians of Eden and the mercy seat. *High* (the identification is the book's own).
- **expanse** (*rāqîaʿ*, 1:22) — the Genesis 1:6 word for the firmament; its presence beneath the throne evokes the created cosmos, with the LORD enthroned above it as Creator. *High* that it is the same word; *moderate* on the deliberate creation-allusion (see Tool 11).
- **gleaming metal** (*ḥašmal*, 1:4, 27) — a rare word (only Ezek 1:4, 27; 8:2), meaning uncertain (amber? electrum? a glowing alloy?); LXX *ēlektron*. Flag as lexically uncertain; nothing load-bearing hangs on the precise metal. *Uncertain — consider a lexicon (HALOT).*
- **spirit / wind** (*rûaḥ*) — both the "stormy wind" that opens the vision (1:4) and the animating "spirit… in the wheels" (1:12, 20–21); a single word threading the vision's power and mobility. *High.*

**Paronomasia / taxonomy checks.** *ruach* recurs ≥3× — the repetition is the signal, but here it is thematic (mobility) rather than a cross-root sound-pun; no paronomasia claimed. No Levitical taxonomy is activated in the vision itself (the priestly clean/unclean grid is Ezekiel's frame elsewhere, not here). `[T]`

### 8. Translations

ESV/NASB/KJV agree on the substance throughout; the divergences are lexical, not doctrinal:

- **1:4, 27 *ḥašmal*:** ESV/NASB "gleaming/glowing metal," KJV "amber," NIV "glowing metal." Genuine lexical uncertainty; note it, preach nothing on it. `[unverified — lexical]`
- **1:22 *rāqîaʿ*:** ESV "expanse," KJV "firmament." The KJV "firmament" audibly preserves the Genesis 1:6 link (same English word as KJV Gen 1) and the ancient solid-dome image; ESV "expanse" is defensible but mutes the creation-echo. Worth flagging where the Genesis allusion is preached. `[T]`
- **1:26 "sapphire":** more likely **lapis lazuli** than the modern gemstone; either way the point is the deep-blue heaven-stone under the throne, echoing Exod 24:10.

**Ancient versions check.** Trigger met: ESV/NASB/KJV agree on "expanse/firmament" (1:22) and the rendering carries exegetical weight (the Genesis-1 allusion). **Translation-tradition note:** LXX *stereōma* and Vulgate *firmamentum* both render *rāqîaʿ* with the "solid vault/firmament" image (as they do at Gen 1:6), preserving the verbal and conceptual link to creation that the modern "expanse" softens — mild support for reading the creation-allusion as live (cross-reported to Tool 11). On *ḥašmal*, the LXX *ēlektron* ("electrum/amber") is as uncertain as the modern guesses — the ancient tradition does not resolve it. *LXX recall for these terms: moderate; verify in NETS if load-bearing.*

**Pulpit edition note:** Pulpit text matches report default (ESV Anglicised); no divergence bears on any finding.

### 9. Tone and Feel

**Awe, dread, and the sublime** — the register of Isaiah 6 and Revelation 4–5. `[T]` The devices: sensory overload (storm-wind, fire flashing "continually", gleaming metal, burning coals, torches, lightning darting "to and fro"); the roar "like the sound of many waters… like the sound of an army" (1:24); the "awe-inspiring crystal" expanse (1:22); and the constant straining of language ("as it were… like the appearance of… the likeness of"). The pacing accelerates through the fire and motion of 1:4–21, then arrests at the still, silent throne (1:25, "when they stood still, they let down their wings") before the enthroned figure. **Soundtrack:** rolling thunder and rushing water building to a roar — then sudden silence — then a single voice. The unit's emotional architecture ends where the sermon's should: face to the ground (1:28). Do not preach this vision briskly or cerebrally; its first work is terror-and-wonder.

### 10. Repetition

- **"likeness / appearance / like" (*demûṯ / marʾeh / ke-*):** the dominant repetition — well over a dozen occurrences — the hedging drumbeat that *is* the doctrine of transcendence (see Headline 2). `[T]`
- **"four":** creatures, faces, wings, wheels, directions — the fourfold totality. `[T]`
- **"wherever the spirit would go, they went" (1:12, echoed 1:20):** the load-bearing repetition — mobility and freedom; the throne is not fixed. `[T]`
- **"without turning as they went" (1:9, 12, 17):** the creatures and wheels never pivot — they move any direction instantly, all faces forward at once (omnidirectional presence). `[T]`
- **"the spirit of the living creatures was in the wheels" (1:20, 21):** stated twice in two verses — the animating unity of the chariot. `[T]`
- **fire / brightness (*ʾēš / nōgah*):** 1:4, 13, 27–28 — frames and fills the vision.

Counting is not the point; the repetitions cluster on two ideas — **God's unspeakable transcendence** (the "likeness" drumbeat) and **God's untrammelled mobility** (the "wherever the spirit… without turning" refrains). Those two are the chapter.

### 11. Quotation/Allusion

Ezekiel 1 contains **no formula-citation and no verbatim quotation** — consistent with the book-overview's finding that Ezekiel is citation-light and carried by *conceptual* allusion and internal repetition. The candidates below were derived first from the passage's own vocabulary (throne, cherubim, expanse, sapphire, bow, glory-cloud, storm), then tested. All are **conceptual/verbal-echo** allusions; confidence flagged throughout.

**Exodus 24:9–10 (+ 40:34–35) → Ezekiel 1:26–28** *(high)*
- *Source context:* at Sinai the elders see "the God of Israel. There was under his feet as it were a pavement of sapphire stone" (Exod 24:10), amid glory "like a devouring fire" (24:17); later the glory-cloud fills the finished tabernacle (40:34–35). The sapphire-under-God and the fire-glory are the Sinai/tabernacle theophany's signature.
- *Book usage (Move 2):* this is the *opening* of the book's live **temple-glory / Sinai** thread — enthroned here (1), departing (8–11), returning (43). The vision inaugurates the source the whole book tracks. `[T]`
- *OT-to-OT (Move 3):* Exod 24 (Sinai), Exod 40 (tabernacle), and 1 Kgs 8 (temple) are already one canonical conversation — the glory that fills God's dwelling. Ezekiel joins that conversation and *relocates it to Babylon*.
- *What it adds:* the Chebar vision is a **Sinai-and-temple theophany in exile** — the sapphire throne and fire-glory that belonged to the mountain and the sanctuary now appear over a foreign canal, and crucially they are *mobile*. The exiles' God has brought his Sinai-glory to them.

**Genesis 3:24 / Exodus 25:18–22 (cherubim) → the living creatures (1:5–14; named cherubim at 10:15, 20)** *(high)*
- *Source context:* cherubim guard the way to Eden's tree of life (Gen 3:24) and overshadow the mercy seat where the LORD is enthroned "between the cherubim" (Exod 25:22). Throne-guardians of sanctuary space.
- *Book usage:* Eden and the sanctuary are live sources across Ezekiel (28:13–14; 41:18–20). `[T]`
- *What it adds:* the throne travels **with its proper guardians**; the enthroned-between-the-cherubim of the ark is now seen airborne and mobile — the mercy-seat throne unfixed from the Most Holy Place.

**Genesis 1:6–7 (*rāqîaʿ*) → Ezekiel 1:22** *(moderate–high)*
- *Source context:* the expanse God makes to separate the waters — the cosmic architecture of creation.
- *What it adds:* the throne rests **above the firmament** — the enthroned One is the Creator over the cosmos, not merely Israel's local deity. The ancient versions' "firmament" (Tool 8) supports the echo. *Moderate–high (verbal identity high; deliberate-allusion inference moderate).*

**Genesis 9:13–16 (the bow) → Ezekiel 1:28** *(moderate)*
- *Source context:* the rainbow set in the cloud as the sign of the Noahic covenant — God's pledge of restraint and mercy after the flood-judgment.
- *What it adds:* the glory-throne of the God who is about to pronounce devastating judgment is **haloed by the covenant sign of mercy**; wrath is encircled by grace. Homiletically rich, and a genuine textual detail (the bow "on the day of rain", 1:28), not imposed. *Moderate* (the Noahic reference is a reasonable inference from a distinctive image).

**Psalm 18:10 / 104:3 (storm-theophany, God riding the cherub/clouds) → Ezekiel 1** *(moderate–high)*
- *Source context:* "He rode on a cherub and flew; he came swiftly on the wings of the wind" (Ps 18:10); "he makes the clouds his chariot; he rides on the wings of the wind" (Ps 104:3). The storm-riding, cherub-borne divine warrior.
- *What it adds:* Ezekiel's *merkabah* (chariot-throne) is the enacted, seen form of the Psalter's storm-riding God — the poetic image made visionary reality. Grounds the "chariot" reading of the vision.

**Prophetic throne-call type-scene: 1 Kings 22:19; Isaiah 6 → Ezekiel 1** *(moderate–high)*
- *Source context:* Micaiah "saw the LORD sitting on his throne" and Isaiah saw "the Lord… high and lifted up" — throne-visions that commission a prophet.
- *What it adds:* Ezekiel's call belongs to the prophetic throne-vision tradition; the *variations* are the message — the throne is **mobile** and the location is **exile**, not the temple.

**Move 4 — Internal echo.** *Planted for later (high):* the enthroned mobile glory → resumes in the valley (3:22–23), **departs** the temple eastward (9:3; 10:4, 18–19; 11:22–23), and **returns** "as the vision that I had seen… by the Chebar canal" (43:2–3, explicit callback). The **sound "like many waters"** (1:24) → recurs at the glory's return (43:2). The **human-form figure** enthroned as *ʾādām* (1:26) is planted against the vocative **"son of man" (*ben-ʾādām*)** that opens the very next verse (2:1) — glory in human form above, mortal man below. `Internal: planted — the book's master-frame and its central irony.` *High.*

### 12. Genre

**Apocalyptic/visionary theophany embedded in prophetic literature** (the *merkabah* vision). The decisive reading rule is **"vision not video"**: the wheels-within-wheels, the four faces, the eye-filled rims are **symbolic** — omnidirectional motion, all-seeing knowledge, the created order gathered under the throne — not a mechanism to be reconstructed or a datum for speculation. Genre governs this passage more than most (see Pitfalls): a literalising reading produces UFOs and angel-engineering; a symbol-reading produces the enthroned, sovereign, all-knowing, ever-present glory. Where the line between symbol and referent is contested (the precise identity of the enthroned figure), flag it rather than resolve it (see Christological Reading). `[T]`

### 13. Copycat

**N/A for imitation** — this is a unique, unrepeatable prophetic **call-vision**, not a narrative with exemplary characters. `[T]` One legitimate posture-note (per the Copycat guidance on passive-receipt acts): the vision is entirely *received* — Ezekiel does nothing, is shown everything, and falls on his face (1:28). The shape of the event implies the congregation's right posture before the glory is **reception and worship**, not achievement or technique — but this is a posture the passage models, not a method to copy. Do not preach "seek visions like Ezekiel."

### 14. Bible Timeline

**Passage location:** the exile, 593 BC — after the 597 deportation, before Jerusalem's fall (587). The temple still stands but its glory is about to leave it (8–11). **Reader location:** this side of the cross and Pentecost, where the glory has "tabernacled among us" in Christ (John 1:14), where the Spirit indwells the church as the temple (1 Cor 3:16; Eph 2:21–22), and where the throne-vision has been reopened to John with the same four living creatures around the Lamb (Rev 4:6–8). **What has happened in between that reframes the reading:** the glory *did* depart, the second temple lacked the glory-cloud, and the glory returned in person in the incarnation — so a Christian reads Ezekiel's mobile, exile-visiting glory as anticipating the God who comes to dwell with his people in Christ and the Spirit, and finally in the New Jerusalem where the throne is the temple (Rev 22:1–3). Not "the temple will be rebuilt with this glory," but "the glory has come, and is coming, in Christ."

### 15. Who Am I?

- **Ezekiel:** the recipient/prophet, not a type of Christ in this passage (the "son of man" thread begins at 2:1 but is a mortal-under-glory designation here). We are **not** Ezekiel-the-visionary — the call is unrepeatable. The identification the text invites is with **the awed worshipper on the ground** before the enthroned glory.
- **The enthroned "likeness with a human appearance" (1:26):** the figure to **behold**, on a trajectory to Christ (see Christological Reading), not a mirror for the reader.
- **The implied exiles:** the ones who need to learn the LORD reigns, present, in Babylon — the **recipients** whose situation (displacement, the apparent absence of God) maps onto every reader who feels God distant. That is where we stand.

### 16. So What?

**Stage 1 — the author's intended response:** to fall on the face in worship (1:28), and to have the mind reset — the LORD reigns, sovereign, free, all-seeing, and *present in exile*; he is not defeated, localised, or absent. Awe before the enthroned glory is the point, before any command is given.

**Stage 2 — worldview, behaviour, motivation.** *Worldview:* God is not bound to our sanctuaries, nations, or expectations; his throne goes where his Spirit wills, and his rims are "full of eyes" (nothing is hidden from him). *Behaviour:* stop domesticating God — presuming his presence where we prefer it, doubting it where we actually are; start living and worshipping before the enthroned, ever-present, all-seeing glory. *Motivation (against moralism):* the throne is ringed by the **bow of mercy** (1:28) — the summons to awe is grounded not in raw terror but in the God who binds his judgment-throne with the covenant sign of grace, fulfilled in the glory who came to dwell with us.

**Four audiences.** *For me:* God sees all and reigns over my "Babylon"; small views of God are rebuked. *For a Christian friend* in displacement or loss: the throne is mobile — God reigns where you are, not only where you wish you were. *For the church:* corporate, face-to-the-ground worship of the enthroned Christ, the glory among the lampstands (Rev 1). *For an unbeliever:* there is a throne, and eyes, and a King — and a bow of mercy around him.

**Prayer:** worship of the enthroned glory; confession of domesticated, too-small views of God; thanks that the glory came near — to exiles then, to us in Christ.

### Textual Variants

Ezekiel's Hebrew (MT) is notoriously difficult and the LXX is often shorter; two items touch this passage:

- **1:14** ("And the living creatures darted to and fro…"): **absent from the Old Greek (LXX)** in many witnesses and suspected by some critics as a gloss. *Preaching implication:* minor — the "darting to and fro" merely intensifies the motion already described in 1:13; build nothing structural on the verse. *Confidence: moderate; verify in BHS/NET.*
- **General:** scattered MT/LXX differences in the description (word order, the odd term) affect fine detail, not the substance of the vision. *Preaching implication:* none material.

Otherwise: no significant, doctrine-affecting textual variants in the passage.

### Historical and Cultural Background

**Setting.** *Time:* 593 BC, the fifth year of Jehoiachin's exile. *Place:* the **Chebar canal** (Akkadian *nāru kabari*), a major irrigation channel near Nippur in southern Babylonia, where a community of Judean deportees was settled (cf. 3:15, Tel-abib). *Political context:* Judah's elite, deported with Jehoiachin in 597, live under Babylonian power; Jerusalem still stands under the vassal Zedekiah, and false hope of a quick return is circulating.

**What the original audience knew.**
- **A priest without a sanctuary.** Ezekiel (1:3) was of priestly line; in Babylon he could never serve at the altar. The vision comes to a man cut off from the temple — and it brings the temple's glory to him. `[S/T]`
- **The land was "unclean."** Foreign soil was reckoned defiled (cf. Amos 7:17; Hos 9:3–4); a theophany of the holy glory *there* would strike the exiles as almost category-breaking — and that is the point. `[S]`
- **Babylonian throne- and guardian-iconography.** Winged composite guardian figures (*karibu*/lamassu), storm-gods (Adad) depicted on or with wheeled thrones, and processional imagery were the visual furniture of the exiles' new world. Ezekiel's vision **uses and subverts** that vocabulary: the composite living creatures are not Babylon's gods but the *servants* who bear the throne of the LORD, who alone reigns. *Moderate–high; the subversion reading is a reasonable inference.* `[I/S]`
- **Dating by the exiled Davidic king** (1:2). The exilic community reckoned years by Jehoiachin's captivity (attested also in Babylonian ration-tablets naming "Yaukin king of Judah") — Ezekiel adopts that covenant-clock rather than the imperial one. `[S], moderate–high`

**What this changes.** The vision is not a generic "big picture of God"; its scandal and comfort are specifically *exilic* — the glory of Sinai and the temple appears in Babylon, mobile and reigning, to a displaced, sanctuary-less people. Miss the setting and the sermon loses the shock.

### Original Audience Reception

**Canonical audience.** *Section:* Nevi'im. *What the canonical reader knows:* the whole Torah (Sinai, the tabernacle glory, the cherubim, the clean/unclean map) and the history through the monarchy to the exile — so the reader recognises the sapphire, the fire-cloud, the cherubim, and the firmament instantly, and feels the wrongness-then-wonder of their appearing in Chaldea. *Register:* the temple-glory and Eden/creation texts are canonical neighbours in the reader's ear.

**The first hearers.** *Who:* deported Judeans, many of the former elite, priests among them; a covenant people dislocated from land and temple. *Situation:* trauma, displacement, and a theological crisis — was the LORD beaten by Babylon's gods, or left behind in Zion? *What they brought:* Zion theology (the LORD dwells in the temple; Jerusalem is inviolable).

**Where the passage fits their world.** They would have known that thrones, cherubim, and glory belonged to the Most Holy Place — and here they are, in the sky over a Babylonian canal, on wheels.

**Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances.**
- *Shocking:* the holy glory manifest on unclean foreign soil.
- *Surprising:* the throne has **wheels** — the LORD is not fixed to Zion.
- *Comforting:* God is **here**, with the exiles, reigning; they are not abandoned.
- *Disturbing:* if the throne can travel to Babylon, it can also **leave** Jerusalem (as 8–11 will show) — the same mobility that comforts also threatens.

**What we bring that they didn't.** Individualism (the vision addresses a covenant-people crisis, not private spirituality); a taste for the esoteric (we chase the mechanics; they felt the throne); and a domesticated deity (their scandal was a God too holy and free for their categories, not too small).

### Biblical-Theological Themes

**Temple / God's presence** *(high).* Ezekiel 1 is a hinge in the canon's presence-story: Eden → tabernacle → temple → **the glory enthroned and about to depart (Ezek 1 → 8–11)** → the glory returns (43) → the Word tabernacles (John 1:14) → the church as temple (Eph 2:21–22) → no temple in the city, for God is its temple (Rev 21:22). The vision shows the presence *unfixed* — the necessary condition for both the tragedy of departure and the hope of return. *Where it sits:* the enthroned glory, mobile, on the eve of abandoning the temple.

**Kingdom / the throne of God** *(high).* The vision is first of all a **throne** (1:26) — the LORD reigns, over Babylon and its gods, over the whole created order (the fourfold creatures, the firmament). It joins the enthroned-LORD stream (1 Kgs 22:19; Isa 6; Dan 7:9; Rev 4) that culminates in the Lamb on the throne.

**Confluence.** The presence-theme and the throne/kingdom-theme meet in **climactic convergence**: the One present in exile is the enthroned Creator-King, and both lines run to the same reality — Christ, the enthroned glory who comes to dwell with his people (Rev 4–5; 21–22). Naming the convergence is the preaching gold: *the God who reigns is the God who draws near.*

### Christological Reading

**Type of Christological connection:** primarily **trajectory**, with a strong **NT-reuse** link (Rev 4) and a flagged, secondary **Christophany** question.

**How the passage points to Christ.**
- *Trajectory (glory / presence):* the mobile glory that will depart a defiled temple and return (Ezek 1 → 8–11 → 43) culminates in Christ, in whom "the Word became flesh and tabernacled among us, and we have seen his glory" (John 1:14) — the glory returns, in person. Passes the four typology tests: **category** (glory/temple/presence — established) ✓; **NT-precedent** (John 1:14; and John 12:41 reads Isaiah's throne-vision as seeing Christ's glory — the analogous move) ✓; **escalation** (the glory is now embodied, not merely seen at three removes) ✓; **authorial-pattern** (the text raises a glory it will move and resolve) ✓. *Confidence: high (trajectory).*
- *NT reuse:* the **four living creatures** reappear around the throne of God and the Lamb (Rev 4:6–8, explicitly fusing Ezekiel's creatures with Isaiah 6's "holy, holy, holy") — so the vision is, in canonical retrospect, a vision of the throne now shared by the Lamb. *Confidence: high (explicit NT reuse).*
- *Christophany (flagged):* the "likeness with a human appearance" **enthroned** as the glory (1:26) is read by many as a pre-incarnate manifestation of the Son (cf. the human-form glory of Rev 1:13–16, itself drawing on Ezekiel and Daniel 7). This is **suggestive and defensible but not the text's plain, self-evident claim** — Ezekiel guards it with "likeness… appearance." *Confidence: moderate; preach it as trajectory/anticipation, not as a settled identification (see Pitfalls and Open Questions).*

**Trajectory.**
- *What the passage anticipates/longs for:* a glory-presence that is with God's people even in exile, and (as the book unfolds) one that returns to dwell permanently.
- *What Christ fulfils:* the glory embodied and dwelling (John 1:14; 2:19–21), the throne shared by the Lamb (Rev 4–5), and finally God-with-us as the city's very name (Ezek 48:35 → Rev 21:3).
- *Already / not yet:* the glory has come in Christ and indwells the church; the full, face-to-face dwelling awaits the New Jerusalem.

**Moralism check.**
- *The "be like X" temptation here:* "cultivate a bigger vision of God / be zealous and reverent like Ezekiel" — turning the vision into a technique for spiritual intensity.
- *The gospel grounding:* the worship the passage evokes is grounded in the **sight of the enthroned glory** — the glory that came near in Christ and is ringed by the bow of mercy — not in the worshipper's effort to feel awed.
- *Christ as hero, not example:* the enthroned glory is the One beheld and worshipped; the sermon's centre is the King on the throne (now the Lamb), not the prophet on the ground.

**Confidence:** high on the trajectory and the Rev 4 reuse; moderate on the enthroned-figure-as-Christophany.

### Difficult / Contested Verses

- **The identity of the enthroned human-form figure (1:26–28).** Christophany vs a visionary anthropomorphism of the glory guarded by "likeness." Do not preach a settled identification; present it as anticipation. (Open Question.)
- **The four faces (1:10).** Later reception mapped them to the four evangelists (Irenaeus) and, differently, to the cardinal points/zodiac (ANE). Both are **reception**, not the text's stated meaning; the text's own point is totality/omni-directional service of the throne. Avoid preaching the evangelist-scheme as Ezekiel's intent. (Pitfall.)
- ***ḥašmal* (1:4, 27)** and the *merkabah*-mysticism tradition. Later Judaism treated ch. 1 as esoteric (the Mishnah, *Ḥagigah* 2:1, restricts public exposition of the *merkavah*). Historically interesting; a caution against esoteric preaching, not a licence for it. `[S], moderate`

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: preaching the mechanism instead of the enthroned glory
- **What it looks like:** a sermon on wheels-within-wheels, cherub anatomy, "eyes everywhere," or (worse) UFOs and ancient-astronaut speculation.
- **Why it's wrong:** it literalises an apocalyptic vision ("vision not video") and buries the one point the chapter and the whole book need — the glory is enthroned, sovereign, all-seeing, and *mobile*.
- **The corrective:** preach the throne and the One on it; let the wheels mean what they symbolise (omnidirectional, unstoppable, all-knowing presence).

### Pitfall: over-claiming the Christophany
- **What it looks like:** "the man on the throne *is* the pre-incarnate Christ" asserted as the plain, certain sense.
- **Why it's wrong:** Ezekiel deliberately hedges ("the likeness… with a human appearance… the appearance of the likeness of the glory"); the text anticipates rather than identifies.
- **The corrective:** preach it as trajectory — the human-form glory that is fulfilled when the Word tabernacles among us and the Lamb shares the throne (John 1:14; Rev 4–5) — and say the identification is anticipatory, not asserted by Ezekiel.

### Pitfall: losing the exile
- **What it looks like:** a generic "get a bigger view of God" sermon with no Babylon in it.
- **Why it's wrong:** the shock and comfort are specifically exilic — the glory of Sinai and the temple appearing on unclean foreign soil to a sanctuary-less people.
- **The corrective:** set the vision by the Chebar; preach the God who reigns and draws near *in* displacement — comfort to the exiled, warning to the presumptuous.

### Pitfall: reading the four faces as the four evangelists (or the zodiac)
- **What it looks like:** "the man, lion, ox, and eagle are Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John."
- **Why it's wrong:** that is later church reception (Irenaeus), not Ezekiel's meaning; the text's point is totality and omni-directional service of the throne.
- **The corrective:** preach the fourfold as the whole created order gathered under the reign of the LORD, and leave the evangelist-scheme to a footnote if at all.

### Pitfall: moralising the vision into a technique
- **What it looks like:** "be reverent / zealous / spiritually intense like Ezekiel."
- **Why it's wrong:** the worship is evoked by the sight of the enthroned glory, not produced by the worshipper's effort.
- **The corrective:** ground awe in the God who is on the throne and came near in Christ, ringed by the bow of mercy.

## Open Questions / Uncertainties

1. **The enthroned figure's identity (1:26).** Christophany vs guarded anthropomorphic vision of the glory. Preach as trajectory; commentary check (e.g. Duguid, Block) recommended before pressing a Christophany claim.
2. ***ḥašmal* (1:4, 27).** Lexically uncertain (amber/electrum/glowing alloy). Nothing load-bearing; verify in HALOT/TDOT if it comes up.
3. **1:14 text-critical status.** Possibly absent from the Old Greek. Build nothing on it; verify in BHS/NET notes.
4. **The "thirtieth year" (1:1).** Most likely Ezekiel's age (priestly age of service, Num 4), but disputed (an era reckoning?). Flag; commentary check.
5. **The four-faces background.** Reasonable to read as totality; the specific ANE/zodiac derivations are speculative — do not preach them as established.
