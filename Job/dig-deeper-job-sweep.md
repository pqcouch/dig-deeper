## Headline Findings (sweep level)

The strongest claims from the whole sweep. Each emerged under several tools, and every word-level element was verified by lemma or exact phrase in the WLC.

1. **God's first speech answers Job's first speech, and it does so in Job's own words.** `[T]` (synthetic)
   - **The womb and its doors.** Job curses the night "because it did not shut the דַּלְתֵי ('doors') of my womb" and asks why he did not die מֵרֶחֶם ("from the womb", 3:10–11). God asks who shut in the sea בִּדְלָתַיִם ("with doors") when it burst מֵרֶחֶם ("from the womb", 38:8). **38:8 is the only verse in the Hebrew Bible where דֶּלֶת ("door") and רֶחֶם ("womb") meet.**
   - **The dawn.** Job wished his night would never see עַפְעַפֵּי־שָׁחַר ("the eyelids of the dawn", 3:9). God says Leviathan's eyes are "like the eyelids of the morning" (41:18 [Heb 41:10]). **The phrase occurs at those two verses and nowhere else.**
   - **Leviathan roused.** Job summoned "those who are prepared עֹרֵר ('to rouse') Leviathan" (3:8). God: "No one is so fierce that he dares יְעוּרֶנּוּ ('to arouse him')" (41:10 [Heb 41:2]) — the same verb, the same monster.
   - **Freedom and the taskmaster.** In death, said Job, the prisoners "do not hear the voice of the נֹגֵשׂ ('taskmaster')" and the slave is חָפְשִׁי ("free", 3:18–19). God sends the wild donkey out חָפְשִׁי ("free"), and "the shoutings of the נוֹגֵשׂ ('driver') he does not hear" (39:5, 7). חָפְשִׁי appears in Job only at 3:19 and 39:5, and נוֹגֵשׂ only at 3:18 and 39:7.
   - **What it shows.** The freedom Job could find only in the grave, God shows him alive in the wilderness; the monster and the dawn Job wanted to call down on his birth are God's own creatures. The speeches from the storm are not a change of subject.
   - *Surfaced by:* Move 4 (units 3, 15, 16), Repetition, Vocabulary, Structure. *High confidence on the words; moderate–high on the design.*

2. **Job speaks *to* God; nobody else in the debate does — and God's verdict turns on it.** `[T]` / `[I]` (synthetic)
   - **The pattern.** Job turns from the friends to God in the second person again and again: 7:7–21; 9:28–31; 10:2–22; 13:20–14:22; 16:7–8; 17:3–4; 30:20–23. **No friend, and not Elihu, ever addresses God in prayer.** They speak about him in the third person throughout (read in full; see Open Questions for a mechanical check).
   - **The verdict.** "You have not spoken אֵלַי ('to Me') what is right, as My servant Job has" (42:7, 8). The preposition is אֶל ("to, toward"). NASB95, ESV and NIV84 all render it "of Me"; Swete has ἐνώπιόν μου ("before me").
   - **What it shows.** The friends' orthodoxy was spoken about God to a sufferer; Job's protest was spoken to God. The book commends the second.
   - *Surfaced by:* Tool 4 (address), Tool 8, Move 4 (units 5–13, 17), Author's Purpose. *High confidence on the pattern; moderate on the weight the preposition bears* — the idiom can mean "concerning".

3. **The book's lawsuit is argued in its own vocabulary, and the words for it cluster as nowhere else.** `[T]` (synthetic)
   - **The counts, by lemma in the WLC.** צדק ("to be in the right", verb) — **17 of its 41 occurrences in the Hebrew Bible** are in Job. יכח ("to argue, reprove, arbitrate") — **17 of 59**. רשׁע ("to condemn, declare guilty", verb) — **11 of 35**. תִּקְוָה ("hope") — **13 of 34**.
   - **The courtroom arc.** Job wants to contend (9:3), has no מוֹכִיחַ ("umpire", 9:33), has prepared his case (13:18), signs his indictment (31:35). God then calls him "him who reproves (מוֹכִיחַ) God" (40:2) and asks, "Will you condemn Me (תַּרְשִׁיעֵנִי) that you may be justified (תִּצְדָּק)?" (40:8).
   - **The turn.** Job had asked, "Do not condemn me (אַל־תַּרְשִׁיעֵנִי)" (10:2). God's question reverses the direction.
   - *Surfaced by:* Repetition, Vocabulary, Move 4 (units 6–9, 15–16), Structure. *High confidence.*

4. **The epilogue speaks the language of Ezekiel 14 — the chapter that names Job — and turns it inside out.** `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for any dependence (synthetic)
   - **Ezekiel 14.** Noah, Daniel and Job are named as men whose righteousness would deliver only themselves (Ezek 14:14, 20). Then: "you will be comforted (וְנִחַמְתֶּם) concerning the calamity (עַל־הָרָעָה) which I have brought (הֵבֵאתִי) against Jerusalem" (14:22), and "you will know that I have not done in vain (חִנָּם) whatever I did" (14:23).
   - **Job.** The family "comforted him (וַיְנַחֲמוּ) concerning all the adversities (עַל כָּל־הָרָעָה) that the LORD had brought (הֵבִיא) on him" (42:11). And the book's keyword is חִנָּם — the Accuser's "for nothing" (1:9) and God's "without cause" (2:3).
   - **The checks.** נחם with רָעָה and a hiphil of בוא, God as subject, stands in the Hebrew Bible at **Ezek 14:22 and Job 42:11** (with Job 2:11 and Lam 1:21 sharing the lemmas in other constructions). נחם and חִנָּם meet in one verse only at **Ezek 14:23**.
   - **What it shows.** Jerusalem's ruin was "not without cause" (Ezek 14:23); Job's was, by God's own admission (2:3). The righteous man Ezekiel names as able to save only himself ends his book interceding for others (42:8–10).
   - *Surfaced by:* Move 2, Move 4 (units 1, 2, 17), Vocabulary, Canonical Position. *High confidence on the verbal cluster; moderate on its significance; direction of dependence open.*

5. **The Satan's patrol is described with the words used of the LORD's own eyes, and the Satan's question is the book's.** `[T]` (synthetic)
   - **The words.** The Satan comes מִשּׁוּט בָּאָרֶץ ("from roaming about on the earth", 1:7; 2:2). The same verb, שׁוּט, describes "the eyes of the LORD which range to and fro throughout the earth" (Zech 4:10; 2 Chr 16:9), and the search through Jerusalem for one man who does justice (Jer 5:1).
   - **The incitement.** God says, "you incited Me (וַתְּסִיתֵנִי) against him" (2:3). The same verb carries the Bible's best-known pairing on the question of who moves whom: "the LORD … incited (וַיָּסֶת) David" (2 Sam 24:1) and "Satan … incited (וַיָּסֶת) David" (1 Chr 21:1).
   - **Addressee-differentiation.** The Satan appears in 1:6–2:7 (fourteen times in eleven verses) and never again. He is not rebuked, restored or answered by name. The book's answer to his question is Job's conduct, not a verdict on the questioner.
   - *Surfaced by:* Vocabulary, Move 2, Move 4 (units 1, 2, 16, 17), Biblical Theology. *High confidence on the words; moderate on what they imply about the figure.*

### Phase 0.5 — the overview's four threads, extracted

- **Christological trajectory** (overview): no direct prophecy.
  - **Trajectory:** the mediator Job cannot find — 9:33 umpire; 16:19–21 witness and advocate; 19:25–27 Redeemer; 33:23–24 Elihu's angel-mediator and ransom — to 1 Tim 2:5–6.
  - **Type** through the servant category: "My servant" twice in the prologue and four times in the epilogue; ruined "without cause"; struck on the cheek and spat upon (Isa 50:6); interceding for those who wronged him (Isa 53:12).
  - **Contrast:** Job is righteous, not sinless (38:2; 40:8; 42:6).
  - **Lower-confidence lines:** wisdom hidden (28 → Col 2:3); walking on the sea (9:8 → Mark 6:48); resurrection (14:14; the Greek of 19:26; 42:17a).
  - **Moralism check:** "be patient like Job".
- **Intertextual map** (overview): dense allusion, no formula citations, and a book that quotes itself.
  - **Live sources:** Gen 1–3; the patriarchal genealogies and idiom (Gen 10; 22; 25; 36; Gen 18:27; 33:19; 35:29); Balaam (Num 23–24); Jer 20:14–18; Ps 8; Ps 88; Ps 107:40; Isa 41:20; Lam 3; the wisdom motto (Prov 1:7; 9:10); Prov 3:11–12; Prov 8:22, 25; the sea-monster texts (Isa 27:1; 51:9; Ps 74; 89; 104); 1 Kgs 5:10; Ps 69:21; the restoration idiom (Deut 30:3).

  1. Preaching the friends.
  2. "The patience of Job".
  3. Supplying the explanation.
  4. Hearing the storm speeches as a put-down.

### Author's Purpose (Tool 1, book level)

The book does not state its purpose. Read off its shape, it does three things. `[I]`

- **It answers the Accuser's question by a life, not a proof.** "Does Job fear God for nothing?" (1:9) is answered by a man who, stripped, will neither curse God nor buy relief by confessing sins he did not commit (27:5–6). The book shows that God can be feared for himself.
- **It tests a doctrine in front of a reader who knows the case.** The reader has God's verdict on Job (1:8; 2:3) before the friends open their mouths, so the retribution calculus is falsified for this case from the start. What remains open is the general question: how a righteous sufferer may speak to God, and what God is like.
- **It brings the sufferer, and the reader, to God himself.** The answer from the storm gives no reason for the suffering. It gives the Creator — and a verdict ("My servant Job … has spoken what is right", 42:7) that vindicates lament addressed to God over orthodoxy spoken against a sufferer.

**Passage-level purpose varies by unit**, and is named under each unit's Positional Necessity Check.

### Genre (Tool 12)

**Wisdom literature in a narrative frame: a prose story (1:1–3:1; 42:7–17) around a poetic disputation (3:2–42:6).** `[T]`

- **Reading rule 1 — attach every line to its speaker.** The dialogue is a debate. True sentences are put to false uses (Paul cites Eliphaz's 5:13 as Scripture at 1 Cor 3:19), and bitter sentences are put to right uses (Job's).
- **Reading rule 2 — lament is not resolved early.** Chapters 3–31 give lament its full length. The book waits twenty-nine chapters for God's answer, and a sermon that answers in the first five minutes has misread the genre.
- **Reading rule 3 — the prose frame is part of the book, not a folk-tale wrapper.** It supplies the reader's privileged knowledge and the verdict. Proposals that the frame and the poem were composed separately are `[S]` and change nothing about how the finished book is read.
- **Reading rule 4 — the storm speeches are poetry of creation, not a science lecture or a scolding.** Their questions are rhetorical and descriptive at once: of 70 verses in 38:2–39:30, 29 carry an interrogative (WLC, by the interrogative particle and pronouns in the lemma index). `[T]`
- **Sub-genres embedded:** a curse on the day of birth (3); hymns of creation in several mouths (5:9–16; 9:4–13; 12:13–25; 25:2–6; 26:5–14; 36:26–37:24); a wisdom poem (28); an oath of clearance with self-imprecations (31, seventeen verses carrying אִם, "if"); a genealogy-like death notice (42:16–17).

### Bible Timeline (Tool 14) and Canonical Position (Phase 0.55)

- **The story's setting.** A patriarchal world: family priesthood (1:5), wealth in flocks, the קְשִׂיטָה (42:11, elsewhere only Gen 33:19; Josh 24:32), 140 years after the restoration, and Isaac's death-formula (42:17 = Gen 35:29). No Sinai, no Temple, no Israel. `[T]` The setting is not a date of composition; that is `[S]` (see the overview).
- **Canonical position** — extracted from the overview rather than re-derived.
  - **Section:** Ketuvim, second in the BHS order (Psalms, Job, Proverbs). `[T]`
  - **Reading implication:** Job stands between the book of lament-and-praise and the book of ordered wisdom, and quotes both. `[I]`
  - **Presupposes (checked):** Gen 1:3; 2:25; 3:1; 3:19; 6:2, 4; 18:27; 22:21; 25:2; 33:19; 35:29; 36:11, 15; Num 23:1, 29; Isa 27:1; 51:9; Ps 74:14; 89:11 [Eng 89:10]; 104:26.
  - **Handoff:** to Proverbs, the fear of the Lord as wisdom, tested; to the canon, a named man (Ezek 14:14, 20; Jas 5:11).
  - **Neighbours:** Psalms before; Proverbs after. Both adjacencies do work.
- **What the sweep adds to Presupposes.** Four more checked references belong in the field:
  - **Deut 28:35** — the covenant curse of "severe boils … from the sole of your foot to the crown of your head", at 2:7. The two verses share שְׁחִין רָע ("sore boils"), מִכַּף רֶגֶל ("from the sole of the foot") and קָדְקֹד ("the crown of the head"); שְׁחִין with קָדְקֹד occurs **only** at these two verses.
  - **Deut 32:39** — "I wound (מָחַצְתִּי) and I heal (אֶרְפָּא)", at 5:18 (Eliphaz). מחץ and רפא meet only at these two verses.
  - **Isa 35:3** — "strengthen the weak hands and make firm the feeble knees", at 4:3–4 (Eliphaz). Six lemmas are shared, and the pairing of hands and knees with רָפֶה ("slack") occurs only in these two places.
  - **Gen 6:9** — צַדִּיק תָּמִים ("righteous, blameless"), Noah's epithet, at 12:4, the only other place the pair stands.
- **Where the reader sits.** This side of the cross. The Accuser has been "thrown down, … he who accuses them before our God day and night" (Rev 12:10); the mediator Job lacked has been named (1 Tim 2:5); the Redeemer lives (Rev 1:17–18). The book is not superseded by that knowledge. It is where the Christian learns to speak to God while the reason is still withheld.

### Historical and Cultural Background

- **Setting (narrative world).** The land of Uz — in Lamentations with "the daughter of Edom" (Lam 4:21) — and the steppe east and south of Canaan. Eliphaz's Teman is proverbial for wisdom: "Is there no longer any wisdom in Teman?" (Jer 49:7; cf. Obad 8–9). `[T]` for the references. The friends come from the Edomite and Arabian orbit, and so does the wisdom they bring. `[I]`
- **What the first hearers knew.**
  - **Shame and the ash-heap.** Sitting "among the ashes" (2:8), tearing robes and throwing dust (2:12), seven days' silence (2:13) — mourning conventions whose meaning the text assumes. Ezekiel sat seven days "appalled" among the exiles (Ezek 3:15), a verified parallel of number and posture. `[T]`
  - **The gate and honour.** Job's past standing is measured at the city gate (29:7), where elders sat and cases were heard; his present disgrace is measured by the contempt of outcasts' sons (30:1–15). Honour and shame are the currency of chapters 29–30. `[I]`
  - **The lawsuit.** Job borrows the procedure of a legal complaint: a statement of the case (13:18), a demand for the charges in writing (31:35), an oath of clearance with self-curses (31). The oath of clearance is a known ancient Near Eastern legal form. `[S]` — held for checking.
  - **The chaos monsters.** Leviathan, Rahab and the Sea were names the first hearers knew from the wider world's myths of combat. `[S]` The Bible uses them as images of God's victory (Ps 74:13–14; Isa 27:1; 51:9). Job's God presents Leviathan as his creature (41:1–34 [Heb 40:25–41:26]), which is a claim, not a borrowing. `[I]`
- **What this changes.** The book is not provincial. It sets a non-Israelite sage in the wisdom-land of Edom, and has the covenant God answer him from the storm. `[I]`

### Original Audience Reception (book level)

- **Canonical audience.** Readers of the Writings, who know the Torah and the Prophets, the exile and the unfinished restoration. They have the Psalter's laments behind them and Proverbs ahead. Default to this audience. `[I]`
- **Shocking.**
  - God boasts of Job to the Accuser and then allows him to be ruined "without cause" (2:3). `[T]`
  - Job curses his birth and accuses God of wronging him (19:6) — and is commended at the end (42:7). `[T]`
  - The three orthodox friends are condemned, and must ask the man they accused to pray for them (42:8). `[T]`
- **Surprising.** God answers, and the answer is a tour of the wild — creatures no one owns, rain on land where no one lives (38:26), a monster no one can tame — with not one word about Job's sin or the Accuser. `[T]`
- **Comforting.** God speaks. The sufferer who spoke to God is vindicated. The broken family eats bread together again (42:11). `[T]`
- **Disturbing.** The reason is never given to Job. And the book lays a verbal trail from Job's suffering to the covenant curses (2:7 ‖ Deut 28:35) while insisting the sufferer is innocent. `[T]` / `[I]`
- **What we bring that they did not.** A therapeutic instinct that wants the explanation; a courtroom in which silence means guilt; and a habit of reading "the patience of Job" as stoic calm. `[I]`

### Biblical-Theological Themes (book level)

1. **Adversary and accusation.** The Accuser in the heavenly court (1:6–2:7; Zech 3:1–2) → the one who incited David (1 Chr 21:1) → Christ tempted, and praying against Satan's sifting (Luke 22:31–32) → "the accuser of our brethren … has been thrown down" (Rev 12:10). The Accuser acts only under God's permission (1:12; 2:6). *High.*
2. **Wisdom and the fear of the Lord.** "The fear of the Lord, that is wisdom" (28:28) → Prov 1:7; 9:10 → the Branch on whom rests "the spirit of wisdom … and the fear of the LORD" (Isa 11:2 — one of only seven verses where יִרְאָה, "fear", and חָכְמָה, "wisdom", meet) → Christ, "the wisdom of God" (1 Cor 1:24), in whom "are hidden all the treasures of wisdom" (Col 2:3). *High.*
3. **Creation and chaos.** Creation by word and boundary (Gen 1; Job 38:8–11) → the sea-monster defeated or tamed (Ps 74; Isa 27:1; Job 41) → the calming of the sea and walking on it (Mark 4:39–41; 6:48) → "the dragon, the serpent of old, who is the devil and Satan" bound (Rev 20:2; cf. 12:9). *Moderate–high.*
4. **Relation.** The three themes converge (*climactic convergence*): the Accuser's question is about whether God is worth fearing; the wisdom poem answers that fearing God is wisdom; and the creation speeches show the God who is worth it, the one who rules even the monster. `[I]`

### Christological Reading (book-level frame)

The overview's frame is confirmed and sharpened. Units 6, 8, 9, 14 and 17 carry the detail.

- **Trajectory — the mediator.** 9:33 → 16:19–21 → 19:25–27 → 33:23–24 (see those units). The sweep adds two verified links:
  - 19:25 pairs גֹּאֵל ("Redeemer") with אַחֲרוֹן ("at the last"), a pairing found elsewhere in the Hebrew Bible **only** at Isa 44:6: "the LORD … his Redeemer (גֹּאֲלוֹ) … 'I am the first and I am the last (אַחֲרוֹן)'". Rev 1:17 has Christ say "I am the first and the last".
  - 16:17 עַל לֹא־חָמָס בְּכַפָּי ("although there is no violence in my hands") shares its opening with Isa 53:9, עַל לֹא־חָמָס עָשָׂה ("because he had done no violence"). The exact phrase occurs only in these two verses.
- **Type — the servant.** The servant category is Isaiah's, and the sweep finds more of Isaiah's servant vocabulary than the overview did: the cheek and spittle (16:10; 30:10; Isa 50:6); no violence (16:17; Isa 53:9); the taskmaster (נוֹגֵשׂ, 3:18; 39:7 — at Isa 53:7 the servant is נִגַּשׂ, "oppressed"); intercession (42:8–10; Isa 53:12). *Moderate.* The category test and the authorial-pattern test pass; the NT-precedent test is weak, since no NT text names Job as a type; escalation passes.
- **Contrast.** Job accuses God (19:6; 40:8) and retracts (42:6). Christ, "while suffering, … uttered no threats, but kept entrusting Himself to Him who judges righteously" (1 Pet 2:23). *High.*
- **Moralism check.** "Be patient like Job" fails on the text (21:4, "why should I not be impatient?"). "Be a good comforter" is closer, but still exemplarist. The gospel ground is God's free delight in his servant, and the mediator given freely (Rom 3:24's δωρεάν, "as a gift" — the same adverb as Swete's 1:9 "for nothing"). That last pairing is a theological rhyme, not an allusion. `[I]`

### Textual and Apparatus Notes (book level)

**The Masoretic frame and paragraphing.** As the WLC transcribes Leningrad:

- Prose accents occur only in 1:1–3:1 and 42:7–17, and the poetic accents throughout 3:2–42:6, including Elihu's prose introduction (32:1–5: dehi 4×, ole 2×, pashta and zaqef 0×).
- There are 39 paragraph markers. Every speech ends with one **except 27:23**, which runs straight into chapter 28.
- These are reading-tradition features, not authorial. **Their manuscript spread was not checked in Logos**; every finding resting on them is tagged `[unchecked — apparatus spread]` and capped at moderate.

**Ketiv forms.** 49 verses of Job carry an unpointed form of two or more consonants (WLC; the ס and פ markers excluded). That is 4.6 % of the book's verses, close to the corrected canonical rate. Those that bear on findings:

| Ref | Ketiv / qere | Bearing |
|---|---|---|
| 13:15 | לא "not" / לוֹ "for him" | "Though He slay me, I will hope in Him" rests on the qere (unit 7) |
| 19:29 | שדין / שַׁדּוּן | "that you may know there is judgement" (unit 9) |
| 38:1; 40:6 | מנ (ה)סערה / מִן | the whirlwind (units 15–16) |
| 42:2 | ידעת "you know" / יָדַעְתִּי "I know" | Job's confession (unit 16) |
| 42:10 | שבית / שְׁבוּת | the restoration idiom (unit 17) |

**An instrument fault caught in this sweep.** The overview's ketiv scan listed 19:29 as having no unpointed word. It does: its last word, שדין, is unpointed, but it was **joined to the sof pasuq (׃)** that ends the verse, and the scan counted that mark as pointing. The scan was re-run with sof pasuq, maqqef and paseq stripped before testing, and the list above is the corrected one. A verse-final ketiv is invisible to a test that treats ׃ as a vowel. (Recorded in the Text-First Declaration.)

**The Greek.** BHS heads its apparatus "about a fifth of this book is lacking in the Greek". The Rahlfs export marks the supplied material with 212 asterisks. Swete (Vaticanus) prints that material unmarked. Greek features bearing on units are classified under the three-way triage where they arise. Three are worth holding at book level:

- **ὁ διάβολος ("the devil") for הַשָּׂטָן ("the Accuser")** throughout. *Category 3* — the New Testament's own word.
- **Swete 38:1 ties the theophany to Elihu.** "After Elihu had ceased speaking, the Lord spoke to Job through whirlwind and clouds." *Category 2* — a plus in the Greek, which reads Elihu as the prelude to God.
- **Swete 42:17a–e** adds a resurrection line and the identification with Jobab of Edom (Gen 36:33). *Category 2* — an early reading of the book, not evidence about its text.

## Unit 1 — 1:1–22 ⭐ For Nothing? The Man, the Council and the First Test

> 1:1 אִישׁ הָיָה בְאֶרֶץ־עוּץ אִיּוֹב שְׁמוֹ וְהָיָה הָאִישׁ הַהוּא תָּם וְיָשָׁר וִירֵא אֱלֹהִים וְסָר מֵרָע
> There was a man in the land of Uz whose name was Job; and that man was blameless, upright, fearing God and turning away from evil.
>
> 1:9 וַיַּעַן הַשָּׂטָן אֶת־יְהוָה וַיֹּאמַר הַחִנָּם יָרֵא אִיּוֹב אֱלֹהִים
> Then Satan answered the LORD, "Does Job fear God for nothing?" *(NASB95)*

### Headline Findings

- **The man's character is stated three times in the same four terms, and the third time God adds the one word that sets the book going.** `[T]`
  - תָּם וְיָשָׁר וִירֵא אֱלֹהִים וְסָר מֵרָע ("blameless, upright, fearing God and turning away from evil") stands at 1:1 (narrator), 1:8 (God) and 2:3 (God), and nowhere else in the book (phrase search, WLC).
  - The fourth term, סוּר מֵרָע ("turning away from evil"), returns only at 28:28, as the definition of understanding.
  - *Surfaced by:* Repetition, Narrator's Comment, Move 4. *High confidence.*
- **The Accuser's question is about God as much as about Job.** `[T]` / `[I]`
  - הַחִנָּם יָרֵא ("does he fear … for nothing?", 1:9) supposes that piety is a transaction: God has "made a hedge about him" and "blessed the work of his hands" (1:10).
  - If that is so, God is loved for his gifts, not for himself. The test is of God's worth as well as Job's integrity.
  - *Surfaced by:* Linking Words, Vocabulary, Author's Purpose. *High confidence.*
- **The euphemism "bless" frames the test, and the book resolves it.** `[T]`
  - Job fears his sons may have "cursed God" — the Hebrew has וּבֵרֲכוּ ("and blessed", 1:5). The Accuser predicts Job "will surely curse You" — יְבָרֲכֶךָּ ("will bless you", 1:11).
  - BHS calls 1:5 *correctio vel euphemismus* ("a correction or euphemism") and cross-refers 1:11 to it — *as BHS prints it*.
  - Job's response ends with a real blessing: יְהִי שֵׁם יְהוָה מְבֹרָךְ ("blessed be the name of the LORD", 1:21). The book ends: "the LORD blessed (בֵּרַךְ) the latter days of Job" (42:12).
  - *Surfaced by:* Vocabulary, Translations, Move 4. *High confidence.*

### 2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

- **Preceding movement.** As the opening, the preceding movement is the canon. In the BHS order the reader comes from the Psalter, whose first psalm promises that the man who delights in the Torah prospers "in whatever he does" (Ps 1:3). Job opens on such a man. `[I]`
- **Necessity answer.** The book must establish two facts before any human speaks: that Job is innocent, by the narrator's word and God's; and that the reader, unlike every character, knows it. Without the prologue the dialogue would be a real trial of Job; with it, it is a trial of a doctrine. `[I]`
- **Implication.** The sermon must hold two perspectives at once — the reader's, from heaven, and Job's, from the ash-heap — because the book is built on the gap between them. `[I]`

**Context.** Petuḥot close 1:5 and 1:22 in Leningrad, as the WLC transcribes it (spread unchecked), marking the portrait and the first test as units. `[T]`

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 1:1–5 | The man | Character (1:1), family and wealth (1:2–3), preventive piety (1:4–5) |
| 1:6–12 | The first council | YHWH commends Job; the Accuser asks "for nothing?"; permission with a limit |
| 1:13–19 | Four messengers | Four disasters in one day, each report cut off by the next |
| 1:20–22 | The response | Mourning, worship, blessing — and the narrator's verdict |

**Binding device.** Alternation between earth and heaven (earth 1:1–5 → heaven 1:6–12 → earth 1:13–22), repeated in chapter 2. The two councils are nearly identical, so the differences carry the weight (unit 2). `[T]`

**The disasters alternate** between human agents and something from heaven or the wilderness: Sabeans (1:15), "the fire of God" (1:16), Chaldeans (1:17), "a great wind" (1:19). `[T]`

### 4. Linking Words

- **כִּי ("for") at 1:5.** Job's sacrifices are grounded in a "perhaps" — אוּלַי חָטְאוּ בָנַי ("perhaps my sons have sinned"). His piety is anxious and preventive, and the narrator adds כָּכָה יַעֲשֶׂה אִיּוֹב כָּל־הַיָּמִים ("thus Job did continually"). `[T]`
- **אִם־לֹא (1:11).** An oath-formula: "if he does not curse You to Your face…", with the curse on the speaker left unspoken. The Accuser swears. `[T]`
- **רַק ("only", 1:12).** "Only do not put forth your hand on him." The Accuser works inside a limit God sets. `[T]`

### 6. Narrator's Comment

- **1:1** — the narrator's own verdict on Job, before any character speaks. `[T]`
- **1:22** — בְּכָל־זֹאת לֹא־חָטָא אִיּוֹב וְלֹא־נָתַן תִּפְלָה לֵאלֹהִים ("Through all this Job did not sin nor did he blame God"). The line recurs in part at 2:10 (phrase search, WLC).
  - **תִּפְלָה ("unseemliness, folly")** occurs three times in the Hebrew Bible: Jer 23:13; Job 1:22; 24:12 (lemma, WLC).
  - At 1:22 Job does not ascribe תִּפְלָה to God. At 24:12 he says God "does not pay attention to תִּפְלָה" — to the wrong being done in the world. See Move 4.

### 7. Vocabulary

- **תָּם ("blameless").** Complete, whole, without moral flaw — not sinless. It is Jacob's epithet (אִישׁ תָּם, Gen 25:27). Job holds 7 of its 14 occurrences in the Hebrew Bible (lemma, WLC): 1:1, 8; 2:3; 8:20; 9:20, 21, 22. The last four are in the mouths of Bildad and Job — the word becomes contested. `[T]`
- **הַשָּׂטָן ("the Accuser").** With the article, a title, not a name: "the adversary". The same form stands at Zech 3:1–2; without the article, שָׂטָן at 1 Chr 21:1. *High confidence* on the title sense.
- **שׁוּט ("to roam", 1:7).** The Accuser comes מִשּׁוּט בָּאָרֶץ ("from roaming about on the earth"). The verb describes "the eyes of the LORD which range to and fro throughout the earth" (Zech 4:10; 2 Chr 16:9), and the search through Jerusalem's streets for one man "who does justice" (Jer 5:1) (lemma, WLC). `[T]` The Accuser's patrol mimics the LORD's scrutiny. `[I]` *Moderate–high.*
- **חִנָּם ("for nothing, without cause", 1:9).** In Job only at 1:9; 2:3; 9:17; 22:6 (lemma, WLC). See the Echo Table under Move 4.
- **בֵּרֵךְ ("bless") as euphemism** — see Headline Findings.
- **Proper-noun inventory.** Uz (1:1); the sons of the east (1:3); the Sabeans (1:15); the Chaldeans (1:17).
  - *Uz* carries canonical freight. It is a son of Aram (Gen 10:23), a son of Nahor, Abraham's brother (Gen 22:21), and in Edom's orbit (Lam 4:21) — all verified.
  - *בְּנֵי־קֶדֶם ("the sons of the east")* are the peoples whose wisdom Solomon's exceeded (1 Kgs 5:10 [Eng 4:30]). Job was "the greatest" of them (1:3). *Moderate.*
  - The others are scenery.

### 8. Translations

- **בֵּרֵךְ at 1:5, 11.** NASB95 and ESV "curse"; NIV84 "cursed / curse". All resolve the euphemism, rightly, but the congregation never hears the irony of "bless" in the Accuser's mouth. The NASB95 margin preserves it. `[T]`
- **1:21 "the LORD gave…".** Swete adds "as it seemed good to the Lord, so it came to pass". *Category 2* (a plus in the Greek); it smooths Job's blessing into acquiescence.
- **Ancient versions check.** Swete's 1:9 has δωρεάν ("for nothing"), but its 2:3 renders the same חִנָּם as διὰ κενῆς ("in vain"). *Category 1* (translation loss): the Greek breaks the one-word link between the Accuser's charge and God's admission.
- **Pulpit divergence note.** N/A — no pulpit text declared.

### 9. Tone and Feel

Calm, formal and patterned — then a cataract. The councils are ceremonious and repetitive. The four messengers arrive at speed, each cut off by the next: עוֹד זֶה מְדַבֵּר וְזֶה בָּא ("while he was still speaking, another also came", 1:16, 17; 1:18 has עַד, where BHS notes many manuscripts read עוֹד). Each ends with the same refrain. **Soundtrack:** a slow chorale broken by four hammer-blows, then a single voice singing the chorale's words. Don't hurry 1:20–21. `[T]`

### 10. Repetition

- **The messengers' refrain**, וָאִמָּלְטָה רַק־אֲנִי לְבַדִּי לְהַגִּיד לָךְ ("and I alone have escaped to tell you"), stands at 1:15, 16, 17, 19 — four times (phrase search, WLC). `[T]`
- **The numbers of completeness.** Seven sons and three daughters; 7,000 sheep and 3,000 camels; 500 yoke and 500 donkeys (1:2–3). The same numbers return, doubled for the property only, at 42:12–13. `[T]`
- **יְהוָה ("the LORD") three times in one verse (1:21)** — in Job's mouth. It is the only verse in which Job uses the covenant name until the quotation at 12:9 (lemma, WLC). `[T]`

### 11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

- **Live sources (from the overview):** Gen 1–3, the patriarchs, the Psalter, Proverbs, Lamentations.

**Gen 2:25; 3:19 → 1:21** *(moderate)*

- *Source context.* The first couple עֲרוּמִּים ("naked") and unashamed (Gen 2:25); then the sentence "to dust (עָפָר) you shall return (תָּשׁוּב)" (Gen 3:19).
- *Book usage.* Job's first speech in the book. The dust-and-return pair recurs at 10:9 and 34:15 (both lemmas in one verse, WLC).
- *What it adds.* Job's עָרֹם יָצָאתִי … וְעָרֹם אָשׁוּב ("naked I came … naked I shall return", 1:21) names mortality in the vocabulary of the first chapters. Eccl 5:15 (Heb 5:14) will reuse it (verified in the Ecclesiastes overview).

**Fire from heaven — 2 Kgs 1:10, 12, 14; 2 Chr 7:1 → 1:16** *(moderate, Move 1 only)*

- "Fire … from heaven … consumed" stands elsewhere only at 2 Kgs 1:10, 12, 14 (Elijah's judgement on the king's men) and 2 Chr 7:1 (God's acceptance of Solomon's sacrifice) (lemma, WLC). Job 1:16 alone has "the fire of God fell (נָפְלָה)".
- The messenger names it אֵשׁ אֱלֹהִים ("the fire of God"): the servant reads the disaster as divine. The reader knows its source is the Accuser acting under permission. `[T]` / `[I]`

**Internal (Move 4).** The prologue is the book's densest planting ground. *Planted for later:*

- **The four-term portrait (1:1)** → 28:28 סוּר מֵרָע ("depart from evil"). The wisdom no one can find is the character the first verse gave Job. `[T]` *High.*
- **The Accuser's חִנָּם (1:9)** → God's "without cause" (2:3), Job's "without cause" (9:17), Eliphaz's "without cause" (22:6). *High.*
- **"Bless" as curse (1:5, 11)** → 42:12. *High.*
- **Job's burnt offerings for his children (1:5)** → the friends' burnt offering and Job's prayer (42:8). *High.*
- **"My servant Job" (1:8)** → 2:3; 42:7–8 (four times). *High.*
- **"Touch" (גַּע, 1:11)** → "the hand of God has touched (נָגְעָה) me" (19:21). *High.*
- **The hedge (שַׂכְתָּ, 1:10)** → "whom God has hedged in (וַיָּסֶךְ)" (3:23) → "who enclosed (וַיָּסֶךְ) the sea with doors" (38:8). *Moderate–high;* 1:10 is a by-form.
- **The sons of God (1:6)** → "all the sons of God shouted for joy" (38:7), with no Accuser among them. *Moderate.*
- **תִּפְלָה (1:22)** → 24:12. Job did not ascribe folly to God, and later complains that God does not regard the folly done on earth. *Moderate;* only three occurrences in the canon.
- **The children's feasts in their brother's house (1:4, 13, 18)** → the family eating bread in Job's house (42:11). *Moderate.*
- **Addressee-differentiation.** The prologue names four parties: God, the Accuser, Job, and (in chapter 2) the wife and three friends. Each is resolved differently. The Accuser is simply dropped after 2:7 (the lemma occurs fourteen times in eleven verses, all in 1:6–2:7). The friends are judged by name (42:7–9). The wife is answered only by echo (unit 2). `[T]`

### 13 / 15. Copycat and Who Am I?

- **Job's worship (1:20–21)** is a normative pattern: grief expressed (torn robe, shaved head) and God blessed in the same act. Scripture elsewhere commends such lament-with-worship. But the book will not let it be the whole pattern — chapters 3–31 follow. `[I]`
- **Job's preventive sacrifices (1:5)** are descriptive. They show anxious piety, not a model for managing God. `[I]`
- **Who am I?** Not the Accuser, not God. The reader is placed as a spectator of the heavenly court, who knows what Job does not — which forbids the reader the friends' error later.

### 16. So What? (brief)

- **Intended response.** To ask the Accuser's question of oneself: do I fear God for nothing?
- **Worldview.** Suffering can arrive from heaven's permission without any fault in the sufferer, and the sufferer need not know why.
- **Candidate FCF.** Transactional faith — loving God for his hedge.
- **Prayer.** That God would be enough, with or without the hedge.

### Passage-specific difficulties

- **The heavenly wager.** *Ethical / apologetic.* God appears to allow a righteous man's children to die to win an argument. The text does not soften it: God says "you incited Me against him to ruin him without cause" (2:3). Handling: acknowledge it; do not explain it away; note that the book itself never tells Job, and that the rest of the book is about how a sufferer speaks to God without the explanation. `[I]`
- **The children's deaths.** *Pastoral.* A landmine for bereaved hearers, and not repaired by "seven sons and three daughters" in 42:13 (unit 17).
- **1:21 at funerals.** Often quoted apart from 1:20 and from chapter 3. Keep the torn robe with the blessing.

## Unit 2 — 2:1–13 ⭐ Skin for Skin: the Second Test, the Wife and the Silent Friends

> 2:3 … וְעֹדֶנּוּ מַחֲזִיק בְּתֻמָּתוֹ וַתְּסִיתֵנִי בוֹ לְבַלְּעוֹ חִנָּם
> "… And he still holds fast his integrity, although you incited Me against him to ruin him without cause."
>
> 2:9 וַתֹּאמֶר לוֹ אִשְׁתּוֹ עֹדְךָ מַחֲזִיק בְּתֻמָּתֶךָ בָּרֵךְ אֱלֹהִים וָמֻת
> Then his wife said to him, "Do you still hold fast your integrity? Curse God and die!" *(NASB95)*

### Headline Findings

- **God admits the ruin was "without cause", and uses the verb that pairs him with Satan elsewhere in the canon.** `[T]`
  - The second council repeats the first almost verbatim, then adds God's sentence: "you incited Me (וַתְּסִיתֵנִי) against him to ruin him חִנָּם ('without cause')" (2:3).
  - סות ("to incite") is the verb of 2 Sam 24:1 (the LORD incited David) and 1 Chr 21:1 (Satan incited David) (lemma, WLC).
  - *Surfaced by:* Repetition, Vocabulary, Move 2. *High confidence on the words.*
- **The wife repeats God's words exactly, with the opposite conclusion.** `[T]`
  - God: וְעֹדֶנּוּ מַחֲזִיק בְּתֻמָּתוֹ ("he still holds fast his integrity", 2:3). The wife: עֹדְךָ מַחֲזִיק בְּתֻמָּתֶךָ ("do you still hold fast your integrity?", 2:9).
  - The phrase occurs only at these two verses (phrase search, WLC). God and the Accuser disagree about Job; the wife unknowingly takes God's words and draws the Accuser's conclusion ("curse God").
  - *Surfaced by:* Repetition, Structure, Move 4. *High confidence.*
- **Job's disease is the covenant curse, verbatim.** `[T]`
  - "Sore boils from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head" (2:7): שְׁחִין רָע, מִכַּף רַגְלוֹ, קָדְקֳדוֹ.
  - Deut 28:35 threatens disobedient Israel with "severe boils (שְׁחִין רָע) … from the sole of your foot (מִכַּף רַגְלְךָ) to the crown of your head (קָדְקֳדֶךָ)". שְׁחִין with קָדְקֹד occurs at these two verses only (lemma, WLC).
  - The innocent man bears the covenant curse — which is exactly what the friends will read as proof of guilt.
  - *Surfaced by:* Vocabulary, Tool 11, Original Audience. *High on the words; moderate on intention.*

### 2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

- **Preceding movement.** Unit 1 ended with Job's blessing and the narrator's verdict (1:22). The Accuser's first challenge has failed. `[T]`
- **Necessity answer.** The test must reach Job's own body — "skin for skin" (2:4) — before the claim "for nothing" can be settled. And the friends must arrive and sit silent before chapter 3, so that Job's curse is spoken into their silence. `[I]`
- **Implication.** 2:11–13 is the hinge from narrative to debate. The friends' best moment is their silence. `[I]`

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 2:1–6 | The second council | Repetition of 1:6–12, with God's added admission (2:3) and a new limit ("only spare his life", 2:6) |
| 2:7–8 | The blow | Boils from sole to crown; the potsherd and the ashes |
| 2:9–10 | The wife | "Curse God and die" / "Shall we accept good … and not adversity?" |
| 2:11–13 | The friends | They come to "sympathize … and comfort", weep, and sit seven days silent |

**Device.** Exact repetition with variation (2:1–6 against 1:6–12), then a triad of human responses: wife, Job, friends. `[T]`

### 5. Parallels

- **2:10b.** גַּם אֶת־הַטּוֹב נְקַבֵּל מֵאֵת הָאֱלֹהִים וְאֶת־הָרָע לֹא נְקַבֵּל ("Shall we indeed accept good from God and not accept adversity?") is an antithetic pair turned into a question. `[T]`
- **רָע ("evil, adversity")** here is misfortune, not moral evil. The same word stands in "turning away from evil" (1:1) and in "all this adversity (הָרָעָה)" (2:11). *High confidence* on the range.

### 7. Vocabulary

- **עוֹר ("skin").** עוֹר בְּעַד־עוֹר ("skin for skin", 2:4) is proverbial and obscure. The word recurs 8 times in Job (lemma, WLC), including 19:26 ("after my skin is destroyed") and 41:7 [Heb 40:31] (Leviathan's skin, which no harpoon fills). `[T]`
- **בָּלַע ("to swallow, ruin", 2:3).** God says the Accuser incited him "to swallow him". Zophar will say the wicked man "swallows riches, but will vomit them up" (20:15). `[T]`
- **הַנְּבָלוֹת ("the foolish women", 2:10).** נָבָל is moral folly — the fool who says there is no God (Ps 14:1). It returns as נְבָלָה ("folly") in God's verdict on the friends (42:8); the nouns are cognate, not one lemma (WLC). `[T]`
- **כְּאֵב ("pain", 2:13).** It recurs only at 16:6, where Job says speaking does not assuage it (lemma, WLC). `[T]`
- **Proper-noun inventory.**
  - Eliphaz the Temanite: the name and the place both belong to Esau's line (Gen 36:11, 15, verified).
  - Bildad the Shuhite: Shuah is Abraham's son by Keturah (Gen 25:2, verified).
  - Zophar the Naamathite: no canonical genealogy.
  - **Load-the-story rule.** Gen 36 is the chapter of Edom's chiefs and kings. The friends' names place their wisdom in Edom, whose reputation for wisdom Jer 49:7 and Obad 8 assume. `[T]` for the names; `[I]` for the effect. *Moderate.*

### 8. Translations

- **2:9 "curse God".** Hebrew בָּרֵךְ ("bless"); BHS cross-refers it to the euphemism note at 1:5.
- **Ancient versions check.** Swete gives the wife a long speech (2:9 plus five clauses), in which she laments her dead children and her own wandering labour. *Category 2* (a plus in the Greek). It humanises her; the Hebrew leaves her one line.
- **Swete 2:3** renders חִנָּם as διὰ κενῆς ("in vain"), where 1:9 had δωρεάν. *Category 1.*
- **Pulpit divergence note.** N/A — no pulpit text declared.

### 10. Repetition

- **2:1–3a repeats 1:6–8** almost word for word. The additions are the point: "to present himself before the LORD" (2:1, added for the Accuser); "he still holds fast his integrity … without cause" (2:3). `[T]`
- **תֻּמָּה ("integrity")** — 2:3, 2:9, 27:5, 31:6 in Job, and elsewhere in the Hebrew Bible only Prov 11:3 (lemma, WLC). The wife's question becomes Job's vow: "Till I die I will not put away my integrity from me" (27:5). `[T]`
- **"In all this Job did not sin"** — 1:22 and 2:10. The second adds בִּשְׂפָתָיו ("with his lips"). The later rabbinic reading "with his lips, but in his heart?" is `[S]`. The text says only what it says. `[T]`

### 11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

**Deut 28:35 → 2:7** *(high on the words)*

- *Source context.* Deuteronomy 28 is the covenant's list of blessings and curses. Verses 27 and 35 threaten "the boils of Egypt" and "severe boils … from the sole of your foot to the crown of your head" on Israel if it forsakes the covenant — curses that end in exile (28:64–68).
- *Book usage.* The first use of Deuteronomy in Job.
- *OT-to-OT.* שְׁחִין ("boil") is the plague of Exod 9:9–11 and Hezekiah's illness (2 Kgs 20:7; Isa 38:21). Deut 28:27 names "the boils of Egypt": the curse turns Egypt's plague on Israel.
- *What it adds.* The book dresses an innocent Gentile in the covenant curse. Any reader who knows Deuteronomy will conclude what the friends conclude — and the prologue has already shown them wrong. The book is contesting a reading of Deuteronomy, not Deuteronomy. `[I]` *Moderate.*

**2 Sam 24:1 // 1 Chr 21:1 → 2:3** *(moderate, Move 1 only)*

- The same census is ascribed to the LORD's anger (2 Sam 24:1) and to Satan's incitement (1 Chr 21:1). Job 2:3 holds both in one sentence: the Accuser incites God. The canon already knows the problem of the double agency. `[T]` / `[I]`

**Ezek 3:15 → 2:13** *(uncertain, Move 1 only)*

- Ezekiel sat "seven days … appalled" among the exiles before he spoke (lemma, WLC). The number and the silence match; nothing else does.

**Internal (Move 4).**

- **The wife's word (2:9)** is answered by echo only. God's verdict uses the noun cognate with Job's rebuke of her (2:10 הַנְּבָלוֹת → 42:8 נְבָלָה), and applies it to the friends. `[T]` *Moderate–high.*
- **"To sympathize and comfort" (2:11)** → 16:2; 21:34 → 42:11. The pair נוּד ("sympathize") and נָחַם ("comfort") occurs in Job only at 2:11 and 42:11 (WLC). *High.*
- **The ashes (2:8)** → 30:19; 42:6 ("dust and ashes"). *High.*
- **Seven days (2:13)** → seven bulls and seven rams (42:8). *Uncertain.*
- **"Spare his life" (2:6)** — the limit is kept: Job does not die until "old and full of days" (42:17). *Moderate.*

### 16. So What? (brief)

- **Intended response.** Accept from God both good and adversity without cursing him — and sit with the sufferer before speaking.
- **Candidate FCF.** The pressure, from those closest, to give up integrity to end the pain.
- **Prayer.** For the grace to hold fast, and the wisdom to be silent.

### Passage-specific difficulties

- **Job's wife.** *Pastoral.* She is often vilified. The text gives her one line, spoken after the death of all her children. Job calls her words foolish; the narrator does not condemn her; she is simply absent from the ending. Handle with restraint. `[T]` / `[I]`
- **"Skin for skin."** *Exegetical.* The idiom is not certainly understood. Don't build on a guess.

## Unit 3 — 3:1–26 ⭐ Let the Day Perish

> 3:3 יֹאבַד יוֹם אִוָּלֶד בּוֹ וְהַלַּיְלָה אָמַר הֹרָה גָבֶר
> "Let the day perish on which I was to be born, and the night which said, 'A boy is conceived.'"
>
> 3:4 הַיּוֹם הַהוּא יְהִי חֹשֶׁךְ
> "May that day be darkness …" *(NASB95)*

### Headline Findings

- **Job's curse is an un-creation, spoken in the grammar of Genesis 1.** `[T]`
  - "May that day be darkness" is יְהִי חֹשֶׁךְ (3:4): the jussive of Gen 1:3, יְהִי אוֹר ("let there be light"), with darkness for light.
  - He asks that "God above" (אֱלוֹהַּ — its first occurrence in the book) not seek that day, and that it have no light (3:4); that darkness and צַלְמָוֶת ("black gloom") claim it (3:5); that it be cut out of the calendar (3:6); that it never see the dawn (3:9).
  - *Surfaced by:* Tool 11, Repetition, Tone. *High confidence.*
- **Job curses his day; he does not curse God.** `[T]`
  - 3:1: וַיְקַלֵּל אֶת־יוֹמוֹ ("and cursed the day of his birth"). For the first time the verb is קלל, not the euphemistic בָּרֵךְ.
  - The Accuser's prediction (1:11; 2:5) is not fulfilled. Job's target is his own existence.
  - *Surfaced by:* Vocabulary, Narrator's Comment, Move 4. *High confidence.*
- **Almost every image in the curse is picked up in God's first speech.** `[T]` (synthetic) — see sweep-level Headline Finding 1.
  - The doors of the womb (3:10–11 → 38:8); the eyelids of the dawn (3:9 → 41:18 [Heb 41:10]); Leviathan roused (3:8 → 41:10 [Heb 41:2]); the free slave who hears no taskmaster (3:18–19 → 39:5–7).
  - *High on the words; moderate–high on the design.*

### 2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

- **Preceding movement.** Seven days of silence (2:13). Job has blessed God (1:21) and accepted adversity (2:10). The friends have not spoken. `[T]`
- **Necessity answer.** The dialogue needs a first word, and it must be Job's. The book will not let the prologue's patience stand as his whole response: after the silence comes the cry. Chapter 3 is the opening speech of the debate, and every friend's first speech is a reply to it (4:2–6). `[I]`
- **Implication.** Chapter 3 is not a lapse from 1:21 but its necessary companion. The same man blesses the name and curses the day, and God later commends him (42:7). `[I]`

**Context.** 3:1 is prose (a petuḥah follows it); 3:2 begins the poetic accentuation that runs to 42:6 (WLC, as transcribed; spread unchecked). `[T]`

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 3:1–2 | Prose introduction | "Job opened his mouth and cursed his day" |
| 3:3–10 | Curse on the day and the night | Un-creation: darkness, no dawn, out of the calendar — "because it did not shut the doors of my womb" |
| 3:11–19 | Why did I not die at birth? | Sheol as rest, where kings and slaves alike are at peace |
| 3:20–26 | Why is light given to the sufferer? | The general lament, ending in Job's own turmoil |

**Device.** A curse, then two "why?" laments (לָמָּה, 3:11, 20). The poem moves from the past (my birth) through the hypothetical (had I died) to the present (my turmoil). It ends on רֹגֶז ("turmoil"), the same word as 3:17. `[T]`

### 5. Parallels

- **3:3.** Day and night in synonymous parallel, each personified: the night "said, 'A boy is conceived'". `[T]`
- **3:17–19.** A merism of the dead: "the wicked … the weary … the prisoners … the small and the great … the slave". Death levels all. `[T]`
- **3:25.** "What I fear comes upon me (פַחַד פָּחַדְתִּי), and what I dread befalls me." The cognate accusative intensifies. It looks back to the anxious piety of 1:5. `[I]` *Moderate.*

### 7. Vocabulary

- **קלל ("to curse; to be light, swift", 3:1).** The lexeme spans "curse", "be slight" and "be swift" (lemma 7043, WLC: 3:1; 7:6; 9:25; 24:18; 40:4). At 40:4 Job says הֵן קַלֹּתִי ("Behold, I am insignificant") — the same lexeme. See Move 4. *Moderate* on any intended echo.
- **צַלְמָוֶת ("deep darkness, black gloom").** Job has 10 of its 18 occurrences in the Hebrew Bible (lemma, WLC), from 3:5 to 38:17 ("the gates of deep darkness"). `[T]`
- **רֹגֶז ("turmoil, agitation").** Job has 5 of 7 occurrences (3:17, 26; 14:1; 37:2; 39:24). At 37:2 it is the "rumbling" of God's thunder: Job's turmoil and the thunder share a word. `[T]` *Moderate* on significance.
- **חָפְשִׁי ("free", 3:19).** Elsewhere mostly the release of slaves in the law (Exod 21; Deut 15; Jer 34) and "free among the dead" (Ps 88:6 [Eng 88:5]). In Job only 3:19 and 39:5 (lemma, WLC). `[T]`
- **נֹגֵשׂ ("taskmaster", 3:18).** The Egyptian taskmasters of Exod 3:7 and 5:6–14; and at Isa 53:7 the servant is נִגַּשׂ ("oppressed"). In Job only 3:18 and 39:7 (lemma, WLC). `[T]`
- **לִוְיָתָן ("Leviathan", 3:8).** See Tool 11.

### 8. Translations

- **3:8.** BHS notes that the original may have read יָם ("sea") for יוֹם ("day"): "those who curse the Sea". It is an editorial proposal, not a manuscript reading (*as BHS prints it*). NASB95, ESV and NIV84 keep "day". `[T]`
- **Ancient versions check.** Swete's 3:8 renders Leviathan as τὸ μέγα κῆτος ("the great sea-monster") — the word for Jonah's fish (Jonah 2:1 LXX), which Matt 12:40 takes up. *Category 3* at a distance; the link is Greek-only.
- **Pulpit divergence note.** N/A — no pulpit text declared.

### 9. Tone and Feel

The rawest lament in the canon: jussives of destruction, then questions without answers. **Soundtrack:** a single voice after seven days of silence, unaccompanied. **Don't preach it cheerfully**, and don't hurry to chapter 38. The book makes the reader wait thirty-five chapters. `[T]` / `[I]`

### 10. Repetition

- **Darkness words.** חֹשֶׁךְ ("darkness") at 3:4, 5; אֹפֶל ("darkness") at 3:6; צַלְמָוֶת at 3:5; the stars darkened (3:9). Against them אוֹר ("light") three times (3:9, 16, 20) (lemma, WLC). `[T]`
- **Rest words.** שָׁקַט ("be quiet") 3:13, 26; נוּחַ ("rest") 3:13, 17, 26; שָׁלַו ("be at ease") 3:26; against רֹגֶז ("turmoil") 3:17, 26. The dead have what the living Job lacks. `[T]`
- **לָמָּה ("why?")** at 3:11, 20, and מַדּוּעַ ("why?") at 3:12. `[T]`

### 11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

**Gen 1:3–5 → 3:3–9** *(high)*

- *Source context.* Creation's first act: God speaks light into darkness and names day and night (Gen 1:3–5).
- *Book usage.* The first use of Genesis 1 in the book. It returns in God's reply (38:4–38).
- *OT-to-OT.* Jer 4:23 ("I looked on the earth, and behold, it was formless and void; and to the heavens, and they had no light") also reverses Genesis 1, in a lament over judgement. Job's un-creation is personal; Jeremiah's is national.
- *What it adds.* Job wants his day undone as God first did the world — but in reverse. God's answer (38:12–15) will ask whether Job has ever "commanded the morning".

**Jer 20:14–18 → 3:1–11** *(high for the link; direction open)*

- *Source context.* Jeremiah, beaten and put in the stocks (Jer 20:2), turns from praise (20:13) to curse: "Cursed be the day when I was born … Why did I ever come forth from the womb to look on trouble and sorrow?" (20:14, 18).
- *The shared words, by lemma.* אָרַר ("curse", Jer 20:14–15; Job 3:8); יוֹם + יָלַד ("the day … I was born", Jer 20:14; Job 3:3); רֶחֶם + יָצָא ("come forth from the womb", Jer 20:18; Job 3:11); עָמָל ("trouble", Jer 20:18; Job 3:10).
- *Book usage.* First use. Jeremiah returns as a partner in Lamentations-shaped material (units 8–9).
- *OT-to-OT.* Jeremiah, the prophet of the exile, and Job, the righteous sufferer, curse their births in the same words. Which is prior cannot be settled from the texts.
- *What it adds.* The curse is not blasphemy; a faithful prophet spoke it. It puts Job in the company of the suffering righteous, not the wicked.

**Leviathan — Ps 74:14; Isa 27:1 → 3:8** *(moderate, Move 1 only)*

- Leviathan is the sea-monster God crushes (Ps 74:14) and will punish (Isa 27:1). Job invokes those who can rouse it — sorcerers who curse days. `[S]` for the magical background. The monster returns as God's creature (41:1–34 [Heb 40:25–41:26]).

**Internal (Move 4).**

- *Answers §earlier:* the curse falls on the day, not God — the Accuser's prediction (1:11; 2:5) fails. `[T]`
- *Planted for later* — the chapter is the book's second great planting ground:
  - **3:10–11 womb and doors** → 38:8. *High.*
  - **3:9 eyelids of the dawn** → 41:18 [Heb 41:10]. *High.*
  - **3:8 rousing Leviathan** → 41:10 [Heb 41:2] (עור, "rouse", in both). *High.*
  - **3:18–19 free, no taskmaster** → 39:5–7. *High.*
  - **3:23 "whom God has hedged in"** → 19:8 ("He has walled up my way") and 38:8 (the same verb, וַיָּסֶךְ, of the sea). *High.*
  - **3:1 קלל** → 40:4 קַלֹּתִי ("I am insignificant"). *Moderate.* The lexeme is wide.
  - **3:26 רֹגֶז** → 37:2 (the thunder's "rumbling") → 39:24 (the warhorse's "rage"). *Uncertain.*

### 16. So What? (brief)

- **Intended response.** Permission to lament to the bottom, and to bring it to God in speech rather than silence or cursing him.
- **Candidate FCF.** The wish not to exist.
- **Prayer.** Ps 88 is the nearest canonical prayer; pray it, unresolved.

### Passage-specific difficulties

- **Suicidal ideation.** *Pastoral — a real landmine, especially at the bedside.* Job longs for death (3:21–22) but does not seek to take his life, and he speaks his longing aloud. Name that distinction gently. Commend speaking the darkness to God and to others, and point to help without making promises about what help will involve.
- **Magic and Leviathan (3:8).** *Apologetic.* Job's image assumes others' beliefs about cursing days; it does not endorse them.

## Unit 4 — 4:1–5:27 Eliphaz: the Night Vision and the Formula

> 4:7 זְכָר־נָא מִי הוּא נָקִי אָבָד וְאֵיפֹה יְשָׁרִים נִכְחָדוּ
> "Remember now, who ever perished being innocent? Or where were the upright destroyed?"
>
> 5:17 הִנֵּה אַשְׁרֵי אֱנוֹשׁ יוֹכִחֶנּוּ אֱלוֹהַּ וּמוּסַר שַׁדַּי אַל־תִּמְאָס
> "Behold, how happy is the man whom God reproves, so do not despise the discipline of the Almighty." *(Eliphaz, NASB95)*

### Headline Findings

- **Eliphaz's first speech is built from Scripture the canonical reader knows — and Hebrews 12 uses the same two texts.** `[T]` (synthetic)
  - **4:3–4 ‖ Isa 35:3.** "You have strengthened weak hands (יָדַיִם רָפוֹת תְּחַזֵּק) … the tottering (כּוֹשֵׁל) … feeble knees (בִרְכַּיִם … תְּאַמֵּץ)". Isa 35:3: "Encourage the exhausted (חַזְּקוּ יָדַיִם רָפוֹת), and strengthen the feeble (כֹּשְׁלוֹת). … knees (בִרְכַּיִם) … make firm (אַמֵּצוּ)". Six lemmas are shared (WLC).
  - **5:17 ‖ Prov 3:11–12.** מוּסָר ("discipline"), מָאַס ("despise") and יָכַח ("reprove") are all present, and Prov 3:13 opens with אַשְׁרֵי ("how blessed"), as Job 5:17 does.
  - **Heb 12:5–6 cites Prov 3:11–12, and Heb 12:12 cites Isa 35:3** ("strengthen the hands that are weak and the knees that are feeble").
  - *What it shows.* Hebrews gathers the same two lines Eliphaz joins, but applies them to sons whose suffering is discipline in love. Eliphaz applies them to a man whose suffering, the reader knows, is not discipline for sin. The words are right; the diagnosis is wrong.
  - *Surfaced by:* Tool 11 (Moves 1–3), Vocabulary, Move 4. *High on the words; moderate on the Hebrews pairing.* Swete's 4:3–4 does not reproduce Isa 35:3's Greek, so Hebrews reached the pairing through Isaiah and Proverbs, not through Job.
- **The vision's question becomes the book's recurring doctrinal question.** `[T]` (synthetic)
  - The voice in the night asks, הַאֱנוֹשׁ מֵאֱלוֹהַּ יִצְדָּק ("Can mankind be just before God?", 4:17).
  - The same lemmas, אֱנוֹשׁ ("man") and צדק ("be righteous"), return at 9:2 (Job), 15:14 (Eliphaz), 25:4 (Bildad) and 33:12 (Elihu). 9:2 and 25:4 share the exact clause וּמַה־יִּצְדַּק אֱנוֹשׁ עִם־אֵל ("how can a man be in the right with God?") (WLC).
  - *High confidence.*
- **Eliphaz rules out a heavenly mediator in his first speech.** `[T]`
  - "Call now, is there anyone who will answer you? And to which of the holy ones (קְדֹשִׁים) will you turn?" (5:1).
  - Job's later longing for an umpire, a witness and a Redeemer (9:33; 16:19; 19:25) is the answer to a door Eliphaz has shut.
  - *Moderate–high* (synthetic).

### 2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

- **Preceding movement.** Job has cursed his birth (3). Eliphaz is the first friend to speak, and presumably the senior (God addresses him in 42:7). `[T]` / `[I]`
- **Necessity answer.** The dialogue needs the doctrine stated at its gentlest and best before it hardens. Eliphaz's first speech is courteous (4:2), personal (4:3–6) and ends with a beautiful promise (5:17–26). Later speeches will be harsher (15; 22). `[I]`
- **Implication.** Preach this speech as the most persuasive version of the friends' case, which is why it is dangerous. `[I]`

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 4:2–6 | Gentle rebuke | You helped others; now you falter. "Is not your fear of God your confidence?" |
| 4:7–11 | The doctrine | The innocent do not perish; those who sow trouble reap it |
| 4:12–21 | The night vision | "Can mankind be just before God?" |
| 5:1–7 | The fool's fate | No holy one will answer; the fool's house cursed; man born to trouble |
| 5:8–16 | "I would seek God" | A hymn to God who reverses fortunes |
| 5:17–27 | The promise | Discipline, deliverance, peace — "we have investigated it" |

### 7. Vocabulary

- **יִרְאָתְךָ … וְתֹם דְּרָכֶיךָ ("your fear of God … the integrity of your ways", 4:6).** Eliphaz uses the words of 1:1 back at Job. תֹּם ("integrity") appears in Job only at 4:6 and 21:23; יִרְאָה ("fear") and תֹּם meet in one verse only here (WLC). `[T]`
- **תַּרְדֵּמָה ("deep sleep", 4:13).** The sleep of Adam's side (Gen 2:21) and Abram's covenant vision (Gen 15:12). Elihu repeats Eliphaz's whole clause, בִּנְפֹל תַּרְדֵּמָה עַל־אֲנָשִׁים ("when deep sleep falls on men", 33:15) — the exact phrase occurs only at these two verses (WLC). `[T]`
- **שֵׁמֶץ ("a whisper", 4:12).** Only here and 26:14 in the Hebrew Bible (lemma, WLC). Eliphaz boasts of the whisper his vision brought. Job replies that all creation tells only "a whisper (שֵׁמֶץ)" of God's ways. `[T]` *High on the words; moderate on the echo.*
- **עֲרוּמִים ("the shrewd", 5:12).** The adjective of the serpent (Gen 3:1). *Moderate.*
- **חקר ("to search out", 5:27).** "We have investigated it (חֲקַרְנוּהָ), and so it is." The verb returns at 28:27, where God "searched it out (חֲקָרָהּ)" — wisdom. `[T]` *Moderate–high.*
- **Proper-noun inventory.** None. The Temanite's name was dealt with in unit 2.

### 8. Translations

- **5:7 וּבְנֵי־רֶשֶׁף ("as sparks").** Literally "sons of Resheph", a flame word that is also a divine name elsewhere in the ancient Near East. `[S]` NASB95 and ESV "sparks"; NIV84 "sparks". *Uncertain* on any mythic freight.
- **Ancient versions check.** Swete's 5:13, ὁ καταλαμβάνων σοφοὺς ἐν τῇ φρονήσει ("who catches the wise in their prudence"), differs from 1 Cor 3:19's ὁ δρασσόμενος … ἐν τῇ πανουργίᾳ ("in their craftiness"). Paul's Greek is closer to the Hebrew בְּעָרְמָם ("in their shrewdness"). *Category 3* — the New Testament's own text, which follows the Hebrew here.
- **Pulpit divergence note.** N/A — no pulpit text declared.

### 10. Repetition

- **עָמָל ("trouble")** at 4:8; 5:6, 7 — the word Job used at 3:10. Eliphaz answers: those who sow עָמָל reap it (4:8); "man is born for trouble" (5:7). Job will call the friends מְנַחֲמֵי עָמָל ("sorry comforters", literally "comforters of trouble", 16:2). `[T]`
- **Lion words** (4:10–11): five different words for lions in two verses — אַרְיֵה, שַׁחַל, כְּפִירִים, לַיִשׁ, לָבִיא. Even the strongest predators are broken. `[T]`

### 11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

**Prov 3:11–12 → 5:17** *(high)*

- *Source context.* A father's instruction to a son (Prov 3:1–12), which ends: "do not reject the discipline of the LORD … for whom the LORD loves He reproves, even as a father corrects the son in whom he delights". Blessing follows (3:13).
- *Book usage.* The first use of Proverbs in Job.
- *OT-to-OT.* Heb 12:5–6 cites it and Heb 12:12 cites Isa 35:3 — the pair Eliphaz uses (above).
- *What it adds.* Proverbs grounds discipline in a father's love; Eliphaz omits "love" and "son" and keeps the reproof. `[T]` The word is true, but it is the wrong word for this sufferer.

**Isa 35:3 → 4:3–4** *(high on the words; direction open)*

- *Source context.* Isaiah 35 is the desert blooming and the redeemed returning to Zion; 35:3–4 is the charge to strengthen the weak because "your God will come … He will save you".
- *Book usage.* The first use of Isaiah in Job; Isa 41:20 (12:9) follows.
- *What it adds.* Eliphaz grants that Job once did Isaiah's work. His rebuke is "physician, heal yourself".

**Deut 32:39 → 5:18** *(high on the words)*

- *Source context.* The Song of Moses: "I put to death and give life. I have wounded (מָחַצְתִּי) and I heal (אֶרְפָּא)" (Deut 32:39). מחץ with רפא occurs only at Deut 32:39 and Job 5:18 (WLC).
- *What it adds.* Eliphaz's gentlest line quotes the covenant God's self-description. `[T]` / `[I]`

**Internal (Move 4).**

- *Answers §earlier.* 4:6 turns Job's character words (1:1) against him. `[T]`
- *Planted for later:*
  - **4:17** → 9:2; 15:14; 25:4; 33:12 (the righteousness question). *High.*
  - **4:13** → 33:15 (Elihu quoting Eliphaz). *High.*
  - **4:12 שֵׁמֶץ** → 26:14. *Moderate–high.*
  - **5:1 "which of the holy ones?"** → 9:33; 16:19; 19:25; 33:23. *Moderate–high.*
  - **5:9** → 9:10 (Job quotes Eliphaz's doxology almost verbatim). *High.*
  - **5:22 "you will laugh (תִּשְׂחָק) at violence"** → the laughter thread (12:4; 30:1; 39:7, 18, 22; 41:29 [Heb 41:21]). *Moderate.*
  - **5:26 "like the stacking (גָּדִישׁ) of grain"** → 21:32, where Job says the wicked man is carried to the grave with honour "and men keep watch over his tomb (גָּדִישׁ)". The word occurs in Job only at these two verses. *Moderate.*
  - **5:27 "we have investigated it"** → 28:27 (God searched out wisdom). *Moderate–high.*

### 16. So What? (brief)

- **Intended response (Eliphaz's).** Accept discipline and hope. **The book's.** Beware true words used to close a case the speaker does not know.
- **Candidate FCF.** The comforter's itch to explain.

### Passage-specific difficulties

- **Quoting Eliphaz.** *Weaponised.* 4:7–8 and 5:17 are regularly quoted as the Bible's teaching. They are Scripture's record of a wrong application (42:7). See Preaching Pitfalls.

## Unit 5 — 6:1–7:21 What Is Man, That You Watch Him?

> 7:17 מָה־אֱנוֹשׁ כִּי תְגַדְּלֶנּוּ וְכִי־תָשִׁית אֵלָיו לִבֶּךָ
> 7:18 וַתִּפְקְדֶנּוּ לִבְקָרִים לִרְגָעִים תִּבְחָנֶנּוּ
> "What is man that You magnify him, and that You are concerned about him, that You examine him every morning and try him every moment?" *(Job, NASB95)*

### Headline Findings

- **Job turns from the friends to God — and from here on he speaks to God, which no friend ever does.** `[T]`
  - 6:1–30 is addressed to the friends. From 7:7 (זְכֹר, "Remember", singular) Job addresses God directly to the end of the chapter.
  - This is the first of the second-person turns to God listed at sweep level (Headline Finding 2).
  - *Surfaced by:* Structure, Linking Words, Move 4. *High confidence.*
- **Job turns Psalm 8's wonder into a complaint of surveillance.** `[T]`
  - Ps 8:5 (Eng 8:4): "What is man (מָה־אֱנוֹשׁ כִּי) that You take thought of him, and the son of man that You care for him (תִפְקְדֶנּוּ)?"
  - Job 7:17–18 has מָה־אֱנוֹשׁ כִּי and וַתִּפְקְדֶנּוּ ("that You examine him") — the same formula and the same verb, with "morning by morning … every moment" turning care into scrutiny. Eliphaz reuses the formula at 15:14.
  - *Surfaced by:* Tool 11, Vocabulary, Tone. *High confidence.*
- **Job asks God for forgiveness in the words of God's own name.** `[T]` / `[I]`
  - "Why then do You not pardon (תִשָּׂא) my transgression (פִשְׁעִי) and take away my iniquity (עֲוֺנִי)?" (7:21).
  - נָשָׂא ("lift, forgive") with פֶּשַׁע ("transgression") and עָוֹן ("iniquity") stands at Exod 34:7 (the LORD "who forgives iniquity, transgression and sin"), Num 14:18, Mic 7:18 and Ps 32:5 (lemma, WLC).
  - Job is not claiming sinlessness. He asks why the forgiving God does not forgive.
  - *Moderate–high.*

### 2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

- **Preceding movement.** Eliphaz has told Job to accept discipline and has promised restoration (5:17–27). `[T]`
- **Necessity answer.** Job must reject the diagnosis without rejecting God, and must find a new addressee when the friends fail. The unit exists so that the turn to God — the book's central act — happens early, in the first reply. `[I]`

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 6:1–7 | The weight of grief | "Oh that my grief were weighed"; the arrows of the Almighty |
| 6:8–13 | A death-wish | "Would that God were willing to crush me" — and the comfort of not having denied the Holy One |
| 6:14–23 | Friends like a wadi | Streams that vanish in summer; the caravans of Tema and Sheba disappointed |
| 6:24–30 | "Teach me" | Show me my error; look me in the face |
| 7:1–6 | Hard service | Man's life a conscript's term, a hireling's day; nights of trouble |
| 7:7–21 | To God | "Remember"; am I the sea?; what is man?; leave me alone; why not pardon? |

### 7. Vocabulary

- **חֶסֶד ("kindness", 6:14).** "For the despairing man there should be kindness from his friend." One of only three occurrences in Job (6:14; 10:12; 37:13; lemma, WLC). Job asks the friends for the covenant loyalty they withhold. `[T]`
- **צָבָא ("hard service", 7:1).** The conscript's term. It recurs at 14:14: "All the days of my struggle (צְבָאִי) I will wait until my change comes." `[T]`
- **הֶבֶל ("breath", 7:16).** "My days are but a breath." One of five in Job (7:16; 9:29; 21:34; 27:12; 35:16; lemma, WLC). The Ecclesiastes keyword, used by Job of his own life. `[T]`
- **נֹצֵר הָאָדָם ("watcher of men", 7:20).** A title of God's care (Prov 24:12 uses the verb of God keeping the soul) turned into surveillance. *Moderate.*
- **Proper-noun inventory.** Tema, Sheba (6:19) — caravan peoples of Abraham's wider kin (Gen 25:3, 15). Scenery in the image. The Holy One (6:10) — the divine title Isaiah favours. *Uncertain* as a link.

### 8. Translations

- **7:20 "Have I sinned?"** Hebrew חָטָאתִי ("I have sinned"), probably conditional: "If I have sinned, what have I done to You?" NASB95 renders it as a question; ESV "If I sin"; NIV84 "If I have sinned". No divergence bearing on a finding.
- **Ancient versions check.** Swete's 7:17, τί γάρ ἐστιν ἄνθρωπος ὅτι ἐμεγάλυνας αὐτόν ("what is man, that You magnified him"), keeps the Psalm 8 formula. Heb 2:6 quotes Ps 8:5 LXX in its own form. No New Testament use of Job 7:17. The Greek carries the echo.
- **Pulpit divergence note.** N/A — no pulpit text declared.

### 10. Repetition

- **Hope and its absence.** תִּקְוָה ("hope") at 6:8 ("my hope") and 7:6 ("without hope"). The word occurs 13 times in Job — 13 of 34 in the Hebrew Bible (lemma, WLC). This is its first appearance in Job's mouth. `[T]`
- **"Life is breath":** רוּחַ חַיָּי ("my life is but breath", 7:7) and הֶבֶל יָמָי ("my days are but a breath", 7:16). `[T]`
- **Bitterness of soul.** "I will complain in the bitterness of my soul" (7:11). מַר ("bitter") with נֶפֶשׁ ("soul") recurs at 3:20; 10:1; 21:25 (WLC). `[T]`

### 11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

**Ps 8:5 (Eng 8:4) → 7:17–18** *(high)*

- *Source context.* Psalm 8 praises the LORD whose name is majestic in all the earth, and marvels that he crowned man with glory and gave him dominion (Ps 8:6–9). The question in v.5 is wonder.
- *Book usage.* The first use of the Psalter in Job. Ps 8 returns at 19:9 (glory and crown stripped) and 40:10 (God invites Job to put on הוֹד וְהָדָר, "honour and majesty" — הָדָר is Ps 8:6's word) (units 9, 16). **(synthetic)**
- *OT-to-OT.* Ps 144:3 asks a similar question with different words (מָה־אָדָם … וַתֵּדָעֵהוּ). It is **not** the source of Job 7:17 (see the overview).
- *What it adds.* Job's parody is not unbelief. It is the psalm read from the ash-heap: God's attention, which the psalm calls glory, feels to Job like a guard on the sea (7:12).

**Exod 34:7 → 7:21** *(moderate–high)*

- *Source context.* The LORD proclaims his name to Moses: compassionate, gracious, "forgiving iniquity, transgression and sin".
- *What it adds.* Job knows the name even though he does not use it here. His complaint appeals to God's own character.

**Internal (Move 4).**

- *Answers §earlier.* 6:14 (friends owe חֶסֶד) answers 2:11 (they came to comfort). `[T]`
- *Planted for later:*
  - **7:12 "Am I the sea, or the sea monster, that You set a guard over me?"** → 38:8–11. God will speak of the sea as a newborn swaddled in cloud, with bars and doors and "thus far you shall come". The guard Job resents is the boundary God sets. *Moderate–high.*
  - **7:17** → 15:14 (Eliphaz reuses the formula). *High.*
  - **7:21 "You will seek me (וְשִׁחַרְתַּנִי), but I will not be"** → 8:5, where Bildad answers "If you would seek (תְּשַׁחֵר) God". The same verb, reversed: Job fears God will look for him too late; Bildad says Job must look for God. *High on the words.*
  - **7:1 צָבָא** → 14:14. *High.*
  - **7:18 "try him (תִּבְחָנֶנּוּ)"** → 23:10 "When He has tried me (בְּחָנַנִי), I shall come forth as gold". The same verb, from complaint to confidence. *Moderate–high.*

### 16. So What? (brief)

- **Intended response.** Complain *to* God, not about him to others.
- **Candidate FCF.** The sense that God's attention is oppressive.
- **Prayer.** Job 7:21, prayed on this side of the cross, where the answer is given.

### Passage-specific difficulties

- **6:8–9 "Would that God were willing to crush me".** *Pastoral.* As at chapter 3: a longing for death spoken to God, not an act. Handle as there.

## Unit 6 — 8:1–10:22 ⭐ Bildad, and "No Umpire Between Us"

> 9:2 אָמְנָם יָדַעְתִּי כִי־כֵן וּמַה־יִּצְדַּק אֱנוֹשׁ עִם־אֵל
> "In truth I know that this is so; but how can a man be in the right before God?"
>
> 9:33 לֹא יֵשׁ־בֵּינֵינוּ מוֹכִיחַ יָשֵׁת יָדוֹ עַל־שְׁנֵינוּ
> "There is no umpire between us, who may lay his hand upon us both." *(Job, NASB95)*

### Headline Findings

- **Job takes Eliphaz's question and turns it into a legal one.** `[T]`
  - Eliphaz's vision asked whether a mortal can be righteous before God (4:17). Job agrees — "In truth I know that this is so" — and asks it again, "how can a man be in the right (יִצְדַּק) before God?" (9:2).
  - He answers with courtroom language: "If one wished to dispute (לָרִיב) with Him, he could not answer Him once in a thousand times (אַחַת מִנִּי־אָלֶף)" (9:3). צדק is now "to win a case", not "to be morally pure".
  - *Surfaced by:* Vocabulary, Repetition, Move 4. *High confidence.*
- **The first of the mediator texts is a lament over an absence.** `[T]`
  - "There is no מוֹכִיחַ ('umpire') between us, who may lay his hand upon us both" (9:33).
  - The participle מוֹכִיחַ occurs in Job only here, at 32:12 (Elihu: "there was no one who refuted Job") and at 40:2 (God: "Let him who reproves God answer it"). God gives Job the title Job said no one could hold.
  - BHS records the reading לוּ ("would that") in some manuscripts, the Greek and the Syriac, which turns the denial into a wish (*as BHS prints it*; spread unchecked).
  - *Surfaced by:* Vocabulary, Tool 8, Move 4, Christological Reading. *High confidence on the words.*
- **Bildad says God does not pervert justice; Job says God has perverted his; Elihu repeats Bildad.** `[T]` (synthetic)
  - הַאֵל יְעַוֵּת מִשְׁפָּט ("Does God pervert justice?", 8:3). עות ("to pervert, bend") is used twice in the verse.
  - Job: "Know then that God has wronged me (עִוְּתָנִי)" (19:6).
  - Elihu: "The Almighty will not pervert (יְעַוֵּת) justice" (34:12), echoing 8:3 almost verbatim.
  - The lexeme occurs in Job only in these three verses. Lam 3:36 uses it of what "the Lord does not approve" (lemma, WLC).
  - *High confidence.*

### 2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

- **Preceding movement.** Job has turned to God and asked why he is watched (7:17–21). `[T]`
- **Necessity answer.** Bildad must speak next, because Job's complaint touches God's justice, and Bildad is the defender of it (8:3). Job's reply then escalates from complaint to lawsuit (9:3). The legal frame that governs the rest of the book begins here, and so does the longing for a mediator that runs to 33:23. `[I]`
- **Implication.** Chapters 9–10 are where Job's problem becomes a problem of *access*: he has a case and no court. `[I]`

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 8:2–7 | Bildad: God is just | Your sons sinned; seek God and your end will increase greatly |
| 8:8–19 | Ask the fathers | The papyrus without water; the spider's web |
| 8:20–22 | God will not reject the blameless | "He will yet fill your mouth with laughter" |
| 9:2–13 | Job: who can contend with Him? | A dark doxology of power (9:4–13) |
| 9:14–24 | Innocent, yet condemned | "Though I am righteous, my mouth will condemn me"; "He destroys the guiltless and the wicked" |
| 9:25–35 | No umpire | Days swift; washing in snow useless; no arbiter |
| 10:1–22 | To God: why? | You made me — why destroy me? Why the womb? Leave me a little |

### 7. Vocabulary

- **תָּם ("blameless").** Bildad: "God will not reject a man of integrity (תָּם)" (8:20). Job, three times in three verses: "I am guiltless (תָּם)" (9:20, 21); "He destroys the guiltless (תָּם) and the wicked" (9:22). Job's epithet from 1:1 is now contested ground. `[T]`
- **מוֹכִיחַ ("umpire, arbiter").** The hiphil participle of יכח — one who decides between parties. יכח occurs 17 times in Job (lemma, WLC), from Eliphaz's "reproves" (5:17) to God's "reprover" (40:2). `[T]`
- **חֶסֶד ("lovingkindness", 10:12).** "You have granted me life and lovingkindness." Job confesses God's past covenant kindness in the middle of his accusation. `[T]`
- **סכך ("to weave, knit together", 10:11).** "You clothe me with skin and flesh, and knit me together (תְּסֹכְכֵנִי) with bones and sinews." Ps 139:13 uses the same verb: "You wove me (תְּסֻכֵּנִי) in my mother's womb (בְּבֶטֶן)" (lemma, WLC). The same lexeme is "hedged in" at 3:23 and "enclosed" at 38:8. `[T]`
- **Proper-noun inventory.** Rahab (9:13, see Tool 11); the Bear, Orion, the Pleiades and "the chambers of the south" (9:9). The constellations return at 38:31 (below). No other names.

### 8. Translations

- **9:33.** NASB95 "There is no umpire"; ESV "There is no arbiter"; **NIV84 "If only there were someone to arbitrate"**, following the לוּ reading. **Pulpit note:** a congregation hearing NIV84 hears a wish; one hearing ESV hears a denial. Both are defensible; say which one the text in front of them is.
- **9:17 "tempest".** Hebrew שְׂעָרָה, a by-form of סְעָרָה ("whirlwind", 38:1). Cognate, not one lemma (WLC). NASB95 "tempest" hides the echo; so do ESV and NIV84.
- **Ancient versions check.** Swete's 9:33 has εἴθε ἦν ὁ μεσίτης ἡμῶν ("would that our mediator were…"), following the wish reading and introducing μεσίτης, the word of 1 Tim 2:5. *Category 2 plus category 3*: a real variant (לוּ / לֹא) whose Greek rendering the New Testament's vocabulary later uses.

### 9. Tone and Feel

Chapter 9 is a hymn sung through clenched teeth. The doxology of 9:4–13 has the grammar of praise — participles of divine action, "who removes mountains … who shakes the earth … who alone stretches out the heavens" — and the content of dread. Chapter 10 turns tender and accusing at once ("Your hands fashioned and made me … yet You would destroy me", 10:8). `[T]`

### 10. Repetition

- **צדק and רשׁע ("be in the right" / "be condemned").** 9:2, 15, 20 (צדק); 9:20, 29; 10:2, 7, 15 (רשׁע) (lemma, WLC). The verdict-words dominate. `[T]`
- **"Answer" (ענה).** 9:3, 14, 15, 16, 32 — Job cannot answer God, and God would not answer him. `[T]`
- **The womb (בֶּטֶן, רֶחֶם).** 10:18–19 return to chapter 3's question: "Why then have You brought me out of the womb (מֵרֶחֶם)?" `[T]`

### 11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

**Amos 4:13; 5:8 → 9:8–9** *(moderate–high)*

- *Source context.* Amos's doxologies interrupt oracles of judgement: God "treads on the high places of the earth (דֹרֵךְ עַל־בָּמֳתֵי אָרֶץ)" (Amos 4:13); he "made the Pleiades and Orion (כִּימָה וּכְסִיל)" (5:8). Both praise the Judge who is coming.
- *The words.* Job 9:8 has "treads on the waves of the sea (דוֹרֵךְ עַל־בָּמֳתֵי יָם)"; 9:9 names the Pleiades and Orion. כִּימָה ("Pleiades") occurs three times in the Hebrew Bible — Amos 5:8; Job 9:9; 38:31 (lemma, WLC).
- *Book usage.* The first link to Amos; the constellations return in God's speech (38:31).
- *OT-to-OT.* Amos uses creation hymns as the frame for judgement; Job uses one as the frame for his own ruin.
- *What it adds.* Job's hymn is orthodox prophetic doxology. The problem is not his theology of God's power but what that power is doing to him.

**Rahab — Isa 51:9; Ps 89:11 [Eng 89:10] → 9:13** *(moderate)*

- *Source context.* Rahab, the sea-monster God cut in pieces, stands for the powers God defeated at creation and at the Red Sea (Isa 51:9–10).
- *Lemma note.* Job's רַהַב (9:13; 26:12) is filed in the WLC index under a different lemma number from Isa 51:9, Ps 87:4 and Ps 89:11. **Both numbers were searched**; the word is the same.
- *What it adds.* If Rahab's helpers crouch beneath God, what chance has Job? `[T]` / `[I]`

**Exod 33:22; 1 Kgs 19:11 → 9:11** *(moderate, Move 1 only)*

- God "passes by" (עבר) Moses and Elijah in self-revelation (Exod 33:22; 1 Kgs 19:11). Job: "Were He to pass by (יַעֲבֹר) me, I would not see Him" (9:11). What was revelation for Moses is concealment for Job. `[I]`
- **Forward link (Greek).** Swete's 9:8 has God "walking on the sea as on dry ground", and 9:11 "if he passes by (παρέλθῃ) me". Mark 6:48 has Jesus "walking on the sea; and He intended to pass by (παρελθεῖν) them". *Category 3*, via the Greek. *Moderate.*

**Internal (Move 4).**

- *Answers §earlier:*
  - 9:2 takes up 4:17. *High.*
  - 9:10 quotes 5:9 almost verbatim: עֹשֶׂה גְדֹלוֹת … וְנִפְלָאוֹת עַד־אֵין מִסְפָּר ("who does great things … wonders without number"). The phrase נִפְלָאוֹת עַד־אֵין מִסְפָּר occurs only at these two verses (WLC). Eliphaz's doxology of comfort becomes Job's doxology of terror. *High.*
  - 10:18 returns to 3:11. *High.*
- *Planted for later:*
  - **9:3 "once in a thousand" (אַחַת מִנִּי־אָלֶף)** → Elihu's "one out of a thousand" (אֶחָד מִנִּי־אָלֶף, 33:23), the mediator-angel. The phrase occurs only at these two verses. *High.*
  - **9:9 constellations** → 38:31. *High.*
  - **9:17 tempest** → 38:1; 40:6. *Moderate* (by-forms).
  - **9:33 umpire** → 16:19–21; 19:25; 33:23; and the title in 40:2. *High.*
  - **10:2 "Do not condemn me (אַל־תַּרְשִׁיעֵנִי)"** → 40:8, "Will you condemn Me (תַּרְשִׁיעֵנִי)?" *High.*
  - **10:9 "You have made me as clay (כַחֹמֶר)"** → Elihu, "I too have been formed out of the clay (מֵחֹמֶר)" (33:6); God, "changed like clay (כְּחֹמֶר) under the seal" (38:14). *Moderate.*
  - **10:17 "You renew Your witnesses (עֵדֶיךָ) against me"** → 16:19, "my witness (עֵדִי) is in heaven". The witnesses change sides. *High.*

### 16. So What? (brief)

- **Intended response.** To feel the need for a mediator before being told of one.
- **Worldview.** Before God no one can win a case by argument (9:2–3). The book will not answer this with a better argument.
- **Candidate FCF.** Standing before God with no one to stand between.

### Christological note

- **9:32–33.** "For He is not a man as I am that I may answer Him … There is no umpire between us, who may lay his hand upon us both." Job describes, as an impossibility, a mediator who shares both natures — "not a man as I am", yet able to lay a hand on both parties.
- The New Testament's μεσίτης ("mediator") is "the man Christ Jesus" (1 Tim 2:5). Swete's 9:33 already has μεσίτης.
- **Trajectory, not prophecy.** Job describes a need; he does not foretell its answer. *High* on the trajectory.
- **10:4–5.** "Have You eyes of flesh? … Are Your days as the days of a mortal?" The incarnation answers the question Job thought was absurd. *Moderate* — a canonical reflection, not a claim that the text anticipates it.

### Passage-specific difficulties

- **8:4 "If your sons sinned against Him…".** *Pastoral.* Bildad attributes the children's deaths to their sin. The reader knows otherwise (1:5, 8). Never let this verse be used of a bereaved family.
- **9:22–24 "He destroys the guiltless and the wicked … He mocks the despair of the innocent."** *Doctrinal / ethical.* Job's accusation of God. The book includes it and later commends Job's speech (42:7) while rebuking his words "without knowledge" (38:2). Preach it as honest lament, not as doctrine.

## Unit 7 — 11:1–14:22 Zophar, and "If a Man Dies, Will He Live Again?"

> 13:15 הֵן יִקְטְלֵנִי לא [לוֹ] אֲיַחֵל אַךְ־דְּרָכַי אֶל־פָּנָיו אוֹכִיחַ
> "Though He slay me, I will hope in Him. Nevertheless I will argue my ways before Him."
>
> 14:14 אִם־יָמוּת גֶּבֶר הֲיִחְיֶה כָּל־יְמֵי צְבָאִי אֲיַחֵל עַד־בּוֹא חֲלִיפָתִי
> "If a man dies, will he live again? All the days of my struggle I will wait until my change comes." *(Job, NASB95)*

### Headline Findings

- **Zophar's wish that God would speak comes true — against Zophar.** `[T]` / `[I]`
  - "But would that God might speak (מִי־יִתֵּן אֱלוֹהַּ דַּבֵּר), and open His lips against you" (11:5).
  - God does speak (38:1), and his anger falls on Zophar and the other two (42:7).
  - מִי יִתֵּן ("oh that") is the book's formula of longing. Job holds 9 of the 19 verses in the Hebrew Bible with the exact phrase (phrase search, WLC), plus 29:2 with a suffix.
  - *Surfaced by:* Move 4, Vocabulary, Narrator's verdict. *High on the words.*
- **Job asks the resurrection question and answers it with a tree.** `[T]` (synthetic)
  - "For there is hope (תִּקְוָה) for a tree, when it is cut down, that it will sprout again (יַחֲלִיף)" (14:7). "But man … lies down and does not rise … they will not awake (יָקִיצוּ)" (14:10–12). "If a man dies, will he live again? … I will wait until my change (חֲלִיפָתִי) comes" (14:14).
  - The tree's "sprouting again" (verb חלף) and Job's "change" (noun חֲלִיפָה) are cognate (WLC). Job hopes that man might be like the tree after all.
  - **The canon later answers with Job's own verb.** קיץ ("to awake") stands at Job 14:12 ("they will not awake"), and at Dan 12:2 ("many of those who sleep in the dust of the ground will awake") and Isa 26:19 ("awake and shout for joy, you who lie in the dust") — both of which also have עָפָר ("dust") (lemma, WLC).
  - *Surfaced by:* Vocabulary, Repetition, Tool 11, Christological Reading. *High on the words; moderate on the trajectory.*
- **Job warns the friends that God will judge their partiality on his behalf — which is exactly what happens.** `[T]`
  - "Will you show partiality (תִּשָּׂאוּן) for Him? … He will surely reprove you if you secretly show partiality (פָּנִים תִּשָּׂאוּן)" (13:8, 10).
  - At 42:7–9 God is angry with the friends and says of Job, "I will accept him (פָּנָיו אֶשָּׂא)" — the same idiom, נשׂא פנים ("lift the face"). See the Echo Table.
  - *High.*

### 2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

- **Preceding movement.** Job has declared himself innocent and longed for an umpire (9–10). `[T]`
- **Necessity answer.** Zophar, the bluntest friend, must now say what the others imply: "Know then that God forgets a part of your iniquity" (11:6). Job's longest speech of the first cycle (12–14) then closes the round by turning from the friends to God and to death itself. The first cycle must end at the grave, because every later hope in Job (16:19; 19:25) is a hope against it. `[I]`

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 11:2–12 | Zophar: God's wisdom | Would that God would speak; can you find out the depths of God? |
| 11:13–20 | Repent and be restored | "Your life would be brighter than noonday" |
| 12:2–25 | Job: I know as much as you | Ask the beasts; with God are wisdom and might — to destroy |
| 13:1–19 | I will argue with God | You smear with lies; will you show partiality for God? "Though He slay me…" |
| 13:20–28 | To God: two requests | Remove Your hand; tell me my sins |
| 14:1–22 | Man born of woman | Few days; hope for a tree, not for man; hide me in Sheol; "will he live again?" |

### 7. Vocabulary

- **תַּעֲלֻמוֹת ("secrets", 11:6).** Zophar wishes God would show Job "the secrets of wisdom". The word occurs three times in the Hebrew Bible: Job 11:6; 28:11; Ps 44:22 [Eng 44:21] (lemma, WLC). At 28:11 man brings "what is hidden (תַעֲלֻמָהּ)" to light by mining — but not wisdom. `[T]` *Moderate–high.*
- **חֵקֶר and תַּכְלִית ("depths", "limit", 11:7).** "Can you discover the depths (חֵקֶר) of God? Can you discover the limits (תַּכְלִית) of the Almighty?" Job has 7 of the 12 occurrences of חֵקֶר (lemma, WLC). God later asks, "Have you … walked in the recesses (חֵקֶר) of the deep?" (38:16). תַּכְלִית recurs at 26:10 and 28:3. `[T]`
- **צַדִּיק תָּמִים ("the just and blameless", 12:4).** Job's description of himself as a laughingstock is Noah's epithet (Gen 6:9), and the pair stands nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible (WLC). Ezekiel names Noah and Job together (Ezek 14:14, 20). `[T]` *Moderate–high* on intention.
- **יְלוּד אִשָּׁה ("born of woman", 14:1).** Job's phrase, reused by Eliphaz (15:14) and Bildad (25:4) — only these three verses in the Hebrew Bible (WLC). Swete's 14:1 γεννητὸς γυναικός is the phrase of Matt 11:11 ("among those born of women"). `[T]`
- **חֲלִיפָה ("change, relief", 14:14).** Also 10:17 ("hardship after hardship", literally "changes"). Cognate with חלף ("to sprout again", 14:7). `[T]`

### 8. Translations

- **13:15 — the ketiv and the qere.** The written text reads לא ("not"): "Behold, He will slay me; I have no hope." The Masoretic reading tradition reads לוֹ ("for Him"): "Though He slay me, I will hope in Him." BHS notes that many manuscripts and the versions read with the qere (*as BHS prints it*; spread unchecked).
  - NASB95, ESV and NIV84 all follow the qere; NIV84's margin gives the ketiv.
  - The second half is unaffected: "Nevertheless I will argue (אוֹכִיחַ) my ways before Him." In both readings the verse is defiant, not merely submissive.
  - Swete reads neither: "If the Mighty One lays hands on me, … I will speak and argue before Him."
  - **Pulpit note:** the most-quoted line in Job rests on a qere. Preach it, but preach the whole verse.
- **13:16 "This also will be my salvation".** Swete's καὶ τοῦτό μοι ἀποβήσεται εἰς σωτηρίαν is quoted exactly in Phil 1:19. *Category 3.*
- **Pulpit divergence note.** N/A — no pulpit text declared.

### 10. Repetition

- **Hope.** תִּקְוָה at 11:18, 20 (Zophar); 14:7, 19 (Job) (lemma, WLC). Zophar promises "there is hope"; Job says there is hope for a tree, and that God destroys "man's hope" (14:19). `[T]`
- **Call and answer.** "Then call, and I will answer" (13:22); "You will call, and I will answer You" (14:15). God reverses the order at 38:3: "I will ask you, and you instruct Me." `[T]`
- **"Wisdom" (חָכְמָה).** 11:6 (Zophar); 12:2, 12, 13; 13:5 (Job). "O that you would be completely silent, and that it would become your wisdom!" (13:5). `[T]`

### 11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

**Isa 41:20 → 12:9** *(high)*

- *Source context.* Isaiah 41:17–20: God will open rivers for the poor and plant the wilderness with trees "that they may see and recognise … that the hand of the LORD has done this". Salvation.
- *Book usage.* Isaiah is live in Job (Isa 35:3 at 4:3–4, unit 4).
- *What it adds.* Job's יַד־יְהוָה עָשְׂתָה זֹּאת ("the hand of the LORD has done this") is the four-word phrase verbatim, and **the dialogue's only use of the covenant name** (lemma, WLC). In Isaiah, what creation learns is salvation; in Job, it is ruin. `[T]` / `[I]`

**Ps 107:40 → 12:21, 24** *(high)*

- *Source context.* Psalm 107 praises God who rescues the wandering, the imprisoned, the sick and the storm-tossed; v.40 celebrates his overturning of princes.
- *What it adds.* Job quotes the psalm's verse in two halves (12:21a; 12:24b) inside a hymn to God's destructive sovereignty (12:13–25). He knows the Psalter, and reads it without its refrain of "Let them give thanks to the LORD for His lovingkindness". `[I]`

**Isa 11:2 → 12:13** *(moderate)*

- *Source context.* The shoot of Jesse, on whom rests "the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and strength, the spirit of knowledge and the fear of the LORD".
- *The words.* Job 12:13: "With Him are wisdom (חָכְמָה) and might (גְבוּרָה); to Him belong counsel (עֵצָה) and understanding (תְבוּנָה)". חָכְמָה, גְבוּרָה and עֵצָה meet in one verse only in Isa 11:2 and Job 12:13 (lemma, WLC).
- *What it adds.* The qualities Job ascribes to God in a hymn of terror are the qualities Isaiah gives the coming King. *Moderate as a canonical conversation;* not a claim of borrowing.

**Isa 40:6–8; Ps 103:15 → 14:2** *(moderate, Move 1 only)*

- "Like a flower (צִיץ) he comes forth and withers." Isaiah and the psalm use the flower of human frailty to set up God's everlasting word and mercy. Job uses it with neither.

**Dan 12:2; Isa 26:19 → 14:12** *(moderate — canonical answer, not allusion)*

- Job: "they will not awake (לֹא יָקִיצוּ)". Dan 12:2: "many … will awake (יָקִיצוּ)"; Isa 26:19: "awake (הָקִיצוּ) … you who lie in the dust". The later texts answer Job's denial with Job's verb.

**Internal (Move 4).**

- *Answers §earlier:*
  - 13:4 "worthless physicians (רֹפְאֵי)" answers Eliphaz's God who "heals (תִּרְפֶּינָה)" (5:18). רפא occurs in Job only at these two verses. *Moderate–high.*
  - 14:1 answers 4:17–19's frailty with Job's own. *Moderate.*
- *Planted for later:*
  - **11:5 "would that God might speak"** → 38:1; 42:7. *High.*
  - **11:7 חֵקֶר** → 38:16. *Moderate–high.*
  - **11:12 the wild donkey (פֶּרֶא)** → 39:5, where God sets the wild donkey free. *Moderate.*
  - **12:7 "ask the beasts (בְהֵמוֹת) … the birds of the heavens"** → Elihu, "who teaches us more than the beasts (בַּהֲמוֹת) of the earth and makes us wiser than the birds of the heavens" (35:11) → God, "Behold now, Behemoth (בְהֵמוֹת)" (40:15). *Moderate–high* (synthetic).
  - **13:7–10 partiality** → 42:7–9. *High.*
  - **13:18 "I have prepared my case (עָרַכְתִּי מִשְׁפָּט)"** → 23:4, "I would present my case (אֶעֶרְכָה … מִשְׁפָּט)" — the only two verses with the phrase. *High.*
  - **13:22; 14:15 call and answer** → 38:3; 40:7; 42:4. *High.*
  - **13:24, 27** → quoted by Elihu at 33:10–11. *High.*
  - **14:1 "born of woman"** → 15:14; 25:4. *High.*
  - **14:7 hope for a tree** → 19:10, "He has uprooted my hope (תִּקְוָתִי) like a tree (כָּעֵץ)". *High.*

### 16. So What? (brief)

- **Intended response.** Keep speaking to God even when hope is at its thinnest (13:15b; 14:13–15).
- **Candidate FCF.** The fear that death ends everything (14:10–12).
- **Prayer.** 14:13–15, prayed this side of the resurrection.

### Passage-specific difficulties

- **13:15 as a slogan.** *Weaponised / textual.* See Translations.
- **14:12 against resurrection.** *Apologetic.* Job's denial is honest lament, and the canon answers it (Dan 12:2; Isa 26:19; 1 Cor 15). Don't make the verse a doctrine, and don't smooth it away.

## Unit 8 — 15:1–17:16 ⭐ "My Witness Is in Heaven"

> 16:19 גַּם־עַתָּה הִנֵּה־בַשָּׁמַיִם עֵדִי וְשָׂהֲדִי בַּמְּרוֹמִים
> "Even now, behold, my witness is in heaven, and my advocate is on high."
>
> 16:21 וְיוֹכַח לְגֶבֶר עִם־אֱלוֹהַּ וּבֶן־אָדָם לְרֵעֵהוּ
> "O that a man might plead with God as a man with his neighbour!" *(Job, NASB95)*

### Headline Findings

- **The witness in heaven does the work of the umpire Job said did not exist.** `[T]` (synthetic)
  - At 9:33 there was no מוֹכִיחַ ("umpire"). At 16:21 Job hopes the witness will וְיוֹכַח ("plead") for a man with God — the same verb, יכח (lemma, WLC). The same verse calls the one pleaded for בֶּן־אָדָם ("a son of man"), which NASB95 renders "a man".
  - And the witnesses God "renewed against me" (10:17, עֵדֶיךָ) now become "my witness (עֵדִי)".
  - *Surfaced by:* Vocabulary, Move 4, Christological Reading. *High on the words.*
- **Job's protest of innocence uses the exact words of Isaiah's servant.** `[T]`
  - עַל לֹא־חָמָס בְּכַפָּי ("although there is no violence in my hands", 16:17) and Isa 53:9 עַל לֹא־חָמָס עָשָׂה ("because he had done no violence").
  - The four-word phrase עַל לֹא־חָמָס occurs only in these two verses (WLC). In the same chapter Job is struck on the cheek (16:10) and handed over to the ungodly (16:11).
  - *Surfaced by:* Tool 11, Vocabulary, Christological Reading. *High on the words; direction of dependence open.*
- **Job asks the earth not to cover his blood — the Abel cry.** `[T]`
  - "O earth, do not cover my blood (אֶרֶץ אַל־תְּכַסִּי דָמִי), and let there be no resting place for my cry" (16:18).
  - Isa 26:21: "the earth will reveal her bloodshed and will no longer cover (תְכַסֶּה) her slain". Gen 4:10: "the voice of your brother's blood is crying (צֹעֲקִים) to Me from the ground". כסה with דָּם and אֶרֶץ are shared with Isa 26:21 (lemma, WLC). The Genesis link is conceptual; the cry-verbs are cognate spellings (צעק / זעק).
  - Heb 12:24: Jesus' blood "speaks better than the blood of Abel".
  - *Moderate–high.*

### 2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

- **Preceding movement.** The first cycle ended at the grave (14). `[T]`
- **Necessity answer.** The second cycle must harden the friends and deepen Job. Eliphaz now attacks Job's יִרְאָה ("fear of God", 15:4), and Job reaches beyond the friends and beyond death for a witness in heaven. The unit exists so that hope is found at the point of greatest isolation — "My friends are my scoffers; my eye weeps to God" (16:20). `[I]`

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 15:2–16 | Eliphaz: your mouth condemns you | You do away with the fear of God; were you the first man? what is man? |
| 15:17–35 | The wicked man's fate | Terror, darkness, the sword; he stretched out his hand against God |
| 16:2–6 | Job: sorry comforters | "I too could speak like you" |
| 16:7–17 | God the assailant | He tore me, handed me over, set me up as His target — though no violence is in my hands |
| 16:18–22 | Earth, do not cover my blood | My witness is in heaven |
| 17:1–16 | The grave ready | A pledge with Yourself; the pit my father; where is my hope? |

### 7. Vocabulary

- **יִרְאָה ("fear of God", 15:4).** "Indeed, you do away with (תָּפֵר) reverence." Eliphaz accuses Job of destroying the quality that defined him (1:1). The verb פרר ("annul") returns in God's question: "Will you really annul (תָּפֵר) My judgement?" (40:8). `[T]`
- **סוֹד אֱלוֹהַּ ("the council of God", 15:8).** "Do you hear the secret counsel of God?" Job later recalls "when the friendship (סוֹד) of God was over my tent" (29:4). The phrase occurs only in these two verses (WLC). `[T]`
- **עֵד / שָׂהֵד ("witness", 16:19).** שָׂהֵד is an Aramaic-type word for a witness, found only here in the Hebrew Bible (lemma, WLC); its Aramaic cousin names Laban's cairn, יְגַר שָׂהֲדוּתָא ("heap of witness", Gen 31:47). *High confidence.* The pair makes the claim legal.
- **מֵלִיץ (16:20).** מְלִיצַי רֵעָי. NASB95 "My friends are my scoffers"; the same form at 33:23 is "a mediator" (מַלְאָךְ מֵלִיץ). The WLC lemma conflates two senses — "interpreter, intercessor" (Gen 42:23) and "scoffer" (Proverbs) — under one number. `[T]` Job's line may mean "my intercessors are my friends!" (bitter irony) or "my friends are my scorners". *Uncertain.*
- **עָרַב ("to be surety", 17:3).** "Lay down, now, a pledge for me with Yourself; who is there that will be my guarantor?" It is Judah's verb when he stands surety for Benjamin (Gen 43:9; 44:32) (lemma, WLC). Heb 7:22 calls Jesus "the guarantee (ἔγγυος) of a better covenant". *Moderate* — the link is Hebrew-only (Swete's 17:3 is different).

### 8. Translations

- **16:21 בֶן־אָדָם.** NASB95 "a man with his neighbour"; ESV "a son of man with his neighbour"; NIV84 "as a man pleads for his friend". ESV keeps the phrase; the others level it.
- **Ancient versions check.**
  - Swete's 16:12 (Heb 16:11): "the Lord handed me over (παρέδωκεν) into the hands of the unjust". παραδίδωμι ("hand over") is the passion verb (Mark 9:31 "handed over into the hands of men"). *Category 3* at a distance — a Greek idiom, not a citation. *Moderate.*
  - Swete's 16:20 (Heb 16:19) has ὁ μάρτυς μου ("my witness").
- **Pulpit divergence note.** N/A — no pulpit text declared. *For an NIV84 engagement,* the "son of man" at 16:21 is not audible.

### 10. Repetition

- **Eliphaz reuses his own vision and Job's phrase.** 15:14–16 repeats 4:17–19 ("what is man, that he should be pure … He puts no trust in His holy ones") and takes Job's "born of woman" (14:1). `[T]`
- **עָמָל ("trouble").** Eliphaz: the wicked "conceive trouble (עָמָל) and bring forth iniquity" (15:35). Job at once: "Sorry comforters (מְנַחֲמֵי עָמָל, 'comforters of trouble') are you all" (16:2). `[T]`
- **Drinking like water.** Eliphaz: man "who drinks iniquity like water (שֹׁתֶה כַמַּיִם)" (15:16). Elihu: "What man is like Job, who drinks up derision like water (יִשְׁתֶּה … כַּמָּיִם)?" (34:7). The only two such verses in Job (lemma, WLC). **Elihu echoes Eliphaz again** (see 4:13 → 33:15). `[T]`
- **Hope, twice in one verse.** "Where now is my hope? And who regards my hope?" (17:15). `[T]`

### 11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

**Isa 53:9 → 16:17** *(high on the words)*

- *Source context.* The fourth Servant Song: the servant, oppressed and afflicted, is assigned a grave with the wicked "because he had done no violence, nor was there any deceit in his mouth" (Isa 53:9). The song ends with his intercession for transgressors (53:12).
- *Book usage.* Isaiah is live in Job (4:3–4; 12:9). The servant material recurs: the cheek and spittle (16:10; 30:10; Isa 50:6); the "taskmaster" (3:18; 39:7; Isa 53:7); intercession (42:8–10; Isa 53:12). **(synthetic)**
- *OT-to-OT.* Isa 50:6 and 53:9 are within one servant sequence. Job's 16:10 ("they have slapped me on the cheek", לְחָיָי) and 16:17 draw on both, whichever text is prior.
- *What it adds.* Job's claim of innocence is framed in the servant's vocabulary. For the Christian reader the righteous sufferer's protest and the Servant's silence meet in one Person. `[I]`

**Lam 3:12, 30; Mic 4:14 [Eng 5:1] → 16:10, 12** *(moderate)*

- God "set me up as His target (לְמַטָּרָה)" (16:12; Lam 3:12). "They have slapped me on the cheek (לְחָיָי)" (16:10; Lam 3:30; Mic 4:14 [Eng 5:1] — the ruler of Israel struck on the cheek). "They gnash their teeth" (16:9; Lam 2:16; Ps 35:16) (lemma, WLC).
- The sufferer of Lamentations — Jerusalem personified in the man of Lam 3 — and Job share an idiom of affliction. *Direction open.*

**Prov 8:25 → 15:7** *(high on the words)*

- "Were you the first man to be born, or were you brought forth before the hills (לִפְנֵי גְבָעוֹת חוֹלָלְתָּ)?" is Wisdom's claim in Prov 8:25 ("before the hills I was brought forth", חוֹלָלְתִּי). Eliphaz mocks Job for claiming what only Wisdom can claim. The book will say only God knows wisdom's place (28:23).

**Internal (Move 4).**

- *Answers §earlier:*
  - 16:19 answers 9:33 and 10:17. *High.*
  - 16:2 answers 2:11 and 15:35. *High.*
  - 15:6 ("your own mouth condemns you [יַרְשִׁיעֲךָ פִיךָ]") turns Job's hypothetical 9:20 ("my mouth will condemn me [פִּי יַרְשִׁיעֵנִי]") into a verdict. *High.*
- *Planted for later:*
  - **15:4 "you do away (תָּפֵר) with reverence"** → 40:8. *Moderate.*
  - **15:8 סוֹד אֱלוֹהַּ** → 29:4. *High.*
  - **15:16** → 34:7. *High.*
  - **16:15 "thrust my horn (קַרְנִי) in the dust"** → the third daughter, Keren-happuch, "horn of antimony" (42:14). Surface form only; the lemmas differ. *Uncertain.*
  - **16:19–21** → 19:25–27. *High.*
  - **16:20 מֵלִיץ** → 33:23. *Moderate.*

### 16. So What? (brief)

- **Intended response.** To look for the witness in heaven when earthly comforters fail.
- **Candidate FCF.** Being condemned by those who should comfort.
- **Prayer.** "My eye weeps to God" (16:20) — the posture, not the words, is the model.

### Christological note

- **Trajectory.** The heavenly witness who pleads "for a son of man with God" (16:21) → "we have an Advocate (παράκλητον) with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous" (1 John 2:1) → "Christ Jesus … who also intercedes for us" (Rom 8:34). *High* on the trajectory.
- **The surety (17:3)** → Heb 7:22. *Moderate.*
- **Keep honest.** Whether Job's witness is God himself (against God as accuser) or a third party is disputed. `[S]` The text holds both: "my witness is in heaven … my eye weeps to God" (16:19–20).

### Passage-specific difficulties

- **16:7–14 God as violent assailant.** *Ethical / pastoral.* Job's imagery is extreme — God tears, gnashes, shatters, shoots. It is lament, not doctrine. The book lets it stand and later commends Job's speech to God. Don't soften it; don't adopt it as theology.

## Unit 9 — 18:1–19:29 ⭐ "I Know That My Redeemer Lives"

> 19:25 וַאֲנִי יָדַעְתִּי גֹּאֲלִי חָי וְאַחֲרוֹן עַל־עָפָר יָקוּם
> 19:26 וְאַחַר עוֹרִי נִקְּפוּ־זֹאת וּמִבְּשָׂרִי אֶחֱזֶה אֱלוֹהַּ
> "As for me, I know that my Redeemer lives, and at the last He will take His stand on the earth. Even after my skin is destroyed, yet from my flesh I shall see God." *(Job, NASB95)*

### Headline Findings

- **"Redeemer … at the last" is a pairing found elsewhere only in Isaiah's self-description of the LORD.** `[T]`
  - גֹּאֲלִי ("my Redeemer") with אַחֲרוֹן ("at the last", 19:25).
  - Isa 44:6: "Thus says the LORD, the King of Israel and his Redeemer (גֹּאֲלוֹ) … 'I am the first and I am the last (אַחֲרוֹן)'".
  - גֹּאֵל and אַחֲרוֹן meet in one verse only at Isa 44:6 and Job 19:25 (lemma, WLC). Christ takes the title "the first and the last" in Rev 1:17.
  - *Surfaced by:* Vocabulary, Tool 11, Christological Reading. *High on the words; moderate on what it implies about the Redeemer's identity; direction open.*
- **Job's certainty comes at the bottom of his isolation, and the chapter's "stranger" words turn round in its last line.** `[T]`
  - 19:13–19 lists everyone who has turned away: brothers, acquaintances, kin, guests, maids, servant, wife, children, the young, intimates. זָר ("stranger, estranged") runs through it: "completely estranged (זָרוּ)" (19:13); "consider me a stranger (לְזָר)" (19:15).
  - Then: "whom my eyes will see and not another (וְלֹא־זָר)" (19:27).
  - זוּר I occurs three times in the chapter (19:13, 15, 27). 19:17 זָרָה ("my breath is offensive") is a homograph with a different lemma (WLC). **The count is three, not four** — see the correction below.
  - *Surfaced by:* Repetition, Structure, Tone. *High.*
- **Job answers Bildad's trap-poem: the net is God's, not his own feet's.** `[T]`
  - Bildad piles up six words for traps into which the wicked man's own feet lead him (18:8–10).
  - Job: "Know then that God has wronged me (עִוְּתָנִי) and has closed His net (מְצוּדוֹ) around me" (19:6).
  - עות answers Bildad's 8:3 directly (unit 6).
  - *High.*

### 2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

- **Preceding movement.** Job's witness in heaven (16:19); his hope apparently going down to Sheol (17:15–16). `[T]`
- **Necessity answer.** Bildad's poem on the wicked man's end (18) — light out, name forgotten, no offspring, "the king of terrors" — is aimed at a man whose children are dead. Job's reply must reach the lowest point of abandonment (19:13–22) so that the book's highest confession (19:25–27) is heard from there. The confession does not end the argument; it follows a list of losses. `[I]`

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 18:2–4 | Bildad: are we beasts? | Rebuke |
| 18:5–21 | The wicked man's end | Light out; traps; terrors; "the firstborn of death"; brimstone; no name, no offspring |
| 19:2–6 | Job: how long will you torment me? | God has wronged me |
| 19:7–12 | God's siege | Walled up; stripped of glory; uprooted like a tree; His troops camp round my tent |

| 19:23–27 | Written in rock; my Redeemer lives | The confession |
| 19:28–29 | Warning | Fear the sword — "that you may know there is judgement" |

### 7. Vocabulary

- **גֹּאֵל ("Redeemer", 19:25).** The kinsman who buys back land and people and avenges blood (Lev 25:25; Num 35:19; Ruth). Isaiah calls the LORD Israel's גֹּאֵל repeatedly (e.g. Isa 41:14; 44:6). In Job the lexeme occurs twice (lemma, WLC): here, and at 3:5, where Job wished darkness would "claim" (יִגְאָלֻהוּ, NASB95) his day — darkness as the day's next-of-kin. `[T]` The kinsman Job wanted for his birthday was darkness; the one he confesses at 19:25 lives. *High confidence* on the legal sense; *moderate* on the echo; the referent is the crux.
- **חָי ("lives").** It sounds the oath-formula of 27:2, חַי־אֵל ("as God lives"). *Moderate.*
- **עָפָר ("dust", 19:25).** "He will take His stand on the dust." NASB95 "on the earth". Job's dust runs through the book: 2:12; 7:21; 16:15; 17:16; 30:19; 42:6. The Redeemer stands on the dust where Job sits. `[I]` *Moderate.*
- **חזה ("to see", 19:26, 27).** "From my flesh I shall see (אֶחֱזֶה) God … whom my eyes will see (רָאוּ)". Both verbs stand in 19:27 (WLC). 42:5 uses ראה: "now my eye sees You". `[T]`
- **Proper-noun inventory.** None beyond God. גָפְרִית ("brimstone", 18:15) is Sodom's (Gen 19:24) — a conceptual allusion (see Tool 11).

### 8. Translations

- **19:25 "Redeemer".** NASB95 and ESV "Redeemer", capitalised — an interpretive choice. NIV84 "Redeemer", with the margin "defender". **Pulpit note:** a capital letter decides the referent for the congregation.
- **19:26.** Hebrew מִבְּשָׂרִי ("from my flesh") is ambiguous: "in my flesh" (still embodied) or "apart from my flesh". NASB95 "from"; ESV "in"; NIV84 "in", with margin "apart from". The verse is the textual and interpretive crux of the book. **Do not build a doctrine of bodily resurrection on the Hebrew of 19:26 alone.**
- **Ancient versions check.** Swete's 19:25–26: "I know that he is everlasting who is about to deliver me; on the earth he will raise up (ἀναστῆσαι) my skin". *Category 2* — the Greek reads resurrection explicitly, and a different text.
- **19:29 ketiv.** שדין (unpointed; qere שַׁדּוּן), "that you may know there is judgement" — the ketiv was invisible to the overview's scan (see the Text-First Declaration).

### 10. Repetition

- **Bildad's six trap words** (18:8–10): רֶשֶׁת ("net"), שְׂבָכָה ("webbing"), פַּח ("snare"), צַמִּים ("trap"), חֶבֶל ("noose"), מַלְכֹּדֶת ("trap"). `[T]`
- **"Pity me, pity me" (חָנֻּנִי חָנֻּנִי, 19:21)** — the only doubled plea in the book. `[T]`
- **Written, inscribed, engraved (19:23–24)** — three verbs for permanence, set against the book's many words for the fleeting. `[T]`

### 11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

**Isa 44:6 → 19:25** *(high on the words)*

- *Source context.* Isaiah 44:6–8: the LORD, King and Redeemer of Israel, "the first and the last, … besides Me there is no God", challenges the idols. The Redeemer is God himself.
- *Book usage.* Isaiah is live in Job (4:3–4; 12:9; 16:17). Bildad's final speech will call man a "worm (תּוֹלֵעָה)" (25:6). Isa 41:14 is the only verse in the Hebrew Bible where "worm" and גֹּאֵל meet: "Do not fear, you worm Jacob … your Redeemer (גֹּאֲלֵךְ) is the Holy One of Israel" (lemma, WLC). **(synthetic)**
- *OT-to-OT.* Isaiah's Redeemer oracles (41:14; 44:6) are one sequence addressed to exiles.
- *What it adds.* The one text elsewhere that pairs "Redeemer" with "last" identifies the Redeemer as the LORD. That supports, without proving, the reading that Job's Redeemer is God himself — God against God. `[I]` *Moderate.*

**Ps 88:9, 19 [Eng 88:8, 18] → 19:13–14** *(moderate)*

- "You have removed (הִרְחַקְתָּ) my acquaintances (מְיֻדָּעַי) far from me" (Ps 88:9). "He has removed (הִרְחִיק) my brothers far from me, and my acquaintances (יֹדְעַי)" (Job 19:13). רחק with ידע in both (lemma, WLC). Ps 88 is the lament with no resolution; it is also where the dead are חָפְשִׁי ("free", Ps 88:6 [Eng 88:5]; cf. Job 3:19).

**Lam 3:7, 9 → 19:8** *(moderate)*

- גָּדַר ("to wall up"): "He has walled me in (גָּדַר בַּעֲדִי)" (Lam 3:7); "He has blocked (גָּדַר) my ways" (Lam 3:9); "He has walled up (גָדַר) my way" (Job 19:8) (lemma, WLC). A third point of contact with Lam 3 (after 16:10, 12). **(synthetic)**

**Ps 8:6 [Eng 8:5] → 19:9** *(moderate)*

- "He has stripped my glory (כְּבוֹדִי) from me and removed the crown (עֲטֶרֶת) from my head" (19:9). Ps 8:6: "You … crown him (תְּעַטְּרֵהוּ) with glory (כָבוֹד) and majesty". Noun and verb are cognate. The second Psalm 8 reversal in Job (after 7:17–18). **(synthetic)**

**Gen 19:24 → 18:15** *(moderate, Move 1 only)*

- גָפְרִית ("brimstone") rained on Sodom. Bildad's wicked man's tent is sprinkled with it — Sodom's end, implied for Job's house.

**Internal (Move 4).**

- *Answers §earlier:*
  - 19:6 answers 8:3 (עות) and 18:8–10 (traps). *High.*
  - 19:8 answers 3:23 ("hedged in"). *High.*
  - 19:10 "uprooted my hope like a tree" answers 14:7. *High.*
  - 19:21 "the hand of God has struck (נָגְעָה) me" answers 1:11; 2:5 (נגע). *High.*
  - 19:25 answers 9:33 and 16:19. *High.*
- *Planted for later:*
  - **19:27 "my eyes will see"** → 42:5. *High.*
  - **19:9 glory and crown** → 31:36 ("I would bind it to myself like a crown [עֲטָרוֹת]") → 40:10 ("clothe yourself with honour and majesty [הוֹד וְהָדָר]"). *Moderate.*
  - **19:25 "on the dust"** → 42:6 ("in dust and ashes"). *Moderate.*
  - **19:29 "that you may know there is judgement"** → 42:7–8. *Moderate.*

**A correction this unit owes to the corpus.** A working note counted "stranger" four times in chapter 19. The WLC lemma index assigns 19:17's זָרָה ("my breath is offensive to my wife") to a different lemma (זוּר II, "be loathsome") from 19:13, 15 and 27 (זוּר I, "be a stranger"). **Three occurrences, not four.** The sound-play is audible; the count is three.

### 16. So What? (brief)

- **Intended response.** To confess the living Redeemer from within the losses, not after them.
- **Candidate FCF.** Total abandonment.
- **Prayer.** 19:25–27, in Christ.

### Christological note

- **Trajectory.** The גֹּאֵל who lives and stands at the last on the dust → the risen Christ, "the first and the last, and the living One" (Rev 1:17–18). *High* on the trajectory.
- **Keep honest.**
  - The referent of גֹּאֵל is contested: God himself; a heavenly third party; or Job's own vindication personified. `[S]`
  - 19:26 does not settle bodily resurrection by itself.
  - The Isa 44:6 link favours "God himself". Handel's "I know that my Redeemer liveth" is a legitimate Christian appropriation, but it is an appropriation.

### Passage-specific difficulties

- **Bildad's 18:19 "no offspring".** *Pastoral.* Aimed at a bereaved father. Name the cruelty.
- **19:25–27 at funerals.** Fine — but restore 19:13–22 to it, or the confession loses its weight.

## Unit 10 — 20:1–21:34 Why Do the Wicked Prosper?

> 21:7 מַדּוּעַ רְשָׁעִים יִחְיוּ עָתְקוּ גַּם־גָּבְרוּ חָיִל
> "Why do the wicked still live, continue on, also become very powerful?"
>
> 21:17 כַּמָּה נֵר־רְשָׁעִים יִדְעָךְ
> "How often is the lamp of the wicked put out?" *(Job, NASB95)*

### Headline Findings

- **Job questions a saying of Proverbs by quoting it.** `[T]`
  - נֵר רְשָׁעִים יִדְעָךְ ("the lamp of the wicked goes out") is a proverb: Prov 13:9; 24:20.
  - Bildad paraphrased it (18:5–6). Job quotes it exactly and prefixes כַּמָּה ("how often?", 21:17).
  - The exact phrase occurs at Job 21:17, Prov 13:9 and Prov 24:20 only (WLC). **Job does not deny the proverb; he asks how often it is true.**
  - *Surfaced by:* Tool 11, Move 4, Genre. *High confidence.*
- **Job's question is Jeremiah's.** `[T]`
  - מַדּוּעַ רְשָׁעִים ("why do the wicked…", 21:7) and Jer 12:1 מַדּוּעַ דֶּרֶךְ רְשָׁעִים צָלֵחָה ("why has the way of the wicked prospered?").
  - מַדּוּעַ with רְשָׁעִים occurs in one verse only in these two (lemma, WLC). This is the second point of contact with Jeremiah's laments (after 3:1–11 ‖ Jer 20:14–18).
  - *High on the words.* **(synthetic)**
- **Job's picture of the wicked man's children answers Bildad and Zophar point by point.** `[T]`
  - Bildad: the wicked man has "no offspring or posterity" (18:19). Zophar: "his sons favour the poor" (20:10).
  - Job: "Their descendants are established with them … They send forth their little ones like the flock, and their children skip about" (21:8, 11).
  - Spoken by a man whose own children are dead. *High.*

### 2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

- **Preceding movement.** Job has confessed his Redeemer and warned the friends of judgement (19:25–29). `[T]`
- **Necessity answer.** Zophar, stung ("the reproof which insults me", 20:3), gives the second cycle's last statement of retribution. Job's reply is the first time he attacks the doctrine head-on with counter-evidence rather than complaint: the wicked prosper and die at ease. The cycle ends with Job refuting the friends' premise, not only their application to him. `[I]`

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 20:2–11 | Zophar: brief triumph | The wicked man's joy is momentary; he perishes like dung |
| 20:12–29 | Evil as food | Sweet in the mouth, poison in the belly; God's anger rained on him — "this is the wicked man's portion" |
| 21:2–6 | Job: listen, then mock | Put your hand over your mouth |
| 21:7–16 | The wicked prosper | Children, flocks, music, an easy death — "Depart from us!" |
| 21:17–26 | How often? | The lamp, the chaff, the sons — one dies at ease, another in bitterness; together in the dust |
| 21:27–34 | Ask the travellers | The wicked man is honoured in death; your answers are falsehood |

### 7. Vocabulary

- **בָּלַע ("to swallow", 20:15, 18).** The wicked man "swallows riches but will vomit them up". God said the Accuser incited him "to swallow (לְבַלְּעוֹ)" Job (2:3). *Moderate.*
- **סוּר מִמֶּנּוּ ("depart from us", 21:14).** The wicked say to God what Job never says. Eliphaz quotes it back as Job's (22:17). `[T]`
- **הֶבֶל ("vainly", 21:34).** "How then will you vainly (הֶבֶל) comfort me?" — נחם ("comfort") again. `[T]`
- **Proper-noun inventory.** None.

### 8. Translations

- **21:13 "suddenly they go down to Sheol".** Hebrew בְּרֶגַע ("in a moment") — an easy death, not a sudden judgement. NASB95 "suddenly" can mislead; ESV "in peace they go down"; NIV84 "go down to the grave in peace". **Pulpit note:** ESV and NIV84 are closer to Job's point.
- **Ancient versions check.** No weighty divergence at 21:7 or 21:17.
- **Pulpit divergence note.** As above.

### 10. Repetition

- **Children** (21:8, 11, 19, 21). `[T]`
- **"The wicked" (רְשָׁעִים)** at 21:7, 16, 17, 28 — the chapter's subject. `[T]`
- **Zophar's food words** (20:12–23): sweet, tongue, palate, food, bowels, venom, swallow, vomit, belly. `[T]`

### 11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

**Prov 13:9; 24:20 → 21:17** *(high)*

- *Source context.* Prov 13:9: "The light of the righteous rejoices, but the lamp of the wicked goes out." Prov 24:20: "there will be no future (אַחֲרִית) for the evil man; the lamp of the wicked will be put out."
- *Book usage.* Proverbs is live in Job (5:17 ‖ Prov 3:11–12; 15:7 ‖ Prov 8:25).
- *What it adds.* Job treats a proverb as what it is — a general truth — and asks about its frequency, which is exactly the Genre tool's rule for Proverbs ("generally true observations, not promises"). The book puts that hermeneutic in Job's mouth and commends him. `[I]` *High.*

**Jer 12:1 → 21:7** *(high on the words)*

- *Source context.* Jeremiah 12:1–4: "Righteous are You, O LORD, that I would plead my case (אָרִיב) with You; indeed I would discuss matters of justice (מִשְׁפָּטִים) with You: Why has the way of the wicked prospered?" A prophet's lawsuit with God.
- *Book usage.* Jer 20:14–18 at 3:1–11 (unit 3). **(synthetic)**
- *OT-to-OT.* Jeremiah 12 uses רִיב and מִשְׁפָּט — the vocabulary of Job's lawsuit.
- *What it adds.* Job's two sharpest complaints — the cursed birthday and the prosperous wicked — are Jeremiah's. The book sets Job alongside the most persecuted of the prophets. `[I]`

**Internal (Move 4).**

- *Answers §earlier:*
  - 21:17 answers 18:5–6. *High.*
  - 21:8, 11 answer 18:19 and 20:10. *High.*
  - 21:5 "put your hand over your mouth" (שִׂימוּ יָד עַל־פֶּה). *High.*
  - 21:34 answers 16:2. *High.*
- *Planted for later:*
  - **21:5** → Job's own "I lay my hand on my mouth" (40:4) and the silenced princes of 29:9. *High.*
  - **21:14–16** → quoted by Eliphaz at 22:17–18. *High.*
  - **21:32 גָּדִישׁ ("tomb")** answers 5:26. *Moderate.*

### 16. So What? (brief)

- **Intended response.** Honesty about what we see: the wicked often prosper. Don't defend God with false data.
- **Candidate FCF.** The scandal of the prosperous wicked.

### Passage-specific difficulties

- **20:4–29 quoted as Scripture's teaching on the wicked.** *Weaponised.* It is Zophar's speech. Its general truth (the wicked face judgement) stands in Scripture elsewhere; its use against Job is condemned (42:7).

## Unit 11 — 22:1–27:23 The Debate Breaks Down

> 22:5 הֲלֹא רָעָתְךָ רַבָּה וְאֵין־קֵץ לַעֲוֺנֹתֶיךָ
> "Is not your wickedness great, and your iniquities without end?" *(Eliphaz)*
>
> 23:3 מִי־יִתֵּן יָדַעְתִּי וְאֶמְצָאֵהוּ אָבוֹא עַד־תְּכוּנָתוֹ
> "Oh that I knew where I might find Him, that I might come to His seat!" *(Job, NASB95)*

### Headline Findings

- **Eliphaz's final speech ends with an unwitting prophecy of the epilogue.** `[T]` / `[I]`
  - "He will deliver one who is not innocent, and he will be delivered through the cleanness of your hands" (22:30).
  - At 42:8–10 Eliphaz, the one "not innocent", is spared through Job's prayer.
  - *Surfaced by:* Move 4, Narrator's verdict, Irony. *Moderate–high* — the fulfilment is narrative, not verbal.
- **Eliphaz's specific charges are answered, item by item, in Job's oath.** `[T]` (synthetic)
  - **The charges:** taking pledges "without cause (חִנָּם)", stripping the naked (עֲרוּמִּים), withholding water and bread, sending widows (אַלְמָנוֹת) away empty, crushing orphans (יְתֹמִים) (22:6–9).
  - **The answer:** Job was "a father to the needy", delivered "the orphan (יָתוֹם)", made "the widow's (אַלְמָנָה) heart sing" (29:12–16). "If I have seen anyone perish for lack of clothing … if he has not been warmed with the fleece of my sheep" (31:19–20). "If I have kept the poor from their desire, or have caused the eyes of the widow (אַלְמָנָה) to fail, or have eaten my morsel alone, and the orphan (יָתוֹם) has not shared it" (31:16–17).
  - אַלְמָנָה ("widow") and יָתוֹם ("orphan") are verified at each point (lemma, WLC).
  - *High.*
- **The third cycle breaks down, and the Masoretic text leaves it broken.** `[T]`
  - Bildad's last speech is six verses (25:1–6). Zophar has no third speech (the formula וַיַּעַן צֹפַר appears at 11:1 and 20:1 only).
  - Job's reply (26–27) includes 27:13, which opens with Zophar's closing words: זֶה חֵלֶק־אָדָם רָשָׁע ("this is the portion of a wicked man"). The four-word phrase occurs only at 20:29 and 27:13 (WLC); the rest of each verse differs.
  - The two options are laid out in the overview; the sweep adds nothing that decides between them. *Contested.*

### 2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

- **Preceding movement.** Job has refuted retribution with counter-evidence (21). `[T]`
- **Necessity answer.** The friends have one move left: to accuse Job of specific sins (22:5–9), inventing them since they cannot find them. When that fails, the debate collapses: Bildad has only a hymn (25), Zophar is silent, and Job speaks alone (26–31). The breakdown is necessary. The human debate must be seen to exhaust itself before chapter 28 says wisdom is not found by such means, and before God speaks. `[I]`
- **Implication.** Preach 22–27 as the end of the argument, not the middle of it. `[I]`

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 22:1–11 | Eliphaz: your wickedness is great | Invented sins |
| 22:12–20 | "What does God know?" | Puts impiety in Job's mouth; quotes Job's 21:14–16 back |
| 22:21–30 | Return and be restored | "Shaddai will be your gold"; "He will deliver one who is not innocent" |
| 23:1–17 | Job: where can I find Him? | "When He has tried me, I shall come forth as gold" |
| 24:1–25 | Why does God not keep court days? | The oppressed poor; the rebels against the light; (24:18–24 disputed) |
| 25:1–6 | Bildad's fragment | Dominion and awe; how can man be righteous? man a worm |
| 26:1–14 | Job: sarcasm, then a hymn | "What a help you are to the weak!" The fringes of His ways |
| 27:1–23 | Job's oath and the wicked man's portion | "As God lives … I will not put away my integrity" |

### 7. Vocabulary

- **סכן ("to be of use", 22:2).** "Can a vigorous man be of use to God?" Elihu reuses the verb twice (34:9; 35:3) (lemma, WLC). `[T]`
- **בָּצֶר ("gold", 22:24–25).** "Place your gold (בֶּצֶר) in the dust … then the Almighty will be your gold (בְּצָרֶיךָ)." A fine promise that Job, in effect, has already kept: "If I have put my confidence in gold (כֶּתֶם)…" (31:24). The gold of Ophir recurs at 28:16, where wisdom cannot be bought with it (אוֹפִיר only at 22:24 and 28:16 in Job; כֶּתֶם at 28:16, 19; 31:24; lemma, WLC). **(synthetic)** *Moderate–high.*
- **תּוֹרָה ("instruction", 22:22).** The only occurrence in Job: "Please receive instruction from His mouth, and establish His words (אֲמָרָיו) in your heart." Job answers at once: "I have treasured the words of His mouth (אִמְרֵי־פִיו)" (23:12) (lemma, WLC). *High.*
- **שֵׁמֶץ ("a whisper", 26:14).** "How faint a word we hear of Him!" — see unit 4 (4:12). `[T]`
- **תֻּמָּה ("integrity", 27:5).** "Till I die I will not put away my integrity from me." The wife's question (2:9) becomes Job's oath. `[T]`
- **Proper-noun inventory.** Ophir (22:24) — the source of Solomon's gold (1 Kgs 9:28), scenery here; it returns at 28:16. Abaddon (26:6) — see unit 12. Rahab (26:12) — see unit 6.

### 8. Translations

- **24:18–24.** NASB95 and NIV84 print the passage as Job's words, as the Hebrew does. **ESV inserts "You say," at 24:18**, turning 24:18–24 into Job's quotation of the friends — an interpretive supply with no word for it in the Hebrew. `[T]` The NASB95 translates what is there and leaves the problem visible. **Pulpit note:** a congregation hearing the ESV hears the attribution question already answered.
- **Ancient versions check.** Swete's 23:10, "He has tested (διέκρινεν) me like gold", keeps the image. 1 Pet 1:7 ("the proof of your faith, being more precious than gold which is perishable, even though tested by fire") is a conceptual rhyme, not a citation. *Moderate.*
- **Pulpit divergence note.** N/A — no pulpit text declared.

### 10. Repetition

- **"Find" and "see".** "Oh that I knew where I might find (וְאֶמְצָאֵהוּ) Him" (23:3); "I go forward but He is not there … I cannot see Him" (23:8–9). Elihu later says, "The Almighty — we cannot find Him (מְצָאנֻהוּ)" (37:23). מצא in both (lemma, WLC). `[T]`

## Unit 12 — 28:1–28 ⭐ Where Can Wisdom Be Found?

> 28:12 וְהַחָכְמָה מֵאַיִן תִּמָּצֵא וְאֵי זֶה מְקוֹם בִּינָה
> "But where can wisdom be found? And where is the place of understanding?"
>
> 28:28 וַיֹּאמֶר לָאָדָם הֵן יִרְאַת אֲדֹנָי הִיא חָכְמָה וְסוּר מֵרָע בִּינָה
> "And to man He said, 'Behold, the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom; and to depart from evil is understanding.'" *(NASB95)*

### Headline Findings

- **The poem's last line defines wisdom as the character the book's first line gave Job.** `[T]` (synthetic)
  - 1:1: יְרֵא אֱלֹהִים וְסָר מֵרָע ("fearing God and turning away from evil"). 28:28: יִרְאַת אֲדֹנָי … וְסוּר מֵרָע ("the fear of the Lord … and to depart from evil").
  - סוּר מֵרָע occurs in Job only at 1:1, 1:8, 2:3 and 28:28 (WLC).
  - **What it shows.** The wisdom that no mine reaches and no gold buys is what Job already had. The friends' search for the cause of his suffering is looking in the wrong place.
  - *Surfaced by:* Move 4, Repetition, Author's Purpose. *High confidence.*
- **God's speech from the storm quotes this poem.** `[T]` (synthetic)
  - **28:26 ‖ 38:25.** "When He set a limit for the rain and a course for the thunderbolt (וְדֶרֶךְ לַחֲזִיז קֹלוֹת)" (28:26) = "Who has cleft … a way for the thunderbolt (וְדֶרֶךְ לַחֲזִיז קֹלוֹת)" (38:25). The phrase occurs only at these two verses, and חֲזִיז ("thunderbolt") only three times in the Hebrew Bible (these two and Zech 10:1) (WLC).
  - **The question-formula.** The poem's refrain asks וְאֵי זֶה מְקוֹם בִּינָה ("where is the place of understanding?", 28:12, 20). God asks אֵי־זֶה הַדֶּרֶךְ ("where is the way…?", 38:19, 24) and of darkness, "where is its place (מְקֹמוֹ)?" (38:19).
  - **What it shows.** Chapter 28 is the prelude God's speech takes up. The poem says only God knows wisdom's "way" and "place" (28:23); God then asks Job about ways and places.
  - *High on the words; moderate–high on the design.*
- **The Masoretic paragraphing marks the refrain structure, and puts no break before the poem.** `[T]`
  - Petuḥot after 28:11 and 28:19 divide the poem into three strophes, the second and third opening with the refrain (28:12 = 28:20). A setumah closes 28:28.
  - There is no marker between 27:23 and 28:1. Every change of speaker in the dialogue, from 3:26/4:1 to 41:26/42:1, is marked by a petuḥah or setumah (as the WLC transcribes Leningrad; spread unchecked).
  - Formally, then, the poem is not marked as a new voice; it continues Job's discourse. And 27:23 ends on מִמְּקֹמוֹ ("from his place") while 28:1 opens with מָקוֹם ("a place") for gold — a verbal hinge. *Moderate* — `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`.

### 2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

- **Preceding movement.** The debate has collapsed (22–27). Every human voice has claimed wisdom: Eliphaz's vision, Bildad's fathers, Zophar's "secrets of wisdom", Job's "I am not inferior to you". `[T]`
- **Necessity answer.** Before the long monologues (29–42:6), the book must declare that wisdom is not found by human search — not by mining (28:1–11), not by purchase (28:13–19), not in the deep or in death (28:14, 22) — but known to God, who gives man one word: fear the Lord (28:23–28). The poem exists here as the hinge: it judges the debate that preceded it and points to the speech that follows. `[I]`
- **Implication.** Whoever speaks it, the poem is the book's own verdict on the debate. Preach it as the book pausing to breathe. `[I]`

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 28:1–11 | Man the miner | Silver, gold, iron, copper; shafts into darkness; "what is hidden he brings out to the light" |
| 28:12 | Refrain | "Where can wisdom be found?" |
| 28:13–19 | Not found, not bought | Not in the land of the living, the deep or the sea; not for gold of Ophir, onyx, sapphire, coral, topaz |
| 28:20 | Refrain | "Where then does wisdom come from?" |
| 28:21–27 | Known to God | Hidden from all living; Abaddon and Death heard a rumour; God understands its way — at creation |
| 28:28 | The word to man | "The fear of the Lord, that is wisdom" |

**Device.** A refrain poem in three strophes, climaxing in a divine saying. `[T]`

### 5. Parallels

- **28:28.** Synonymous: fear of the Lord = wisdom; departing from evil = understanding. The pair חָכְמָה / בִּינָה ("wisdom / understanding") runs through the poem (28:12, 20, 28) (lemma, WLC). `[T]`
- **28:14.** "The deep says, 'It is not in me'; and the sea says, 'It is not with me.'" Personified synonymy; the cosmic places disown wisdom. `[T]`

### 7. Vocabulary

- **אֲדֹנָי ("the Lord", 28:28).** Its only occurrence in Job (WLC). The BHS apparatus records that many manuscripts read יהוה ("the LORD") (*mlt Mss יהוה*, as BHS prints it). Either way the poem ends on a divine name other than El/Eloah/Shaddai. `[T]`
- **תַּעֲלֻמָה ("what is hidden", 28:11).** Man brings "what is hidden" to light in mining. Zophar wished God would show Job "the secrets (תַּעֲלֻמוֹת) of wisdom" (11:6). The word occurs three times in the Hebrew Bible (Job 11:6; 28:11; Ps 44:22 [Eng 44:21]) (lemma, WLC). Man can uncover what is hidden in rock; he cannot uncover wisdom. **(synthetic)** *Moderate–high.*
- **חקר ("to search out") and תַּכְלִית ("limit").** Man "searches out (חוֹקֵר) to the farthest limit (תַּכְלִית)" (28:3); God "searched it out (חֲקָרָהּ)" (28:27). Eliphaz boasted "we have investigated (חֲקַרְנוּהָ) it" (5:27). Zophar asked whether Job could find "the limit (תַּכְלִית) of the Almighty" (11:7). תַּכְלִית occurs in Job only at 11:7, 26:10 and 28:3 (lemma, WLC). **(synthetic)** *Moderate–high.*
- **בְנֵי־שָׁחַץ ("the proud beasts", 28:8).** Only here and 41:34 [Heb 41:26], where Leviathan is "king over all the sons of pride" (lemma, WLC). `[T]`
- **Proper-noun inventory.** Ophir (28:16); Cush (28:19, "the topaz of Ethiopia"); Abaddon (28:22). Abaddon occurs five times in the Hebrew Bible: Job 26:6; 28:22; 31:12; Prov 15:11; Ps 88:12 [Eng 88:11] (lemma, WLC). Scenery and cosmic geography.

### 8. Translations

- **28:28 "the fear of the Lord".** ESV and NIV84 agree. Swete: ἡ θεοσέβειά ἐστιν σοφία ("godliness is wisdom"), and at 2:3 Swete calls Job θεοσεβής ("godly"). *Category 1* — the Greek keeps the link between 28:28 and the portrait of Job by a different word.
- **Pulpit divergence note.** N/A — no pulpit text declared.

### 10. Repetition

- **מָקוֹם ("place")** at 28:1, 6, 12, 20, 23. **דֶּרֶךְ ("way")** at 28:23, 26. God knows the "place" and the "way" (28:23). `[T]`
- **Precious things.** Silver, gold, iron, copper (28:1–2); sapphire and gold dust (28:6); gold of Ophir, onyx, sapphire, gold, glass, fine gold, coral, crystal, pearls, topaz, pure gold (28:15–19). The accumulation is the argument: nothing on the list is wisdom. `[T]`

### 11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

**Prov 1:7; 9:10; Ps 111:10 → 28:28** *(high)*

- *Source context.* Prov 1:7 heads the book of Proverbs: "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge; fools despise wisdom and instruction." Prov 9:10 closes its first section: "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom". Ps 111:10 ends a hymn to God's works.
- *The words.* יִרְאָה ("fear") and חָכְמָה ("wisdom") meet in one verse in seven places in the Hebrew Bible: Isa 11:2; 33:6; Job 28:28; Prov 1:7; 9:10; 15:33; Ps 111:10 (lemma, WLC).
- *Book usage.* Proverbs is live in Job (5:17; 15:7; 21:17).
- *OT-to-OT.* Isa 11:2 puts "the spirit of wisdom … and the fear of the LORD" on the shoot of Jesse.
- *What it adds.* Proverbs states the motto as a first principle. Job reaches it at the end of a search, after suffering. In canonical order Job comes first and Proverbs second: the reader of the Writings meets the motto tested before meeting it taught. `[I]`

**Isa 40:12 → 28:25** *(moderate)*

- *Source context.* "Who has measured (מָדַד) the waters in the hollow of His hand, and marked off (תִּכֵּן) the heavens by the span … and weighed the mountains in a balance?" (Isa 40:12). God's incomparable wisdom, and in 40:13 "who has directed (תִּכֵּן) the Spirit of the LORD?"
- *The words.* Job 28:25: "He imparted weight to the wind and meted out (תִּכֵּן) the waters by measure (מִדָּה)." תכן ("to measure, mete out") occurs in Job only here (lemma, WLC), and with מַיִם ("waters") in both verses.
- *What it adds.* The creation of wisdom in Job 28 and the incomparable Creator of Isaiah 40 use one measuring language. **Paul later pairs Isa 40:13 (Rom 11:34) with Job 41:11 [Heb 41:3] (Rom 11:35)**, joining two texts already in conversation (see unit 16). **(synthetic)** *Moderate.*

**Prov 3:19; 8:22–31 → 28:23–27** *(moderate, Move 1 only)*

- "The LORD by wisdom founded (יָסַד) the earth, by understanding He established (כּוֹנֵן) the heavens" (Prov 3:19). Job 28:27 "established it (הֱכִינָהּ)"; 38:4 "when I laid the foundation (בְּיָסְדִי) of the earth". In Proverbs God creates by wisdom; in Job 28 God "saw" and "established" wisdom at creation. The two books speak of wisdom and creation in one vocabulary.

**"The land of the living" → 28:13** *(uncertain, Move 1 only)*

- "It is not found in the land of the living (בְּאֶרֶץ הַחַיִּים)." The phrase recurs in the servant's being "cut off from the land of the living" (Isa 53:8; Jer 11:19, Jeremiah as a lamb led to slaughter) (phrase search, WLC). A canonical rhyme only.

**Internal (Move 4).**

- *Answers §earlier:*
  - 28:28 answers 1:1, 8; 2:3. *High.*
  - 28:11 answers 11:6. *Moderate–high.*
  - 28:27 answers 5:27. *Moderate–high.*
  - 28:16 answers 22:24. *Moderate.*
- *Planted for later:*
  - **28:26** → 38:25. *High.*
  - **28:12, 20 "where is the place?"** → 38:19, 24. *High.*
  - **28:8 בְנֵי־שָׁחַץ** → 41:34 [Heb 41:26]. *High.*
  - **28:14 the deep and the sea** → 38:16, 30; 41:31–32 [Heb 41:23–24]. *Moderate.*
  - **28:22 Abaddon and Death** → "Have the gates of death been revealed to you?" (38:17). *Moderate.*
  - **28:28 "to depart from evil"** → Job's oath (31) as a portrait of turning from evil. *Moderate.*

### 16. So What? (brief)

- **Intended response.** Stop searching for wisdom in the places human skill can reach; fear God.
- **Worldview.** Wisdom is not a resource to be mined but a relationship to be lived.
- **Candidate FCF.** Our confidence that enough effort will uncover the answer.
- **Prayer.** For the fear of the Lord.

### Christological note

- **Wisdom hidden, then given.** Wisdom "hidden from the eyes of all living" (28:21) → Christ, "in whom are hidden (ἀπόκρυφοι) all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (Col 2:3), "the wisdom of God" (1 Cor 1:24). *Moderate–high.*
- **The Branch.** Isa 11:2's shoot of Jesse bears the spirit of wisdom and of the fear of the LORD — the two terms of 28:28 embodied in one person. *Moderate.*
- **Moralism check.** "Fear God and you will be wise" is true and incomplete. The poem's point is that wisdom is God's gift in a word (28:28, "to man He said"), not an achievement.

### Passage-specific difficulties

- **Who speaks?** *Interpretive.* See Headline Finding 3 and the overview. Name the question; don't let it swallow the sermon.

## Unit 13 — 29:1–31:40 Then and Now: the Oath of Clearance

> 29:2 מִי־יִתְּנֵנִי כְיַרְחֵי־קֶדֶם כִּימֵי אֱלוֹהַּ יִשְׁמְרֵנִי
> "Oh that I were as in months gone by, as in the days when God watched over me."
>
> 31:35 מִי יִתֶּן־לִי שֹׁמֵעַ לִי הֶן־תָּוִי שַׁדַּי יַעֲנֵנִי
> "Oh that I had one to hear me! Behold, here is my signature; let the Almighty answer me!" *(Job, NASB95)*

### Headline Findings

- **Job ends his words by signing his case and demanding an answer.** `[T]`
  - "Behold, here is my signature (תָּוִי); let the Almighty answer me!" (31:35). "The words of Job are ended" (31:40).
  - God answers in the very next speech he makes (38:1). Elihu comes between.
  - תָּו ("mark, signature") occurs three times in the Hebrew Bible: Job 31:35, and Ezek 9:4, 6 — the mark on the foreheads of those who grieve over Jerusalem's abominations, who alone are spared (lemma, WLC). *Uncertain* as an allusion; noted.
  - *Surfaced by:* Structure, Move 4, Vocabulary. *High on the words.*
- **Job was the comforter the friends failed to be.** `[T]`
  - "I dwelt as a king among the troops, as one who comforted (יְנַחֵם) the mourners" (29:25).
  - נחם runs from the friends' purpose (2:11) through Job's verdict on them (16:2; 21:34) to the family who actually comfort him (42:11) (lemma, WLC). **(synthetic)** *High.*
- **God's "cruel" in Job's mouth is answered by God's "fierce" of Leviathan.** `[T]`
  - "You have become cruel (אַכְזָר) to me" (30:21). God: "No one is so fierce (אַכְזָר) that he dares to arouse him; who then is he that can stand before Me?" (41:10 [Heb 41:2]).
  - אַכְזָר occurs four times in the Hebrew Bible: Deut 32:33; Lam 4:3; Job 30:21; 41:10 [Heb 41:2] (lemma, WLC).
  - **What it shows.** The ferocity Job attributes to God, God displays in his creature — and claims a greater.
  - *Moderate–high.*

### 2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

- **Preceding movement.** The wisdom poem has said wisdom is the fear of God (28:28). `[T]`
- **Necessity answer.** Job must now lay out his whole case: what he was (29), what he is (30), and his oath that he did not deserve the difference (31). The unit is the counterpart of chapter 3 — a closing soliloquy to match the opening one — and it is the formal completion of the lawsuit, which is what makes God's answer possible. `[I]`
- **Implication.** Chapter 31 is Job's portrait of the fear of God and departing from evil (28:28) in practice. `[I]`

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 29:1–11 | Then: God's friendship | God's lamp over my head; honour at the gate |
| 29:12–17 | Then: justice for the poor | Eyes to the blind, feet to the lame, father to the needy |
| 29:18–25 | Then: expectation and honour | "I shall die in my nest"; a king among troops; a comforter of mourners |
| 30:1–15 | Now: mocked by outcasts | Sons of fools; spitting |
| 30:16–31 | Now: God cruel | No answer; dust and ashes; brother of jackals; harp to mourning |
| 31:1–34 | The oath | Lust, falsehood, adultery, slaves, the poor, gold, idolatry, enemies, hospitality, hidden sin |
| 31:35–37 | The signature | "Let the Almighty answer me" |
| 31:38–40 | The land | "If my land cries out against me…" — "The words of Job are ended" |

### 4. Linking Words

- **אִם ("if")** in the oath: 17 verses of chapter 31 carry it (lemma, WLC). Each "if" implies a self-curse, sometimes spoken ("let my shoulder fall from the socket", 31:22), sometimes left unspoken. `[T]`
- **וְעַתָּה ("but now")** at 30:1, 9, 16 marks the turn from then to now three times. `[T]`

### 7. Vocabulary

- **סוֹד אֱלוֹהַּ ("the friendship of God", 29:4).** Eliphaz mocked, "Do you hear the secret counsel (סוֹד) of God?" (15:8). Job had it. `[T]`
- **נֵר ("lamp", 29:3).** "When His lamp shone over my head." The only other lamps in Job are the wicked's, put out (18:6; 21:17) (lemma, WLC). `[T]`
- **בִּרְכַּת ("the blessing", 29:13).** "The blessing of the one ready to perish came upon me." The noun occurs in Job only here; with the verb's literal use at 31:20 ("his loins … thanked [בֵרֲכוּנִי] me"), it reclaims the book's euphemised word (1:5, 11; 2:5, 9). `[T]`
- **צֶדֶק לָבַשְׁתִּי ("I put on righteousness", 29:14).** The idiom is priestly: "Let Your priests be clothed with righteousness (יִלְבְּשׁוּ צֶדֶק)" (Ps 132:9). God himself "put on righteousness (צְדָקָה) like a breastplate" (Isa 59:17) (lemma, WLC). *Moderate.*
- **חלף ("renewed", 29:20).** "My bow is renewed (תַחֲלִיף) in my hand." It is the tree's "sprouting again" (14:7) (lemma, WLC). *Moderate.*
- **Proper-noun inventory.** Adam (31:33, כְאָדָם — "like Adam" or "as men do"; the lemma index reads the proper name). See the overview.

### 8. Translations

- **31:33 כְאָדָם.** NASB95 "like Adam"; ESV "as others do"; NIV84 "as men do", with margin "Or *as Adam did*". **Pulpit note:** the Adam reading is audible only in NASB95.
- **29:18 "as the sand (חוֹל)".** Some take חוֹל here as "the phoenix". `[S]` All three English versions have "sand" in the text (NIV84 "grains of sand").
- **Ancient versions check.** No weighty divergence bearing on a finding.

### 10. Repetition

- **Widow and orphan.** 29:12, 13; 31:16, 17, 21 (lemma, WLC). They answer Eliphaz's charges (22:9). `[T]`
- **Scales.** "Oh that my grief were actually weighed … in the balances (בְּמֹאזְנַיִם)" (6:2); "Let Him weigh me with accurate scales (בְמֹאזְנֵי־צֶדֶק), and let God know my integrity (תֻּמָּתִי)" (31:6). The only two uses of מֹאזְנַיִם ("scales") in Job (lemma, WLC). `[T]`
- **Honour and laughter.** "I smiled (אֶשְׂחַק) on them" (29:24); "Those younger than I mock (שָׂחֲקוּ) me" (30:1). `[T]`

### 11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

**Gen 3:8–10 → 31:33** *(moderate)*

- *Source context.* The man and woman hide from God among the trees.
- *What it adds.* Job denies "covering my transgressions like Adam, hiding my iniquity in my bosom". No verb is shared with Genesis 3 (the overview's non-link); the link rests on the name. Job claims the opposite of Adam's concealment — openness before God.

**Gen 4:10–12; 3:17–18 → 31:38–40** *(moderate)*

- "If my land (אַדְמָתִי) cries out (תִזְעָק) against me" (31:38). Abel's blood "is crying (צֹעֲקִים) to Me from the ground (הָאֲדָמָה)" (Gen 4:10) — cognate cry-verbs and the same noun (lemma, WLC). "Let briars grow instead of wheat" (31:40) recalls the cursed ground of Gen 3:18, with different plant words — conceptual.

**Deut 4:19 → 31:26–28** *(moderate)*

- Worship of the sun and moon (Deut 4:19; 17:3). Job's "if I have looked at the sun (אוֹר, literally 'light') … or the moon (יָרֵחַ)" disowns the idolatry Deuteronomy forbids. The moon is the same word; Job calls the sun "light" where Deuteronomy says שֶׁמֶשׁ ("sun") (lemma, WLC).

**Isa 50:6 → 30:10** *(moderate)*

- "They do not refrain from spitting (רֹק) at my face" (30:10). The servant: "I did not cover My face from humiliation and spitting (וָרֹק)" (Isa 50:6). רֹק ("spittle") occurs three times in the Hebrew Bible: Isa 50:6; Job 7:19; 30:10 (lemma, WLC). See unit 8. **(synthetic)**

**Internal (Move 4).**

- *Answers §earlier:*
  - 29:12–17 and 31:16–22 answer 22:6–9. *High.*
  - 29:4 answers 15:8. *High.*
  - 29:25 answers 2:11; 16:2. *High.*
  - 31:6 answers 6:2. *High.*
  - 31:36 "I would bind it to myself like a crown (עֲטָרוֹת)" answers 19:9. *Moderate.*
  - 30:19 "dust and ashes" answers 2:8. *High.*
- *Planted for later:*
  - **31:35 "let the Almighty answer me"** → 38:1. *High.*
  - **30:19 "dust and ashes"** → 42:6. *High.*
  - **30:21 אַכְזָר** → 41:10 [Heb 41:2]. *Moderate–high.*
  - **30:20 "I cry out (אֲשַׁוַּע) to You for help, but You do not answer me"** → 38:41, where the raven's young "cry (יְשַׁוֵּעוּ) to God" and God provides for them. שׁוע occurs 8 times in Job (lemma, WLC). *High.*
  - **30:29 "a brother to jackals (תַּנִּים) and a companion of ostriches"** → the ostrich in God's speech (39:13–18, a different word, רְנָנִים). *Uncertain.*
  - **29:9; 21:5 hand on mouth** → 40:4. *High.*

### 16. So What? (brief)

- **Intended response.** To see what integrity looks like in private, with slaves, the poor, gold, enemies and one's own eyes (31).
- **Candidate FCF.** Loss of standing; and the question whether goodness is worth it when it earns contempt.

### Passage-specific difficulties

- **31:1 "I have made a covenant with my eyes".** *Pastoral.* A strong text on lust (compare Matt 5:28). Use it, but in its place: it is part of an oath of innocence, not a technique.
- **Self-righteousness?** Elihu says Job was "righteous in his own eyes" (32:1). The book says Job "spoke what is right" (42:7). Both are in the text; don't resolve it by caricature.

## Unit 14 — 32:1–37:24 Elihu

> 32:2 וַיִּחַר אַף אֱלִיהוּא בֶן־בַּרַכְאֵל הַבּוּזִי מִמִּשְׁפַּחַת רָם בְּאִיּוֹב חָרָה אַפּוֹ עַל־צַדְּקוֹ נַפְשׁוֹ מֵאֱלֹהִים
> But the anger of Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite, of the family of Ram burned; against Job his anger burned because he justified himself before God.
>
> 33:23 אִם־יֵשׁ עָלָיו מַלְאָךְ מֵלִיץ אֶחָד מִנִּי־אָלֶף לְהַגִּיד לְאָדָם יָשְׁרוֹ
> "If there is an angel as mediator for him, one out of a thousand, to remind a man what is right for him…" *(Elihu, NASB95)*

### Headline Findings

- **Elihu quotes almost everyone, and prepares for God's speech.** `[T]` (synthetic)
  - **Quoting Job:** 33:10–11 (from 13:24, 27, near-verbatim; 33:9 paraphrases his protestations), 34:3 (from 12:11), 34:5 (from 27:2).
  - **Echoing Eliphaz:** 33:15 (from 4:13), 34:7 (from 15:16).
  - **Echoing Bildad:** 34:12 (from 8:3).
  - **Each of these (33:9 apart) shares an exact or near-exact phrase with its source** (WLC).
  - **Anticipating God:** "Job speaks without knowledge" (34:35; 35:16) → "words without knowledge" (38:2). "Stand and consider the wonders (נִפְלְאוֹת) of God" (37:14) → "things too wonderful (נִפְלָאוֹת) for me" (42:3). "Do you know…? (הֲתֵדַע)" (37:15, 16) → "Do you know…? (הֲיָדַעְתָּ)" (38:33; 39:1). And the storm he describes (36:27–37:24) is the storm God speaks from (38:1).
  - *Surfaced by:* Move 4, Repetition, Structure. *High on the words; the status these facts give Elihu is contested.*
- **Elihu is the first to name a mediator who ransoms.** `[T]`
  - "If there is an angel as mediator (מַלְאָךְ מֵלִיץ), one out of a thousand (אֶחָד מִנִּי־אָלֶף) … then let him be gracious to him, and say, 'Deliver him from going down to the pit, I have found a ransom (כֹפֶר)'" (33:23–24).
  - "One out of a thousand" answers Job's "not once in a thousand (אַחַת מִנִּי־אָלֶף)" (9:3); the phrase occurs only in these two verses (WLC). מֵלִיץ takes up 16:20.
  - The psalm says no man can pay God a ransom (כָּפְרוֹ) for his brother (Ps 49:8 [Eng 49:7]).
  - *Moderate–high* (synthetic).
- **Elihu's anger anticipates God's — but God's falls only on the three.** `[T]`
  - חרה ("to burn", of anger) occurs four times of Elihu in 32:2–5, at Job and at the friends. At 42:7 God says, "My wrath is kindled (חָרָה אַפִּי) against you and against your two friends."
  - In Job the verb occurs only at 19:11 (God's anger at Job, in Job's complaint), 32:2–5 and 42:7 (lemma, WLC).
  - Elihu is angry with Job; God is not. Elihu is never mentioned again.
  - *High.*

### 2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

- **Preceding movement.** "The words of Job are ended" (31:40); the three friends "ceased answering Job" (32:1). Job has demanded that the Almighty answer (31:35). `[T]`
- **Necessity answer.** Two options, both textually grounded. `[I]` / `[S]`
  - **(a) Elihu delays the answer, so that God's arrival is not on Job's schedule.** He is a fourth human voice who restates much of the friends' case (34:11 "He pays a man according to his work").
  - **(b) Elihu prepares the answer.** He shifts suffering from punishment to discipline (33:19–30; 36:8–15), anticipates God's charge of "words without knowledge", and ends in the storm. Swete's 38:1 reads it this way explicitly ("after Elihu had ceased speaking").
  - The text is compatible with both. God neither rebukes Elihu nor commends him.
- **Implication.** Preach Elihu as the voice that is half right: right about the storm, the discipline and the mediator; wrong in his anger at Job. `[I]` *Moderate.*

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 32:1–5 | Prose introduction | Elihu's genealogy and anger (×4) |
| 32:6–22 | Why the young man speaks | "It is a spirit in man"; full of words like new wineskins |
| 33:1–33 | Speech 1, to Job by name | God speaks in dreams and in pain; the mediator and ransom |
| 34:1–37 | Speech 2, to the wise | God does not pervert justice; Job speaks without knowledge |
| 35:1–16 | Speech 3 | Does righteousness profit God? Why the cry goes unanswered |
| 36:1–37:24 | Speech 4 | God disciplines to deliver; the storm of God's majesty; "Listen to this, O Job" |

### 6. Narrator's Comment

- **32:1–5** is narrator's prose — the only prose between 3:2 and 42:6 — yet the Masoretic accents treat it as poetry (see Book-Level Foundations; spread unchecked). It gives Elihu a full genealogy, which no other speaker receives, and says four times that he was angry. `[T]`

### 7. Vocabulary

- **Elihu's names.** אֱלִיהוּא ("He is my God"); בַּרַכְאֵל ("God blesses") — the book's ברך again; הַבּוּזִי ("the Buzite"), from Buz, brother of Uz (Gen 22:21, verified); מִמִּשְׁפַּחַת רָם ("of the family of Ram"). A Ram stands in Judah's line (Ruth 4:19; 1 Chr 2:9), but the identification cannot be shown. *Uncertain.*
- **Addressing Job by name.** Elihu calls Job by name at 33:1, 33:31 and 37:14. No friend does so in the debate: in chapters 4–31, אִיּוֹב ("Job") occurs only in the speech formulas and the closing notice at 31:40 (lemma, WLC). `[T]`
- **נִשְׁמַת שַׁדַּי ("the breath of the Almighty", 32:8; 33:4).** "The Spirit of God has made me, and the breath of the Almighty gives me life" (33:4). נְשָׁמָה ("breath") with אַף ("nostrils") at 27:3 recalls Gen 2:7 (both lemmas, WLC). Elihu claims the Spirit that makes every man (32:8) as the source of his speech. `[T]` / `[I]`
- **כֹּפֶר ("ransom", 33:24; 36:18).** Only these two in Job (lemma, WLC). `[T]`
- **עָנִי בְעָנְיוֹ ("the afflicted in their affliction", 36:15).** "He delivers the afflicted in their affliction, and opens their ear in time of oppression." A paronomasia (עָנִי / עֳנִי, *ʿānî / ʿŏnî*) that carries Elihu's thesis: affliction is God's means of speaking. `[T]`

### 8. Translations

- **33:23 מַלְאָךְ מֵלִיץ.** NASB95 "an angel as mediator"; ESV "an angel, a mediator"; NIV84 "an angel on his side as a mediator". Consistent.
- **Ancient versions check.** Swete's 33:23–24 is a different text — "a thousand death-bearing angels" — with no ransom. *Category 2.* **The ransom reading is Hebrew-only.**
- **Swete 38:1** — see the Necessity answer. *Category 2.*
- **Pulpit divergence note.** N/A — no pulpit text declared.

### 10. Repetition

- **"Listen" (שָׁמַע, הַאֲזִין).** The summons to hear: שׁמע at 33:1, 31, 33; 34:2, 10, 16; 37:2; האזין at 32:11; 33:1; 34:2, 16; 37:14 (lemma, WLC). Elihu demands a hearing as insistently as Job did. `[T]`
- **"Words" (מִלָּה).** 14 of the book's 34 occurrences fall in Elihu's six chapters: 32:11, 14, 15, 18; 33:1, 8, 32; 34:2, 3, 16; 35:4, 16; 36:2, 4 (lemma, WLC). The young man "full of words (מִלִּים)" (32:18). `[T]`
- **Profit and use (סכן).** 34:9; 35:3 — taking up Eliphaz's 22:2 (lemma, WLC). `[T]`

### 11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

**Ps 49:8 [Eng 49:7] → 33:24** *(moderate)*

- *Source context.* Psalm 49, a wisdom psalm: "No man can by any means redeem his brother or give to God a ransom (כָּפְרוֹ) for him" (v.8); "But God will redeem my soul from the power of Sheol" (v.16).
- *What it adds.* Elihu's angel finds the ransom no man can pay. The New Testament's λύτρον ("ransom", Mark 10:45) and ἀντίλυτρον (1 Tim 2:6) are the fulfilment the Christian reader hears. `[I]`

**Gen 2:7 → 33:4** *(moderate, Move 1 only)*

- God breathes נִשְׁמַת חַיִּים ("the breath of life") into man's nostrils. Elihu: the breath of the Almighty gives me life — the claim of every man, which Elihu makes the ground of his authority.

**Internal (Move 4).**

- *Answers §earlier:* the quotations listed under Headline Findings. And **35:11** "who teaches us more than the beasts of the earth and makes us wiser than the birds of the heavens" inverts Job's "ask the beasts … the birds of the heavens" (12:7) — both lemma-pairs in both verses (WLC). *High.*
- *Planted for later:*
  - **34:35; 35:16** → 38:2. *High.*
  - **37:14 נִפְלְאוֹת** → 42:3. *High.*
  - **37:15–16 "Do you know…?"** → 38:33; 39:1. The interrogative forms הֲתֵדַע / הֲיָדַעְתָּ occur in Job only at these four verses (surface search, WLC). *High.*
  - **37:2 "the rumbling (רֹגֶז) of His voice"** → 38:1. *Moderate.*
  - **37:6 snow** → 38:22 "the storehouses of the snow". *Moderate.*
  - **37:23 "the Almighty — we cannot find Him (מְצָאנֻהוּ)"** answers 23:3 ("that I might find Him"). God then comes to Job. *High on the words.*
  - **32:2 anger** → 42:7. *High.*

### 16. So What? (brief)

- **Intended response (Elihu's).** Humble yourself under God's discipline and consider his wonders. **The book's.** Take what is true in Elihu — God speaks through affliction; a mediator ransoms; creation displays God — and leave his anger.
- **Candidate FCF.** The young zealot's certainty; and, from the other side, the sufferer's need of a ransom.

### Christological note

- **The mediator and the ransom (33:23–24)** are the richest single point of the mediator trajectory. They are Hebrew-only.
- The angel is "one out of a thousand", not God himself. Christ is both the mediator and the one who gives himself as the ransom (1 Tim 2:5–6). **Trajectory with escalation.** *Moderate–high.*

### Passage-specific difficulties

- **Elihu's status.** *Interpretive.* Name the options; do not caricature him as a windbag or canonise him as the book's answer.
- **36:14 "among the cult prostitutes (בַּקְּדֵשִׁים)".** *Translational.* NASB95 and ESV so render it; NIV84 "among male prostitutes of the shrines". It describes the shameful death of the godless young; it is not a topic for a sermon on Elihu.

## Unit 15 — 38:1–40:5 ⭐ The LORD Answers from the Whirlwind

> 38:1–2 וַיַּעַן־יְהוָה אֶת־אִיּוֹב מִן הַסְּעָרָה וַיֹּאמַר׃ מִי זֶה מַחְשִׁיךְ עֵצָה בְמִלִּין בְּלִי־דָעַת
> Then the LORD answered Job out of the whirlwind and said, "Who is this that darkens counsel by words without knowledge?"
>
> 38:4 אֵיפֹה הָיִיתָ בְּיָסְדִי־אָרֶץ הַגֵּד אִם־יָדַעְתָּ בִינָה
> "Where were you when I laid the foundation of the earth? Tell Me, if you have understanding." *(NASB95)*

### Headline Findings

- **God answers Job's opening curse point by point.** `[T]` (synthetic)
  - This is Headline Finding 1 of the sweep, worked here at unit level.
  - **Womb and doors.** Job cursed the night because "it did not shut the doors (דַּלְתֵי) of my mother's womb" and asked "Why did I not die at birth (מֵרֶחֶם)?" (3:10–11). God: "Who enclosed the sea with doors (בִדְלָתַיִם) when, bursting forth, it went out from the womb (מֵרֶחֶם)?" (38:8).
  - **The dawn.** Job asked that the night "not see the eyelids of the dawn (שָׁחַר)" (3:9). God: "Have you ever in your life commanded the morning, and caused the dawn (שַׁחַר) to know its place?" (38:12).
  - **Deep darkness.** Job summoned צַלְמָוֶת ("deep darkness") on his day (3:5). God: "Have you seen the gates of deep darkness (צַלְמָוֶת)?" (38:17).
  - **Free and the taskmaster.** Job longed for Sheol, where the slave is "free (חָפְשִׁי)" and hears not "the voice of the taskmaster (נוֹגֵשׂ)" (3:18–19). God: "Who sent out the wild donkey free (חָפְשִׁי)? … he does not hear the shoutings of the driver (נוֹגֵשׂ)" (39:5, 7).
  - **What it shows.** God's speech is an answer to the man who wished un-creation on his birthday: God speaks of creation, the sea at its birth, the dawn in its place, and a freedom that is found in life, not death.
  - *Surfaced by:* Move 4, Vocabulary, Structure. *High on the words; moderate–high on the design.*
- **God questions; he does not explain.** `[T]`
  - 29 of the 70 verses of 38:2–39:30 carry an interrogative (surface search, WLC). God never mentions the Satan, the wager, or Job's sin. He asks where Job was, what he knows, what he can do.
  - The question-form was prepared by Elihu (37:15–16) and by the wisdom poem ("where is the place?", 28:12, 20 → 38:19, 24).
  - *High.*
- **The "place" of each thing is known to God.** `[T]` (synthetic)
  - The dawn is caused "to know its place (מְקֹמוֹ)" (38:12); light and darkness have a "place (מְקֹמוֹ)" (38:19).
  - The wisdom poem asked "where is the place (מְקוֹם) of understanding?" and answered "God … knows its place (מְקוֹמָהּ)" (28:12, 20, 23) (lemma, WLC).
  - The creation speech is the demonstration of 28:23. *Moderate–high.*

### 2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

- **Preceding movement.** Job has signed his case: "let the Almighty answer me" (31:35). Elihu has spoken of the storm (37). `[T]`
- **Necessity answer.** Only God's answer can end the book; every human voice has been heard and has failed to resolve it. God answers — "the LORD answered Job" (38:1) — and the first divine speech since chapter 2 is given the name YHWH, missing from the dialogue since 12:9. The answer must come out of the storm that has been building (37), and must address the curse that began the dialogue (3). `[T]` / `[I]`
- **Implication.** God's answer is not the answer Job asked for, but it is an answer. Preach it as the LORD speaking to Job — not about him. `[I]`

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 38:1–3 | Summons | "Words without knowledge"; "gird up your loins like a man" |
| 38:4–21 | Earth, sea, dawn, deep, light | Foundations; sea's doors; dawn's place; gates of death; "you know, for you were born then" |
| 38:22–38 | Weather and stars | Snow, hail, rain, thunderbolt, ice; Pleiades, Orion; clouds |
| 38:39–39:30 | Ten creatures | Lion, raven, mountain goat, deer, wild donkey, wild ox, ostrich, horse, hawk, eagle |
| 40:1–2 | Challenge | "Will the faultfinder contend with the Almighty?" |
| 40:3–5 | Job's first reply | "I am insignificant … I lay my hand on my mouth" |

**Device.** A cosmic tour from the earth's foundations to the eagle's nest, told as questions. `[T]`

### 6. Narrator's Comment

- **38:1** — "the LORD answered Job out of the whirlwind". The ketiv writes מִן הַסְּעָרָה as one word (מנהסערה), as at 40:6 (WLC). `[T]`
- **40:1, 3, 6** — the dialogue formulas return. Only God and Job speak in 38:1–42:6. `[T]`

### 7. Vocabulary

- **סְעָרָה ("whirlwind", 38:1; 40:6).** In 9:17 Job said God "bruises me with a tempest (בִּשְׂעָרָה)" — the by-form (see the Text-First Declaration). The storm he feared as the instrument of his crushing becomes the place from which God speaks. **(synthetic)** *Moderate–high.*
- **"Where were you…? (אֵיפֹה הָיִיתָ)" (38:4) and "you were born then (אָז תִּוָּלֵד)" (38:21).** Eliphaz: "Were you the first man to be born (תִּוָּלֵד), or were you brought forth before the hills?" (15:7). ילד ("be born") in both (lemma, WLC). God's irony at 38:21 is the question Eliphaz put first, asked now with authority. *Moderate–high.*
- **Pleiades and Orion (כִּימָה, כְּסִיל, 38:31).** Job praised God who "makes the Bear, Orion (כְּסִיל) and the Pleiades (כִימָה)" (9:9). Amos 5:8 has the same pair. God asks Job whether he can bind them. `[T]`
- **חֻקּוֹת שָׁמַיִם ("the ordinances of the heavens", 38:33).** The phrase occurs at Job 38:33 and Jer 33:25 only (WLC). In Jeremiah God's covenant with David is as fixed as "the fixed patterns of heaven and earth". *Moderate.*
- **The ostrich (39:13–18).** "God has made her forget wisdom (חָכְמָה), and has not given her a share of understanding (בִּינָה)" (39:17). The pair of 28:12, 20, 28 (lemma, WLC). God gives and withholds wisdom as he pleases. *Moderate–high.*
- **"I am insignificant (קַלֹּתִי)" (40:4).** קלל — the root of "curse" (3:1) and of "light, swift" (7:6; 9:25; 24:18) (lemma 7043, WLC). The man who began by cursing (קלל) his day now says "I am light (קלל)". The homograph-heavy lemma limits confidence. *Moderate.*
- **Proper-noun inventory.** None but the constellations: Pleiades, Orion (38:31), Mazzaroth (38:32), the Bear (38:32).

### 8. Translations

- **40:2 "the faultfinder" (יִסּוֹר).** NASB95 "Will the faultfinder contend with the Almighty?"; ESV "Shall a faultfinder contend…?"; NIV84 "Will the one who contends with the Almighty correct him?" The Hebrew is difficult. The second line — "Let him who reproves (מוֹכִיחַ) God answer it" — is secure, and takes up 9:33 (see Headline Finding 3 of the sweep).
- **Ancient versions check.** Swete's 38:1 ("after Elihu had ceased speaking") — see unit 14. *Category 2.*
- **Pulpit divergence note.** N/A — no pulpit text declared.

### 10. Repetition

- **Laughter (שׂחק).** The wild donkey "scorns (יִשְׂחַק) the tumult of the city" (39:7); the ostrich "laughs (תִּשְׂחַק) at the horse and his rider" (39:18); the war-horse "laughs (יִשְׂחַק) at fear" (39:22) (lemma, WLC). Job's laughter has become others' scorn of him (12:4; 30:1). In God's world the creatures laugh. `[T]`
- **Know (ידע).** 38:3, 4, 5, 12, 18, 21, 33; 39:1, 2 (lemma, WLC). God's refrain is "Do you know?" `[T]`

### 11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

**Ps 147:9; Luke 12:24 → 38:41** *(high)*

- *Source context.* "He gives to the beast its food, and to the young ravens (לִבְנֵי עֹרֵב) which cry (יִקְרָאוּ)" (Ps 147:9).
- *Book usage.* In Job 38:41 the ravens' young "cry to God (יְשַׁוְּעוּ)" — the verb of Job's own unanswered cries (19:7; 24:12; 30:20, 28) (lemma, WLC).
- *NT.* "Consider the ravens … God feeds them" (Luke 12:24, SBLGNT κόρακας).
- *What it adds.* The God who seemed not to hear Job's cry (30:20) is the God who hears the raven's. **(synthetic)** *High on the words.*

**Matt 24:28 → 39:30** *(moderate)*

- "Where the slain are, there is he (the eagle)" (39:30); "Wherever the corpse is, there the vultures will gather" (Matt 24:28). Proverbial; Move 1 only.

**Jer 33:25 → 38:33** *(moderate, Move 1 only)*

- See Vocabulary.

**Internal (Move 4).**

- *Answers §earlier:*
  - 38:8–17 and 39:5–7 answer chapter 3. *High.*
  - 38:2 answers 34:35 and 35:16 ("without knowledge"). *High.*
  - 38:7 "all the sons of God (בְּנֵי אֱלֹהִים) shouted for joy" answers 1:6 and 2:1, where the sons of God came before the LORD — and the Satan with them. At creation the sons of God rejoice; the accuser is absent. בְּנֵי (הָ)אֱלֹהִים occurs in the Hebrew Bible only at Gen 6:2, 4; Job 1:6; 2:1; 38:7 (WLC). *High on the words.*
  - 38:12–15 answers 24:13–17 — the dawn shakes the wicked from the earth. *Moderate–high.*
  - 38:21 answers 15:7. *Moderate–high.*
  - 38:25 = 28:26. *High.*
  - 38:41 answers 30:20. *High.*
  - 39:17 answers 28. *Moderate–high.*
  - 40:2 answers 9:33 and 31:35 — Job asked for an answer; God asks him for one. *High.*
  - 40:4 "I lay my hand on my mouth" answers 21:5 and 29:9, where Job said others did so. *High.*
  - 40:5 "Once I have spoken … twice" answers Elihu's 33:14 and echoes Ps 62:12. *Moderate.*
- *Planted for later:*
  - **40:2 רִיב ("contend")** → 42:3–6. *Moderate.*
  - **38:3 "I will ask you, and you instruct Me (וְהוֹדִיעֵנִי)"** → repeated at 40:7, and quoted by Job at 42:4. *High.*

### 16. So What? (brief)

- **Intended response.** Silence before the Creator; and wonder.
- **Worldview.** The world is not organised around my case. God governs sea, dawn, rain and raven, and delights in creatures that are no use to man.
- **Candidate FCF.** The demand that God explain himself.
- **Prayer.** "I lay my hand on my mouth" (40:4).

### Christological note

- **The voice from the storm.** The LORD speaks out of the סְעָרָה; in the Gospels Christ rebukes the storm, and the disciples ask, "Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey Him?" (Mark 4:41). The question God asks Job about the sea (38:8–11) is answered in the boat. **(synthetic)** *Moderate.*
- **Moralism check.** "Be humble like Job" misses the point. The speech reveals God before it corrects Job.

### Passage-specific difficulties

- **God never mentions the Satan or the wager.** *Doctrinal / pastoral.* The reader knows what Job never learns. The sufferer is not owed the explanation.
- **"Gird up your loins like a man" (38:3).** *Pastoral.* God treats Job as a man able to answer — a dignity, not only a rebuke.

## Unit 16 — 40:6–42:6 ⭐ Behemoth, Leviathan, and "Now My Eye Sees You"

> 40:8 הַאַף תָּפֵר מִשְׁפָּטִי תַּרְשִׁיעֵנִי לְמַעַן תִּצְדָּק
> "Will you really annul My judgement? Will you condemn Me that you may be justified?"
>
> 42:5–6 לְשֵׁמַע־אֹזֶן שְׁמַעְתִּיךָ וְעַתָּה עֵינִי רָאָתְךְ׃ עַל־כֵּן אֶמְאַס וְנִחַמְתִּי עַל־עָפָר וָאֵפֶר
> "I have heard of You by the hearing of the ear; but now my eye sees You; therefore I retract, and I repent in dust and ashes." *(NASB95)*

### Headline Findings

- **God names the charge: Job has condemned God to justify himself.** `[T]`
  - "Will you condemn Me (תַּרְשִׁיעֵנִי) that you may be justified (תִּצְדָּק)?" (40:8).
  - Job's words: "Do not condemn me (אַל־תַּרְשִׁיעֵנִי)" (10:2); "Though I am righteous (אֶצְדָּק), my mouth will condemn me (יַרְשִׁיעֵנִי)" (9:20). "Will you annul (תָּפֵר)?" takes Eliphaz's "you annul (תָּפֵר) the fear of God" (15:4) (lemma, WLC).
  - **What it shows.** God's charge is not Job's alleged sins (the friends' charge) but Job's argument: to be righteous, he has made God unjust. The lawsuit vocabulary of the dialogue (Headline Finding 3 of the sweep) is summed up in one verse.
  - *High.*
- **Job's "cruel" God answers with his own fierce creature, and a question Paul quotes.** `[T]`
  - "No one is so fierce (אַכְזָר) that he dares to arouse (יְעוּרֶנּוּ) him; who then is he that can stand (יִתְיַצָּב) before Me?" (41:10 [Heb 41:2]).
    - אַכְזָר answers 30:21.
    - עור ("rouse") answers 3:8, "those … prepared to rouse Leviathan".
    - יצב hithpael is the verb of the sons of God, and the Satan, "presenting themselves" before the LORD (1:6; 2:1) (lemma, WLC).
  - "Who has given to Me first, that I should repay him? Whatever is under the whole heaven is Mine" (41:11 [Heb 41:3]).
    - Paul: "Or who has first given to Him that it might be paid back to him again?" (Rom 11:35, SBLGNT), after Isa 40:13 at Rom 11:34.
    - Paul follows the Hebrew here, not Swete, whose 41:2 reads "who will withstand me and endure?" *Category 3.*
  - *High on the words; moderate–high on the 1:6 connection.*
- **Job's final reply quotes God twice and turns on a crux.** `[T]`
  - 42:3a quotes 38:2 ("Who is this that hides counsel without knowledge?"); 42:4 quotes 38:3 and 40:7 ("I will ask you, and you instruct Me") (WLC).
  - "I have heard of You … but now my eye sees You" (42:5) — the hope of 19:27, "my eyes will see", fulfilled in life.
  - 42:6 — the crux. Five words, each disputed:
    - אֶמְאַס — "I retract / I despise [myself] / I reject". No object is stated. The same form stands at 9:21, "I despise my life" (WLC).
    - וְנִחַמְתִּי עַל — "I repent in / I am comforted concerning / I relent of". נחם runs through the book (2:11; 42:11).
    - עָפָר וָאֵפֶר ("dust and ashes") — Job's own description of his state (30:19), and Abraham's before God (Gen 18:27). The phrase occurs only in these three verses (WLC).
  - *High on the words; the meaning is contested.*

### 2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

- **Preceding movement.** Job has laid his hand on his mouth but not yet answered (40:4–5). `[T]`
- **Necessity answer.** One speech has silenced Job. A second must bring him to confession. It does so by naming the charge (40:8), inviting Job to govern the proud (40:9–14), and showing two creatures that only God can master (40:15–41:34). Job's reply (42:1–6) must follow; without it the book would end in silence rather than seeing. `[I]`
- **Implication.** The Behemoth and Leviathan chapters are not natural-history filler; they are the argument of 40:8–14 made visible. `[I]`

### 3. Structure

| Verses (Eng) | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 40:6–7 | Summons | "Gird up your loins … I will ask you" |
| 40:8–14 | The charge and the challenge | "Will you condemn Me?" Put on majesty; humble the proud; "then your own right hand can save you" |
| 40:15–24 | Behemoth | "which I made as well as you"; "the first of the ways of God" |
| 41:1–34 | Leviathan | Can you catch him? Make a covenant with him? "Who can stand before Me?" "King over all the sons of pride" |
| 42:1–6 | Job's reply | "I know You can do all things … now my eye sees You" |

Hebrew 40:25–32 = English 41:1–8; Hebrew 41:1–26 = English 41:9–34. References give English with the Hebrew in brackets.

### 7. Vocabulary

- **"Adorn yourself with eminence and dignity, and clothe yourself with honour and majesty (וְהוֹד וְהָדָר תִּלְבָּשׁ)" (40:10).** The psalmist says of God, "You are clothed with splendour and majesty (הוֹד וְהָדָר לָבָשְׁתָּ)" (Ps 104:1). הוֹד with הָדָר occurs in eight verses (e.g. Ps 96:6; 104:1; 145:5), and with לבשׁ ("clothe") in Job 40:10 and Ps 104:1 (lemma, WLC). God invites Job to put on God's own garments. *High on the words.*
- **The crown and the glory.** Ps 8:6 [Eng 8:5]: God crowned man (תְּעַטְּרֵהוּ) "with glory (כָבוֹד) and majesty (הָדָר)". Job: God "has stripped my honour (כְּבוֹדִי) from me and removed the crown (עֲטֶרֶת) from my head" (19:9). כבוד with עטר occurs only at Ps 8:6 and Job 19:9 (lemma, WLC). Job 7:17 has already parodied Psalm 8 (unit 3). *Moderate–high* **(synthetic).**
- **"Your own right hand can save you (תּוֹשִׁעַ לְךָ יְמִינֶךָ)" (40:14).** "His right hand and His holy arm have gained the victory (הוֹשִׁיעָה) for Him" (Ps 98:1). "His own arm brought salvation (וַתּוֹשַׁע)" (Isa 59:16; 63:5) (lemma, WLC). Salvation by one's own right hand belongs to God alone. *Moderate–high.*
- **בְהֵמוֹת ("Behemoth", 40:15).** The plural of "beast". "Ask the beasts (בְהֵמוֹת), and let them teach you" (12:7) — the identical form (WLC). "Which I made as well as you (עִמָּךְ)": Job is set beside the beast as a fellow creature. *Moderate.*
- **"The first of the ways of God (רֵאשִׁית דַּרְכֵי־אֵל)" (40:19).** Wisdom: "The LORD possessed me at the beginning of His way (רֵאשִׁית דַּרְכּוֹ)" (Prov 8:22) (lemma, WLC). *Moderate.*
- **יַרְדֵּן ("the Jordan", 40:23).** The only occurrence in Job, and the book's only Israelite place-name (lemma, WLC). *Uncertain* as to significance; noted.
- **"A servant forever (עֶבֶד עוֹלָם)" (41:4 [Heb 40:28]).** The phrase of the bond-servant who chooses to stay (Deut 15:17) and of David in Achish's service (1 Sam 27:12) — the only three occurrences (WLC). "Will he make a covenant (בְרִית) with you?" Leviathan will not be domesticated. *Moderate.*
- **"The eyelids of the dawn (עַפְעַפֵּי־שָׁחַר)" (41:18 [Heb 41:10]).** Job's curse (3:9) had wished them unseen; God gives them to Leviathan. The phrase occurs only at 3:9 and 41:18 [Heb 41:10] (WLC). *High.*
- **"King over all the sons of pride (בְּנֵי־שָׁחַץ)" (41:34 [Heb 41:26]).** See 28:8. *High.*
- **Proper-noun inventory.** Behemoth; Leviathan (3:8; 40:25 [Eng 41:1]); the Jordan.

### 8. Translations

- **42:6.** NASB95 "Therefore I retract, and I repent in dust and ashes"; ESV "therefore I despise myself, and repent in dust and ashes"; NIV84 "Therefore I despise myself and repent in dust and ashes." The NASB95 and NIV84/ESV readings of אֶמְאַס differ materially. **Pulpit note:** a congregation hearing ESV or NIV84 hears self-loathing; NASB95 hears retraction of words. The Hebrew supplies no object; the preacher should say so.
- **Ancient versions check.**
  - **42:6** Swete: "Therefore I despised myself and melted away, and I consider myself earth and ashes (γῆν καὶ σποδόν)." *Category 1–2.* The self-contempt reading is ancient.
  - **40:25 [Eng 41:1]** Swete: "Will you draw a dragon (δράκοντα) with a hook?" The Greek names Leviathan δράκων, the word of Rev 12:9 ("the great dragon … the serpent of old, who is called the devil and Satan") (SBLGNT). *Category 1.* The Greek reader could hear Leviathan and the Satan together; the Hebrew does not make the identification. **(synthetic)** *Moderate.*
  - **41:11 [Heb 41:3]** — see Headline Finding 2. *Category 3.*

### 10. Repetition

- **"Can you…?"** The questions of 40:25–41:26 [Eng 41:1–34] ask whether Job can catch, bind, covenant with, sell, or pierce Leviathan. `[T]`
- **Pride (גאה, גָּאוֹן).** 40:10–12 ("look on everyone who is proud [גֵּאֶה], and humble him"); 41:34 [Heb 41:26] ("sons of pride"). `[T]`
- **Seeing.** "Look on (רְאֵה) everyone who is proud" (40:11–12); "now my eye sees (רָאָתְךְ) You" (42:5). `[T]`

### 11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

**Rom 11:35 ← 41:11 [Heb 41:3]** *(high)*

- *Source context.* God's claim of absolute ownership: no creature has given God anything he must repay.
- *NT text.* Rom 11:33–36 closes Paul's argument on Israel and the nations with a doxology built on Isa 40:13 (v.34) and Job 41:11 (v.35), ending "for from Him and through Him and to Him are all things."
- *Triage.* Category 3: Paul's Greek is closer to the Hebrew than to Swete.
- *What it adds.* Paul reads God's answer to Job as the ground of grace: God owes no one, so all he gives is gift. The Job text is not incidental in Romans 11; it is the last scriptural word before the doxology. *High.*

**Isa 27:1; Ps 74:14; 104:26 → Leviathan** *(high)*

- Leviathan the fleeing and twisting serpent whom the LORD will punish (Isa 27:1); whose heads God crushed (Ps 74:14); whom God formed to sport in the sea (Ps 104:26). Job 3:8 and 26:13 have already named him. In God's speech Leviathan is God's creature, untamed by man but not by God.
- *Move 4.* 41:10 [Heb 41:2] answers 3:8.

**Rev 12:9; 20:2 (via Swete δράκων)** *(moderate)*

- See Translations. The chaos-monster and the accuser converge in the Greek tradition and in Revelation. The Hebrew text of Job does not make the identification; the canonical reader may. **Trajectory, not equation.**

**Internal (Move 4).**

- *Answers §earlier:*
  - 40:8 answers 9:20; 10:2; 15:4. *High.*
  - 40:10 answers 19:9 (via Ps 8). *Moderate–high.*
  - 40:15 answers 12:7. *Moderate.*
  - 41:10 [Heb 41:2] answers 3:8; 30:21; 1:6. *High / moderate–high.*
  - 41:18 [Heb 41:10] answers 3:9. *High.*
  - 41:34 [Heb 41:26] answers 28:8. *High.*
  - 42:3–4 answers 38:2–3; 40:7. *High.*
  - 42:5 answers 19:26–27 and 23:8–9. *High.*
  - 42:6 answers 2:8; 30:19; 9:21. *High on the words.*
- *Planted for later:*
  - **42:6 נחם** → 42:11. *High.*

### 16. So What? (brief)

- **Intended response.** To see God, and to stop justifying oneself at God's expense.
- **Worldview.** God alone can govern pride and chaos. The creature that terrifies man is God's pet (compare Ps 104:26).
- **Candidate FCF.** Self-justification in suffering — defending my righteousness by accusing God.
- **Prayer.** "I have heard of You … but now my eye sees You."

### Christological note

- **"Your own right hand can save you" (40:14)** is the one thing Job cannot do. The arm of the LORD that saves (Isa 53:1; 59:16) is revealed in the servant. **Trajectory.** *Moderate–high.*
- **Leviathan and the dragon.** The one who can "stand before" God and master the serpent is God; Revelation shows the dragon defeated by the Lamb (Rev 12:9–11). **Trajectory via the canon, not the Hebrew of Job.** *Moderate.*
- **Seeing God (42:5).** "No one has seen God at any time; the only begotten God … has explained Him" (John 1:18). Job's seeing is a gift that anticipates the Son's revelation. *Moderate.*
- **Moralism check.** Job's repentance is not the key that unlocks restoration; God has already declared Job right (42:7) and restores him through intercession (42:10).

### Passage-specific difficulties

- **42:6.** *Translational / doctrinal.* Show the options. Do not build a sermon on self-loathing from ESV/NIV84 alone.
- **Behemoth and Leviathan identified as dinosaurs, or as purely mythical.** *Apologetic.* The text calls them God's creatures and uses them to humble human pride; either identification is beside the text's point.

## Unit 17 — 42:7–17 ⭐ "My Servant Job": Verdict, Prayer and Restoration

> 42:7 חָרָה אַפִּי בְךָ וּבִשְׁנֵי רֵעֶיךָ כִּי לֹא דִבַּרְתֶּם אֵלַי נְכוֹנָה כְּעַבְדִּי אִיּוֹב
> "My wrath is kindled against you and against your two friends, because you have not spoken of Me what is right as My servant Job has." *(NASB95)*
>
> 42:10 וַיהוָה שָׁב אֶת־שְׁבִית אִיּוֹב בְּהִתְפַּלְלוֹ בְּעַד רֵעֵהוּ
> The LORD restored the fortunes of Job when he prayed for his friends.

### Headline Findings

- **God's verdict turns on speech "to" God.** `[T]`
  - "You have not spoken to Me (אֵלַי) what is right (נְכוֹנָה)" (42:7, 8). NASB95 "of Me"; the preposition is אֶל.
  - Job alone addressed God in the dialogue (Headline Finding 2 of the sweep). Swete: ἐνώπιόν μου ("before Me").
  - *High on the words; the reading "to" rather than "of" is moderate–high.*
- **Job is "My servant" four times, and restored when he intercedes.** `[T]`
  - עַבְדִּי ("My servant") occurs once in 42:7 and three times in 42:8 (WLC), matching 1:8 and 2:3.
  - The friends must bring "seven bulls and seven rams" and go to Job; "My servant Job will pray for you. For I will accept him (פָנָיו אֶשָּׂא)" (42:8).
  - The LORD restored Job's fortunes "when he prayed for his friend (רֵעֵהוּ)" (42:10, singular, WLC).
  - **What it shows.** Eliphaz's promise that the innocent would deliver the guilty (22:30) is fulfilled against him. The accused becomes the intercessor.
  - *High.*
- **The restoration is doubled, except the children.** `[T]`
  - "The LORD increased all that Job had twofold (לְמִשְׁנֶה)" (42:10). The flocks and herds exactly double 1:3: 7,000 → 14,000 sheep; 3,000 → 6,000 camels; 500 → 1,000 yoke of oxen; 500 → 1,000 donkeys (42:12).
  - The children are not doubled: seven sons and three daughters (42:13), as at 1:2. **(synthetic)** The reader may infer that the first ten are not lost. `[I]` *Moderate.*
  - מִשְׁנֶה with שׁוב and hope: "Return to the stronghold, O prisoners of hope (הַתִּקְוָה); this very day I am declaring that I will restore double (מִשְׁנֶה) to you" (Zech 9:12). Isa 61:7: "instead of your shame you will have a double portion (מִשְׁנֶה)" (lemma, WLC). *Moderate.*

### 2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

- **Preceding movement.** Job has seen God and retracted (42:1–6). `[T]`
- **Necessity answer.** The prose frame must close as it opened: the heavenly verdict (1:8; 2:3 "My servant Job") must be restated on earth, the friends judged, the comforters of 2:11 replaced by true ones, and the losses of chapter 1 reversed. Without the epilogue God's approval of Job would be private; with it, it is public — and the friends' theology of retribution is judged, even as God blesses. `[I]`
- **Implication.** The epilogue does not vindicate retribution theology: the friends are condemned for it before Job is blessed. `[I]`

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 42:7–9 | The verdict | Wrath at the three; "My servant Job"; sacrifice and intercession; "the LORD accepted Job" |
| 42:10–11 | Restoration begins | Fortunes restored as Job prays; brothers and sisters comfort him; the money and the gold ring |
| 42:12–15 | Double blessing | Flocks doubled; seven sons, three named daughters; inheritance among the brothers |
| 42:16–17 | Death | 140 years; four generations; "old and full of days" |

### 7. Vocabulary

- **חרה ("to burn", of anger, 42:7).** God's anger falls on the three, not on Job and not on Elihu (see unit 14). `[T]`
- **"Folly" (נְבָלָה, 42:8).** "So that I may not do with you according to your folly (נְבָלָה)." The noun's only occurrence in Job; the root נבל gave Job's word for his wife's counsel, "as one of the foolish women (הַנְּבָלוֹת) speaks" (2:10) (lemma, WLC). The folly that Job refused in his wife is the friends'. *Moderate.*
- **"I will accept him (פָנָיו אֶשָּׂא)" (42:8, 9).** "To lift the face" — the idiom Job used against the friends: "Will you show partiality (הֲפָנָיו תִּשָּׂאוּן) for Him?" (13:8, 10) (lemma, WLC). They showed God partiality and are judged; Job's face is lifted. *High.*
- **שָׁב אֶת־שְׁבִית ("restored the fortunes", 42:10).** Ketiv שבית, qere שְׁבוּת (WLC). The idiom of restoration from exile: שׁוב with שְׁבוּת at Deut 30:3; Jer 29:14 (ketiv שבית, as here); Ps 126:4 (lemma, WLC). Job's restoration is told in the words Israel used for return from exile. *Moderate.*
- **קְשִׂיטָה ("a piece of money", 42:11).** Three times in the Hebrew Bible: Gen 33:19; Josh 24:32 (Jacob's purchase at Shechem); Job 42:11 (lemma, WLC). A patriarchal coin. **Setting note** — it supports the book's patriarchal colouring; it does not date the book. *Moderate.*
- **"A ring of gold (נֶזֶם זָהָב)" (42:11).** As Abraham's servant gave Rebekah (Gen 24:22). `[T]`
- **The daughters' names (42:14).** Jemimah (יְמִימָה, perhaps "dove"), Keziah (קְצִיעָה, "cassia"), Keren-happuch (קֶרֶן הַפּוּךְ, "horn of eye-paint"). The man who "laid my horn (קַרְנִי) in the dust" (16:15) names a daughter "horn" — but the lemma index splits the two (7163 against 7161). *Uncertain.*
- **שִׁבְעָנָה ("seven", 42:13).** An unusual form (WLC). `[T]`
- **"Old and full of days (זָקֵן וּשְׂבַע יָמִים)" (42:17).** The phrase is used of Isaac (Gen 35:29) and David (1 Chr 23:1); Abraham is "old and satisfied (זָקֵן וְשָׂבֵעַ)" (Gen 25:8) (WLC). Job dies as the patriarchs died. *High on the words.*

### 8. Translations

- **42:7 אֵלַי.** NASB95, ESV and NIV84 all have "of Me". **Pulpit note:** the congregation will not hear "to Me"; if the finding is used, say so.
- **Ancient versions check.**
  - **42:10** Swete: "and the Lord forgave them their sin" — an added clause. *Category 2.*
  - **42:11** Swete: ἀμνάδα ("a ewe-lamb") for קְשִׂיטָה. *Category 1–2.*
  - **42:14** Swete: Ἡμέρα ("Day"), Κασία, Ἀμαλθείας κέρας ("horn of Amalthea", the horn of plenty). *Category 1.*
  - **42:17a–e** Swete adds a long appendix: "It is written that he will rise again with those whom the Lord raises up." Job is identified with Jobab, living "in the land of Ausitis, on the borders of Idumaea and Arabia", "fifth from Abraham", said to be translated "from the Syriac book". *Category 2.* The resurrection sentence reads 19:25–26 the way Swete had translated it (ἀναστῆσαι). **None of this is in the Hebrew.**
- **Pulpit divergence note.** N/A — no pulpit text declared.

### 10. Repetition

- **The prologue's numbers and words return.** "My servant Job" (1:8; 2:3; 42:7–8); burnt offerings for others (1:5; 42:8); the flock figures (1:3; 42:12); seven sons and three daughters (1:2; 42:13); comfort (נחם, 2:11; 42:11); the evil (רָעָה) the LORD brought (2:11; 42:11). `[T]`
- **Absences.** The Satan does not return: all 14 occurrences of הַשָּׂטָן (in 11 verses) are in 1:6–2:7 (WLC). Nor does Job's wife; nor Elihu. `[T]`

### 11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

**Num 23:1, 29 → 42:8** *(moderate)*

- "Seven bulls and seven rams (שִׁבְעָה פָרִים וְשִׁבְעָה אֵילִים)": the exact phrase occurs at Num 23:1, 29 (Balaam's altars); 1 Chr 15:26 (the Levites bringing up the ark); Job 42:8 (WLC).
- *What it adds.* A large, patriarchal-style sacrifice outside Israel's cult, like Balaam's. The friends offer; Job prays. *Moderate.*

### 16. So What? (brief)

- **Intended response.** Trust God's verdict; pray for those who wronged you.
- **Worldview.** God declares right the one who spoke honestly to him, rebukes those who defended him falsely, and blesses at the end — freely.
- **Candidate FCF.** The fear that honest lament is sin; and the temptation to treat restoration as the point.
- **Prayer.** For the friends — for those who hurt us with their theology.

### Christological note

- **The righteous sufferer who intercedes for his accusers.** Job prays for the friends whose words wounded him; God accepts them for his sake. Christ "while being reviled, did not revile in return" (1 Pet 2:23); he "is at the right hand of God, who also intercedes for us" (Rom 8:34) and "always lives to make intercession" (Heb 7:25). **Type with escalation.** *High.*
- **Double restoration and resurrection.** Swete's appendix reads the epilogue as resurrection; the Hebrew does not. Keep the distinction.
- **Moralism check.** "Pray for your enemies and God will double your blessings" inverts the book. God blesses freely (42:12, "the LORD blessed").

### Passage-specific difficulties

- **The prosperity reading.** *Pastoral.* The epilogue is not a promise that every sufferer will be restored in this life. The friends' theology is condemned in the same breath.
- **Replacement children.** *Pastoral.* The ten new children do not erase the ten who died. The text's undoubled number allows (but does not state) that the first ten are counted still.

## Preaching Pitfalls

The overview's six book-level traps are all confirmed by the sweep. These are the passage-level pitfalls the sweep surfaced in addition.

1. **Hearing the friends' "invented sins" as wisdom (unit 11).**
   - *How it sounds:* 22:21–30 preached as an evangelistic invitation: "Yield now and be at peace with Him".
   - *Why it is wrong:* it follows 22:5–9, a list of sins Job did not commit. The invitation is conditioned on a false charge.
   - *Corrective:* 22:21–30 may be true of someone. It was false of Job. Read it with 22:5 and 42:7.

2. **Letting the ESV answer the attribution question at 24:18 (unit 11).**
   - *How it sounds:* "As Job's friends say, the wicked are swift on the face of the waters…"
   - *Why it is wrong:* "You say" is not in the Hebrew. NASB95 and NIV84 leave the words in Job's mouth.
   - *Corrective:* when preaching from the ESV, tell the congregation the quotation marks are the translators'.

3. **Crowning Elihu or dismissing him (unit 14).**
   - *How it sounds:* either "Elihu finally gets it right" or "Elihu is a pompous windbag".
   - *Why it is wrong:* the text gives both grounds — anger at Job (32:2) and the storm and the ransom (33:24; 37). God neither rebukes nor commends him.
   - *Corrective:* preach what Elihu says that the book confirms (God speaks through affliction; the storm), and what it does not (his anger).

4. **Building self-loathing out of 42:6 (unit 16).**
   - *How it sounds:* "Only when you despise yourself will you see God."
   - *Why it is wrong:* the verb has no object, NASB95 has "I retract", and the "dust and ashes" is the phrase of Abraham interceding (Gen 18:27).
   - *Corrective:* name the options; build on 42:5 ("now my eye sees You"), which is plain.

5. **Equating Leviathan with Satan from the Hebrew (unit 16).**
   - *How it sounds:* "Leviathan is the devil, and God tells Job he has him on a leash."
   - *Why it is wrong:* the Hebrew presents Leviathan as God's creature (41:1–34; Ps 104:26). The identification runs through the Greek δράκων and Revelation, not through Job.
   - *Corrective:* trace it as a canonical trajectory, flagged as such.

6. **Reading 28:28 as moralism (unit 12).**
   - *How it sounds:* "Be wise: fear God and avoid evil."
   - *Why it is wrong:* the poem's point is that wisdom is God's to give ("to man He said", 28:28), after every human search has failed.
   - *Corrective:* preach 28:28 as gift and as a verdict on the debate, and note that it describes the man of 1:1.
