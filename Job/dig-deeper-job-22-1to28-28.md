### Eliphaz (22:1–30)

*Closes with a petuḥah (פ) in BHS.*

> **22:1** וַ֭יַּעַן אֱלִיפַ֥ז הַֽתֵּמָנִ֗י וַיֹּאמַֽר׃
>
> Then Eliphaz the Temanite responded,

> **22:2** הַלְאֵ֥ל יִסְכָּן־גָּ֑בֶרᵃ כִּֽי־יִסְכֹּ֖ן עָלֵ֣ימֹוᵇ מַשְׂכִּֽיל׃
>
> “Can a vigorous man be of use to God, Or a wise man be useful to himself?

> **22:3** הַחֵ֣פֶץ לְ֭שַׁדַּי כִּ֣י תִצְדָּ֑ק וְאִם־בֶּ֝֗צַע כִּֽי־תַתֵּ֥ם דְּרָכֶֽיךָ׃
>
> “Is there any pleasure to the Almighty if you are righteous, Or profit if you make your ways perfect?

> **22:4** הֲֽ֭מִיִּרְאָ֣תְךָ יֹכִיחֶ֑ךָ יָבֹ֥וא עִ֝מְּךָ֗ בַּמִּשְׁפָּֽט׃
>
> “Is it because of your reverence that He reproves you, That He enters into judgment against you?

> **22:5** הֲלֹ֣א רָעָֽתְךָ֣ רַבָּ֑ה וְאֵֽין־קֵ֝֗ץ לַעֲוֹנֹתֶֽיךָ׃
>
> “Is not your wickedness great, And your iniquities without end?

> **22:6** כִּֽי־תַחְבֹּ֣ל אַחֶ֣יךָ חִנָּ֑ם וּבִגְדֵ֖י עֲרוּמִּ֣ים תַּפְשִֽׁיט׃
>
> “For you have taken pledges of your brothers without cause, And stripped men naked.

> **22:7** לֹא־מַ֭יִם עָיֵ֣ף תַּשְׁקֶ֑ה וּ֝מֵרָעֵ֗ב תִּֽמְנַֽע־לָֽחֶם׃
>
> “To the weary you have given no water to drink, And from the hungry you have withheld bread.

> **22:8** וְאִ֣ישׁ זְ֭רֹועַ לֹ֣ו הָאָ֑רֶץ וּנְשׂ֥וּא פָ֝נִ֗ים יֵ֣שֶׁב בָּֽהּ׃
>
> “But the earth belongs to the mighty man, And the honorable man dwells in it.

> **22:9** אַ֭לְמָנֹות שִׁלַּ֣חְתָּ רֵיקָ֑ם וּזְרֹעֹ֖ות יְתֹמִ֣ים יְדֻכָּֽאᵃ׃
>
> “You have sent widows away empty, And the strength of the orphans has been crushed.

> **22:10** עַל־כֵּ֭ן סְבִיבֹותֶ֣יךָ פַחִ֑ים וִֽ֝יבַהֶלְךָᵃ פַּ֣חַד פִּתְאֹֽם׃
>
> “Therefore snares surround you, And sudden dread terrifies you,

> **22:11** ᵃאֹו־חֹ֥שֶׁךְᵃ לֹֽא־תִרְאֶ֑ה וְֽשִׁפְעַת־מַ֥יִם תְּכַסֶּֽךָּ׃
>
> Or darkness, so that you cannot see, And an abundance of water covers you.

> **22:12** הֲֽלֹא־אֱ֭לֹוהַּ גֹּ֣בַהּ שָׁמָ֑יִם וּרְאֵ֤ה רֹ֖אשׁ כֹּוכָבִ֣ים כִּי־רָֽמּוּ׃
>
> “Is not God in the height of heaven? Look also at the distant stars, how high they are!

> **22:13** וְֽ֭אָמַרְתָּ מַה־יָּ֣דַֽע אֵ֑ל הַבְעַ֖ד עֲרָפֶ֣ל יִשְׁפֹּֽוט׃
>
> “You say, ‘What does God know? Can He judge through the thick darkness?

> **22:14** עָבִ֣ים סֵֽתֶר־לֹ֖ו וְלֹ֣א יִרְאֶ֑ה וְח֥וּג שָׁ֝מַ֗יִם יִתְהַלָּֽךְ׃
>
> ‘Clouds are a hiding place for Him, so that He cannot see; And He walks on the vault of heaven.’

> **22:15** הַאֹ֣רַח עֹולָ֣ם תִּשְׁמֹ֑ר אֲשֶׁ֖ר דָּרְכ֣וּ מְתֵי־אָֽוֶן׃
>
> “Will you keep to the ancient path Which wicked men have trod,

> **22:16** אֲשֶֽׁר־קֻמְּט֥וּ וְלֹאᵃ־עֵ֑ת נָ֝הָ֗ר יוּצַ֥ק יְסֹודָֽם׃
>
> Who were snatched away before their time, Whose foundations were washed away by a river?

> **22:17** הָאֹמְרִ֣ים לָ֭אֵל ס֣וּר מִמֶּ֑נּוּ וּמַה־יִּפְעַ֖ל שַׁדַּ֣י לָֽמֹוᵃ׃
>
> “They said to God, ‘Depart from us!’ And ‘What can the Almighty do to them?’

> **22:18** וְה֤וּא מִלֵּ֣א בָתֵּיהֶ֣ם טֹ֑וב וַעֲצַ֥ת רְ֝שָׁעִ֗ים רָ֣חֲקָה מֶֽנִּי׃
>
> “Yet He filled their houses with good things; But the counsel of the wicked is far from me.

> **22:19** יִרְא֣וּ צַדִּיקִ֣ים וְיִשְׂמָ֑חוּ וְ֝נָקִ֗י יִלְעַג־לָֽמֹו׃
>
> “The righteous see and are glad, And the innocent mock them,

> **22:20** אִם־לֹ֣א נִכְחַ֣ד קִימָ֑נוּ וְ֝יִתְרָ֗ם אָ֣כְלָה אֵֽשׁ׃
>
> Saying, ‘Truly our adversaries are cut off, And their abundance the fire has consumed.’

> **22:21** הַסְכֶּן־נָ֣א עִמֹּ֑ו וּשְׁלםᵃ בָּ֝הֶ֗ם תְּֽבֹואַתְךָ֥ᵇ טֹובָֽה׃
>
> “Yield now and be at peace with Him; Thereby good will come to you.

> **22:22** קַח־נָ֣א מִפִּ֣יו תֹּורָ֑ה וְשִׂ֥ים אֲ֝מָרָ֗יו בִּלְבָבֶֽךָ׃
>
> “Please receive instruction from His mouth And establish His words in your heart.

> **22:23** אִם־תָּשׁ֣וּב עַד־שַׁ֭דַּי תִּבָּנֶ֑ה תַּרְחִ֥יק עַ֝וְלָ֗ה מֵאָהֳלֶֽךָ׃
>
> “If you return to the Almighty, you will be restored; If you remove unrighteousness far from your tent,

> **22:24** וְשִׁיתᵃ־עַל־עָפָ֥ר בָּ֑צֶר וּבְצ֖וּרᵇ נְחָלִ֣ים אֹופִֽיר׃
>
> And place your gold in the dust, And the gold of Ophir among the stones of the brooks,

> **22:25** וְהָיָ֣ה שַׁדַּ֣י בְּצָרֶ֑יךָ וְכֶ֖סֶף תֹּועָפֹ֣ות לָֽךְ׃
>
> Then the Almighty will be your gold And choice silver to you.

> **22:26** כִּי־אָ֭ז עַל־שַׁדַּ֣י תִּתְעַנָּ֑ג וְתִשָּׂ֖א אֶל־אֱלֹ֣והַּ פָּנֶֽיךָ׃
>
> “For then you will delight in the Almighty And lift up your face to God.

> **22:27** תַּעְתִּ֣יר אֵ֭לָיו וְיִשְׁמָעֶ֑ךָּ וּנְדָרֶ֥יךָ תְשַׁלֵּֽם׃
>
> “You will pray to Him, and He will hear you; And you will pay your vows.

> **22:28** וְֽתִגְזַר־אֹ֖ומֶר וְיָ֣קָם לָ֑ךְ וְעַל־דְּ֝רָכֶ֗יךָ נָ֣גַֽהּ אֹֽור׃
>
> “You will also decree a thing, and it will be established for you; And light will shine on your ways.

> **22:29** כִּֽי־ᵃהִ֭שְׁפִּילוּ וַתֹּ֣אמֶרᵃ גֵּוָ֑ה וְשַׁ֖ח עֵינַ֣יִם יֹושִֽׁעַ׃
>
> “When you are cast down, you will speak with confidence, And the humble person He will save.

> **22:30** יְֽמַלֵּ֥ט אִֽיᵃ־נָקִ֑י וְ֝נִמְלַ֗ט בְּבֹ֣ר כַּפֶּֽיךָ׃  פ
>
> “He will deliver one who is not innocent, And he will be delivered through the cleanness of your hands.”

### Job (23:1–24:25)

*No marker after 23:17; closes with a setumah (ס) after 24:25 in BHS.*

> **23:1** וַיַּ֥עַן אִיֹּ֗וב וַיֹּאמַֽר׃
>
> Then Job replied,

> **23:2** גַּם־הַ֭יֹּום מְרִ֣י שִׂחִ֑י יָ֝דִ֗י כָּבְדָ֥ה עַל־אַנְחָתִֽי׃
>

>
> “Oh that I knew where I might find Him, That I might come to His seat!

> **23:4** אֶעֶרְכָ֣ה לְפָנָ֣יו מִשְׁפָּ֑ט וּ֝פִ֗י אֲמַלֵּ֥א תֹוכָחֹֽות׃
>
> “I would present my case before Him And fill my mouth with arguments.

> **23:5** אֵ֭דְעָה מִלִּ֣ים יַעֲנֵ֑נִי וְ֝אָבִ֗ינָה מַה־יֹּ֥אמַר לִֽי׃

> “I would learn the words which He would answer, And perceive what He would say to me.

>
> “There the upright would reason with Him; And I would be delivered forever from my Judge.

> **23:8** הֵ֤ן קֶ֣דֶם אֶהֱלֹ֣ךְ וְאֵינֶ֑נּוּ וְ֝אָחֹ֗ור וְֽלֹא־אָבִ֥ין לֹֽו׃
>
> “Behold, I go forward but He is not there, And backward, but I cannot perceive Him;

>
> “But He knows the way I take; When He has tried me, I shall come forth as gold.

> **23:11** בַּ֭אֲשֻׁרֹו אָחֲזָ֣ה רַגְלִ֑י דַּרְכֹּ֖ו שָׁמַ֣רְתִּי וְלֹא־אָֽט׃
>
> “My foot has held fast to His path; I have kept His way and not turned aside.

> **23:12** מִצְוַ֣ת שְׂ֭פָתָיו וְלֹ֣אᵃ אָמִ֑ישׁ מֵ֝חֻקִּ֗יᵇ צָפַ֥נְתִּי אִמְרֵי־פִֽיו׃

> “I have not departed from the command of His lips; I have treasured the words of His mouth more than my necessary food.

> **23:13** וְה֣וּא בְ֭אֶחָד וּמִ֣יᵃ יְשִׁיבֶ֑נּוּ וְנַפְשֹׁ֖ו אִוְּתָ֣ה וַיָּֽעַשׂ׃
>
> “But He is unique and who can turn Him? And what His soul desires, that He does.

> **23:14** כִּ֭י יַשְׁלִ֣ים חֻקִּ֑י וְכָהֵ֖נָּה רַבֹּ֣ות עִמֹּֽו׃
>
> “For He performs what is appointed for me, And many such decrees are with Him.

> **23:15** עַל־כֵּ֭ן מִפָּנָ֣יו אֶבָּהֵ֑ל אֶ֝תְבֹּונֵ֗ן וְאֶפְחַ֥ד מִמֶּֽנּוּ׃
>
> “Therefore, I would be dismayed at His presence; When I consider, I am terrified of Him.

> **23:16** וְ֭אֵל הֵרַ֣ךְ לִבִּ֑י וְ֝שַׁדַּ֗י הִבְהִילָֽנִי׃
>
> “It is God who has made my heart faint, And the Almighty who has dismayed me,

> **23:17** כִּֽי־לֹ֣א נִ֭צְמַתִּי מִפְּנֵי־חֹ֑שֶׁךְ וּ֝מִפָּנַ֗יᵃ כִּסָּה־אֹֽפֶל׃

> But I am not silenced by the darkness, Nor deep gloom which covers me.

>
> “Why are times not stored up by the Almighty, And why do those who know Him not see His days?

>
> “Some remove the landmarks; They seize and devour flocks.

>
> “They drive away the donkeys of the orphans; They take the widow’s ox for a pledge.

> **24:4** יַטּ֣וּ אֶבְיֹונִ֣ים מִדָּ֑רֶךְ יַ֥חַד חֻ֝בְּא֗וּ עֲנִיֵּי־אָֽרֶץ׃

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims, each surfaced by several tools:

1. **The third round is where the conversation ends. Eliphaz turns from describing the wicked man to accusing Job to his face; after that, no one in the debate speaks to anyone. Job addresses neither the friends nor God through forty-two verses, Bildad addresses no one, Zophar does not speak, and Job's last words to the friends are three verses of sarcasm and two of reproach.** **New** for the address counts. `[T]` for the counts; `[I]` for the design
   - **Eliphaz's direct accusation.** Verses containing a second-person singular, by friend's speech (WLC morphology):
     - round one: Eliphaz 17 of 48; Bildad 10 of 22; Zophar 12 of 20;
     - round two: Eliphaz 11 of 35; Bildad 3 of 21; Zophar 1 of 29;
     - **round three: Eliphaz 22 of 30**; Bildad 0 of 6; Zophar no speech.
     - Eliphaz's last speech is the most direct of the nine friends' speeches (73% of its verses). In round two the friends spoke about "the wicked man"; Eliphaz now says "you": "Is not your wickedness great?" (22:5); "you have taken pledges of your brothers" (22:6).
   - **Then silence.** Job's reply, 23:1–24:25, has **no second-person form in any of its 42 verses**: it speaks of God in the third person ("Oh that I knew where I might find Him", 23:3) and of the oppressors in the third person. Bildad's six verses (25:1–6) address no one. Job's only singulars in the round are to Bildad, in sarcasm: "What a help you are to the weak!" (26:2–4). His only plurals are to the three: "Far be it from me that I should declare you right" (27:5); "I will instruct you … why then do you act foolishly?" (27:11–12). Chapter 28 addresses no one.
   - **No address to God.** Job does not speak to God anywhere in 22:1–28:28. His verses containing a second-person singular fall from 50 in round one to 9 in round two (the 15–21 dig found his address to God ending at 17:4) to 3 in round three, all of them to Bildad. He will not address God again until 30:20–23.
   - **The text registers the end three ways** `[S: audit 4]`:
     - Bildad's last speech is half borrowed: 25:4b repeats Job's 9:2b, and 25:5b repeats Eliphaz's 15:15b;
     - the formula changes: Job last "answers" (וַיַּעַן) at 26:1; at 27:1 and 29:1 he "takes up his discourse again", and "answered" returns for him only after the LORD speaks (40:3; 42:1);
     - the narrator says it: "these three men ceased answering Job" (32:1), and Elihu is angry "because they had found no answer" (32:3).
   - **The friends' share shrinks.** Verses spoken by the friends: 90 in round one, 85 in round two, **36** in round three (WLC). `[T]`
   - *Surfaced by:* Structure (the address test), Narrator's Comment, Repetition, claim audit 4. *High on the data; moderate–high on the design* `[S: audit 4]`.

2. **Eliphaz's last speech joins invented charges to a true offer, and both come back to him. Job turns the charges into a description of the oppressors God leaves unpunished, then into his own record and oath; Eliphaz's offer speaks in the words of Isaiah 58, and is fulfilled at the end of the book with Eliphaz as the one rescued.** `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the design
   - **The charges** (22:6–9): pledges taken "without cause", the naked stripped, water and bread withheld, widows sent away empty, orphans crushed. Nothing in the book supports them; the narrator and the LORD have called Job blameless (1:1, 8; 2:3). `[T]`
   - **Job's first answer: the charges fit the oppressors, and God does nothing** (24:2–12). **New** for the exclusive pairs:
     - **"Pledge"** (חבל I, "take in pledge"): in Job only 22:6 ("you have taken pledges of your brothers"), 24:3 ("they take the widow's ox for a pledge") and 24:9 ("against the poor they take a pledge") (lemma 2254 a).
     - **Widow + orphan** in one verse: in Job only 22:9 and 24:3 (lemma).
     - **Naked** (עָרוֹם): 22:6 → 24:7, 10 ("naked without clothing"); and the word of 1:21 ("Naked I came from my mother's womb") runs on to 26:6 ("Naked is Sheol before Him") (sweep; verified).
     - **The baseline.** 22:5–11's closest neighbour anywhere in Job is chapter 24 (five shared rare lemmas: pledge, orphan, widow, naked, hungry). Its prominence (z = 3.89) is ordinary against sixty random seven-verse Job passages (median 3.65), so the link rests on the exclusive pairs, not on a window. `[T]`
   - **Job's second and third answers: his record and his oath.** "I delivered … the orphan who had no helper … I made the widow's heart sing" (29:12–13); "If I have seen anyone perish for lack of clothing (מִבְּלִי לְבוּשׁ)" (31:19) — the phrase of 24:7, 10, and in Job only there (lemma); "If I have … caused the eyes of the widow to fail, or have eaten my morsel alone, and the orphan has not shared it" (31:16–17) (sweep; verified). *High.*
   - **The offer speaks in the words of Isaiah 58.** **New.**
     - Isaiah's true fast: "Is it not to divide your bread with the hungry (לָרָעֵב לַחְמֶךָ) … when you see the naked (עָרֹם), to cover him?" (Isa 58:7). "Then (אָז) your light will break out like the dawn" (58:8); "Then you will call, and the LORD will answer" (58:9); "your light will rise in darkness" (58:10); "Then (אָז) you will take delight in the LORD (תִּתְעַנַּג עַל־יְהוָה)" (58:14).
     - Eliphaz: Job withheld bread from the hungry (רָעֵב, לֶחֶם) and stripped the naked (22:6–7); if he returns, "then (אָז) you will delight in the Almighty (עַל־שַׁדַּי תִּתְעַנָּג)" (22:26); "you will pray to Him, and He will hear you" (22:27); "light will shine on your ways" (22:28); "you will be restored (תִּבָּנֶה, 'built up')" (22:23; Isa 58:12 "Those from among you will rebuild").
     - **Exclusives.** ענג ("delight", hithpael) with אָז ("then"): only Isa 58:14 and Job 22:26. Hungry + naked in one verse: only Isa 58:7 and Job 24:10, where Job moves Isaiah's victims into his catalogue (lemma).
     - **Rank.** Isa 58 ties for **first** of 887 chapters against 22:5–30 (lemmas in up to 150 verses, ten-verse windows; eight shared), with Isa 59, the chapter claim audit 3 found high against a third of all Job passages; 2 of 19 Job-passage nulls rank Isa 58 as high. It is second at the 800 cap, but ninth and twelfth at the 400 cap. The rank alone would not carry the link; the two exclusive pairs and the matching shape of charge and promise do.
     - *Moderate–high on the words (two exclusive pairs); moderate on the use.* Eliphaz's offer is Isaiah's promise to a people who feed the hungry and clothe the naked — made to a man he has just accused, falsely, of doing neither. `[I]`
   - **The offer turned back on Eliphaz.** "Then you will delight in the Almighty" (22:26) becomes Job's question about the godless: "Will he take delight in the Almighty?" (27:10). ענג occurs in Job only at these two verses, both with עַל־שַׁדַּי (lemma). The Greek keeps the link with another word: παρρησιασθήσῃ ("you will have boldness", 22:26) and παρρησίαν ("boldness", 27:10) (Swete). **New.** *High on the words.*
   - **And fulfilled in reverse.** "He will deliver one who is not innocent, and he will be delivered through the cleanness of your hands" (22:30). At the end the LORD tells Eliphaz, "My servant Job will pray for you" (42:8), and restores Job "when he prayed for his friends" (42:10) (sweep). The irony rests on the Hebrew אִי־נָקִי ("not innocent"): BHS proposes reading אֱלֹהִים ("God"), and the Greek reads "He will deliver the innocent", with its second line asterisked (Textual Variants). *Moderate–high, in the Hebrew.*
   - **Eliphaz's frame.** סכן ("be of use") opens and turns the speech: "Can a vigorous man be of use (יִסְכָּן) to God?" (22:2); "Yield now (הַסְכֶּן־נָא) and be at peace with Him" (22:21) (lemma; Elihu takes it up, 34:9; 35:3, sweep). `[T]`
   - *Surfaced by:* Move 4, Vocabulary, Tool 11, the baselines, Translations (Greek). *High on the internal links; moderate–high on Isaiah 58.*

3. **Job's reply (23–24) answers in two directions: inward, he cannot find God but God knows his way and will find him gold; outward, God does not hold the oppressors to account. "Way" is the unit's leading word, and gold its thread.** `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the reading
   - **Inward (23).**
     - "Oh that I knew where I might find Him" (23:3). ידע + מצא in Job only 23:3 and 28:13 (28 dig; *low* as a link to 28 `[S: audit 4]`, but the wish is the unit's question).
     - "I would present my case (אֶעֶרְכָה מִשְׁפָּט) before Him" (23:4). ערך ("set in order") with מִשְׁפָּט ("case") only 13:18 and 23:4 (lemma). Job resumes the lawsuit of chapter 13. **New.**
     - East, west, north, south: "He is not there … I cannot see Him" (23:8–9).
     - "But He knows the way (דֶּרֶךְ) I take; when He has tried me (בְּחָנַנִי), I shall come forth as gold (כַּזָּהָב)" (23:10). Job's "try" answers his own complaint, "that You examine (תִּבְחָנֶנּוּ) him every moment" (7:18) (sweep). ידע + דֶּרֶךְ in Job only 23:10 and 28:23 (*moderate* `[S: audit 4]`).
     - "I have kept His way (דַּרְכּוֹ) and not turned aside … I have treasured the words of His mouth" (23:11–12) — the reply to Eliphaz's "receive instruction from His mouth" (22:22), in the shared instruction idiom of Prov 2:1; 7:1 (*moderate* `[S: audit 4]`).
     - "He performs what is appointed (חֻקִּי) for me" (23:14). חֹק ("decree") is the word God sets for the rain (28:26) and the sea (38:10; with 26:10) (lemma). The decree Job dreads is the decree that orders creation. **New**, *moderate*.
   - **Outward (24).**
     - "Why are times not stored up by the Almighty, and why do those who know Him not see His days?" (24:1).
     - The catalogue of oppression (24:2–12) speaks in the words of Deuteronomy's law of the vulnerable — landmarks, the orphan's donkey, the widow's ox in pledge, the needy pushed off the road (Deut 24:10–21; 19:14; 27:17; *moderate–high* `[S: audit 4]`).
     - **The prologue's verdict turned.** "Through all this Job did not sin, nor did he ascribe wrong (תִּפְלָה) to God" (1:22, literally "give folly to God"). Now: "yet God does not lay wrong (לֹא־יָשִׂים תִּפְלָה)" to the oppressors' charge (24:12). תִּפְלָה occurs only at Job 1:22, Job 24:12 and Jer 23:13 (lemma). Job, who would not charge God with wrong, now says God charges no one with wrong. The ESV and NIV84 keep the link ("charge God with wrong" / "God charges no one with wrong"); the NASB95 loses it ("blame God" / "does not pay attention to folly"). Two manuscripts and the Syriac read תְּפִלָּה ("prayer") at 24:12, and the Greek has a different line (Textual Variants). *Moderate–high, in the Hebrew* (sweep: moderate).
     - **The rebels against the light.** "Those who rebel against the light … do not know its ways (דְרָכָיו)" (24:13). אוֹר ("light") with דֶּרֶךְ ("way") in one verse occurs in Job only at 22:28 (Eliphaz: "light will shine on your ways"), 24:13, and 38:19, 24 (the LORD: "Where is the way to the dwelling of light?") (lemma). **New.** And the night-workers of 24:13–17 are shaken out by the dawn at 38:12–15 (sweep).

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

- **The purpose the book states.** Only through the frame: the Accuser's "Does Job fear God for nothing?" (1:9) and the LORD's verdict, "you have not spoken of Me what is right as My servant Job has" (42:7). `[T]`
- **This unit's place in that purpose.**
  - The friends' last move is open accusation (22:5–9). It fails, and their side of the debate shrinks to six borrowed verses (25) and then to nothing.
  - Job keeps his integrity under oath (27:2–6), the thing the Accuser said he would not do and his wife told him to give up (2:9).
  - The poem then says what wisdom is, in the words the book used of Job (28:28). The reader learns that the man the friends accuse is what wisdom looks like in a human life. `[T]` for the content; `[I]` for the function.
- **For the reader who knows the prologue.** The reader knows that Eliphaz's charges are invented, that Job's oath is true, and that the "fear" Eliphaz questioned is the very thing the LORD praised. The unit asks the reader to watch a true man accused and to hear wisdom defined without the friends' help. `[I]`

### 2. Context

#### Canonical position (Phase 0.55)

- **Section.** Ketuvim, second book (BHS order: Psalms, Job, Proverbs). `[T]`
- **Reading implication.** The unit closes the human debate and gives the definition of wisdom (28:28) that the Psalter has taught (Ps 34:12, 15 [Eng 34:11, 14]; 111:10) and Proverbs will set out for daily life (Prov 1:7; 9:10) `[S: audit 4]`. `[I]`
- **Presupposes (checked).** Deut 24:10–21 with 19:14; 27:17 (moderate–high, `[S: audit 4]`); Isa 58:6–14 (moderate; new); Ps 8:4–5 (moderate–high on the use; new); Ps 78:64 (high on the words; new); Lam 2:15 (moderate–high; new); Ps 18:10–12 at 22:13–14 (moderate; new); Isa 27:1 (lexical high, `[S: audit 4]`); the chapter 28 list in the 28 dig.
- **Handoff.** Prov 1:7; 9:10 (the motto); Elihu's "breath of the Almighty" (32:8; 33:4); the LORD's speech (38:12–15, 19, 24–25).
- **Neighbours.** Psalms before, Proverbs after. `[T]`

#### Positional Necessity Check

- **What the preceding movement requires.**
  - Round two ended with Job's evidence that the wicked prosper and die at peace (21) and his verdict: "your answers remain full of falsehood" (21:34).
  - The friends have described the wicked man three times and Job has denied that it fits the world. They have one move left — to say it fits Job. `[T]` for the content; `[I]` for the necessity.
- **Why this unit is here.**
  - It makes the friends' doctrine say openly what it had implied: Job is wicked (22:5).
  - It lets the debate run out in the text itself (Headline 1), so that no human argument is left standing before the monologues (29–31; 32–37) and the LORD's speech (38–41).
  - It places the definition of wisdom at the point where the human search has failed, and before Job's final defence (29–31), so that the reader knows what "departing from evil" means before Job swears to it in chapter 31. `[I]`

#### Surrounding context

- **Before: round two (15:1–21:34).** The friends' three portraits of the wicked man; Job's witness, surety and Redeemer; Job's counter-evidence (21) (15–21 dig).
- **After: Job's last speech (29:1–31:40).** "Job again took up his discourse" (29:1); his past (29), his present (30), his oath (31), ending "The words of Job are ended" (31:40).

### 4. Linking Words

- **"For" and "therefore" in Eliphaz's charges.** "For (כִּי) you have taken pledges" (22:6); "Therefore (עַל־כֵּן) snares surround you" (22:10). The friends' causal logic, now applied to Job by name. `[T]`
- **"You say" (וְאָמַרְתָּ, 22:13).** Eliphaz puts words in Job's mouth that Job never said: "What does God know? Can He judge through the thick darkness?" The impious question has a canonical form in Ps 73:11 ("How does God know?"), there on the lips of the wicked (ידע + אֵל in a question; no lexical window supports a source — rank 685 of 887). `[T]`; low–moderate as a link.
- **"If you return … then" (אִם … כִּי־אָז, 22:23, 26).** Eliphaz's offer is conditional, like Isaiah 58's (Headline 2). `[T]`
- **"But He" (וְהוּא, 23:13).** Job's turn from his confidence ("I shall come forth as gold") to his fear ("But He is unique, and who can turn Him?"). `[T]`
- **"Why?" (מַדּוּעַ, 24:1).** The unit's one direct question about God's justice, put by Job to no one. `[T]`
- **"If it is not so" (וְאִם־לֹא אֵפוֹ, 24:25).** Job claims chapter 24 as his own, including 24:18–24 `[S: audit 4]`. `[T]`
- **"How much less" (אַף כִּי, 25:6).** Bildad's argument from the greater to the less, borrowed from 15:16 and 4:19 `[S: audit 4]`. `[T]`
- **"As God lives … far be it from me" (27:2, 5).** The oath formula: Job binds himself. `[T]`

### 5. Parallels

- **Eliphaz's offer.** "Place your gold in the dust // and the gold of Ophir among the stones of the brooks" (22:24); "the Almighty will be your gold // and choice silver to you" (22:25). The parallels make God Job's treasure. `[T]`
- **Job's search.** "I go forward but He is not there // and backward, but I cannot perceive Him" (23:8); "When He acts on the left, I cannot behold Him // He turns on the right, I cannot see Him" (23:9). Four directions, four failures. `[T]`
- **Bildad's comparison.** Moon // stars (25:5); man // son of man; maggot // worm (25:6). `[T]`
- **Job's hymn.** Sheol // Abaddon (26:6); north // earth (26:7); sea // Rahab (26:12); heavens // fleeing serpent (26:13). `[T]`
- **The oath.** "My lips // my tongue" (27:4); "my integrity // my righteousness" (27:5–6). `[T]`

### 6. Narrator's Comment

- **None in the unit.** In the dialogue the narrator speaks only through the speech formulas, until "The words of Job are ended" (31:40) and 32:1–5. `[T]`
- **But the formulas speak.** The change from "answered" to "took up his discourse" after 26:1 (Headline 1), and the absence of any formula for Zophar, are the narrator's only comments; 32:1–5 then says outright that the friends "ceased answering Job" `[S: audit 4]`. `[T]` / `[I]`

### 7. Vocabulary

- **סכן ("be of use; yield").** 22:2 and 22:21 (with 15:3; 34:9; 35:3 in Job). Eliphaz's two halves turn on it: man cannot profit God; so "yield" to him. `[T]`
- **חבל I ("take in pledge").** In Job only 22:6; 24:3, 9 (lemma 2254 a; the homograph חבל II, "act corruptly / be broken", is at 17:1; 34:31). The law: "you shall not take a widow's garment in pledge" (Deut 24:17) `[S: audit 4]`. `[T]`
- **עָרוֹם ("naked").** 1:21; 22:6; 24:7, 10; 26:6. From Job's own birth to Sheol's exposure before God (sweep; verified). `[T]`
- **תִּפְלָה ("wrong, folly").** Job 1:22; 24:12; Jer 23:13 only (Headline 3). `[T]`
- **ענג ("delight", hithpael).** In Job only 22:26 and 27:10, both with עַל־שַׁדַּי (Headline 2). `[T]`
- **דֶּרֶךְ ("way").** 11 of Job's 32 verses with the word fall in this unit (Headline 3). `[T]`
- **שַׁדַּי ("the Almighty").** 11 of Job's 31 verses fall in this unit: Eliphaz five times (22:3, 17, 23, 25, 26), Job six (23:16; 24:1; 27:2, 10, 11, 13); none in 25, 26 or 28 (lemma). Its densest stretch in the book is 21:15–27:13 (13 of 31). The unit is about whether the Almighty can be found, bought, delighted in or accused. `[T]`; `[I]` for the theme.
- **תֻּמָּה ("integrity").** Job 2:3, 9; 27:5; 31:6; Prov 11:3 only (Headline 5). With תָּם ("blameless", 1:1, 8; 2:3; 8:20; 9:20–22) and the verb תמם (22:3; 31:40). `[T]`
- **רְמִיָּה ("deceit").** In Job only 13:7 and 27:4, both with עַוְלָה ("injustice") (Headline 5). `[T]`
- **רִמָּה ("maggot") and תּוֹלֵעָה ("worm").** רִמָּה: 7:5; 17:14; 21:26; 24:20; 25:6. תּוֹלֵעָה in Job only 25:6; with רִמָּה, only Isa 14:11 and Job 25:6 (Headline 4). `[T]`
- **נְשָׁמָה ("breath").** 4:9; 26:4; 27:3; 32:8; 33:4; 34:14; 37:10 (Headline 4). `[T]`
- **חֹק ("decree, portion").** 14:5, 13; 23:12 (MT מֵחֻקִּי; see Textual Variants), 14; 26:10; 28:26; 38:10. God's decree for Job (14:5; 23:14), for the boundary of light and darkness (26:10), for the rain (28:26) and for the sea (38:10). `[T]`
- **אוֹפִיר / בֶּצֶר ("Ophir" / "gold ore").** אוֹפִיר in Job only 22:24 and 28:16; בֶּצֶר only 22:24–25 in the Hebrew Bible (lemma 1220). The Old Greek lacked 22:24b, the Ophir line (asterisked in Rahlfs) (sweep; 28 dig; verified). `[T]`
- **חוּג ("circle").** The noun at 22:14 (Eliphaz's imputed speech: God "walks on the vault of heaven"), Isa 40:22 and Prov 8:27; the verb only at 26:10 (Job's hymn) `[S: audit 4]`. Eliphaz says Job pictures a God too high to see; Job's own hymn pictures a God who draws the boundary of light and darkness. `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the contrast.
- **בְּלִימָה ("nothing", 26:7).** Only here in the Hebrew Bible (lemma): God "hangs the earth on nothing". `[T]`
- **Proper-noun inventory.** Ophir (22:24); Abaddon (26:6, with 28:22); Rahab (26:12; filed 7293 in Job, 7294 in Isaiah — the homograph split noted in the overview). `[T]`

### 8. Translations

- **24:18 — the ESV's "You say,".** The ESV opens 24:18 with "You say," and so presents 24:18–24 as Job quoting the friends. There is no word for it in the Hebrew; the NASB95 and NIV84 print the verses as Job's own. Claim audit 4 found that the text attributes them to Job (high) and that "Job quoting the friends" is weak for 24:18–25, whose vocabulary is not the friends' and which Job claims as his own at 24:25 `[S: audit 4]`. *Category 3* (interpretive supply). **Pulpit note:** a congregation hearing the ESV hears the question already decided. If the preacher reads 24:18–24 at all, he should say that the words "You say" are the translators', not the text's.
- **24:12 — "wrong" (תִּפְלָה).** ESV "yet God charges no one with wrong" and 1:22 "Job did not sin or charge God with wrong"; NIV84 "God charges no-one with wrongdoing" and 1:22 "by charging God with wrongdoing". The NASB95 has "does not pay attention to folly" and 1:22 "nor did he blame God". **The pulpit text keeps the link the Hebrew makes; the study text does not.** *Category 2.*
- **22:6; 24:3, 9 — "pledge".** ESV "exacted pledges" (22:6), "take … for a pledge" (24:3), "take a pledge" (24:9) — one word throughout, so the congregation can hear Eliphaz's charge become Job's description of the oppressors. NASB95 also keeps "pledge" throughout. NIV84 breaks it: "demanded security" (22:6), "in pledge" (24:3), "seized for a debt" (24:9). *Category 2.*
- **22:26; 27:10 — "delight in the Almighty".** ESV "delight yourself in the Almighty" / "take delight in the Almighty"; NASB95 "delight in the Almighty" / "take delight in the Almighty"; NIV84 "find delight" in both. All three keep the link. *Category 1.*
- **23:2.** NASB95 "Even today my complaint is rebellion (מְרִי); His hand is heavy despite my groaning"; ESV "Today also my complaint is bitter; my hand is heavy on account of my groaning"; NIV84 "his hand", with the margin "Septuagint and Syriac; Hebrew *the hand on me*". The Hebrew has מְרִי ("rebellion" or, read as מַר, "bitter") and יָדִי ("my hand"). *Category 3.*
- **23:12.** NASB95 "more than my necessary food (מֵחֻקִּי)"; ESV "more than my portion of food"; NIV84 "more than my daily bread". BHS suggests בְּחֵקִי ("in my bosom") with the Greek and Vulgate. *Category 3.*
- **22:29.** The verse is obscure. NASB95 "When you are cast down, you will speak with confidence"; ESV "For when they are humbled you say, 'It is because of pride'"; NIV84 "When men are brought low and you say, 'Lift them up!'". BHS marks the first half-line as corrupt. *Category 3.*
- **22:30.** All three keep the Hebrew's "not innocent" (NASB95 "one who is not innocent"; ESV and NIV84 "even the one / one who is not innocent"). The Greek reads "the innocent", and BHS proposes אֱלֹהִים (Textual Variants). The irony of Headline 2 is in the Hebrew and in all three English versions. *Category 1.*
- **27:13.** NASB95 "from God"; ESV "with God" (Hebrew עִם־אֵל; BHS proposes מֵאֵל, "from God", as at 20:29). *Category 2.*
- **26:4; 27:3 — "breath" (נְשָׁמָה).** ESV "whose breath has come out from you?" (26:4) and "as long as my breath is in me" (27:3) keeps the chain that Elihu takes up ("the breath of the Almighty", 32:8; 33:4). NASB95 has "whose spirit was expressed through you?" and "as long as life is in me"; NIV84 "whose spirit spoke from your mouth?". *Category 2* — the pulpit text serves the chain better.
- **25:6; 27:2–6.** All three agree in substance ("maggot … worm"; "As God lives … my integrity"). *Category 1.*
- **Pulpit divergence note.** Two points bear on the findings. (1) At 24:18 the ESV supplies "You say," — the preacher should not build on it. (2) At 24:12 the ESV carries the 1:22 link better than the NASB95, and at 26:4 and 27:3 it keeps "breath". Elsewhere the ESV serves the unit well.

### 9. Tone and Feel

- **Eliphaz: from counsel to prosecution.** The courtesy of 4:2 ("If one ventures a word with you, will you become impatient?") is gone. "Is not your wickedness great, and your iniquities without end?" (22:5). Yet his last eight verses are the warmest promise any friend makes (22:23–30). Accusation and tenderness side by side. `[T]` / `[I]`
- **Job: longing, then dread, then protest.** "Oh that I knew where I might find Him" (23:3); "Therefore, I would be dismayed at His presence … It is God who has made my heart faint" (23:15–16); "Why are times not stored up by the Almighty?" (24:1). `[T]`
- **Bildad: worn out.** Six verses, half borrowed (Headline 1). `[I]`
- **Job: scorn, then awe.** "What a help you are to the weak!" (26:2), then the hymn, ending in a whisper (26:14). `[T]`
- **Job: oath.** Solemn and fierce: "Till I die I will not put away my integrity" (27:5). `[T]`
- **The poem: calm** (28 dig). After the oath, the register drops. `[I]`

**Isa 58:6–14 → 22:5–30** *(moderate; new)*

- *Move 1, source context.* Isaiah 58 answers Israel's complaint that God ignores their fasting (58:3). The fast God chooses is justice: loose the bonds, "divide your bread with the hungry … when you see the naked, cover him" (58:6–7). "Then your light will break out like the dawn … Then you will call, and the LORD will answer; you will cry, and He will say, 'Here I am'" (58:8–9). "Then you will take delight in the LORD" (58:14). `[T]`
- *The words.* Headline 2: ענג + אָז only Isa 58:14 / Job 22:26; hungry + naked in one verse only Isa 58:7 / Job 24:10; with bread, light, darkness, call-and-answer, "build" (Isa 58:12 / Job 22:23). `[T]`
- *Move 2, book usage.* Isaiah is live in Job across the book (overview; claim audits 2–4): 40:12–13 at 28:25; 44:6 at 19:25; 27:1 at 26:13; 30:8 at 19:23. `[S: audit]` / `[S: audit 4]`
- *Move 3, OT-to-OT.* The promise of answered prayer to those who care for the poor runs from Deut 24:13 ("it will be righteousness for you before the LORD") through Isa 58 to Ps 41:2 [Eng 41:1]. `[I]`
- *What it adds.* Eliphaz gives Job Isaiah's promise. But Isaiah's promise is to people who feed the hungry and clothe the naked, and Eliphaz has just accused Job of doing neither. Job later swears he did both (31:16–20). Eliphaz's theology is right about God and wrong about Job. `[I]`
- *Rival.* Isa 59 ties with Isa 58 in rank; but Isa 59 tops a third of all Job passages (claim audit 3), and Isa 58 alone has the two exclusives. `[T]`

**Ps 18:10–12 [Eng 18:9–11; 2 Sam 22:10–12] → 22:13–14** *(moderate; new)*

- *Move 1.* David's theophany: "He bowed the heavens also, and came down with thick darkness (עֲרָפֶל) under His feet … He made darkness His hiding place (סִתְרוֹ) … thick clouds (עָבֵי) of the sky" (Ps 18:10–12). God comes down through the darkness to rescue.
- *The words.* Eliphaz's imputed speech: "Can He judge through the thick darkness (עֲרָפֶל)? Clouds (עָבִים) are a hiding place (סֵתֶר) for Him, so that He cannot see" (22:13–14). עָב + סֵתֶר in one verse only Ps 18:12 and Job 22:14; the parallel 2 Sam 22:12 has סֻכּוֹת ("booths") for סִתְרוֹ, so the contact is with the Psalm's form. `[T]`
- *What it adds.* Eliphaz says Job pictures the theophany's darkness as a screen that blinds God. In the Psalm the same darkness is the cloud in which God comes to save. `[I]`

**Ps 8:4–5 → 25:5–6** *(moderate–high on the use; new)*

- *Move 1.* Psalm 8 sets human smallness under the heavens, moon and stars against God's care: "what is man that You take thought of him … And You crown him with glory and majesty!" (Ps 8:5–6 [Eng 8:4–5]). `[T]`
- *The words.* Headline 4: moon and stars within one verse of אֱנוֹשׁ / אָדָם only Ps 8:4–5 and Job 25:5–6. `[T]`
- *Move 2.* Psalm 8 is contested across the dialogue: Job turns it into a complaint ("What is man that You magnify him, that You are concerned about him?", 7:17); Eliphaz into an indictment ("What is man, that he should be pure?", 15:14); now Bildad into contempt. Queue #48 ("the friends and Job contest the same texts") gains a third instance. `[T]` / `[I]`
- *What it adds.* Psalm 8 ends in praise of God's name and man's crown; Bildad ends with man a maggot. The friends' last word on man is the opposite of the Psalm's. The New Testament reads Psalm 8 of Christ, crowned after suffering (Heb 2:6–9). `[I]`

**Isa 14:11 → 25:6** *(moderate; new)* — רִמָּה + תּוֹלֵעָה only Isa 14:11 and Job 25:6; Isa 14:11 is the king of Babylon's bed of maggots in Sheol. Claim audit 3 rated Isa 14:11 at Job 21:26 moderate (`[S: audit]`). Bildad's "maggot" and "worm" belong to the vocabulary of the grave. `[T]` / `[I]`

**Ps 78:64 → 27:15** *(high on the words; new)*

- *Move 1.* Psalm 78 recounts Israel's history of forgetting; at Shiloh God "also delivered His people to the sword … His priests fell by the sword, and His widows could not weep" (Ps 78:62, 64). `[T]`
- *The words.* 27:15b = Ps 78:64b exactly (וְאַלְמְנֹתָיו לֹא תִבְכֶּינָה); the sword precedes in both (27:14; Ps 78:64a). `[T]`
- *Move 2, recurrence.* Psalm 78 stands behind Job at 20:23 (78:49, "He sent upon them His burning anger"; `[S: audit]`), here at 27:15, and at 28:10 (78:15, "He split the rocks in the wilderness"; 28 dig). Three contacts, three different parts of the psalm. **Ps 78 is a live source in Job.** *Moderate–high* as a pattern.
- *What it adds.* The wicked man's end in 27:13–23 is told in the words of God's judgement on his own people at Shiloh. `[I]`
- *The Greek.* Job's Greek ("no one will pity their widows") does not keep the wording of the Greek Psalm ("their widows will not be wept for"). The link is Hebrew. `[T]`

### 12. Genre

- **Disputation that stops.** The forms of the dialogue — accusation (22:5–11), exhortation with promise (22:21–30), complaint (23), lament over injustice (24), hymn (25:2–3; 26:5–14), oath (27:2–6), "portion of the wicked" poem (27:13–23) — and then a wisdom poem (28). `[T]`
- **The oath** is a legal form: Job binds himself by God's life (27:2) before the final oath of clearance (31). `[T]`
- **Genre implication.** The unit should be read as the end of the argument, not as one more round: its forms shrink, borrow and break off. `[I]`

### 13. Copycat

- **Not to copy:** Eliphaz's move from doctrine to accusation — inventing sins to fit a theory of suffering (22:5–9) — and Bildad's recycling of other men's lines into contempt (25). `[I]`
- **To learn from:** Job's refusal to say what he believes is false, even to end the argument (27:4–5); his care for the oppressed, which he will swear to (29; 31). Not "be like Job" as a model of patience, but "hold to God and to the truth when you cannot find Him" (23:8–12). `[I]`

### 14. Bible Timeline

- **Setting.** Patriarchal in colour (no temple, law or king named) `[T]`; date of composition not stated `[S]`.
- **Reading order.** In the Ketuvim, after the Psalms (Ps 8; 18; 73; 78 behind this unit) and before Proverbs. `[T]`

### 15. Who Am I?

- **Eliphaz** — the believer who is right about God's promises and wrong about the person in front of him. `[I]`
- **Bildad** — the believer who has run out of things to say and repeats other people's lines. `[I]`
- **The oppressed of chapter 24** — the widow, the orphan, the naked, the hungry, crying out in the city while "God does not lay wrong to the charge" of their oppressors. `[T]` / `[I]`
- **Job** — the man who cannot find God, holds to his way anyway, and will not lie to win. `[I]`

### 16. So What?

- **Intended response.** To see where the friends' theology ends — in false accusation, borrowed words and silence — and to hold, with Job, to the God who knows the way we take, even when we cannot find Him. `[I]`
- **Worldview.** God's promises are true (Isa 58; Eliphaz's 22:21–30), but they are not a diagnosis of the sufferer. The world is full of unpunished wrong (24). Integrity is not surrendered to win an argument (27). Wisdom is the fear of the Lord, not the right explanation (28). `[I]`
- **Candidate FCF (for point-purpose).**
  - "When suffering goes on and explanations fail, we either accuse the sufferer to keep our theology, or give up on God to keep our honesty."
  - Alternative: "We want to find God and have Him explain himself; when He is hidden, we are tempted to let go of our integrity or of Him."
- **Prayer.** For the fear of the Lord when He cannot be found; for mercy towards sufferers; for truthfulness.

### Historical and Cultural Background

- **Pledges.** The law protected the poor man's pledge: the cloak returned by sunset (Exod 22:25–26 [Eng 22:26–27]; Deut 24:12–13), no millstone taken (Deut 24:6), no widow's garment (Deut 24:17) `[T]`. Eliphaz's charge (22:6) and Job's catalogue (24:3, 9) assume that law.
- **Landmarks.** Moving a boundary stone was cursed (Deut 19:14; 27:17; Prov 22:28; 23:10) `[T]`; 24:2 opens the catalogue with it `[S: audit 4]`.
- **Rahab and the fleeing serpent.** The sea monster of Israel's hymns has parallels in Ugaritic texts (Lotan, "the fleeing serpent") `[S: recalled]`. Job and Isaiah share the epithet (Isa 27:1) `[S: audit 4]`.
- **Ophir.** Solomon's source of gold (1 Kgs 9:28; 10:11) `[T]`; location unknown `[S]`.
- **Oaths.** "As God lives" binds the speaker under God's judgement (e.g. 1 Sam 14:39; 2 Sam 2:27) `[T]`.

### Christological Reading

- **Category: trajectory and contrast, not prophecy.** `[I]`
- **The accused innocent who prays for his accusers.** Eliphaz accuses Job of sins he did not commit (22:5–9) and promises that "the one who is not innocent" will be delivered through the cleanness of Job's hands (22:30). At the end Eliphaz is delivered through Job's prayer (42:8–10). The canon's fullest instance is Christ, falsely accused, who prayed for those who wronged him (Luke 23:34) and "always lives to make intercession" (Heb 7:25). *Moderate* — canonical reflection on a Hebrew irony. `[I]`
- **Gold tried in the fire.** "When He has tried me, I shall come forth as gold" (23:10): the image is taken up for the faith of believers in 1 Pet 1:6–7 ("the proof of your faith, being more precious than gold which is perishable, even though tested by fire") (sweep; conceptual, not a citation). *Moderate.* `[I]`
- **The Son of Man.** Bildad calls "the son of man" a worm (25:6). Psalm 8's "son of man" is crowned (Ps 8:6); the New Testament reads the psalm of Jesus, crowned through suffering (Heb 2:6–9). *Low–moderate* — contrast. `[I]`
- **The way.** "He knows the way I take" (23:10); "God understands its way" (28:23). Christ calls himself "the way" (John 14:6). *Low* — a word-picture, not a link. `[I]`

Job keeps his integrity while unable to find God and with no promise of restoration; the friends' promise of restoration (22:21–30) is precisely the theology the book corrects. The sermon's subject is the God who knows the way Job takes when Job cannot find him. `[I]`

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: Preaching 22–27 as one more round

- **What it looks like:** a sermon that treats the third round like the first two — three speeches, three replies.
- **Why it's wrong:** the round breaks down in the text itself: Eliphaz accuses, Job addresses no one, Bildad borrows, Zophar is silent (Headline 1).
- **Corrective:** preach the unit as the end of the argument, and chapter 28 as what remains when argument has failed.

### Pitfall: Quoting Eliphaz's promise as God's word to the sufferer

- **What it looks like:** "Return to the Almighty and you will be restored … then you will delight in the Almighty" (22:23–26) offered as comfort.
- **Why it's wrong:** the promise is Isaiah's and true, but Eliphaz attaches it to false charges, and God says the friends "have not spoken of Me what is right" (42:7). Job's restoration comes when he prays for Eliphaz (42:10), not when he confesses Eliphaz's charges.
- **Corrective:** give the promise its own context (Isa 58), and show what Eliphaz does with it.

### Pitfall: Building on the ESV's "You say," at 24:18

- **What it looks like:** "Here Job quotes the friends' view…"
- **Why it's wrong:** the words are not in the Hebrew; the text gives 24:18–24 to Job, and Job claims it at 24:25 `[S: audit 4]`.
- **Corrective:** if the verses are read, say that the attribution is debated and that the Hebrew gives them to Job.

### Pitfall: "Man is a worm" as the Bible's anthropology

- **What it looks like:** using 25:6 as a proof-text for human worthlessness.
- **Why it's wrong:** it is Bildad's last, borrowed word, turning Psalm 8 upside down; the book's verdict goes against the friends (42:7).
- **Corrective:** set 25:6 beside Ps 8:4–5 and Heb 2:6–9.

### Pitfall: Using 24:2–12 near the poor, or 22:5–9 near the suffering, without care

- **What it looks like:** passing over the cry of the oppressed (24:12) quickly, or reading Eliphaz's charges without saying they are false.
- **Why it's wrong:** chapter 24 is the Bible giving voice to the poor's complaint against God's apparent inaction; 22:5–9 is a false accusation of a sufferer.
- **Corrective:** let 24:12 be heard as lament; name 22:5–9 as slander.

- **Apparatus features** (the paragraphing; 25:1; 22:30; 24:12) name BHS as their witness. Spread across other manuscripts remains `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`.
- **Window ranks.** Every rank cited was tested against a Job-passage null, and the new canonical candidates were run under two to four settings (claim audit 4's rule); where a rank failed the stability check (Ps 8 at two settings; Isa 58 at the 400 cap), the report says so, and the rating rests on the exclusive words.
- **Address test.** Second-person forms read from the WLC morphology (verb person, pronoun and suffix codes), counted by verse; quoted speech was not separated mechanically, so the friends' counts include a few quoted second persons (e.g. 22:13 "You say" counts as address to Job, which it is).
- **Counts name their edition:** WLC for every lemma and morphology count; BHS for the text, markers and apparatus; Swete and Rahlfs for the Greek.
