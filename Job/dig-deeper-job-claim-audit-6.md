## Method

The method is the one the standing rule prescribes, as at audit 5:

- **two blind auditors**, not three;
- **run one after the other**, not in parallel;
- **auditor A takes the single contacts** — wording, rarity, rivals, Greek;
- **auditor B takes the fields, designs and measured claims** — block tests, chance baselines, rank stability.

**Blindness.** Each auditor was a fresh subagent. Each was given only the corpus, a written brief, and its claims **stated as proposals to break, with the proposed rating attached**. Neither saw the book overview, the sweep, any Job dig, the queue's reasoning, earlier audits, project documents or memory. Auditor B was barred from auditor A's work. Both were told not to search the web and to rate History of Interpretation "not assessable from the corpus" rather than guess it — **which is why seven claims are routed to Logos rather than ruled on.**

**The corpus in the cloud.** The `_texts` corpus was copied into the session workspace for the run: the WLC text and lemma index, Swete, the SBLGNT, the Job Logos exports (the BHS apparatus, NASB95, ESV) and the folder's search tools. **This is a change of logistics from audit 5, not of method**: the archive used for the transfer was written into a disposable folder inside the prep directory, and the auditors read the same files the digs read.

**Positive control.** Every auditor, and the main session, began with the same check: **חֶסֶד ("covenant-kindness") in Job returns 6:14; 10:12; 37:13** (WLC). Every reported absence or "only" was preceded by a search that returned its own source verse.

**Shape words.** Every *only*, *identical*, *verbatim*, *exactly* or *the same phrase* was tested by an ordered phrase search on the surface text, **plain and skeletal**, and each occurrence is quoted as the text prints it. The briefs carried the standing line: *where you report that a phrase is identical, verbatim, exact or found only in certain places, quote each occurrence in full as the text prints it.*

**Four rules in the briefs**, each of which has caught an error in this project before: the positive control; shape words by ordered phrase, not by lemma; **a nil from a phrase search is not an absence** (prefixes, an intervening כָּל, maqqef and the ketiv all defeat a word-initial pattern — this has produced a false nil in each of the last three digs); and **lemma fields split on part of speech and on homographs**, so a zero from a co-occurrence should prompt suspicion of the part of speech before any conclusion.

**Baselines (Part B).** Auditor B had not seen the earlier runs' scan settings and built its own, stating them at the head of its part. **The four design claims under test are not four independent claims but one methodological claim tested four times** — *that a late block of Job is deliberately shaped as a reply to an early block* — so a baseline that defeats one defeats all four, and the Headlines of all three digs with it. Every rank is reported with **a Job null**: how many other Job blocks of the same length rank some other chapter as high.

**Spot checks.** The main session re-ran the results the verdicts turn on, independently and from the same corpus, before and after the auditors reported. They are listed in their own section below. **One of them changed a verdict.**

## Positional Frame (Phase 0.6)

Run before the claims were scoped, as the mode requires, because scoping first would answer the positional question inside a frame the claim-level scope had already fixed.

**What has the book set up that these claims serve?**

- **The dialogue ends with its own question unanswered.** Job demanded an answer (13:22; 31:35) and the friends said none would come. Elihu fills the gap with a promise of new words (32:14) and runs into the storm at 38:1.
- **Sermon 8's two speeches divide on a vocabulary.** The first (38:1–40:5) is a creation tour; the second (40:6–42:6) opens with a juridical charge at 40:8 and then spends thirty-four verses on one creature.
- **Sermon 9 (42:7–17)** delivers the verdict the dialogue never reached, and does it in prose, in the prologue's vocabulary, with no word addressed to Job.

**What the claims under test say.** Taken together they make one large claim and several small ones. The large one: **that the end of Job is built as a reply to its beginning — speech 1 to chapter 3, speech 2 to chapter 29, the epilogue to chapters 8 and 13 — and that the replies are detectable as rare shared wording.** The small ones: that Psalm 104 and Proverbs 8 are quarried for the monsters' titles; that the justice-vocabulary is withheld and then released; that an exile formula is applied uniquely to one man; that four New Testament texts receive this material; and that a crux is decided by a construction.

**Why this matters here.** **Ten of these claims stand in the Headline Findings of the three digs, and the backbones for Sermons 8 and 9 will lift those headlines.** More sharply: the four design claims share a method, so they share a fate. If Job blocks routinely share six near-exclusive contacts with some other chapter, every one of the four falls, and the series' two closing sermons lose their spine. That is why the design baselines are the real work of this audit, and why no design claim in this batch was rated above *moderate* when it was queued.

## Part A: Single Contacts (auditor A)

Fourteen claims, seventeen rulings — A11 carries four data and each is ruled on separately. Auditor A's own summary of its work is worth recording before the rulings: **of the seventeen items, ten distributions were exactly right as proposed. Everything it broke, it broke on grammar rather than arithmetic** — a stem, a preposition, a syntactic unit, a voice, a mood. *The counting in these claims is good; the grammatical descriptions attached to the counts are where they fail.* That is the most useful sentence in this audit and it should govern how the next dig writes up a distribution.

Two versification facts the auditor settled before anything else, and which anyone re-running this work needs: **Psalms with superscriptions are offset by one** (Hebrew Ps 21:6 = English 21:5; Hebrew 45:4 = English 45:3; Hebrew 5:10 = English 5:9), and **Swete's Job runs on its own numbering** — Hebrew Job 40:10 is Swete 40:5, Hebrew 40:19 is Swete 40:14, and Hebrew 41:3 falls in Swete 41:2. **Three traditions, three numberings, in one claim-set.**

### A-full tier

#### A1 — הוֹד + הָדָר + לָבַשׁ at Ps 104:1 and Job 40:10 (#172)

**Source:** the 40:6–42:6 dig, Headline 1. Proposed: words *high*, use *moderate–high*.
**Claim:** that the three words co-occur in exactly two verses of the Hebrew Bible, that the pair הוֹד + הָדָר alone stands in eight, and that **Job 40:10 is the only verse where a human is told to put them on.**

**Counts confirmed exactly, three times over.** The three-lemma co-occurrence returns **Ps 104:1 and Job 40:10**; the pair returns **eight verses — Ps 21:6; 45:4; 96:6; 104:1; 111:3; 145:5; Job 40:10; 1 Chr 16:27**. The main session reproduced both independently.

**A shape-test result worth keeping.** The ordered surface phrase הוד והדר returns, on the plain pass, Ps 21:6; 96:6; 104:1; 111:3; 1 Chr 16:27 — **and not Job 40:10**, because Job prefixes a conjunctive waw (וְהוֹד וְהָדָר). The skeletal pass recovers it. *A fourth consecutive instance of the false nil.*

**Two rivals, and the second is substantial.**

- **Ps 45:4** [English 45:3] — חֲגוֹר־חַרְבְּךָ עַל־יָרֵךְ גִּבּוֹר **הוֹדְךָ וַהֲדָרֶךָ** — **a human addressed by an imperative, with the same noun pair.** The clothing verb differs (חָגַר, "gird", not לָבַשׁ) and the nouns are suffixed, so the three-lemma count is untouched; but "the only verse where a human is told to put it on" needs the qualifier **"with the verb לָבַשׁ"**.
- **Ps 93:1** — יְהוָה מָלָךְ **גֵּאוּת לָבֵשׁ** לָבֵשׁ יְהוָה עֹז הִתְאַזָּר. **לָבַשׁ with גֵּאוּת stands in exactly one verse of the Hebrew Bible, and this is it** — and **גֵּאוּת is from the same root as Job 40:10's own גָּאוֹן.** With לָבַשׁ + עֹז in four verses (Isa 51:9; 52:1; Ps 93:1; 2 Chr 6:41), the divine-robing idiom is plainly wider than one psalm. Main session reproduced both.

**So Job 40:10 carries two word-fields at once** — גָּאוֹן וָגֹבַהּ and הוֹד וְהָדָר — and **Ps 93:1 shares one of them with it, and the verb.** The verse is an invitation into the royal-divine robing idiom, of which Ps 104:1 and Ps 93:1 are two instances, rather than a citation of Ps 104:1 in particular.

**Greek.** Swete at Job 40:10 (its 40:5) has ἀνάλαβε δὴ ὕψος καὶ δύναμιν, **δόξαν δὲ καὶ τιμὴν ἀμφίεσαι**; at Ps 104:1 (its 103:1), ἐξομολόγησιν καὶ εὐπρέπειαν **ἐνεδύσω**. **Different clothing verbs and completely different noun pairs: the contact does not survive into Greek.** The taunt does — Swete keeps the middle imperative.

|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Psalter vocabulary on any dating |
| Volume | **Possible** | three lemmas, two verses — but a short idiom, and the surface forms differ (prefixed waw; jussive against perfect) |
| Recurrence | Weak | neither הוֹד nor הָדָר occurs elsewhere in Job |
| Thematic coherence | **Strong** | the robe-reversal is exactly what 40:8–14 is doing |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | |
| History of interpretation | **Not assessable from the corpus** | routed to Logos |
| Satisfaction | Possible | illuminating, but Ps 93:1 shares the honour |

**Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.**
**Reasoning.** Every count is exactly right and the robe-reversal reading holds. But the uniqueness claim needs "with לָבַשׁ" because Ps 45:4 commands a human in the same noun pair, and Ps 93:1 — one verse in the canon, sharing both לָבַשׁ and the גאה root with Job 40:10 — shows the dig reaching for a **motif** rather than a psalm.
**Dig action required:** patch Headline 1 of the 40:6–42:6 dig. **Headline touched: yes.**

#### A2 — לִוְיָתָן + שׂחק at Ps 104:26 and Job 40:29 [English 41:5] (#173)

**Source:** the 40:6–42:6 dig, Headline 4. Proposed: words *high*, use *moderate–high*.

**Confirmed, and tighter than claimed.** לִוְיָתָן stands in **five verses, six occurrences — Isa 27:1 (twice); Ps 74:14; Ps 104:26; Job 3:8; Job 40:25**. With שָׂחַק: **exactly one verse, Ps 104:26.** Main session reproduced.

> Ps 104:26 שָׁם אֳנִיּוֹת יְהַלֵּכוּן לִוְיָתָן זֶה־יָצַרְתָּ **לְשַׂחֶק־בּוֹ**׃
> Job 40:29 **הַֽתְשַׂחֶק־בּוֹ** כַּצִּפּוֹר וְתִקְשְׁרֶנּוּ לְנַעֲרוֹתֶיךָ׃

**Both piel, both bound by maqqef to בּוֹ.** The claim said Job reproduces the verb and the preposition-with-suffix; **the maqqef binding is shared too**, which the dig did not notice.

**The rival hunt is what makes this the strongest claim in the set.** The auditor read all **36** verses of שׂחק. The verb normally takes לְ ("laugh at": Judg 16:25; Ps 2:4; 37:13; 59:9; Job 5:22; 39:22; 41:21), or עַל (Ps 52:8; Job 30:1), or אֶל (Job 29:24), or stands absolute (Jer 30:19; 31:4; Prov 26:19; Eccl 3:4), or takes בְּ **of place** (Zech 8:5; Prov 8:31). **בְּ with a personal suffix, meaning "sport with a creature", occurs nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible.**

**Greek — and this is the one claim Swete strengthens.** Swete Ps 104:26 (its 103:26): δράκων οὗτος ὃν ἔπλασας **ἐμπαίζειν αὐτῷ**. Swete Job 40:29 (its 40:24): **παίξῃ δὲ ἐν αὐτῷ** ὥσπερ ὀρνέῳ. **Both a παίζω-family verb with a pronoun, both keeping the "formed as a plaything" sense. The contact survives translation.**

| Thematic coherence | **Strong** | Ps 104 says God sports with Leviathan; Job asks whether *Job* can |
| Historical plausibility | Strong | |

**Verdict: Confirmed.**
**Reasoning.** The distribution is exact, the construction canon-unique to the two verses, the maqqef binding shared, and Swete independently preserves the link. **The strongest claim in the batch, and the only one where the Greek corroborates.**
**Dig action:** none, except to add the maqqef observation. **Headline touched: no** (strengthened, not changed).

#### A3 — רֵאשִׁית + דֶּרֶךְ at Prov 8:22 and Job 40:19 (#174)

**Source:** the 40:6–42:6 dig, Headline 4 — **and the book overview's Intertextual Map, where it already stands rated *high*.** Proposed: words *high*, use *moderate*.

**Confirmed exactly.** The co-occurrence returns **Job 40:19 and Prov 8:22**. רֵאשִׁית stands in 49 verses canon-wide; **in Job in exactly three — 8:7, 40:19, 42:12.** Main session reproduced.

> Prov 8:22 יְהוָה קָנָנִי **רֵאשִׁית דַּרְכּוֹ** קֶדֶם מִפְעָלָיו מֵאָז׃
> Job 40:19 הוּא **רֵאשִׁית דַּרְכֵי־אֵל** הָעֹשׂוֹ יַגֵּשׁ חַרְבּוֹ׃

Adjacent and in order in both; Job has the plural construct with the divine name explicit, Proverbs the singular with a suffix.

**Rival named and rejected:** רֹאשׁ + דֶּרֶךְ, "head of the way", stands in **22 verses** — including Prov 8:2, in the same chapter — but it is a road junction, not a temporal priority. Worth naming because a looser search sweeps it in.

**A distribution worth the look.** רֵאשִׁית in Job is **biographical at 8:7 and 42:12** ("your beginning … your latter end") and **cosmological only at 40:19.** The word is not a Joban favourite reused casually; it means something different the third time.

**Greek — half the force lost.** Swete Prov 8:22 has **ἀρχὴν ὁδῶν αὐτοῦ**, faithful. Swete Job 40:19 (its 40:14) has **ἀρχὴ πλάσματος Κυρίου** — "the beginning of the Lord's **moulding**", replacing "ways" with "thing moulded". **A Greek reader would not see Behemoth given Wisdom's title.**

#### A4 — Rom 11:35 and Job 41:3 [English 41:11] (#175)

**Source:** the 40:6–42:6 dig, Christological Reading and its triage; **and the book overview in two places** — Table C (rated *high*) and the "five features of the Greek" list. Proposed: verbal correspondence *high*, Job as source *moderate–high*, **with the load-bearing claim that the New Testament here follows the Masoretic Hebrew against the Old Greek.**

**The verbal correspondence to the Hebrew is confirmed.** קָדַם with שָׁלַם co-occurs in **exactly one verse of the Hebrew Bible, Job 41:3**. The grammar diverges, though, and the dig did not say so: **הִקְדִּימַנִי is hiphil perfect 3ms with a 1cs suffix and וַאֲשַׁלֵּם piel imperfect 1cs — "who has come first to me, that *I* should repay?" — while Paul's ἀνταποδοθήσεται is future passive third person.** Paul has turned God's first-person active into an impersonal passive.

**The load-bearing claim fails, and this is the most consequential break in the audit.**

> **Swete Job 21:31** τίς ἀπαγγελεῖ ἐπὶ προσώπου αὐτοῦ τὴν ὁδὸν αὐτοῦ; καὶ ἃ αὐτὸς ἐποίησεν, **τίς ἀνταποδώσει αὐτῷ;**
> **Rom 11:35** ἢ **τίς** προέδωκεν **αὐτῷ**, καὶ **ἀνταποδοθήσεται αὐτῷ**;

**τίς together with any form of ἀνταποδίδωμι occurs in exactly one verse of the whole Greek Old Testament, and it is in Job.** The main session re-ran this independently across all of Swete with Unicode normalisation and reproduced it: **one verse, Job 21:31.** It shares with Rom 11:35 the interrogative, the verb and the dative, differing only in voice — **matching Paul better than Job 41:3 matches his person and voice.**

**So the inference "the NT follows the MT against the LXX" is unnecessary and probably wrong. Paul did not need the Hebrew of Job 41:3 to write that line.** Swete Job 9:4 confirms the translator's habit: **τίς σκληρὸς γενόμενος ἐναντίον αὐτοῦ ὑπέμεινεν;** — it read שָׁלַם there as "endured", which is also what it does at 41:2.

**And the frame is more Joban than the dig realised.** Rom 11:33 has **ἀνεξιχνίαστοι αἱ ὁδοὶ αὐτοῦ**, and **ἀνεξιχνίαστος occurs in Swete at Job 5:9, Job 9:10, Job 34:24 and nowhere else in the Greek Old Testament** except Ode 8:6 — always in the formula "doing great and untraceable things". Main session reproduced. Rom 11:34 is **Isa 40:13** almost verbatim. **So Rom 11:33–35 is a Joban frame around an Isaianic quotation, with Job supplying the vocabulary of both outer lines.** That is evidence *for* Job and against the maxim theory — and a better finding than the one it replaces.

**Rivals inside Job, both real:** **Job 9:4** (מִי־הִקְשָׁה אֵלָיו וַיִּשְׁלָם — the same shape as 41:3, with שָׁלַם in the **third person**, closer to Paul's) and **Job 21:31** (מִי יְשַׁלֶּם־לוֹ — with **שָׁלַם + לוֹ**, a dative matching Paul's αὐτῷ). Isa 42:19 tested and rejected: its מְשֻׁלָּם is a different sense.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Job available in both languages |
| Volume | **Strong for Job as a book; Possible for 41:3 in particular** | the Hebrew pair is canon-unique, but the Greek points to 21:31 |
| Recurrence | **Strong** | ἀνεξιχνίαστος at Rom 11:33 is a Job word in Swete; three Joban candidates for v. 35 |
| Thematic coherence | **Strong** | "God is in no one's debt" is Job's argument and Paul's |
| Historical plausibility | Strong | |
| History of interpretation | **Not assessable from the corpus** | |
| Satisfaction | Strong for the Joban reading; **Weak for the MT-against-LXX reading** | |

**Verdict: Needs reframing.**
**Reasoning.** The Hebrew correspondence is real and canon-unique, but the conclusion built on it collapses: Swete Job 21:31 is the only verse in the Greek Old Testament holding both τίς and ἀνταποδίδωμι, and it matches Rom 11:35's verb, interrogative and dative better than Job 41:3 matches its person and voice. **The claim should become: Rom 11:33–35 is saturated with Joban language, nearest Job 21:31 in Greek and Job 41:3 or 9:4 in Hebrew — a stronger claim about Job that drops the Masoretic-against-Greek argument entirely.**
**Dig action:** patch the 40:6–42:6 dig's Christological Reading **and withdraw its category-3-inverted triage claim.** **Overview action: amend two places.** **Headline touched: no** (it is the Christological Reading and the triage), **but the dig's Declaration asserts the inverted triage as a finding and must be corrected.**

#### A5 — נְכוֹנָה at Ps 5:10 and Job 42:7–8 (#182)

**Source:** the 42:7–17 dig, Tool 7 and Difficult Verses. Proposed: distribution *high*, allusion *low–moderate*.

**Distribution confirmed exactly, including the plain/skeletal split.** Plain pass: **1 Kgs 2:46; Ps 5:10; Job 42:7; Job 42:8.** The skeletal pass adds three, all different words — **Gen 37:21** (נַכֶּנּוּ, from נָכָה, "strike"), **Ezek 16:7** (נָכֹנוּ, a 3cp perfect, "breasts were formed"), **Prov 19:29** (נָכוֹנוּ, "judgements are prepared"). Main session reproduced.

**The absolute use confirmed**: 1 Kgs 2:46 is a kingdom "established"; **only Ps 5:10 and Job 42:7–8 use the form absolutely, as the content of speech.**

**A deduction the dig did not make, and it is fatal to the allusion.** **The two Job verses are the identical eight-word clause repeated verbatim.** So the four-verse distribution is **three contexts and two idioms**, and Job's contribution is one formula said twice. *A distribution of four is not rare when two of the four are the same clause.*

**Greek.** Swete renders with **ἀληθές**, "true", at both 42:7 and 42:8. A Greek reader sees no link to Ps 5 at all.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | |
| Volume | **Weak** | one word, and Job's two instances are one formula |
| Recurrence | Weak | no other contact between Job 42 and Ps 5 |
| Thematic coherence | Weak | slander in Ps 5 against doctrinal error in Job |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | |
| History of interpretation | **Not assessable from the corpus** | |
| Satisfaction | Weak | |

**Verdict: the distribution Confirmed; the allusion Discard.**
**Reasoning.** Every count is right, including the homograph analysis; but a single word of a common root, with Job's two occurrences being one repeated clause and no second point of contact, cannot bear an allusion. **Which is roughly what the dig's own *low–moderate* rating said** — the dig was right to flag it low and right to queue it rather than use it.
**Dig action:** none; the dig already rated it low. **Queue action: close #182, allusion discarded, the distribution retained as a lexical observation.** **Headline touched: no.**

#### A6 — נָחַם + רָעָה + בּוֹא (#184, and the correction to audit 5)

**Source:** the 42:7–17 dig's Corrections, which used this triple to correct **claim audit 5's #152**. Proposed: lemma triple *high*, the Lam 1:21 contact *moderate*.

**Two breaks, and one of them is a correction to a correction of mine.**

**First, the stem.** The lemma triple returns **four verses — Ezek 14:22; Job 2:11; Job 42:11; Lam 1:21**. But the claim, and the dig's Declaration, and the queue entry that corrected audit 5, all specify **the hiphil of בּוֹא**. Reading the morphology:

| Verse | Form | Stem |

**And the main session's own spot checks added a finding neither the dig nor the auditor had, which reframes the whole claim.** **נָחַם followed by עַל occurs in 39 verses of the Hebrew Bible. The object of עַל is always either a decreed action or outcome — the evil (Exod 32:14; Jer 18:8; Ezek 14:22; Jonah 3:10), the good (Jer 18:10) — or a person or persons mourned (2 Sam 13:39, Amnon; Jer 31:15, her children; Ezek 32:31, his multitude; Deut 32:36, Ps 90:13, Ps 135:14, His servants). **And Swete read neither of the two options the dig was weighing.** **διὸ ἐφαύλισα ἐμαυτὸν καὶ ἐτάκην· ἥγημαι δὲ ἐγὼ ἐμαυτὸν γῆν καὶ σποδόν** — it supplies an object, renders וְנִחַמְתִּי as **ἐτάκην**, "I melted away", and **has no preposition at all**, turning the phrase into a predicate accusative. **The BHS apparatus records no note whatever at 42:6** — notes run at 42:2, 3, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 15, 16 and nothing at 6. **Verdict on the four data: (i) Confirmed with nuance · (ii) Needs reframing · (iii) Confirmed · (iv) datum Confirmed, inference Discard.**

**Edition.** WLC as the observation layer for every count and rank; the BHS apparatus as the citation of record where a reading is load-bearing; Swete as the Greek observation layer, with its diplomatic Vaticanus basis named wherever it bears on a verdict.

**Unit of contact.** A lemma entry **as the index carries it — with its homograph letter** — prefixed morphemes stripped. Noun and cognate verb are separate entries; homographs are separate entries. **The key does not separate stems, so every chain's stems were checked by hand and every split is reported.**

**Rarity ceilings: four — a lemma standing in at most 50, 150, 400 or 800 verses of the Hebrew Bible.** Proper nouns excluded by default, with one sensitivity run keeping them.

**Three measures: single-lemma overlap; same-verse rare pairs** (the stricter one, testing whether rare words *cluster* rather than merely co-occur across two long blocks); and **the Dice coefficient on the rare-lemma sets, which is primary** — because raw count rewards long chapters and per-verse density rewards very short ones (Job 25 has six verses and tops every density table as an artefact).

**The two nulls, and the second is the one that decides a design claim.**
- **Per-chapter control** — hold the claimed partner chapter fixed and rank the target block among *all* Job windows of the same verse length. *Is the claimed block really this chapter's best partner?*
- **Design null** — for every same-length window, score its own best partner chapter, and report the share whose own-best equals or exceeds the claimed pair's score.

### B3 — the epilogue as the discharge of Job 13:8–10 (#185)

**Source:** the 42:7–17 dig, **Headline 1**. Proposed: chains *high*, design *moderate–high*.

**(a) נָשָׂא with פָּנִים — count confirmed exactly, field semantically split.** The nine verses are exactly the nine claimed. **But they are not one idiom.** 11:15 and 22:26 have *you* lift *your own* face — confidence, not partiality. 22:8 is nominal, "the man whose face is lifted". **So the partiality idiom proper stands in seven verses, not nine — and the epilogue's two instances carry a different sense from 13:8–10**: there culpable flattery to be avoided, here favourable acceptance granted. **And there is a tension the dig did not register: what God does for Job at 42:8–9 is, in the same words, what 34:19 says God never does for princes.**

**(b) יָכַח — the count is wrong, and this is the main session's error, not the dig's alone.** **יָכַח stands in 15 verses and 17 occurrences of Job: 5:17, 6:25, 6:26, 9:33, 13:3, 13:10, 13:15, 15:3, 16:21, 19:5, 22:4, 23:7, 32:12, 33:19, 40:2.** The dig said twelve. **The main session's own spot check also returned twelve — because the search output was piped through a fixed-length tail that silently discarded the first three verses.** Re-run in full, the tool returns fifteen. *This is the second truncation artefact in this session's shell usage, and the first one reached a Headline.* See the gate lessons.

**The "only" step survives narrowly, in a corrected form.** God is the subject of this verb at **5:17** (object: the man), **22:4** (object: Job) and **13:10** (object: the friends); at 40:2 the relation reverses. **So 13:10 is the only verse where God reproves *the friends* — but that is a class defined narrowly enough to have exactly one member**, and the honest form of the finding is the three-verse one.

**And this is decisive against the design: יָכַח does not occur anywhere in Job 42:7–17.** Its last occurrence in the book is **40:2**. **The prediction's own verb is absent from its alleged discharge.** What 42:7 says is חָרָה אַפִּי בְךָ, "my wrath has burned against you". *The conceptual fulfilment is real; the lexical link is not.*

**The design fails worst of the four.**

| Ceiling | Chapter 13's rank of 41 | Shared rare lemmas with the epilogue | The epilogue's top partners |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 20th | **0** | Job 2, Job 1 |
| 150 | 26th | **0** | Job 2, Job 1 |
| 400 | 38th | **0** | Job 1, Job 2 |
| 800 | 29th | 5 | Job 1, Job 2 |

**At three of four ceilings chapter 13 shares no rare lexical material at all with the epilogue**, and **443 to 508 of 511 same-length windows are better partners for chapter 13.** **The epilogue's real partner is the prologue, by roughly a factor of two over anything else** — through צֶמֶד "yoke", אָתוֹן "she-ass", גָּמָל "camel", בַּעַד "on behalf of", אָחוֹת "sister": *the livestock inventory being doubled, which is the design the book advertises.*

**Verdict: Needs reframing.** The thematic observation — God does rebuke the friends, as Job said He would — is sound and worth keeping **as a thematic correspondence.** The lexical scaffolding carries a count error, a sense shift, and the absence of the prediction's own verb from the epilogue; and **chapter 13 is among the worst, not the best, early partners for 42:7–17.** **Headline touched: yes — this is the 42:7–17 dig's Headline 1.**

### B4 — chapter 8 answered twice in the epilogue (#186)

### B5 — the withheld justice vocabulary, and its arrival at 40:8 (#164)

**Source:** the 38:1–40:5 dig's Convergent 6 and the 40:6–42:6 dig's **Headline 2**. Proposed: data *high*, reading *moderate*.

**The distributions are confirmed and widened.**

| Field | Verses in Job | In 38:1–40:5 |
|---|---|---|
| מִשְׁפָּט | **23** ✓ | **none** |
| צָדַק (verb) | **17** | **none** |
| צֶדֶק (noun) | 7 | **none** |

| **צַדִּיק (adjective)** | **7 — omitted by the claim** | **none** |

**The claim's "17 verses" names one entry only — the verb.** The root has **four** entries, not three; the claim omits צַדִּיק entirely. **The absence is confirmed for a wider field than the dig tested** — not one of the five occurs anywhere in the block. *The main session's own earlier refinement, that the root is three lemmas, was itself incomplete: it is four.*

**The three-way at 40:8 is confirmed twice over**, and the part-of-speech trap holds — though the auditor rightly notes the zero is a predictable consequence of the verse's morphology rather than a finding in itself.

**Two corrections to the data, and the second breaks the reading.**

**First, the moral field is not absent from the first speech.** **רָשָׁע, "the wicked", stands in 26 verses of Job — two of them inside the supposedly empty block, at 38:13 and 38:15.** *The 38:1–40:5 dig recorded this in its own verified-chains list and then built a Convergent Finding that reads past it.* **Any claim that the first speech declines forensic or moral categories must account for those two verses.**

**Second, the absence is not a property of the block.**

| Longest justice-free runs in Job |
|---|
| **87 verses — Job 19:8 to 22:2** |
| 79 verses — Job 37:24 to 40:7 |
| 77 verses — Job 1:1 to 4:16 |
| 67 verses — Job 40:9 to 42:17 |

**The longest justice-free stretch in the book is in Job's own replies to Bildad and Zophar, and it is longer than the one containing the first divine speech.** Of 995 sliding 76-verse windows, 18 contain no justice-field verse — about 2 % — **but those 18 are overlapping slides of essentially two regions, one of which is Job 19–21.** *So a justice-free 76-verse stretch of Job is uncommon but not remarkable, and it is not unique to the whirlwind. The reading that the first speech deliberately withholds justice language cannot be carried by the distribution, because the same distribution withholds it from Job's own chapters and from the prologue.*

**But the auditor then supplied a better finding than the one it broke — two of them.**

1. **Job 40:8 is the only verse carrying מִשְׁפָּט or any צדק word in the last 147 verses of the book.** A 79-verse justice-free run ends at 40:8; a 67-verse run begins at 40:9 and runs to the end. **One word of justice stands between Elihu's last words and the final verse.** *That is a stronger and more defensible statement than "it arrives at 40:8", and it is the form the digs should carry.*
2. **מִשְׁפָּט with the hiphil of רָשַׁע and with צָדַק stands in exactly two verses of the whole Hebrew Bible — Deuteronomy 25:1 and Job 40:8.** Main session reproduced. **Deut 25:1 is the courtroom rule: "they shall justify the righteous and condemn the wicked."** Job 40:8 asks הַאַף תָּפֵר מִשְׁפָּטִי תַּרְשִׁיעֵנִי לְמַעַן תִּצְדָּק — *will you condemn Me that you may be justified?* **God's challenge is phrased in the exact three-verb vocabulary of the Deuteronomic rule, and inverts it.** *This is the best new finding of the audit.* Queued as a design claim needing its own baseline.

**Verdict: Needs reframing.** Data confirmed and in two respects strengthened; the root field is wider than stated; **the reading of the absence as remarkable is not supported.** **Headline touched: yes.**

### B6 — "restore the fortunes", and the only individual (#187)

**Source:** the 42:7–17 dig, **Headline 4**. Proposed: count *high*, the "only individual" step *moderate–high*.

**The count is exact — the 26 are the 26.** And one nuance the dig did not state: **שְׁבוּת never occurs without שׁוּב.** The noun stands in those same 26 verses and no others, **so the "co-occurrence in 26 verses" imposes no restriction at all — it is simply the frequency of the noun.** *The figure is right; the framing makes a raw result sound like a filtered one.*

**The auditor classified all 26 subjects independently from the Hebrew, and the "only individual" step survives.** Twenty-five have a nation, a people, a land, a city or a lineage as subject. **Job 42:10 is the only one in which שְׁבוּת is bound to a single named human being.**

**The two borderline cases, and why both are collective.** **Ezek 16:53** is the strongest candidate for an individual reading, since Sodom, Samaria and Jerusalem are grammatically feminine singular — **but the verse itself adds וּבְנוֹתֶיהָ, "and her daughters", twice, and its first object is a third feminine plural suffix.** **Lam 2:14** addresses a feminine singular "thou" whose *prophets* and *iniquity* are named in the same verse: a corporate figure, a city personified. **Jer 33:26** names David, but what is restored is "their" fortunes — the offspring of the patriarchs.

**Three nuances to carry.** **The ketiv:** Job 42:10 prints שבית unpointed, and **BHS reads Qere שְׁבוּת, Ketiv שְׁבִית** — so the dig's phrase is true of the *qere*; the consonantal text has the שְׁבִית byform, and **nine of the 26 carry an unpointed form.** *The 26-verse figure rests on the index's lemmatisation of a ketiv/qere byform pair, which should be cited from the apparatus rather than from a surface search.* **The Greek:** Swete has no restoration idiom at 42:10 at all — ηὔξησεν τὸν Ἰώβ, "the Lord **increased** Job" — **so the finding is about the Hebrew idiom alone.**

**Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.** **The only one of the four measured or design claims in Part B to survive largely intact** — and the dig's Headline 4 stands, with the ketiv, the never-without-שׁוּב point and the Greek silence added. **Headline touched: yes, for the additions only.**

### B7 — the cluster in Ezekiel 14 (#183)

**Source:** the 42:7–17 dig, Tool 11 and Book-Overview Tension 3. Proposed: cluster *moderate–high*, one element *low*.

**Element 1 — the name. Confirmed, and stronger than claimed.** **אִיּוֹב stands outside the book of Job in exactly two verses of the entire Hebrew Bible: Ezek 14:14 and 14:20** — six verses apart, both in the Noah–Daniel–Job triad, both with בְצִדְקָתָם. **This needs no baseline: there is no base rate for a name that occurs twice.**

**Element 2 — the construction. Rare, but stem-split, incomplete, and absent from the oldest Greek witness here.** The three-way stands in exactly four verses ✓. Three problems:
- **The dig omitted Job 2:11**, which is one of the four and arguably Ezekiel's closer match in role. **So the construction is a prologue-to-epilogue inclusio inside Job which Ezek 14:22 shares — not a two-member link.**
- **The stems differ.** Job 42:11 is **piel** (וַיְנַחֲמוּ אֹתוֹ); **Ezek 14:22 is niphal** (וְנִחַמְתֶּם). The piel with an object marker appears at **14:23** — which has no עַל־הָרָעָה clause. **The match straddles two Ezekiel verses in two stems.**
- **And Swete lacks the clause entirely.** Confirmed by the main session: **Eze 14:22 ends καὶ ὄψεσθε τὰς ὁδοὺς αὐτῶν, "and you shall see their ways", and 14:23 begins καὶ τὰ ἐνθυμήματα αὐτῶν — the whole of "and you shall be consoled concerning the evil which I brought upon Jerusalem" is absent, and so is 14:23's "they shall comfort you".** *Whether that is a Greek shortening or a witness to a shorter Hebrew cannot be settled from this corpus: there is no Ezekiel apparatus and no Rahlfs or Göttingen Ezekiel here.*

**Element 3 — חִנָּם. At base rate; the dig's own *low* was right.** The counts are exact ✓, but the word stands in **31 verses across 11 books** — about 0.13 % of verses, so roughly a 3 % chance of at least one in a 23-verse chapter. **A one-in-thirty coincidence is not a design.** In its favour, the sense at 14:23 is a close match to Job 2:3. *It is a good semantic parallel and not independent evidence.*

**And the auditor made a point about the shape of the question that the dig should have made itself:** once a chapter is known to name Job twice, **the other "contacts" are conditioned on an already-extraordinary chapter and cannot count as independent confirmations of the same hypothesis.**

**Verdict: Uncertain — flag for research.** **Ezekiel 14 demonstrably knows a figure called Job and ranks him with Noah and Daniel for righteousness; whether it also knows the wording of Job's epilogue is a separate question that the construction match raises and does not settle.** Element 1 needs no further work; element 3 should be dropped from the argument or carried only as a sense parallel; **element 2 is where the value lies and it cannot be settled here.** **Routed to Logos with a new question.** **Headline touched: no** — but Book-Overview Tension 3 must not go into the overview as proposed.

## Spot Checks

## Critical Assessment

As the standing preference requires, the gaps in this audit's own reasoning.

**1. The design null is not a p-value, and I have not let it be used as one.** Auditor B said so itself: the sliding windows overlap, so "85 % of 467" is not 397 independent trials. **What survives the dependence objection is the median** — the claimed score is below the null median in all four cases — **and the per-chapter control, which is normalisation-independent: hold the chapter fixed, vary the block, and none of the four claimed blocks is its chapter's best same-length partner.** That is the test the verdicts rest on, and it is the right one.

**2. The baseline merges stems, which inflates every contact count including the claims'.** B checked stems by hand for every named chain and reported each split, but did not re-run the baseline with stems separated. **The inflation is roughly uniform, so ranks would probably hold; but a rank decided by a hair's breadth should be redone that way.** No verdict here turns on such a rank.

**3. Dice is the auditor's choice, not the claims'.** Raw count put chapter 3 in the top 7 % of the null and per-verse density put it below the median; Dice is the scale-free compromise. **A different normalisation could move a rank by one or two places. It could not rescue a design claim, for the reason in (1).**

**4. The audit cannot see thematic design at all, and says so.** B's closing caution is the honest frame for all four reframings: **where the design claims fail, they fail as *lexically detectable* designs.** God does rebuke the friends at 42:7; Bildad's "latter end" is fulfilled; Job did sit as a king in chapter 29. **Those observations are untouched, and the digs should carry them as thematic correspondences.** What the audit refutes is the claim that rare shared wording demonstrates them — which is the claim that was made.

**5. Seven claims received no verdict, and that is a real limit, not a formality.** Four are apparatus-spread questions and the corpus holds one manuscript tradition; three are history-of-interpretation or cultural-background questions. **Both auditors were instructed to score History of Interpretation "not assessable from the corpus" rather than guess, and both did, on every claim.** So **no verdict in this audit incorporates any evidence about what interpreters have held** — which means a *Confirmed* here means "the text supports it", never "the field agrees".

**6. ### Q5 (#176) — the 42:6 construction. **Upgraded, and the audit's own argument turns out to be Boda's.**

**The "concerning" reading is attested in the literature**, which the audit could not know: **Muenchow** notes that עַל is conventionally translated locatively "but it could also function referentially as part of the idiom נחם עַל, meaning 'about' or 'concerning,' with dust and ashes identifying the object of the action."

**And the audit's decisive finding is independently in print. Boda** gives the four uses of נחם + עַל — pity for others, grief concerning a completed or intended action, comfort concerning a calamity, relief by vengeance — and observes that **"these formulations emphasize actions, persons, and emotional states — not substances."** He then draws the audit's own inference: *"since one cannot have compassion on or take vengeance on one's mortality, the phrase can mean only that Job is changing his mind concerning his mortality or is comforted concerning it."*

**A new argument the audit did not have: Pohl** reads the second verb as **niphal** within the idiom נחם עַל, "change one's mind concerning", **with the Masoretic accentuation indicating that עַל marks the object for both verbs** — so the accents themselves support the object reading.

**One caution that weakens a plank.** **Muenchow notes the LXX's "I regard myself as dirt and ashes" may reflect a verb other than נִחַמְתִּי in its Hebrew *Vorlage*.** *So Swete is weaker evidence for construing the Masoretic text than the audit treated it as being: it may be translating a different Hebrew, not construing this one.*

**Verdict revised: Needs reframing → Confirmed with nuance. The reading moves from *moderate* to *moderate–high*.** The direction ("concerning", not "in") is attested, argued from the object-type by Boda and from the accentuation by Pohl. **The self-designation gloss stays available but is no longer carried by the Greek**, which may witness another text. `[revised: logos-research]`

> Muenchow, "Dust and Dirt in Job 42:6", *JBL* 108 (1989), 597–598 · Pohl, *Ethical God-Talk in the Book of Job* (LHBOTS 698; T&T Clark, 2020), 242–243 · Boda, *'Return to Me': A Biblical Theology of Repentance* (NSBT 35; Apollos/IVP, 2015), 112–113.

### Q6 (#189) — the 42:7 against 38:2 tension. **A useful negative.**

**Ortlund devotes an article to exactly this question** and reports that commentators struggle; **one scholar pronounces the problem insoluble and argues 42:7 must refer to Job's words in chapters 1–2 rather than to the debate** — *a fifth resolution the audit did not list.* The resolutions found: Job's agonised search is relatively better than the friends' reductive certainties; Job's motives were good even where his words were not correct; **Job's right speech is his denial that suffering always and only follows sin, which is the friends' error.** **Sproul** adds that Job spoke honestly while the friends, though formally blameless, falsely accused him.

**But the audit's preferred resolution is not in the literature searched.** *"The search results do not specifically address whether any commentator distinguishes the preposition אֵלַי, appeals to נְכוֹנָה as 'reliable' rather than 'accurate', or treats the tension as genuinely unresolved."*

**Verdict: the audit's resolution (1) stands as its own argument from the text and must not be presented as the commentators' view.** The honest form for the Sermon 9 backbone: *the preposition אֵלַי is doing work that the literature has not pressed; the majority resolutions are the motive reading and the "suffering is not always from sin" reading; and at least one scholar thinks the problem insoluble.* **Add the chapters 1–2 resolution to the dig's list of four.** `[revised: logos-research]`

> Ortlund, "How Did Job Speak Rightly about God?", *Themelios* 43.3 (2018), 350–352 · *Reformation Study Bible* (2015), 824.

### Q10 (#175, #193) — Romans 11:33–35. **The audit over-corrected, and this reverses part of its most consequential break.**

**The commentators hold the reading the audit withdrew. Middendorf** gives Rom 11:35 as **"Paul's adaptation of the Hebrew of Job 41:3 (ET 41:11)"**, and **no source in the library proposes Job 21:31 or Job 9:4.** The sources do not address the Greek divergence at all.

**So the audit's break was too strong.** The Swete Job 21:31 datum stands — **τίς with any form of ἀνταποδίδωμι occurs in exactly one verse of the Greek Old Testament** — and remains an unnoticed complication worth flagging. **But the inference that Paul drew on it is the audit's own and is unsupported; the scholarly view is that Paul adapted the Hebrew, which is what the dig and the overview originally said.**

**The second half of the question came back strongly.** **Janzen** notes that **ἀνεξιχνίαστος occurs in the LXX only in Job (3×)**, rendering אין חקר at 5:9 and 9:10 and לא חקר at 34:24, and that Paul "indicates Job and Deutero-Isaiah as primary scriptural contexts". **And there is a monograph on precisely this: Naselli, *From Typology to Doxology: Paul's Use of Isaiah and Job in Romans 11:34–35*.**

**Verdicts revised.** **#175: the original Hebrew-source reading is RESTORED as the scholarly view**, at *moderate–high*; **the dig's withdrawn triage claim stays withdrawn**, because the Greek antecedent exists whether or not Paul used it, and no triage claim should rest on an absence the audit has shown is not absolute. **#193 is Confirmed and well attested — and Naselli is the work to read before the Sermon 8 backbone.** `[revised: logos-research]`

> Middendorf, *Romans 9–16* (Concordia Commentary, 2016), 1141 · Janzen, "'He Makes Peace in His High Heaven': Job and Paul in Resonance", in *Reading Job Intertextually* (Bloomsbury, 2013), 248 · Morgan, review of Naselli, *From Typology to Doxology*, *Themelios* 37.3 (2012), 518–519.

### Q7 (#179) — the royal hunt. **Uncertain → Confirmed, at high.**

**The background is attested and a JBL article makes the audit's exact point. Egyptian pharaohs performed ritual harpooning of hippopotamuses and occasionally crocodiles as part of their enthronement preparation**, demonstrating the capacity to eliminate chaos and preserve order; Egyptian royalty regularly sponsored hippo hunts, and **the successive appearance of the two animals in Job 40–41 supports identifying Behemoth and Leviathan with Egypt's most hunted creatures.** **Dick** sets it against the Assyrian lion hunt: the hippo symbolised chaos and **only the divine pharaoh and Horus could overcome it**, whereas **Leviathan defies any attempt at human conquest or sport — "Job cannot hook, harpoon, combat, or carve up Leviathan — powers that were delegated to the Assyrian or Egyptian king… whereas Horus shares control with human kings, YHWH does not delegate this dominion to any human."**

**And the link to the royal boast is explicitly drawn by commentators**: God tells Job at 40:11 to bring the proud low, and 41:34 says Leviathan looks down on all that are haughty — **"Job cannot humble the proud, nor can he subdue the king over the proud."**

**Verdict: Confirmed at high. This strengthens the 40:6–42:6 dig's Headlines 1 and 3 and should be cited in the Sermon 8 backbone.** `[revised: logos-research]`

> Zuck, "Job", *Bible Knowledge Commentary* (1985), 1:771 · *Apologetics Study Bible* (2007), 781 · **Dick, "The Neo-Assyrian Royal Lion Hunt and Yahweh's Answer to Job", *JBL* 125 (2006), 267–268** · Walton & Longman, *How to Read Job* (IVP, 2015), 83 · Walton & Vizcaino, *NIVAC: Job* (2012), 409.

### Q8 (#192) — daughters inheriting. **Uncertain → Confirmed: genuinely exceptional.**

**In the ancient Near East of the third and second millennia daughters could inherit land in three ways — in the absence of sons, after the sons, or *among* the sons, as at Job 42:15.** But **normal Israelite practice was that daughters did not inherit**; where sons were alive they had no right, and **Job 42:15 "describes an altogether exceptional procedure."** Commentators call it *atypical*, *surprising*, Job "far ahead of his time"; **Glahn** adds that Job makes no distinction between sons and daughters, treats the later daughters better than the earlier ones, and that **his restored household contains no slaves either.**

**Verdict: the audit's position is confirmed — the verse is striking and is not a norm.** Pitfall 6 stands and is now sourced on both sides: *it is remarkable, and no text legislates from it.* `[revised: logos-research]`

> Ashley, *The Book of Numbers* (NICOT, 2022), 485 · Easton, "Heir", *ISBE* (1915), 1369 · Glahn, *A Woman's Place Is in the Story* (IVP Academic, 2026), 149 · Kynes & Kynes, *Wrestling with Job* (IVP Academic, 2022), 202.

### Q9 (#191) — the daughters' names. **Lexical half settled; the wry reading unsupported.**

**Keziah is cassia**, the aromatic of Ps 45:8, with some variation over the botanical referent. **Keren-happuch is "container of antimony — a beautiful colour often used by women in cosmetics as eyeshadow", or "the horn of cosmetics".** **Jemimah is "dove", or "beautiful as the day", or "dove or daylight".** *The antimony connection to Jezebel and to Jerusalem is implicit in the sources but not drawn.*

**Two readings, and neither is the audit's wry one.** The mainstream is **paternal delight** — the names represent "the joyful new beginning for Job"; a minority reading finds Job "telling his story in the names of his daughters", the cosmetic imagery carrying a spiritual sense about stripping away pride.

**Verdict: Uncertain → the lexical claims Confirmed, the wry reading left unsupported and dropped to *low*.** Pitfall 5 keeps the names and the beauty-word distribution; **it should not imply a wry note the literature does not find.** `[revised: logos-research]`

> Holbert, "Keziah (Person)", *Anchor Yale Bible Dictionary* (1992), 4:31–32 · Lawson, *Job* (Holman OT Commentary, 2005), 368 · Sharp (2012), 65–66 · Bost, *Dictionnaire de la Bible* (2005), 482.

### Q3 (#169) — the suspended letters. **Sourced, and still reception.**

**The pair is part of the traditional list: four letters stand suspended in the Masoretic Text — nun at Judg 18:30, and ayin at Job 38:13, 38:15 and Ps 80:14 [Eng 80:13].** On the sense, **the Talmud eliminates the letter and reads רשים, a word carrying both "rulers" and "poor" — the tyrants below who are poor and powerless above — "out of respect to King David the rulers in this case were not identified with the wicked; hence the spelling רשעים."** An alternative transposes shin and ayin to read **רעשים, "storms"**, which is said to bring the verses "into entire harmony with the context and in accord with the previous chapter."

**Verdict: the audit's treatment stands — this is reception, not composition — but it is now sourced, and the "storms" alternative is worth a footnote in a sermon on the whirlwind speech.** Still barred from the overview. `[revised: logos-research]`

> Wegner, *The Journey from Texts to Translations* (Baker Academic, 2004), 173 · *The Jewish Encyclopedia* (1901–1906), 11:603.
