## Method

The method is the one the standing rule now prescribes:

- **two blind auditors**, not three;
- **run one after the other**, not in parallel;
- **auditor 1 takes the single contacts** (wording, rarity, rivals, Greek);
- **auditor 2 takes the fields and designs** (block tests, chance baselines, rank stability).

**Blindness.** Each auditor was a fresh subagent. Each was given only:

- the corpus;
- a written brief;
- its claims, stated as proposals to break, with the proposed rating attached.

Neither saw the book overview, the sweep, the Job digs, the queue's reasoning, earlier audits, project documents or memory. Auditor B was barred from auditor A's work.

**The corpus in the cloud.** The `_texts/` corpus was copied into the session workspace for the run: the WLC text and lemma index, Swete, the SBLGNT, the Old Testament Logos exports and the folder's search tools.

**Positive control.** Every auditor, and the main session, began with the same check: חֶסֶד ("lovingkindness") in Job returns 6:14; 10:12; 37:13. Every reported absence or "only" was preceded by a search that returned its own source verse.

**Shape words.** Following the round-10 rule, every *only*, *identical*, *verbatim* or *the same phrase* was tested by an ordered phrase search on the surface text, both plain and skeletal. Each such occurrence is quoted as the text prints it.

**Baselines (Part B).** Auditor B had not seen the earlier runs' scan settings. It built its own and stated them at the head of its part:

- same-verse word pairs in ten-verse windows, under rarity ceilings of 150, 400 and 800 verses a word;
- chapter density at two ceilings;
- loose windows;
- rarity-weighted single words;
- and for every rank, **a Job null**: how many other Job blocks of the same length rank the same chapter as high.

Speaker baselines (B7, B8, B10, B11) normalise by verses of speech.

**Spot checks.** The main session re-ran 25 of the results the verdicts turn on (see Spot Checks). It also rebuilt the measurement on which B9 rests with its own counting.

## Positional Frame (Phase 0.6)

**What has the book set up that these claims serve?**

- **Third round (22–28).** The debate ends:
  - Eliphaz's direct accusation (22);
  - Job's unaddressed reply (23–24);
  - Bildad's borrowed six verses (25);
  - Job's oath and the portion of the wicked (27);
  - the poem on wisdom (28).
- **Job's last speech (29–31):**
  - his former life as the protector of the poor (29);
  - its reversal (30);
  - his oath of clearance, ending with a demand for an answer (31:35).
- **Elihu (32–37):** an unannounced fourth speaker, angry at Job and the friends, who promises new words (32:14) and leads into the LORD's speech (38:1).

**What the claims under test say.** These three units speak in the words of Scripture outside Job:

- Isa 58 in Eliphaz's offer and in Job's oath;
- Ps 8, Isa 14, Ps 78 and Lam 2 in the third round;
- Ps 72, Ps 22, Ps 32, Isa 61, Ps 102 and a dozen smaller texts in Job 29–31;
- Ezek 14, Ps 62, Ps 49, Ps 104, Ps 29, Ps 56 and Proverbs in Elihu.

Elihu's speeches are also said to have a measurable shape: he reuses Eliphaz most, he draws on the Psalter, he anticipates the LORD, and the Old Greek shortened him most.

**Why this matters here.** Every one of these claims is canonical or synthetic, the kind the project's audits have found most prone to overreach. Ten of them appear in the Headline Findings of the three digs, and the backbones for Sermons 5–7 will lift those headlines.

## Part A: Single Contacts (auditor A)

*Blind audit. Corpus only: WLC (reading text and lemma index), Swete, the Rahlfs-Hanhart, BHS, NASB95, ESV and NIV84 Logos exports. Hebrew versification throughout; English verse numbers in square brackets where they differ.*

**Positive control.** חֶסֶד ("steadfast love") in Job by lemma: Job 6:14; 10:12; 37:13 — the three expected verses (WLC). Before each reported absence or "only", the same search was confirmed to return the source verse itself.

**Null method used below.** Where a chapter rank is quoted, the Job passage was scanned against every chapter of ten or more verses in the Hebrew Bible for rare shared word-pairs (pairs found together in no more than a set number of verses of the Hebrew Bible — the pair ceiling; ceilings of 5, 10 and 20 verses tried). The Job null is the number of other, non-overlapping Job blocks of the same length that rank the same chapter at least as high (with at least one shared pair).

### A-full tier

#### A1: Ps 8:4–5 [Eng 8:3–4] behind Job 25:5–6
**Claim as proposed:** Moon + stars within one verse of אֱנוֹשׁ ("mortal man") / אָדָם ("man") only at Ps 8:4–5 and Job 25:5–6; Bildad's "how much less man, that maggot" inverts Ps 8's "what is man that You remember him"; Ps 8 is contested elsewhere in Job (7:17; 15:14).
**Proposed rating:** words high; use moderate–high.
**Evidence (in words):**
- יָרֵחַ ("moon") is in 26 verses, כּוֹכָב ("star") in 37; together in one verse in 12 (Gen 37:9; Deut 4:19; Isa 13:10; Jer 31:35; Ezek 32:7; Joel 2:10; 4:15; Ps 8:4; 136:9; 148:3; Job 25:5; Eccl 12:2) (WLC). [T]
- Moon + star with אֱנוֹשׁ ("mortal", 41 verses) within one verse: Ps 8:4 and Job 25:5 only. The same with אָדָם ("man") in place of אֱנוֹשׁ: Ps 8:4 and Job 25:5 only (WLC). The search returns its own source verse, so the "only" stands. [T]
- אֱנוֹשׁ + בֶּן ("son") + אָדָם in one verse (the "mortal // son of man" pair): Isa 51:12; 56:2; Ps 8:5; 90:3; 144:3; Job 25:6 — six verses (WLC). Only Ps 8 adds the moon and stars. [T]
- The ordered phrase מָה־אֱנוֹשׁ ("what is mortal man") occurs only at Ps 8:5; Job 7:17; Job 15:14 (WLC, plain and skeletal; the skeletal pass adds a false hit at Gen 5:11, the name Enosh). Job 7:17–18 reuses Ps 8:5's verb פקד ("visit"). So "Ps 8 is contested elsewhere" is textually sound. [T]
- Chapter scan of Job 25 (6 verses): Ps 8 ranks 1st of 822 chapters at a pair ceiling of 10 verses and at a pair ceiling of 20 verses; at a pair ceiling of 5 verses it has no qualifying pair (the pairs are slightly too common). Job null: 1 of 176 other six-verse Job blocks ranks Ps 8 as high at a pair ceiling of 10 verses; 0 of 176 at a pair ceiling of 20 verses. [T]
- Bildad's "how much less" (אַף כִּי) is an a fortiori from the heavenly bodies down to man; Ps 8 moves from the heavens to wonder that God remembers man. The direction is reversed. [I]
**Rivals:** The closest text is **inside Job**. Job 25:4–6 is a reprise of Eliphaz at Job 15:14–16: "they are not pure in His sight" (לֹא־זַכּוּ בְעֵינָיו) occurs only at Job 15:15 and Job 25:5 (WLC, ordered phrase, plain and skeletal: Job 15:15 הֵן בקדשו לֹא יַאֲמִין וְשָׁמַיִם לֹא־זַכּוּ בְעֵינָיו; Job 25:5 הֵן עַד־יָרֵחַ וְלֹא יַאֲהִיל וְכוֹכָבִים לֹא־זַכּוּ בְעֵינָיו); both have אַף כִּי next (15:16; 25:6); 25:4a repeats 9:2b verbatim (וּמַה־יִּצְדַּק אֱנוֹשׁ עִם־אֵל). Bildad's change from Eliphaz's "heavens" to "moon … stars", and his addition of "son of man", are precisely the moves that bring Ps 8:4–5 into view. Ps 144:3 has the man/son-of-man question but no heavenly bodies; Ps 90:3 likewise. [T]
**Greek:** The contact survives in both Greek texts. Swete and Rahlfs Job 25:5–6: σελήνῃ ("moon") … ἄστρα ("stars") … ἄνθρωπος … υἱὸς ἀνθρώπου; Ps 8:4–5: σελήνην καὶ ἀστέρας … τί ἐστιν ἄνθρωπος … υἱὸς ἀνθρώπου. σελην- + ἀστ- + ἀνθρωπ- within one verse: Job 25:5 and Ps 8:4 only in Swete. No asterisk in Rahlfs at 25:5–6 (Old Greek). [T]
**Hays criteria:**

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Possible | Relative dating of Ps 8 and Job not derivable from the text [I] |
| Volume | Strong | Four-word cluster (moon, stars, mortal, son of man) unique to the two passages |
| Recurrence | Strong | מָה־אֱנוֹשׁ elsewhere only at Job 7:17 and 15:14 |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | Worth of man before God is the dispute's question |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Not testable from the corpus |
| History of interpretation | Not checked | — |
| Satisfaction | Strong | The reversal (a fortiori down, not wonder up) reads as deliberate |

**Verdict.** Confirmed with nuance. The word contact is exclusive and survives in the Greek; the scan rank holds at two settings and beats the Job null. The nuance: Bildad's immediate model is Eliphaz (15:14–16), and the Ps 8 colouring is what Bildad adds to it.
**Final rating:** words **high**; use (Bildad turns Ps 8, building on Eliphaz 15:14–16) **moderate–high**.
**A preacher may safely say:** Bildad borrows Eliphaz's argument but dresses it in Psalm 8's moon, stars and "son of man" — and turns the psalm's wonder into contempt.

#### A2: Ps 78:64 at Job 27:15
**Claim as proposed:** וְאַלְמְנֹתָיו לֹא תִבְכֶּינָה ("and his widows will not weep") verbatim only at Ps 78:64 and Job 27:15; the sword stands before both; the Greek of Job does not keep the wording.
**Proposed rating:** phrase high.
**Evidence (in words):**
- Ordered phrase, plain and skeletal: only Ps 78:64 and Job 27:15 (WLC). The two lines as printed: Ps 78:64 כֹּהֲנָיו בַּחֶרֶב נָפָלוּ וְאַלְמְנֹתָיו לֹא תִבְכֶּינָה; Job 27:15 שרידו בַּמָּוֶת יִקָּבֵרוּ וְאַלְמְנֹתָיו לֹא תִבְכֶּינָה. The second half-line is identical, letter for letter and accent for accent. [T]
- The Hebrew is "**his** widows" in both. "Their widows" in the Psalm is an English rendering (antecedent "his priests" in a collective), not a Hebrew difference. [T]
- Rarity: אַלְמָנָה ("widow", 54 verses) + בכה ("weep", 100 verses) in one verse: Ps 78:64 and Job 27:15 only (WLC). With חֶרֶב ("sword", 371 verses) within one verse: the same two only. [T]
- Sword precedes: Ps 78:64a "his priests fell by the sword"; Job 27:14 "if his sons multiply, it is for the sword". [T]
- Job 27:15a is a ketiv (שרידו, qere "his survivors"; BHS apparatus notes Q/K); it does not touch the shared half-line. [T]
**Rivals:** No other verse has the widows-not-weeping pairing. Ps 78 as a chapter does not stand out in a scan of Job 27:13–23 (rank 67 at a pair ceiling of 5 verses, 124 at a pair ceiling of 10 verses — expected for a 72-verse chapter whose density is diluted); the scan ranks Lam 2 and Zech 9 highest (see A3). The contact is a single verbatim colon, not a chapter-level pattern. [T]
**Greek:** Not kept. Swete and Rahlfs Job 27:15: χήρας δὲ αὐτῶν οὐθεὶς ἐλεήσει ("their widows no one will pity"); Ps 77:64 [Heb 78:64] αἱ χῆραι αὐτῶν οὐ κλαυσθήσονται ("their widows will not be wept for"). Only χήρα is shared. Rahlfs prints Job 27:15a (οἱ δὲ περιόντες αὐτοῦ …), which Swete lacks; it carries no asterisk in the export. [T]
**Hays criteria:**

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Possible | Relative date not derivable [I] |
| Volume | Strong | Three-word colon verbatim; pair exclusive |
| Recurrence | Weak | Not tested here (Part B) |
| Thematic coherence | Possible | Judgement on the wicked's house (Job) vs on Israel (Ps); both a curse of unmourned death |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Not testable |
| History of interpretation | Not checked | — |
| Satisfaction | Possible | A stock curse-formula would explain it equally well with only two attestations |

**Verdict.** Confirmed. The phrase is verbatim and exclusive in the Hebrew; correct the gloss ("his widows" in both).
**Final rating:** phrase **high**; direction of borrowing undetermined.
**A preacher may safely say:** Job 27:15 uses, word for word, a line found elsewhere only in Psalm 78:64 — the widows who cannot even mourn.

#### A3: Lam 2:15 at Job 27:23
**Claim as proposed:** ספק ("clap") + שׁרק ("hiss") in one verse only at Lam 2:15 and Job 27:23; the hissing line is asterisked in Rahlfs' Job; Lam 2 is held elsewhere to be a source of Job 16.
**Proposed rating:** moderate–high.
**Evidence (in words):**
- ספק/שׂפק ("clap", the clapping entry, 10 verses including the "suffice" homograph at 1 Kgs 20:10) + שׁרק ("hiss", 12 verses) in one verse: Job 27:23 and Lam 2:15 only (WLC). The search returns Lam 2:15 itself. [T]
- Clap + כַּף ("palm"): Num 24:10; Job 27:23; Lam 2:15 only (WLC). So the full gesture (clap palms … hiss) is Lam 2:15 and Job 27:23 only. [T]
- Wording: Lam 2:15 סָפְקוּ עָלַיִךְ כַּפַּיִם … שָׁרְקוּ; Job 27:23 יִשְׂפֹּק עָלֵימוֹ כַפֵּימוֹ וְיִשְׁרֹק עָלָיו. Same order (clap — over [victim] — palms — hiss), with עַל in both. Job spells the verb with sin. [T]
- Scan of Job 27:13–23 (11 verses): Lam 2 ranks 2nd at a pair ceiling of 5 verses and 1st at a pair ceiling of 10 verses of 822 chapters. Job null: 2 of 95 other eleven-verse Job blocks rank Lam 2 as high at a pair ceiling of 5 verses; 0 of 95 at a pair ceiling of 10 verses. The rank holds at two settings and beats the null. [T]
**Rivals:** Zeph 2:15 has hiss + "shake (נוע) the hand" + "everyone who passes by", and shares hiss + shake (נוע) with Lam 2:15 alone; but it has no clapping and does not register against Job 27 in a scan (no qualifying pair). Hiss + "passer-by" (עֹבֵר) is a stock curse-formula (1 Kgs 9:8; Jer 19:8; 49:17; 50:13; Zeph 2:15; Lam 2:15). Job 27:23 lacks the "passer-by" element; what it shares exclusively with Lam 2:15 is the clapping. Job 34:37 uses the clap verb alone (Elihu). [T]
**Greek:** Survives in Swete: κροτ- ("clap") + συρ- ("hiss") in one verse only Job 27:23 and Lam 2:15 (κροτήσει … χεῖρας … συριεῖ; ἐκρότησαν … χεῖρας … ἐσύρισαν). In Rahlfs, the hissing colon of 27:23 (καὶ συριεῖ αὐτὸν ἐκ τοῦ τόπου αὐτοῦ) opens with the asterisk and is closed by the metobelus: confirmed. The clapping colon stands outside that asterisk, so the Old Greek had the clap but not the hiss. (The export also has asterisks at 27:21b and 27:22b with no separate metobelus; the exact limits of those stretches should be checked in the printed edition.) [T]
**Hays criteria:**

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Possible | Dating not derivable; if Lamentations is post-587, Job's use is later than that |
| Volume | Strong | Exclusive clap-palms-hiss sequence, same order |
| Recurrence | Possible | Lam 2 as source of Job 16 not tested here |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | Public derision of the fallen (Jerusalem / the wicked man) |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Not testable |
| History of interpretation | Not checked | — |
| Satisfaction | Possible | The derision gesture could be idiomatic; only two attestations of the full form |

**Verdict.** Confirmed with nuance. The exclusive contact and the chapter-level rank both stand; the asterisk claim is correct for the hissing colon. A curse-gesture idiom remains a live alternative to literary dependence.
**Final rating:** **moderate–high**.
**A preacher may safely say:** The clapping and hissing over the wicked man in Job 27:23 is the gesture the passers-by make over fallen Jerusalem in Lamentations 2:15 — the only two places it is described that way.

#### A4: Ps 72:12 at Job 29:12
**Claim as proposed:** עָנִי ("afflicted") + מְשַׁוֵּעַ ("who cries for help") + עֹזֵר ("helper") together only at Ps 72:12 and Job 29:12; Swete renders the "who cries" phrase in both as ἐκ χειρὸς δυνάστου; πτωχ- + δυνάστου otherwise only Lev 19:15.
**Proposed rating:** words high.
**Evidence (in words):**
- The two verses as printed: Ps 72:12 כִּי־יַצִּיל אֶבְיוֹן מְשַׁוֵּעַ וְעָנִי וְאֵין־עֹזֵר לוֹ; Job 29:12 כִּי־אֲמַלֵּט עָנִי מְשַׁוֵּעַ וְיָתוֹם וְלֹא־עֹזֵר לוֹ. Same frame: כִּי + verb of rescue + needy one + מְשַׁוֵּעַ + second needy one + negative + עֹזֵר לוֹ. [T]
- Rarity (WLC): עָנִי ("afflicted") in 74 verses; שׁוע ("cry for help", piel) in 21; עֹזֵר ("helper", participle of עזר) in 77. All three together: Ps 72:12; Job 29:12 only. Any two of them (cry + helper; afflicted + helper): the same two verses only. Afflicted + cry: Ps 22:25; 72:12; Job 29:12. [T]
- The participle מְשַׁוֵּעַ as a surface form: Ps 72:12 and Job 29:12 only (WLC, plain); "helper to him" (עֹזֵר לוֹ) as an ordered phrase: Ps 72:12 and Job 29:12 only (plain; the skeletal pass adds unrelated forms). [T]
- Chapter scan of the whole of Job 29 (25 verses): Ps 72 ranks 2nd at a pair ceiling of 5 verses and 5th at a pair ceiling of 10 verses of 822 chapters; Job null: 0 of 40 other 25-verse Job blocks rank Ps 72 as high at either setting. [T]
**Rivals:** Ps 22:25 shares afflicted + cry only (no helper; see A5). Ps 10:14 shares orphan + helper (see A21). Neither matches the full frame. [T]
**Greek:** Survives, and strongly. Swete Job 29:12 διέσωσα γὰρ πτωχὸν ἐκ χειρὸς δυνάστου, καὶ ὀρφανῷ ᾧ οὐκ ἦν βοηθὸς ἐβοήθησα; Ps 71:12 [Heb 72:12] ἐρύσατο πτωχὸν ἐκ χειρὸς δυνάστου, καὶ πένητα ᾧ οὐχ ὑπῆρχεν βοηθός (Rahlfs the same in substance). In both, "from the hand of the mighty" stands where the Hebrew has the participle "who cries" — a shared non-literal rendering. πτωχ- ("poor") + δυνάστου ("of the mighty") in one verse: Job 29:12; Lev 19:15; Ps 71:12 only (Swete) — the claim holds. But the phrase ἐκ χειρὸς δυνάστου also occurs at Job 5:15 (rendering a real "from the hand of the strong", מִיַּד חָזָק), so it is in the Job translator's own stock; the shared rendering may be the translator reaching for his phrase, or knowledge of the Greek Psalter. [T][I]
**Hays criteria:**

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Possible | Dating not derivable |
| Volume | Strong | Three-word exclusive cluster in the same syntactic frame |
| Recurrence | Possible | Ps 72 elsewhere in Job 29 left to Part B; chapter rank suggests more |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | Job claims the ideal king's rescue of the poor for himself |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Not testable |
| History of interpretation | Possible | The Greek translators of both render the line alike [T] |
| Satisfaction | Strong | Job's self-portrait in royal terms is apt to ch. 29 |

**Verdict.** Confirmed, with one correction to the Greek: the shared rendering is real, but ἐκ χειρὸς δυνάστου is also at Job 5:15 and should not be presented as unique to the two verses.
**Final rating:** words **high**.
**A preacher may safely say:** Job describes his past rescue of the poor in the very words Psalm 72:12 uses of the ideal king — the only two verses in the Bible that put those words together.

#### A5: Ps 22 at Job 29:12; 30:20; 31:35
**Claim as proposed:** (a) עָנִי ("afflicted") + שׁוע ("cry for help") in one verse only Ps 22:25; Ps 72:12; Job 29:12; (b) "You do not answer", Ps 22:3 [Eng 22:2] and Job 30:20; (c) the play of עָנִי ("afflicted") with עָנָה ("answer") in both. These are to lift Ps 22 above an earlier "low".
**Proposed rating:** moderate.

- (a) Afflicted + cry for help in one verse: Ps 22:25; Ps 72:12; Job 29:12 only (WLC). Confirmed. But Ps 72:12 also shares עֹזֵר ("helper") and the whole syntactic frame with Job 29:12, and Ps 22:25 does not (A4). Ps 22:25 uses the noun-infinitive בְשַׁוְּעוֹ, not the participle. The ordered phrase "and You do not answer" (וְלֹא תַעֲנֶה) is at Exod 23:2; Deut 5:20 (different sense) and Ps 22:3; Job's form with suffix (וְלֹא תַעֲנֵנִי) only at Job 30:20 (WLC). So the closest Hebrew rivals for Job 30:20 are Ps 18:42 ("they cried for help … He did not answer them") and Job's own 19:7, not Ps 22:3. [T]

Lam 3, Ps 88, Hab 1 and Jer 20 do not rank in the top 40 at either setting. None of the proposed lament rivals beats Ps 22 at chapter level either; the lament field is generic. [T]

Swete Ps 21:3 [Heb 22:3] κεκράξομαι ἡμέρας πρὸς σὲ καὶ οὐκ εἰσακούσῃ; Job 30:20 κέκραγα δὲ πρὸς σὲ καὶ οὐκ ἀκούεις μου (Rahlfs: οὐκ εἰσακούεις μου). κεκρα- ("cry") + "to you" (πρὸς σέ) + "not" in one verse: Job 30:20 and Ps 21:3 only (Swete). Job 30:20b is asterisked in Rahlfs; 30:20a is Old Greek. Job 31:35's "let the Almighty answer me" (שַׁדַּי יַעֲנֵנִי) is not answered in the Greek (χεῖρα δὲ κυρίου εἰ μὴ ἐδεδοίκειν). [T][I]

**Verdict.** Needs reframing. None of the three lifts Ps 22 as a Hebrew source: (a) is Ps 72's contact, (b) is a common idiom with closer rivals (Ps 18:42; Job 19:7), (c) is generic. The Greek convergence at Job 30:20 / Ps 21:3 is a genuine reception-level datum worth recording as such.

#### A6: Ps 32:5 at Job 31:33 (and the Gen 3 link)
**Claim as proposed:** כסה ("cover") + פֶּשַׁע ("transgression") + עָוֺן ("iniquity") in one verse only Ps 32:5 and Job 31:33; Prov 28:13 has cover + transgression (six verses share that pair). כְאָדָם is "like Adam" (NASB95) / "as others do" (ESV); the Greek reads "unintentionally". The Gen 3 link rests on כְאָדָם alone.
**Proposed rating:** words high; deliberate echo moderate.
**Evidence (in words):**
- Cover (149 verses) + transgression (90) + iniquity (215) in one verse: Ps 32:5 and Job 31:33 only (WLC). The search returns Ps 32:5 itself. [T]
- Cover + transgression: Ps 32:1; 32:5; Job 31:33; Prov 10:12; 17:9; 28:13 — six verses, as claimed. Cover + iniquity: Isa 26:21; Ps 32:5; 85:3; Job 31:33; Neh 3:37. [T]
- Both verses are first person with the same suffixed forms: Ps 32:5 וַעֲוֺנִי לֹא־כִסִּיתִי … עֲלֵי פְשָׁעַי; Job 31:33 אִם־כִּסִּיתִי כְאָדָם פְּשָׁעָי … עֲוֺנִי — the same verb form כִּסִּיתִי ("I covered"), with "my transgressions" and "my iniquity". Ps 32 denies covering and confesses; Job's oath denies covering. [T]
- Cover + transgression + confess (ידה): Ps 32:5 and Prov 28:13 only — Job 31:33 has no confession, so Prov 28:13 is close to Ps 32 in idea but not to Job in wording. [T]
- Job-internal rival: "my transgression" + "my iniquity" in one verse is Job's own pairing — Job 7:21; 14:17; 31:33 (surface forms, WLC), with 13:23 near it. What Job 31:33 adds to his own idiom is the verb "cover", and that is what points to Ps 32:5. [T]
**Rivals:** Prov 28:13 ("he who covers his transgressions will not prosper") is the strongest proverbial rival, but lacks עָוֺן and the first person. Job's own earlier pairing (7:21; 14:17) supplies two of the three words. [T]
**Greek:** Not kept. Swete and Rahlfs Job 31:33 εἰ δὲ καὶ ἁμαρτὼν ἀκουσίως ἔκρυψα τὴν ἁμαρτίαν μου ("if, having sinned unintentionally, I hid my sin") — one sin-word, the verb κρύπτω, no Adam, and no second colon in this verse. Ps 31:5 [Heb 32:5] τὴν ἀνομίαν μου οὐκ ἐκάλυψα uses καλύπτω. The Greek contact reduces to "hide my sin". [T]
**The Gen 3 link.** The WLC lemma index tags כְאָדָם at Job 31:33 as the **proper name Adam**, as it does at Hos 6:7; it tags Ps 82:7's identical כְּאָדָם as the common noun 'man'. The ordered form כְאָדָם occurs only at Hos 6:7; Ps 82:7; Job 31:33 (WLC). That tagging is an editorial decision, not a datum. Gen 3's vocabulary of hiding is חבא ("hide oneself", 3:8, 10) and of fear ירא ("fear", 3:10); Job 31:33–34 uses טמן ("hide, bury"), חֹב ("bosom"), ערץ ("dread") and חתת ("terrify") — no shared lemma beyond אָדָם. (Fear + חבא in one verse is Gen 3:10 and Job 5:21 only — not at 31:33–34.) NASB95 "like Adam"; ESV "as others do"; NIV84 "as men do" with a footnote "as Adam did". So the Gen 3 link does rest on כְאָדָם alone. [T]
**Hays criteria:**

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Possible | Not derivable |
| Volume | Strong | Exclusive triple in the same first-person form, same verb form |
| Recurrence | Weak | Not shown elsewhere in Job |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | Concealment vs confession of sin |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Not testable |
| History of interpretation | Weak | The Greek does not see it |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Two of the three words are Job's own stock; the verb carries the weight |

**Verdict.** Confirmed with nuance for Ps 32:5 (exclusive triple; but two of its three words are Job's own pairing). For Gen 3: Uncertain — rests on one ambiguous word whose reading as a name is an editorial decision.
**Final rating:** words **high**; deliberate echo of Ps 32:5 **moderate**; Gen 3 at 31:33 **low–moderate**.
**A preacher may safely say:** Job swears he has not done what Psalm 32 says the sinner must stop doing — "covering" his transgression and iniquity; "like Adam" is one possible reading of a word that may simply mean "as people do".

#### A7: Ezek 14:14 at Job 32:1 (with Ezek 14:22 and Job 42:8–10)
**Claim as proposed:** "These three men" (שְׁלֹשֶׁת הָאֲנָשִׁים הָאֵלֶּה) only at Ezek 14:14, 16, 18 and Job 32:1; Ezek 14:22 shares hear/calamity + come + comfort with Job 2:11 and 42:11; Job 42:8–10 inverts Ezek 14:14–20.
**Proposed rating:** phrase high; design moderate.
**Evidence (in words):**
- Ordered phrase, plain: Ezek 14:14; 14:16; Job 32:1; skeletal adds Ezek 14:18 (which has a prefixed "and", וּשְׁלֹשֶׁת) (WLC). As printed: Ezek 14:14 וְהָיוּ שְׁלֹשֶׁת הָאֲנָשִׁים הָאֵלֶּה בְּתוֹכָהּ נֹחַ דנאל וְאִיּוֹב; Ezek 14:16 שְׁלֹשֶׁת הָאֲנָשִׁים הָאֵלֶּה בְּתוֹכָהּ; Ezek 14:18 וּשְׁלֹשֶׁת הָאֲנָשִׁים הָאֵלֶּה בְּתוֹכָהּ; Job 32:1 וַיִּשְׁבְּתוּ שְׁלֹשֶׁת הָאֲנָשִׁים הָאֵלֶּה מֵעֲנוֹת אֶת־אִיּוֹב. The claim stands. [T]
- Rarity: each word is very common (the numeral "three", "men", "these"); the rarity is in the construction. "These men" (הָאֲנָשִׁים הָאֵלֶּה) is ordinary narrative Hebrew — 15 verses (e.g. Gen 34:21; Num 16:30; 2 Kgs 20:14; Jer 38:16; Ezek 14:3). Numeral + "men" occurs in 37 verses; numeral + "**the** men" in 15 (e.g. Deut 19:17; Josh 2:4, 23; Judg 18:7, 14, 17; 1 Kgs 21:13; Jer 41:2; Job 32:5). Numeral + "the men" + "these" is found only in Ezek 14:14, 16, 18 and Job 32:1 (WLC, surface search of every numeral two to ten). So the full phrase is exclusive, but it is built from an ordinary narrative pattern, and Job 32:5 has "the three men" without "these" four verses later. [T]
- Ezekiel names Job (with Noah and Daniel) at 14:14 and 14:20: confirmed. Job's name occurs outside the book of Job only in Ezek 14:14, 20 (the proper name is in 52 verses in all). [T]
- Ezek 14:22: comfort (נחם) + calamity (רָעָה) + come/bring (בוא) in one verse: Ezek 14:22; Job 2:11; Job 42:11; Lam 1:21 only (WLC). The narrower phrase "concerning the calamity that [subject] brought upon" (עַל … הָרָעָה אֲשֶׁר הֵבִיא/הֵבֵאתִי עַל) with comfort: Ezek 14:22 and Job 42:11 only (the phrase without comfort also at Jer 44:2 and 2 Sam 19:8). This is a real and rare contact between Ezek 14:22 and Job 42:11 — closer than the one with Job 2:11, where "hear" + comfort + calamity also includes Lam 1:21. [T]
- Job 42:7–10 against Ezek 14:12–20: apart from Job's name, the two passages share no word found in fewer than 600 verses. Ezekiel's verb is נצל ("deliver"), absent from Job 42. The "inversion" (three saved by one man's intercession vs three righteous saving only themselves) is a thematic reading, not a lexical one. [T][I]
**Rivals:** For "these three men", none. For comfort-over-calamity, Lam 1:21 (no comforter; enemies heard of my calamity) shares the field with Job 2:11. [T]
**Greek:** Not kept at Job 32:1: Swete and Rahlfs read οἱ τρεῖς φίλοι αὐτοῦ ("his three friends"), against Ezek 14:14, 16 οἱ τρεῖς ἄνδρες οὗτοι. At Job 42:11 / Ezek 14:22 the Greek keeps ἐπάγω ("bring upon") in both (ἐπὶ πᾶσιν οἷς ἐπήγαγεν αὐτῷ ὁ κύριος; ἐπὶ τὰ κακά, ἃ ἐπήγαγον), but Ezekiel's Greek has "you will regret" (μεταμεληθήσεσθε), not "comfort". [T]
**Tests:**

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Ordered phrase, plain and skeletal | Ezek 14:14, 16, 18; Job 32:1 only |
| Is "the N men these" ordinary? | "These men" common (15 vv.); numeral + the men common (15 vv.); with "these" only Ezek 14 + Job 32:1 |
| Word rarity | All three words very common |
| Ezek 14:22 ~ Job 42:11 | Comfort + "concerning the calamity that X brought upon": exclusive pair |
| Ezek 14:22 ~ Job 2:11 | Shared with Lam 1:21; weaker |
| Job 42:8–10 inverts Ezek 14:14–20 | No lexical support beyond the name Job |
| Greek | Phrase lost at 32:1; "bring upon" kept at 42:11 |

**Verdict.** Confirmed with nuance. The phrase is exclusive; but it is a natural narrative construction, so its weight rests on its coinciding with Ezekiel's only mention of Job. The Ezek 14:22 ~ Job 42:11 contact is real and should be stated more precisely than "hear/calamity + come + comfort". The inversion of Ezek 14:14–20 at Job 42:8–10 is an inference only.
**Final rating:** phrase **moderate–high** (exclusive, but from common parts); Ezek 14:22 ~ Job 42:11 **moderate**; design (42:8–10 inverting Ezek 14) **low–moderate**.
**A preacher may safely say:** The narrator calls the friends "these three men" — the exact words Ezekiel uses of Noah, Daniel and Job — and nowhere else in Scripture is that phrase used.

#### A8: Ps 62:12–13 [Eng 62:11–12] at Job 33:14; 34:11; 40:5
**Claim as proposed:** אַחַת ("once") + שְׁתַּיִם ("twice") + דבר ("speak") in one verse only Ps 62:12; Job 33:14; 40:5; "You recompense a man according to his work" (Ps 62:13) ~ Job 34:11 (also Prov 24:12). ESV renders 33:14 "in one way, and in two".
**Proposed rating:** moderate–high.
**Evidence (in words):**
- The lines as printed: Ps 62:12 אַחַת דִּבֶּר אֱלֹהִים שְׁתַּיִם־זוּ שָׁמָעְתִּי; Job 33:14 כִּי־בְאַחַת יְדַבֶּר־אֵל וּבִשְׁתַּיִם לֹא יְשׁוּרֶנָּה; Job 40:5 אַחַת דִּבַּרְתִּי וְלֹא אֶעֱנֶה וּשְׁתַּיִם וְלֹא אוֹסִיף. [T]
- One (739 verses) + two (646) + speak (1,049) in one verse: Num 17:21; Ps 62:12; Job 33:14; Job 40:5; Dan 11:27 (WLC). So the "only" fails mechanically — but Num 17:21 (twelve rods, one per leader) and Dan 11:27 (two kings speak lies at one table) are not the idiom. With the feminine numerals standing alone as adverbs ("once … twice"), the idiom occurs at 2 Kgs 6:10 ("not once and not twice", לֹא אַחַת וְלֹא שְׁתָּיִם); Ps 62:12; Job 33:14; Job 40:5 (WLC surface search). Of those, speaking + once/twice is Ps 62:12 and Job 33:14; 40:5 only. [T]
- Is one + two a common numerical idiom? The graded numerical saying elsewhere uses higher numbers — six/seven (Prov 6:16; Job 5:19), three/four (Prov 30:15, 18, 21, 29; Amos 1–2), seven/eight (Mic 5:4; Eccl 11:2). One/two is rare; its use with God's speaking is confined to Ps 62 and Job (Elihu at 33:14, Job at 40:5). [T]
- Job 34:11 כִּי פֹעַל אָדָם יְשַׁלֶּם־לוֹ וּכְאֹרַח אִישׁ יַמְצִאֶנּוּ. Shared with each rival: Ps 62:13 — repay (שׁלם piel) + man (אִישׁ) (that pair is in 12 verses); Prov 24:12 — work (פֹּעַל) + man (אָדָם), with a different verb (שׁוב hiphil); Jer 25:14 and 50:29 — repay + work (פֹּעַל) (that pair is in four verses: Jer 25:14; 50:29; Job 34:11; Ruth 2:12); Jer 17:10 and 32:19 — "to each man according to his ways", with דֶּרֶךְ where Job has its synonym אֹרַח; Ps 28:4 — work (פֹּעַל) only. Repay + work + man: Job 34:11 alone. Ps 62:13 is one of five equal rivals, not the closest. [T]
**Rivals:** For 33:14 and 40:5, 2 Kgs 6:10 has the idiom without speech; no closer text. For 34:11, Jer 25:14 / 50:29 (repay + work) and Prov 24:12 (work + man) are as close as Ps 62:13. [T]
**Greek:** Survives for the once/twice line. Ps 61:12 [Heb 62:12] ἅπαξ ἐλάλησεν ὁ θεός, δύο ταῦτα ἤκουσα; Job 33:14 ἐν γὰρ τῷ ἅπαξ λαλήσαι ὁ κύριος, ἐν δὲ τῷ δευτέρῳ; Job 40:5 (Swete 39:35) ἅπαξ λελάληκα, ἐπὶ δὲ τῷ δευτέρῳ οὐ προσθήσω. "Once" + "spoke" (ἅπαξ + ἐλάλ-/λελάλ-) in Swete: Ps 61:12; Job 39:35 [Heb 40:5]; Deut 9:13 (different sense). For 34:11, Greek Job ἀποδιδοῖ ἀνθρώπῳ καθὰ ποιεῖ ἕκαστος does not reproduce Ps 61:13 / Prov 24:12 ἀποδώσεις ἑκάστῳ κατὰ τὰ ἔργα αὐτοῦ; 34:11b is asterisked in Rahlfs. [T]
**Hays criteria:**

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Possible | Not derivable |
| Volume | Strong (33:14; 40:5) / Weak (34:11) | Rare idiom with "speak", same order; 34:11 is a general formula |
| Recurrence | Strong | Used twice in Job, by two speakers |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | God's speaking and the hearer's response (Ps 62: power and steadfast love) |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Not testable |
| History of interpretation | Possible | Greek keeps the once/twice form in all three |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Could be a shared idiom of speech rather than a quotation |

**Verdict.** Confirmed with nuance for 33:14 and 40:5 (rare idiom, exclusive with "speak"; correct the "only" — the bare triple also hits Num 17:21 and Dan 11:27, which are not the idiom). Needs reframing for 34:11: retribution "according to his work" has several equal rivals and cannot be pinned to Ps 62:13.
**Final rating:** 33:14 / 40:5 ~ Ps 62:12 **moderate–high**; 34:11 ~ Ps 62:13 **low–moderate**.
**A preacher may safely say:** "God speaks once, even twice" (Elihu) and "once I have spoken … twice" (Job) use a turn of phrase found elsewhere with speaking only in Psalm 62:11; Elihu's "He repays a man according to his work" is a common biblical principle, not a quotation of one text.

#### A9: Ps 49:8–9 [Eng 49:7–8] at Job 33:24
**Claim as proposed:** כֹּפֶר ("ransom") + פדה ("redeem") with death/the pit. Ps 49 says no man can redeem a brother or give God a ransom; Elihu's angel says, "Deliver him from going down to the pit; I have found a ransom". The Greek Job 33:23–24 is said to lack the mediator and the ransom.
**Proposed rating:** moderate.
**Evidence (in words):**
- The verb at Job 33:24 is **not** פדה. The text reads פְּדָעֵהוּ, which the WLC lemma index files under a separate root found only here (one verse in the Hebrew Bible). The BHS apparatus marks it "uncertain; perhaps read with two manuscripts פרעהו" ("let him go"). The common "redeem him" rests on an emendation or on taking it as a by-form. פדה itself does occur four verses later, at Job 33:28 (פָּדָה נפשי מֵעֲבֹר בַּשָּׁחַת, 'He has redeemed my soul from going down to the pit', with a ketiv). [T]
- Ransom (כֹּפֶר, the ransom entry, 13 verses) + pit (שַׁחַת, 23 verses) within two verses: Ps 49:8 (with 49:10 "he will not see the pit") and Job 33:24 only (WLC). Ransom + redeem + pit within four verses: Ps 49:8 and Job 33:24 (with 33:28) only. Ransom + redeem in one verse: Ps 49:8 only. [T]
- Redeem + pit within one verse: Job 33:28 only. [T]
- Ps 49 itself turns at 49:16: "But God will redeem my soul from the hand of Sheol" (אַךְ־אֱלֹהִים יִפְדֶּה נַפְשִׁי מִיַּד־שְׁאוֹל). Elihu's scene matches the Psalm's turn rather than inverting the whole psalm: man cannot pay (49:8), God redeems (49:16; Job 33:24, 28). [T][I]
**Rivals:** Exod 21:30 has ransom + "redemption-price of his life" (פִּדְיוֹן) — with Ps 49:8–9 the only two passages pairing ransom with that noun — but no pit; it is legal, not a rescue from death. Exod 30:12; Prov 13:8; Isa 43:3 have ransom without redemption or the pit. Hos 13:14 and Ps 49:16 have redeem + Sheol + death (the only two within one verse), but no ransom. Ps 103:4 has the pit with גאל ("redeem", another verb). Num 35:31–32 have ransom + death in a legal sense. Ps 49 is the only text with all three elements. [T]
**Greek:** Confirmed that the Greek lacks the scene. Swete and Rahlfs Job 33:23–24: "if there be a thousand death-bringing angels, not one of them shall wound him … he will hold him back from falling into death; he will renew his body like plaster on a wall" — no interceding mediator, no ransom (no ἐξίλασμα or λύτρον). Ps 48:8–9 [Heb 49:8–9] has λυτροῦται … ἐξίλασμα … λυτρώσεως. Job 33:28 Greek "save my soul from going into corruption (διαφθοράν)" is close in sense to Ps 48:10 "he will not see corruption (καταφθοράν)", but the words differ. The contact does not survive in the Greek. [T]
**Hays criteria:**

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Possible | Not derivable |
| Volume | Possible | Ransom + pit exclusive within two verses; but the "redeem" verb at 33:24 is uncertain, and פדה is at 33:28 |
| Recurrence | Weak | Not shown elsewhere in Job (ransom at Job 36:18 is a bribe) |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | Ransom of a life from death is the subject of both |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Not testable |
| History of interpretation | Weak | The Greek does not see it |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Fits Ps 49:16 as well as 49:8; "inversion" overstated |

**Verdict.** Confirmed with nuance. The ransom + pit (+ redeem at 33:28) cluster is exclusive to Ps 49 and Job 33, but the claim must not cite פדה at 33:24, and the relation is better described as Elihu's angel supplying what Ps 49:8 says man cannot, in line with the Psalm's own turn at 49:16.
**Final rating:** **moderate**.
**A preacher may safely say:** Psalm 49 says no one can pay God a ransom for a life; Elihu imagines a messenger who says "I have found a ransom" — and the pit, the ransom and redemption come together nowhere else but these two passages.

### A-light tier

#### A10: Lam 4:8 at Job 30:30
**Claim as proposed:** עוֹר ("skin") + עֶצֶם ("bone") + שׁחר ("be black"), the "black" words filed under two entries; rivals Ps 102:4–6; Lam 5:10. **Proposed rating:** possible.
**Evidence:** The black-root is filed under **three** entries: the verb (Job 30:30 only), the noun שְׁחוֹר 'blackness' (Lam 4:8 only) and the adjective (6 verses). Skin + bone + any black-entry within one verse: Job 30:30 and Lam 4:8 only (WLC). Skin + bone alone: 7 verses (Mic 3:2, 3; Job 10:11; 19:20; 30:30; Lam 3:4; 4:8). [T]
**Rivals:** Job 30:30b ("my bones burn", חרר) matches Ps 102:4 instead: bone + burn in one verse only Ezek 24:10; Ps 102:4; Job 30:30. Lam 5:10 (skin hot as an oven) has no bone and no blackness. So 30:30a leans on Lam 4:8, 30:30b on Ps 102:4. [T]
**Greek:** Survives: σκοτ- ("darken") + δέρμα ("skin") + ὀστ- ("bone") in one verse only Job 30:30 and Lam 4:8 (Swete); 30:30 is Old Greek in Rahlfs. [T]
**Verdict.** Confirmed with nuance (a two-source line: Lam 4:8 for the blackened skin, Ps 102:4 for the burning bones; a stock picture of wasting is possible).
**Final rating:** **moderate**.
**A preacher may safely say:** Job's blackened skin over his bones is the picture Lamentations 4:8 paints of Jerusalem's starving nobles.

#### A11: Ps 18:10–12 [Eng 18:9–11] behind Job 22:13–14
**Claim as proposed:** עָב ("thick cloud") + סֵתֶר ("hiding place") in one verse only Ps 18:12 and Job 22:14 (2 Sam 22:12 has סֻכּוֹת); with עֲרָפֶל ("thick darkness"). **Proposed rating:** moderate.
**Evidence:** Cloud (32 verses) + hiding place (36) in one verse: Ps 18:12 and Job 22:14 only (WLC). With thick darkness (15 verses) within two verses: the same two only (Ps 18:10; Job 22:13). 2 Sam 22:12 lacks סֵתֶר (it reads וַיָּשֶׁת חֹשֶׁךְ סְבִיבֹתָיו סֻכּוֹת), so the contact is with the Psalter form of the hymn: confirmed. Both also have "heavens" (Ps 18:10; Job 22:14). [T]
**Rivals:** No other verse pairs cloud and hiding place; Exod 20:21; Deut 4:11; Ps 97:2 have thick darkness with other cloud-words. [T]
**Greek:** Partly kept: ἀποκρυφ- ("hiding") + νεφελ- ("cloud") in one verse: Job 22:14; Ps 17:12 [Heb 18:12]; 2 Sam 22:12 (Swete) — the Greek of 2 Samuel has the word its Hebrew lacks, so in Greek the contact does not discriminate Psalm from Samuel. Rahlfs asterisks Job 22:13b ("does He judge through the thick darkness?") and 22:14b; 22:14a (clouds his hiding place) is Old Greek. [T]
**Verdict.** Confirmed with nuance: Eliphaz puts in the wicked man's mouth the theophany-cloud of Ps 18 turned into a screen ("He cannot see").
**Final rating:** **moderate**.
**A preacher may safely say:** The wicked man's "thick clouds are a hiding place for Him, so that He cannot see" twists Psalm 18's picture of God hidden in cloud as He comes to save.

#### A12: Isa 14:11 at Job 25:6
**Claim as proposed:** רִמָּה ("maggot") + תּוֹלֵעָה ("worm") in one verse only Isa 14:11 and Job 25:6. **Proposed rating:** moderate.
**Evidence:** Maggot (7 verses) + worm in one verse, and within one verse: Isa 14:11 and Job 25:6 only (WLC) — confirmed. But maggot is a Job word: five of its seven verses are in Job (7:5; 17:14; 21:26; 24:20; 25:6), the others Exod 16:24 and Isa 14:11. The pair is the natural one for decay. [T]
**Rivals:** Exod 16:20, 24 (the manna breeds worms and maggots, four verses apart). Within Job 25 the frame is Ps 8 and Eliphaz 15:14–16 (A1), not Isa 14. Isa 14:11 is about the fallen king's bed in Sheol; Job 25:6 is about what man *is* before God. [T][I]
**Greek:** Partly: σκώληξ ("worm") in both; Job's σαπρία ("rottenness") against Isaiah's σῆψις ("decay") (Swete and Rahlfs). [T]
**Verdict.** Confirmed (words) / Needs reframing (as a source): a natural pair from a word Job uses often, with no further contact to Isa 14.
**Final rating:** **low–moderate**.
**A preacher may safely say:** "Maggot" and "worm" stand together only here and in Isaiah's taunt over the fallen king — a vivid shared image, not a proven borrowing.

#### A13: Lam 3:14 at Job 30:9 (and the Lam 3 field)
**Claim as proposed:** נְגִינָתָם ("their song") + הָיִיתִי ("I have become") only Lam 3:14 and Job 30:9; also שׂחק ("laugh", 30:1), עֳנִי ("affliction", Lam 3:1 ~ 30:16, 27), שׁוע ("cry for help", Lam 3:8 ~ 30:20, 28). **Proposed rating:** words high; field moderate–high.
**Evidence:** "Their song" as an ordered form: Job 30:9 and Lam 3:14 only (WLC, plain and skeletal); song-word + "be" in one verse: the same two only. As printed: Lam 3:14 הָיִיתִי שְּׂחֹק לְכָל־עַמִּי נְגִינָתָם כָּל־הַיּוֹם; Job 30:9 וְעַתָּה נְגִינָתָם הָיִיתִי. The field: song (נְגִינָה) + laugh (verb or noun) + affliction (עֳנִי) + cry for help (שׁוע) all in one chapter — Job 30 and Lam 3 only; even three of the four together occur in no other chapter (WLC). [T]
**Rivals:** Ps 69:13 ("I am the song of the drunkards", נְגִינוֹת) is the nearest rival for the mocking-song motif, but has none of the rest of the field. The verse-level rare-pair scan does not rank Lam 3 high against Job 30 (rank 128 at a pair ceiling of 5 verses, 170 at a pair ceiling of 10 verses), because the contacts are spread across verses rather than paired within them. [T]
**Greek:** Not kept: Job 30:9 κιθάρα ἐγώ εἰμι αὐτῶν ("I am their lyre"); Lam 3:14 ψαλμὸς αὐτῶν ("their psalm") (Swete; Rahlfs the same; 30:9 is Old Greek). [T]
**Verdict.** Confirmed. The phrase is exclusive, and the chapter-level field (four lemma-groups found together only in Job 30 and Lam 3) is the stronger evidence.
**Final rating:** words **high**; field **moderate–high**.
**A preacher may safely say:** "I have become their song" — Job's words in 30:9 are the words of the afflicted man of Lamentations 3:14, and the two chapters share his laughter-of-enemies, his affliction and his unanswered cry.

#### A14: Gen 4:10–12 at Job 31:38–39
**Claim as proposed:** אֲדָמָה ("ground") + כֹּחַ ("strength") within one verse of each other only Gen 4:11–12; Exod 32:11–12 (unrelated); Job 31:38–39. **Proposed rating:** moderate–high.
**Evidence:** Ground (211 verses) + strength (121) within one verse: Gen 4:11, 12; Exod 32:12; Job 31:38 (WLC) — confirmed; in one verse, Gen 4:12 only. The sharper datum: the ground's "its strength" (כֹּחָהּ) occurs as a form only at Gen 4:12 and Job 31:39 (WLC, plain; the skeletal pass floods with other suffixed forms). Both passages have the ground crying out — Gen 4:10 with צעק, Job 31:38 with its synonym זעק (different entries: ground + the one verb in Gen 4:10 only, ground + the other in Job 31:38 only). Job 31:39 also has the owner's life (נֶפֶשׁ) taken. [T]
**Rivals:** Exod 32:11–12 is the "strength" of God's hand, unrelated: confirmed. No other passage personifies the ground as crying against a wrongdoer. [T]
**Greek:** Survives and grows: τὴν ἰσχὺν αὐτῆς ("its strength") of the land at Gen 4:12 and Job 31:39 (also Ezek 32:16; 34:27, Swete); Greek Job 31:38 "the land groaned" (ἐστέναξεν) echoes Cain's "groaning (στένων) and trembling" in Gen 4:12. Old Greek in Rahlfs. [T]
**Verdict.** Confirmed: the ground that cries and withholds "its strength" — Cain's curse invoked by Job on himself.
**Final rating:** **moderate–high**.
**A preacher may safely say:** Job's last oath calls down Cain's curse on himself if he has wronged his land: the ground that cries out and the soil that withholds its strength.

#### A15: Deut 32:13–14 at Job 29:6
**Claim as proposed:** Butter + rock + oil within one verse of each other only Deut 32:13–14 and Job 29:6 (with Job 20:17). **Proposed rating:** high on words.
**Evidence:** Butter (חֶמְאָה, 9 verses) + rock (צוּר, 73) + oil (שֶׁמֶן, 176) within one verse: Deut 32:14 (with 32:13) and Job 29:6 only (WLC) — confirmed. Rock + oil in one verse: Deut 32:13 and Job 29:6 only; butter + rock, and butter + oil, within one verse: the same two only. As printed: Deut 32:13 … וְשֶׁמֶן מֵחַלְמִישׁ צוּר; 32:14 חֶמְאַת בָּקָר; Job 29:6 בִּרְחֹץ הֲלִיכַי בְּחֵמָה וְצוּר יָצוּק עִמָּדִי פַּלְגֵי־שָׁמֶן (Job spells butter defectively, חֵמָה). Job 20:17 (Zophar: "streams … of honey and butter") shares honey + butter with Deut 32:13–14 and Job's "streams" (two related entries פְּלַגּוֹת / פֶּלֶג). [T]
**Rivals:** Butter + honey is common land-of-plenty language (2 Sam 17:29; Isa 7:15, 22; Job 20:17); only Deut 32 has the oil from the rock. [T]
**Greek:** Partly: Greek Job 29:6 "my ways ran with butter, my mountains with milk" drops rock and oil but pairs βούτυρον + γάλα, as Deut 32:14 does (that pair also Gen 18:8; Judg 5:25; Isa 7:22; Prov 24:68 [Greek plus], Swete). Old Greek in Rahlfs. [T]
**Verdict.** Confirmed: Job describes his past blessing in the Song of Moses' picture of Israel's abundance.
**Final rating:** words **high**; allusion **moderate–high**.
**A preacher may safely say:** "The rock poured out streams of oil for me" — Job's memory of blessing borrows the Song of Moses' oil from the flinty rock (Deut 32:13).

#### A16: Ps 38:7, 12 [Eng 38:6, 11] at Job 30:28, 10
**Claim as proposed:** קֹדֵר ("mourning, dark") + הלך ("go") only Ps 35:14; 38:7; 43:2; Job 30:28; friends stand aloof (Ps 38:12 ~ Job 30:10). **Proposed rating:** moderate–high (phrase).
**Evidence:** "Go" is a split verb: searching both of its entries, mourning + go in one verse is Ps 35:14; 38:7; **42:10**; 43:2; Job 30:28 (and 1 Kgs 18:45, the sky growing dark — not the idiom) (WLC). The claim missed Ps 42:10 (לָמָּה־קֹדֵר אֵלֵךְ). The exact form "mourning I go about" (קֹדֵר הִלַּכְתִּי) as an ordered phrase: Ps 38:7 and Job 30:28 only (plain and skeletal): Ps 38:7 כָּל־הַיּוֹם קֹדֵר הִלָּכְתִּי; Job 30:28 קֹדֵר הִלַּכְתִּי בְּלֹא חַמָּה. [T] The "friends aloof" contact does not hold: Ps 38:12 has "my friends … stand far off" (noun מֵרָחֹק), Job 30:10 the verb "they keep far from me", but Job's subject is the despised young men of 30:1–8, not his friends; the two words are different entries. [T]
**Rivals:** Ps 42:10 / 43:2 and Ps 35:14 for the idiom. Chapter scan of Job 30 at a pair ceiling of 10 verses: Ps 35 ranks 15th, Ps 38 29th, Ps 42 36th (Job nulls 0, 0 and 2 of 32); at a pair ceiling of 5 verses Ps 38 falls to 68th. Ps 38 does not hold a high rank across settings. [T]
**Greek:** The verb of going is kept, the mourning word not: Job 30:28 στένων πεπόρευμαι ("groaning I have gone"); Ps 37:7 σκυθρωπάζων ἐπορευόμην ("going about sad-faced") (Swete; Old Greek in Rahlfs). [T]
**Verdict.** Confirmed with nuance for the phrase (exact form exclusive to Ps 38:7 and Job 30:28; the idiom is a lament stock phrase found in four psalms). Discard the 30:10 ~ 38:12 contact.
**Final rating:** phrase **moderate**; "friends aloof" **low**.
**A preacher may safely say:** "I go about mourning" is the language of the lament psalms — word for word Psalm 38:6 [Eng].

#### A17: Mic 1:8 at Job 30:29, 31
**Claim as proposed:** Jackals + ostriches at Isa 34:13; 43:20; Mic 1:8; Job 30:29; only Micah adds אֵבֶל ("mourning", Job 30:31). **Proposed rating:** moderate.
**Evidence:** Jackals (תַּנִּים) + ostriches (בְּנוֹת יַעֲנָה) in one verse: Isa 34:13; 43:20; Mic 1:8; Job 30:29 only (WLC) — confirmed. With mourning (אֵבֶל, 22 verses) within two verses: Mic 1:8 and Job 30:29 (with 30:31) only — confirmed. Mic 1:8 also has "I will go" (a form of the split verb הלך) beside Job 30:28's "I go about mourning". As printed: Mic 1:8 … אֶעֱשֶׂה מִסְפֵּד כַּתַּנִּים וְאֵבֶל כִּבְנוֹת יַעֲנָה; Job 30:29 אָח הָיִיתִי לְתַנִּים וְרֵעַ לִבְנוֹת יַעֲנָה. Micah's simile (mourning *like* jackals) is Job's identity ("I have become a brother to jackals"). [T]
**Rivals:** Isa 34:13 and 43:20 share the animal pair (desolation; wilderness praise) without lament. Mic 1 ranks 4th of 822 chapters against Job 30 at both a pair ceiling of 5 verses and a pair ceiling of 10 verses (Job null 0 of 32), but its rare pairs all come from this one verse, which Isa 34:13 shares — Isa 34 ranks as high. The chapter rank does not discriminate Micah from Isaiah; only the mourning word does. [T]
**Greek:** Partly: σειρήν ("siren", for ostrich/jackal) in Job 30:29 and Mic 1:8 (also Isa 13:21; 34:13; 43:20; Jer 27:39). Greek Job 30:31 has πάθος ("suffering"), not Micah's πένθος ("mourning"), so the discriminating word is lost. Old Greek in Rahlfs. [T]
**Verdict.** Confirmed with nuance: a lament image shared with Micah, distinguished from Isaiah only by the mourning word two verses on.
**Final rating:** **moderate**.
**A preacher may safely say:** Job mourns among jackals and ostriches as the prophet Micah does over Samaria and Judah.

#### A18: Exod 22:20–26 [Eng 22:21–27] at Job 30:11, 20; 31:16–21
**Claim as proposed:** "Do not afflict (ענה piel) widow or orphan … I will surely hear"; כְּסוּת ("covering", Exod 22:26 ketiv; Job 24:7; 31:19); with פְּלִלִים ("judges", Exod 21:22; Deut 32:31; Job 31:11). **Proposed rating:** moderate.
**Evidence:** Widow + orphan + covering within five verses: Exod 22:21–26; Job 24:3–7; Job 31:16–21 only (WLC) — a three-place exclusive that recurs twice in Job. Covering is in 8 verses, not 3 (Gen 20:16; Exod 21:10; 22:26; Deut 22:12; Isa 50:3; Job 24:7; 26:6; 31:19); the Exod 22:26 form is a ketiv (כסותה), found by lemma and not by surface search. The "judges" word (פְּלִלִים) is in only Exod 21:22; Deut 32:31; Job 31:11, with the related adjective at Job 31:28 alone — so Job's oath-chapter and the Book of the Covenant share a very rare legal term. Widow + orphan alone is common (30 verses). [T] The 30:11 / 30:20 legs are weak: afflict (piel) is in 76 verses, and Job 30:11 has God afflicting Job, with no widow or orphan; Job 30:20 uses שׁוע, not Exodus's צעק. [T]
**Rivals:** Deut 24:17 / 27:19 and Ps 94:6 have widow + orphan in legal or lament settings, but none has the covering. [T]
**Greek:** Not kept for the covering: Greek Job 31:19 "if I overlooked the naked perishing and did not clothe him" (ἠμφίασα), against Exod 22:26 [Eng 22:27] περιβόλαιον … ἱμάτιον (Swete; Rahlfs Exodus the same). Greek Job 30:11 ἐκάκωσέν με uses the same verb as Exod 22:21 [Eng 22:22] πᾶσαν χήραν καὶ ὀρφανὸν οὐ κακώσετε (Swete), but κακόω is the standard rendering of the afflict-verb, so this adds nothing. [T]
**Verdict.** Confirmed with nuance for 31:16–21 (and 24:3–7) ~ Exod 22:21–26 on widow + orphan + covering, with the rare "judges" term at 31:11 as support; Discard the 30:11 and 30:20 legs.
**Final rating:** 31:16–21 ~ Exod 22 **moderate**; 30:11, 20 **low**.
**A preacher may safely say:** Job's oath of innocence toward the widow, the orphan and the man without a covering keeps the Book of the Covenant's law of Exodus 22.

#### A19: Deut 28:30–33 at Job 31:8, 10
**Claim as proposed:** Wife + another (16 verses; Deut 28:30 the only covenant curse among them); sow and another eats. **Proposed rating:** moderate.
**Evidence:** Wife (אִשָּׁה) + another (אַחֵר) in one verse: 16 verses (WLC) — confirmed. Among them Deut 28:30 is the only Torah curse, but Jer 6:12 and 8:10 ("their wives to others") are prophetic judgements of the same kind, so "the only covenant curse" needs that qualification. Sow + another + eat in one verse: Job 31:8 alone (WLC); its nearest curse-forms are Lev 26:16 ("you will sow in vain, your enemies will eat it"), Deut 28:33 ("a people you do not know will eat"), Mic 6:15. Wife + another + sow + eat within eight verses: Job 31:8–12 only — Deut 28:30–33 lacks "sow" (the sowing curse is at 28:38). [T]
**Rivals:** Lev 26:16 matches 31:8 as closely as Deut 28:33 does; Jer 6:12 and 8:10 match 31:10 as closely as Deut 28:30 does. [T]
**Greek:** Not distinctive: Job 31:10 ἀρέσαι … ἡ γυνή μου ἑτέρῳ; Deut 28:30 ἀνὴρ ἕτερος ἕξει αὐτήν — ἕτερος shared; 31:8 ἄλλοι φάγοισαν. Old Greek in Rahlfs. [T]
**Verdict.** Confirmed with nuance: Job invokes the futility curses of the covenant (Lev 26 / Deut 28 family) on himself; the contact is with the curse-type, not with Deut 28:30–33 alone.
**Final rating:** **moderate** (curse-type); Deut 28 specifically **low–moderate**.
**A preacher may safely say:** Job's self-curses — "let me sow and another eat; let my wife grind for another" — are the curses Moses set before Israel for covenant breach.

#### A20: Prov 8:3 (9:3, 14) at Job 29:7
**Claim as proposed:** קֶרֶת ("town") only at Job 29:7 and in Proverbs (8:3; 9:3, 14; 11:11): Wisdom at the city gate. **Proposed rating:** moderate.
**Evidence:** Town (קֶרֶת): Job 29:7; Prov 8:3; 9:3; 9:14; 11:11 only (WLC) — confirmed. Town + gate (שַׁעַר) in one verse: Job 29:7 and Prov 8:3 only. Job 29:7 also has "the square" (רְחוֹב) and "my seat" (מוֹשָׁב); gate + square within one verse is common (11 verses, including Prov 1:21, where Wisdom cries in the squares and at the gates). Prov 9:14 has Folly's seat (a different word, כִּסֵּא) "on the heights of the town". [T]
**Rivals:** The gate-and-square setting is ordinary civic language (Gen 19:1; Amos 5:15; Neh 8). What is distinctive is the rare, poetic town-word, otherwise used only by Proverbs, and three of its four Proverbs uses are in the Wisdom/Folly speeches. [T]
**Greek:** Not kept: Job 29:7 ἐν πόλει … ἐν δὲ πλατείαις ("in the city … in the squares"); Prov 8:3 παρὰ πύλαις δυναστῶν (Swete; Rahlfs the same). [T]
**Verdict.** Confirmed (word) with nuance: one rare word shared with Proverbs' wisdom poems; it can show shared poetic vocabulary as easily as an allusion to Wisdom's station.
**Final rating:** **low–moderate** to **moderate**; give **moderate** only with the Prov 8:3 town + gate pairing stated.
**A preacher may safely say:** Job takes his seat at the gate of the "town" — a poetic word otherwise found only in Proverbs, where Wisdom calls at the gates.

#### A21: Ps 10:14–15 at Job 29:12, 17; 31:21–22
**Claim as proposed:** Orphan + helper + break within two verses only Ps 10:14–15; Job uses the cognate עֶזְרָה and the variant אֶזְרוֹעַ ("arm") at 31:21–22; Greek Ps 9:35 πτωχ- + βοηθ- + ὀρφαν- shared with Job 29:12 only. **Proposed rating:** moderate.
**Evidence:** Orphan (יָתוֹם) + helper (עוֹזֵר) in one verse: Ps 10:14 and Job 29:12 only (WLC) — a sharper datum than the claim gives. Orphan + helper + break within two verses: Ps 10:14 only; with the cognate "help" (עֶזְרָה) in place of the participle: Job 31:21 only — so 31:21–22 matches Ps 10:14–15 only by allowing the cognate and the variant arm-word (the variant is filed under its own entry; break + that arm-word, Job 31:22 alone; break + the usual arm-word, 8 verses including Ps 10:15 and **Job 38:15**). Orphan + break within two verses: Ps 10:14 and Job 31:21 only. [T]
**Rivals:** Job 29:17 ("I broke the fangs of the unjust") matches Ps 3:8 ("You break the teeth of the wicked") — break + teeth only Ps 3:8 and Job 29:17 — not Ps 10. Job 38:15 ("the uplifted arm is broken"), God's own reply, is an internal rival for the arm-breaking. [T]
**Greek:** Confirmed: πτωχ- + βοηθ- + ὀρφαν- in one verse: Ps 9:35 [Heb 10:14] and Job 29:12 only (Swete); βοηθ- + ὀρφαν- adds Job 31:21. Note that "poor" (πτωχός) at Ps 9:35 renders the obscure חֵלֶכָה, so the Greek contact is partly the translator's. [T]
**Verdict.** Confirmed with nuance for 29:12 ~ Ps 10:14 (orphan + helper exclusive; Greek agrees). Needs reframing for 29:17 (Ps 3:8 is the closer text) and for 31:21–22 (cognate and variant only; Job 38:15 rival).
**Final rating:** 29:12 ~ Ps 10:14 **moderate**; 29:17 ~ Ps 10:15 **low**; 31:21–22 ~ Ps 10:14–15 **low–moderate**.
**A preacher may safely say:** Job claims to have been for the orphan what Psalm 10:14 says God is — "the helper of the orphan".

#### A22: Zech 3:5 at Job 29:14
**Claim as proposed:** צָנִיף ("turban") only Isa 3:23; 62:3; Zech 3:5; Job 29:14 (the accused high priest re-clothed). **Proposed rating:** low–moderate.
**Evidence:** Turban: Isa 3:23; 62:3 (ketiv, a by-form); Zech 3:5 (twice); Job 29:14 — four verses (WLC) — confirmed. Turban + clothe (לבשׁ) in one verse: Zech 3:5 and Job 29:14 only. [T]
**Rivals:** Ps 132:9 ("let Your priests be clothed with righteousness"): clothe + righteousness (צֶדֶק) in one verse only Ps 132:9 and Job 29:14 — an equally exclusive and more directly priestly contact; Isa 59:17; 61:10 clothe with the cognate צְדָקָה. Isa 62:3 has the "royal turban". Job's robe (מְעִיל) and turban are both priestly-royal garments. [T]
**Greek:** Not kept: Job 29:14 has no turban (ἠμφιασάμην δὲ κρίμα ἴσα διπλοΐδι, 'I put on justice like a double cloak'); Zech 3:5 κίδαριν. [T]
**Verdict.** Uncertain — flag for research. The word contact is real but Ps 132:9 is as close; the "accused high priest" reading adds a narrative parallel the text does not mark.
**Final rating:** **low–moderate**.
**A preacher may safely say:** Job wore righteousness and justice like the robe and turban of a priest or king.

#### A23: Lam 2:12 at Job 30:16
**Claim as proposed:** Hitpael שׁפך ("pour out") + נֶפֶשׁ ("soul"); the hitpael only Lam 2:12; 4:1; Job 30:16. **Proposed rating:** moderate.
**Evidence:** The hitpael of "pour out" (by morphology): Job 30:16; Lam 2:12; Lam 4:1 only (WLC) — confirmed. Hitpael + soul: Job 30:16 and Lam 2:12 only (Lam 4:1 is the holy stones). Pour out (any stem) + soul in one verse: 7 verses (1 Sam 1:15; Ezek 17:17; 22:27; Ps 42:5; Job 30:16; Lam 2:12, 19). [T]
**Rivals:** Ps 42:5 is closer in wording: "pour out" + "upon me" (עָלַי) + "my soul" (נַפְשִׁי) in one verse: Ps 42:5 and Job 30:16 only (WLC). As printed: Ps 42:5 וְאֶשְׁפְּכָה עָלַי נַפְשִׁי; Job 30:16 עָלַי תִּשְׁתַּפֵּךְ נַפְשִׁי. Lam 2:12 shares the rare stem; Ps 42:5 shares the phrase. Lam 2:19 ("pour out your heart like water") is nearby. [T]

**Final rating:** Lam 2:12 **low–moderate**; the pair (Lam 2:12 / Ps 42:5) as lament diction **moderate**.
**A preacher may safely say:** "My soul is poured out within me" is the lament language of Psalm 42 and Lamentations 2.

#### A24: Ps 104:29 at Job 34:14–15
**Claim as proposed:** רוּחַ ("spirit") + אסף ("gather") + גוע ("expire") + עָפָר ("dust") + שׁוב ("return") together only in Ps 104:29 and Job 34:14–15; rivals Gen 3:19; Eccl 12:7; Ps 146:4; Ps 90:3. **Proposed rating:** moderate–high.

As printed: Ps 104:29 תֹּסֵף רוּחָם יִגְוָעוּן וְאֶל־עֲפָרָם יְשׁוּבוּן; Job 34:14–15 רוּחוֹ וְנִשְׁמָתוֹ אֵלָיו יֶאֱסֹף׃ יִגְוַע כָּל־בָּשָׂר יָחַד וְאָדָם עַל־עָפָר יָשׁוּב — same sequence: gather spirit → expire → return to dust. ## Part B: Fields, Design and Measured Claims (auditor B)

Blind auditor B. Hebrew from the WLC reading text and lemma index; Greek from Swete and the Rahlfs export of Job (and of Isaiah, Psalms, Proverbs, Lamentations where cited). All counts are WLC unless stated. Hebrew versification throughout.

**How the baselines were built (applies to every claim below).** I did not reuse the earlier run's scanning settings, which I have not seen; I built my own and report several. A *pair* is two content words (words in no more than 1,500 verses of the Hebrew Bible) standing in one verse of the Job passage. Settings used:
- **Same-verse, ten-verse windows:** the pair must also stand in one verse of the comparison chapter; a chapter scores the most such pairs found in any run of ten consecutive verses. Run with a rarity ceiling of 150, 400 and 800 verses per word.
- **Chapter density:** the same pairs, counted over the whole comparison chapter and divided by its verses (chapters of ten verses or more), at ceilings of 150 and 400 verses per word; and a version where the pair itself (not each word) occurs in no more than five verses of the Hebrew Bible.
- **Loose windows:** both words of a Job pair anywhere within a ten-verse window of the comparison chapter.
- **Single shared words:** shared words under a ceiling (150 or 400 verses), each weighted by its rarity, best window the length of the proposed source.
- **Job null:** the same measure run from every other non-overlapping block of Job's poetry (3:1–42:6) of the same length; I report how many of those blocks rank the proposed source as high as the claimed passage does.
Ranks are among the 887 non-Job chapters of the Hebrew Bible (822 for the density settings, which need ten verses). Where many chapters tie, the tie is reported.

Ranked 1st of 887 (tied with Isa 59) at a 150-verse ceiling in ten-verse windows, 2nd at 800, 9th/12th at 400; 2 of 19 Job nulls as high.
**Proposed rating:** words moderate–high; use moderate.
**Evidence (in words):**
- [T] ענג ("delight") + אָז ("then") in one verse: Isa 58:14; Job 22:26 only (WLC). Confirmed. ענג is in 10 verses; every Isa/Job/Psalm instance is hithpael ("take delight"). אָז is in 133 verses.
- [T] But the closer *form* "delight oneself upon (עַל) the divine name" is wider: Isa 58:14 תִּתְעַנַּג עַל־יְהוָה; Ps 37:4 וְהִתְעַנַּג עַל־יְהוָה; Job 22:26 עַל־שַׁדַּי תִּתְעַנָּג; and Job 27:10 עַל־שַׁדַּי יִתְעַנָּג — Job himself uses Eliphaz's phrase. Ps 37:4 is an equal rival for everything but אָז.
- [T] Hungry + naked: רָעֵב ("hungry", 20 verses) + עָרוֹם ("naked", 15 verses) in one verse: Isa 58:7; Job 24:10 only. **True only for that spelling.** With the other "naked" noun עֵירֹם, hungry + naked also stand together at Ezek 18:7 and 18:16. And Job 24:10 is Job's speech, not Eliphaz's; within Eliphaz's speech the pair falls in adjacent verses, 22:6 (עֲרוּמִּים) and 22:7 (רָעֵב). Hungry + naked within one verse either side: Isa 58:7; Job 22:7; Job 24:10.
- [T] Bread + hungry (לֶחֶם + רָעֵב) in one verse: 1 Sam 2:5; Isa 58:7; Ezek 18:7; 18:16; Ps 146:7; Job 22:7; Prov 25:21 (7 verses).
- [T] Light (אוֹר, 110 verses) 22:28 ~ 58:8, 10; darkness (חֹשֶׁךְ, 77) 22:11 ~ 58:10; cover (כסה, 149) 22:11 ~ 58:7 (different sense: water covering you / covering the naked). These fall in different verses on each side, not as matched pairs.
- [T] **"Call and answer" is not in Job 22.** Neither קרא ("call") nor ענה ("answer") occurs in 22:5–30; 22:27 has עתר ("pray") + שׁמע ("hear"). The contact is thematic only.
- [T] "Build": בנה 22:23 ~ 58:12 — a common verb (344 verses); בנה + שׁוב ("return/restore") in one verse is in 22 verses.
- [T] Only two same-verse pairs link Job 22:5–30 to Isa 58 at any ceiling: delight + then (22:26 ~ 58:14) and bread + hungry (22:7 ~ 58:7).
**Rivals:** Isa 59 has the larger single-word overlap (1st of 887 at 150 and 2nd at 400 in the single-word measure). Ps 37:4 rivals 22:26.

Hungry survives (πεινάω, Job 22:7; 24:10; Isa 58:7) and naked (γυμνός, Job 22:6; 24:10; Isa 58:7), but bread does not (ψωμός in Job, ἄρτος in Isaiah). "Build" does not survive: Job 22:23 has ταπεινώσῃς σεαυτόν ("humble yourself").

#### B2: Isa 58 across Job 29–31 (with B1)
**Claim as proposed:** Job's oath of clearance answers Eliphaz in Isa 58's terms: the works of 58:7 (~ 31:17, 19–20, 32); שׁוע ("cry for help") + ענה ("answer") in one verse at Isa 58:9; Jonah 2:3; Ps 18:42; Job 19:7; 30:20; אוֹר ("light") + אֲפֵלָה ("gloom") only Isa 58:10; 59:9 against Job 30:26.
**Proposed rating:** moderate (synthetic); needs a baseline against other prophetic social-justice chapters.
**Evidence (in words):**
- [T] שׁוע ("cry for help", verb, 21 verses) + ענה ("answer") in one verse: Isa 58:9; Jonah 2:3; Ps 18:42; Job 19:7; Job 30:20 — five verses, confirmed. But the Job verses are *negated* ("I cry to you and you do not answer me", 30:20; "I cry for help, and there is no justice", 19:7 with "I am not answered"), and so is Ps 18:42 ("to the LORD, and he did not answer them"). Isa 58:9 and Jonah 2:3 are positive. Ps 18:42 is as close a frame for 30:20 as Isa 58:9.
- [T] Light + gloom: אוֹר + אֲפֵלָה in one verse: Isa 58:10; 59:9 only — confirmed. Job 30:26 uses the related noun אֹפֶל, and אוֹר + אֹפֶל in one verse is Job 30:26 only; Amos 5:20 has אוֹר + the adjective אָפֵל. Counting the whole word-family, light + gloom stands in four verses: Isa 58:10; 59:9; Amos 5:20; Job 30:26. Isa 59:9 ("we hope for light, and behold darkness; for brightness, but we walk in gloom") is the closer frame for 30:26 ("I waited for light, and gloom came"): both have *hope/wait* + light + gloom, which Isa 58:10 lacks.
- [T] The works are thematic, not lexical. Job 31:17 has פַּת ("morsel"), not Isa 58:7's לֶחֶם ("bread"); 31:19 has לְבוּשׁ ("clothing") and כְּסוּת ("covering"), not עָרוֹם ("naked") or the verb כסה ("cover"); 31:32 has the sojourner (גֵּר) lodging, not Isa 58:7's מְרוּדִים ("homeless", 3 verses: Isa 58:7; Lam 1:7; 3:19 — absent from Job). Job's own "naked" is at 31:19 only in the Greek (below).
- [T] Single-word overlap is real but includes many different-sense matches: פֶּרֶץ ("breach", 18 verses) 30:14 ~ 58:12; אֵפֶר ("ashes") 30:19 ~ 58:5; נְתִיבָה ("path") 30:13 ~ 58:12; צֶדֶק ("righteousness") 29:14; 31:6 ~ 58:2, 8.
**Rivals:** Among the named social-justice chapters, Isa 59 runs closest (2nd–10th); Isa 10 and Amos 2 come next under one setting each; Ezek 18, Ezek 22, Jer 5, 7, 22, Mic 2–3 and Zech 7 never come near. The strongest non-prophetic rival is Lam 4 (1st or 2nd in three settings), then Ps 72 and Isa 50.
**Greek:** "Cry … answer" partly survives: Job 30:20 κέκραγα … οὐκ εἰσακούεις (Rahlfs; Swete ἀκούεις), Isa 58:9 βοήσῃ … εἰσακούσεταί σου — "hear" is shared, "cry" is not. Light + gloom does not survive: Job 30:26 has no "light" in either Greek text. One contact is *stronger* in Greek: Job 31:19 ὑπερεῖδον γυμνόν ("I overlooked the naked") ~ Isa 58:7 ἐὰν ἴδῃς γυμνόν … οὐχ ὑπερόψῃ ("if you see the naked … you shall not overlook"), Swete and Rahlfs alike. The second half of 30:20 is asterisked in Rahlfs (Old Greek lacked it).

|---|---|---|---|
| Same-verse pairs, ceiling 150, ten-verse windows | 21st (one pair; large tie) | Isa 59, 2nd | 21 of 31 as high — fails |
| Same-verse pairs, ceiling 400, ten-verse windows | 3rd (tied with 4) | Isa 59, 3rd (tied) | 4 of 31 |
| Chapter density, ceiling 400 | 3rd | Isa 59, 10th | 8 of 31 |
| Chapter density, pair in ≤ 5 verses | 8th | Amos 2, 13th | 9 of 31 |
| Single shared words, ceiling 150 | **1st** | Isa 1, 10th | 3 of 31 |

Against the comparison set asked for, Isa 58 outranks every one of the 14 prophetic chapters in five of six settings (Isa 59 ahead at the 150 ceiling only). On shared vocabulary it is the single closest chapter in the Hebrew Bible to Job 29–31, and only 3 of 31 other Job blocks rank it as high. On paired contacts it holds 3rd–8th at three settings but fails at the strictest.
**Verdict.** Confirmed with nuance. Isa 58 does stand out against Job 29–31, at two or more settings and above about 90 % of Job nulls on shared vocabulary; but much of that overlap is word-sharing without matched sense, the works are thematic (Job uses other words for bread, naked and homeless), the cry/answer frame is shared with Ps 18:42 and the light/gloom frame is closer to Isa 59:9. The "answer to Eliphaz" reading is an inference [I] the vocabulary permits but does not compel.

#### B3: Ps 72 as a "royal portrait" behind Job 29:12–25
**Claim as proposed:** Beyond the given contact at 29:12, Ps 72 shapes the whole of 29:12–25: righteousness + justice (72:2 ~ 29:14); crushing the oppressor (72:4 ~ 29:17); rain (72:6 ~ 29:23); "as a king among the troops" (29:25); and the colophon of 72:20 ~ 31:40c (form only).
**Proposed rating:** royal portrait moderate; colophon low–moderate.
**Evidence (in words):**
- [T] Given and re-checked: עָנִי ("afflicted") + שׁוע ("cry for help") + עזר ("help") in one verse: Ps 72:12; Job 29:12 only. (עָנִי + שׁוע alone adds Ps 22:25.) Both verses end with the same clause shape: וְאֵין־עֹזֵר לוֹ / וְלֹא־עֹזֵר לוֹ ("and he has no helper").
- [T] צֶדֶק ("righteousness") + מִשְׁפָּט ("justice") in one verse: 30 verses, including Job 8:3; 29:14; 35:2 — a stock pair, not evidence. Job 29:14's distinctive element is the *clothing* verb (לבשׁ): righteousness + justice + clothe in one verse is Job 29:14 only, so Ps 72:2 lacks what makes 29:14 distinctive.
- [T] 72:4 ~ 29:17 shares no word: Ps 72:4 has דכא ("crush") + עשׁק ("oppressor"); Job 29:17 has שׁבר ("break") + מְתַלְּעוֹת ("jaws") + עַוָּל ("unjust"). Thematic only.
- [T] מָטָר ("rain") is in 36 verses, six of them in Job (5:10; 28:26; 29:23; 36:27; 37:6; 38:28). Weak.
- [T] מֶלֶךְ ("king") at 29:25 is among the commonest nouns; no lexical tie to Ps 72.
- [T] Colophon: Ps 72:20 כָּלּוּ תְפִלּוֹת דָּוִד ("the prayers of David are ended"); Job 31:40c תַּמּוּ דִּבְרֵי אִיּוֹב ("the words of Job are ended"). Different verbs (כלה / תמם). The nearest formal rival is Jer 51:64 עַד־הֵנָּה דִּבְרֵי יִרְמְיָהוּ ("thus far the words of Jeremiah"), which shares "the words of" + name, as Ps 72:20 does not.
**Rivals:** Isa 61 is 2nd behind Ps 72 under three pair settings and ahead of it on chapter density; Ps 37 leads every single-word setting (see B4). None of the named royal psalms (2, 18, 20, 21, 45, 89, 101, 110, 132, 144) or Prov 31, Isa 11, Jer 22 comes close: the best is Ps 132 (3rd–26th, on the "clothe" pair — see B4); Prov 31 is 13th on single words only.

"From the hand of the mighty" is in neither Hebrew verse; in Swete the phrase ἐκ χειρὸς δυνάστου occurs only at Ps 71:12; Job 5:15; Job 29:12. Rain survives as ὑετός (Job 29:23; Ps 71:6). The colophon does not converge: Ps 71:20 Ἐξέλιπον οἱ ὕμνοι; Job 31:40 Καὶ ἐπαύσατο Ιωβ ῥήμασιν. 29:25b–c is asterisked in Rahlfs.

The "royal portrait" across 29:12–25 is not supported by the vocabulary: the further proposed links are a stock pair, a shared theme without shared words, a common noun and a different-verb colophon. Isa 61 is a stronger partner for the rest of the passage.
**Final rating:** the 29:12 contact moderate–high; royal portrait low–moderate; colophon low.
**A preacher may safely say:** "In 29:12 Job describes himself in the words Psalm 72 uses of the ideal king — rescuing the poor who cries, the one who has no helper."

- [T] לבשׁ + מְעִיל in one verse: Exod 29:5; Lev 8:7; 2 Sam 13:18; Isa 59:17; 61:10; Ezek 26:16; Ps 109:29; Job 29:14 — eight verses, confirmed. Clothe + robe + righteousness (צְדָקָה) in one verse: Isa 59:17; 61:10 only — Job 29:14 has the other noun, צֶדֶק.
- [T] לבשׁ + צֶדֶק in one verse: Ps 132:9; Job 29:14 only — confirmed. (Clothe + justice, מִשְׁפָּט: Ezek 7:27; Job 29:14.)

Weak.

Comfort survives (Isa 61:2 παρακαλέσαι πάντας τοὺς πενθοῦντας; Job 29:25c παθεινοὺς παρακαλῶν) but only in the asterisked, later-supplied text of Job, and "mourners" does not (πενθοῦντας / παθεινούς). Clothe survives (ἐνέδυσεν, Isa 61:10; ἐνδεδύκειν, Job 29:14) but Isa 61:10's righteousness becomes εὐφροσύνης ("gladness"); the Greek pair clothe + righteousness belongs to Ps 131[132]:9 ἐνδύσονται δικαιοσύνην (Rahlfs; Swete ἐνεδύσαντο) ~ Job 29:14 δικαιοσύνην ἐνδεδύκειν. Ashes survive as σποδός (Isa 61:3; Job 30:19).

#### B5: Ps 102 as a field behind Job 30:16–31
**Claim as proposed:** Ps 102 is a field behind Job's lament: nine shared lemmas each in fewer than 400 verses, among them חרר ("burn") + עֶצֶם ("bone") at Ezek 24:10; Ps 102:4; Job 30:30; ashes, garment, pour out, מוֹעֵד ("appointed time"), answer; and Ps 102:11 "lift up / cast down" ~ 30:22. Rivals said to be Lam 4:8 and 4:3.
**Proposed rating:** moderate; needs a baseline against all chapters and against other lament psalms.
**Evidence (in words):**
- [T] חרר ("burn") + עֶצֶם ("bone") in one verse: Ezek 24:10; Ps 102:4 (Hebrew numbering; English 102:3); Job 30:30 — confirmed, three verses. חרר is in 11 verses.
- [T] Nine shared content words under 400 verses — confirmed: חרר (11 verses), אֵפֶר "ashes" (22), לְבוּשׁ "garment" (33), עָפָר "dust" (103), עֶצֶם "bone" (108), שׁפך "pour out" (111), כֹּחַ "strength" (121), מוֹעֵד "appointed time" (213), ענה "answer" (316). - [T] Ps 102:11 lift up (נשׂא) + cast down (שׁלך) in one verse: seven verses; Job 30:22 has "lift up" (נשׂא) with "make ride" (רכב) and "toss about" (מוג), not "cast down". Thematic only.
- [T] Rivals re-checked. Lam 4:8: skin (עוֹר) + bone, with "blacker than soot" (שְׁחוֹר) ~ Job 30:30 "my skin turns black (שׁחר) … my bone burns". Mic 1:8 ("I will make lament like the jackals, mourning like the ostriches") is the closest frame, being the only one that is *lament*.
**Rivals:** Ezek 24 (shares the burn + bone pair at 24:10, and leads every single-word setting); Mic 1 (1st at a 800 ceiling and on pair-rarity density, through jackals + ostriches); Lam 3 and Lam 4; Ps 22 (2nd on single words, ahead of Ps 102).
**Greek:** The burn + bone contact does not survive in Job: Job 30:30 τὰ δὲ ὀστᾶ μου ἀπὸ καύματος ("my bones from heat") against Ps 101[102]:4 τὰ ὀστᾶ μου ὡσεὶ φρύγιον συνεφρύγησαν ("my bones were parched like kindling"), Swete and Rahlfs; only "bones" is shared. The Greek of 30:30 is closer to Lam 4:8: ἐσκότωται … δέρμα … ὀστᾶ ~ ἐσκότασεν … δέρμα … ὀστέα ("darkened … skin … bones"). The second half of 30:22 is asterisked in Rahlfs.
**Tests (Job 30:16–31, 16 verses; Job null = 62 non-overlapping 16-verse blocks):**

**Final rating:** moderate–high for "the Old Greek lacked about a quarter of Elihu, more than any other speaker"; low–moderate for "more than any other part of Job".
**A preacher may safely say:** "The earliest Greek translation of Job lacked roughly a quarter of Elihu's speeches — more than for any other speaker, though Job 26–28 was shortened as heavily."

**The contacts mostly stand; the patterns mostly fall.** Single verbal contacts held at or near their proposed ratings: Ps 78:64 at 27:15, Lam 2:15 at 27:23, Ps 72:12 at 29:12, Ps 32:5 at 31:33, Ps 8 at 25:5–6, Ps 104:29, Ps 56:14 and the Elihu–Eliphaz–Bildad reuse. ## Critical Assessment

**What the audit does well.**

- **Two blind auditors**, run one after another, tested 42 rulings, and every decisive result was reproduced in the main session.
- **The design claims met real baselines.** For the first time in the Job series, the speaker-level claims (B7, B8, B10, B11) were normalised by verses of speech, and two of them fell.
- **The skeletal pass earned its place.** At "these three men" it found Ezek 14:18, which a plain search misses.

**Its gaps.**

1. **History of interpretation was not checked.** No commentary was opened, so every Hays row for that criterion reads "Not checked". A Logos round on the claims rated *moderate–high* or above, before they enter the overview, would close this. They are Ps 72:12, Ps 78:64, Lam 2:15, Ps 32:5, Ps 104:29, Ps 56:14 and Ps 62:12.
2. **The light tier is light.** Twenty-two contacts had one rarity figure and one rival each. A verdict of "Confirmed" there means that the contact is real and no closer rival was found in one search, not that the link is settled.
3. **The baselines are the auditor's own.** Auditor B built new measures because it was blind to the earlier runs' settings. That is the right discipline, but it means "did not reproduce" (B1's rank) may partly reflect different settings. The verdict does not rest on the rank, because the field is moderate on its words alone.
4. **The Rahlfs signs are read from an export** in which some asterisks never close (27:21–23). The Old Greek figures are approximate until checked in the printed edition or the Göttingen *Iob*, which Patrick does not own.
5. **Small numbers.** Speaker comparisons rest on tens of contacts, so a single link moves a rank. B8 and B11 are reported as tendencies, and are rated accordingly.
6. **Echo chamber.** Both auditors worked only from the corpus and the claims as stated. Neither could test whether the claims were posed in a way that tilts the result. The proposing runs chose which contacts to name; contacts never proposed could not be audited.
