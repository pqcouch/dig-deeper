### Eliphaz (4:1–5:27)

> **4:1** וַ֭יַּעַן אֱלִיפַ֥ז הַֽתֵּימָנִ֗י וַיֹּאמַֽר׃
>
> Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered,

> **4:2** הֲנִסָּ֬ה דָבָ֣ר אֵלֶ֣יךָ תִּלְאֶ֑ה וַעְצֹ֥ר בְּ֝מִלִּ֗ין מִ֣י יוּכָֽל׃
>
> “If one ventures a word with you, will you become impatient? But who can refrain from speaking?

> **4:3** הִ֭נֵּה יִסַּ֣רְתָּ רַבִּ֑ים וְיָדַ֖יִם רָפֹ֣ות תְּחַזֵּֽק׃
>
> “Behold you have admonished many, And you have strengthened weak hands.

> **4:4** כֹּ֖ושֵׁל יְקִימ֣וּן מִלֶּ֑יךָ וּבִרְכַּ֖יִם כֹּרְעֹ֣ות תְּאַמֵּֽץ׃
>
> “Your words have helped the tottering to stand, And you have strengthened feeble knees.

> **4:5** כִּ֤י עַתָּ֨ה׀ תָּבֹ֣וא אֵלֶ֣יךָ וַתֵּ֑לֶא תִּגַּ֥ע עָ֝דֶ֗יךָ וַתִּבָּהֵֽל׃
>
> “But now it has come to you, and you are impatient; It touches you, and you are dismayed.

> **4:6** הֲלֹ֣א יִ֭רְאָתְךָ כִּסְלָתֶ֑ךָ תִּ֝קְוָתְךָ֗ᵃ וְתֹ֣םᵇ דְּרָכֶֽיךָ׃
>
> “Is not your fear of God your confidence, And the integrity of your ways your hope?

> **4:7** זְכָר־נָ֗א מִ֤י ה֣וּא נָקִ֣י אָבָ֑ד וְ֝אֵיפֹ֗ה יְשָׁרִ֥ים נִכְחָֽדוּ׃
>
> “Remember now, who ever perished being innocent? Or where were the upright destroyed?

> **4:8** כַּאֲשֶׁ֣ר רָ֭אִיתִי חֹ֣רְשֵׁי אָ֑וֶן וְזֹרְעֵ֖י עָמָ֣ל יִקְצְרֻֽהוּ׃
>
> “According to what I have seen, those who plow iniquity And those who sow trouble harvest it.

> **4:9** מִנִּשְׁמַ֣ת אֱלֹ֣והַᵃ יֹאבֵ֑דוּ וּמֵר֖וּחַ אַפֹּ֣ו יִכְלֽוּ׃
>
> “By the breath of God they perish, And by the blast of His anger they come to an end.

> **4:10** שַׁאֲגַ֣ת אַ֭רְיֵה וְקֹ֣ול שָׁ֑חַל וְשִׁנֵּ֖י כְפִירִ֣ים נִתָּֽעוּ׃
>
> “The roaring of the lion and the voice of the fierce lion, And the teeth of the young lions are broken.

> **4:11** לַ֭יִשׁ אֹבֵ֣ד מִבְּלִי־טָ֑רֶף וּבְנֵ֥י לָ֝בִ֗יא יִתְפָּרָֽדוּ׃
>
> “The lion perishes for lack of prey, And the whelps of the lioness are scattered.

> **4:12** וְ֭אֵלַי דָּבָ֣ר יְגֻנָּ֑ב וַתִּקַּ֥ח אָ֝זְנִ֗י שֵׁ֣מֶץ מֶֽנְהֽוּ׃
>
> “Now a word was brought to me stealthily, And my ear received a whisper of it.

> **4:13** בִּ֭שְׂעִפִּים מֵחֶזְיֹנֹ֣ות לָ֑יְלָה בִּנְפֹ֥ל תַּ֝רְדֵּמָ֗ה עַל־אֲנָשִֽׁים׃
>
> “Amid disquieting thoughts from the visions of the night, When deep sleep falls on men,

> **4:14** פַּ֣חַד קְ֭רָאַנִי וּרְעָדָ֑ה וְרֹ֖בᵃ עַצְמֹותַ֣י הִפְחִֽיד׃
>
> Dread came upon me, and trembling, And made all my bones shake.

> **4:15** וְ֭רוּחַ עַל־פָּנַ֣י יַחֲלֹ֑ף תְּ֝סַמֵּ֗ר שַֽׂעֲרַ֥ת בְּשָׂרִֽי׃
>
> “Then a spirit passed by my face; The hair of my flesh bristled up.

> **4:16** יַעֲמֹ֤ד׀ וְֽלֹא־אַכִּ֬יר מַרְאֵ֗הוּ תְּ֭מוּנָה לְנֶ֣גֶד עֵינָ֑י דְּמָמָ֖ה וָקֹ֣ול אֶשְׁמָֽע׃
>
> “It stood still, but I could not discern its appearance; A form was before my eyes; There was silence, then I heard a voice:

> **4:17** הַֽ֭אֱנֹושׁ מֵאֱלֹ֣והַᵃ יִצְדָּ֑ק אִ֥ם מֵ֝עֹשֵׂ֗הוּ יִטְהַר־גָּֽבֶר׃
>
> ‘Can mankind be just before God? Can a man be pure before his Maker?

> **4:18** הֵ֣ן בַּ֭עֲבָדָיו לֹ֣א יַאֲמִ֑ין וּ֝בְמַלְאָכָ֗יו יָשִׂ֥ים תָּהֳלָֽהᵃ׃
>
> ‘He puts no trust even in His servants; And against His angels He charges error.

> **4:19** אַ֤ף׀ שֹׁכְנֵ֬י בָֽתֵּי־חֹ֗מֶר אֲשֶׁר־בֶּעָפָ֥ר יְסֹודָ֑ם יְ֝דַכְּא֗וּם לִפְנֵיᵃ־עָֽשׁ׃
>
> ‘How much more those who dwell in houses of clay, Whose foundation is in the dust, Who are crushed before the moth!

> **4:20** מִבֹּ֣קֶר לָעֶ֣רֶב יֻכַּ֑תּוּ מִבְּלִ֥י מֵ֝שִׂ֗יםᵃ לָנֶ֥צַח יֹאבֵֽדוּ׃
>
> ‘Between morning and evening they are broken in pieces; Unobserved, they perish forever.

> **4:21** הֲלֹא־נִסַּ֣ע יִתְרָ֣ם בָּ֑םᵃ יָ֝מ֗וּתוּ וְלֹ֣א בְחָכְמָֽה׃
>
> ‘Is not their tent-cord plucked up within them? They die, yet without wisdom.’

> **5:1** קְֽרָא־נָ֭א הֲיֵ֣שׁ עֹונֶ֑ךָּ וְאֶל־מִ֖י מִקְּדֹשִׁ֣ים תִּפְנֶֽה׃
>
> “Call now, is there anyone who will answer you? And to which of the holy ones will you turn?

> **5:2** כִּֽי־לֶֽ֭אֱוִיל יַהֲרָג־כָּ֑עַשׂᵃ וּ֝פֹתֶ֗ה תָּמִ֥ית קִנְאָֽה׃
>
> “For anger slays the foolish man, And jealousy kills the simple.

> **5:3** אֲֽנִי־רָ֭אִיתִי אֱוִ֣יל מַשְׁרִ֑ישׁ וָאֶקֹּ֖ובᵃ נָוֵ֣הוּ פִתְאֹֽם׃
>
> “I have seen the foolish taking root, And I cursed his abode immediately.

> **5:4** יִרְחֲק֣וּ בָנָ֣יו מִיֶּ֑שַׁע וְיִֽדַּכְּא֥וּ בַ֝שַּׁ֗עַר וְאֵ֣ין מַצִּֽיל׃
>
> “His sons are far from safety, They are even oppressed in the gate, And there is no deliverer.

> **5:5** אֲשֶׁ֤ר קְצִירֹ֨וᵃ׀ רָ֘עֵ֤ב יֹאכֵ֗ל ᵇוְאֶֽל־מִצִּנִּ֥ים יִקָּחֵ֑הוּᵇ וְשָׁאַ֖ףᶜ צַמִּ֣יםᵈ חֵילָֽםᵉ׃
>
> “His harvest the hungry devour And take it to a place of thorns, And the schemer is eager for their wealth.

> **5:6** כִּ֤י׀ לֹא־יֵצֵ֣א מֵעָפָ֣ר אָ֑וֶן וּ֝מֵאֲדָמָ֗ה לֹא־יִצְמַ֥ח עָמָֽל׃
>
> “For affliction does not come from the dust, Nor does trouble sprout from the ground,

> **5:7** כִּֽי־אָ֭דָם לְעָמָ֣ל יוּלָּ֑דᵃ וּבְנֵי־רֶ֝֗שֶׁף יַגְבִּ֥יהוּ עֽוּף׃
>
> For man is born for trouble, As sparks fly upward.

> **5:8** אוּלָ֗ם אֲ֭נִי אֶדְרֹ֣שׁ אֶל־אֵ֑ל וְאֶל־אֱ֝לֹהִ֗ים אָשִׂ֥ים דִּבְרָתִֽי׃
>
> “But as for me, I would seek God, And I would place my cause before God;

> **5:9** עֹשֶׂ֣ה גְ֭דֹלֹות וְאֵ֣ין חֵ֑קֶר נִ֝פְלָאֹ֗ות עַד־אֵ֥ין מִסְפָּֽר׃
>
> Who does great and unsearchable things, Wonders without number.

> **5:10** הַנֹּתֵ֣ן מָ֭טָר עַל־פְּנֵי־אָ֑רֶץ וְשֹׁ֥לֵֽחַ מַ֝יִםᵃ עַל־פְּנֵ֥י חוּצֹֽות׃
>
> “He gives rain on the earth And sends water on the fields,

> **5:11** לָשׂ֣וּם שְׁפָלִ֣ים לְמָרֹ֑ום וְ֝קֹדְרִ֗ים שָׂ֣גְבוּ יֶֽשַׁע׃
>
> So that He sets on high those who are lowly, And those who mourn are lifted to safety.

> **5:12** מֵ֭פֵר מַחְשְׁבֹ֣ות עֲרוּמִ֑ים וְֽלֹא־תַעֲשֶׂ֥ינָה יְ֝דֵיהֶ֗ם תּוּשִׁיָּֽה׃
>
> “He frustrates the plotting of the shrewd, So that their hands cannot attain success.

> **5:13** לֹכֵ֣ד חֲכָמִ֣ים בְּעָרְמָ֑ם וַעֲצַ֖ת נִפְתָּלִ֣ים נִמְהָֽרָה׃
>
> “He captures the wise by their own shrewdness, And the advice of the cunning is quickly thwarted.

> **5:14** יֹומָ֥ם יְפַגְּשׁוּ־חֹ֑שֶׁךְ וְ֝כַלַּ֗יְלָה יְֽמַשְׁשׁ֥וּ בַֽצָּהֳרָֽיִם׃
>
> “By day they meet with darkness, And grope at noon as in the night.

> **5:15** וַיֹּ֣שַׁע מֵ֭חֶרֶב מִפִּיהֶ֑םᵃ וּמִיַּ֖ד חָזָ֣ק אֶבְיֹֽון׃
>
> “But He saves from the sword of their mouth, And the poor from the hand of the mighty.

> **5:16** וַתְּהִ֣י לַדַּ֣ל תִּקְוָ֑ה וְ֝עֹלָ֗תָה קָ֣פְצָה פִּֽיהָ׃
>
> “So the helpless has hope, And unrighteousness must shut its mouth.

> **5:17** הִנֵּ֤ה אַשְׁרֵ֣י אֱ֭נֹושׁ יֹוכִחֶ֣נּֽוּ אֱלֹ֑והַּ וּמוּסַ֥ר שַׁ֝דַּ֗י אַל־תִּמְאָֽס׃
>
> “Behold, how happy is the man whom God reproves, So do not despise the discipline of the Almighty.

> **5:18** כִּ֤י ה֣וּא יַכְאִ֣יב וְיֶחְבָּ֑שׁ יִ֝מְחַ֗ץ וְיָדָ֥וᵃ תִּרְפֶּֽינָה׃
>
> “For He inflicts pain, and gives relief; He wounds, and His hands also heal.

> **5:19** בְּשֵׁ֣שׁ צָ֭רֹות יַצִּילֶ֑ךָּ וּבְשֶׁ֓בַע׀ לֹא־יִגַּ֖ע בְּךָ֣ רָֽע׃
>
> “From six troubles He will deliver you, Even in seven evil will not touch you.

> **5:20** בְּ֭רָעָב פָּֽדְךָ֣ מִמָּ֑וֶת וּ֝בְמִלְחָמָ֗ה מִ֣ידֵי חָֽרֶב׃
>
> “In famine He will redeem you from death, And in war from the power of the sword.

> **5:21** בְּשֹׁ֣וטᵃ לָ֭שֹׁון תֵּחָבֵ֑א וְֽלֹא־תִירָ֥א מִ֝שֹּׁ֗ד כִּ֣י יָבֹֽוא׃
>
> “You will be hidden from the scourge of the tongue, And you will not be afraid of violence when it comes.

> **5:22** לְשֹׁ֣ד וּלְכָפָ֣ן תִּשְׂחָ֑ק וּֽמֵחַיַּ֥ת הָ֝אָ֗רֶץ אַל־תִּירָֽא׃
>
> “You will laugh at violence and famine, And you will not be afraid of wild beasts.

> **5:23** כִּ֤י עִם־אַבְנֵ֣י הַשָּׂדֶ֣ה בְרִיתֶ֑ךָ וְחַיַּ֥ת הַ֝שָּׂדֶ֗ה הָשְׁלְמָה־לָֽךְ׃
>
> “For you will be in league with the stones of the field, And the beasts of the field will be at peace with you.

> **5:24** וְֽ֭יָדַעְתָּ כִּי־שָׁלֹ֣ום אָהֳלֶ֑ךָ וּֽפָקַדְתָּ֥ נָ֝וְךָᵃ וְלֹ֣א תֶחֱטָֽא׃
>
> “You will know that your tent is secure, For you will visit your abode and fear no loss.

> **5:25** וְֽ֭יָדַעְתָּ כִּי־רַ֣ב זַרְעֶ֑ךָ וְ֝צֶאֱצָאֶ֗יךָ כְּעֵ֣שֶׂב הָאָֽרֶץ׃
>
> “You will know also that your descendants will be many, And your offspring as the grass of the earth.

> **5:26** תָּבֹ֣וא בְכֶ֣לַח אֱלֵי־קָ֑בֶר כַּעֲלֹ֖ות גָּדִ֣ישׁ בְּעִתֹּֽו׃
>
> “You will come to the grave in full vigor, Like the stacking of grain in its season.

> **5:27** הִנֵּה־זֹ֭את חֲקַרְנ֥וּהָ כֶּֽן־הִ֑יא שְׁ֝מָעֶ֗נָּה וְאַתָּ֥ה דַֽע־לָֽךְ׃  פ
>
> “Behold this; we have investigated it, and so it is. Hear it, and know for yourself.”

### Job (6:1–7:21)

> **6:1** וַיַּ֥עַן אִיֹּ֗וב וַיֹּאמַֽר׃
>
> Then Job answered,

> **6:2** ל֗וּ שָׁקֹ֣ול יִשָּׁקֵ֣ל כַּעְשִׂ֑י וְ֝הַיָּתִ֗יᵃ בְּֽמֹאזְנַ֥יִם יִשְׂאוּ־יָֽחַד׃
>
> “Oh that my grief were actually weighed And laid in the balances together with my calamity!

> **6:3** כִּֽי־עַתָּ֗ה מֵחֹ֣ול יַמִּ֣ים יִכְבָּ֑ד עַל־כֵּ֝֗ן דְּבָרַ֥י לָֽעוּ׃
>
> “For then it would be heavier than the sand of the seas; Therefore my words have been rash.

> **6:4** כִּ֤י חִצֵּ֪י שַׁדַּ֡י עִמָּדִ֗י אֲשֶׁ֣ר חֲ֭מָתָם שֹׁתָ֣ה רוּחִ֑י בִּעוּתֵ֖י אֱלֹ֣והַּ יַֽעַרְכֽוּנִיᵃ׃
>
> “For the arrows of the Almighty are within me, Their poison my spirit drinks; The terrors of God are arrayed against me.

> **6:5** הֲיִֽנְהַק־פֶּ֥רֶא עֲלֵי־דֶ֑שֶׁא אִ֥ם יִגְעֶה־שֹּׁ֝֗ור עַל־בְּלִילֹֽו׃
>
> “Does the wild donkey bray over his grass, Or does the ox low over his fodder?

> **6:6** הֲיֵאָכֵ֣ל תָּ֭פֵל מִבְּלִי־מֶ֑לַח אִם־יֶשׁ־טַ֝֗עַם בְּרִ֣יר חַלָּמֽוּת׃
>
> “Can something tasteless be eaten without salt, Or is there any taste in the white of an egg?

> **6:7** מֵאֲנָ֣ה לִנְגֹּ֣ועַ נַפְשִׁ֑י ᵃהֵ֝֗מָּה כִּדְוֵ֥יᵃ לַחְמִֽי׃
>
> “My soul refuses to touch them; They are like loathsome food to me.

> **6:8** מִֽי־יִ֭תֵּן תָּבֹ֣וא שֶֽׁאֱלָתִ֑י וְ֝תִקְוָתִ֗י יִתֵּ֥ן אֱלֹֽוהַּ׃
>
> “Oh that my request might come to pass, And that God would grant my longing!

> **6:9** וְיֹאֵ֣ל אֱ֭לֹוהַּ וִֽידַכְּאֵ֑נִי יַתֵּ֥ר יָ֝דֹ֗ו וִֽיבַצְּעֵֽנִי׃
>
> “Would that God were willing to crush me, That He would loose His hand and cut me off!

> **6:10** ᵃוּ֥תְהִי עֹ֨ודᵃ׀ נֶ֘חָ֤מָתִ֗י וַאֲסַלְּדָ֣ה בְ֭חִילָה לֹ֣א יַחְמֹ֑ול כִּי־לֹ֥א כִ֝חַ֗דְתִּי אִמְרֵ֥י קָדֹֽושׁ׃
>
> “But it is still my consolation, And I rejoice in unsparing pain, That I have not denied the words of the Holy One.

> **6:11** מַה־כֹּחִ֥י כִֽי־אֲיַחֵ֑ל וּמַה־קִּ֝צִּ֗י כִּֽי־אַאֲרִ֥יךְ נַפְשִֽׁי׃
>
> “What is my strength, that I should wait? And what is my end, that I should endure?

> **6:12** אִם־כֹּ֣חַ אֲבָנִ֣ים כֹּחִ֑י אִֽם־בְּשָׂרִ֥י נָחֽוּשׁ׃
>
> “Is my strength the strength of stones, Or is my flesh bronze?

> **6:13** הַאִ֬םᵃ אֵ֣ין עֶזְרָתִ֣י בִ֑י וְ֝תֻשִׁיָּ֗ה נִדְּחָ֥ה מִמֶּֽנִּי׃
>
> “Is it that my help is not within me, And that deliverance is driven from me?

> **6:14** ᵃלַמָּ֣סᵃ ᵇ מֵרֵעֵ֣הוּ חָ֑סֶד וְיִרְאַ֖ת שַׁדַּ֣י יַעֲזֹֽוב׃
>
> “For the despairing man there should be kindness from his friend; So that he does not forsake the fear of the Almighty.

> **6:15** אַ֭חַי בָּגְד֣וּ כְמֹו־נָ֑חַל כַּאֲפִ֖יק נְחָלִ֣ים יַעֲבֹֽרוּ׃
>
> “My brothers have acted deceitfully like a wadi, Like the torrents of wadis which vanish,

> **6:16** הַקֹּדְרִ֥ים מִנִּי־קָ֑רַח עָ֝לֵ֗ימֹו יִתְעַלֶּם־שָֽׁלֶג׃
>
> Which are turbid because of ice And into which the snow melts.

> **6:17** בְּ֭עֵת יְזֹרְב֣וּ נִצְמָ֑תוּ בְּ֝חֻמֹּ֗וᵃ נִדְעֲכ֥וּ מִמְּקֹומָֽם׃
>
> “When they become waterless, they are silent, When it is hot, they vanish from their place.

> **6:18** יִ֭לָּ֣פְתוּ אָרְחֹ֣ות דַּרְכָּ֑ם יַעֲל֖וּ בַתֹּ֣הוּ וְיֹאבֵֽדוּ׃
>
> “The paths of their course wind along, They go up into nothing and perish.

> **6:19** הִ֭בִּיטוּ אָרְחֹ֣ותᵃ תֵּמָ֑א הֲלִיכֹ֥ת שְׁ֝בָ֗א קִוּוּ־לָֽמֹו׃
>
> “The caravans of Tema looked, The travelers of Sheba hoped for them.

> **6:20** בֹּ֥שׁוּ כִּֽי־בָטָ֑חᵃ בָּ֥אוּ עָ֝דֶ֗יהָ וַיֶּחְפָּֽרוּ׃
>
> “They were disappointed for they had trusted, They came there and were confounded.

> **6:21** כִּֽי־עַ֭תָּה הֱיִ֣יתֶם לֹ֑אᵃ תִּֽרְא֥וּ חֲ֝תַ֗ת וַתִּירָֽאוּ׃
>
> “Indeed, you have now become such, You see a terror and are afraid.

> **6:22** הֲֽכִי־אָ֭מַרְתִּי הָ֣בוּ לִ֑י וּ֝מִכֹּחֲכֶ֗ם שִׁחֲד֥וּ בַעֲדִֽי׃
>
> “Have I said, ‘Give me something,’ Or, ‘Offer a bribe for me from your wealth,’

> **6:23** וּמַלְּט֥וּנִי מִיַּד־צָ֑ר וּמִיַּ֖ד עָרִיצִ֣ים תִּפְדּֽוּנִי׃
>
> Or, ‘Deliver me from the hand of the adversary,’ Or, ‘Redeem me from the hand of the tyrants’?

> **6:24** הֹ֭ורוּנִי וַאֲנִ֣י אַחֲרִ֑ישׁ וּמַה־שָּׁ֝גִ֗יתִי הָבִ֥ינוּ לִֽי׃
>
> “Teach me, and I will be silent; And show me how I have erred.

> **6:25** מַה־נִּמְרְצ֥וּᵃ אִמְרֵי־יֹ֑שֶׁר וּמַה־יֹּוכִ֖יחַ הֹוכֵ֣חַ מִכֶּֽם׃
>
> “How painful are honest words! But what does your argument prove?

> **6:26** הַלְהֹוכַ֣ח מִלִּ֣ים תַּחְשֹׁ֑בוּ וּ֝לְר֗וּחַ אִמְרֵ֥י נֹאָֽשׁ׃
>
> “Do you intend to reprove my words, When the words of one in despair belong to the wind?

> **6:27** אַף־עַל־יָתֹ֥ום תַּפִּ֑ילוּ וְ֝תִכְר֗וּ עַל־רֵֽיעֲכֶֽם׃
>
> “You would even cast lots for the orphans And barter over your friend.

> **6:28** וְ֭עַתָּה הֹואִ֣ילוּ פְנוּ־בִ֑י וְעַל־פְּ֝נֵיכֶ֗ם אִם־אֲכַזֵּֽב׃
>
> “Now please look at me, And see if I lie to your face.

> **6:29** שֻֽׁבוּ־נָ֭א אַל־תְּהִ֣י עַוְלָ֑ה וְשֻׁ֥ביּᵃ עֹ֝ודᵇ צִדְקִי־בָֽהּ׃
>
> “Desist now, let there be no injustice; Even desist, my righteousness is yet in it.

> **6:30** הֲיֵשׁ־בִּלְשֹׁונִ֥י עַוְלָ֑ה אִם־חִ֝כִּ֗י לֹא־יָבִ֥ין הַוֹּֽות׃
>
> “Is there injustice on my tongue? Cannot my palate discern calamities?

> **7:1** הֲלֹא־צָבָ֣א לֶאֱנֹ֣ושׁ עֲלֵ־ᵃ אָ֑רֶץ וְכִימֵ֖י שָׂכִ֣יר יָמָֽיו׃
>
> “Is not man forced to labor on earth, And are not his days like the days of a hired man?

> **7:2** כְּעֶ֥בֶד יִשְׁאַף־צֵ֑ל וּ֝כְשָׂכִ֗יר יְקַוֶּ֥ה פָעֳלֹֽו׃
>
> “As a slave who pants for the shade, And as a hired man who eagerly waits for his wages,

> **7:3** כֵּ֤ן הָנְחַ֣לְתִּי לִ֭י יַרְחֵי־שָׁ֑וְא וְלֵילֹ֥ות עָ֝מָ֗ל מִנּוּ־לִֽי׃
>
> So am I allotted months of vanity, And nights of trouble are appointed me.

> **7:4** אִם־שָׁכַ֗בְתִּי וְאָמַ֗רְתִּי מָתַ֣י אָ֭קוּםᵃ וּמִדַּדᵇ־עָ֑רֶב וְשָׂבַ֖עְתִּי נְדֻדִ֣ים עֲדֵי־נָֽשֶׁף׃
>
> “When I lie down I say, ‘When shall I arise?’ But the night continues, And I am continually tossing until dawn.

> **7:5** לָ֘בַ֤שׁ בְּשָׂרִ֣י רִ֭מָּה וְג֣יּשׁᵃ עָפָ֑רᵇ עֹורִ֥י רָ֝גַ֗ע וַיִּמָּאֵֽס׃
>
> “My flesh is clothed with worms and a crust of dirt, My skin hardens and runs.

> **7:6** יָמַ֣י קַ֭לּוּ מִנִּי־אָ֑רֶג וַ֝יִּכְל֗וּ בְּאֶ֣פֶס תִּקְוָֽה׃
>
> “My days are swifter than a weaver’s shuttle, And come to an end without hope.

> **7:7** זְ֭כֹר כִּי־ר֣וּחַ חַיָּ֑י לֹא־תָשׁ֥וּב עֵ֝ינִ֗י לִרְאֹ֥ות טֹֽוב׃
>
> “Remember that my life is but breath; My eye will not again see good.

> **7:8** לֹֽא־תְ֭שׁוּרֵנִי עֵ֣ין רֹ֑אִי עֵינֶ֖יךָ בִּ֣י וְאֵינֶֽנִּי׃
>
> “The eye of him who sees me will behold me no longer; Your eyes will be on me, but I will not be.

> **7:9** כָּלָ֣ה עָ֭נָןᵃ וַיֵּלַ֑ךְ כֵּ֥ן יֹורֵ֥ד שְׁ֝אֹ֗ול לֹ֣א יַעֲלֶֽה׃
>
> “When a cloud vanishes, it is gone, So he who goes down to Sheol does not come up.

> **7:10** לֹא־יָשׁ֣וּב עֹ֣וד לְבֵיתֹ֑ו וְלֹא־יַכִּירֶ֖נּוּ עֹ֣וד מְקֹמֹֽו׃
>
> “He will not return again to his house, Nor will his place know him anymore.

> **7:11** גַּם־אֲנִי֮ לֹ֤א אֶחֱשָׂ֫ךְ פִּ֥י אֲֽ֭דַבְּרָה בְּצַ֣ר רוּחִ֑י אָ֝שִׂ֗יחָה בְּמַ֣ר נַפְשִֽׁי׃
>
> “Therefore I will not restrain my mouth; I will speak in the anguish of my spirit, I will complain in the bitterness of my soul.

> **7:12** הֲֽיָם־אָ֭נִי אִם־תַּנִּ֑ין כִּֽי־תָשִׂ֖ים עָלַ֣י מִשְׁמָֽר׃
>
> “Am I the sea, or the sea monster, That You set a guard over me?

> **7:13** כִּֽי־אָ֭מַרְתִּי תְּנַחֲמֵ֣נִי עַרְשִׂ֑י יִשָּׂ֥א בְ֝שִׂיחִ֗י מִשְׁכָּבִֽי׃
>
> “If I say, ‘My bed will comfort me, My couch will ease my complaint,’

> **7:14** וְחִתַּתַּ֥נִי בַחֲלֹמֹ֑ות וּֽמֵחֶזְיֹנֹ֥ות תְּבַעֲתַֽנִּי׃
>
> Then You frighten me with dreams And terrify me by visions;

> **7:15** וַתִּבְחַ֣ר מַחֲנָ֣ק נַפְשִׁ֑י מָ֝֗וֶת מֵֽעַצְמֹותָֽיᵃ׃
>
> So that my soul would choose suffocation, Death rather than my pains.

> **7:16** מָ֭אַסְתִּי לֹא־לְעֹלָ֣ם אֶֽחְיֶ֑ה חֲדַ֥ל מִ֝מֶּ֗נִּי כִּי־הֶ֥בֶל יָמָֽי׃
>
> “I waste away; I will not live forever. Leave me alone, for my days are but a breath.

> **7:17** מָֽה־אֱ֭נֹושׁ כִּ֣י תְגַדְּלֶ֑נּוּ וְכִי־תָשִׁ֖ית אֵלָ֣יו לִבֶּֽךָ׃
>
> “What is man that You magnify him, And that You are concerned about him,

> **7:18** וַתִּפְקְדֶ֥נּוּ לִבְקָרִ֑ים לִ֝רְגָעִ֗ים תִּבְחָנֶֽנּוּ׃
>
> That You examine him every morning And try him every moment?

> **7:19** כַּ֭מָּה לֹא־תִשְׁעֶ֣ה מִמֶּ֑נִּי לֹֽא־תַ֝רְפֵּ֗נִי עַד־בִּלְעִ֥י רֻקִּֽי׃
>
> “Will You never turn Your gaze away from me, Nor let me alone until I swallow my spittle?

> **7:20** חָטָ֡אתִי מָ֤ה אֶפְעַ֨ל׀ לָךְ֮ נֹצֵ֪ר הָאָ֫דָ֥ם לָ֤מָה שַׂמְתַּ֣נִי לְמִפְגָּ֣ע לָ֑ךְ וָאֶהְיֶ֖ה עָלַ֣יᵃ לְמַשָּֽׂא׃
>
> “Have I sinned? What have I done to You, O watcher of men? Why have You set me as Your target, So that I am a burden to myself?

> **7:21** וּמֶ֤ה׀ לֹא־תִשָּׂ֣א פִשְׁעִי֮ וְתַעֲבִ֪יר אֶת־עֲוֹ֫נִ֥י כִּֽי־עַ֭תָּה לֶעָפָ֣ר אֶשְׁכָּ֑ב וְשִׁ֖חֲרְתַּ֣נִיᵃ וְאֵינֶֽנִּי׃  פ
>
> “Why then do You not pardon my transgression And take away my iniquity? For now I will lie down in the dust; And You will seek me, but I will not be.”

### Bildad (8:1–22)

> **8:1** וַ֭יַּעַן בִּלְדַּ֥ד הַשּׁוּחִ֗י וַיֹאמַֽרᵃ׃
>
> Then Bildad the Shuhite answered,

> **8:2** עַד־אָ֥ן תְּמַלֶּל־אֵ֑לֶּה וְר֥וּחַ כַּ֝בִּירᵃ אִמְרֵי־פִֽיךָ׃
>
> “How long will you say these things, And the words of your mouth be a mighty wind?

> **8:3** הַ֭אֵל יְעַוֵּ֣ת מִשְׁפָּ֑ט וְאִם־שַׁ֝דַּ֗י יְעַוֵּֽת־צֶֽדֶק׃
>
> “Does God pervert justice? Or does the Almighty pervert what is right?

> **8:4** אִם־בָּנֶ֥יךָ חָֽטְאוּ־לֹ֑ו וַֽ֝יְשַׁלְּחֵ֗ם בְּיַד־פִּשְׁעָֽם׃
>
> “If your sons sinned against Him, Then He delivered them into the power of their transgression.

> **8:5** אִם־אַ֭תָּה תְּשַׁחֵ֣ר אֶל־אֵ֑ל וְאֶל־שַׁ֝דַּ֗י תִּתְחַנָּֽן׃
>
> “If you would seek God And implore the compassion of the Almighty,

> **8:6** אִם־זַ֥ךְ וְיָשָׁ֗ר אָ֥תָּה ᵃכִּי־עַ֭תָּה יָעִ֣יר עָלֶ֑יךָᵃ וְ֝שִׁלַּ֗ם נְוַ֣ת צִדְקֶֽךָ׃
>
> If you are pure and upright, Surely now He would rouse Himself for you And restore your righteous estate.

> **8:7** וְהָיָ֣ה רֵאשִׁיתְךָ֣ מִצְעָ֑ר וְ֝אַחֲרִיתְךָ֗ יִשְׂגֶּ֥ה מְאֹֽד׃
>
> “Though your beginning was insignificant, Yet your end will increase greatly.

> **8:8** כִּֽי־שְׁאַל־נָ֭א לְדֹ֣ר רִישֹׁ֑ון וְ֝כֹונֵ֗ן לְחֵ֣קֶר אֲבֹותָֽם׃
>
> “Please inquire of past generations, And consider the things searched out by their fathers.

> **8:9** כִּֽי־תְמֹ֣ול אֲ֭נַחְנוּ וְלֹ֣א נֵדָ֑ע כִּ֤י צֵ֖ל יָמֵ֣ינוּ עֲלֵי־אָֽרֶץ׃
>
> “For we are only of yesterday and know nothing, Because our days on earth are as a shadow.

> **8:10** הֲלֹא־הֵ֣ם יֹ֭ורוּךָ יֹ֣אמְרוּ לָ֑ךְ וּ֝מִלִּבָּ֗ם יֹוצִ֥אוּ מִלִּֽים׃
>
> “Will they not teach you and tell you, And bring forth words from their minds?

> **8:11** הֲיִֽגְאֶה־גֹּ֭מֶא בְּלֹ֣א בִצָּ֑הᵃ יִשְׂגֶּה־אָ֥חוּ בְלִי־מָֽיִם׃
>
> “Can the papyrus grow up without a marsh? Can the rushes grow without water?

> **8:12** עֹדֶ֣נּוּ בְ֭אִבֹּו לֹ֣א יִקָּטֵ֑ף וְלִפְנֵ֖י כָל־חָצִ֣יר יִיבָֽשׁ׃
>
> “While it is still green and not cut down, Yet it withers before any other plant.

> **8:13** כֵּ֗ן אָ֭רְחֹותᵃ כָּל־שֹׁ֣כְחֵי אֵ֑ל וְתִקְוַ֖ת חָנֵ֣ף תֹּאבֵֽד׃
>
> “So are the paths of all who forget God; And the hope of the godless will perish,

> **8:14** ᵃאֲשֶׁר־יָקֹ֥וטᵃ ᵇ כִּסְלֹ֑ו וּבֵ֥ית עַ֝כָּבִ֗ישׁ מִבְטַחֹֽו׃
>
> Whose confidence is fragile, And whose trust a spider’s web.

> **8:15** יִשָּׁעֵ֣ן ᵃעַל־בֵּ֭יתֹוᵃ וְלֹ֣א יַעֲמֹ֑ד יַחֲזִ֥יק בֹּ֝֗ו וְלֹ֣א יָקֽוּם׃
>
> “He trusts in his house, but it does not stand; He holds fast to it, but it does not endure.

> **8:16** רָטֹ֣ב ה֖וּא לִפְנֵי־שָׁ֑מֶשׁ וְעַ֥ל גַּ֝נָּתֹ֗ו יֹֽנַקְתֹּ֥ו תֵצֵֽא׃
>
> “He thrives before the sun, And his shoots spread out over his garden.

> **8:17** עַל־גַּ֭ל שָֽׁרָשָׁ֣יו יְסֻבָּ֑כוּ בֵּ֖יתᵃ אֲבָנִ֣ים יֶחֱזֶֽה׃
>
> “His roots wrap around a rock pile, He grasps a house of stones.

> **8:18** אִם־יְבַלְּעֶ֥נּוּ מִמְּקֹומֹ֑ו וְכִ֥חֶשׁ בֹּ֝֗ו לֹ֣א רְאִיתִֽיךָ׃
>
> “If he is removed from his place, Then it will deny him, saying, ‘I never saw you.’

> **8:19** הֶן־ה֭וּא מְשֹׂ֣ושׂ דַּרְכֹּ֑ו וּ֝מֵעָפָ֗ר אַחֵ֥ר יִצְמָֽחוּ׃
>
> “Behold, this is the joy of His way; And out of the dust others will spring.

> **8:20** הֶן־אֵ֭ל לֹ֣א יִמְאַס־תָּ֑ם וְלֹֽא־יַ֝חֲזִ֗יק בְּיַד־מְרֵעִֽים׃
>
> “Lo, God will not reject a man of integrity, Nor will He support the evildoers.

> **8:21** עַדᵃ־יְמַלֵּ֣הᵇ שְׂחֹ֣וק פִּ֑יךָ וּשְׂפָתֶ֥יךָ תְרוּעָֽה׃
>
> “He will yet fill your mouth with laughter And your lips with shouting.

> **8:22** שֹׂנְאֶ֥יךָ יִלְבְּשׁוּ־בֹ֑שֶׁת וְאֹ֖הֶל רְשָׁעִ֣ים אֵינֶֽנּוּ׃  פ
>
> “Those who hate you will be clothed with shame, And the tent of the wicked will be no longer.”

### Job (9:1–10:22)

> **9:1** וַיַּ֥עַן אִיֹּ֗וב וַיֹּאמַֽר׃
>
> Then Job answered,

> **9:2** אָ֭מְנָם יָדַ֣עְתִּי כִי־כֵ֑ן וּמַה־יִּצְדַּ֖ק אֱנֹ֣ושׁ עִם־אֵֽל׃
>
> “In truth I know that this is so; But how can a man be in the right before God?

> **9:3** אִם־יַ֭חְפֹּץ לָרִ֣יב עִמֹּ֑ו לֹֽא־יַ֝עֲנֶ֗נּוּ אַחַ֥ת מִנִּי־אָֽלֶף׃
>
> “If one wished to dispute with Him, He could not answer Him once in a thousand times.

> **9:4** חֲכַ֣ם לֵ֭בָב וְאַמִּ֣יץ כֹּ֑חַ מִֽי־הִקְשָׁ֥ה אֵ֝לָ֗יו וַיִּשְׁלָֽם׃
>
> “Wise in heart and mighty in strength, Who has defied Him without harm?

> **9:5** הַמַּעְתִּ֣יק הָ֭רִים וְלֹ֣א יָדָ֑עוּ אֲשֶׁ֖ר הֲפָכָ֣ם בְּאַפֹּֽו׃
>
> “It is God who removes the mountains, they know not how, When He overturns them in His anger;

> **9:6** הַמַּרְגִּ֣יז אֶ֭רֶץ מִמְּקֹומָ֑הּ וְ֝עַמּוּדֶ֗יהָ יִתְפַלָּצֽוּןᵃ׃
>
> Who shakes the earth out of its place, And its pillars tremble;

> **9:7** הָאֹמֵ֣ר לַ֭חֶרֶס וְלֹ֣א יִזְרָ֑ח וּבְעַ֖ד כֹּוכָבִ֣ים יַחְתֹּֽם׃
>
> Who commands the sun not to shine, And sets a seal upon the stars;

> **9:8** נֹטֶ֣ה שָׁמַ֣יִם לְבַדֹּ֑ו וְ֝דֹורֵ֗ךְ עַל־בָּ֥מֳתֵי יָֽםᵃ׃
>
> Who alone stretches out the heavens And tramples down the waves of the sea;

> **9:9** עֹֽשֶׂה־עָ֭שׁᵃ כְּסִ֥יל וְכִימָ֗ה וְחַדְרֵ֥י תֵמָֽן׃
>
> Who makes the Bear, Orion and the Pleiades, And the chambers of the south;

> **9:10** עֹשֶׂ֣ה גְ֭דֹלֹות עַד־אֵ֣ין חֵ֑קֶר וְנִפְלָאֹ֗ות עַד־אֵ֥ין מִסְפָּֽר׃
>
> Who does great things, unfathomable, And wondrous works without number.

> **9:11** הֵ֤ן יַעֲבֹ֣ר עָ֭לַי וְלֹ֣א אֶרְאֶ֑ה וְ֝יַחֲלֹ֗ףᵃ וְֽלֹא־אָבִ֥ין לֹֽו׃
>
> “Were He to pass by me, I would not see Him; Were He to move past me, I would not perceive Him.

> **9:12** הֵ֣ן יַ֭חְתֹּףᵃ מִ֣י יְשִׁיבֶ֑נּוּ מִֽי־יֹאמַ֥ר אֵ֝לָ֗יו מַֽה־תַּעֲשֶֽׂה׃
>
> “Were He to snatch away, who could restrain Him? Who could say to Him, ‘What are You doing?’

> **9:13** אֱ֭לֹוהַּ לֹא־יָשִׁ֣יב אַפֹּ֑ו תַּחְתָּ֥ו שָׁ֝חֲח֗וּ עֹ֣זְרֵי רָֽהַב׃
>
> “God will not turn back His anger; Beneath Him crouch the helpers of Rahab.

> **9:14** אַ֭ף כִּֽי־אָנֹכִ֣י אֶֽעֱנֶ֑נּוּ אֶבְחֲרָ֖ה דְבָרַ֣י עִמֹּֽו׃
>
> “How then can I answer Him, And choose my words before Him?

> **9:15** אֲשֶׁ֣ר אִם־צָ֭דַקְתִּי לֹ֣א אֶעֱנֶ֑ה לִ֝מְשֹׁפְטִ֗י אֶתְחַנָּֽן׃
>
> “For though I were right, I could not answer; I would have to implore the mercy of my judge.

> **9:16** אִם־קָרָ֥אתִי וַֽיַּעֲנֵ֑נִיᵃ לֹֽא־אַ֝אֲמִ֗ין כִּֽי־יַאֲזִ֥ין קֹולִֽי׃
>
> “If I called and He answered me, I could not believe that He was listening to my voice.

> **9:17** אֲשֶׁר־בִּשְׂעָרָ֥הᵃ יְשׁוּפֵ֑נִי וְהִרְבָּ֖ה פְצָעַ֣י חִנָּֽם׃
>
> “For He bruises me with a tempest And multiplies my wounds without cause.

> **9:18** לֹֽא־יִ֭תְּנֵנִי הָשֵׁ֣ב רוּחִ֑י כִּ֥י יַ֝שְׂבִּעַ֗נִי מַמְּרֹרִֽים׃
>
> “He will not allow me to get my breath, But saturates me with bitterness.

> **9:19** אִם־לְכֹ֣חַ אַמִּ֣יץ הִנֵּ֑ה וְאִם־לְ֝מִשְׁפָּ֗ט מִ֣י יֹועִידֵֽנִי׃
>
> “If it is a matter of power, behold, He is the strong one! And if it is a matter of justice, who can summon Him?

> **9:20** אִם־אֶ֭צְדָּק פִּ֣י יַרְשִׁיעֵ֑נִי תָּֽם־אָ֝֗נִי וַֽיַּעְקְשֵֽׁנִי׃
>
> “Though I am righteous, my mouth will condemn me; Though I am guiltless, He will declare me guilty.

> **9:21** תָּֽם־אָ֭נִי לֹֽא־אֵדַ֥ע נַפְשִׁ֗י אֶמְאַ֥ס חַיָּֽי׃
>
> “I am guiltless; I do not take notice of myself; I despise my life.

> **9:22** אַחַ֗ת הִ֥יא עַל־כֵּ֥ן אָמַ֑רְתִּי תָּ֥ם וְ֝רָשָׁ֗ע ה֣וּא מְכַלֶּֽה׃
>
> “It is all one; therefore I say, ‘He destroys the guiltless and the wicked.’

> **9:23** אִם־שֹׁ֭וט יָמִ֣ית פִּתְאֹ֑ם לְמַסַּ֖ת נְקִיִּ֣ם יִלְעָֽג׃
>
> “If the scourge kills suddenly, He mocks the despair of the innocent.

> **9:24** אֶ֤רֶץ׀ נִתְּנָ֬ה בְֽיַד־רָשָׁ֗ע פְּנֵֽי־שֹׁפְטֶ֥יהָ יְכַסֶּ֑ה אִם־לֹ֖א אֵפֹ֣וא מִי־הֽוּא׃
>
> “The earth is given into the hand of the wicked; He covers the faces of its judges. If it is not He, then who is it?

> **9:25** וְיָמַ֣יᵃ קַ֭לּוּ מִנִּי־רָ֑ץ בָּֽ֝רְח֗וּ לֹא־רָא֥וּ טֹובָֽה׃
>
> “Now my days are swifter than a runner; They flee away, they see no good.

> **9:26** חָ֭לְפוּ עִם־אֳנִיֹּ֣ות אֵבֶ֑ה כְּ֝נֶ֗שֶׁר יָט֥וּשׂ עֲלֵי־אֹֽכֶל׃
>
> “They slip by like reed boats, Like an eagle that swoops on its prey.

> **9:27** אִם־אָ֭מְרִיᵃ אֶשְׁכְּחָ֣ה שִׂיחִ֑י אֶעֶזְבָ֖ה פָנַ֣י וְאַבְלִֽיגָה׃
>
> “Though I say, ‘I will forget my complaint, I will leave off my sad countenance and be cheerful,’

> **9:28** יָגֹ֥רְתִּי כָל־עַצְּבֹתָ֑י יָ֝דַ֗עְתִּי כִּי־לֹ֥א תְנַקֵּֽנִי׃
>
> I am afraid of all my pains, I know that You will not acquit me.

> **9:29** אָנֹכִ֥יᵃ אֶרְשָׁ֑ע לָמָּה־זֶּ֝֗ה הֶ֣בֶל אִיגָֽע׃
>
> “I am accounted wicked, Why then should I toil in vain?

> **9:30** אִם־הִתְרָחַ֥צְתִּי בְמֵו־ᵃ שָׁ֑לֶג וַ֝הֲזִכֹּ֗ותִי בְּבֹ֣ר כַּפָּֽי׃
>
> “If I should wash myself with snow And cleanse my hands with lye,

> **9:31** אָ֭ז בַּשַּׁ֣חַתᵃ תִּטְבְּלֵ֑נִי וְ֝תִֽעֲב֗וּנִי שַׂלְמֹותָֽי׃
>
> Yet You would plunge me into the pit, And my own clothes would abhor me.

> **9:32** כִּי־לֹא־אִ֣ישׁ כָּמֹ֣נִי אֶֽעֱנֶ֑נּוּ נָבֹ֥וא יַ֝חְדָּ֗ו בַּמִּשְׁפָּֽט׃
>
> “For He is not a man as I am that I may answer Him, That we may go to court together.

> **9:33** לֹ֣אᵃ יֵשׁ־בֵּינֵ֣ינוּ מֹוכִ֑יחַ יָשֵׁ֖ת יָדֹ֣ו עַל־שְׁנֵֽינוּ׃
>
> “There is no umpire between us, Who may lay his hand upon us both.

> **9:34** יָסֵ֣ר מֵעָלַ֣י שִׁבְטֹ֑ו וְ֝אֵמָתֹ֗ו אַֽל־תְּבַעֲתַֽנִּי׃
>
> “Let Him remove His rod from me, And let not dread of Him terrify me.

> **9:35** אַֽ֭דַבְּרָהᵃ וְלֹ֣א אִירָאֶ֑נּוּ כִּ֥י לֹא־כֵ֥ן אָ֝נֹכִ֗י עִמָּדִֽי׃
>
> “Then I would speak and not fear Him; But I am not like that in myself.

> **10:1** נָֽקְטָ֥הᵃ נַפְשִׁ֗י בְּחַ֫יָּ֥י אֶֽעֶזְבָ֣ה עָלַ֣י שִׂיחִ֑י אֲ֝דַבְּרָה֗ᵇ בְּמַ֣ר נַפְשִֽׁי׃
>
> “I loathe my own life; I will give full vent to my complaint; I will speak in the bitterness of my soul.

> **10:2** אֹמַ֣ר אֶל־אֱ֭לֹוהַּ אַל־תַּרְשִׁיעֵ֑נִי הֹֽ֝ודִיעֵ֗נִי עַ֣ל מַה־תְּרִיבֵֽנִי׃
>
> “I will say to God, ‘Do not condemn me; Let me know why You contend with me.

> **10:3** הֲטֹ֤וב לְךָ֨׀ כִּֽי־תַעֲשֹׁ֗ק כִּֽי־תִ֭מְאַס יְגִ֣יעַ כַּפֶּ֑יךָ וְעַל־עֲצַ֖ת רְשָׁעִ֣ים הֹופָֽעְתָּ׃
>
> ‘Is it right for You indeed to oppress, To reject the labor of Your hands, And to look favorably on the schemes of the wicked?

> **10:4** הַעֵינֵ֣י בָשָׂ֣ר לָ֑ךְ אִם־כִּרְאֹ֖ות אֱנֹ֣ושׁ תִּרְאֶֽה׃
>
> ‘Have You eyes of flesh? Or do You see as a man sees?

> **10:5** הֲכִימֵ֣י אֱנֹ֣ושׁ יָמֶ֑יךָ אִם־שְׁ֝נֹותֶ֗יךָ כִּ֣ימֵי גָֽבֶר׃
>
> ‘Are Your days as the days of a mortal, Or Your years as man’s years,

> **10:6** כִּֽי־תְבַקֵּ֥שׁ לַעֲוֹנִ֑י וּ֭לְחַטָּאתִ֥י תִדְרֹֽושׁ׃
>
> That You should seek for my guilt And search after my sin?

> **10:7** עַֽל־דַּ֭עְתְּךָ כִּי־לֹ֣א אֶרְשָׁ֑ע וְאֵ֖ין מִיָּדְךָ֣ מַצִּֽיל׃
>
> ‘According to Your knowledge I am indeed not guilty, Yet there is no deliverance from Your hand.

> **10:8** יָדֶ֣יךָ עִ֭צְּבוּנִי וַֽיַּעֲשׂ֑וּנִי ᵃיַ֥חַד סָ֝בִ֗יבᵃ וַֽתְּבַלְּעֵֽנִי׃
>
> ‘Your hands fashioned and made me altogether, And would You destroy me?

> **10:9** זְכָר־נָ֭א כִּי־כַחֹ֣מֶר עֲשִׂיתָ֑נִי וְֽאֶל־עָפָ֥ר תְּשִׁיבֵֽנִי׃
>
> ‘Remember now, that You have made me as clay; And would You turn me into dust again?

> **10:10** הֲלֹ֣א כֶ֭חָלָב תַּתִּיכֵ֑נִי וְ֝כַגְּבִנָּ֗ה תַּקְפִּיאֵֽנִי׃
>
> ‘Did You not pour me out like milk And curdle me like cheese;

> **10:11** עֹ֣ור וּ֭בָשָׂר תַּלְבִּישֵׁ֑נִי וּֽבַעֲצָמֹ֥ות וְ֝גִידִ֗ים תְּסֹכְכֵֽנִי׃
>
> Clothe me with skin and flesh, And knit me together with bones and sinews?

> **10:12** חַיִּ֣ים וָ֭חֶסֶד עָשִׂ֣יתָ עִמָּדִ֑י וּ֝פְקֻדָּתְךָ֗ שָֽׁמְרָ֥ה רוּחִֽי׃
>
> ‘You have granted me life and lovingkindness; And Your care has preserved my spirit.

> **10:13** וְ֭אֵלֶּה צָפַ֣נְתָּ בִלְבָבֶ֑ךָ יָ֝דַ֗עְתִּי כִּי־זֹ֥את עִמָּֽךְ׃
>
> ‘Yet these things You have concealed in Your heart; I know that this is within You:

> **10:14** אִם־חָטָ֥אתִי וּשְׁמַרְתָּ֑נִי וּ֝מֵעֲוֹנִ֗י לֹ֣א תְנַקֵּֽנִי׃
>
> If I sin, then You would take note of me, And would not acquit me of my guilt.

> **10:15** אִם־רָשַׁ֡עְתִּי אַלְלַ֬י לִ֗י וְ֭צָדַקְתִּי לֹא־אֶשָּׂ֣א רֹאשִׁ֑י שְׂבַ֥ע קָ֝לֹ֗ון וּרְאֵ֥הᵃ עָנְיִֽי׃
>
> ‘If I am wicked, woe to me! And if I am righteous, I dare not lift up my head. I am sated with disgrace and conscious of my misery.

> **10:16** וְ֭יִגְאֶה כַּשַּׁ֣חַל תְּצוּדֵ֑נִי וְ֝תָשֹׁ֗ב תִּתְפַּלָּא־בִֽי׃
>
> ‘Should my head be lifted up, You would hunt me like a lion; And again You would show Your power against me.

> **10:17** תְּחַדֵּ֬שׁ עֵדֶ֨יךָ׀ נֶגְדִּ֗י וְתֶ֣רֶב כַּֽ֭עַשְׂךָ עִמָּדִ֑י חֲלִיפֹ֖ות וְצָבָ֣א עִמִּֽי׃
>
> ‘You renew Your witnesses against me And increase Your anger toward me; Hardship after hardship is with me.

> **10:18** וְלָ֣מָּה מֵ֭רֶחֶם הֹצֵאתָ֑נִי אֶ֝גְוַ֗ע וְעַ֣יִן לֹא־תִרְאֵֽנִי׃
>
> ‘Why then have You brought me out of the womb? Would that I had died and no eye had seen me!

> ‘I should have been as though I had not been, Carried from womb to tomb.’

> Before I go—and I shall not return— To the land of darkness and deep shadow,

>

### Zophar (11:1–20)

> **11:1** וַ֭יַּעַן צֹפַ֥ר הַֽנַּעֲמָתִ֗י וַיֹּאמַֽר׃
>
> Then Zophar the Naamathite answered,

> **11:2** הֲרֹ֣ב דְּ֭בָרִים לֹ֣א יֵעָנֶ֑ה וְאִם־אִ֖ישׁ שְׂפָתַ֣יִם יִצְדָּֽק׃
>
> “Shall a multitude of words go unanswered, And a talkative man be acquitted?

> **11:3** בַּ֭דֶּיךָᵃ מְתִ֣ים יַחֲרִ֑ישׁוᵇ וַ֝תִּלְעַ֗גᶜ וְאֵ֣ין מַכְלִֽם׃
>
> “Shall your boasts silence men? And shall you scoff and none rebuke?

> **11:4** וַ֭תֹּאמֶר זַ֣ךְ לִקְחִ֑י וּ֝בַ֗ר הָיִ֥יתִי בְעֵינֶֽיךָ׃
>
> “For you have said, ‘My teaching is pure, And I am innocent in your eyes.’

> **11:5** וְֽאוּלָ֗ם מִֽי־יִתֵּ֣ן אֱלֹ֣והַּ דַּבֵּ֑ר וְיִפְתַּ֖ח שְׂפָתָ֣יו עִמָּֽךְ׃
>
> “But would that God might speak, And open His lips against you,

> **11:6** וְיַגֶּד־לְךָ֨׀ תַּֽעֲלֻמֹ֣ות חָכְמָה֮ כִּֽי־כִפְלַ֪יִםᵃ לְֽת֫וּשִׁיָּ֥ה וְדַ֡ע כִּֽי־יַשֶּׁ֥ה לְךָ֥ אֱ֝לֹ֗והַᵇ מֵעֲוֹנֶֽךָ׃
>
> And show you the secrets of wisdom! For sound wisdom has two sides. Know then that God forgets a part of your iniquity.

> **11:7** הַחֵ֣קֶר אֱלֹ֣והַᵃ תִּמְצָ֑א אִ֤ם עַד־תַּכְלִ֖ית שַׁדַּ֣יᵇ תִּמְצָֽא׃
>
> “Can you discover the depths of God? Can you discover the limits of the Almighty?

> **11:8** ᵃגָּבְהֵ֣י שָׁ֭מַיִםᵃ מַה־תִּפְעָ֑ל עֲמֻקָּ֥ה מִ֝שְּׁאֹ֗ול מַה־תֵּדָֽע׃
>
> “They are high as the heavens, what can you do? Deeper than Sheol, what can you know?

> **11:9** אֲרֻכָּ֣ה מֵאֶ֣רֶץ מִדָּ֑הּᵃ וּ֝רְחָבָ֗ה מִנִּי־יָֽם׃
>
> “Its measure is longer than the earth And broader than the sea.

> **11:10** אִם־יַחֲלֹ֥ף וְיַסְגִּ֑יר וְ֝יַקְהִ֗יל וּמִ֣י יְשִׁיבֶֽנּוּ׃
>
> “If He passes by or shuts up, Or calls an assembly, who can restrain Him?

> **11:11** כִּי־ה֭וּא יָדַ֣ע מְתֵי־שָׁ֑וְא וַיַּרְא־אָ֝֗וֶן וְלֹ֣אᵃ יִתְבֹּונָֽן׃
>
> “For He knows false men, And He sees iniquity without investigating.

> **11:12** וְאִ֣ישׁ נָ֭בוּב יִלָּבֵ֑ב וְעַ֥יִר ᵃפֶּ֝֗רֶא אָדָ֥םᵃ יִוָּלֵֽד׃
>
> “An idiot will become intelligent When the foal of a wild donkey is born a man.

> **11:13** אִם־אַ֭תָּ֗ה הֲכִינֹ֣ותָ לִבֶּ֑ךָ וּפָרַשְׂתָּ֖ אֵלָ֣יו כַּפֶּֽךָ׃
>
> “If you would direct your heart right And spread out your hand to Him,

> **11:14** אִם־אָ֣וֶן בְּ֭יָדְךָ הַרְחִיקֵ֑הוּ וְאַל־תַּשְׁכֵּ֖ן בְּאֹהָלֶ֣יךָ עַוְלָֽה׃
>
> If iniquity is in your hand, put it far away, And do not let wickedness dwell in your tents;

> **11:15** כִּי־אָ֤ז׀ תִּשָּׂ֣א פָנֶ֣יךָ מִמּ֑וּם וְהָיִ֥יתָ מֻ֝צָ֗ק וְלֹ֣א תִירָֽא׃
>
> “Then, indeed, you could lift up your face without moral defect, And you would be steadfast and not fear.

> **11:16** כִּי־אַ֭תָּה עָמָ֣ל תִּשְׁכָּ֑ח כְּמַ֖יִם עָבְר֣וּ תִזְכֹּֽר׃
>
> “For you would forget your trouble, As waters that have passed by, you would remember it.

> **11:17** וּֽ֭מִצָּהֳרַיִם יָק֣וּם חָ֑לֶד תָּ֝עֻ֗פָהᵃ כַּבֹּ֥קֶר תִּהְיֶֽה׃
>
> “Your life would be brighter than noonday; Darkness would be like the morning.

> **11:18** וּֽ֭בָטַחְתָּ כִּי־יֵ֣שׁ תִּקְוָ֑ה וְ֝חָפַרְתָּ֗ᵃ לָבֶ֥טַח תִּשְׁכָּֽב׃
>
> “Then you would trust, because there is hope; And you would look around and rest securely.

> **11:19** וְֽ֭רָבַצְתָּ וְאֵ֣ין מַחֲרִ֑יד וְחִלּ֖וּ פָנֶ֣יךָ רַבִּֽים׃
>
> “You would lie down and none would disturb you, And many would entreat your favor.

> **11:20** וְעֵינֵ֥י רְשָׁעִ֗ים תִּ֫כְלֶ֥ינָה וּ֭מָנֹוס אָבַ֣ד מִנְהֶ֑ם וְ֝תִקְוָתָ֗ם מַֽפַּח־נָֽפֶשׁ׃  פ
>
> “But the eyes of the wicked will fail, And there will be no escape for them; And their hope is to breathe their last.”

### Job (12:1–14:22)

> **12:1** וַיַּ֥עַן אִיֹּ֗וב וַיֹּאמַֽר׃
>
> Then Job responded,

> **12:2** אָ֭מְנָם כִּ֣י אַתֶּם־עָ֑ם וְ֝עִמָּכֶ֗ם תָּמ֥וּת חָכְמָֽה׃
>
> “Truly then you are the people, And with you wisdom will die!

> **12:3** גַּם־לִ֤י לֵבָ֨ב׀ כְּֽמֹוכֶ֗ם ᵃלֹא־נֹפֵ֣ל אָנֹכִ֣י מִכֶּ֑םᵃ וְאֶת־מִי־אֵ֥ין כְּמֹו־אֵֽלֶּה׃

>
> “I am a joke to my friends, The one who called on God and He answered him; The just and blameless man is a joke.

> **12:5** ᵃלַפִּ֣יד בּ֭וּז לְעַשְׁתּ֣וּת שַׁאֲנָ֑ן נָ֝כֹ֗ון לְמֹ֣ועֲדֵי רָֽגֶל׃
>

> **12:6** יִשְׁלָ֤יוּ אֹֽהָלִ֨ים׀ לְשֹׁ֥דְדִ֗ים וּֽ֭בַטֻּחֹות לְמַרְגִּ֣יזֵי אֵ֑ל לַאֲשֶׁ֤ר הֵבִ֖יא אֱלֹ֣והַּ בְּיָדֹֽו׃

> **12:7** וְֽאוּלָ֗ם שְׁאַל־נָ֣א בְהֵמֹ֣ות וְתֹרֶ֑ךָּ וְעֹ֥וף הַ֝שָּׁמַ֗יִם וְיַגֶּד־לָֽךְ׃
>
> “But now ask the beasts, and let them teach you; And the birds of the heavens, and let them tell you.

> **12:8** אֹ֤ו ᵃשִׂ֣יחַ לָאָ֣רֶץᵃ וְתֹרֶ֑ךָּ וִֽיסַפְּר֥וּ לְ֝ךָ֗ דְּגֵ֣י הַיָּֽם׃
>
> “Or speak to the earth, and let it teach you; And let the fish of the sea declare to you.

> **12:9** מִ֭י לֹא־יָדַ֣ע בְּכָל־אֵ֑לֶּה כִּ֥י יַד־יְ֝הוָהᵃ עָ֣שְׂתָה זֹּֽאת׃
>
> “Who among all these does not know That the hand of the LORD has done this,

>
> In whose hand is the life of every living thing, And the breath of all mankind?

>
> “Does not the ear test words, As the palate tastes its food?

> **12:12** בִּֽישִׁישִׁ֥יםᵃ חָכְמָ֑ה וְאֹ֖רֶךְ יָמִ֣ים תְּבוּנָֽה׃
>
> “Wisdom is with aged men, With long life is understanding.

> **12:13** עִ֭מֹּו חָכְמָ֣ה וּגְבוּרָ֑ה לֹ֝֗ו עֵצָ֥ה וּתְבוּנָֽה׃
>
> “With Him are wisdom and might; To Him belong counsel and understanding.

>
> “With Him are strength and sound wisdom, The misled and the misleader belong to Him.

>

> **12:18** מוּסַ֣רᵃ מְלָכִ֣ים פִּתֵּ֑חַ וַיֶּאְסֹ֥ר אֵ֝זֹ֗ור בְּמָתְנֵיהֶֽם׃
>
> “He loosens the bond of kings And binds their loins with a girdle.

> **12:19** מֹולִ֣יךְ כֹּהֲנִ֣ים שֹׁולָ֑ל וְאֵֽתָנִ֣ים יְסַלֵּֽף׃
>
> “He makes priests walk barefoot And overthrows the secure ones.

>
> “He pours contempt on nobles And loosens the belt of the strong.

> **12:22** מְגַלֶּ֣ה עֲ֭מֻקֹות מִנִּי־חֹ֑שֶׁךְ וַיֹּצֵ֖א לָאֹ֣ור צַלְמָֽוֶת׃
>

> **12:23** מַשְׂגִּ֣יאᵃ לַ֭גֹּויִם וַֽיְאַבְּדֵ֑ם שֹׁטֵ֥חַ לַ֝גֹּויִ֗םᵇ וַיַּנְחֵֽםᶜ׃

>
> “They grope in darkness with no light, And He makes them stagger like a drunken man.

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims, each surfaced by several tools:

1. **The first round is an argument about hope — and it ends with Job saying that God destroys it.** **New.** `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the design
   - **One word in five of the six speeches.** תִּקְוָה ("hope") stands in Job in 12 verses (13 times); **9 of the 12 fall in this round** (WLC, lemma 8615 b). Eliphaz: "the integrity of your ways your hope" (4:6); "the helpless has hope" (5:16). Job: "that God would grant my longing (וְתִקְוָתִי)" (6:8) — and the longing is that God would crush him (6:9); "my days … come to an end without hope" (7:6). Bildad: "the hope of the godless will perish" (8:13). Zophar: "you would trust, because there is hope" (11:18); "their hope is to breathe their last" (11:20). Job: "there is hope for a tree" (14:7); "So You destroy man's hope" (14:19). Only chapters 9–10, the speech of the lawsuit Job cannot win, lack it.
   - **It brackets the round.** The first occurrence is in Eliphaz's first argument (4:6) and the last in Job's last stanza (14:19). The vision's question, "Can mankind (אֱנוֹשׁ) be just before God?" (4:17), and Eliphaz's "hope" (4:6) meet in one verse at the close: "So You destroy the hope (תִקְוַת) of man (אֱנוֹשׁ)" (14:19).
   - **The speakers answer one another with it.** Zophar's last line takes the words of Job's 7:6: כלה ("come to an end, fail") with תִּקְוָה occurs **only at Job 7:6 and 11:20** in the Hebrew Bible (lemma). What Job said of his own days, Zophar says of the wicked.
   - **Job's death-wish changes shape across the round.** "Would that God were willing to crush me" (6:9) → "my soul would choose suffocation, death" (7:15) → "Would that I had died" (10:18) → "Oh that You would hide me in Sheol … that You would set a limit for me and remember me!" (14:13). The wish to be ended becomes a wish to be kept, and the prayer is to the God whose wrath Job is under.
   - **The Greek.** ἐλπίς carries the chain in Swete at 4:6; 5:16; 6:8; 7:6; 8:13; 11:18, 20; 14:7. At 14:19 Swete has ὑπομονὴν ἀνθρώπου ἀπώλεσας ("You destroyed the ὑπομονή of man") — its only ὑπομονή in Greek Job. James's "you have heard of the ὑπομονή of Job" (Jas 5:11) therefore uses the word the Greek gives to the hope God destroys here. *(Category 3 is not claimed: James does not quote 14:19.)*
   - *Surfaced by:* Repetition, Structure, Tool 11 (Move 4), Translations, Original Audience. *High on the words; moderate–high on the design.*

2. **"Call now — is there anyone who will answer you?" The round runs from Eliphaz's taunt to Job's "You will call, and I will answer You".** **New** as a chain; 13:22 and 14:15 are from the 13–14 dig. `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the arc
   - **The chain.** קרא ("call") with ענה ("answer") in one verse occurs in Job at 5:1; 9:16; 12:4; 13:22; 14:15 and 19:16 — **five of its six verses in this round** (lemma). Eliphaz: "Call now, is there anyone who will answer you?" (5:1). Job: "If I called and He answered me, I could not believe that He was listening to my voice" (9:16). "The one who called on God and He answered him" — now a laughingstock (12:4). "Then call, and I will answer; or let me speak, then reply to me" (13:22). "You will call, and I will answer You; You will long for the work of Your hands" (14:15).
   - **The address shows the same movement.** Every second-person form in the friends' three speeches is singular and addressed to Job: Eliphaz 40, Bildad 17, Zophar 33 (WLC morphology; the pausal לָךְ forms counted as masculine). **No friend ever speaks to God.** Every one of Job's speeches ends addressed to God: from 7:7 ("Remember"), from 9:28 and through chapter 10, and from 13:20 to 14:20. In 9–10 Job does not address the friends at all.
   - **Where it goes.** "Then the LORD answered (וַיַּעַן) Job out of the whirlwind" (38:1), and God reverses the order: "I will ask you, and you instruct Me" (38:3; 40:7; 42:4) (13–14 dig; overview).
   - *Surfaced by:* Structure, Original Languages (the address test), Repetition, Move 4. *High.*

3. **The round is built out of one another's words: the friends hand Job's lines back to him as accusations, and Job hands theirs back as protest.** **New** except where marked. `[T]` for each verbal link; `[I]` for the pattern
   - **Bildad's first line answers Job's last image of words.** אֵמֶר ("word") with רוּחַ ("wind") occurs only at Job 6:26, 8:2 and Prov 17:27 (lemma). Job: "the words of one in despair belong to the wind" (6:26). Bildad: "How long will you say these things, and the words of your mouth be a mighty wind?" (8:2).
   - **Bildad's appeal answers Job's last verse.** "You will seek me (וְשִׁחַרְתַּנִי), but I will not be" (7:21) → "If you would seek (תְּשַׁחֵר) God" (8:5). The piel of שׁחר occurs in Job only at 7:21; 8:5; 24:5 (lemma; sweep, confirmed). Job's last word is וְאֵינֶנִּי ("and I will not be"); Bildad's last word is אֵינֶנּוּ ("will be no longer", 8:22).
   - **Bildad reworks Job's picture of the vanished dead.** "Nor will his place (מְקֹמוֹ) know him anymore" (7:10) → "If he is removed from his place (מִמְּקוֹמוֹ), then it will deny him, saying, 'I never saw you'" (8:18).
   - **Zophar quotes Job's hymn back at him.** חלף ("pass by"), the hiphil of שׁוב ("restrain, turn back") and מִי ("who?") within two verses occur in the Hebrew Bible **only** at Job 9:11–12 and 11:10 (lemma). Job: "Were He to move past me (יַחֲלֹף) … who could restrain Him (מִי יְשִׁיבֶנּוּ)?" (9:11–12). Zophar: "If He passes by (יַחֲלֹף) … who can restrain Him (וּמִי יְשִׁיבֶנּוּ)?" (11:10). **Swete makes the borrowing verbatim**: 11:10 ends τίς ἐρεῖ αὐτῷ Τί ἐποίησας; ("who will say to Him, 'What have You done?'"), the words of Swete's 9:12. The Greek translator heard the quotation.
   - **Job parodies Bildad.** שְׁאַל־נָא ("please ask") occurs in Job only at 8:8 and 12:7 (phrase search; the hiphil of ירה, "teach", follows in both, 8:10; 12:7–8). Bildad: "Please inquire of past generations … Will they not teach you?" Job: "But now ask the beasts, and let them teach you".
   - **Job's tree rewrites Bildad's plant.** יוֹנֶקֶת ("shoots") with שֹׁרֶשׁ ("root") within two verses occurs in Job only at 8:16–17 and 14:7–8 (and in the Hebrew Bible at Hos 14:7 and Ps 80:12) (lemma). Bildad's godless man is a plant that "thrives before the sun" and is torn up, while "out of the dust others will spring" (8:16–19). Job's tree is cut down and sprouts again "at the scent of water", its stump dying "in the dry soil (בֶּעָפָר)" — and man does not (14:7–12).
   - **Job answers Eliphaz.** "In famine He will redeem you (פָּדְךָ) from death, and in war from the power (מִידֵי) of the sword" (5:20) → "Have I said … 'Redeem me (תִּפְדּוּנִי) from the hand (מִיַּד) of the tyrants'?" (6:22–23). יָד with פדה occurs in Job only at these two verses (lemma). And "Is not your fear of God (יִרְאָתְךָ) your confidence?" (4:6) → "so that he does not forsake the fear (וְיִרְאַת) of the Almighty" (6:14): יִרְאָה occurs in Job only at 4:6; 6:14; 15:4; 22:4; 28:28.

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**At book level** the overview's purpose stands: to show a righteous man who will neither curse God nor buy relief by confessing sins he did not commit, to expose the friends' theology as speaking wrongly of God (42:7), and to bring sufferer and reader to the fear of God that wants God himself. `[I]` (overview)

**At unit level** the first round does three things the book needs before anything else can happen. `[I]`

- **It states the friends' doctrine in full, three times, and lets the reader test it against what the prologue has shown.** Eliphaz argues from experience and a night vision ("According to what I have seen", 4:8; "a word was brought to me", 4:12). Bildad argues from tradition ("Please inquire of past generations", 8:8). Zophar wishes God himself would speak ("But would that God might speak", 11:5). `[T]` for the three appeals; `[I]` for the scheme. All three share one premise, put most bluntly by Bildad: the man who is "pure and upright" is restored (8:6), the godless perishes (8:13). The reader knows from 1:1, 8 and 2:3 that Job is "blameless and upright" and that God was incited against him "without cause".
- **It turns Job from the friends to God.** Job's first speech turns at 7:7; his second hardly speaks to the friends at all; his third ends "You will call, and I will answer You" (14:15). The address test (Tool 3 and Original Languages) makes this measurable. `[T]`
- **It puts hope on trial.** Every speaker defines it, and the round closes on "So You destroy man's hope" (14:19) and on the one hope Job can still form — to be hidden in Sheol and remembered (14:13). `[T]` for the words (Headline 1).

**Why it matters for the sermon.** This round is the book's longest stretch of argument before any speaker repeats himself. A sermon on it must preach **the conversation**, not three speeches by friends and three by Job in sequence: the meaning lies in who takes up whose words. `[I]`

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement.** The prologue has established three things the reader cannot unknow: God's verdict on Job (1:1, 8; 2:3), the Accuser's question whether Job fears God "for nothing" (1:9), and God's own admission that he was incited "to ruin him without cause" (2:3). The three friends came "to sympathize with him and comfort him" (2:11) and sat seven days in silence (2:13). Then Job broke the silence with a curse on his day and the question "Why is light given to him who suffers?" (3:20), ending "I am not at ease … but turmoil comes" (3:26). `[T]` (unit dig 1:1–3:1; Job 3 dig)

**Necessity answer.** The book must now let the comforters speak, because chapter 3 has said in a lament what the friends' doctrine cannot allow: that a man may suffer without cause and wish he had never been born. The friends' answer is the one the prologue has already ruled out for the reader, so the round can be heard as a test of a doctrine rather than a debate between equals. And Job must reply three times, because the turn the book needs — from lament spoken to no one (3) to a case argued with God (13–14) — cannot happen in one speech. `[I]`

**Implication for this unit.** The round is not an exchange of views ending in a draw. It is the movement of one man from "Let the day perish" (3:3) to "Oh that You would hide me in Sheol … and remember me!" (14:13), with the friends' words as the resistance he pushes against. The friends do not change in the round; Job does. The friends' speeches get **shorter** — Eliphaz 48 verses with the formula, Bildad 22, Zophar 20 — and Job's get **longer** — 51, 57, 75 (WLC). `[T]` for the counts; `[I]` for what they suggest.

**Revisit after Tool 11.** Move 2 sharpens the answer. The friends' Scripture is the covenant's — the Song of Moses, the curses of Deuteronomy, Proverbs' discipline — and Job answers from the Psalter's laments and the Servant's courtroom (Headline 5). The round therefore puts two ways of reading the same Bible side by side, and the reader knows which one God will call "right" (42:7). `[I]`

#### Surrounding context

- **Before:** Job 3, the curse and the "why?" (Job 3 dig). Eliphaz answers it directly: Job's "Let the day perish (יֹאבַד)" (3:3) is met by "who ever perished (אָבָד) being innocent?" (4:7) (4–5 dig).
- **After:** round two opens with Eliphaz quoting Job's own words against him — "What is man, that he should be pure, or he who is born of a woman, that he should be righteous?" (15:14, from 7:17 and 14:1) — and charging him, "you do away with reverence (יִרְאָה)" (15:4), the word Job used at 6:14. `[T]` (overview Table B; 13–14 dig)
- **Section:** 4–14 is the first of three rounds (4–14; 15–21; 22–27). The Masoretic paragraphing marks it as six speeches and nothing else (Tool 3).

### 4. Linking Words

The poetry argues by question, condition and pivot more than by "therefore". Five connectives carry the round. `[T]` for every occurrence (WLC lemma).

- **אִם ("if") — the friends' conditions, Job's impossibilities.** Bildad's speech turns on three: "If your sons sinned … If you would seek God … If you are pure and upright" (8:4–6). Zophar's has two: "If you would direct your heart right … If iniquity is in your hand" (11:13–14). Both are conditions of restoration: do this, and God will act. Job's אִם is different. In 9–10 it occurs fifteen times, most of them impossible suppositions: "If one wished to dispute with Him" (9:3); "If I called and He answered me" (9:16); "If it is a matter of power … if it is a matter of justice" (9:19). The friends' "if" offers a way back; Job's "if" shows there is none. In 14:7–14 the conditions turn again — "though its roots grow old" (14:8); "If a man dies, will he live again?" (14:14) — into a question Job cannot answer and will not drop. `[I]` for the contrast.
- **אוּלָם ("but, however") — the pivot word.** It occurs ten times in Job, and **six of them are in this round**: 5:8; 11:5; 12:7; 13:3, 4; 14:18. Its first two uses are the Accuser's: "But (וְאוּלָם) put forth Your hand now" (1:11; 2:5). In the round it marks every turn from argument to appeal. Eliphaz: "But as for me (אוּלָם אֲנִי), I would seek God" (5:8). Zophar: "But (וְאוּלָם) would that God might speak" (11:5). Job: "But now (וְאוּלָם) ask the beasts" (12:7); "But I (אוּלָם אֲנִי) would speak to the Almighty" (13:3) — Eliphaz's two words, taken back (4–5 dig). **New** for the distribution. *High on the data.*
- **כִּי־עַתָּה ("for now") — Bildad takes Job's last clause.** Job's last verse: "For now (כִּי־עַתָּה) I will lie down in the dust; and You will seek me (וְשִׁחַרְתַּנִי), but I will not be" (7:21). Bildad's appeal: "If you would seek (תְּשַׁחֵר) God … surely now (כִּי־עַתָּה) He would rouse Himself for you" (8:5–6). The piel of שׁחר within a verse of כִּי־עַתָּה occurs **only at Job 7:21 and 8:5–6** (lemma and normalised phrase). Bildad reverses both: Job said God would look for him and find nothing; Bildad says Job should look for God and find restoration "now". **New** for כִּי־עַתָּה. *High.*
- **מִי־יִתֵּן ("Oh that…", literally "who will give?") — the round's wishes.** 6:8; 11:5; 13:5; 14:4; 14:13 (normalised phrase; a skeletal match at 3:20, לָמָּה יִתֵּן, was discarded). Job wishes God would crush him (6:8–9); Zophar wishes God would speak against Job (11:5); Job wishes the friends would be silent (13:5); Job asks who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean (14:4); Job wishes God would hide him in Sheol and remember him (14:13). Zophar's wish is granted — God does speak (38:1), and against Zophar (42:7) (sweep). `[T]` / `[I]`
- **כִּי ("for") — the grounds.** In Job's speeches כִּי usually grounds a complaint in God's action: "For the arrows of the Almighty are within me" (6:4); "For He bruises me with a tempest" (9:17); "For You write bitter things against me" (13:26). In the friends' it grounds a promise: "For you will be in league with the stones of the field" (5:23); "For you would forget your trouble" (11:16). `[I]`

### 5. Parallels

Hebrew poetry throughout. Five pairs that clarify meaning in the three new speeches (the solo digs work the rest):

- **6:5 — emblematic.** "Does the wild donkey (פֶּרֶא) bray over his grass, or does the ox low over his fodder?" Animals complain only when they lack food; Job's cry has a cause. Zophar answers with the same animal: "An idiot will become intelligent when the foal of a wild donkey (פֶּרֶא) is born a man" (11:12). פֶּרֶא occurs in Job only at 6:5; 11:12; 24:5; 39:5 (lemma). Job's donkey brays for a reason; Zophar's donkey is Job. **New.** `[T]` / `[I]` *Moderate–high.*
- **7:9–10 — synonymous, then extended.** "When a cloud vanishes, it is gone, so he who goes down to Sheol does not come up. He will not return again to his house, nor will his place know him anymore." Bildad's 8:18 takes the second line (Headline 3).
- **8:11–12 — the papyrus.** "Can the papyrus grow up without a marsh? Can the rushes grow without water? While it is still green and not cut down, yet it withers before any other plant." The parallel applies in 8:13: "So are the paths of all who forget God". Job's 14:7–9 inverts the picture: the tree that is cut down revives "at the scent of water" (Headline 3).
- **11:8–9 — the four dimensions.** "high as the heavens … deeper than Sheol … longer than the earth … broader than the sea". Job answers the second line in his hymn: "He reveals mysteries (עֲמֻקוֹת, 'deep things') from the darkness" (12:22). עָמֹק occurs in Job only at 11:8 and 12:22 (lemma). Zophar says the depths of God are beyond Job's knowing; Job says God brings the deep things out — and the deep darkness with them. **New.** *Moderate–high.*
- **12:14–15 — the double הֵן ("Behold").** "He tears down … He imprisons (יִסְגֹּר) a man … He restrains the waters … He sends them out". The verb סגר ("shut, imprison") answers Zophar's "If He passes by or shuts up (וְיַסְגִּיר)" (11:10); in Job סגר occurs at 3:10; 11:10; 12:14; 16:11; 41:7 (lemma). Zophar's God shuts up in judgement; Job's God shuts up a man "and there can be no release". **New.** *Moderate.*

### 6. Narrator's Comment

The narrator speaks only in the six speech formulas (4:1; 6:1; 8:1; 9:1; 11:1; 12:1). He names Eliphaz "the Temanite", Bildad "the Shuhite" and Zophar "the Naamathite" each time, and Job without epithet. He offers no verdict until 42:7. `[T]`

**The absence is a pattern-break worth naming, within the gate.** The prologue's narrator commented freely: "Through all this Job did not sin nor did he blame God" (1:22); "In all this Job did not sin with his lips" (2:10). From 3:1 to 42:6 he comments on nothing, so the reader must weigh every line by its speaker and by what the prologue has shown. The established pattern is 1:1, 22; 2:10 (narrator's evaluations) against their absence through the dialogue. `[T]` for the data; *moderate* as a reading.

### 7. Vocabulary

Eight words carry the round. Each is given in the Hebrew with its distribution verified by lemma (WLC); the glosses are the NASB95's where a verse is quoted.

**תִּקְוָה ("hope").** 12 verses in Job, 9 of them here: 4:6; 5:16; 6:8; 7:6; 8:13; 11:18, 20; 14:7, 19; then 17:15; 19:10; 27:8 (lemma 8615 b; 31 verses in the Hebrew Bible, so Job holds more than a third). The word means an expectation one waits for, from the root קוה ("wait"). Each speaker gives it a different object. **Eliphaz** puts it in Job's integrity (4:6) and in God's care for the poor (5:16). **Job** makes it the wish for death (6:8–9) and then says his days end without it (7:6). **Bildad** gives it to the godless only to say it will perish (8:13). **Zophar** promises it on condition of repentance (11:18) and defines the wicked's hope as death (11:20). **Job** finds it in a tree and denies it to man (14:7, 19). **New** as a round-level chain. *High.* Its later uses carry the thread: "Where now is my hope?" (17:15); "He has uprooted my hope like a tree" (19:10, the only other verse with תִּקְוָה and עֵץ, "tree", lemma).

**צדק ("be in the right") and יכח ("argue, reprove") — the lawsuit.** See Headline 4. צדק as a verb stands in 17 verses of Job, seven here; יכח in 15, seven here (lemma). The overview's "Leitwort of the argument" is confirmed. `[T]` *High.*

**צָבָא ("hard service, conscription") and שָׂכִיר ("hired man").** צָבָא ordinarily means an army or host; in Job it means a term of compulsory service, and it occurs only at 7:1; 10:17; 14:14 — once in each of Job's speeches. NASB95 renders it three ways ("forced to labor", "Hardship after hardship", "my struggle"); ESV twice as "service" (7:1; 14:14) and once as "fresh troops" (10:17) (Tool 8). שָׂכִיר at 7:1, 2; 14:6. **New** as a bracket of Job's speeches (Tool 3). *High on the words.*

**תּוּשִׁיָּה ("success, sound wisdom").** 11 verses in the Hebrew Bible; in Job 5:12; 6:13; 11:6; 12:16; 26:3 — four of them here, in four different mouths (lemma). Eliphaz: God frustrates the shrewd so "their hands cannot attain success" (5:12). Job: "deliverance (וְתֻשִׁיָּה) is driven from me" (6:13). Zophar: "sound wisdom (לְתוּשִׁיָּה) has two sides" (11:6). Job: "With Him are strength and sound wisdom" (12:16). The word passes round the circle like a coin: the crafty lack it, Job has lost it, Zophar claims to know its secret, and Job gives it back to God. **New.** *High on the data; moderate–high on the design.*

**חֵקֶר ("searching, unsearchable depth").** 12 verses in the Hebrew Bible; in Job 5:9; 8:8; 9:10; 11:7; 34:24; 36:26; 38:16. **All four speakers of this round use it**: Eliphaz's God does things "unsearchable (וְאֵין חֵקֶר)" (5:9); Bildad sends Job to "the things searched out (לְחֵקֶר) by their fathers" (8:8); Job repeats Eliphaz (9:10); Zophar asks, "Can you discover the depths (הַחֵקֶר) of God?" (11:7). God will ask, "Have you … walked in the recesses (וּבְחֵקֶר) of the deep?" (38:16) (lemma; sweep for 11:7 → 38:16). Eliphaz's speech also ends with the cognate verb: "we have investigated it (חֲקַרְנוּהָ)" (5:27); Job answers, "Will it be well when He examines (יַחְקֹר) you?" (13:9) (lemma 2713). **New** for the four-speaker distribution. *High on the data.*

**אֱנוֹשׁ ("mortal man").** 18 verses in Job, 9 here: 4:17; 5:17; 7:1, 17; 9:2; 10:4, 5; 13:9; 14:19 (lemma). The vision asks whether אֱנוֹשׁ can be in the right (4:17); Job takes up the question (9:2), asks what אֱנוֹשׁ is that God should watch him (7:17, Ps 8:5), asks whether God's days are like a אֱנוֹשׁ's (10:5), and ends, "So You destroy the hope of אֱנוֹשׁ" (14:19). `[T]` *High on the data.*

**חֶסֶד ("kindness, lovingkindness").** Three verses in Job, two of them here (6:14; 10:12; 37:13) — the positive control for every search in this run. Job asks the friends for it (6:14) and confesses that God gave it (10:12). Its first use in the book is a claim on friends, its second a claim on God. `[T]` (sweep; 9–10 dig).

**קָדוֹשׁ ("holy one").** In Job only at 5:1; 6:10; 15:15 (lemma). Eliphaz: "to which of the holy ones (מִקְּדֹשִׁים) will you turn?" (5:1). Job: "I have not denied the words of the Holy One (קָדוֹשׁ)" (6:10). The plural of Eliphaz's taunt becomes the singular of Job's loyalty. **New.** *Moderate–high.*

**Proper-noun and divine-name inventory.**

- **Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, Zophar the Naamathite** (4:1; 8:1; 11:1). Gentilics from Abraham's wider family and Edom (overview, Presenting Situation). No canonical freight beyond that is activated in this round; the irony of "Eliphaz the Temanite" against Jer 49:7 is queued (#40).
- **Tema and Sheba** (6:19). Caravan peoples (Gen 25:3, 15). The name תֵּימָא ("Tema", 8485) is a different lemma from תֵּימָנִי ("Temanite", 8489), but the two sound alike, and Job's caravans of Tema "were disappointed" at the dry wadi in the speech that answers the Temanite. *Uncertain* — a possible sound-play only; name-wordplay rule applied, no narrative corroboration beyond the scene itself.
- **Rahab** (9:13), **the Bear, Orion, the Pleiades** (9:9) — 9–10 dig. **Sheol** (7:9; 11:8; 14:13) — once in each of the three exchanges.
- **Divine names** (WLC lemma counts by speech): Eliphaz אֱלוֹהַּ 3, אֵל 1, אֱלֹהִים 1, שַׁדַּי 1, "the holy ones" 1; Job 6–7 אֱלוֹהַּ 3, שַׁדַּי 2, "the Holy One" 1; Bildad אֵל 4, שַׁדַּי 2; Job 9–10 אֱלוֹהַּ 2, אֵל 1; Zophar אֱלוֹהַּ 3, שַׁדַּי 1; Job 12–14 אֵל 4, אֱלוֹהַּ 2, שַׁדַּי 1 and **יְהוָה once, at 12:9** — the dialogue's only use of the covenant name, inside the verbatim phrase of Isa 41:20 (overview). In 9–10, where Job speaks most directly to God, he names him least: three times in 57 verses. He calls him "You". `[T]`

**Pulpit divergence note.** The pulpit text is the ESV. For this sermon it is the better English for the hope chain and the "in the right" chain, and the weaker for call-and-answer. **Three places need a word from the pulpit**: 7:20 (the ESV prints the scribal-correction reading); 12:4 (the ESV supplies "I … me"); and 6:14 (the ESV turns a plea into a charge). None of them changes the sermon's direction, but at 12:4 a hearer could take Job to be claiming that God *has* answered him, which is the one thing the round withholds.

#### Ancient versions check

The Greek is a secondary witness for this run, and Greek Job is about a sixth shorter than the Hebrew (BHS head note; overview). In 4–14 the Rahlfs export marks **15 asterisks**: none in 4–6 or 8; one each in 7, 10 and 11; three each in 9 and 12; two in 13; four in 14 (asterisk signs counted by chapter). Each divergence is placed in the three-way triage (category 1 = translation loss; 2 = substantive variant; 3 = the NT's own text).

- **7:20 — "a burden to You".** Category 2 (see Textual Variants). The Greek agrees with the reading behind the scribal correction.
- **8:13 — "the paths" or "the end".** Swete reads τὰ ἔσχατα ("the last things, the end") for אָרְחוֹת ("paths"); BHS notes the Greek = אַחֲרִית. Category 2, probably a graphic confusion of ארחות and אחרית. The Greek thereby repeats 8:7's אַחֲרִית ("your end will increase greatly") — the very word God fulfils in 42:12. *Moderate*; not load-bearing.
- **9:16 — "and he did not answer".** **Swete (Vaticanus) and Rahlfs disagree.** Swete: ἐάν τε καλέσω καὶ μὴ ὑπακούσῃ ("if I call and he does not answer"); Rahlfs: καὶ ὑπακούσῃ ("and he answers"), with the Hebrew. BHS notes the reading of Codex Vaticanus (G-B in its siglum). A difference inside the Greek tradition, category 2 within the Greek; the Hebrew and Rahlfs agree. Report as an edition difference; nothing rests on it.
- **11:10 — Zophar's quotation made verbatim.** Swete's 11:10 ends τίς ἐρεῖ αὐτῷ Τί ἐποίησας; ("who will say to Him, 'What have You done?'"), identical with Swete's 9:12b. The Hebrew 11:10 has the words of 9:11–12a, not 9:12b. Category 1 in reverse: the translator **heard the quotation and strengthened it**. Evidence that an ancient reader took 11:10 as Zophar quoting Job. *High* as a datum about reception.
- **12:4 and 12:9 — the Greek lacks the call-and-answer line and the covenant name.** 12:4 has only "for a righteous and blameless man has become a mockery"; 12:9b, "that the hand of the LORD has done this", stands under Origen's asterisk in Rahlfs — supplied from the Hebrew, absent from the Old Greek. Category 2 (shorter Greek). **The dialogue's only יְהוָה and one link of Headline 2 belong to the Hebrew.**
- **14:19 — ὑπομονή.** "So You destroy man's hope" becomes καὶ ὑπομονὴν ἀνθρώπου ἀπώλεσας. ὑπομονή is the Greek translators' regular word for Hebrew "hope" (Swete Ps 9:19; 61:6; 70:5 = Hebrew Ps 9:19; 62:6; 71:5; and Jer 14:8; 17:13; 1 Chr 29:15), and in Greek Job it stands only here. ὑπομένω renders יחל ("wait") at 6:11 and 14:14. Category 1 for the Hebrew–Greek relation; for Jas 5:11 see Tool 11. *Moderate.*
- **7:1; 10:17 — πειρατήριον.** Swete renders צָבָא with πειρατήριον at both (and at 19:12; Gen 49:19; Ps 17:30 it means a raiding band). At 7:1 the Greek reads "Is not the life of man a πειρατήριον on earth?" — an ordeal or a raid. The Greek keeps 7:1 and 10:17 together and loses 14:14 (ἡμέρας τοῦ βίου, "the days of his life"). Category 1. *Uncertain* whether the Greek reader heard "trial" (πειρασμός) in it.
- **6:14.** Swete has an entirely different line: "Mercy has renounced me, and the visitation of the Lord has overlooked me". Category 2 (or free rendering of a hard text). Not usable as a witness to either English reading.
- **Agreements.** The Greek keeps the hope chain (ἐλπίς at 4:6; 5:16; 6:8; 7:6; 8:13; 11:18, 20; 14:7) and the call/answer verbs (καλέω with ὑπακούω at 5:1; 9:16; 13:22; 14:15). Brief note only.

No **Translation-tradition split** in the strict sense (ancient against modern on a weighty rendering where the moderns agree) except **7:20**, where the moderns do *not* agree: the NASB95 follows the Masoretic text, the ESV the Greek and the scribal tradition.

### 9. Tone and Feel

**Two registers in one room, and they never meet.** `[T]` for the devices; `[I]` for the effect.

- **The friends speak in the register of instruction**: calm, general, third person about "the wicked" and "the godless", with conditional promises and nature pictures that work like proverbs (lions, papyrus, a spider's web, a wild donkey's colt). Eliphaz is courteous ("If one ventures a word with you", 4:2); Bildad is impatient ("How long?", 8:2); Zophar is contemptuous ("An idiot will become intelligent when…", 11:12). The friends grow shorter and blunter as the round goes on (Tool 3).
- **Job speaks in the register of lament and litigation**: first person, "my", "my days", "my flesh", and from 7:7 the second person singular to God. His images are of his own body and of God's violence: arrows and poison (6:4), worms and a crust of dirt (7:5), a guard on the sea (7:12), a target (7:20), a lion that hunts (10:16), stocks on the feet (13:27), a garment eaten by moths (13:28).
- **Sarcasm enters with Job's third speech.** "Truly then you are the people, and with you wisdom will die!" (12:2). "But now ask the beasts" (12:7) — a parody of Bildad's "Please inquire of past generations" (8:8). Nothing else in the round is funny.
- **The tone darkens towards the end and then lifts once.** The round's last stanzas are its bleakest — "man lies down and does not rise" (14:12); "So You destroy man's hope" (14:19). Between them stands its one lyric of longing: "Oh that You would hide me in Sheol … You will call, and I will answer You; You will long for the work of Your hands" (14:13–15).
- **Soundtrack.** Three voices in turn reading a statute; a fourth voice, one man, between them, which gets louder and longer each time and finally sings — briefly, in a minor key — before it falls silent at the grave.

**For the sermon.** Do not make the friends sound foolish; they sound wise, which is the danger. Do not make Job sound serene; he is not. Preach the round's ending as it ends — on the grave and on hope destroyed — and let the one lyric of 14:13–15 stand as what it is: a wish Job cannot yet believe.

**The Greek and James 5:11** *(moderate — new)*. "You have heard of the endurance (τὴν ὑπομονὴν) of Job and have seen the outcome of the Lord's dealings" (Jas 5:11, SBLGNT). ### 12. Genre

**Poetic disputation inside a narrative frame** (overview). Within it, the round mixes four sub-genres, and each needs its own reading rule. `[T]` for the forms; `[I]` for the rules.

- **Wisdom instruction** (the friends): general sayings about "the wicked" and "the godless", nature-proverbs, conditional promises. *Rule:* a true general saying can be a false verdict on a particular case. Paul cites 5:13 as Scripture (1 Cor 3:19); God says the friends did not speak rightly (42:7). Both are true.
- **Lament** (Job 6:2–13; 7; 10; 14): complaint addressed to God, with the Psalter's forms — "Remember", "Why?", "How long?", "Turn Your gaze away" (Ps 39:14). *Rule:* lament is speech to God, not a doctrine about him; do not resolve it too quickly.
- **Lawsuit** (9:2–35; 13:3–28): the vocabulary of a court — contend, answer, be in the right, condemn, arbiter, case. *Rule:* Job's legal claims are the claims of a plaintiff, not settled verdicts.
- **Hymn** (5:9–16; 9:5–10; 12:13–25): participial praise of God's acts. *Rule:* the form is praise; the content may be dread. A hymn in Job's mouth can be an accusation (9–10 dig).

**The genre clue the round itself gives.** The friends never pray; Job never stops. The book's verdict (42:7) will reward the one who spoke *to* God over those who spoke *about* him (13–14 dig).

### 14. Bible Timeline

**Where the passage sits.** Job's setting is patriarchal and outside Israel (overview). The round's language, though, is the language of Israel's Scriptures — the Song of Moses, the curses of Deuteronomy, the Psalter, Proverbs, Isaiah, Jeremiah — so the canonical reader hears a Gentile sufferer argued with in Israel's own words. `[T]` / `[I]`

**Canonical position.** Ketuvim, second book in the BHS order (Psalms, Job, Proverbs). The reader of the Writings has the Torah and the Prophets behind him, the Psalter immediately behind, and Proverbs immediately ahead. In this round Job takes up the Psalter (8; 39; 44; 107) and the friends take up Proverbs' sayings (3:11–12; 22:8, 22). The reader meets Proverbs next, after hearing its sayings misapplied here. `[I]` **Where canonical and compositional order diverge** — Job before Proverbs in the canon; the dates of both unknown — the direction of dependence for every shared saying is left open.

**Where the reader sits.** After the cross and resurrection. The question the round ends on — "If a man dies, will he live again?" (14:14) — has been answered (1 Cor 15:20–22). The mediator Job lacked (9:33) has been given (1 Tim 2:5). The hope God "destroys" in 14:19 is the hope a man has in himself and in the friends' formula; the hope of 14:13–15 — to be hidden, remembered and called — is given in Christ (Col 3:3; John 5:28–29). `[I]`

**How that reframes the reading.** The Christian reader must not answer Job too quickly, because Job himself is given no answer in this round, and the book's own answer comes only at 38–42. But he reads 14:13–15 knowing that its wish is not a fantasy. `[I]`

| **Job** | **Representative sufferer**, and a **type** of the righteous sufferer through the servant category | Not "me" as a model of patience. His words become the believer's words in lament; his innocence before the charge belongs to Christ alone (overview, Contrast) |
| **The friends** | **Warning — and mirror** | Every preacher, counsellor and church member is more often Eliphaz than Job: pious, well-read, sure the sufferer must have a reason. The mirror is the point (13–14 dig) |
| **Bildad** in particular | **Warning** | Tradition used to silence grief; the bereaved told their children sinned |
| **Zophar** | **Warning** | The longing for God to speak — against someone else |
| **God** | **Silent, addressed, accused** | He is spoken *of* by the friends and spoken *to* by Job; he answers no one in this round. The reader knows from the prologue what God has said of Job; Job does not |

**Commonly misread as "me":** the friends' promises — "You will laugh at violence" (5:22); "He will yet fill your mouth with laughter" (8:21); "you would trust, because there is hope" (11:18) — quoted as encouragements for believers. They are spoken by men God rebukes, as a verdict on a man God has vindicated.

### 16. So What?

**Stage 1 — the response the round seeks.** `[I]` That the reader, who knows Job is innocent, should **refuse the friends' reading of suffering** while **learning Job's practice of bringing it to God** — and should feel, with Job, how much the sufferer needs God himself to answer, which the round does not supply.

**Stage 2.**

| Domain | Application |
|---|---|
| **Worldview** | Suffering is not a reliable sign of sin. Hope rests on God's call, not on a formula (14:15). God is not one of the friends: he can be argued with, and he will answer |
| **Stop** | Explaining other people's suffering; telling the bereaved what their loved ones did; using true texts (Prov 3:11–12; Ps 107) as verdicts; treating long laments as unbelief |
| **Start** | Kindness to the despairing (6:14); silence before speaking (2:13; 13:5); praying the lament psalms (39; 44; 88) as Job does; speaking *to* God about God |
| **Motivation** | The book's own end: God calls Job "My servant" and the friends wrong (42:7–8); and the gospel, in which the Mediator Job longed for has come and the dead are called and answer (John 5:25, 28–29) |

**Four audiences.**

| Audience | What the round means |
|---|---|
| **For me** | Where have I been Bildad — reaching for the fathers, or for "if you would seek God", when a friend needed kindness? Where do I hold back my own lament from God because it sounds faithless? |
| **For a Christian friend** | The friend who cannot stop asking "why?" may be speaking more rightly than the one with answers. Sit with him; let him pray Ps 39 and Ps 44 |
| **For the church** | A congregation needs a vocabulary of lament as much as of praise. Job's words are in the Bible to be prayed |
| **For an unbeliever** | The Bible does not hide the hardest question about God. It lets a man accuse God at length, and it does not call him an unbeliever for doing so. The answer, when it comes, is a Person, not a formula |

**Prayer.** "Remember that my life is but breath" (7:7); "Oh that You would hide me … and remember me" (14:13) — prayed this side of Easter, where the One who calls has called his own by name (John 10:3; 11:43).

**Grammatical features.**

- **The address shift is grammatical and countable.** The friends use only second masculine singular (Job); Job moves between second masculine plural (the friends) and second masculine singular (God). The shift to God is marked in each speech by an imperative or a direct question: זְכֹר ("Remember", 7:7); תְנַקֵּנִי ("You will not acquit me", 9:28); and the pair of imperatives "do not do … remove … let not" at 13:20–21. `[T]` *High.*
- **The jussives and wishes of 14:13.** מִי יִתֵּן בִּשְׁאוֹל תַּצְפִּנֵנִי תַּסְתִּירֵנִי … תָּשִׁית לִי חֹק וְתִזְכְּרֵנִי ("Oh that You would hide me in Sheol, that You would conceal me … that You would set a limit for me and remember me!"). Five yiqtol verbs after מִי יִתֵּן, each with a first-person suffix: the grammar of longing addressed to God. `[T]` (13–14 dig).
- **Bildad's conditionals** (8:4–6) chain three אִם clauses to one apodosis — "surely now He would rouse Himself for you" (כִּי־עַתָּה יָעִיר עָלֶיךָ, 8:6) — so that restoration depends on Job's purity. BHS notes that one manuscript omits that apodosis (BHS note a–a at 8:6). `[T]`

### Textual Variants

The Hebrew is the text of record. Category 2 variants only (substantive), each placed in the triage; translation losses are under Tools 7–8.

#### 7:20 — "a burden to myself" or "a burden to You"

- **The issue.** The Masoretic text has עָלַי ("upon me, to myself"). BHS notes *Tiq soph* and the Greek reading עָלֶיךָ ("upon You").
- **Evidence.** The Masorah lists 7:20 among the *tiqqune sopherim*, places where the scribes are said to have altered a phrase thought irreverent to God. The Greek (Swete) reads εἰμὶ δὲ ἐπὶ σοὶ φορτίον ("I am a burden upon You"). `[T]` *as BHS prints it*; whether the list records real alterations or a later theory about difficult phrases is debated `[S]`.
- **Position.** Both readings make sense. "To You" fits the line before ("Why have You set me as Your target?") and Job's boldness elsewhere; "to myself" is the harder reading theologically only if one assumes the scribes changed it. **No default either way.** *Moderate* for "to You" as the earlier reading, on the Greek and the scribal note together.
- **Preaching.** The ESV prints "to you"; the NASB95 "to myself". If the verse is used, say that the ESV follows an ancient reading. Nothing in the sermon should rest on either.
- **Apparatus.** A finding about a Masoretic note: `[unchecked — apparatus spread]` for the manuscripts.

#### 6:21; 6:29; 7:1; 7:5 — ketiv and qere

- **6:21** ketiv לא ("not, nothing"), qere לוֹ ("to it"): ESV follows the ketiv, NASB95 the qere (Tool 8). BHS proposes לְאַל ("to me"). *No position taken*; the ESV's reading suits the wadi picture.
- **6:29** ketiv וְשׁוּבִי (feminine singular), qere וְשׁוּבוּ ("desist", plural); BHS reads with the qere. Not load-bearing.
- **7:1** ketiv עַל, qere עֲלֵי ("on"); **7:5** ketiv וְגִישׁ, qere וְגוּשׁ ("crust"). Orthographic. Every chain touching these verses was confirmed by lemma, not by surface search (gate e3).

#### 8:13 — "paths" or "end"

The Greek reads τὰ ἔσχατα ("the end") = אַחֲרִית for אָרְחוֹת ("paths") (BHS). Probably a graphic confusion. *Low* for the Greek reading as original; noted because it repeats 8:7's אַחֲרִית.

#### 9:16 — Swete against Rahlfs

Swete (Vaticanus) negates: "if I call and he does not answer"; Rahlfs and the Hebrew do not. An edition difference within the Greek. Nothing rests on it.

#### 12:4b and 12:9b — the shorter Greek

The Old Greek lacks 12:4's "I am a joke to my friends, the one who called on God and He answered him" and 12:9b's "that the hand of the LORD has done this" (asterisked in Rahlfs). Category 2: the Greek of Job is a shorter edition or a free abbreviation, and the question is unsettled `[S]`. **The Hebrew is the text of record; the two findings that rest on these lines (the call-and-answer link at 12:4; the dialogue's only יְהוָה) are findings about the Hebrew.**

#### 9:33 and 13:15

See the 9–10 and 13–14 digs: 9:33 לֹא / לוּ ("there is no" / "would that"); 13:15 ketiv לא / qere לוֹ ("I have no hope" / "I will hope in Him"). Both are load-bearing for any sermon that quotes them; both carry `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`.

### Historical and Cultural Background

**Setting.** The narrative world is patriarchal and Edomite–Arabian (overview): Uz, Teman, Shuah, Naamah, Tema, Sheba. The period of composition is unknown `[S]`; nothing here depends on it.

**What the first hearers knew.** `[I]` unless marked.

- **Wadis.** The seasonal stream-beds of the desert run full in the rains, swollen with melted snow and ice from the heights (6:16), and are dry in summer (6:17). A caravan that turns aside to a wadi in the dry season and finds nothing may die. Job's picture is not of disappointment but of betrayal with mortal consequences (6:18, "they go up into nothing and perish"). `[T]` for the details in the text.
- **Caravans of Tema and Sheba** (6:19). The incense and trade routes of north and south Arabia; Tema an oasis, Sheba the southern kingdom (Gen 25:3, 15; 1 Kgs 10). `[S]` for the geography.
- **The hired labourer and the conscript** (7:1–2). The day-labourer was paid at evening (Lev 19:13; Deut 24:15) and longed for it; the conscript served out a fixed term. Job's life is both: labour without rest and service without discharge. `[T]` for the Torah references.
- **Papyrus** (8:11). גֹּמֶא grows only in marsh; cut off from water it withers at once. Bildad's picture assumes a hearer who knows Nile or Huleh marsh. `[S]`
- **The gate** (5:4), **the court** (9:32), **the arbiter** (9:33) — the elders' court at the gate, where a case is pleaded before someone who can "lay his hand upon us both". `[S]`
- **The fathers' wisdom** (8:8–10). An appeal to the inherited sayings of earlier generations was the ordinary authority of ancient wisdom. Bildad's argument would have sounded humble and orthodox. `[I]`

**What this changes.** The friends' speeches would have sounded like good, traditional counsel to an ancient hearer, which is exactly why the book must let God rule on them (42:7). And Job's wadi (6:15–20) is a sharper charge than the English suggests: friends who fail a man in his drought leave him to die.

### Original Audience Reception

**Canonical audience.**

- **Section:** Ketuvim. The reader knows the Torah, the Prophets and the Psalter — and so recognises the Song of Moses, the curses, the laments and the Servant in these speeches (Tool 11).
- **What the canonical reader knows that no character knows:** the prologue (1:6–2:7) and God's verdict on Job.
- **Canonical register:** the Psalter behind, Proverbs ahead. The reader hears the psalms of lament in Job's mouth and Proverbs' sayings in the friends'.

**The first hearers.** Israel reading its Writings — likely a people who knew suffering they could not explain from their own sins, and knew the Deuteronomic answer that suffering follows sin. `[I]` *Low* for any specific occasion (overview).

**Where the round fits their world.** They would have recognised the friends' theology as their own: Deuteronomy's curses and blessings, Proverbs' discipline, the Psalter's "the way of the wicked will perish" (Ps 1:6). And they would have recognised Job's language as their own prayers (Ps 39; 44; 88). The round sets their Bible's two voices against each other.

**Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances.**

- **Shocking.** Job's accusations: "You destroy man's hope" (14:19); "He destroys the guiltless and the wicked" (9:22). That such words stand in Scripture, unrebuked until 38:2, would have startled a reader trained in Proverbs.
- **Surprising.** That the friends' Scripture is accurate and their verdict false. That Bildad's appeal to the fathers is answered by an appeal to the beasts.
- **Comforting.** That lament addressed to God is not unbelief; that a man may refuse to confess what he has not done; that the hope of 14:13–15 can be prayed.
- **Disturbing.** 8:4 — for any reader who has lost children. And Zophar's 11:6 — for any who have been told they deserved worse.

**What we bring that they didn't.** A therapeutic instinct that treats lament as a stage to be passed through; a habit of quoting single verses as promises; and, conversely, a resurrection faith that can answer 14:14 too fast.

**Candidate Fallen Condition Focus.**

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **What they felt** | The friends' answer was their own Bible's answer — and it did not fit the sufferer in front of them; and the sufferer's prayers seemed to go unanswered (9:16; 13:24) `[T]` / `[I]` |
| **Candidate FCF (shared concern)** | **When suffering comes, we either reach for an explanation that blames the sufferer, or we lose hope that God will ever answer — and we are tempted to stop speaking to him.** |

**Implication for the sermon.** Name both temptations — to be Eliphaz and to be silent — and show that Job's way, speaking to God when God seems to destroy hope, is the way the book vindicates.

### Biblical-Theological Themes

**(1) Hope and resurrection.**

- **Where this passage sits:** the round ends with a hope for a tree and none for man (14:7–12), and a wish to be hidden in Sheol and remembered (14:13–15).
- **Before:** Gen 3:19 (dust; 10:9); the Psalter's "Will You perform wonders for the dead?" (Ps 88:11).
- **After:** Job's own "I know that my Redeemer lives" (19:25); Isa 26:19 and Dan 12:2, which answer 14:12 with its own verb קיץ ("awake") (sweep; 13–14 dig); John 5:28–29; 11:11, 25; 1 Cor 15. Greek Job 14:14 already says "he will live" (ζήσεται) (13–14 dig).
- **Contribution:** The round shows hope reduced to its last form — the wish that God would call and the dead man answer. That is the precise shape the gospel fulfils: "an hour is coming, in which all who are in the tombs will hear His voice, and will come forth" (John 5:28–29). *High* for the trajectory; *low* for any claim that Job foresaw it.

**(2) The lawsuit and the mediator.**

- **Where this passage sits:** "How can a man be in the right before God?" (4:17; 9:2) and "There is no umpire between us" (9:33), ending with "I know that I will be vindicated" (13:18).
- **After:** 16:19–21; 19:25; 33:23; the Servant whose vindicator is near (Isa 50:8, in Job's mouth at 13:19); Rom 8:33–34, "God is the one who justifies; who is the one who condemns?" — which draws on Isa 50:8 (claim audit 2, C1: a reader's convergence, *moderate*).
- **Contribution:** The round asks the question Paul answers. *High* for the trajectory.

**(3) Lament as faithful speech.**

- **Where this passage sits:** Job prays Ps 39, Ps 44 and Ps 8 against God; the friends never pray.
- **After:** 42:7 ("you have not spoken of Me what is right as My servant Job has"); Christ's own use of the lament psalms (Matt 27:46; Ps 22:2); Heb 5:7.
- **Contribution:** The book's verdict on lament is given in advance to the reader by the prologue and stated at the end; this round is where the lament is spoken.

**Confluence.** *Climactic convergence:* the lawsuit, the hope and the lament meet in 14:13–15, where Job's legal demand ("call, and I will answer", 13:22) becomes a lament's longing ("You will call, and I will answer You", 14:15) whose content is resurrection.

### Christological Reading

**Type of connection:** trajectory (the mediator; resurrection), typology through the servant category, and contrast. For the typology, the four tests: theological category (servant): passes; NT precedent of the same kind (Isa 50:8 applied to Christ in Rom 8:33–34): passes; escalation: passes; authorial pattern — the text uses the Servant's words (13:19, 28) but does not signal a type: fails. **Three of four: moderate.**

**How the round points to Christ.**

- **The answer Job asks for.** "You will call, and I will answer You" (14:15). The God who will not answer in this round answers in the storm (38:1) and finally in the Son, who calls the dead by name (John 11:43; 5:25). `[I]` *Moderate–high* as trajectory.
- **The hope God destroys and the hope God gives.** The round ends on God destroying man's hope (14:19). The New Testament's "living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead" (1 Pet 1:3) is hope that God has made, not one he destroys. `[I]` *Moderate.*
- **The mediator.** 9:33 (9–10 dig): "There is no umpire between us, who may lay his hand upon us both"; 1 Tim 2:5. *High* for the trajectory.
- **The Servant's courtroom.** Job asks the Servant's question (13:19; Isa 50:8) but wears out where the Servant's accusers do (13:28; Isa 50:9). Christ is the Servant who is vindicated (Rom 8:33–34). `[S: audit]` *Moderate–high* for the link; *moderate* for the type.
- **Contrast.** Job is righteous, not sinless: "Have I sinned? What have I done to You?" (7:20); "Why then do You not pardon my transgression?" (7:21). Christ "committed no sin … while being reviled, He did not revile in return" (1 Pet 2:22–23). *High.*

**Moralism check.**

- **The "be like X" temptation:** "Be patient like Job" — the round shows him impatient; or "Don't be like the friends" — which makes the sermon a lecture on bad counsellors.
- **The gospel grounding:** the God who seemed to destroy Job's hope is the God who answers; the Mediator Job lacked is given; the resurrection Job could not believe is accomplished.
- **Christ as hero:** not Job's endurance but the God who calls and the Son who answers for us.

**Confidence:** *high* on the trajectory of mediator and resurrection; *moderate* on the servant type.

### Difficult / Contested Verses

#### 8:4 — "If your sons sinned against Him"

- **Category:** pastoral (weaponised).
- **The difficulty:** Bildad attributes the children's deaths to their sin.
- **Rhetorical function:** the first condition in Bildad's argument for restoration (8:4–6); it answers Job's prologue fear (1:5) — the reader knows otherwise (1:12, 18–19).
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the verse is a friend's verdict, rebuked by God (42:7). It teaches what *not* to say.
- **Handling:** address it directly; never let it be heard as Scripture's word to the bereaved.

#### 6:8–9; 7:15–16; 10:18–19 — the wish for death

- **Category:** pastoral.
- **Rhetorical function:** lament addressed to God, moving across the round towards 14:13 — from "crush me" (6:9) to "hide me … and remember me" (14:13).
- **Handling:** acknowledge that the Bible gives words to the despairing, and that Job speaks them *to God*. Name gently that such thoughts should be spoken to God and to others, and point to the round's own movement towards hope. (If anyone in the room is in that place, the sermon should say plainly that help is available.)

#### 7:20 — the burden

- **Category:** textual (see Textual Variants). Mention only if the verse is read.

#### 9:22–24 (9–10 dig); 14:19 — "You destroy man's hope"

- **Category:** doctrinal / ethical.
- **Rhetorical function:** the round's closing accusation, framed by the hope of 14:13–15.
- **Options:** (a) Job is wrong, and 38:2 rebukes it; (b) Job is speaking honestly of experience, and 42:7 commends his honesty; (c) both — the content is "without knowledge", the address is "right".
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** (c) fits both 38:2 and 42:7.
- **Handling:** preach it as lament; do not turn it into doctrine, and do not soften it.

#### 14:12, 14 — "man lies down and does not rise"; "If a man dies, will he live again?"

- **Category:** apologetic. See the 13–14 dig: Job's denial and question stand; the canon answers (Isa 26:19; Dan 12:2; 1 Cor 15).

#### 13:15 — "Though He slay me"

- **Category:** textual / weaponised. See the 13–14 dig: a qere; preach the whole verse.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: Three sermons in one — "what Eliphaz said, what Bildad said, what Zophar said"

- **What it looks like:** a tour of six speeches in order, each summarised, with a moral at the end.
- **Why it's wrong:** the meaning of the round is in the exchange — who takes up whose words (Headline 3) and where the conversation goes (Headlines 1–2). A tour loses both.
- **The corrective:** preach the round's two threads — hope and call-and-answer — and show the speeches answering one another along them.

### Pitfall: Quoting the friends as Scripture's promises

- **What it looks like:** "Bildad says, 'He will yet fill your mouth with laughter'" (8:21); "Zophar says, 'you would trust, because there is hope'" (11:18), offered as encouragement.
- **Why it's wrong:** these are verdicts on Job's case by men God rebukes (42:7). In this very round Job answers 8:21 with "I am a joke to my friends" (12:4) and 11:18 with "So You destroy man's hope" (14:19).
- **The corrective:** quote every line with its speaker, and with the line that answers it.

### Pitfall: Making the friends fools

- **What it looks like:** caricature — three pompous men who obviously have it wrong.
- **Why it's wrong:** they speak the Song of Moses, Proverbs and the Psalter accurately (Headline 5). Their error is the application, not the vocabulary. A congregation that laughs at them will not see itself in them.
- **The corrective:** let the friends sound wise, then show where the wisdom fails the man in front of them — 8:4 above all.

### Pitfall: "Job was patient" — or "Job lost his faith"

- **What it looks like:** either smoothing Job into calm endurance, or treating chapters 6–14 as a fall from the faith of 1:21.
- **Why it's wrong:** Job speaks *to* God throughout (Tool 3) and God will call his speech "right" (42:7). His faith is shown in the address, not in calm. James's ὑπομονή is the holding on, not the serenity (Tool 11).
- **The corrective:** present lament addressed to God as faith in its hardest form.

### Pitfall: Answering 14:14 too fast

- **What it looks like:** "Job asks, 'If a man dies, will he live again?' — and Jesus says yes!" in the first five minutes.
- **Why it's wrong:** the round ends on hope destroyed (14:19) and the grave (14:22). The book's answer comes at 38–42 and the canon's at the resurrection. Rushing to it robs the hearers of the question.
- **The corrective:** let the question and the longing of 14:13–15 stand; bring the gospel as the answer to a question the congregation now feels.

### Pitfall: Using 8:4 or 11:6 near a grieving family

- **What it looks like:** any application suggesting that suffering or bereavement reveals hidden sin.
- **Why it's wrong:** 1:5, 8; 2:3; 42:7. This is the book's central warning.
- **The corrective:** name the verses as the error the book exposes.
