## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims, each surfaced by several tools:

1. **Job stops speaking *about* God to the friends and starts speaking *to* God — and charges the friends with speaking *for* him.** `[T]`
   - **The prepositions.** "But I would speak to (אֶל, "to") the Almighty, and I desire to argue with (אֶל) God" (13:3). "Will you speak what is unjust for God (הַלְאֵל, "for God"), and speak what is deceitful for Him (וְלוֹ, "for him")? … Will you contend for God (לָאֵל)?" (13:7–8). Job speaks *to* God; the friends speak *for* him. אֱלִל ("worthless", 13:4), elsewhere the Bible's word for idols, sits between the two (Tool 7).
   - **The address.** The speech turns at 13:20. From 13:2 to 13:17 there are 23 second-person plural forms, addressed to the friends; 13:18–19 have none; from 13:20 to 14:20 there are 41 second-person singular forms, addressed to God, and no plural at all (WLC morphology). The friends are never addressed again in this speech.
   - **The court.** יכח ("argue, reprove") at 13:3, 10 (×2), 15; רִיב ("contention", "contend") at 13:6, 8, 19; מִשְׁפָּט ("case, judgement") at 13:18; 14:3; צדק ("be in the right") at 13:18 (lemma). "I have prepared my case; I know that I will be vindicated" (13:18) reverses 9:2, "In truth I know that this is so; but how can a man be in the right before God?" ידע ("know") with צדק in one verse occurs only at Gen 38:26; Job 9:2; 13:18 (lemma).
   - **The book's verdict takes up both halves.** Job warns that God will "surely reprove" those who "show partiality" (נשׂא פָּנִים, literally "lift the face", 13:8, 10) for him. At the end God's wrath is kindled against the friends, and he says of Job, "I will accept him (פָּנָיו אֶשָּׂא, literally "I will lift his face")" (42:8), "because you have not spoken of Me (אֵלַי, literally "to me") what is right, as My servant Job has" (42:7–8). The preposition is the one of 13:3; the English versions render it "of". Job's charge against the friends at 13:7, עַוְלָה ("what is unjust") with רְמִיָּה ("deceit"), becomes his own oath at 27:4 — the only other verse with the pair (lemma).
   - *Surfaced by:* Original Languages (the address test), Structure, Vocabulary, Linking Words, Move 4. *High.* The introductory-sermon backbone rests its second movement on 13:3, 7–8; this confirms it with the morphology.

2. **Job pleads his case in the words of Isaiah's Servant (Isa 50:6–9) — but he has no helper, and it is he who wears out like a moth-eaten garment.** `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the inversion
   - **The courtroom question.** "Who will contend (יָרִיב, "contend") with me?" (13:19) // "Who will contend (יָרִיב) with Me?" (Isa 50:8). מִי ("who?") with רִיב in one verse occurs only at these two verses in the Hebrew Bible (lemma). Isa 50:8 also holds צדק ("vindicate") and מִשְׁפָּט ("judgment": "Who has a case against Me?"); Job 13:18–19 holds the same four words across two verses. And Job's מִי־הוּא ("who is he who …?") with a first-person object is the form of Isa 50:9, "Who is he who condemns Me?" (מִי־הוּא יַרְשִׁיעֵנִי).
   - **The confidence.** "I know (יָדַעְתִּי) that I will be vindicated (אֶצְדָּק)" (13:18) // "I know (וָאֵדַע) that I will not be ashamed. He who vindicates (מַצְדִּיקִי) Me is near" (Isa 50:7–8).
   - **The moth.** "Like a garment (כְּבֶגֶד) that is moth-eaten (אֲכָלוֹ עָשׁ)" — "he wastes away" (יִבְלֶה) (13:28) // "they will all wear out (יִבְלוּ) like a garment (כַּבֶּגֶד); the moth (עָשׁ) will eat them" (Isa 50:9). בלה ("wear out") with בֶּגֶד ("garment") and עָשׁ ("moth") in one verse occurs only at these two verses (lemma).
   - **The face.** The Servant "did not cover (לֹא הִסְתַּרְתִּי) My face" (Isa 50:6); Job asks why God "hides (תַסְתִּיר) [His] face" (13:24) and promises "I will not hide (אֶסָּתֵר) from Your face" (13:20).
   - **The inversion.** The Servant has a vindicator "near" and says twice, "the Lord GOD helps (יַעֲזָר) Me" (Isa 50:7, 9); his accusers wear out. Job has a case and no helper, and it is Job who wears out like the garment. The Greek keeps the link: Swete's Job 13:19 τίς … ὁ κριθησόμενός μοι against Isa 50:8 τίς ὁ κρινόμενός μοι; Job 13:28 ἱμάτιον σητόβρωτον ("a moth-eaten garment") against Isa 50:9 ὡς ἱμάτιον … σής.
   - **Psalm 44 sits beside it.** Job 13:24, "Why do You hide Your face?", is word for word Ps 44:25 [Eng 44:24], the lament of the faithful who have "not forgotten" God (Ps 44:18 [Eng 44:17]). Paul's climax in Rom 8:33–36 uses both texts — Isa 50:8–9 ("God is the one who justifies; who is the one who condemns?") `[S]` and Ps 44:23 [Eng 44:22] by quotation (SBLGNT).
   - *Surfaced by:* Tool 11 (triad), Vocabulary, Translations (the Greek), Christological Reading. *High on the data; moderate–high for a deliberate link; the direction of dependence is open* (Tool 11). **Load-bearing and unaudited** — see the claim-audit note at the end.

3. **"If a man dies, will he live again?" (14:14). Job gives the tree a hope he denies to man, and asks the question in the words the canon later uses to answer it.** `[T]` for the words; trajectory `[I]`
   - **The tree.** "There is hope (תִּקְוָה, "hope") for a tree, when it is cut down, that it will sprout again (יַחֲלִיף, "sprout again")" (14:7). Its root (שֹׁרֶשׁ) grows old and its stump (גִּזְעוֹ, "its stump") dies in the dust (14:8). גֶּזַע ("stump") with שֹׁרֶשׁ ("root") in one verse occurs only at Job 14:8 and Isa 11:1, "a shoot will spring from the stem (מִגֵּזַע) of Jesse, and a branch from his roots (מִשָּׁרָשָׁיו)" (lemma).
   - **The man.** "So man lies down and does not rise (לֹא־יָקוּם). Until the heavens are no longer, he will not awake (לֹא יָקִיצוּ)" (14:12). The phrase לֹא יָקִיצוּ ("they will not awake") occurs elsewhere only at Jer 51:39, 57, "sleep a perpetual sleep and not wake up" (phrase search, confirmed by lemma).
   - **The answer in his own words.** קוּם ("rise") with קיץ ("awake") meets in one verse only at Hab 2:7, Isa 26:19 and Job 14:12; קיץ with עָפָר ("dust") only at Isa 26:19 and Dan 12:2 (lemma). Isa 26:19: "Your dead (מֵתֶיךָ) will live (יִחְיוּ); their corpses will rise (יְקוּמוּן). You who lie in the dust (עָפָר), awake (הָקִיצוּ) and shout for joy". Dan 12:2: "Many of those who sleep in the dust of the ground will awake (יָקִיצוּ)" — the very form Job negates. Job's question has מות ("die") and חיה ("live") in one verse, the only such verse in Job (14:14); Isa 26:19 answers with both.
   - **Renewal.** The tree "will sprout again" (חלף, 14:7); Job will wait "until my change (חֲלִיפָתִי, "my change, renewal") comes" (14:14) — one root. The tree's word becomes the man's hope.
   - **The Greek answers the question.** Swete's 14:14 reads "If a man dies, he will live (ζήσεται) … I will wait until I come to be again (ἕως πάλιν γένωμαι)" — and this line is Old Greek, not asterisked in the Rahlfs export. The asterisked line at 14:12 has οὐκ ἐξυπνισθήσονται ἐξ ὕπνου ("they will not be awakened from sleep"); ἐξυπνίζω occurs in the New Testament only at John 11:11, of Lazarus.
   - *Surfaced by:* Parallels, Vocabulary, Repetition, Tool 11, Translations, Christological Reading. *High on the words; moderate on whether Job is reaching beyond the grave* — his own answer in 14:18–22 is no.

4. **The face hidden, the man hidden: Job's one sustained hope is that God would hide him, remember him, and cover his sin (14:13–17).** `[T]` for the words; the grammar `[I]`
   - **The verb turns three times.** סתר ("hide"): "I will not hide (אֶסָּתֵר) from Your face" (13:20); "Why do You hide (תַסְתִּיר) Your face?" (13:24); "Oh that You would … conceal me (תַּסְתִּירֵנִי) until Your wrath returns" (14:13). Job will not hide from God; God hides from Job; Job asks God to hide him.
   - **The limit turns.** חֹק ("limit"): the bound God sets "so that he cannot pass" (14:5) becomes "set a limit (חֹק) for me and remember me" (14:13). The barrier becomes an appointment.
   - **The sin is named in the Exodus words.** "How many are my iniquities (עֲוֹנוֹת) and sins (חַטָּאוֹת)? Make known to me my rebellion (פִּשְׁעִי) and my sin (חַטָּאתִי)" (13:23). The three words of Exod 34:7 — "who forgives iniquity, transgression and sin" — stand together in one verse at Exod 34:7; Lev 16:21; Ps 32:5; Isa 59:12; Ezek 21:29; Dan 9:24; Job 13:23 (lemma).
   - **The hope is forgiveness.** "For then (כִּי־עַתָּה) You would number my steps; You would not keep watch over my sin; my transgression would be sealed up in a bag, and You would cover over my iniquity" (14:16–17, ESV). In Job, כִּי־עַתָּה after a wish or a condition introduces the unreal consequence (6:3; 8:6; 13:19; and 3:13 after the "why"s), and 14:13 opens with the wish formula מִי יִתֵּן ("Oh that"). The NASB95 instead reads present surveillance ("For now You number my steps … You wrap up my iniquity"), as does Swete. *Moderate–high* for the ESV's reading. 7:21 had asked, "Why then do You not pardon my transgression (פִשְׁעִי) and take away my iniquity (עֲוֹנִי)?"; 14:17 has the same two nouns with the same suffix, and imagines the answer.
   - **The friends' plaster and God's.** טפל ("smear, plaster") occurs only at 13:4 ("you smear with lies"), 14:17 ("and You plaster over my iniquity", literally) and Ps 119:69 (lemma). The friends plaster lies over the truth; Job hopes God will plaster over his sin.
   - *Surfaced by:* Linking Words, Vocabulary, Translations, Repetition, Tool 11, Move 4. *High on the words.*

5. **"You destroy man's hope" (14:19). Job takes the friends' sentence on the wicked and makes God its subject and mankind its object — and the whole speech closes in flesh and soul.** `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the effect
   - **Hope perishing.** תִּקְוָה ("hope") with אבד ("perish") meets in Job only at 8:13 (Bildad: "the hope of the godless will perish"), 11:20 (Zophar, the last line of the speech Job is answering: "their hope is to breathe their last"), and 14:19, "So You destroy (הֶאֱבַדְתָּ) man's hope" (lemma). Bildad and Zophar said the wicked lose hope. Job says God destroys the hope of אֱנוֹשׁ ("man, mortal") — anyone.
   - **The tree replanted.** תִּקְוָה with עֵץ ("tree") occurs only at 14:7 and 19:10, "He has uprooted my hope like a tree" (lemma). What Job grants the tree here he will say God has torn up in him.
   - **The inclusio.** נֶפֶשׁ ("life, soul") with בָּשָׂר ("flesh") occurs in Job only at 12:10, 13:14 and 14:22 (lemma) — the opening, middle and close of Job's reply. God holds "the life of every living thing" in his hand (12:10); Job takes "my flesh in my teeth and puts my life in my hand" (13:14); the speech ends, "his flesh (בְּשָׂרוֹ) pains him, and his soul (וְנַפְשׁוֹ) mourns" (14:22). These are the two things the Accuser asked to touch: "his bone and his flesh (בְּשָׂרוֹ)" (2:5) and "his life (נַפְשׁוֹ)" (2:6).
   - *Surfaced by:* Structure, Repetition, Move 4, Tone. *High on the data; moderate on reading 14:22 against 2:5–6.*

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book level (overview, confirmed).** The book shows a righteous man who will neither curse God nor buy relief by confessing sins he did not commit, exposes the friends' tidy theology as speaking wrongly of God (42:7), and brings the sufferer to a fear of God that wants God himself. Chapters 13–14 supply the clearest single text in the dialogue for the overview's reading of 42:7: right speech is speech *to* God, not speech *for* him (Headline 1). `[I]`

**Unit level.** 13:1–14:22 does four things. `[T]` / `[I]`
- **It dismisses the friends as God's advocates.** They speak "for God" (13:7–8), smear lies (13:4), and are "worthless physicians" (13:4). Job warns them that the God they defend will examine them (13:9–11). `[T]`
- **It makes the lawsuit real.** In 9–10 Job imagined a trial and found it impossible. Here he announces that he has "prepared my case" (13:18), asks for two conditions (13:20–21), offers the procedure — "call, and I will answer; or let me speak, then reply to me" (13:22) — and asks for the charge sheet (13:23). `[T]`
- **It sets man's brevity against God's scrutiny.** 14:1–6: a man born of woman, short-lived, a flower, a shadow — and God opens his eyes on such a one and brings him to judgement (14:3). `[T]`
- **It asks the question of life after death, and lets it go.** The tree's hope (14:7–9), man's sleep (14:10–12), the wish to be hidden in Sheol and remembered (14:13–17), and then the erosion of all hope (14:18–22). `[T]` for the movement; `[I]` that the wish is the speech's high point.

**Selection.** Job says nothing about:
- his children (except "his sons", 14:21, in the general third person);
- the specific sins the friends have hinted at (8:4; 11:6, 14);
- any sin he could name — he asks God to name it (13:23).

He speaks to the friends about how they speak, and then to God about the trial, man's frailty and death. `[T]` for the absences; `[I]` for the effect.

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement.** `[T]`
- **Zophar (11)** has said that Job's talk silences men (יַחֲרִישׁוּ, 11:3), that God exacts less than Job's guilt deserves (11:6), that God would speak against Job if he would only open his lips (11:5, the wish formula מִי־יִתֵּן), and that if Job puts iniquity away "you would trust, because there is hope (תִּקְוָה)" (11:18). His last line is the wicked man's fate: "their hope (וְתִקְוָתָם) is to breathe their last" (11:20).
- **Job (12)** has opened his reply by mocking their wisdom ("Truly then you are the people, and with you wisdom will die!", 12:2), said "I am not inferior to you" (12:3), and given a catalogue of God's overturning power (12:13–25), with "In whose hand is the life of every living thing" (12:10).
- **Round one as a whole** has given each friend his turn (4–5; 8; 11). Job's speech in 9–10 found no court and no umpire (9:33) and ended asking to be left alone (10:20).

**Necessity answer.** 13–14 must come here, at the end of round one, and must be Job's. `[I]`
- **Zophar's silencing needs its answer.** He said Job's talk silences men (11:3); Job tells the friends to be silent (13:5, 13). He wished God would speak (11:5); Job wishes the friends would be silent (13:5) and asks God to call (13:22), with the same wish formula.
- **Zophar's hope needs its answer.** He promised hope to the repentant and its loss to the wicked (11:18, 20). Job grants hope to a tree (14:7) and says God destroys the hope of man (14:19).
- **9:2's question needs its sequel.** "How can a man be in the right before God?" (9:2) becomes "I know that I will be vindicated" (13:18).
- **9:34 needs to be prayed.** "Let not dread of Him terrify me" (9:34) becomes "let not the dread of You terrify me" (13:21) — the same nouns and verb, now addressed to God (9–10 report, Headline 5).
- **The round needs a close.** Each round of the dialogue ends with Job. This speech ends round one with death, as chapter 3 began the dialogue with death (Tool 3).

**Implication.** 13–14 is where Job abandons the friends as a court and turns to God as both judge and only hope. A sermon on these chapters must hold the two halves together: the defiance of 13:13–19 and the plea of 14:13–17 are addressed to the same God. `[I]`

#### Surrounding context

- **Before:** chapter 12, the first third of this reply. Its "I am not inferior to you" (12:3) is repeated at 13:2 (the phrase לֹא־נֹפֵל אָנֹכִי מִכֶּם occurs only at these two verses — phrase search; BHS cross-refers them). Its catalogue of God's power (12:13–25) is what Job's eye has "seen" (13:1).
- **After:** Eliphaz opens round two (15). He picks up "born of woman" (15:14; Headline 3, Tool 11 Move 4), charges, "you do away with reverence" (15:4), and says "your own mouth condemns you" (15:6).
- **Section:** the end of round one of the dialogue (4–14). This is Job's third reply.
- **Masoretic paragraphing:** one paragraph, 12:1–14:22, with a petuḥah after 11:20 and after 14:22 (Leningrad, as BHS prints it). The chapter divisions at 13:1 and 14:1 are not Masoretic breaks. `[T]`; `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`.

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Mode | Key markers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13:1–12 | **I. You speak for God; I would speak to him** | to the friends (2nd plural) | "my eye has seen … my ear has heard" (13:1); אוּלָם ("but") ×2 (13:3, 4); "for God" (13:7–8); the questions of 13:7–11 |
| 13:13–19 | **II. Let come on me what may** | to the friends, then to no one | "Be silent before me so that I may speak" (13:13); "Though He slay me" (13:15); "I have prepared my case" (13:18); "Who will contend with me?" (13:19) |
| 13:20–28 | **III. Two things, and the charge sheet** | to God (2nd singular) | "Only two things do not do to me" (13:20); "call, and I will answer" (13:22); "Why do You hide Your face?" (13:24); the moth (13:28) |
| 14:1–6 | **IV. Man, born of woman** | about man, to God | "Man, who is born of woman" (14:1); "You also open Your eyes on him" (14:3); "Turn Your gaze from him" (14:6) |
| 14:7–17 | **V. Hope for a tree — and for me?** | about the tree and man; then to God | "there is hope for a tree" (14:7); "man lies down and does not rise" (14:12); "Oh that You would hide me" (14:13); "You will call, and I will answer You" (14:15) |
| 14:18–22 | **VI. You destroy man's hope** | about man, to God | אוּלָם ("But", 14:18); "You destroy man's hope" (14:19); "his flesh … his soul" (14:22) |

`[T]` for every marker (lemma and morphology).

**Stanza lengths.** 12, 7, 9, 6, 11, 5 verses. The ESV paragraphs fall at 13:1, 13:13, 14:1, 14:7 and 14:18; it does not break at 13:20, where the address changes. `[T]`

**Device: two speeches in one, hinged on silence.** `[T]` for the markers; `[I]` for the design.
- **The first half (I–II)** is to the friends and ends with "For then I would be silent (אַחֲרִישׁ) and die" (13:19).
- **The second half (III–VI)** is to God and ends with death described in the third person (14:20–22).
- **חרשׁ ("be silent")** marks the joints: "O that you would be completely silent (הַחֲרֵשׁ תַּחֲרִישׁוּן)" (13:5); "Be silent (הַחֲרִישׁוּ) before me so that I may speak" (13:13); "For then I would be silent (אַחֲרִישׁ) and die" (13:19). The friends must be silent so that Job can speak; Job will be silent only if someone can contend with him.

**Two matching appeals.** "Then call, and I will answer (וּקְרָא וְאָנֹכִי אֶעֱנֶה)" (13:22) and "You will call, and I will answer You (תִּקְרָא וְאָנֹכִי אֶעֱנֶךָּ)" (14:15) stand at the head of the second half and at its high point. The first is a demand for a hearing; the second is a longing to be called back. `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the shift. *High.*

**The arc of hope.** The second half runs: God's face hidden (13:24) → man's brevity (14:1–6) → the tree's hope (14:7–9) → man's sleep (14:10–12) → the wish (14:13–17) → hope destroyed (14:18–22). The two תִּקְוָה ("hope") verses, 14:7 and 14:19, bracket the stanzas on the tree and man. `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the bracket.

**Bookend with chapter 3, and the end of a round.** The dialogue began with Job wishing he had "lain down (שָׁכַבְתִּי) … slept (יָשַׁנְתִּי)" in death (3:13). Round one ends with "man lies down (שָׁכַב) and does not rise … nor be aroused out of his sleep (מִשְּׁנָתָם)" (14:12). Both verses join שׁכב ("lie down") to a word for sleep, and both are Job's. In chapter 3 the sleep of death was rest; here it is the sleep from which no one wakes — and Job wishes for a waking. `[T]` for the words (lemma: שׁכב with יָשֵׁן in Job only at 3:13, with שֵׁנָה only at 14:12); `[I]` for the bookend. *Moderate–high.*

### 4. Linking Words

- **הֵן ("behold", 13:1, 15) and הִנֵּה ("behold", 13:18).** Three exclamations mark the speech's claims: what Job has seen (13:1), what God may do to him (13:15), and what Job has done (13:18). `[T]`
- **אוּלָם ("but, however", 13:3, 4; 14:18).** The strong adversative. "But I would speak to the Almighty" (13:3) sets Job against the friends' knowledge; "But you smear with lies" (13:4) sets the friends against Job's intent; "But the falling mountain crumbles away" (14:18) sets erosion against the hope just voiced. The third אוּלָם undoes the wish of 14:13–17. `[T]` for the word; `[I]` for the effect.
- **The rhetorical questions of 13:7–11.** Introduced by הַ ("will you?", 13:7, 8, 9, 11) and paired with אִם ("or", 13:8, 9). Each asks the friends whether advocacy for God will survive God's own examination. `[T]`
- **אִם ("if", 13:10; 14:5, 7, 8, 14).** "He will surely reprove you if (אִם) you secretly show partiality" (13:10). In chapter 14 the conditionals carry the tree and the man: "if it is cut down" (14:7), "though its roots grow old" (14:8), "If a man dies" (14:14). `[T]`
- **The questions with מָה ("what?", 13:13, 14, 23, 24).** "Let come on me what (מָה) may" (13:13); "Why (עַל־מָה) should I take my flesh in my teeth?" (13:14); "How many (כַּמָּה) are my iniquities?" (13:23); "Why (לָמָּה) do You hide Your face?" (13:24). The question of 13:24 is the one "why" addressed to God in the speech. `[T]`
- **הֵן יִקְטְלֵנִי ("Though He slay me", 13:15).** הֵן here is concessive ("even if, though") or presentative ("behold, He will slay me"). The decision belongs with the ketiv/qere at the same verse (Textual Variants; Difficult Verses). `[T]` for the word; the force is contested.
- **גַּם־הוּא ("This also", 13:16).** "This also will be my salvation": the הוּא ("this, it") points back to the act of arguing before God's face (13:15), or forward to the reason "for a godless man may not come before His presence". The ESV reads it as forward: "This will be my salvation, that the godless shall not come before him." `[T]` for the words; the reference `[I]`.
- **כִּי־עַתָּה ("for then / for now", 13:19; 14:16).** In Job the phrase introduces the consequence of a wish or condition at 6:3, 8:6 and 13:19 (and at 3:13 after the "why"s); at 4:5, 6:21 and 7:21 it means "for now, as things are" (phrase search). At 13:19 the sense is unreal: "For then I would be silent and die". At 14:16 it follows the wish of 14:13 (מִי יִתֵּן, "Oh that"), so the unreal sense is the more natural (Headline 4). `[T]` for the data; `[I]` for 14:16. *Moderate–high.*
- **אָז ("then", 13:20).** "Only two things do not do to me, then (אָז) I will not hide from Your face." The condition of Job's appearing in court. `[T]`
- **כִּי ("for", 14:7).** "For there is hope for a tree" — a new start, contrasting the tree with the man of 14:1–6. `[T]`
- **וְ ("but", 14:10, 12).** "But man (וְגֶבֶר) dies" (14:10); "So man (וְאִישׁ) lies down" (14:12). The conjunction sets man against the tree. `[T]`
- **אַךְ ("only, surely", 13:15, 20; 14:22).** "Nevertheless (אַךְ) I will argue my ways before Him" (13:15); "Only (אַךְ) two things do not do to me" (13:20); "But (אַךְ) his body pains him" (14:22). The restrictive particle marks the turns. `[T]`

### 5. Parallels

- **13:1 — synonymous.** "My eye has seen all this, my ear has heard and understood it." עַיִן ("eye"), אֹזֶן ("ear"), ראה ("see") and שׁמע ("hear") meet in one verse in Job only at 13:1, 29:11 and 42:5 (lemma; Move 4). `[T]`
- **13:4 — synonymous, with the metaphor of the physician.** "You smear with lies (טֹפְלֵי־שָׁקֶר); you are all worthless physicians (רֹפְאֵי אֱלִל)." The physicians plaster rather than heal. `[T]`
- **13:7–8 — four synonymous questions in two verses.** Speak unjustly for God // speak deceit for him; show partiality for him // contend for God. `[T]`
- **13:12 — synonymous, sarcastic.** "Your memorable sayings are proverbs of ashes (אֵפֶר); your defences are defences of clay (חֹמֶר)." אֵפֶר with חֹמֶר meets only here and at 30:19, where Job says God "has cast me into the mire (לַחֹמֶר), and I have become like dust and ashes (וָאֵפֶר)" (lemma). The material he mocks in their arguments becomes what he says he is. `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the irony.
- **13:14 — synonymous.** "Take my flesh in my teeth // put my life in my hands." Both idioms mean risking one's life. נֶפֶשׁ ("life") with כַּף ("palm") and שׂים ("put") occurs at Judg 12:3; 1 Sam 19:5; 28:21 and here (lemma), always of someone who risked death. `[T]`
- **13:25 — synonymous.** "Will You cause a driven leaf (עָלֶה נִדָּף) to tremble? Or will You pursue (תִּרְדֹּף) the dry chaff?" עָלֶה נִדָּף occurs only here and at Lev 26:36, which also has רדף ("pursue") (Tool 11). `[T]`
- **13:27 — synthetic, three lines.** Stocks, watched paths, marked soles. 33:11 quotes the first two lines (Move 4). `[T]`
- **14:1–2 — the brevity of man in four images.** Born of woman; short of days, full of turmoil; a flower that withers; a shadow that flees. `[T]`
- **14:7–9 against 14:10–12 — an extended antithetical comparison.** The tree has hope; it sprouts again; its shoots do not fail; at the scent of water it flourishes. "But man dies … expires, and where is he?" (14:10). The two halves are linked by death: the tree's stump "dies" (יָמוּת, 14:8); man "dies" (יָמוּת, 14:10). `[T]`
- **14:11–12 — a simile across two verses.** Water vanishing from the sea and a river drying up // a man who lies down and does not rise. The phrase וְנָהָר יֶחֱרַב וְיָבֵשׁ ("and a river becomes parched and dried up") occurs elsewhere only at Isa 19:5 (Tool 11). `[T]`
- **14:18–19 — four images of erosion, then the point.** Mountain, rock, stones, soil — "So You destroy man's hope." `[T]`
- **14:21 — antithetical.** "His sons achieve honour, but he does not know it; or they become insignificant, but he does not perceive it." `[T]`
- **14:22 — synonymous.** "His flesh pains him // his soul mourns for him." `[T]`

### 6. Narrator's Comment

**N/A for the speech itself** — 13:1–14:22 is character speech with no narrative intrusion. The narrative formula stands at 12:1 ("Then Job responded"). `[T]`

**What the narrator has already said stands over the speech.** The narrator has told the reader that the Accuser asked to touch Job's "bone and his flesh" (2:5) and was forbidden his "life" (2:6). When Job takes "my flesh in my teeth and puts my life in my hands" (13:14) and ends "his flesh pains him, and his soul mourns" (14:22), the reader knows who has had his flesh and who has guarded his life (Headline 5). When Job warns the friends that God will "reprove" those who show partiality for him (13:10), the reader will see it happen: "My wrath is kindled against you … because you have not spoken of Me what is right" (42:7). `[T]` for the texts; `[I]` for their bearing.

### 7. Vocabulary

**(a) The friends' words, and Job's words for them.** `[T]` (lemma, WLC)
- **אֱלִל ("worthless", 13:4).** "Worthless physicians (רֹפְאֵי אֱלִל)". The word occurs 17 times in the Hebrew Bible, only here in Job; everywhere else it means "idols" or "worthless gods" (Lev 19:4; 26:1; Ps 96:5, "all the gods of the peoples are idols"; Isa 2:8, 18, 20; Hab 2:18) (lemma). Set between "I would argue with God (אֵל)" (13:3) and "Will you speak … for God (לָאֵל)" (13:7–8), it sounds like a pun: the friends' אֵל is an אֱלִל. *High on the data; moderate on the play* (a sound-play with corroboration from the argument of 13:7–8).
- **טפל ("smear, plaster over", 13:4; 14:17).** Three occurrences in the Hebrew Bible: Job 13:4; 14:17; Ps 119:69, "The arrogant have forged a lie (שֶׁקֶר) against me" (lemma). Ps 119:69 and Job 13:4 both join the verb to שֶׁקֶר ("lie"). *High.*
- **רֹפֵא ("physician", 13:4).** In Job only at 5:18 (Eliphaz: "He wounds, and His hands also heal (תִּרְפֶּינָה)") and 13:4. Eliphaz promised that God heals; Job says the friends are the healers, and worthless ones. `[T]` for the forms (lemma, 7495); `[I]` for the answer. *Moderate.*
- **חרשׁ ("be silent") with חָכְמָה ("wisdom").** "O that you would be completely silent, and that it would become your wisdom" (13:5). The two words meet only here and at 33:33, where Elihu turns them on Job: "Keep silent (הַחֲרֵשׁ), and I will teach you wisdom (חָכְמָה)" (lemma). Prov 17:28 makes the proverb with the adjective: "Even a fool, when he keeps silent, is considered wise." *High.*
- **עַוְלָה ("injustice") with רְמִיָּה ("deceit", 13:7).** Only here and at 27:4, Job's oath: "My lips certainly will not speak unjustly, nor will my tongue mutter deceit" (lemma). What Job charges the friends with, he swears he will not do. *High.*
- **נשׂא פָּנִים ("show partiality", literally "lift the face", 13:8, 10).** In Job at 11:15 (Zophar: "you could lift up your face"); 13:8, 10; 22:8, 26; 32:21 (Elihu: "Let me now be partial to no one"); 34:19; 42:8, 9 (lemma co-occurrence). At 34:19 Elihu joins the idiom to "the work of His hands" (14:15); at 42:8–9 God "lifts Job's face" (Headline 1). *High.*
- **התל ("deceive, mock", 13:9).** Nine verses in the Hebrew Bible, among them Laban changing Jacob's wages (Gen 31:7), Delilah's complaint (Judg 16:10, 13, 15) and Elijah's mockery of Baal (1 Kgs 18:27) (lemma). One does not deceive God as one deceives a man. *Moderate.*
- **שְׂאֵת ("majesty", 13:11).** In Job at 13:11; 31:23 ("because of His majesty I can do nothing"); 41:17 [Eng 41:25] (Leviathan: "When he raises himself up, the mighty fear") (lemma). Job warns the friends of the majesty that will make him confess (31:23) and that God displays in Leviathan. *Moderate.*

**(b) The lawsuit and the resolve.** `[T]`
- **יכח ("argue, reprove", 13:3, 10 ×2, 15).** Job "desires to argue (וְהוֹכֵחַ) with God" (13:3); God "will surely reprove (הוֹכֵחַ יוֹכִיחַ) you" (13:10); Job "will argue (אוֹכִיחַ) my ways before Him" (13:15). Its noun תּוֹכַחַת ("argument") is at 13:6; with ערך מִשְׁפָּט ("prepare a case", Tool 11 Move 4), 23:4 repeats both: "I would present my case (מִשְׁפָּט) before Him and fill my mouth with arguments (תוֹכָחוֹת)". The participle is the מוֹכִיחַ ("umpire") of 9:33 (9–10 report). *High.*
- **ערך מִשְׁפָּט ("prepare a case", 13:18).** Only at 13:18 and 23:4 (lemma co-occurrence). *High.*
- **קטל ("slay", 13:15).** Three occurrences: Job 13:15; 24:14 ("The murderer arises at dawn; he kills the poor and the needy"); Ps 139:19 ("O that You would slay the wicked, O God") (lemma). An Aramaic-coloured word for killing. *High on the data.*
- **יחל ("wait, hope", 13:15; 14:14).** In Job at 6:11 ("What is my strength, that I should wait (אֲיַחֵל)?"); 13:15; 14:14; 29:21, 23; 30:26; 32:11, 16 (lemma). The form אֲיַחֵל ("I will wait") stands at 6:11, 13:15 and 14:14. In 6:11 Job had no strength to wait; at 14:14 he would wait "all the days of my struggle". At 13:15 the ketiv makes it a denial (Textual Variants). *High.*
- **יְשׁוּעָה ("salvation", 13:16).** In Job only at 13:16 and 30:15, "my prosperity (יְשֻׁעָתִי) has passed away like a cloud" (lemma). *Moderate.*
- **חָנֵף ("godless", 13:16).** Eight times in Job (8:13; 13:16; 15:34; 17:8; 20:5; 27:8; 34:30; 36:13) and five elsewhere (lemma). Bildad introduced it: "the hope of the godless will perish" (8:13). Job turns it round: the godless cannot come before God, so Job's very willingness to stand there is his salvation (13:16). *High on the data.*

**(c) The charge sheet.** `[T]`
- **עָוֹן ("iniquity"), חַטָּאת ("sin"), פֶּשַׁע ("transgression").** 13:23 holds all three (Headline 4). עָוֹן also at 13:26 and 14:17; חַטָּאת at 14:16; פֶּשַׁע at 14:17. פֶּשַׁע with עָוֹן in one verse occurs in Job at 7:21; 13:23; 14:17; 31:33; 33:9 (lemma).
- **עֲוֹנוֹת נְעוּרָי ("the iniquities of my youth", 13:26).** נְעוּרִים ("youth") with עָוֹן occurs only here; with חַטָּאת, only at Ps 25:7, "Do not remember the sins of my youth" (lemma). *Moderate.*
- **מְרֹרוֹת ("bitter things", 13:26).** Four occurrences: Deut 32:32 ("their clusters, bitter"); Job 13:26; 20:14, 25 (Zophar, of the wicked man's food and the arrow through his gall) (lemma). *Moderate.*
- **סַד ("stocks", 13:27).** Only here and at 33:11, where Elihu quotes 13:27 (Move 4) (lemma). *High.*
- **חקה ("set a limit, inscribe", 13:27).** תִּתְחַקֶּה, "You set a limit for (or "carve marks on") the soles of my feet". Elsewhere the verb means "carve, engrave" (1 Kgs 6:35; Ezek 8:10; 23:14) (lemma). The hithpael here is unique; it may mean marking the feet so that their tracks can be followed `[S]`. *Moderate.*
- **רָקָב ("rot", 13:28) and עָשׁ ("moth").** רָקָב occurs at Hab 3:16; Hos 5:12 ("I am like a moth to Ephraim and like rottenness to the house of Judah"); Job 13:28; Prov 12:4; 14:30. Moth and rot together occur only at Hos 5:12 and Job 13:28 (lemma co-occurrence, Tool 11). The BHS apparatus proposes כְּרֹקֶב ("like a wineskin"), with the Greek, Syriac and Targum (Textual Variants). *High on the data.*

**(d) Man and the tree.** `[T]`
- **יְלוּד אִשָּׁה ("born of woman", 14:1).** Three occurrences in the Hebrew Bible, all in Job: 14:1; 15:14 (Eliphaz); 25:4 (Bildad) (lemma co-occurrence). Both friends take it up to ask how one born of woman can be righteous. *High.*
- **קְצַר יָמִים וּשְׂבַע־רֹגֶז ("short of days and full of turmoil", 14:1).** שָׂבֵעַ ("full") with יוֹם ("day") occurs at Gen 35:29 (Isaac), 1 Chr 29:28 (David), Job 14:1 and 42:17, "And Job died, an old man and full of days" (lemma co-occurrence). רֹגֶז ("turmoil") is the last word of chapter 3 ("and turmoil comes", 3:26) and occurs in Job at 3:17, 26; 14:1; 37:2; 39:24 (lemma). *High* (Move 4).
- **צִיץ ("flower", 14:2).** The flower of Isa 40:6–8 ("the flower of the field … the flower fades") and Ps 103:15 ("As for man, his days are like grass; as a flower of the field, so he flourishes"); elsewhere the carved flowers of the temple (1 Kgs 6:18) and the gold "plate" of the priest's turban (Exod 28:36) (lemma). *Moderate–high* as a shared image.
- **צֵל ("shadow", 14:2).** In Job at 7:2; 8:9 (Bildad: "our days on earth are as a shadow"); 14:2; 17:7 (lemma). Job agrees with Bildad's line and turns it into a complaint. *Moderate.*
- **חֹק ("limit, decree", 14:5, 13).** In Job at 14:5, 13; 23:12, 14; 26:10; 28:26; 38:10 (lemma). At 38:10 God speaks of the sea: "I placed boundaries (חֻקִּי, "my limit") on it". The limit God sets for man (14:5) and the limit Job asks for (14:13) are the word God will use of his limit on the sea. *Moderate–high* (Move 4).
- **שָׂכִיר ("hired man", 14:6).** In Job only at 7:1, 2 and 14:6 (lemma). 7:1–2: "Is not man forced to labour (צָבָא) on earth, and are not his days like the days of a hired man?" The image returns with צָבָא in 14:14. *High.*
- **תִּקְוָה ("hope", 14:7, 19).** Twelve times in Job: 4:6; 5:16; 6:8; 7:6; 8:13; 11:18, 20; 14:7, 19; 17:15; 19:10; 27:8 (lemma). Eliphaz opened the dialogue asking, "Is not … your hope (וְתִקְוָתְךָ) the integrity of your ways?" (4:6). *High.*
- **חלף ("sprout again", 14:7) and חֲלִיפָה ("change, renewal", 14:14).** Same root; in this unit the WLC files them under 2498 and 2487. חֲלִיפָה with צָבָא ("struggle, host") occurs only at 10:17 ("hardship after hardship", literally "changes and a host") and 14:14 (lemma; 9–10 report). *High.*
- **גֶּזַע ("stump", 14:8).** Three occurrences: Isa 11:1; 40:24; Job 14:8 (lemma). *High on the data.*
- **יוֹנֶקֶת ("shoot", 14:7).** Six occurrences: Job 8:16 (Bildad: the godless man's "shoots spread out over his garden"); 14:7; 15:30; Ezek 17:22; Hos 14:7; Ps 80:12 (lemma). Bildad's godless man has shoots that are torn up (8:16–18); Job's tree has shoots that do not fail. *Moderate.*
- **צָבָא ("struggle, service", 14:14).** In Job at 7:1; 10:17; 14:14 (lemma). The word for military service or forced labour. *High.*
- **כסף ("long for", 14:15).** Five occurrences: Gen 31:30 (Laban: "you longed greatly for your father's house"); Ps 17:12; 84:3 [Eng 84:2] ("My soul longed and even yearned for the courts of the LORD"); Zeph 2:1; Job 14:15 (lemma). Here only is God its subject. *High on the data.*
- **צְרוֹר ("bag", 14:17) and חתם ("seal").** צְרוֹר: a pouch for money or valuables (Gen 42:35; Prov 7:20; Hag 1:6), and "the bundle of the living" (1 Sam 25:29). חתם with פֶּשַׁע occurs only at Job 14:17 and Dan 9:24, "to seal up (וּלְחָתֹם, ketiv) sins" (lemma). *Moderate.*

**(e) Names.**
- **Inventory.** שַׁדַּי ("the Almighty", 13:3); אֵל ("God", 13:3, 7, 8); שְׁאוֹל ("Sheol", 14:13). No personal or place names. `[T]`
- **The divine names fall in 13:3–8 only.** From 13:20 on, God is "You", 41 times (Headline 1). `[T]`

**Paronomasia check (words repeated three or more times).** חרשׁ, יכח, פָּנִים, סתר, יוֹם, מות and the אֵל-forms were checked. Three sound-plays are worth recording:
- **אֵל ("God", 13:3, 7, 8) and אֱלִל ("worthless", 13:4).** Above. *Moderate.* `[T]` for the forms.
- **אִיּוֹב ("Job") and אוֹיֵב ("enemy", 13:24).** "Why do You … consider me Your enemy (לְאוֹיֵב)?" The two words differ by one transposition. The play is ancient — the rabbis drew it (Babylonian Talmud, Bava Batra 16a) `[S]`. חשׁב ("count") with אוֹיֵב occurs at 1 Sam 18:25; Job 13:24; 33:10 (lemma), and Elihu's 33:10 quotes Job: "He counts me as His enemy". *Moderate*, and no higher: a sound-play without independent corroboration in the text (the hard rule).
- **חלף ("sprout again", 14:7) and חֲלִיפָה ("my change", 14:14).** A root-play rather than a sound-play (Headline 3). *Moderate–high.*

**What the table shows.**
- **On 14:16–17 the ESV and NIV84 make the verses part of Job's hope; the NASB95 makes them present surveillance.** This is the largest divergence in the unit and it changes the shape of the stanza. The ESV and NIV84 follow the כִּי־עַתָּה pattern (Headline 4; Tool 4). The NASB95 follows the Greek. For this unit's Headline 4 the pulpit text is better than the study text.
- **On 14:3 the ESV keeps the Hebrew "me"** — "and bring me into judgment with you" — so the congregation hears Job turn from "man" to himself in the middle of the general lament. The NASB95 and NIV84 follow the ancient versions with "him" (Textual Variants).
- **On 13:28 the ESV and NIV84 read "Man wastes away"**, joining the verse to chapter 14; the NASB95 reads "While I am decaying", joining it to 13:27. The Hebrew has "and he" (וְהוּא). The ESV's reading makes the Isa 50:9 inversion less personal: in the Hebrew and the NASB95 it is the speaker who wears out (Headline 2).
- **The ESV renders צדק at 13:18 "I shall be in the right"** — the phrase it used at 9:2, 15, 20 and 10:15 (9–10 report), and at 40:8. The congregation can hear 13:18 answer 9:2. The NASB95 and NIV84 have "vindicated" here.
- **The ESV breaks the טפל chain** — "whitewash" (13:4) against "cover over" (14:17). The NASB95 has "smear" and "wrap up", which also breaks it. No English version lets the plaster of 13:4 be heard at 14:17.
- **On 14:22 no English version keeps "flesh … soul".** All three have "body … himself". The inclusio with 12:10 and 13:14 is lost in English (Headline 5).
- **On 13:15 every version prints the qere.** NIV84's margin alone gives the ketiv: "He will surely slay me; I have no hope".

**Ancient versions check.** The modern versions agree on several renderings that carry weight — 13:15, 14:4, 14:14 — so the check runs. Swete, triaged:
- **13:7 — "before the Lord" for "for God".** πότερον οὐκ ἔναντι Κυρίου λαλεῖτε, ἔναντι δὲ αὐτοῦ φθέγγεσθε δόλον; ("Do you not speak before the Lord, and utter deceit before him?"). *Category 1.* The Greek loses Headline 1's distinction between speaking to God and speaking for him.
- **13:15 — neither ketiv nor qere.** ἐάν με χειρώσηται ὁ δυνάστης, ἐπεὶ καὶ ἦρκται, ἦ μὴν λαλήσω καὶ ἐλέγξω ἐναντίον αὐτοῦ ("If the Mighty One lays his hand on me, since he has begun, indeed I will speak and argue before him"). *Category 1.* The Greek has no word for "hope" here.
- **13:16 — Paul's words.** καὶ τοῦτό μοι ἀποβήσεται εἰς σωτηρίαν ("and this will turn out for my salvation"). Phil 1:19 has τοῦτό μοι ἀποβήσεται εἰς σωτηρίαν word for word (SBLGNT). *Category 3* (Tool 11).
- **13:28 — σητόβρωτον ("moth-eaten").** Only here in Swete's LXX; in the New Testament only at Jas 5:2, "your garments have become moth-eaten (σητόβρωτα)" — in the letter that names Job at 5:11. *Category 3; moderate.*
- **14:4–5 — run together.** τίς γὰρ καθαρὸς ἔσται ἀπὸ ῥύπου; ἀλλ᾽ οὐθείς, ἐὰν καὶ μία ἡμέρα ὁ βίος αὐτοῦ ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς ("For who will be clean from filth? Not one, even if his life on earth be one day"). *Category 1*, and the source of a long tradition of reading 14:4 of infant sin (Difficult Verses).
- **14:12 — asterisked.** ἄνθρωπος δὲ κοιμηθεὶς οὐ μὴ ἀναστῇ … καὶ οὐκ ἐξυπνισθήσονται ἐξ ὕπνου αὐτῶν ("a man who has fallen asleep will not rise … and they will not be awakened from their sleep"). The Rahlfs export asterisks the line: later, chiefly Theodotionic. ἐξυπνίζω occurs in the LXX at Judg 16:14, 20; 1 Kgs 3:15; Job 14:12, and in the New Testament only at John 11:11, "Our friend Lazarus has fallen asleep (κεκοίμηται); but I go, so that I may awaken him (ἐξυπνίσω)". *Category 3; moderate* — the verbal link runs through a Hexaplaric line.
- **14:14 — the question answered.** ἐὰν γὰρ ἀποθάνῃ ἄνθρωπος, ζήσεται … ὑπομενῶ ἕως πάλιν γένωμαι ("For if a man dies, he will live … I will wait until I come to be again"). Old Greek, unasterisked. *Category 1* — a rendering that is also an early reading of the book.
- **14:15 — "do not reject the works of Your hands"** (τὰ δὲ ἔργα τῶν χειρῶν σου μὴ ἀποποιοῦ). *Category 1.* Compare 10:3, "that You should reject the labour of Your hands".
- **14:16–17 — surveillance.** "You have numbered my practices, and none of my sins will escape You; You sealed up my transgressions in a bag, and You marked whether I transgressed unwillingly." *Category 1.* The NASB95's reading.
- **14:18 — παλαιωθήσεται ("will grow old").** "and a rock will grow old from its place". The verb of 13:28 (παλαιοῦται) and of Isa 50:9 (παλαιωθήσεσθε). *Category 1.*
- **14:19 — ὑπομονήν ("endurance").** καὶ ὑπομονὴν ἀνθρώπου ἀπώλεσας ("and You destroyed man's endurance"). Asterisked in the Rahlfs export, with 14:18b. ὑπομονή occurs only here in Swete's Job. Jas 5:11: "You have heard of the endurance (ὑπομονήν) of Job and have seen the outcome of the Lord's dealings, that the Lord is full of compassion and is merciful." *Category 3; uncertain* — one word, in a Hexaplaric line.
- **The Old Greek's gaps.** The Rahlfs export also asterisks 13:19b and 13:20b. `[T]`, *as the Rahlfs export prints it*. Nothing here rests on the Old Greek's shorter text.

### 9. Tone and Feel

**The register is defiance turning to longing: a man who will risk death to be heard, and then asks to be hidden in death until God remembers him.** `[T]` for the devices; `[I]` for the register.
- **13:1–12 — scorn.** Short, sarcastic lines. The friends are plasterers of lies and quacks (13:4); their silence would be their wisdom (13:5); their maxims are ash and their defences clay (13:12). Then the warning turns grave: will they deceive God as one deceives a man (13:9)? Will not his majesty terrify them (13:11)?
- **13:13–19 — risk.** "Let come on me what may" (13:13). "Why should I take my flesh in my teeth?" (13:14). "Though He slay me" (13:15). The courage of a man who expects to die and will speak anyway. Then confidence, abruptly: "I know that I will be vindicated" (13:18).
- **13:20–28 — the plea and the charge.** The voice drops to direct address — "Only two things do not do to me" (13:20) — and then rises into complaint: the hidden face, the enemy, the driven leaf, the stocks. It ends in decay, moth and rot (13:28).
- **14:1–12 — elegy.** The tone becomes general and quiet. Man, flower, shadow, hired man (14:1–6). Then a moment of brightness — the tree that sprouts at the scent of water (14:7–9) — quenched by "But man dies" (14:10).
- **14:13–17 — longing.** Three wishes in one breath: hide me, conceal me, remember me (14:13). The question (14:14). Then the tenderest lines in Job's speeches so far: "You will call, and I will answer You; You will long for the work of Your hands" (14:15).
- **14:18–22 — erosion.** Mountain, rock, stones, soil, hope. The last verses are in the third person, as though Job has already gone: a man who does not know whether his sons are honoured, alone with his flesh and his soul.

**The soundtrack.** A courtroom speech that becomes a funeral lament with one verse of hope in the middle. **For the preacher:**
- Do not lift 13:15 out as serene trust; it is spoken by a man who expects God to kill him and will argue anyway.
- Let 14:13–17 be heard as the high point. It is the first time in the book that Job imagines God wanting him.
- Do not end at 14:15. The speech ends at 14:22, in the dark, and round two begins there.

**Isaiah 50:6–9 → 13:18–28** *(high on the words; moderate–high on a deliberate link; direction open)* `[T]` / `[I]` (synthetic)

*Move 1 — Source context.*
- The third Servant Song (Isa 50:4–9, with its coda in 50:10–11). The Servant is taught by God "morning by morning" (50:4) and does not turn back (50:5).
- **The suffering.** "I gave My back to those who strike Me, and My cheeks to those who pluck out the beard; I did not cover (לֹא הִסְתַּרְתִּי) My face from humiliation and spitting" (50:6).
- **The confidence.** "For the Lord GOD helps (יַעֲזָר) Me, therefore I am not disgraced … and I know (וָאֵדַע) that I will not be ashamed. He who vindicates (מַצְדִּיקִי) Me is near; who will contend (מִי־יָרִיב) with Me? Let us stand up to each other; who has a case (מִשְׁפָּטִי) against Me? Let him draw near to Me. Behold, the Lord GOD helps Me; who is he who condemns Me (מִי־הוּא יַרְשִׁיעֵנִי)? Behold, they will all wear out (יִבְלוּ) like a garment (כַּבֶּגֶד); the moth (עָשׁ) will eat them" (50:7–9).
- **The coda.** "Who is among you that fears (יְרֵא) the LORD, that obeys the voice of His servant, that walks in darkness and has no light? Let him trust in the name of the LORD" (50:10).
- The register is a courtroom of vindication: the Servant suffers, and the case is already his.

*The verbal data* (lemma, WLC):
- **מִי with רִיב ("who will contend?")** — in one verse only at Isa 50:8 and Job 13:19.
- **בלה ("wear out") with בֶּגֶד ("garment") and עָשׁ ("moth")** — in one verse only at Isa 50:9 and Job 13:28. בֶּגֶד with עָשׁ: Isa 50:9; 51:8; Job 13:28.
- **The four court words.** Isa 50:8 holds מִי, רִיב, צדק and מִשְׁפָּט in one verse — the only verse in the Hebrew Bible with צדק and the verb רִיב (with the noun, Deut 25:1; 2 Sam 15:4). Job 13:18–19 holds the same four across two verses.
- **"I know … vindicated / not ashamed."** ידע with צדק at Job 13:18; ידע at Isa 50:7 with צדק at 50:8.
- **סתר with פָּנִים ("hide the face")** — Isa 50:6; Job 13:20, 24 (and 34:29 in Job).
- **מִי־הוּא with a first-person object** — Isa 50:9 ("who is he who condemns Me?"); Job 13:19 ("who is he who will contend with me?"). The phrase מִי־הוּא itself is common; the shape is the point, not the phrase.
- **The Greek** (Swete): Job 13:19 τίς γάρ ἐστιν ὁ κριθησόμενός μοι against Isa 50:8 τίς ὁ κρινόμενός μοι; Job 13:18 δίκαιος ἀναφανοῦμαι ("I will be shown to be righteous") against Isa 50:8 ὁ δικαιώσας με ("He who justifies me"); Job 13:28 παλαιοῦται … ἱμάτιον σητόβρωτον against Isa 50:9 ὡς ἱμάτιον παλαιωθήσεσθε καὶ σὴς καταφάγεται. Swete's 14:18 adds the same verb again: "a rock will grow old (παλαιωθήσεται)".

*Move 2 — Book usage.* Isaiah's Servant is already in the overview's map: the cheek and spitting of Isa 50:6 at 16:10 and 30:10 (with רֹק, "spittle", at 7:19 — sweep). The 9–10 dig added the second Song (Isa 49:4 at 9:29). **Isa 50 now touches Job at 13:18–28, 16:10 and 30:10 — three speeches — and the Servant Songs at four places.** `[T]` for the links; the pattern is synthetic.

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* Isa 51:8 repeats the moth image of 50:9 against the Servant's enemies ("the moth will eat them like a garment"). Isa 54:17 extends the court to the Servant's offspring: "every tongue that accuses you in judgment you will condemn". Paul builds Rom 8:33–34 from Isa 50:8–9: "God is the one who justifies (ὁ δικαιῶν); who is the one who condemns?" `[S]` (a standard identification). `[T]` for Isaiah's own repetitions.

*Direction of dependence.* Canonically, Isaiah stands among the Nevi'im and is read before Job; compositionally, neither book's date is fixed by the text. Who echoes whom is **open**. The canonical reader meets the Servant first and hears Job in his words. `[I]`

*What it adds.*
- Job takes the Servant's courtroom on his lips — "I know that I will be vindicated … Who will contend with me?" — but without the Servant's two certainties. **The Servant says, "the Lord GOD helps Me", twice; Job has no helper.** In Job the verb עזר ("help") occurs only at 9:13; 26:2; 29:12; 30:13, never of God helping Job.
- **The moth eats the wrong garment.** In Isaiah the Servant's accusers wear out like a moth-eaten garment. In Job it is the speaker: "he wastes away like a rotten thing, like a garment that is moth-eaten" (13:28).
- **The face is reversed.** The Servant does not hide his face from those who spit; Job says he will not hide from God's face (13:20), and asks why God hides his (13:24).
- **The coda describes Job.** "Who is among you that fears the LORD … that walks in darkness and has no light?" (Isa 50:10). The book opened with a man who "feared God" (1:1) and has him walking in darkness (3; 10:21–22). `[I]`
- The canonical reader sees a righteous servant of God (1:8; 2:3) pleading in the Servant's words without the Servant's assurance — and knows how the Servant's case ends. *Moderate–high.* **Load-bearing; unaudited** (Headline 2).

**Psalm 44 → 13:9, 24; 14:12 (with 11:6)** *(high on the words; moderate–high as a source)* `[T]` / `[I]`

*Move 1 — Source context.*
- A communal lament of the sons of Korah. It begins with God's past victories for Israel (44:2–9 [Eng 44:1–8]), turns to present defeat and shame (44:10–17), and then protests innocence.
- **The protest.** "All this has come upon us, but we have not forgotten You, and we have not dealt falsely with Your covenant" (44:18 [Eng 44:17]). "If we had forgotten the name of our God … would not God find this out (יַחֲקָר, "search out")? For He knows the secrets (תַּעֲלֻמוֹת) of the heart" (44:21–22 [Eng 44:20–21]).
- **The cry.** "But for Your sake we are killed all day long; we are considered as sheep to be slaughtered. Arouse Yourself (עוּרָה), why do You sleep (תִישַׁן), O Lord? Awake (הָקִיצָה), do not reject us forever. Why do You hide Your face (לָמָּה־פָנֶיךָ תַסְתִּיר) and forget our affliction and our oppression? For our soul has sunk down into the dust (לֶעָפָר) … Rise up (קוּמָה), be our help (עֶזְרָתָה), and redeem us for the sake of Your lovingkindness" (44:23–27 [Eng 44:22–26]).
- The register is the faithful innocent's complaint: suffering that is not for sin.

*The verbal data* (lemma and phrase, WLC):
- **לָמָּה־פָנֶיךָ תַסְתִּיר ("Why do You hide Your face?")** — word for word only at Ps 44:25 and Job 13:24 (phrase search). מָה with סתר and פָּנִים: Deut 32:20; Job 13:24; Ps 44:25; 88:15.
- **עור ("arouse") with קיץ ("awake") and the sleep root (ישׁן / שֵׁנָה)** — in one verse only at Ps 44:24 and Job 14:12 (lemma co-occurrence). Job: "he will not awake (יָקִיצוּ) nor be aroused (יֵעֹרוּ) out of his sleep (מִשְּׁנָתָם)".
- **חקר ("search") of the hidden** — Ps 44:22; Job 13:9 ("Will it be well when He examines (יַחְקֹר) you?"). Zophar's 11:6–7 has תַּעֲלֻמוֹת ("secrets") and חֵקֶר ("depths"); תַּעֲלֻמוֹת occurs only at Job 11:6; 28:11; Ps 44:22 (sweep, Unit 7).
- **Dust and rising** — עָפָר (Ps 44:26; Job 14:8, 19) and קוּם (Ps 44:27; Job 14:12).
- **The New Testament.** Rom 8:36 quotes Ps 44:23: ἕνεκεν σοῦ θανατούμεθα ὅλην τὴν ἡμέραν (SBLGNT; Swete Ps 43:23 identical).

*Move 2 — Book usage.* The Psalter is a live source in the overview (8, 88, 107), and the digs have added 22, 39 and 113. **Psalm 44 touches the Zophar–Job exchange three times — 11:6 (Zophar), 13:24 and 14:12 (Job) — and 28:11.** It is new to the map. And Psalm 88, already live, stands beside it at 13:24: "O LORD, why do You reject my soul? Why do You hide Your face from me?" (Ps 88:15 [Eng 88:14]). Both are Korahite laments. `[T]`

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* Psalm 44's "Awake, why do You sleep?" belongs to a small Psalter idiom: God is called to rouse himself (Ps 7:7; 35:23; 59:5–6). Its "hide Your face" is a frequent Psalter complaint (סתר + פָּנִים at Ps 13:2 [Eng 13:1]; 27:9; 44:25; 69:18; 88:15; 102:3; 143:7, among others — lemma). Deut 32:20 is the source of the phrase: "I will hide My face from them" — there, from the faithless. `[T]` for the references; `[I]` for "source".

*What it adds.*
- The friends have treated Job's suffering as evidence of sin. Psalm 44 is the Psalter's own witness that God's people may suffer "for Your sake" while they have "not forgotten" him. When Job asks "Why do You hide Your face?" he asks it in the words of that psalm.
- **But the psalm's sleeper is God, and Job's is man.** The psalmist cries to God, "Awake, why do You sleep?" Job says of man, "he will not awake nor be aroused out of his sleep" (14:12). The psalm's call to God becomes Job's description of the dead. `[I]`
- Paul quotes Ps 44:23 at the climax of Romans 8 and answers it: "in all these things we overwhelmingly conquer through Him who loved us" (Rom 8:37). *Moderate–high.*

*Move 1 — Source context.*
- **Isa 26:19** stands in the Isaiah apocalypse (Isa 24–27), in a song of the redeemed city. After the people's failure ("We could not accomplish deliverance for the earth", 26:18), God speaks: "Your dead will live (יִחְיוּ מֵתֶיךָ); their corpses will rise (יְקוּמוּן). You who lie in the dust (עָפָר), awake (הָקִיצוּ) and shout for joy, for your dew is as the dew of the dawn, and the earth will give birth to the departed spirits." Then: "Come, my people, enter into your rooms and close your doors behind you; hide (חֲבִי) for a little while until indignation runs its course (עַד־יַעֲבָר־זָעַם)" (26:20).
- **Dan 12:2** closes Daniel's last vision: "Many of those who sleep (מִיְּשֵׁנֵי) in the dust (עָפָר) of the ground will awake (יָקִיצוּ), these to everlasting life (לְחַיֵּי עוֹלָם), but the others to disgrace and everlasting contempt."
- The register in both is promise: the dead will rise.

*The verbal data* (lemma, WLC):
- **קוּם ("rise") with קיץ ("awake")** — in one verse only at Hab 2:7; Isa 26:19; Job 14:12.
- **קיץ with עָפָר ("dust")** — only Isa 26:19 and Dan 12:2. Job has עָפָר at 14:8, 19.
- **Dan 12:2's יָקִיצוּ ("they will awake")** is the very form Job negates: לֹא יָקִיצוּ ("they will not awake", 14:12).
- **מות ("die") with חיה ("live")** — Isa 26:19 has both ("your dead will live"); in Job, only 14:14 ("If a man dies, will he live?").
- **Hidden until wrath passes.** Isa 26:20 and Job 14:13 share the thought — hidden "until" God's anger is over — but not the words (חבה and זַעַם against סתר, צפן and אַף). A concept link only.

*Move 2 — Book usage.* The sweep found Isa 26:21 behind 16:18 ("O earth, do not cover my blood"). **Isaiah 24–27 thus touches Job at 14:12–14 and 16:18.** `[T]` for the data; synthetic.

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* Dan 12:2 reads as a development of Isa 26:19 — the dust, the awaking, the life — adding the two destinies `[S]`. Ezek 37 (the dry bones; the 9–10 dig found Ezek 37:6, 8 at 10:11) is the third great Old Testament text of the dead raised. `[I]`

*What it adds.* Job's question in 14:14 and his denial in 14:12 are answered in the canon with his own verbs: rise, awake, live, dust. The answer is not given to Job in this speech; the canonical reader hears it. **Preach the answer as the canon's, not as Job's** (Pitfalls). *Moderate.*

**Isaiah 11:1 → 14:7–9** *(high on the data; moderate on a deliberate link)* `[T]` / `[I]`

*Move 1 — Source context.* Isaiah 10:33–34 ends with God felling Assyria like a forest: "the LORD … will lop off the boughs … He will cut down the thickets of the forest with an iron axe". Then: "a shoot (חֹטֶר) will spring from the stem (מִגֵּזַע) of Jesse, and a branch (נֵצֶר) from his roots (מִשָּׁרָשָׁיו) will bear fruit. The Spirit of the LORD will rest on Him, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and strength …" (11:1–2). The felled tree of David's line sprouts again.

*The verbal data.*
- גֶּזַע ("stump") occurs three times: Isa 11:1; 40:24; Job 14:8. גֶּזַע with שֹׁרֶשׁ ("root") in one verse only at Isa 11:1 and Job 14:8 (lemma).
- Job 14:7–9 has the felled tree (כרת, "cut down"), the stump, the root, and the shoot (יוֹנֶקֶת) that "will sprout again".

*Move 2 — Book usage.* The sweep found Isa 11:2 at 12:13 — "With Him are wisdom and might; to Him belong counsel and understanding" (חָכְמָה, גְבוּרָה and עֵצָה in one verse only there and at Isa 11:2) — and Isa 11:2 again behind 28:28 (the fear of the LORD and wisdom). **Isa 11:1–2 now touches Job three times, and twice in this one speech (12:13; 14:8).** `[T]` for the data; the pattern is synthetic.

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* The felled tree with a living stump returns in Isa 6:13 ("the holy seed is its stump", a different word), Isa 53:2 ("He grew up before Him like a tender shoot (כַּיּוֹנֵק), and like a root (וְכַשֹּׁרֶשׁ) out of parched ground") and Jer 23:5 ("a righteous Branch"). Isa 53:2's יוֹנֵק is a different lemma from Job's יוֹנֶקֶת (14:7), with the same root letters. `[T]` for the lemmas.

*What it adds.* Job imagines what the canon promises of David's line: a felled tree with hope, a dead stump that sprouts "at the scent of water". He grants it to the tree and denies it to man (14:10). The canonical reader knows a Man of whom it is said. `[I]` *Moderate.* See Christological Reading.

**Exodus 34:7 → 13:23; 14:16–17 (with 7:21)** *(moderate–high; synthetic; extends the 9–10 dig's Exodus 33–34 entry)* `[T]` / `[I]`

*Move 1 — Source context.* The proclamation of the Name: "compassionate and gracious, slow to anger, and abounding in lovingkindness and truth; who keeps lovingkindness for thousands, who forgives (נֹשֵׂא, "bears") iniquity (עָוֹן), transgression (פֶּשַׁע) and sin (חַטָּאָה); yet He will by no means leave the guilty unpunished" (Exod 34:6–7). Moses had asked to see God's face and was told "My face shall not be seen" (33:23), and God covered him with his hand (כַּפִּי, 33:22) as he passed.

*The verbal data* (lemma, WLC):
- **עָוֹן, פֶּשַׁע, חַטָּאת in one verse** — Exod 34:7; Lev 16:21 (the Day of Atonement); Ps 32:5; Isa 59:12; Ezek 21:29; Dan 9:24; Job 13:23.
- **נשׂא ("forgive") with פֶּשַׁע and עָוֹן** — Exod 34:7; Num 14:18; Mic 7:18; Ps 32:5; Job 7:21 ("Why then do You not pardon (תִשָּׂא) my transgression and take away my iniquity?").
- **The hand and the face.** "Remove Your hand (כַּפְּךָ) from me" (13:21) against "I will cover you with My hand" (Exod 33:22); "Why do You hide Your face?" (13:24) against "My face shall not be seen" (33:23). `[I]` for these.

*Move 2 — Book usage.* The 9–10 dig found Exod 33:19–34:7 at 9:11 and 10:12–15. **With 7:21 and 13:23, the Sinai proclamation now touches Job at four places**, and every time the clause Job hears is the one for the guilty. `[T]` for the data; synthetic.

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* Ps 32:5 is the Psalter's answer: "I acknowledged my sin to You, and my iniquity I did not hide; I said, 'I will confess my transgressions to the LORD'; and You forgave the guilt of my sin." It holds all three words and the verb נשׂא. Dan 9:24 holds all three with חתם ("seal", as a ketiv): "to finish the transgression, to seal up (ketiv) sins, to make atonement for iniquity". `[T]`

*What it adds.* Job asks God to name his sins in the words of the Name (13:23), and in 14:16–17 imagines God doing what the Name says — sin unwatched, transgression sealed away, iniquity covered over. Ps 32 confesses and is forgiven; Job asks to be told what to confess. `[I]` *Moderate–high.*

**Moderate-confidence allusions (Move 1 only).**

- **Jer 51:39, 57 → 14:12** *(moderate–high on the phrase).* לֹא יָקִיצוּ ("they will not awake") occurs only at Jer 51:39, 57 and Job 14:12 (phrase search, confirmed by lemma). In Jeremiah it is God's sentence on Babylon's princes: "I will make them drunk … and may sleep a perpetual sleep (שְׁנַת־עוֹלָם) and not wake up". Job uses the judgement-sleep of the oppressor for every man. `[T]` for the data; `[I]` for the effect.
- **Isa 19:5 → 14:11** *(high on the phrase).* וְנָהָר יֶחֱרַב וְיָבֵשׁ ("and the river will be parched and dry") occurs only at Isa 19:5 and Job 14:11 (phrase search); both verses begin with water (מַיִם) and the sea (יָם). In Isaiah it is the drying of the Nile in the oracle against Egypt (19:1–15). Job uses the image of a judgement on Egypt for the ordinary death of man. `[T]`
- **Lev 26:36 → 13:25** *(moderate–high).* עָלֶה נִדָּף ("a driven leaf") occurs only at Lev 26:36 and Job 13:25, and both have רדף ("pursue") (lemma). In Leviticus it is a covenant curse on the disobedient: "the sound of a driven leaf will chase them". Job asks why God pursues him as though he were under the covenant curse. `[T]` for the data; `[I]` for the effect. (The prologue has Deuteronomy 28's curses in view — earlier digs.)
- **Hos 5:12 → 13:28** *(moderate).* רָקָב ("rot") with עָשׁ ("moth") meets only at Hos 5:12 and Job 13:28 (lemma co-occurrence). "Therefore I am like a moth to Ephraim and like rottenness to the house of Judah" — God as the moth and the rot. `[T]`
- **Hos 13:12; Dan 9:24 → 14:17** *(moderate).* "The iniquity (עֲוֹן) of Ephraim is bound up (צָרוּר); his sin is stored up (צְפוּנָה)" (Hos 13:12) — sin kept for judgement. צָרוּר is not Job's צְרוֹר ("bag", a different lemma), but the image is the same; and Job's צפן ("hide") is at 14:13. Dan 9:24 joins חתם ("seal") and פֶּשַׁע — only there and at Job 14:17 (lemma) — for the ending of sin. **The two images point opposite ways**, and that is the crux of 14:17 (Difficult Verses). `[T]` for the data; `[I]` for the bearing.
- **Ps 143:2 → 14:3 (with 9:2)** *(moderate).* "And do not enter (תָּבוֹא) into judgment (בְמִשְׁפָּט) with Your servant, for in Your sight no man living is righteous (יִצְדַּק)." Job: "and bring (תָבִיא) me into judgment (בְמִשְׁפָּט) with Yourself" (14:3). בוא with מִשְׁפָּט is common (39 verses); in Job only at 9:32; 14:3; 22:4 (lemma). Ps 143:2 joins Job's 14:3 to his 9:2 ("how can a man be in the right before God?"). Paul uses Ps 143:2 at Rom 3:20 and Gal 2:16 ("by the works of the Law no flesh will be justified"). `[T]` for the data; `[I]` for the link.
- **Ps 25:7 → 13:26** *(moderate).* "Do not remember the sins (חַטֹּאות) of my youth (נְעוּרַי) or my transgressions" — נְעוּרִים with a sin-word occurs only here and at Job 13:26 ("the iniquities of my youth") (lemma). The psalmist asks God not to remember; Job says God writes them down. `[T]`
- **Isa 40:6–8; Ps 103:15 → 14:2** *(moderate–high as a shared image).* Man as a flower (צִיץ) that fades. Isaiah sets it against "the word of our God stands forever" (40:8); Ps 103 against "the lovingkindness of the LORD is from everlasting to everlasting" (103:17). Job sets it against God's scrutiny (14:3). `[T]` for the image; `[I]` for the contrast.
- **Ps 90:5–6 → 14:7–12** *(uncertain–moderate; synthetic).* Ps 90:5–6 holds חלף ("sprout anew") twice, שֵׁנָה ("sleep") and יבשׁ ("wither"); Job 14:7–12 holds all three (lemma). Moses' prayer on human brevity ends, "Do return, O LORD; how long will it be?" (90:13). `[T]` for the lemmas; capped at *uncertain–moderate* until audited.
- **Ps 119:69 → 13:4** *(moderate).* "The arrogant have forged a lie (טָפְלוּ … שֶׁקֶר) against me." The only other verse with טפל and שֶׁקֶר (lemma). The psalmist is the righteous man smeared with lies — Job's position before the friends. `[T]`
- **Prov 17:28 → 13:5** *(moderate).* "Even a fool, when he keeps silent (מַחֲרִישׁ), is considered wise (חָכָם)." Job's sarcasm is a proverb applied. `[T]`
- **Judg 12:3; 1 Sam 19:5; 28:21 → 13:14** *(high on the idiom).* "I took my life in my hands" (Jephthah); "he took his life in his hand and struck the Philistine" (Jonathan of David); "I have taken my life in my hand" (the medium of Endor) — always of risking death (lemma). `[T]`
- **Ps 139:19 → 13:15** *(uncertain).* קטל ("slay") occurs three times: Job 13:15; 24:14; Ps 139:19 ("O that You would slay the wicked, O God"). The psalmist asks God to slay the wicked; Job expects God to slay him. A rare verb shared, nothing more. `[T]`

**Psalm 39 again — 14:6** *(high; the 9–10 dig's live source, confirmed).* "Turn Your gaze from him (שְׁעֵה מֵעָלָיו) that he may rest (וְיֶחְדָּל), until he fulfills his day like a hired man" (14:6) against Ps 39:14 [Eng 39:13], "Turn Your gaze away from me (הָשַׁע מִמֶּנִּי)". שׁעה in Job only at 7:19 and 14:6; חדל with יוֹם ("day") only at 7:16; 10:20; 14:6 (lemma). `[T]`

**Psalm 88 again — 13:24** *(moderate–high; overview live source).* "Why do You hide Your face from me?" (Ps 88:15 [Eng 88:14]). See Psalm 44 above.

*Addressee-differentiation.*
- **Parties from the prologue.** God (spoken of in 13:3–11, spoken to from 13:20); the friends (addressed until 13:17, then dropped); the Accuser (never named; audible only in "flesh … life", 13:14; 14:22).
- **Three parties answer this speech later.** The friends quote it (15:14; 18:4; 22:4; 25:4). Elihu quotes it more than any other speech (33:7, 10–11, 31–33; 34:19, 29). God answers its demand (38:1, 3) and its warning about partiality (42:7–9).

### 12. Genre

**Hebrew poetry: a disputation speech that becomes a lawsuit plea, then an elegy on mortality with a prayer at its heart.** `[T]`
- **Embedded forms.**
  - *Disputation with the friends (13:1–12).* Rhetorical questions, sarcasm, proverbs turned (13:5, 12).
  - *A plaintiff's declaration (13:13–19).* "I have prepared my case" (13:18) — the language of a party about to plead.
  - *A complaint to God (13:20–28).* Conditions, a request to be told the charge, a "why", images of persecution.
  - *An elegy on man (14:1–12, 18–22).* Wisdom's language of brevity: flower, shadow, water, stone.
  - *A wish-prayer (14:13–17).* Three wishes introduced by מִי יִתֵּן ("Oh that").
- **Reading rules.**
  - It is character speech; God will rebuke Job's words "without knowledge" (38:2) and say he spoke what is right (42:7). Report it as what Job says.
  - It is poetry: God does not literally put feet in stocks (13:27) or write bitter things (13:26).
  - The wish of 14:13–17 is a wish. It shows what Job hopes for, not what he believes will happen.
- **What the genre does not license.**
  - It does not teach that there is no resurrection (14:12); it reports a man's lament, which the canon answers.
  - It does not teach that God is Job's enemy (13:24); it reports how it feels to Job. `[I]`

### 13. Copycat

| Speech act | Category | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Telling the friends to be silent (13:5, 13) | **Descriptive**; with a warning for comforters | Job's rebuke of bad comfort; the book will endorse it (42:7) |
| Warning against speaking falsely for God (13:7–11) | **Normative principle** | God does reprove the friends (42:7–9). A warning to every preacher |
| "Though He slay me" (13:15) | **Normative pattern, read whole** | Holding on to God when he seems to be the killer — and arguing to his face. Not quietism (Difficult Verses) |
| Demanding a hearing (13:18–22) | **Descriptive**, with the Copycat caveat | God gives a hearing, but on his own terms (38:3). Not a licence to demand an audit of God |
| Asking to be told one's sins (13:23) | **Normative pattern** | The honest prayer of a man who will confess what he is shown (Ps 139:23–24) |
| Lamenting human brevity (14:1–12) | **Descriptive**, true as far as it goes | Agrees with Ps 90; 103; Isa 40 |
| Wishing to be hidden and remembered (14:13–17) | **Normative longing** | The longing the gospel meets (Christological Reading) |
| "You destroy man's hope" (14:19) | **Descriptive — not normative** | An honest end to a lament; the book does not end there (19:25; 42:5, 17) |

**Common copycat misreading.** "Job had the courage to tell God what he thought, so we can make demands of God." The book commends Job's direction of speech — to God — and his refusal to lie about himself. It does not commend the claim that God owes him an audit: God's reply is to ask the questions (38:3). `[I]`

**Where the reader sits.** This side of the empty tomb. `[I]`
- **The court has a verdict.** "Who will bring a charge against God's elect? God is the one who justifies; who is the one who condemns? Christ Jesus is He who died, yes, rather who was raised, who is at the right hand of God, who also intercedes for us" (Rom 8:33–34). Paul builds it from the Servant's words (Isa 50:8–9) `[S]` and goes on to quote Psalm 44 (Rom 8:36).
- **The question has an answer.** "If a man dies, will he live again?" (14:14). "I am the resurrection and the life; he who believes in Me will live even if he dies" (John 11:25), said of a friend who had "fallen asleep" (John 11:11).
- **The covering has been made.** "Blessed are those whose lawless deeds have been forgiven, and whose sins have been covered" (Rom 4:7, quoting Ps 32:1).

**How it reframes the reading.** The Christian may not preach 14:12 as the last word, nor make 14:14 a creed Job held. The Christian must hear Job's question as the one the gospel answers, and must hear 14:13–17 as a longing God has met. `[I]`

### 15. Who Am I?

| Speaker / figure | Function |
|---|---|
| **Job** | The **righteous sufferer** who turns from his comforters to God. **Representative** of every believer who has been told his suffering proves his guilt. **Example** of speech to God, of refusing false confession, and of hope held in the form of a question. **Not** an example of the charge that God destroys man's hope (14:19) as a final word |
| **God** | Spoken of as the one whose majesty the friends should fear (13:11), then spoken to as judge (13:20–23), hunter (13:24–27), watcher (14:3, 6, 16), and the one who might call and long for the work of his hands (14:15). Silent |
| **The friends** | Addressed and dismissed: advocates for God who will be judged for it (13:7–11). They will quote the speech back (15:14; 18:4; 22:4; 25:4) |
| **Man, born of woman** (14:1) | Every human being: short-lived, full of turmoil, under scrutiny, without the tree's hope |
| **The tree** (14:7–9) | The image of what man lacks — and what the canon promises in Jesse's stump (Isa 11:1) |
| **The reader** | Knows the prologue's "flesh" and "life" (2:5–6); knows the book's ending — God answers, the friends are reproved, Job dies "full of days" (42:17) |

**The "Job-is-me" trap here cuts two ways.** The sufferer may hear 13:24 and 14:19 as permission to conclude that God is against him. The comfortable may hear 13:4–12 and assume they are Job, not the friends. The text invites every hearer to ask whether he has spoken falsely for God, and every sufferer to bring his case to God himself. `[I]`

### 16. So What?

**Stage 1 — the author's intended response.** To recognise false comfort that defends God at the expense of truth, and to fear it. To see a righteous man risk everything to bring his case to God himself. To feel the full weight of human mortality, and to long with Job for a God who would hide, remember, call and cover. `[T]` / `[I]`

**Stage 2 — worldview, behaviour, motivation.**

| Domain | Application |
|---|---|
| **Worldview** | God does not need advocates who lie for him (13:7–8). Human life is brief and under God's eye (14:1–6). Death, seen from below, has no tree's hope (14:7–12) — but God is the one who could call the dead (14:15), and the canon says he will (Isa 26:19; Dan 12:2) |
| **Stop** | Defending God with arguments that are untrue to the sufferer's case. Quoting 13:15 as calm trust. Treating 14:4 as a proof-text detached from Job's plea. Ending a sermon on chapter 14 before 14:22 |
| **Start** | Speaking to God about the case, not only about God to others (13:3). Praying Psalm 44 and Psalm 39 in the dark. Asking God to show us our sin (13:23). Holding the hope of resurrection as the answer to Job's question, given in Christ |
| **Motivation** | God longs for the work of his hands (14:15). The one Job could only wish for — a God who covers sin and calls the dead — has spoken in Christ |

**Grammatical and syntactical features.**
- **The address test.** 13:2–17: 23 second-person plural forms (the friends); 13:18–19: none; 13:20–28: 19 second-person singular; chapter 14: 22 second-person singular and no plural (WLC morphology). For comparison, chapter 12 has 4 plural and 6 singular — and 12:7–8's singulars address the beasts and the earth. `[T]`
- **13:7–8 — the fronted prepositional phrases.** הַלְאֵל ("for God?") stands first in its clause, before the verb; so does לָאֵל in 13:8b. The emphasis falls on whom the friends speak *for*. `[T]` for the word order; `[I]` for the emphasis.
- **13:15 — ketiv and qere.** Ketiv לא ("not"): "Behold, He will slay me; I have no hope" (or "I will not wait"). Qere לוֹ ("for him"): "Though He slay me, I will hope in Him." Both are possible Hebrew; the ketiv fits the context of 13:13–14 (risking death) and the אַךְ ("nevertheless") of 13:15b; the qere fits 14:14's waiting. See Textual Variants. `[T]` for the forms.
- **13:28 — וְהוּא ("and he").** A switch to the third person within the address to God (13:20–27 is "You … me"). Either "and he (= I, this man)" or the beginning of the generalisation that chapter 14 continues. `[T]` for the form.
- **14:3 — וְאֹתִי ("and me").** In a verse about "such a one" (עַל־זֶה), the object turns to "me". The Greek, Syriac and Latin have "him". The first person is the harder reading and Job's habit: the general turns personal. `[T]` for the form; `[I]` for the preference.
- **14:4 — מִי־יִתֵּן ("who will give?").** The formula is elsewhere in Job always the wish "Oh that …" (6:8; 11:5; 13:5; 14:13; 19:23; 23:3; 31:31, 35). Read as a wish: "Oh that a clean thing could come from an unclean! Not one." Read as a question: "Who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean? No one." The English versions all take the question. `[T]` for the formula's use; *moderate* for the wish reading.
- **14:13–17 — wish, then consequence.** מִי יִתֵּן with three imperfects ("hide … conceal … set … remember", 14:13), the question (14:14a), the resolve (14:14b), two imperfects of what God would do (14:15), then כִּי־עַתָּה and four clauses (14:16–17). On the Job pattern (Tool 4), 14:15–17 is the content of the hope. `[I]` *Moderate–high.*
- **14:12 — עַד־בִּלְתִּי שָׁמַיִם ("until the heavens are no longer").** An idiom for "never". `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the sense.

**Wordplay and sound.**
- **אֵל / אֱלִל (13:3–8).** The friends defend God (אֵל) with worthless (אֱלִל) medicine. *Moderate.*
- **אִיּוֹב / אוֹיֵב (13:24).** "Why do You consider me Your enemy?" `[S]` (Bava Batra 16a). *Moderate*; no corroboration from the text, so no higher.
- **חלף / חֲלִיפָה (14:7, 14).** *Moderate–high.*
- **חרשׁ (13:5, 13, 19).** The friends' silence and Job's silence in death, with one root. `[T]`

### Textual Variants

**Variation inside the Hebrew** (BHS apparatus for chapters 13–14, *as BHS prints it*):
- **13:15 — ketiv לא, qere לוֹ.**
  - *The issue:* "He will slay me; I have no hope" or "Though He slay me, I will hope in Him".
  - *Evidence:* BHS: *mlt Mss Vrs ut Q* ("many manuscripts and the versions read as the qere"). Swete reads neither ("If the Mighty One lays his hand on me … I will speak and argue before Him"). The Masoretic reading tradition supplies the qere; the consonantal text has the ketiv.
  - *Most likely sense:* undecided. The ketiv suits the immediate context — 13:14 is about risking death, and 13:15b begins "nevertheless" (אַךְ), which reads more naturally after "I have no hope". The qere suits 14:14 ("I will wait") and the tradition.
  - *Preaching implication:* **real.** The most quoted verse of the book rests on a qere. Either way, 13:15b stands: "I will argue my ways to his face." The verse is defiant in both readings.
  - *Confidence:* moderate. `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`.
- **13:14 — עַל־מָה ("why").** Absent from the Greek; BHS: delete as dittography (repeated from 13:13's מָה). If deleted, 13:14 becomes a statement: "I will take my flesh in my teeth and put my life in my hand." *Moderate*; the sense of risk is the same.
- **13:28 — כְּרָקָב ("like a rotten thing").** BHS: perhaps read כְּרֹקֶב ("like a wineskin"), with the Greek (ἴσα ἀσκῷ, "like a wineskin"), the Syriac and the Targum. Wineskin and garment would make a pair of worn-out containers (cf. Ps 119:83). The moth-and-garment link with Isa 50:9 is untouched either way; the rot-and-moth link with Hos 5:12 depends on the Masoretic reading. *Moderate.*
- **14:3 — וְאֹתִי ("and me").** BHS: the Greek, Syriac and Latin have a third-person suffix. ESV keeps "me"; NASB95 and NIV84 follow the versions. *Moderate*; see Original Language Observations.
- **14:5 — ketiv חקו, qere חֻקָּיו ("his limit" / "his limits").** Singular or plural; no difference in sense. *Minor.*
- **14:10 — וְאַיּוֹ ("and where is he?").** BHS: one manuscript and the Greek read וָאָיִן ("and he is not"), cf. the Syriac. NIV84's "is no more" reflects this sense. *Minor.* BHS also suggests a different verb form for וַיֶּחֱלָשׁ. *Minor.*
- **14:18 — נוֹפֵל יִבּוֹל ("falling … crumbles").** BHS proposes נָפוֹל יִפּוֹל ("will surely fall"). *Minor.*
- **13:1, 6; 14:4, 6, 7, 19.** 13:1: some manuscripts read כָּל־אֵלֶּה ("all these"). 13:6: the Greek reads "the argument of my mouth". 14:4: one manuscript omits the verse. 14:6, 7: single-manuscript spellings. 14:19: a conjecture on סְפִיחֶיהָ ("its torrents"). None bears on a finding.
- **Cross-references printed by BHS.** 12:3 = 13:2; 13:27 cf. 33:11; 18:4 cf. 14:18 — three of this report's Move 4 links, noted by the editors themselves.
- **Paragraphing.** A petuḥah after 11:20 and after 14:22 and none inside 12:1–14:22, in Leningrad as BHS prints it. `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`.
- **Ketiv.** 13:15 (לא) and 14:5 (חקו). Checked mechanically; every chain touching them was confirmed by lemma. In the comparison texts, Dan 9:24's חתם is a ketiv (ולחתם חטאות), and the chain with 14:17 rests on it.

**Category 2 — Hebrew and Greek.**
- **13:15** — the Greek supports neither reading.
- **13:28** — the Greek's "wineskin" reflects כְּרֹקֶב, a variant of the consonants' vowels. *Category 2, moderate.*
- **14:3** — the Greek's "him". *Category 2, moderate.*
- **14:14** — the Old Greek's "he will live" is a rendering, not a variant: the Hebrew question particle הֲ was read or rendered as a statement. *Category 1.*
- **The Hexaplaric lines** — 13:19b, 13:20b, 14:12 and 14:18b–19 are asterisked in the Rahlfs export. *Category 2*; the John 11:11 and Jas 5:11 links run through them.
- **13:15 is the variant that affects the sermon; 14:3 and 13:28 affect what the congregation hears.**

### Historical and Cultural Background

**Setting.** The story's world is patriarchal and outside Israel (overview). The composition date is `[S]` and not needed here.

**What the original audience knew.**
1. **The physician.** Healers in Israel and its neighbours combined remedies with ritual and explanation `[S]`. Job's "worthless physicians" (13:4) have a diagnosis — sin — and the wrong one. *Moderate.*
2. **Partiality in court.** "Lifting the face" of a party was the classic corruption of a judge (Lev 19:15; Deut 1:17; 10:17; 16:19) `[T]`. Job accuses the friends of acting as corrupt judges *in God's favour*. `[T]` for the texts; `[I]` for the application.
3. **The plaintiff's procedure.** Preparing a case (ערך מִשְׁפָּט), calling and answering, and asking the opponent to state the charge belong to the language of litigation at the gate (cf. Isa 41:1, 21; 43:9, 26) `[T]` / `[I]`.
4. **The stocks.** A wooden frame for the feet of prisoners (Jer 20:2–3; 29:26, a different word) `[T]`. The "marks on the soles" (13:27) may be brands or cords that let a prisoner's tracks be followed `[S]`. *Uncertain.*
5. **The tree that sprouts from the stump.** Olive, sycamore and terebinth regenerate from the stump when cut `[S]` — the sight behind Isa 6:13 and 11:1. *Moderate.*
6. **Sheol.** The realm of the dead, below, from which no one returns (7:9; 10:21) `[T]`. Job's wish is to be hidden there *temporarily* — a thought with no Old Testament precedent in these terms. `[I]`
7. **The bag and the seal.** Valuables and documents were tied in a pouch and sealed (Gen 42:35; 1 Sam 25:29; Jer 32:10–14) `[T]`. A sealed bag can be kept as evidence or put away for good — the ambiguity of 14:17. `[I]`

**What this changes.** Job speaks the language of the courtroom to friends who have played corrupt judges, and the language of a prisoner to a God he thinks is his jailer. Then he reaches for an image he knows from the orchard — the cut tree that grows again — and for a Sheol that might be a hiding-place rather than a prison. `[I]`

### Original Audience Reception

**Canonical audience.** Readers of the Writings with the Torah and the Prophets behind them, who have just read the Psalter, and who know the exile's laments (Ps 44; Lam 3) and the Servant's courtroom (Isa 50). `[I]`

**What they would have heard.**
- **A friend's rebuke that fits their own comforters.** Those who had heard "your suffering proves your sin" would hear 13:4–12 as their own reply. `[I]`
- **Psalm 44 in a single man's mouth.** The communal lament of the faithful-but-defeated, "Why do You hide Your face?", spoken by one righteous man. `[I]`
- **The Servant without his help.** "Who will contend with me? … I know that I will be vindicated" — the Servant's confidence — followed by the moth eating the speaker rather than his enemies (13:28). They would feel the gap. `[I]`
- **A question about the dead in the vocabulary of Isaiah and Daniel.** "Rise", "awake", "live", "dust". A reader who knew Isa 26:19 would hear Job ask the question the prophet had answered. `[I]`

**Expectations created.**
- That God will answer the summons (13:22). He does (38:1).
- That the friends will be judged for their partiality (13:10). They are (42:7–9).
- That the tree's hope (14:7) will return. It does, torn up (19:10).
- That the question of 14:14 will be pressed. It is (19:25–27).

**What would have surprised them.** That a man who calls God his enemy (13:24) also imagines God *longing* for him (14:15) — the only verse in the Hebrew Bible with God as the subject of כסף ("long for"). `[T]` for the data; `[I]` for the surprise.

### Biblical-Theological Themes

**1. Speaking rightly of God — to him, not for him.** `[T]` / `[I]`
- Job 13:3, 7–8 → 42:7–8 ("you have not spoken of Me what is right").
- Advocates for God who say what is untrue are judged (13:9–11; 42:7). The prophets condemn the false "Peace, peace" (Jer 6:14; 8:11) and those who whitewash (טוּחַ, "plaster") a flimsy wall (Ezek 13:10–15) — a different verb, the same image as טפל (13:4). `[T]` for the texts.
- **For the preacher:** the warning of 13:7–11 is addressed to the professional speakers for God.

**2. The righteous sufferer's lawsuit.** `[T]`
- Job 13:18–19 → Isa 50:7–9 → Rom 8:33–34.
- The Servant's courtroom is won before it opens: "He who vindicates Me is near". Job's is not yet. Paul gives the Servant's verdict to all "in Christ Jesus".

**3. The hidden face.** `[T]`
- Deut 31:17–18; 32:20 (God hides his face from the faithless) → Ps 13:2 [Eng 13:1]; 44:25; 88:15 (the faithful ask why) → Job 13:24 → Isa 50:6 (the Servant does not hide his face from shame) → Isa 53:3 ("like one from whom men hide their face") → Num 6:25–26 (the blessing: the LORD make his face shine, lift up his face).
- **The irony of 13:24 and 42:8–9:** Job complains that God hides his face; at the end God "lifts Job's face". `[T]` for the idiom; `[I]` for the bracket.

**4. Covering sin.** `[T]`
- Exod 34:7 → Ps 32:1, 5 → Job 7:21; 13:23; 14:16–17 → Mic 7:18–19 → Rom 4:7–8.
- Job imagines God sealing transgression and plastering over iniquity. The canon's word for the act is "cover" and "bear" (כסה, נשׂא). The atonement is the means Job does not name.

**5. Death as sleep and the hope of waking.** `[T]`
- Job 3:13 (sleep as rest) → 14:12 (sleep without waking) → Ps 44:24 (God asked to wake) → Isa 26:19; Dan 12:2 (the dead awake) → John 11:11–25 → 1 Thess 4:13–14; 1 Cor 15:20, 51–52.
- **For the preacher:** Job's "will he live again?" is the right question; the canon's answer is resurrection, not survival.

**6. The tree and the stump.** `[T]`
- Ps 1:3 (the righteous like a tree by streams of water) → Job 14:7–9 (a tree revived "at the scent of water") → Job 19:10 (hope uprooted like a tree) → Isa 6:13; 11:1; 53:2 → Rom 11:16–24.
- Ps 1:3 and Job 14:9 both have water and the tree; Ps 1 promises the righteous what Job grants only to the tree. `[I]` *Moderate.*

### Christological Reading

*(For the OT passage: the canonical trajectory, offered as raw material for `/point-purpose`.)*

**1. The Servant's courtroom, won (Isa 50:6–9 → Rom 8:33–34).** `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the trajectory
- Job pleads "I know that I will be vindicated … Who will contend with me?" in the Servant's words, without the Servant's help (Headline 2).
- The Servant who did not hide his face (Isa 50:6) is the Christ of the passion: struck, spat on (Mark 14:65; 15:19), silent before his accusers, vindicated in resurrection.
- Paul builds his courtroom from the Servant's: "Who will bring a charge against God's elect? God is the one who justifies; who is the one who condemns? Christ Jesus is He who died, yes, rather who was raised" (Rom 8:33–34) `[S]` for the Isaiah source.
- **Category:** *trajectory* — Job's courtroom, in the Servant's words, finds its verdict in the Servant's work. *Moderate–high.*

**2. "For Your sake we are killed all day long" (Ps 44 → Rom 8:36).** `[T]`
- Job's "Why do You hide Your face?" (13:24) is Psalm 44's. Paul quotes Ps 44:23 of the church in Christ, and answers it: "in all these things we overwhelmingly conquer through Him who loved us" (Rom 8:36–37).
- **Category:** *trajectory* — the innocent sufferer's lament is taken up by those united to the innocent Sufferer. *Moderate–high.*

**3. "If a man dies, will he live again?" (14:14 → John 11).** `[T]` for the Greek; `[I]` for the trajectory
- Job: man "will not be awakened from his sleep" (14:12, Swete, asterisked: οὐκ ἐξυπνισθήσονται). Jesus: "Our friend Lazarus has fallen asleep; but I go, so that I may awaken him (ἐξυπνίσω)" (John 11:11) — the only ἐξυπνίζω in the New Testament. Then: "I am the resurrection and the life; he who believes in Me will live even if he dies" (John 11:25).
- The Old Greek of 14:14 already answers "he will live" (ζήσεται); John 11:25 has the same verb, ζήσεται.
- **Category:** *trajectory*, through the Greek. *Moderate* — the lexical link runs through a Hexaplaric line; the thematic link stands without it.

**4. The stump of Jesse (Isa 11:1 → 14:7–9).** `[I]`
- Job's tree, "cut down", with a dead stump, sprouting "at the scent of water", is the image Isaiah uses for the Messiah from David's felled line. Job grants the tree what he denies to man; in the Messiah the two meet — a Man who is the shoot from the stump, raised from death.
- **Category:** *contrast and trajectory*. *Moderate.*

**5. "You will call, and I will answer You; You will long for the work of Your hands" (14:13–17).** `[I]`
- Job's one sustained hope: to be hidden through wrath, remembered at a set time, called back by a God who longs for his creature, his sin sealed and covered.
- The gospel gives the shape of it: hidden "with Christ in God" (Col 3:3); a set time for the dead to hear his voice ("an hour is coming, in which all who are in the tombs will hear His voice, and will come forth", John 5:28–29); sin covered (Rom 4:7) and its record "canceled … nailed to the cross" (Col 2:14).
- **Category:** *trajectory*. *Moderate.* Do not make Job a believer in these things; let his wish be the shape the gospel fills.

**6. "Born of woman" (14:1 → Gal 4:4).** `[I]`
- Job's phrase for frail mankind (Swete γεννητὸς γυναικός, the phrase of Matt 11:11). "God sent forth His Son, born of a woman (γενόμενον ἐκ γυναικός)" (Gal 4:4) — a different Greek verb, the same human condition. The Son took the "few days and full of trouble" of 14:1. *Uncertain–moderate* as a link; *moderate–high* as theology.

**The moralism check.** The trap is to preach Job as a model of "faith that holds on" from 13:15 alone. Job holds on by arguing, and his hope (14:13–17) is for something God must do. The sermon should point to what God has done in Christ, not to Job's tenacity as our resource. `[I]`

### Difficult / Contested Verses

**13:15 — "Though He slay me".**
- **Category:** textual and doctrinal (a weaponised verse of piety).
- **Honest exegesis:** the qere gives hope; the ketiv gives its absence; both keep the second half, "I will argue my ways to his face". The verse is not resignation. Even on the qere, the hope is the hope of a man who will contend.
- **Handling:** read the whole verse; say that the Hebrew can be read two ways; do not build the sermon on "trust" alone.

**13:24 — "consider me Your enemy".**
- **Category:** doctrinal and pastoral.
- **Honest exegesis:** the language of lament (Ps 44; 88; Lam 2:4–5, "The Lord has become like an enemy"). Elihu will quote it as a charge (33:10). God does not call Job his enemy; he calls him "My servant" (42:7–8).
- **Handling:** let it stand as how it feels; set beside it what God says.

**14:4 — "Who can make the clean out of the unclean? No one!"**
- **Category:** doctrinal (a proof-text for original sin).
- **Honest exegesis:** in context it is a plea, not a doctrine: man is frail and impure by nature, so why scrutinise him (14:3) — "turn Your gaze from him" (14:6). The formula may even be a wish ("Oh that a clean thing could come from the unclean!"). Eliphaz and Bildad make it a doctrine against Job (15:14–16; 25:4–6). The Greek ("who will be clean from filth? not one, even if his life be one day") fed the later tradition.
- **Handling:** the truth of human impurity is biblical (Ps 51:5); this verse uses it to ask for mercy. Preach it as Job uses it, and note that the friends' use of it is rebuked (42:7).

**14:12, 14 — resurrection.**
- **Category:** apologetic.
- **Honest exegesis:** 14:12 denies that the dead wake "until the heavens are no longer"; 14:14 asks whether a man will live again and, in the context of 14:13–17, wishes it. Job does not affirm resurrection here; he reaches for it.
- **Handling:** honest lament, answered by the canon (Isa 26:19; Dan 12:2; John 11). Don't make the verse a doctrine, and don't smooth it away (sweep).

**14:16–17 — surveillance or forgiveness?**
- **Category:** translational, with pastoral weight.
- **Honest exegesis:** the כִּי־עַתָּה pattern and the wish of 14:13 favour the ESV/NIV84 (future, hope of forgiveness). The Greek and NASB95 read present surveillance. The sealed bag is ambiguous (Hos 13:12, sin stored; Dan 9:24, sin ended).
- **Handling:** with the ESV in the pew, preach it as Job's hope; mention the alternative only if the NASB95 is being used. *Moderate–high.*

**14:19, 22 — no hope.**
- **Category:** pastoral.
- **Honest exegesis:** the end of the speech is dark. It is the end of round one, not of the book.
- **Handling:** don't end a sermon at 14:15 as though the speech did. Name the dark, and point forward (19:25; 42:5, 17) and beyond (John 11).

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: "Though He slay me" as serene trust

- **What it looks like:** 13:15a quoted on its own, as the motto of quiet faith in suffering.
- **Why it's wrong:** it rests on a qere (the ketiv reads "I have no hope"), and the second half — "I will argue my ways to his face" — is defiance. The verse is spoken by a man who expects to die for speaking.
- **The corrective:** read the whole verse, and 13:13–14 before it. The faith here is the faith that will not stop speaking to God, even when God seems to be the killer. Say that the Hebrew can be read two ways.

### Pitfall: Preaching against the friends without seeing ourselves

- **What it looks like:** "Job's friends were bad comforters; don't be like them" — and the congregation agrees comfortably.
- **Why it's wrong:** 13:7–11 is aimed at people who speak for God: preachers, elders, Christians with an answer. The friends' fault is not unkindness but untruth in God's defence. That is a preacher's sin.
- **The corrective:** let the warning land on the pulpit first. "Will you speak what is unjust for God?" is a question the preacher must answer before the congregation does.

### Pitfall: Making 14:14 Job's creed

- **What it looks like:** "Job believed in the resurrection: 'If a man dies, shall he live again? … till my renewal should come.'"
- **Why it's wrong:** 14:12 denies it, 14:13–17 wishes for something like it, and 14:18–22 lets the hope erode. Job asks; he does not affirm. (The Old Greek turns the question into an affirmation — an early reading, not the Hebrew.)
- **The corrective:** preach the question as Job's, and the answer as the canon's (Isa 26:19; Dan 12:2) and Christ's (John 11:25). The question is more powerful honestly asked.

### Pitfall: Using 14:4 as a proof-text detached from its plea

- **What it looks like:** "Who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean? Not one" — cited for original sin, and the sermon moves on to doctrine.
- **Why it's wrong:** Job uses human impurity to plead for mercy ("look away from him", 14:6). The friends turn it into an accusation (15:14; 25:4), and they are rebuked (42:7).
- **The corrective:** if the doctrine is drawn, draw it as Job does: our frailty is a reason to ask God for mercy, not a stick to beat the suffering.

### Pitfall: Moralising the tree

- **What it looks like:** "Like the tree, you can bounce back — just find the scent of water."
- **Why it's wrong:** the tree is the contrast: "But man dies" (14:10). The point is that man, unlike the tree, has no natural renewal.
- **The corrective:** let the tree show what we lack, and then the Shoot from the stump (Isa 11:1) who gives it.

### Pitfall: Stopping at 14:15

- **What it looks like:** a sermon on "You would long for the work of your hands" that ends on the high note.
- **Why it's wrong:** the speech ends at 14:22, in erosion and mourning, and the round ends there. Ending early teaches a congregation that lament always resolves in the same breath.
- **The corrective:** read through 14:22. Name the dark. Then point beyond Job's speech to the book's end and to Christ.
