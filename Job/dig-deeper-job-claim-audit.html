Claim Audit: Job — The Book’s Macro-Structure for a Sermon Series

Passages audited: the whole book, at the level of its major units (1:1–42:17) Date: 28 September 2026

Revised: 8 October 2026, v1.1. Searches are now stated in words: the original-language words in their own script with a gloss, the edition searched, and the result; word-index numbers, search commands and script names have been removed. No verdict, count, rank or rating has changed (toolkit amendments round 8). Purpose: To test two structural proposals against the Hebrew text and the early Greek before a sermon series is planned: - (a) Patrick’s nine-unit proposal: 1:1–3:1; 3; 4–14; 15–21; 22–28; 29–31; 32–37; 38:1–42:6 (I and II); 42:7–17. - (b) James Bejon, “The Book of Job: Structure & Message” (Tyndale House, Cambridge; January 2020): an introduction, five fivefold scenes and a conclusion, with the debate divided into five “acts” by the word וַיֹּאמַר (“and he said”).

Primary texts: WLC Hebrew with its lemma index (_texts/hebrew-wlc/03-Ketuvim/02-Job.txt); Swete LXX (Vaticanus); the Logos Rahlfs LXX export with its Origenic signs (asterisk and metobelus), read to tell what the Old Greek had; the BHS apparatus export; SBLGNT. Study text: NASB95. Pulpit text: not yet declared for the series. Book-overview context: Job/book-overview-job.md (v0.1.1), the sweep, and the solo digs on 1:1–22 and 2:1–13 — all checking-stage sources. Secondary source under test: Bejon (2020), uploaded by Patrick. Its citations of Clines, Alter, Ash and Steinmann are reported as Bejon’s and are not checked here.

Warrant tags: [T] derivable from the text itself · [I] a reasonable inference from the text · [S] supplied by a secondary source, held provisionally.

Scope and Method

Why the Hays criteria are adapted here. The Hays criteria test allusions. Most claims in this audit are about structure — where the book divides — so each structural claim is tested instead against the evidence the text itself carries. Six criteria are used:

Criterion What it asks Witness
Formula Is the division marked by a speech or narrative formula — who “answered”, who “continued”, who “said”? WLC wording [T]
Paragraphing Does a petuḥah (פ) or setumah (ס) fall at the proposed seam? Leningrad, as the WLC transcribes it [T]; reading tradition, not authorial; spread unchecked
Register Does the seam coincide with the change between prose and poetry, and between the prose and poetic accent systems? WLC [T] (overview counts)
Content Does a change of speaker, addressee, subject or setting coincide with the seam? WLC [T] / [I]
Greek Does the early translation keep the same division markers, and did the Old Greek (unasterisked in Rahlfs) have them? Swete; Rahlfs export
Verbal architecture Do repeated phrases open or close the unit, or tie it to another? WLC lemma and phrase search [T]

Where a claim is an allusion (Bejon’s 2 Kgs 1 pun; 19:3 and Num 14:22), the Hays criteria are applied briefly. Every count and “only” was checked in the WLC by lemma or normalised phrase, with a positive control.

Verdict scale (the skill’s): Confirmed · Confirmed with nuance · Needs reframing · Uncertain — flag for research · Discard.

Positional Necessity Check (book level)

Preceding movement. In the BHS order Job follows the Psalter, whose promises to “the man who fears the LORD” (Pss 1; 112; 128) the prologue puts under test (Job 1 dig). [I]

Necessity answer for the structure. Job’s architecture is built around a gap in knowledge. The reader is shown heaven (1:6–12; 2:1–7); the speakers never are. So any structure the book carries must do two things. It must mark the move from the frame, where the reader knows, into the poetry, where the speakers argue without knowing. And it must mark the move back into the frame, where God gives his verdict. The seams at 3:1/3:2 and 42:6/42:7 are therefore the book’s load-bearing joints. Every proposal is tested first against them. [I]

Implication. A structure that crosses either of those joints needs strong evidence. Bejon’s “Scene 5, 3:2–42:8” crosses the second.

Four facts this table establishes. [T]

  1. The text uses three different speech formulas, and they are not interchangeable.
    • “Answered and said” (וַיַּעַן … וַיֹּאמַר) introduces a new turn.
    • “Continued” (וַיֹּסֶף) introduces the same speaker going on: Job at 27:1 and 29:1, Elihu at 36:1. These are the only three in the book.
    • The narrator’s “It came about after…” (וַיְהִי אַחַר) opens the epilogue (42:7).
  2. The “whirlwind” formula occurs twice — 38:1 and 40:6 only (both with the ketiv מנ). It is the book’s own way of dividing the divine speech into two.
  3. The narrator speaks twice inside the poetry, both times at the same seam. “The words of Job are ended” (31:40) and “these three men ceased answering Job” (32:1), followed by the prose introduction of Elihu (32:2–5).
  4. The Old Greek had every one of these markers. None of the formula verses carries an asterisk in the Rahlfs export (27:1, 29:1, 32:1, 36:1, 38:1, 40:1, 40:3, 42:1). The Greek preserves the identical 27:1 / 29:1 formula and both storm openings. It also adds two readings of the seams: “Job ceased from words” at 31:40/32:1, and “after Elihu ceased speaking” at 38:1. The Greek thus confirms, not merely preserves, the divisions at 31/32 and 37/38.

Claims Audited — Patrick’s Proposal

P1: Unit 1 is the narrative prologue, 1:1–3:1

Claim: “Narrative — scenes in heaven and on earth — Job 1:1–3:1.”

Criterion Score Reasoning
Formula Strong 3:1 is narrative (“Afterward Job opened his mouth”); 3:2 opens the first speech with “answered and said”
Paragraphing Strong פ after 3:1
Register Strong Prose accents throughout 1:1–3:1 (pashta and zaqef; no dehi or ole); poetic accents from 3:2 (overview counts, WLC)
Content Strong Heaven and earth alternate (1:1–5 earth; 1:6–12 heaven; 1:13–22 earth; 2:1–7 heaven; 2:7–13 earth), and 3:1 ends it with Job’s curse on his day
Greek Possible Swete joins 3:1 to 3:2 (“…cursed his day, saying”), reading 3:1 as the introduction to the lament rather than the end of the prologue
Verbal architecture Strong וַיְהִי הַיּוֹם (“now there was a day”) marks 1:6, 1:13 and 2:1 — its only three occurrences in Job (phrase search)

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning: 3:1 is a hinge. In the Hebrew it closes the prologue: a prose verse with a paragraph marker, whose verb קלל (“curse”) is the narrator’s first use of the real word after four euphemisms (1:5, 11; 2:5, 9). In the Greek it opens the lament. Either way, 1:1–3:1 is a sound unit. For preaching it is two chapters, each of which has already needed a full solo dig. Leningrad divides it into four paragraphs: 1:1–5 | 1:6–22 | 2:1–10 | 2:11–3:1.

Overview action: None to the boundary. The series plan below discusses whether one sermon can carry both chapters.

P2: Unit 2 is Job’s lament, chapter 3

Claim: “Job’s lament — Job 3.”

Criterion Score Reasoning
Formula Strong 3:2 “Job answered and said”, though no one has spoken (Bejon notes this too); 4:1 “Eliphaz answered”
Paragraphing Strong פ after 3:1 and 3:26
Register Strong The first poetry in the book
Content Strong No second person in the whole chapter (a form-search of 3:2–26 returned no address). Job speaks to no one — not to the friends and not to God. It is a soliloquy
Greek Strong Preserved
Verbal architecture Strong God’s speeches answer chapter 3 point by point: the doors of the womb, the eyelids of the dawn, Leviathan roused, “free” and “taskmaster”, deep darkness (sweep, Headline Finding 1; all verified)

Verdict: Confirmed.

Reasoning: Formally chapter 3 is the first turn of the exchange, and Eliphaz answers it (4:1). Functionally it stands apart as the only speech in the dialogue addressed to no one. And the book’s climax answers it directly (38–41). It deserves its own sermon.

Overview action: None.

P3–P4: Units 3 and 4 are the first two cycles, 4–14 and 15–21

Claim: “First set of interactions between Job and his friends — Job 4–14”; “Second set — Job 15–21.”

Criterion Score Reasoning
Formula Strong Eliphaz (4:1; 15:1), Bildad (8:1; 18:1), Zophar (11:1; 20:1), each followed by Job’s reply (6:1; 9:1; 12:1 — 16:1; 19:1; 21:1)
Paragraphing Neutral Every change of speaker is marked, so the markers do not privilege one grouping
Content Strong The friends speak in the same order in both rounds, and Eliphaz opens each. Eliphaz’s opening of round 2 (15:2 “Should a wise man answer with windy knowledge?”) answers Job’s closing speech of round 1 (12:2 “Truly then you are the people, and with you wisdom will die!”). Job’s speeches 12–14 answer Zophar and all three together (plural address, 12:2; 13:4–5)
Greek Strong All formulas present and unasterisked
Verbal architecture Possible 14:22 and 21:34 end on death and on the friends’ “falsehood” (21:34); a thematic close, not a formula

Verdict: Confirmed.

Reasoning: The text marks turns, not rounds. The rounds are read off the repeated order of speakers, and Eliphaz’s position at the head of each is the clearest signal. God also addresses Eliphaz first at the end (42:7). A cycle that runs “friend speaks, Job replies” keeps each of Job’s speeches with the speech it answers. That is the traditional division, and Patrick’s.

Overview action: None.

P5: Unit 5 is the third cycle with the wisdom poem, 22–28

Claim: “Third set of interactions — Job 22–28.”

Criterion Score Reasoning
Formula Strong (with a twist) Eliphaz (22:1), Job (23:1), Bildad (25:1), Job (26:1) — then Job “continued” (27:1), not “answered”. There is no Zophar: his formula occurs only at 11:1 and 20:1
Paragraphing Strong No marker between 27:23 and 28:1, so the wisdom poem is attached to the 27:1 discourse. ס after 28:28 closes it
Content Possible The cycle collapses: Bildad’s last speech is six verses (25:1–6), Zophar has none, and Job’s closing words in 27 sound like the friends’ doctrine (27:13 opens with Zophar’s closing words from 20:29)
Greek Strong 27:1 and 29:1 carry the same Greek formula. Chapter 28 is present in the Old Greek, though a number of its half-lines are asterisked (14 asterisks in the Rahlfs export)
Verbal architecture Strong The 27:1 formula opens and the 29:1 formula re-opens; 28:28 ends on “the fear of the Lord … to depart from evil”, the words of 1:1

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning: The text supports ending the unit at 28:28 — a ס marker and a fresh formula at 29:1. But the formulas group the material slightly differently from “a third cycle”: - 22–26 is the third round of turns. - 27–28 is Job’s first “continued discourse” (27:1), and 29–31 his second (29:1). The two are bracketed by the identical formula.

So ch 28 belongs with ch 27 as part of Job’s own discourse. It is not a separate speaker, and it is not part of the friends’ round. Patrick’s unit 5 (22–28) holds together as “the debate runs out, and wisdom is not found by argument”. A preacher could equally take 22–27 and 28 as two sermons; the text allows both. It does not support attaching 28 to 29–31, because 29:1 re-opens.

Overview action: None to the boundary. The overview’s contested judgement 2 (“Who speaks chapter 28?”) should add that 27:1 and 29:1 are the only two occurrences of the formula, in Hebrew and in Greek.

P6: Unit 6 is Job’s closing statement, 29–31, and 29:1 continues from 28

Claim: “Job’s closing statement — Job 29–31 (although it seems, Job 29:1, that this is a continuation of Job 28).”

Criterion Score Reasoning
Formula Strong 29:1 וַיֹּסֶף אִיּוֹב שְׂאֵת מְשָׁלוֹ וַיֹּאמַר (“Then Job continued his discourse and said”) — continuation
Paragraphing Strong ס before (28:28); פ after 31:40
Register Strong Closed by the narrator’s own words, תַּמּוּ דִּבְרֵי אִיּוֹב (“The words of Job are ended”, 31:40) — a closure formula found nowhere else in the book
Content Strong Then and now (29–30) and the oath of clearance (31), ending in Job’s signed demand that “the Almighty answer me” (31:35)
Greek Strong Same formula; the Greek repeats the closure at 32:1 (“And Job ceased from words”)

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning: Patrick’s instinct is right: וַיֹּסֶף means Job “continued, went on” — not a reply to anyone. But what 29:1 continues is Job’s discourse, begun again at 27:1, rather than chapter 28 as such. That discourse had also continued through chapter 28 (no marker at 28:1). Chapters 27–31 are therefore one long closing speech by Job in two panels, 27–28 and 29–31, each opened by the same formula and the whole closed by the narrator at 31:40. The sermon division at 28/29 is sound; the preacher should say that 29–31 is Job speaking on, not a new round.

Overview action: None.

P7: Unit 7 is Elihu, 32–37

Claim: “Elihu’s words — Job 32–37.”

Criterion Score Reasoning
Formula Strong Narrator’s prose introduction (32:1–5); “Elihu answered” (32:6; 34:1; 35:1); “Elihu continued” (36:1)
Paragraphing Strong פ after 31:40, 32:1, 32:5 and 37:24
Register Strong The only narrator’s prose between 3:2 and 42:6 (32:1–5), though Leningrad gives it poetic accents (overview)
Content Strong A new speaker, angry four times (32:2–5), never mentioned again
Greek Strong “After Elihu ceased speaking” (38:1) — the Greek marks the end of the unit explicitly

Verdict: Confirmed.

Reasoning: No seam in the book is better marked than 31:40/32:1. It carries the narrator’s closure of Job’s words, the narrator’s note of the friends’ silence, a prose introduction, and three paragraph markers in six verses. The Greek marks the other end.

Overview action: None.

P8: Unit 8 is the LORD’s speeches and Job’s replies, 38:1–42:6, in two parts, answering chapter 3

Claim: “I. God’s first speech to Job, and Job’s humble response, and II. God’s second speech and Job’s confession — Job 38:1–42:6 (confirmed as the intended range) — in which God answers Job’s lament (Job 3).”

Criterion Score Reasoning
Formula Strong “The LORD answered Job out of the whirlwind” at 38:1 and 40:6 only. “Job answered the LORD” at 40:3 and 42:1 only. God is the subject of ענה (“answer”) only in these three verses: 38:1; 40:1; 40:6
Paragraphing Strong פ after 39:30, 40:2 and 40:5 (Part I’s speech, its closing challenge, Job’s reply); ס after 41:26 [Eng 41:34]; פ after 42:6 (Part II ends)
Register Strong Poetry to 42:6; prose from 42:7
Content Strong Part I: creation from foundations to the eagle (38:4–39:30), closing challenge (40:1–2), Job’s “I am insignificant … I lay my hand on my mouth” (40:3–5). Part II: “Will you condemn Me?” (40:8), Behemoth and Leviathan, Job’s “now my eye sees You” (42:1–6)
Greek Strong Both storm openings kept: “through whirlwind and clouds” (38:1), “out of the cloud” (40:6)
Verbal architecture Strong The answer to chapter 3: six verbal links, each verified (sweep and Job 1 dig)

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance — the nuance concerns the label “confession” for 42:1–6.

Reasoning: The book itself divides the divine speech in two, with the whirlwind formula at 38:1 and 40:6, and gives Job a reply after each. The claim that God answers chapter 3 is among the best-attested findings of the sweep. The nuance: 42:6 is a crux. The verb אֶמְאַס has no object (“I retract” NASB95; “I despise myself” ESV and NIV84), and וְנִחַמְתִּי can mean “repent” or “be comforted”. “Confession” is fair for 42:2–5; for 42:6 the preacher should show the options (Job 2 dig and sweep, Unit 16).

Overview action: None to the boundary.

P9: Unit 9 is the prose epilogue, 42:7–17

Claim: “Narrative — Job 42:7–17.”

Criterion Score Reasoning
Formula Strong The narrator’s וַיְהִי אַחַר דִּבֶּר יְהוָה (“It came about after the LORD had spoken these words to Job”)
Paragraphing Strong פ after 42:6; inside, ס after 42:11 and 42:15 (42:7–11
Register Strong Prose accents resume at 42:7
Content Strong Verdict, intercession, restoration, death
Greek Strong “It happened after the Lord had spoken all these words to Job”; the Greek appendix (42:17a–e) extends the unit, and nowhere shifts the seam

Verdict: Confirmed.

Overview action: None.

Claims Audited — Bejon (2020)

B1: The YHWH–Satan dialogues are fivefold and alternating (1:7–12; 2:2–6)

Claim: YHWH speaks (1:7a), Satan answers (1:7b), YHWH speaks (1:8), Satan responds with a wager (1:9), YHWH accepts (1:12); and the same in 2:2–6.

Test: The speech formulas give exactly five turns in each council: וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה (1:7a, 8, 12; 2:2a, 3, 6) and וַיַּעַן הַשָּׂטָן (1:7b, 9; 2:2b, 4). [T]

Verdict: Confirmed. The ABABA shape begins and ends with God. That matches the book’s larger claim that God is first mover and final arbiter.

B2: YHWH is never said to “answer” Satan (note 4)

Claim: “ויען … ויאמר” is frequent, yet “YHWH is never said to ‘answer’ (ענה) Satan … he is not a God who is answerable to his creation.”

Test: - The Satan “answers” four times (1:7, 9; 2:2, 4); God only “says” to him. [T] - But God is the subject of ענה (“answer”) three times in the book — all with Job as object: “the LORD answered Job” (38:1; 40:1; 40:6). [T]

Verdict: Needs reframing. The observation is correct and significant. But the text’s point is the reverse of Bejon’s gloss: God does not answer the Accuser, and he does answer Job, in the same verb, three times. Job asked for an answer (13:22; 31:35); the Accuser gets none. A better formulation: God will not be answerable to the Accuser; he chooses to answer his servant.

B3: The Satan’s “roaming” (שׁוּט) sounds like the “scourge” (שׁוֹט) of 9:23 (note 5)

Test: - The verb שׁוּט (“to go to and fro”, 1:7; 2:2) and the noun שׁוֹט (“whip, scourge”) are different lemmas with the same consonants. - The noun occurs in Job at 5:21 (Eliphaz: “the scourge of the tongue”) and 9:23 (Job: “If the scourge kills suddenly, He mocks the despair of the innocent”) (lemma).

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. The play is audible, but it is sound-play between two words, not one word repeated. Add 5:21. Moderate.

B4: The four messengers alternate human and divine disasters, and escalate 1–2–3–4

Claim: Human, God, human, God. The sources run from one (Sheba, a singular noun with singular verbs, “rather than the expected Sheva’im”), to two (שָׁמַיִם, “a dual”), to three (“three bands”), to four (“four corners”).

Test: - Alternation. Human (1:15), “fire of God from heaven” (1:16), human (1:17), “a great wind from across the wilderness” (1:19) [T]. Confirmed. - Singular Sheba. וַתִּפֹּל שְׁבָא וַתִּקָּחֵם — a people’s name with feminine singular verbs [T]. The plural gentilic שְׁבָאִים does exist (Joel 4:8 [Eng 3:8], lemma), so the singular is a choice. But feminine singular verbs with a people’s name are ordinary Hebrew. - “Dual” heavens. The dual reading of שָׁמַיִם is a grammatical debate [S], not a datum. - Three bands (שְׁלֹשָׁה רָאשִׁים) and four corners (אַרְבַּע פִּנּוֹת) — [T].

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. The alternation is strong. The 1–2–3–4 escalation is suggestive at the ends (three bands, four corners) and weak in the middle (“dual” heavens). Moderate.

B5: The disasters “fall”, and Job “falls” to worship

Test: נפל (“fall”) at 1:15, 16, 19, 20 — four times (lemma). [T]

Verdict: Confirmed. It was independently found in the Job 1 dig (Headline 2).

B6: 1:21 is fivefold, the fifth line the surprise

Test: Five lines: came naked / return naked / the LORD gave / the LORD took / blessed be the name. The fifth is the only one that is not a statement of fact. It uses ברך (“bless”) in its literal sense after the Accuser had predicted its curse-sense (1:11). [T]

Verdict: Confirmed.

B7: 3:1’s “cursed” (וַיְקַלֵּל) creates suspense, since Job curses his day, not God (note 8)

Test: קלל (“curse”) appears in chapters 1–3 only at 3:1 (lemma). The narrator has used the euphemism ברך for “curse” four times (1:5, 11; 2:5, 9) and uses the real word once — of Job’s day. [T]

Verdict: Confirmed. The suspense is a reader-effect [I]; the vocabulary contrast is [T].

B8: 2:7–3:1 is fivefold: the accuser tempts, Job resists, the wife tempts, Job resists, the friends visit

Test: - In 2:7 the Accuser does not “tempt” Job; he “struck” him (וַיַּךְ). No resistance is narrated after 2:7–8: Job takes a potsherd and sits in the ashes. - The wife’s temptation and Job’s resistance (2:9–10) are explicit. - Leningrad divides the section 2:7–10 | 2:11–3:1.

Verdict: Needs reframing. The text supports a three-part sequence: the blow (2:7–8), the wife and Job’s refusal (2:9–10), the friends and the silence (2:11–13) — with 3:1 as the hinge. Acts 1–2 of Bejon’s five are not in the text.

B9: The friends’ silence is portrayed “quite ambiguously” (note 10)

Claim: “Job had no-one to speak to him (אין דבר אליו), as if someone should have spoken.”

Test: - The clause is וְאֵין־דֹּבֵר אֵלָיו דָּבָר (“with no one speaking a word to him”), and the narrator gives the reason at once: כִּי רָאוּ כִּי־גָדַל הַכְּאֵב מְאֹד (“for they saw that his pain was very great”, 2:13). That is a sympathetic inside view. - Job himself later says, “O that you would be completely silent, and that it would become your wisdom!” (13:5). [T]

Verdict: Discard as stated. The text frames the silence as their wisdom, both through the narrator’s reason clause and through Job’s own verdict (Job 2 dig, Headline 5).

B10: וַיְהִי הַיּוֹם marks each new scene, except the last

Test: וַיְהִי הַיּוֹם occurs in Job at 1:6, 1:13 and 2:1 only (phrase search). No marker introduces 2:7. [T]

Verdict: Confirmed.

B11: The five scenes of 1:1–3:1 have these boundaries: 1:1–5 | 1:6–12 | 1:13–21 | 2:1–5 | 2:6–3:1

Test: - 1:1–5 — confirmed (פ after 1:5). - 1:13–21 — should be 1:13–22. 1:22 is the narrator’s verdict closing the scene, and the פ falls after 1:22. Bejon himself writes “1.13–22” elsewhere in the paper. - 2:1–5 — should be 2:1–6. By Bejon’s own analysis (B1) the dialogue’s fifth element is YHWH’s acceptance in 2:6; and the scene changes at 2:7: “So Satan went out from the presence of the LORD”. - 2:6–3:1 — therefore 2:7–3:1.

Verdict: Needs reframing — boundaries corrected to 1:1–5 | 1:6–12 | 1:13–22 | 2:1–6 | 2:7–3:1. The five-scene analysis of the prologue then stands and is well supported.

B12: The whole book is an introduction, five scenes, and a conclusion, with Scene 5 = 3:2–42:8 and the conclusion 42:9–17

Test: - The book’s two register seams are 3:1/3:2 and 42:6/42:7 (accents, paragraph markers, the narrator’s formula at 42:7, and the Greek). Bejon’s Scene 5 honours the first and crosses the second. - There is no paragraph marker at 42:8/42:9. Leningrad’s paragraph runs 42:7–11, and 42:9 is the friends’ compliance with the command of 42:8. - For a sermon series, a thirty-nine-chapter “scene” is not a unit.

Verdict: Needs reframing. The macro-shape “frame — debate — frame” is right, and the alternating five-scene prologue is well founded (after B11’s corrections). But the debate runs 3:2–42:6, and the conclusion 42:7–17.

B13: Introduction and conclusion mirror each other in reverse order (1:1–5 ↔︎ 42:9–17)

Claim: Integrity (1:1) ↔︎ death (42:16); descendants (1:2) ↔︎ descendants (42:13); cattle (1:3) ↔︎ cattle (42:12); feasts (1:4) ↔︎ feast (42:11); sacrifices for his sons (1:5) ↔︎ prayer for his friends (42:9).

Test: - The order does reverse. The conclusion runs: sacrifice and intercession (42:8–10) → a meal in Job’s house (42:11) → livestock (42:12) → children (42:13–15) → long life and death (42:16–17). - Verbal ties: - seven sons and three daughters (1:2 = 42:13) - the livestock exactly doubled (1:3 → 42:12) - עֹלָה (“burnt offering”, 1:5 → 42:8) - eating in the house (לֶאֱכֹל, 1:4; וַיֹּאכְלוּ לֶחֶם בְּבֵיתוֹ, 42:11) - The weakest pair is 1:1 ↔︎ 42:16–17, with no shared vocabulary. Bejon’s own point is that integrity is not restated (B17).

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. The chiastic order is real and verified (moderate–high). The inner pair should read 1:5 ↔︎ 42:8–10, not 42:9, since the offering is commanded in 42:8 and the prayer and its effect are 42:10. The outer pair is positional only.

B14: Job offered “seven sacrifices … on behalf of his (seven) sons” (1:5)

Test: The text says “offering burnt offerings according to the number of them all (מִסְפַּר כֻּלָּם)” (1:5). The sisters were at the feasts (1:4), and “them” are the children Job “consecrated”. No number is given; if it counts all the children, it is ten. The friends’ offering is seven bulls and seven rams (42:8). [T]

Verdict: Discard as stated. The number seven is not in 1:5. The link between 1:5 and 42:8 through עֹלָה stands.

B15: At 42:8, נְבָלָה qualifies God’s action and may mean “destruction” (Mishnaic Hebrew) (note 16)

Test: - עשׂה נְבָלָה (“to do an outrage, a disgraceful thing”) occurs at Gen 34:7; Deut 22:21; Josh 7:15; Judg 19:23, 24; 20:6, 10; 2 Sam 13:12; Isa 32:6; Jer 29:23; Job 42:8 (lemma co-occurrence). In every other case the subject is human. Only at Job 42:8 is it God: לְבִלְתִּי עֲשׂוֹת עִמָּכֶם נְבָלָה (“so that I may not do with you [an outrage / according to folly]”). [T] - The Job 2 dig found the cognate נְבָלוֹת (“foolish women”) at 2:10.

Verdict: Needs reframing. Bejon’s syntactic observation is right, and more striking than he says: God is the subject of an idiom used everywhere else of human outrage. The Mishnaic sense “destruction” [S] is not needed. The biblical idiom already gives “that I may not deal disgracefully with you”. The folly sense still sounds through, via 2:10.

B16: Job’s children are not doubled because they are not lost — they will rise (19:25–26)

Test: - The non-doubling is [T]: seven sons and three daughters (1:2; 42:13), against doubled livestock. - The explanation is [I] at best. 19:25–26 is a crux of the Hebrew, and it concerns Job, not his children. The Greek appendix (Swete 42:17a) reads resurrection for Job (“it is written that he will rise again”), not for the children. - 42:13’s anomalous שִׁבְעָנָה (“seven”) is read by some as “fourteen” [S].

Verdict: Uncertain — flag for research. The explanation is pastorally powerful (Bejon’s note 17), but the text does not state it.

B17: Job’s integrity (תֻּמָּה) is not restated in the conclusion, because it was never lost

Test: תֻּמָּה occurs at 2:3, 9; 27:5; 31:6 and not in chapter 42 (lemma). [T]

Verdict: Confirmed (the absence). The reason is [I].

B18: The feasts and the servants are not restored (with a suggested link to Noah’s wine, Gen 9)

Test: - מִשְׁתֶּה (“feast”) occurs only at 1:4, 5; עֲבֻדָּה (“servants”) only at 1:3 in Job (lemma). Neither returns in chapter 42. [T] - No vocabulary links 42 to Gen 9.

Verdict: the absences Confirmed; the Gen 9 inference Discard — no textual warrant.

B19: 42:10’s qatal (שָׁב) parallels 1:4’s habitual (הָלְכוּ), so the restoration was gradual (note 18)

Test: - 1:4 וְהָלְכוּ is a weqatal — the habitual form (“used to go”). - 42:10 וַיהוָה שָׁב is subject + qatal. It is a different construction, not habitual, and typically marks a completed or background action. - The temporal sense “as he prayed” comes from the infinitive בְּהִתְפַּלְלוֹ (“when he prayed”), not from the verb. [T]

Verdict: Discard the grammatical parallel. A gradual restoration remains a possible reading [I], but it cannot rest on the verb form.

B20: The debate is divided by וַיֹּאמַר into four six-speech acts and a fifth five-speech act

Claim: - Act 1: 3:2 – 11:1 - Act 2: 12:1 – 20:1 - Act 3: 21:1 – 27:1 - Act 4: 29:1 (Job), then Elihu at 32:6, 34:1, 35:1 and 36:1, then YHWH at 38:1 - Act 5: YHWH 40:1, Job 40:3, YHWH 40:6, Job 42:1, YHWH 42:7

Test: - The inventory is exact. All 29 speech-introductions are listed correctly: 28 from 3:2 to 42:1, plus 42:7. [T] - But the grouping into sixes ignores what the formulas themselves distinguish. - Continued (וַיֹּסֶף) is not answered (וַיַּעַן). Act 3’s sixth “speech” (27:1) and Act 4’s first (29:1) are the same speaker continuing, under an identical formula. The text binds them together, and Bejon puts them in different acts. - The narrator’s closure at 31:40 and prose at 32:1–5 fall inside Act 4. The best-marked seam in the book (P7) is invisible to a count of וַיֹּאמַר. - The first divine speech is split. 38:1 (Act 4) and 40:1 (Act 5) belong to one speech. The storm formula marks 38:1 and 40:6 as the two openings, and 40:1–2 is the first speech’s closing challenge (פ after 39:30 and 40:2). - Act 5 crosses the poetry/prose seam. 42:7 opens with the narrator’s וַיְהִי אַחַר and prose accents. - Acts 1 and 2 begin with Job and end with Zophar. That separates each of Job’s replies to Zophar (12–14) from the speech it answers. - Greek: every formula is in the Old Greek, and the Greek adds marks at exactly the seams the sixfold grouping ignores: 32:1 and 38:1.

Verdict: Needs reframing. Bejon’s inventory of speech formulas is accurate and useful, and his fivefold Act 5 (by formula) is correct in form. But the grouping into acts of six is imposed on the text. The text’s own discriminators give the macro-structure Patrick proposes: - the three speech formulas; - the narrator’s closure at 31:40; - the prose introduction of Elihu; - the twin storm formula; - the prose seam at 42:7.

B21: “Zophar declines to speak” in the third round

Test: The formula for Zophar stands only at 11:1 and 20:1 (phrase search). [T] “Declines” is [I].

Verdict: Confirmed (the absence).

B22: At 2:7 the Satan leaves heaven and acts directly on earth

Test: In chapter 1 the harm comes through agents and elements. At 2:7, “Satan went out … and smote (וַיַּךְ) Job” himself. [T]

Verdict: Confirmed.

B23: Scene 3 teases with heavenly news: מַלְאָךְ (“angel / messenger”, 1:14) and an expected “man of God” (אִישׁ אֱלֹהִים) that turns out to be “the fire of God” (אֵשׁ אֱלֹהִים, 1:16) (note 11)

Test: - מַלְאָךְ occurs in Job at 1:14 (a human messenger) and 4:18 and 33:23 (heavenly beings) (lemma). [T] - The אִישׁ / אֵשׁ play has a canonical precedent Bejon does not cite. In 2 Kgs 1:10, 12, Elijah says, “If I am a man of God (אִישׁ אֱלֹהִים), let fire (אֵשׁ) come down…”, and “the fire of God (אֵשׁ אֱלֹהִים) came down”. אִישׁ with אֱלֹהִים and אֵשׁ in one verse occurs only in 2 Kgs 1:10, 12, and אֵשׁ אֱלֹהִים only at 2 Kgs 1:12 and Job 1:16 (lemma and phrase search).

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance — strengthened. The pun is Kings’ before it is Job’s, and Job 1:16 activates it. Moderate–high.

B24: Job says the friends have reproached him “ten times” (19:3), though they have spoken only five times

Test: - Five friends’ speeches precede chapter 19 (4–5; 8; 11; 15; 18). [T] - But זֶה עֶשֶׂר פְּעָמִים (“these ten times”) is an exact phrase shared only with Num 14:22, where the LORD says Israel “have put Me to the test these ten times” (phrase search).

Hays (brief): availability strong; volume strong (exact three-word phrase, only two occurrences); thematic coherence possible (testing / reproach).

Verdict: Needs reframing. “Ten times” is an idiom — possibly an allusion to the wilderness testing of God — not a count to be compared with five. Moderate.

B25: Satan and the friends both think mechanistically; חִנָּם means “without a cause visible from that perspective”

Test: חִנָּם at 1:9 (the Accuser) and 2:3 (God) is [T]. Bejon’s reading that חִנָּם is perspective-relative (the cause is Job’s integrity, or God’s hidden purpose) is [I].

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. It is a fruitful reading and consistent with the text. But the text lets God say “without cause” without supplying the hidden cause, and the preacher should not supply it either.

B26: Job’s structure is “irreducible”, which tells against multiple authorship

Test: This is a claim about composition, beyond the text’s own statements [S]. The audit finds real, verified patterning (B1, B5, B6, B10, B13) alongside patterns imposed on the text (B8, B20).

Verdict: Uncertain — flag for research. The verified patterning supports reading the book as a crafted whole. It cannot settle authorship. Bejon himself concedes that “the book of Job never seems to rigidly conform to any one pattern”.

What Both Proposals Missed

  1. The “continued” formula. Neither proposal names וַיֹּסֶף (“continued”) as a separate formula. It occurs only at 27:1, 29:1 and 36:1, and it is the text’s own statement that 27–31 is one closing discourse by Job in two panels. Patrick’s note on 29:1 comes closest. [T]
  2. The twin storm formula (38:1; 40:6). Patrick’s I/II division tracks it without naming it; Bejon’s Acts 4–5 split across it. It is the clearest division of the divine speeches. [T]
  3. The Greek confirms the major seams by adding to them. “And Job ceased from words” (31:40/32:1) and “after Elihu ceased speaking” (38:1) are the translators’ own markings of Patrick’s units 6/7 and 7/8. And the Old Greek had every formula (none asterisked). [T] (Swete; Rahlfs)
  4. God “answers” only Job. God is the subject of ענה three times (38:1; 40:1; 40:6), always with Job as object. This sharpens both Bejon’s note 4 and Patrick’s unit 8. [T]
  5. Chapter 3 addresses no one. It has no second person. That sets the lament apart from every speech that follows, all of which address the friends, God, or both. [T] (form search)

Two options, both text-supported, if the series can take more or needs fewer.

  • Ten sermons: split unit 5 into 22:1–27:23 and 28:1–28. The text allows it: ch 28 begins without a marker but ends with one, and it stands as a poem with its own refrain (28:12 = 28:20).
  • Eight sermons: join units 6 and 7 only if necessary. The text does not favour this — 31:40/32:1 is the best-marked seam in the book.

Guardrail for a nine-sermon series. Units 3, 4 and 5 are eleven, seven and seven chapters long. Preach them from their anchor readings, and let the whole be read aloud in the week before, or summarised. Their argument lives in the turns, which the anchors should show: who answers whom, and where Job turns to God.

Open Questions

  1. Rahlfs half-line asterisks in 28 and 32–37. The Old Greek had all the unit markers, but was shorter inside the units (for example 14 asterisks in 28, 23 in 36). Does that bear on the series? Not on the boundaries.
  2. Whether 3:1 opens or closes. Hebrew: it closes the prologue. Greek: it opens the lament. Both readings are available to the preacher.
  3. The children not doubled (B16) and the gradual restoration (B19) — interpretive questions for the epilogue sermon, not structural ones. They need a commentary check (Clines 2013 is cited by Bejon; unverified here).
  4. Apparatus spread. Every paragraph-marker datum is from Leningrad as the WLC transcribes it. A Logos pass on the petuḥot at 1:22, 2:10, 28:28, 31:40, 32:1 and 42:6 would move them from [unchecked — apparatus spread] to cited.
  5. Bejon’s citations (Clines, Alter, Ash, Steinmann, Newsom, Timmer) were not checked. Where the audit agrees with a claim he attributes to them, the agreement rests on the text, not on the citation.
