### Chapter 29 — then (29:1–25)

*No paragraph marker after 29:25 in BHS.*

**29:1** וַיֹּ֣סֶף אִ֭יֹּוב שְׂאֵ֥ת מְשָׁלֹ֗ו וַיֹּאמַֽר׃  
And Job again took up his discourse and said,

**29:2** מִֽי־יִתְּנֵ֥נִי כְיַרְחֵי־קֶ֑דֶם כִּ֝ימֵ֗י אֱלֹ֣והַּ יִשְׁמְרֵֽנִי׃  
Oh that I were as in months gone by, / As in the days when God watched over me;

**29:3** בְּהִלֹּ֣וᵃ נֵ֭רֹו עֲלֵ֣י רֹאשִׁ֑י לְ֝אֹורֹוᵇ אֵ֣לֶךְ חֹֽשֶׁךְ׃  
When His lamp shone over my head, / And by His light I walked through darkness;

**29:4** כַּאֲשֶׁ֣ר הָ֭יִיתִי בִּימֵ֣י חָרְפִּ֑י בְּסֹ֥ודᵃ אֱ֝לֹ֗והַּ עֲלֵ֣י אָהֳלִֽי׃  
As I was in the prime of my days, / When the friendship of God was over my tent;

**29:5** בְּעֹ֣וד שַׁ֭דַּי עִמָּדִ֑י סְבִ֖יבֹותַ֣י נְעָרָֽי׃  
When the Almighty was yet with me, / And my children were around me;

**29:6** בִּרְחֹ֣ץ הֲלִיכַ֣י בְּחֵמָ֑הᵃ וְצ֥וּר יָצ֥וּק עִ֝מָּדִ֗י פַּלְגֵי־שָֽׁמֶן׃  
When my steps were bathed in butter, / And the rock poured out for me streams of oil!

**29:7** בְּצֵ֣אתִי שַׁ֣עַר עֲלֵי־קָ֑רֶת בָּ֝רְחֹ֗וב אָכִ֥ין מֹושָׁבִֽי׃  
When I went out to the gate of the city, / When I took my seat in the square,

**29:8** רָא֣וּנִי נְעָרִ֣ים וְנֶחְבָּ֑אוּ וִֽ֝ישִׁישִׁים קָ֣מוּ עָמָֽדוּ׃  
The young men saw me and hid themselves, / And the old men arose and stood.

**29:9** שָׂ֭רִים עָצְר֣וּ בְמִלִּ֑ים וְ֝כַ֗ף יָשִׂ֥ימוּ לְפִיהֶֽם׃  
The princes stopped talking / And put their hands on their mouths;

**29:10** קֹול־נְגִידִ֥ים נֶחְבָּ֑אוּ וּ֝לְשֹׁונָ֗ם לְחִכָּ֥ם דָּבֵֽקָה׃  
The voice of the nobles was hushed, / And their tongue stuck to their palate.

**29:11** כִּ֤י אֹ֣זֶן שָׁ֭מְעָה וַֽתְּאַשְּׁרֵ֑נִי וְעַ֥יִןᵃ רָ֝אֲתָ֗ה וַתְּעִידֵֽנִי׃  
For when the ear heard, it called me blessed, / And when the eye saw, it gave witness of me,

**29:12** כִּֽי־אֲ֭מַלֵּט עָנִ֣י מְשַׁוֵּ֑עַ וְ֝יָתֹ֗ום וְֽלֹאᵃ־עֹזֵ֥ר לֹֽו׃  
Because I delivered the poor who cried for help, / And the orphan who had no helper.

**29:13** בִּרְכַּ֣ת אֹ֭בֵד עָלַ֣י תָּבֹ֑א וְלֵ֖ב אַלְמָנָ֣ה אַרְנִֽן׃  
The blessing of the one ready to perish came upon me, / And I made the widow’s heart sing for joy.

**29:14** צֶ֣דֶק לָ֭בַשְׁתִּי וַיִּלְבָּשֵׁ֑נִי כִּמְעִ֥יל וְ֝צָנִ֗יף מִשְׁפָּטִֽי׃  
I put on righteousness, and it clothed me; / My justice was like a robe and a turban.

**29:15** עֵינַ֣יִם הָ֭יִיתִי לַֽעִוֵּ֑ר וְרַגְלַ֖יִם לַפִּסֵּ֣חַ אָֽנִי׃  
I was eyes to the blind / And feet to the lame.

**29:16** אָ֣ב אָ֭נֹכִֽי לָֽאֶבְיֹונִ֑ים וְרִ֖ב לֹא־יָדַ֣עְתִּי אֶחְקְרֵֽהוּ׃  
I was a father to the needy, / And I investigated the case which I did not know.

**29:17** וָֽ֭אֲשַׁבְּרָה מְתַלְּעֹ֣ות עַוָּ֑ל וּ֝מִשִּׁנָּ֗יו אַשְׁלִ֥יךְᵃ טָֽרֶף׃  
I broke the jaws of the wicked / And snatched the prey from his teeth.

**29:18** וָ֭אֹמַר עִם־קִנִּ֣י אֶגְוָ֑ע וְ֝כַחֹ֗ול אַרְבֶּ֥ה יָמִֽים׃  
Then I thought, ‘I shall die in my nest, / And I shall multiply my days as the sand.

**29:19** שָׁרְשִׁ֣י פָת֣וּחַ אֱלֵי־מָ֑יִם וְ֝טַ֗ל יָלִ֥ין בִּקְצִירִֽי׃  
‘My root is spread out to the waters, / And dew lies all night on my branch.

**29:20** כְּ֭בֹודִי חָדָ֣שׁ עִמָּדִ֑י וְ֝קַשְׁתִּ֗י בְּיָדִ֥י תַחֲלִֽיף׃  
‘My glory is ever new with me, / And my bow is renewed in my hand.’

**29:21** לִֽי־שָׁמְע֥וּ וְיִחֵ֑לּוּ וְ֝יִדְּמ֗וּ ᵃלְמֹ֣ו עֲצָתִֽיᵃ׃  
To me they listened and waited, / And kept silent for my counsel.

**29:22** אַחֲרֵ֣י דְ֭בָרִי לֹ֣א יִשְׁנ֑וּ וְ֝עָלֵ֗ימֹו תִּטֹּ֥ף מִלָּתִֽי׃  
After my words they did not speak again, / And my speech dropped on them.

**29:23** וְיִֽחֲל֣וּ כַמָּטָ֣ר לִ֑י וּ֝פִיהֶ֗ם פָּעֲר֥וּ לְמַלְקֹֽושׁ׃  
They waited for me as for the rain, / And opened their mouth as for the spring rain.

**29:24** אֶשְׂחַ֣ק אֲ֭לֵהֶם לֹ֣אᵃ יַאֲמִ֑ינוּ וְאֹ֥ור פָּ֝נַ֗י לֹ֣א יַפִּילֽוּן׃  
I smiled on them when they did not believe, / And the light of my face they did not cast down.

**29:25** אֶֽבֲחַ֣ר דַּרְכָּם֮ וְאֵשֵׁ֪ב רֹ֥אשׁ וְ֭אֶשְׁכֹּון כְּמֶ֣לֶךְ בַּגְּד֑וּד כַּאֲשֶׁ֖ר אֲבֵלִ֣ים יְנַחֵֽם׃  
I chose a way for them and sat as chief, / And dwelt as a king among the troops, / As one who comforted the mourners.

### Chapter 30 — and now (30:1–31)

*No paragraph marker after 30:31 in BHS.*

**30:1** וְעַתָּ֤ה׀ שָֽׂחֲק֣וּ עָלַי֮ צְעִירִ֥ים מִמֶּ֗נִּי לְיָ֫מִ֥ים אֲשֶׁר־מָאַ֥סְתִּי אֲבֹותָ֑ם לָ֝שִׁ֗ית עִם־כַּלְבֵ֥י צֹאנִֽי׃  
But now those younger than I mock me, / Whose fathers I disdained to put with the dogs of my flock.

**30:2** גַּם־כֹּ֣חַ יְ֭דֵיהֶם לָ֣מָּה לִּ֑י עָ֝לֵ֗ימֹו אָ֣בַד כָּֽלַח׃  
Indeed, what good was the strength of their hands to me? / Vigor had perished from them.

**30:3** בְּחֶ֥סֶר וּבְכָפָ֗ן גַּ֫לְמ֥וּד הַֽעֹרְקִ֥ים צִיָּ֑ה אֶ֝֗מֶשׁᵃ שֹׁואָ֥ה וּמְשֹׁאָֽה׃  
From want and famine they are gaunt / Who gnaw the dry ground by night in waste and desolation,

**30:4** הַקֹּטְפִ֣ים מַלּ֣וּחַ עֲלֵי־שִׂ֑יחַ וְשֹׁ֖רֶשׁ רְתָמִ֣ים לַחְמָֽםᵃ׃  
Who pluck mallow by the bushes, / And whose food is the root of the broom shrub.

**30:5** מִן־גֵּ֥ו יְגֹרָ֑שׁוּ יָרִ֥יעוּ עָ֝לֵ֗ימֹו כַּגַּנָּֽב׃  
They are driven from the community; / They shout against them as against a thief,

**30:6** בַּעֲר֣וּץ נְחָלִ֣ים לִשְׁכֹּ֑ן חֹרֵ֖י עָפָ֣ר וְכֵפִֽים׃  
So that they dwell in dreadful valleys, / In holes of the earth and of the rocks.

**30:7** בֵּין־שִׂיחִ֥ים יִנְהָ֑קוּ תַּ֖חַת חָר֣וּל יְסֻפָּֽחוּ׃  
Among the bushes they cry out; / Under the nettles they are gathered together.

**30:8** בְּֽנֵי־נָ֭בָל גַּם־בְּנֵ֣י בְלִי־שֵׁ֑ם נִ֝כְּא֗וּ מִן־הָאָֽרֶץ׃  
Fools, even those without a name, / They were scourged from the land.

**30:9** וְ֭עַתָּה נְגִינָתָ֣ם הָיִ֑יתִי וָאֱהִ֖י לָהֶ֣ם לְמִלָּֽה׃  
And now I have become their taunt, / I have even become a byword to them.

**30:10** תִּֽ֭עֲבוּנִי רָ֣חֲקוּ מֶ֑נִּי וּ֝מִפָּנַ֗י לֹא־חָ֥שְׂכוּ רֹֽק׃  
They abhor me and stand aloof from me, / And they do not refrain from spitting at my face.

**30:11** כִּֽי־יִתְרִ֣וᵃ פִ֭תַּח וַיְעַנֵּ֑נִי וְ֝רֶ֗סֶן מִפָּנַ֥י שִׁלֵּֽחוּ׃  
Because He has loosed His bowstring and afflicted me, / They have cast off the bridle before me.

**30:12** עַל־יָמִין֮ פִּרְחַ֪חᵃ יָ֫ק֥וּמוּ רַגְלַ֥י שִׁלֵּ֑חוּ וַיָּסֹ֥לּוּ עָ֝לַ֗י אָרְחֹ֥ות אֵידָֽם׃  
On the right hand their brood arises; / They thrust aside my feet and build up against me their ways of destruction.

**30:13** נָתְס֗וּ נְֽתִיבָ֫תִ֥י לְהַוָּתִֽי יֹעִ֑ילוּ לֹ֖א עֹזֵ֣רᵃ לָֽמֹו׃  
They break up my path, / They profit from my destruction; / No one restrains them.

**30:14** כְּפֶ֣רֶץ רָחָ֣ב יֶאֱתָ֑יוּ תַּ֥חַת שֹׁ֝אָ֗ה הִתְגַּלְגָּֽלוּ׃  
As through a wide breach they come, / Amid the tempest they roll on.

**30:15** הָהְפַּ֥ךְᵃ עָלַ֗י בַּלָּ֫הֹ֥ות תִּרְדֹּ֣ףᵇ כָּ֭רוּחַ נְדִבָתִ֑יᶜ וּ֝כְעָ֗ב עָבְרָ֥ה יְשֻׁעָתִֽי׃  
Terrors are turned against me; / They pursue my honor as the wind, / And my prosperity has passed away like a cloud.

**30:16** וְעַתָּ֗ה עָ֭לַי תִּשְׁתַּפֵּ֣ךְ נַפְשִׁ֑י יֹ֖אחֲז֣וּנִי יְמֵי־עֹֽנִי׃  
And now my soul is poured out within me; / Days of affliction have seized me.

**30:17** לַ֗יְלָה עֲ֭צָמַי נִקַּ֣ר מֵעָלָ֑י וְ֝עֹרְקַ֗י לֹ֣א יִשְׁכָּבֽוּן׃  
At night it pierces my bones within me, / And my gnawing pains take no rest.

**30:18** בְּרָב־כֹּ֖חַ יִתְחַפֵּ֣שׂ לְבוּשִׁ֑י כְּפִ֖י כֻתָּנְתִּ֣י יַֽאַזְרֵֽנִי׃  
By a great force my garment is distorted; / It binds me about as the collar of my coat.

**30:19** הֹרָ֥נִי לַחֹ֑מֶר וָ֝אֶתְמַשֵּׁ֗ל כֶּעָפָ֥ר וָאֵֽפֶר׃  
He has cast me into the mire, / And I have become like dust and ashes.

**30:20** אֲשַׁוַּ֣ע אֵ֭לֶיךָ וְלֹ֣א תַעֲנֵ֑נִי עָ֝מַ֗דְתִּי וַתִּתְבֹּ֥נֶןᵃ בִּֽי׃  
I cry out to You for help, but You do not answer me; / I stand up, and You turn Your attention against me.

**30:21** תֵּהָפֵ֣ךְ לְאַכְזָ֣ר לִ֑י בְּעֹ֖צֶם יָדְךָ֣ תִשְׂטְמֵֽנִי׃  
You have become cruel to me; / With the might of Your hand You persecute me.

**30:22** תִּשָּׂאֵ֣נִי אֶל־ר֖וּחַ תַּרְכִּיבֵ֑נִי וּ֝תְמֹגְגֵ֗נִי תֻּשִׁוָּֽהᵃ׃  
You lift me up to the wind and cause me to ride; / And You dissolve me in a storm.

**30:23** כִּֽי־יָ֭דַעְתִּי מָ֣וֶת תְּשִׁיבֵ֑נִי וּבֵ֖ית מֹועֵ֣ד לְכָל־חָֽי׃  
For I know that You will bring me to death / And to the house of meeting for all living.

**30:24** אַ֣ךְ לֹא־בְ֭עִיᵃ יִשְׁלַח־יָ֑ד אִם־בְּ֝פִידֹ֗ו ᵇלָהֶ֥ן שֽׁוּעַᵇ׃  
Yet does not one in a heap of ruins stretch out his hand, / Or in his disaster therefore cry out for help?

**30:25** אִם־לֹ֣א בָ֭כִיתִי לִקְשֵׁה־יֹ֑ום עָֽגְמָ֥ה נַ֝פְשִׁ֗י לָאֶבְיֹֽון׃  
Have I not wept for the one whose life is hard? / Was not my soul grieved for the needy?

**30:26** כִּ֤י טֹ֣וב קִ֭וִּיתִי וַיָּ֣בֹא רָ֑ע וַֽאֲיַחֲלָ֥ה לְ֝אֹ֗ור וַיָּ֥בֹא אֹֽפֶל׃  
When I expected good, then evil came; / When I waited for light, then darkness came.

**30:27** מֵעַ֖י רֻתְּח֥וּ וְלֹא־דָ֗מּוּ קִדְּמֻ֥נִי יְמֵי־עֹֽנִי׃  
I am seething within and cannot relax; / Days of affliction confront me.

**30:28** קֹדֵ֣ר הִ֭לַּכְתִּי בְּלֹ֣א חַמָּ֑הᵃ קַ֖מְתִּי בַקָּהָ֣ל אֲשַׁוֵּֽעַ׃  
I go about mourning without comfort; / I stand up in the assembly and cry out for help.

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims that emerged across several tools:

1. **Chapter 30 takes chapter 29 apart, word by word — and at the centre of the reversal the man who answered the cry of the poor becomes a poor man whose own cry is not answered.** `[T]` Three times chapter 30 says וְעַתָּה ("and now", 30:1, 9, 16), and what follows each "now" undoes a line of chapter 29. The verified reversals (WLC lemma, each word checked in both verses):
   - **laughter:** "I smiled (אֶשְׂחַק, 'laughed') on them" (29:24) → "now those younger than I mock (שָׂחֲקוּ) me" (30:1);
   - **the cry for help:** the poor man "who cried for help (מְשַׁוֵּעַ)" was delivered by Job (29:12) → "I cry out (אֲשַׁוַּע) to You for help, but You do not answer me" (30:20), and again "I stand up in the assembly and cry out for help (אֲשַׁוֵּעַ)" (30:28);
   - **the helper:** the orphan "who had no helper (וְלֹא־עֹזֵר לוֹ)" (29:12) → the rabble against Job, "no helper (לֹא עֹזֵר) for them" (30:13, MT; see Tool 8);
   - **affliction:** Job delivered "the afflicted (עָנִי, NASB 'the poor')" (29:12) → God "has afflicted me (וַיְעַנֵּנִי)" (30:11), and "days of affliction (יְמֵי־עֹנִי)" seize and confront him (30:16, 27) — the same root;
   - **standing up:** the old men "arose and stood (קָמוּ עָמָדוּ)" when Job came (29:8) → the rabble "arise (יָקוּמוּ)" against him (30:12), and Job "stands up (קַמְתִּי)" to cry (30:28);
   - **waiting:** men "waited (יִחֵלּוּ, וְיִחֲלוּ)" for Job's word (29:21, 23) → "I waited (וַאֲיַחֲלָה) for light, then darkness came" (30:26);
   - **clothing:** "I put on righteousness, and it clothed me" (29:14) → "By a great force my garment (לְבוּשִׁי) is distorted" (30:18);
   - **the bow:** "my bow is renewed in my hand" (29:20) → "He has loosed his bowstring" or "my cord" (30:11; a ketiv–qere, Tool 8);
   - **light:** "by His light (אוֹר) I walked through darkness" and "the light of my face" (29:3, 24) → "I waited for light (אוֹר), then darkness came" (30:26);
   - **comfort:** "as one who comforted the mourners (אֲבֵלִים)" (29:25) → "my harp is turned to mourning (אֵבֶל)" (30:31).

   **The first of these reversals is anchored outside the book.** The cry of 29:12 is Ps 72:12 almost word for word: כִּי־יַצִּיל אֶבְיוֹן מְשַׁוֵּעַ וְעָנִי וְאֵין־עֹזֵר לוֹ ("For he will deliver the needy when he cries for help, the afflicted also, and him who has no helper"). עָנִי ("afflicted"), מְשַׁוֵּעַ ("crying for help") and עֹזֵר ("helper") stand together in **only these two verses** of the Hebrew Bible (WLC). In Ps 72 it is the king who delivers; in Job 29 it is Job, who "dwelt as a king among the troops" (29:25). The Greek translators read both verses alike, rendering מְשַׁוֵּעַ the same way at both: πτωχὸν ἐκ χειρὸς δυνάστου ("the poor from the hand of the mighty", Swete Ps 71:12 and Job 29:12, the only two verses with that rendering). Surfaced by: Structure, Linking Words (the three "now"s), Repetition, Vocabulary, Tone, Tool 11, Original Languages. *High confidence for the internal reversals; high for the Ps 72:12 contact; moderate for reading the whole of chapter 29 as a royal portrait.*

2. **Chapter 31 is a sworn oath of clearance, and its clauses answer the charges laid against Job in the debate, one by one.** `[T]` Seventeen verses of the chapter carry אִם ("if"), and four of them call down a curse on Job himself (31:8, 10, 22, 40). The oath answers named speakers:
   - **Eliphaz:** "You have sent widows away empty, and the strength of the orphans has been crushed (זְרֹעוֹת יְתֹמִים יְדֻכָּא, lit. 'the arms of the orphans')" (22:9) → "If I have lifted up my hand against the orphan … let my shoulder fall from the socket, and my arm (וְאֶזְרֹעִי) be broken off" (31:21–22). In Job "orphan" and "arm" fall within two verses only at 22:8–9 and 31:21–22 (WLC, counting both spellings of "arm"). Job swears a curse on his own arm that matches the charge exactly;
   - **Job's own catalogue of the destitute:** "naked, without clothing (מִבְּלִי לְבוּשׁ) … no covering (וְאֵין כְּסוּת)" (24:7) → "if I have seen anyone perish for lack of clothing (מִבְּלִי לְבוּשׁ), or that the needy had no covering (וְאֵין כְּסוּת)" (31:19). Clothing and covering meet in one verse **only** at 24:7 and 31:19 in the Hebrew Bible (WLC) `[S: prior dig]`;
   - **Zophar and the formula of the third round:** "This is the wicked man's portion (חֵלֶק) from God, and the heritage (נַחֲלַת) decreed to him by God" (20:29; Job repeats it at 27:13) → "And what is the portion (חֵלֶק) of God from above, or the heritage (נַחֲלַת) of the Almighty from on high?" (31:2). These are the only three verses in Job with both words (WLC);
   - **Bildad:** the wicked man's "trust (מִבְטַחוֹ)" is a spider's web and is torn from his tent (8:14; 18:14) → "If I have … called fine gold my trust (מִבְטַחִי)" (31:24). The only three uses of the word in Job (WLC).

   And it answers the prologue: "let God know my integrity (תֻּמָּתִי)" (31:6). תֻּמָּה ("integrity") occurs only at 2:3 (God), 2:9 (the wife), 27:5 (Job), 31:6 (Job) and Prov 11:3 (WLC). **Job asks God to know what the reader has already heard God say** (2:3). Surfaced by: Structure, Linking Words, Repetition, Vocabulary, Genre (the oath), Tool 11 Move 4. *High confidence.*

3. **What Job swears is an uncovered life, not a sinless one.** `[T]` "Have I covered my transgressions like Adam (or: as men do), by hiding my iniquity in my bosom?" (31:33). The verb כסה ("cover") with פֶּשַׁע ("transgression") **and** עָוֺן ("iniquity") occurs in **only two verses** of the Hebrew Bible: here and Ps 32:5, "I acknowledged my sin to You, and my iniquity (וַעֲוֺנִי) I did not hide (לֹא־כִסִּיתִי); I said, 'I will confess my transgressions (פְשָׁעַי) to the LORD'" (WLC). Ps 32 is the psalm of the man whose silence wasted his bones (32:3) until he stopped covering his sin, and whom God then covered (32:1). Job does not claim to have no sin: he has asked God to pardon his transgression (7:21), to show him his sins (13:23), and has complained that God makes him "inherit the iniquities of my youth" (13:26). What he swears is that he has not concealed anything. Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Tool 11, Difficult Verses, Christological Reading. *High confidence for the contact; moderate for a deliberate echo of Ps 32; whether כְאָדָם means "like Adam" or "as men do" is genuinely open (Tool 8).*

4. **The speech ends by demanding the answer the book will give, and by planting the words of Job's final reply.** `[T]`
   - **The demand:** "I cry out to You for help, but You do not answer (תַעֲנֵנִי) me" (30:20) → "Oh that I had one to hear me! Behold, here is my signature; let the Almighty answer (יַעֲנֵנִי) me!" (31:35) → "Then the LORD answered (וַיַּעַן) Job out of the whirlwind" (38:1);
   - **the case written down:** "the indictment (סֵפֶר) which my adversary has written (כָּתַב)" (31:35) completes the wish of 19:23 that his words be "written (וְיִכָּתְבוּן) … in a book (בַּסֵּפֶר)" — the only two verses in Job with both words (WLC);
   - **the close:** the narrator ends the unit, "The words of Job are ended (תַּמּוּ)". The verb is the root of the book's word for Job: תָּם ("blameless", 1:1, 8; 2:3) and תֻּמָּה ("integrity", 31:6);
   - **planted for chapter 42:** "I have become like dust and ashes (כֶּעָפָר וָאֵפֶר)" (30:19) → "I … repent in dust and ashes (עַל־עָפָר וָאֵפֶר)" (42:6), the phrase occurring only there and at Gen 18:27 (WLC; the overview's finding, confirmed). The three "and now"s of chapter 30 are answered by Job's last "and now": "I have heard of You by the hearing of the ear; but now (וְעַתָּה) my eye sees You" (42:5). And the ear and eye that once bore witness to Job (29:11) are the ear and eye with which he meets God (42:5).

   Surfaced by: Linking Words, Repetition, Narrator's Comment, Tool 11 Move 4, Vocabulary. *High confidence for the chains; moderate for reading the verb תַּמּוּ as a play on Job's integrity.*

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**The book.** With the overview: the book asks whether anyone fears God חִנָּם ("for nothing", 1:9), refuses the friends' verdict that suffering proves guilt, and brings God to answer, not with a reason, but with himself. `[S]` (overview microscript)

**The unit.** Job 29–31 is the case Job lays before God when every human voice has gone quiet. The third round has broken down (25:1–6; no third speech from Zophar), and the poem of chapter 28 has ended by defining wisdom in the very words of Job's portrait (28:28 → 1:1). `[S: prior dig]` Job now says three things, one per chapter:
- **what I was** (29), with God named first;
- **what I am** (30), with God unnamed until Job turns to him in direct address;
- **what I swear** (31), under curse, signed, and handed up.

Then the narrator closes the human debate: "The words of Job are ended" (31:40). `[T]`

**What the unit contributes to the book's question.** The Accuser's charge was that Job feared God because God had "made a hedge about him and his house and all that he has" (1:10). `[T]` Chapter 29 is the inventory of that hedge, seen from inside: God watching over him (29:2), God's lamp over his head (29:3), "the friendship of God over my tent" (29:4), "the Almighty yet with me, and my children (נְעָרַי) around me" (29:5), butter and oil (29:6), honour at the gate (29:7–10). Chapter 30 shows it all gone. Chapter 31 shows a man who, with nothing left, still swears by God, fears God (31:23) and asks God to weigh him (31:6). `[I]` **29–31 is the book's fullest answer, from Job's side, to 1:9:** the things are gone, and the integrity holds.

**One observation the preacher can trust.** The first thing Job misses is not his wealth but God. Verses 2–5 name God three times (אֱלוֹהַּ, "God", at 29:2 and 29:4; שַׁדַּי, "the Almighty", at 29:5) and point to him a fourth time ("His lamp", 29:3) before any possession, honour or child is mentioned, and the children come in the same breath as "the Almighty was yet with me" (29:5). `[T]` for the order; `[I]` for its meaning. A man who loved God's gifts more than God would have begun elsewhere.

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement.** Four things have been set up that 29–31 now provides. `[T]`
- **A demand for a hearing:** "I would speak to the Almighty, and I desire to argue with God" (13:3); "Then call, and I will answer; or let me speak, then reply to me" (13:22); "Oh that I knew where I might find Him … I would present my case before Him" (23:3–4).
- **An oath already begun:** "As God lives, who has taken away my right … till I die I will not put away my integrity from me" (27:2, 5). That oath is sworn on Job's integrity in general; chapter 31 itemises it.
- **A definition of wisdom in Job's own terms:** "the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom; and to depart from evil is understanding" (28:28), the words of 1:1, 8 and 2:3. `[S: prior dig]` The reader arrives at 29–31 knowing that wisdom looks like the man described in 1:1, and chapter 31 then shows that man in detail.
- **The friends' silence:** Bildad's last speech is six verses; Zophar has none; Job no longer "answers" (וַיַּעַן) anyone after 26:1, but "again takes up his discourse" (27:1; 29:1). `[S: prior dig]`

**Necessity answer.** 29–31 exists here because the human court has adjourned and Job's case must be put to the only Judge left. It cannot come earlier: until the friends fall silent, Job is answering them; after it, he is addressing God. It cannot come later, because 32:1 begins, "Then these three men ceased answering Job, because he was righteous in his own eyes", and Elihu's anger (32:2) is a reaction to this speech. The LORD's first words, "Who is this that darkens counsel …?" (38:2), are an answer to 31:35's "let the Almighty answer me". `[T]` for the sequence; `[I]` for the necessity.

**The bracket with chapter 3.** Job's opening soliloquy cursed his day (3:1); his closing soliloquy remembers his days (29:2, 4, 18) and laments his "days of affliction" (30:16, 27). Two lines of chapter 3 return in chapter 30:
- **3:9 → 30:26.** In chapter 3 Job cursed the night of his conception: "let it wait (יְקַו) for light (לְאוֹר) but have none". In chapter 30 the curse has come on him: "When I expected (קִוִּיתִי) good, then evil came; when I waited for light (לְאוֹר), then darkness came". קוה ("wait for") with אוֹר ("light") occurs only at Isa 59:9, Jer 13:16, Job 3:9 and Job 30:26 (WLC). `[T]` *high*.
- **3:26 → 30:26–27.** "I am not at ease, nor am I quiet … and turmoil comes (וַיָּבֹא)" (3:26); "evil came (וַיָּבֹא) … darkness came (וַיָּבֹא) … I am seething within and cannot relax (וְלֹא־דָמּוּ)" (30:26–27). `[I]` *moderate* (common words, same shape).

And chapter 31 answers chapter 3's womb. Job cursed the womb for not closing on him (3:10–11); in 31:15 the womb is where "the same one fashioned us", Job and his slave alike. רֶחֶם ("womb") and בֶּטֶן ("belly, womb") meet in one verse only at Jer 1:5, Job 3:11, Job 31:15, Ps 22:11 and Ps 58:4 (WLC) `[S: prior dig]`. **The womb Job cursed for his own sake becomes the ground of his duty to another.** `[I]`

**Implication for this passage.** 29–31 is not a third complaint after chapters 3 and 23. It is a summing-up for the court, built to close the human debate and to compel the divine answer. The preacher who reads it as Job's self-pity or self-righteousness has missed its genre (see Tool 12 and the Pitfalls).

#### Immediate context

- **Before:** 27:1–23 (the oath on integrity, "As God lives …") and 28:1–28 (where wisdom is found). The same formula introduces 27:1 and 29:1, וַיֹּסֶף אִיּוֹב שְׂאֵת מְשָׁלוֹ ("And Job again took up his discourse"), and it occurs nowhere else (WLC). `[S: prior dig]`
- **After:** the narrator's prose (32:1–5) and Elihu's four speeches (32–37); then the LORD's answer (38:1).
- **Section:** the overview's Movement IV, "three discourses and a meeting" — Job (29–31), Elihu (32–37), the LORD (38–41) with Job's replies. `[S]`

**Devices.** `[T]`
- **Chapter 29 is framed.** Two comparisons with כַּאֲשֶׁר ("as when", "as one who") open and close it: "as I was (כַּאֲשֶׁר הָיִיתִי) in the prime of my days" (29:4); "as one who (כַּאֲשֶׁר) comforted the mourners" (29:25). The frame B / B′ is tied by silence and by מִלָּה ("word"): the princes "stopped talking (עָצְרוּ בְמִלִּים)" (29:9); "after my words they did not speak again … my speech (מִלָּתִי) dropped on them" (29:22). At the centre is the justice (29:11–17). The chiastic arrangement is `[I]`, *moderate*. Its homiletic payoff is that **Job's honour rested on Job's justice**: the two כִּי clauses of 29:11–12 say so.
- **The whole unit is framed by מִי יִתֵּן ("Oh that …").** "Oh that I were as in months gone by (מִי־יִתְּנֵנִי)" (29:2) and "Oh that I had one to hear me (מִי יִתֶּן־לִי)" (31:35). The longing for the past becomes a longing for a hearing. `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the frame (the wish-formula is frequent in Job: 6:8; 11:5; 13:5; 14:4, 13; 19:23; 23:3; 29:2; 31:31, 35, WLC).
- **Chapter 31 is framed by God counting Job's steps.** "Does He not see my ways and number (יִסְפּוֹר) all my steps (צְעָדַי)?" (31:4) → "I would declare to Him the number (מִסְפַּר) of my steps (צְעָדַי)" (31:37). Job has lived as one whose steps are counted, so he can count them back. The pair occurs elsewhere only at 14:16, where the counting was hostile: "You number my steps, You do not observe my sin" (WLC). `[T]` *high*.
- **The land after the signature (31:38–40b).** Many commentators move these lines before 31:35 `[S: recalled]`. As the text stands, the last curse returns to the first: "Let me sow and another eat, and let my crops be uprooted" (31:8) → "Let briars grow instead of wheat" (31:40). The chapter begins and ends in the field. `[I]` *moderate*. The petuḥah after 31:40 in BHS confirms only that the unit ends there.
- **Paragraphing.** Neither chapter 29 nor chapter 30 ends with a paragraph marker in BHS. The only internal marker in the unit is the petuḥah after 31:7, which separates the second "if" from its curse. In Leningrad, as BHS prints it, the whole unit is one discourse. This is a reading tradition, not authorial. `[T]` for the markers *as BHS prints them*; spread across other manuscripts `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`. Nothing in this report rests on a marker alone.

### 4. Linking Words

- **כִּי … כִּי (29:11–12).** "For (כִּי) when the ear heard, it called me blessed … because (כִּי) I delivered the poor who cried for help." ←for← The esteem of chapter 29 is grounded in the justice. `[T]`
- **וְעַתָּה ("and now") ×3 (30:1, 9, 16).** Each opens a panel of chapter 30, and each is a pivot against chapter 29. The same word turns the book at 42:5: "but now (וְעַתָּה) my eye sees You". In Job, עַתָּה ("now") occurs in 18 verses (WLC); only chapter 6 also has it three times (6:3, 21, 28), and only in chapter 30 does it open each panel of a speech. `[T]`
- **כִּי (30:11).** "Because (כִּי) He has loosed His bowstring and afflicted me, they have cast off the bridle before me." ←for← The rabble's contempt is grounded in what God has done. Job does not blame the outcasts for the cause. `[T]`
- **כִּי יָדַעְתִּי (30:23).** "For I know that You will bring me to death." Job's last "I know" before God answers; it stands against the "I know" of 19:25 (see Tool 11). `[S: prior dig]`
- **אִם … (31).** The oath formula. Three shapes:
  - **"If X, (then) let Y"** — the full self-curse (31:7–8, 9–10, 21–22, 38–40);
  - **"If X, (for) it would be Y"** — the curse replaced by a judgement (31:24–28: "that too would have been an iniquity calling for judgment, for I would have denied God above");
  - **"If X …" with no apodosis at all** (31:29–34). The sentence is left hanging, as if the curse were too obvious to say. `[T]` for the forms; `[S: recalled]` for the term *aposiopesis*.
  - **אִם־לֹא (31:36)** is not conditional but an asseveration: "Surely I would carry it on my shoulder." `[T]`
- **The motive clauses of chapter 31 all point to God.** "For (כִּי) that would be a lustful crime … for (כִּי) it would be fire" (31:11–12); "What then could I do when God arises?" (31:14); "Did not He who made me in the womb make him?" (31:15); "For (כִּי) calamity from God is a terror to me" (31:23); "for (כִּי) I would have denied God above" (31:28). **Job's ethic is grounded in God, not in reputation.** `[T]` This is the answer to any reading of 31 as moralism.

### 5. Parallels

Selected parallels that clarify meaning: `[T]`
- **29:14:** "I put on righteousness, and it clothed me (וַיִּלְבָּשֵׁנִי)". The verb is turned round in the same line: Job wore righteousness and righteousness wore Job. The b-line pairs צֶדֶק ("righteousness") with מִשְׁפָּטִי ("my justice"), and לבשׁ with מְעִיל ("robe") and צָנִיף ("turban").
- **29:15–16:** "eyes to the blind and feet to the lame … a father to the needy". The parallel defines "father" as one who supplies what is missing.
- **30:26:** "When I expected good (טוֹב), then evil (רָע) came; when I waited for light (אוֹר), then darkness (אֹפֶל) came." Synonymous lines, each antithetical within itself.
- **30:29:** "a brother to jackals and a companion of ostriches" — the desert-mourner's pair (see Tool 11, Mic 1:8).
- **31:26:** "the sun (אוֹר, lit. 'light') when it shone, or the moon (יָרֵחַ) going in splendour." The parallel shows that אוֹר here is the sun.
- **31:40:** "briars instead of wheat, and stinkweed instead of barley."

**Break in pattern.** 31:40c, "The words of Job are ended", is the narrator's line, not Job's (Tool 6).

### 6. Narrator's Comment

Two intrusions bracket the unit:
- **29:1 — the heading.** It repeats 27:1 exactly. `[T]`
- **31:40c — "The words of Job are ended (תַּמּוּ דִּבְרֵי אִיּוֹב)."** In the dialogue the narrator otherwise speaks only through the speech formulas; this is the first time he steps out to close something. `[S: prior dig]` (22–28 dig). Three features make the line weighty:
  - **It closes the human debate.** Job's next words (40:3–5) are spoken to God and are a refusal to go on: "I have spoken once, and I will not answer." `[T]`
  - **Its verb is the root of Job's name for himself.** תמם ("be complete, finished") is the verb of תָּם ("blameless", 1:1, 8; 2:3; 8:20; 9:20–22) and תֻּמָּה ("integrity", 31:6). `[T]` for the root; `[I]` for a play. *Moderate.*
  - **Colophons of this kind.** "The prayers of David the son of Jesse are ended (כָּלּוּ)" (Ps 72:20) uses a different verb but does the same job, at the end of the psalm whose v.12 stands behind Job 29:12. Deut 31:24 has the same verb with the same noun: "when Moses finished writing the words (דִּבְרֵי) of this law in a book (סֵפֶר) until they were complete (עַד תֻּמָּם)". תמם with דִּבְרֵי occurs only at Deut 31:24, 30, Josh 4:10 and Job 31:40 (WLC). `[T]` for the words; *low–moderate* for any design.


### 7. Vocabulary

**Proper-noun inventory.** `[T]`
- **People:** אִיּוֹב ("Job", 29:1; 31:40). No other person is named; the friends are not addressed.
- **Divine names:** אֱלוֹהַּ ("God", 29:2, 4; 31:2, 6); שַׁדַּי ("the Almighty", 29:5; 31:2, 35); אֵל ("God", 31:14, 23, 28). **No divine name occurs in chapter 30.** There God is "he" (30:11, 19) until Job turns to him as "you" (30:20–23). The covenant name is absent, as throughout the dialogue.
- **Places:** none. **Underworld:** אֲבַדּוֹן ("Abaddon", 31:12).
- **A name or a noun:** כְאָדָם ("like Adam" or "as man/men do", 31:33) — see Tool 8 and Tool 11.
- **Gentilics, numbers:** none.

No place name or number carries canonical freight here. The divine-name pattern is the one finding: God is named in the memory (29) and in the oath (31), but not in the complaint until he is addressed (30). `[T]`

**Wordplay — עָנִי ("afflicted") and עָנָה ("answer").** The root of 29:12's עָנִי ("afflicted") and of 30:11's וַיְעַנֵּנִי ("he afflicted me") is spelled with the same three consonants as the verb of 30:20's תַעֲנֵנִי ("You do not answer me") and 31:35's יַעֲנֵנִי ("let the Almighty answer me"). They are two different roots. But the play has **canonical precedent in the psalm of the forsaken sufferer**: "I cry by day, but You do not answer (וְלֹא תַעֲנֶה)" (Ps 22:3 [Eng 22:2]); "You answer me (עֲנִיתָנִי)" (Ps 22:22 [Eng 22:21]); "He has not despised … the affliction of the afflicted (עֱנוּת עָנִי) … when he cried to Him for help (וּבְשַׁוְּעוֹ), He heard" (Ps 22:25 [Eng 22:24]). עָנִי ("afflicted") with שׁוע ("cry for help") occurs only at Ps 22:25, Ps 72:12 and Job 29:12 (WLC). `[T]` for the forms; `[I]` for the play. *Moderate*: Ps 22 joins the same pair, but claim audit 3 rated Ps 22 a weak source in Job `[S: audit]`. Transliteration is kept here only because the sound is the point: עָנִי (*ʿānî*, "afflicted"), וַיְעַנֵּנִי (*wayʿannēnî*, "he afflicted me"), תַעֲנֵנִי (*taʿănēnî*, "You answer me").

**Classification.** The vocabulary of 29:12–17 and 31:13–23 belongs to the law's protection of the weak: widow, orphan, poor, sojourner, the cry heard (Exod 22:20–26 [Eng 22:21–27]; Deut 24:10–22). The law's taxonomy is activated (Tool 11). `[I]`

**Ancient versions check.** The NASB95, ESV and KJV agree on several weight-bearing renderings, so the Greek was checked at each. Each divergence is classified first under the three-way triage:
- **31:33 — translation-tradition split (category 2, or the translator's paraphrase).** Swete and Rahlfs: εἰ δὲ καὶ ἁμαρτὼν ἀκουσίως ἔκρυψα τὴν ἁμαρτίαν μου ("if, having sinned unintentionally, I hid my sin"). The Greek has no "Adam" and no "man"; it makes the sin unintentional. *Moderate:* the Greek of Job paraphrases freely, so this may be the translator's habit rather than his *Vorlage*. **Preaching implication:** nothing should hang on "Adam" alone; the Ps 32:5 contact (Headline 3) does not depend on it.
- **29:18 — category 1 (translation loss), with a reception note.** Hebrew וְכַחוֹל ("and as the sand") becomes ὥσπερ στέλεχος φοίνικος ("like the trunk of a palm"). φοῖνιξ means "palm" and also the phoenix; a Jewish tradition read חוֹל here as the phoenix `[S: recalled]`. The English versions keep "sand".
- **30:24 — category 2, a real split on a crux.** Hebrew: "Yet does not one in a heap of ruins stretch out his hand …?" (NASB95). Greek: εἰ γὰρ ὄφελον δυναίμην ἐμαυτὸν χειρώσασθαι ("O that I could lay hands on myself"). **The Greek makes it a death-wish.** The Hebrew is obscure, and BHS prints several conjectures. *Uncertain.* Do not build on either.
- **31:35–37 — category 2 or paraphrase.** The Greek reads: χεῖρα δὲ κυρίου εἰ μὴ ἐδεδοίκειν ("if I had not feared the hand of the Lord"), and turns the indictment into a creditor's bond that Job tore up "taking nothing from the debtor" (31:37). The signature and the demand that the Almighty answer are gone. *Moderate.*
- **29:12 — reception (category 1).** Both Greek translators, of Ps 72:12 [LXX 71:12] and of Job 29:12, read מְשַׁוֵּעַ ("crying for help") as מִשּׁוֹעַ ("from the noble"), giving ἐκ χειρὸς δυνάστου ("from the hand of the mighty"). Either the Job translator knew the Greek Psalter here or both drew on a shared reading tradition. **Either way, the two verses were read together in antiquity.** `[T]` for the Greek; `[I]` for the inference.

**Pulpit divergence note.** The pulpit text is the ESV. **Five places need a word from the pulpit:**
- **31:33** — "as others do" may hide "like Adam";
- **30:11 and 30:19** — the ESV names God where Job does not;
- **29:11** — "approved" loses "bore witness";
- **30:13** — here the ESV is the better guide;
- **30:28** — here too the ESV is the better guide.

The NIV84, if used elsewhere, agrees with the ESV at 30:28, follows the qere at 30:11 ("God has unstrung my bow"), and puts "as Adam did" in the margin at 31:33.

### 9. Tone and Feel

**Chapter 29: warm and stately, with a royal cadence.** It is full of light, oil, dew, rain and silence. The young withdraw, the old stand, and princes put their hands on their mouths. The comparisons are of abundance: "as the sand" (29:18), "as for the rain … the spring rain" (29:23), "as a king among the troops" (29:25). It is the most serene speech Job makes in the book. `[T]`

**Chapter 30: bitter, then broken.** It opens in contempt. The outcasts of 30:1–8 are described with a harshness that is uncomfortable: "whose fathers I disdained to put with the dogs of my flock". Then the tone turns. The images of siege (30:12–14: breach, tempest), of a body consumed (30:17, 30) and of a soul "poured out" (30:16) carry it to the only direct address to God in the unit (30:20–23) and to a closing dirge: jackals, ostriches, harp and flute (30:29–31). `[T]` **Soundtrack:** a lyre that goes out of tune and ends as a lament.

**Chapter 31: judicial, solemn, measured; at its end, almost regal.** The cadence is the oath — "if … let" — and it hardens as it goes. The anger rises at 31:35–37: a crown, a prince, an approach. Then it falls back to the soil (31:38–40). `[T]`

**For the preacher.** Do not preach chapter 29 as a cosy memory, chapter 30 as self-pity, or chapter 31 as a moral checklist. 29 is a testimony, 30 is a lament addressed in the end to God, and 31 is a sworn plea in court. The tonal register is the genre. And the contempt of 30:1–8 is not to be sanitised: Job speaks as the honour-culture man he was (Difficult Verses).

#### The high-confidence contacts — full triad

**Ps 72:12 → Job 29:12** *(high for the words; moderate for the royal portrait as a whole)*

*Source context:* Ps 72 is a prayer for the king, "the king's son" (72:1), that he may judge the people "with righteousness (בְצֶדֶק) and Your afflicted (עֲנִיֶּיךָ) with justice (בְמִשְׁפָּט)" (72:2). He will "crush the oppressor" (72:4) and come down "like rain (כְּמָטָר) upon the mown grass" (72:6). Then: "For he will deliver the needy when he cries for help (מְשַׁוֵּעַ), the afflicted (וְעָנִי) also, and him who has no helper (וְאֵין־עֹזֵר לוֹ). He will have compassion on the poor and needy … and their blood will be precious in his sight" (72:12–14). The psalm closes Book II of the Psalter with a doxology and a colophon: "The prayers of David the son of Jesse are ended" (72:20). Verse 12 is the hinge of the psalm: it gives the reason (כִּי, "for") why the nations will serve this king (72:11). Its register is hope.

*Book usage:* the Psalter is a live source in Job: Ps 8:5 at 7:17–18; Ps 107:40 at 12:21, 24; Ps 88 at 19:13; Ps 102 at 19:20 and 30:30 `[S: audit]`. **No earlier use of Ps 72 in Job** has been found by the earlier runs, apart from a passing mention of 72:14 in the 19 dig's canon list `[S: prior dig]`.

*OT-to-OT:* Ps 72's king does what God does in Ps 10: "You have been the helper (עוֹזֵר) of the orphan (יָתוֹם) … Break the arm (זְרוֹעַ) of the wicked" (Ps 10:14–15). Orphan, helper and "break" fall within two verses only there in the Hebrew Bible (WLC). Job 29:12, 17 ("the orphan who had no helper … I broke the jaws of the wicked") and 31:21–22 (the orphan; "my help"; "my arm … broken") use the same words. **The king of Ps 72 is a human copy of the God of Ps 10, and Job at his best was a copy of that king.** `[I]` *moderate*. Isa 11:4 (the shoot of Jesse judges the poor with righteousness) belongs to the same ideal `[S: recalled]`.

*What it adds:* chapter 29 is Job's account of himself in the terms of the righteous king. Righteousness and justice are his robe (29:14 → 72:2). He breaks the oppressor (29:17 → 72:4). His words fall like rain (29:23 → 72:6). He "dwelt as a king" (29:25). He delivered the afflicted who cried, with no helper (29:12 = 72:12). `[I]` That is why the fall in chapter 30 is so steep: **the king who answered the cry becomes the afflicted man who cries (30:20, 28) and has no answer.** Ps 72's colophon and 31:40c's may be a formal echo too (Tool 6), but that is *low–moderate*.

**Ps 32:5 → Job 31:33** *(high for the words — exclusive; moderate for design)*

*Source context:* Ps 32 opens, "How blessed is he whose transgression (פֶּשַׁע) is forgiven, whose sin is covered (כְּסוּי)" (32:1). It then tells how silence wasted the psalmist's bones (32:3–4), until "I acknowledged my sin to You, and my iniquity I did not hide (לֹא־כִסִּיתִי); I said, 'I will confess my transgressions to the LORD'; and You forgave the guilt of my sin" (32:5). Verse 5 is the turning point of the psalm. Its register is relief after misery.

*Book usage:* no earlier use of Ps 32 in Job found by the earlier runs; the Psalter is live (above). Proverbs, Job's other neighbour, has the same saying as a maxim: "He who conceals (מְכַסֶּה) his transgressions (פְשָׁעָיו) will not prosper, but he who confesses and forsakes them will find compassion" (Prov 28:13). כסה with פֶּשַׁע occurs in six verses (Ps 32:1, 5; Prov 10:12; 17:9; 28:13; Job 31:33); with עָוֺן as well, only Ps 32:5 and Job 31:33 (WLC).

*OT-to-OT:* if כְאָדָם means "like Adam", the line also points to Gen 3:8–10, where the man hid (וַיִּתְחַבֵּא, "hid himself") from God among the trees. Gen 3 and Ps 32 are already in conversation: concealment versus confession, covering by the guilty versus covering by God (Gen 3:7, 21; Ps 32:1). Job 31:33 shares no verb with Gen 3:8–10; the Adam link rests on the one word, as the overview's gate found `[S]`. Hos 6:7 has the same ambiguity, "like Adam / like men, they transgressed the covenant", and Job 31 opens with a covenant (31:1). `[I]` *low–moderate.*

*What it adds:* **Job's oath is a claim of honesty before God, not of sinlessness.** He has not done what Adam did, what men do, what Ps 32's psalmist did until his bones wasted: he has not covered his transgression. He has named sins and asked for pardon (7:21; 13:23, 26; 14:16–17). That is why he can say "let Him weigh me with accurate scales" (31:6): he hides nothing from the scales. **The gospel is in the source psalm itself.** The blessed man is the one whose sin God covers, and Paul reads that psalm as the righteousness credited apart from works (Rom 4:6–8). `[I]`

**Lam 3:14 → Job 30:9** *(high for the words — exclusive)*

*Source context:* Lam 3 is the voice of "the man who has seen affliction (עֳנִי) because of the rod of His wrath" (3:1). God has walled him in and besieged him (3:5–9): "Even when I cry out and call for help (וַאֲשַׁוֵּעַ), He shuts out my prayer" (3:8). Then: "I have become (הָיִיתִי) a laughingstock (שְׂחֹק) to all my people, their mocking song (נְגִינָתָם) all the day" (3:14). The register is the depth of lament before the turn of 3:21–24.

*Book usage:* Lamentations is a live source in Job. Lam 3:7–9 stands behind 19:7–8 *(high)*, and Lam 2:10–17 behind Job 16 *(high)*; Lam 3:12–13, 30 at 16:10–13 `[S: audit]`. Here the man of Lam 3 speaks again through Job.

*OT-to-OT:* Lam 3:14 has three of the words of Job 30: "I have become" (הָיִיתִי) and "their mocking song" (נְגִינָתָם) at 30:9 — the only two verses with both (WLC); and the root of "laughingstock" (שׂחק) at 30:1. Lam 3:1 has עֳנִי ("affliction", Job 30:16, 27) and Lam 3:8 has שׁוע ("cry for help", 30:20, 28). **Five of chapter 30's key words are in Lam 3:1–14.** `[T]`

*What it adds:* the outcasts' song in 30:9 is the mocking song of Lam 3:14, sung over the man of affliction. Job is speaking as that man again. And Lam 3 goes on to the turn ("The LORD's lovingkindnesses indeed never cease", 3:22), as the book of Job goes on to 38:1. `[I]`

**Isa 50:6 → Job 30:10** *(high for the words — exclusive; low–moderate for the Servant as design)*

*Source context:* the third Servant song (Isa 50:4–9): "I gave My back to those who strike Me, and My cheeks to those who pluck out the beard; I did not cover My face (פָּנַי) from humiliation and spitting (וָרֹק)" (50:6). Then: "He who vindicates Me is near; who will contend with Me (מִי־יָרִיב אִתִּי)? … Who has a case against Me (מִי־בַעַל מִשְׁפָּטִי)? Let him draw near to Me" (50:8). The register is steadfast confidence under abuse.

*Book usage:* Isa 50:6–9 at 13:18–28 and 16:10 *(held)* `[S: audit]`. Job used רֹק ("spittle") of himself at 7:19. The word occurs only at Isa 50:6, Job 7:19 and Job 30:10, and with פָּנִים ("face") only Isa 50:6 and Job 30:10 (WLC) `[S: prior dig]` — the overview's Christological item 2, confirmed.

*OT-to-OT:* the Servant's challenge, "who will contend with me? who is my adversary?" (50:8), is close to Job's "the indictment which my adversary (אִישׁ רִיבִי) has written" (31:35): both speakers want the case heard, and both have the root רִיב ("contend, case"). `[I]` *moderate*.

*What it adds:* the Servant who did not hide his face from spitting is the pattern that Job's suffering partly fits. Job is spat on; the Servant gives his face to it willingly and trusts his Vindicator. Claim audit 3 set the Servant as a design behind Job 16–17 at *low* `[S: audit]`; this run adds a second exclusive word but no new evidence of design. **The canonical reader hears the Servant here; whether the author meant it remains open.**

**Gen 4:10–12 → Job 31:38–39** *(moderate–high)*

*Source context:* "The voice of your brother's blood is crying (צֹעֲקִים) to Me from the ground (מִן־הָאֲדָמָה). Now you are cursed from the ground … When you cultivate the ground, it will no longer yield its strength (כֹּחָהּ) to you" (Gen 4:10–12). The murderer's land cries out, and its strength is withheld.

*Book usage:* Gen 4:10 already stands behind Job 16:18, "O earth, do not cover my blood, and let there be no resting place for my cry (לְזַעֲקָתִי)" — confirmed by Delitzsch in Logos round 4 `[S: audit]`. **The same text is used twice in Job, in reverse.** At 16:18 Job is Abel, whose blood cries for him. At 31:38–39 he swears he is not Cain: "If my land (אַדְמָתִי) cries out (תִזְעָק) against me … if I have eaten its strength (כֹּחָהּ) without money, or have caused its owners to lose their lives (וְנֶפֶשׁ בְּעָלֶיהָ הִפָּחְתִּי)".

*OT-to-OT:* זעק and צעק are by-forms of one verb. אֲדָמָה ("ground") with כֹּחַ ("strength") within one verse occurs only at Gen 4:11–12, Exod 32:11–12 (an unrelated "strength") and Job 31:38–39 (WLC). The land's cry is also the field of Hab 2:11 ("the stone will cry out from the wall") `[I]`.

*What it adds:* the oath ends with the first murderer. Job swears that no land of his has been bought with blood. The thorn that replaces wheat (31:40) is the soil's answer to a Cain. **Gen 3:17–18's thorns are the thematic background, but the shared words are with Gen 4, not Gen 3.** `[T]`

**Deut 32:13–14 → Job 29:6** *(high for the words; moderate as an allusion)*

*Source context:* in the Song of Moses, God fed Jacob in the wilderness: "He made him ride (יַרְכִּבֵהוּ) on the high places of the earth … He made him suck honey from the rock, and oil (וְשֶׁמֶן) from the flinty rock (צוּר), curds (חֶמְאַת) of cows …" (Deut 32:13–14). Then: "But Jeshurun grew fat and kicked … he forsook God who made him" (32:15).

*Book usage:* Deut 32 is live in Job: 32:32–33 at Job 20:14–16, *high* `[S: audit]`. Inside Job, Zophar's wicked man "does not look at the streams, the rivers flowing with honey and curds (חֶמְאָה)" (20:17) `[S: prior dig]`. Butter (חֵמָה / חֶמְאָה), rock and oil fall within one verse only at Deut 32:13–14 and Job 29:6 (WLC). In Job "butter" occurs only at 20:17 and 29:6.

*OT-to-OT:* Isa 58:14 quotes Deut 32:13 verbatim: "I will make you ride (וְהִרְכַּבְתִּיךָ) on the heights of the earth". And Eliphaz's promise of 22:26 is Isa 58:14's ("then you will delight in the Almighty", queue #111, unaudited). Job 30:22 has the same verb, the only place in Job: "You lift me up to the wind and cause me to ride (תַּרְכִּיבֵנִי)". **Synthetic and moderate at best:** Deut 32:13 → Isa 58:14 → Eliphaz (22:26); Job had the Deut 32:13–14 abundance (29:6); now God makes him ride the storm (30:22).

*What it adds:* Job's prosperity is described as Israel's wilderness blessing. And the Song's warning, "Jeshurun grew fat and kicked", is exactly what Job swears he did not do: he did not trust gold or rejoice in wealth (31:24–25), and he did not deny God (31:28). **29:6 answers Zophar (20:17), and 31:24–28 answers Deut 32:15.** `[I]`

### 12. Genre

Hebrew poetry throughout; the accentuation is the poetic system from 3:2 to 42:6 (overview) `[S]`. Within the poetry, three sub-genres, one per chapter:
- **29: a testimony of former estate.** Close to the "memory of better days" in laments (Ps 42:5 [Eng 42:4]; Lam 1:7), but here it is evidence for the court. `[I]`
- **30: a personal lament**, ending in direct address to God (30:20–23) and a dirge (30:29–31). Reading rule: give permission to grieve, and do not resolve too fast. `[T]`
- **31: an oath of clearance** with conditional self-curses, closed with a signature and a demand that the opponent produce his written charge. The form is legal: the self-curse puts the case into God's hands (cf. the oath "before God" for a disputed deposit, Exod 22:7–10 [Heb 22:6–9]) `[S: recalled]`. **Reading rule:** the "ifs" are not a list of virtues to admire or to copy; they are a sworn denial under penalty, and the curses are real. `[I]`

The genre decides the tone of the sermon. Job is not boasting in chapter 31; he is testifying under oath, calling down ruin on himself if he lies.

### 13. Copycat

Non-narrative, but Job is a character, so label his acts:
- **31:16–23, 31–32 — care for the widow, the orphan, the poor, the stranger:** *normative pattern*. It is commanded elsewhere (Exod 22:20–26; Deut 24:17–22; Isa 58:6–7; Jas 1:27), and here it is shown in a life.
- **31:1 — the covenant with the eyes:** *normative pattern*, grounded in God's sight (31:4); cf. Matt 5:28.
- **31:13–15 — the slaves' cause heard because one God made both in the womb:** *normative pattern* in its ground (shared creatureliness), though the institution is not commended.
- **31:29–30 — no curse on an enemy:** *normative pattern*; cf. Prov 24:17; Exod 23:4–5; Matt 5:44.
- **31:35–37 — demanding that God answer and produce his charge:** *unique / descriptive*. God answers it (38:1), but he also rebukes the words in which it was made (38:2; 40:2, 8). It is not a template for prayer.
- **30:1–8 — contempt for the outcasts:** *descriptive*; not commended.
- **30:21 — "You have become cruel to me":** *descriptive*. It is honest lament, which God does not condemn as he condemns the friends (42:7), but its content is answered and corrected by God (40:8).

### 14. Bible Timeline

**Canonical position (Phase 0.55).** Job stands in the **Ketuvim**, second, between Psalms and Proverbs in the BHS order. `[T]` For this unit the two neighbours do real work:
- **Psalms before:** Ps 72:12 (29:12), Ps 32:5 (31:33), Ps 22 (30:20), Ps 38:7 (30:28) and Ps 102 (30:16–30). Book II of the Psalter closes with Ps 72's colophon; Job 31 closes with its own (Tool 6).
- **Proverbs after:** Prov 8:3; 9:3, 14 (קֶרֶת, "city", 29:7); Prov 11:3 (תֻּמָּה, "integrity", 31:6, the only other use); Prov 28:13 (concealing transgressions, 31:33); Prov 24:17 (rejoicing at an enemy's fall, 31:29). `[T]` for the words; the direction of dependence is open.

**Where the story sits.** The book sets Job in a patriarchal world outside Israel (overview) `[S]`. Yet his oath assumes the moral world of Sinai: the widow and orphan, the stranger, the pledged garment, the slave's cause, the ban on astral worship. `[T]` for the content; `[I]` for "Sinai" — Job never names the law.

**Where we stand.** On this side of the cross, two things are different:
- **The Answer Job demanded has been given.** God has spoken from the whirlwind (38:1) and "in these last days … in His Son" (Heb 1:2). `[I]`
- **The Advocate Job longed for (16:19–21) has come.** The verdict Job swore to win by his own record (31:6) is given to the ungodly as a gift (Rom 4:5–8, quoting Ps 32:1–2). `[I]`

### 15. Who Am I?

- **Job in chapter 29: a figure of the righteous king, and so a type pointing to the King** (Ps 72; Isa 61). He is not first an example to imitate. *Moderate* (Christological Reading).
- **Job in chapter 30: the righteous sufferer**, the man of Lam 3 and of Ps 22, 38 and 102, mocked and spat on, crying and unanswered. **Christ fits this before we do** (Matt 27:46; 26:67). The sufferer in the congregation may also rightly pray Job's words, and lament addressed to God is vindicated (42:7).
- **Job in chapter 31: the man under oath.** No hearer can swear chapter 31 without remainder. The honest reader is not Job here but **the Adam of 31:33** — the one who covers. The gospel then supplies Ps 32:1: the blessed man is the one whose sin God covers. One man could swear chapter 31 without qualification: "Which one of you convicts Me of sin?" (John 8:46); "who committed no sin" (1 Pet 2:22, from Isa 53:9). `[I]`
- **The outcasts of 30:1–8: a foil**, and also a warning about how even a righteous man can speak of the poor he has not helped.
- **The widow, the orphan, the stranger and the slave of chapter 31: the neighbours whom the church is to serve** (Jas 1:27).

### 16. So What?

**Stage 1 — the author's intended response.** The reader is meant to:
- feel the full weight of loss after a life lived well (29–30);
- hear lament addressed to God as legitimate speech (30:20–23);
- see what integrity looks like in the concrete — eyes, wife, slaves, poor, money, enemies, guests, honesty about sin, land (31);
- and then wait, with Job, for the One who must answer (31:35 → 38:1).

The unit does not resolve Job's case; it hands it up to God. `[I]`

**Stage 2 — worldview.**
- **Integrity is not prosperity insurance.** The man of chapter 31 is the man of chapter 30.
- **God's silence is not God's verdict.** God answers in chapter 38, and his verdict comes in 42:7.
- **Righteousness has a social shape.** For Job it was justice for those with no helper, not private piety.

**Stage 2 — behaviour.**
- *Stop:* concealing sin "like Adam" (31:33); reading another's ruin as proof of guilt; rejoicing at an enemy's fall (31:29); trusting gold (31:24).
- *Start:* making a covenant with your eyes (31:1); hearing the complaint of those under you (31:13–15); feeding, clothing and housing the poor and the stranger (31:16–20, 32); bringing your cry to God, not only about him (30:20).

**Motivation.**
- **God sees and counts every step** (31:4).
- **The poor were made by the same God, in the same womb** (31:15).
- **God's calamity is to be feared** (31:23).
- **Above all,** the God who did not answer Job at once did answer him (38:1) — and has answered in Christ, who took Job's place among the mocked and the unanswered (Ps 22:2 → Matt 27:46) and was "heard because of His piety" (Heb 5:7). `[I]`

**Four audiences.**
- **For me:** does my integrity depend on my circumstances, and do I cover or confess?
- **For a Christian friend:** a believer whose former blessings are gone, who needs permission to lament and an assurance that heaven's silence is not God's last word.
- **For the church:** do we have Job's eyes for the poor, the widow, the fatherless and the stranger, and his ear for those under us?
- **For the unbeliever:** chapter 31 is the standard by which we would want to be weighed (31:6). Who can stand there? The answer is the One who stood there for us.

**Prayer.**
- Sorry that I cover my sin and hide behind my record.
- Thank you that you answered Job, and that in Christ you have answered us.
- Please, by your Spirit, give me Job's eyes for the poor and his honesty before you.

**Grammatical and syntactical features.**
- **29:14 — the reciprocal verb.** "I put on (לָבַשְׁתִּי) righteousness, and it put me on (וַיִּלְבָּשֵׁנִי)": the same verb with subject and object reversed. Righteousness is not only what Job wore; it is what made him. *High.*
- **29:2 — מִי־יִתְּנֵנִי ("Who will make me …?", i.e. "Oh that I were …")** with a suffix on the verb: literally "who will give me as in months gone by". *High.*

### Textual Variants

Classified under the three-way triage. Only category 2 belongs here; category 1 items are under Tool 8, and none of the unit is quoted in the New Testament.

**29:4 — בְּסוֹד ("in the friendship") or בְּסוֹךְ ("when [God] hedged").**
- **Evidence:** BHS proposes בְּסוֹךְ, citing the Greek, Symmachus and the Syriac. Swete: ὅτε ὁ θεὸς ἐπισκοπὴν ἐποιεῖτο τοῦ οἴκου μου ("when God made visitation of my house").
- **Weighing:** the MT gives an internal chain (15:8; 19:19; 29:4). The proposed reading would give a link to the Accuser's "hedge" (1:10, where the verb is שׂוּךְ, a by-form). Either reading fits; the Greek's "visitation" is not plainly either.
- **Position:** keep the MT. *Moderate.* **Preaching:** say "friendship"; the hedge of 1:10 is in view either way.

**30:11 — ketiv יתרו ("his cord") / qere יִתְרִי ("my cord").**
- **Evidence:** BHS: the Syriac and the Targum follow the qere; the Greek the ketiv ("opening his quiver").
- **Weighing:** both readings make sense.
  - The ketiv continues Job's picture of God as archer (6:4; 16:12–13).
  - The qere completes the reversal of 29:20 ("my bow is renewed in my hand").
- **Position:** undecided. *Moderate either way.* **Preaching:** the ESV's "my cord" is defensible; mention that the written text may say "His".

**30:13 — עֹזֵר ("helper"), with BHS's conjecture עֹצֵר ("restrainer").** No manuscript support is cited for the conjecture; NASB95's "No one restrains them" reflects it. **Keep the MT**, which repeats 29:12. *High.*

**30:22 — ketiv תשוה / qere תֻּשִׁיָּה.**
- **Evidence:** the qere means "sound wisdom, success". BHS says to read with the ketiv, pointed תְּשֻׁוָּה ("storm, roar"); cf. 36:29 for the noun.
- **Position:** NASB95 and ESV both render "storm" with BHS. *Moderate.*

**30:24 — a crux.** BHS gives several conjectures. The Greek reads a death-wish (Tool 8). *Uncertain.* Preach the line as a cry for help from the ruins, if at all.

**30:28 — חַמָּה ("sun"), with BHS's conjecture נֶחָמָה ("comfort", cf. 6:10).** No witness is cited for the conjecture. **Keep the MT.** *Moderate–high.*

**31:11 — ketiv and qere exchange the genders of the pronouns.** No change of sense. BHS notes a mixed construction in עֲוֹן פְּלִילִים. *Negligible.*

**31:18 — conjectures (God as subject: "He raised me"; or "I raised him").**
- **Position:** keep the MT. The orphan grew up with Job as with a father; from birth Job guided the widow. The ESV's supplied nouns are a fair reading. *Moderate.*

**31:21 — BHS's conjecture עֲלֵי־תָם ("against the blameless").** No witness; it would give "the blameless" where the MT has "the orphan". **Keep the MT.** *High.*

**31:33 — no variant in BHS.** The Greek paraphrases (Tool 8).

### Historical and Cultural Background

**Setting.** A patriarchal-style magnate of "the east" (1:3), whose standing is exercised in the city gate. `[T]` for the gate; the date and place of composition are `[S]` and do not affect these findings.

**What the original audience knew.**
- **The city gate was the court.** The elders sat there to hear cases (Ruth 4:1–11; Deut 21:19; 22:15; Amos 5:12, 15). Job's seat "in the square" and "at the gate" (29:7; 31:21) was a judge's seat. "I saw I had support in the gate" (31:21) means he could have won a case against the orphan. `[S: recalled]` for the passages outside Job; *high*.
- **Honour and shame.** The young withdrawing, the old standing, the princes silent (29:8–10): the gestures of public honour. Mockery, spitting and becoming a song (30:9–10) were the gestures of public shame. `[S: recalled]` *high*.
- **The oath with self-curse.** Swearing "if I have done X, may Y happen to me" handed the case to God, who would enforce the curse. Cf. the oath before God for a disputed deposit (Exod 22:7–10 [Heb 22:6–9]) and the oath at the altar (1 Kgs 8:31–32). `[S: recalled]` *high* for the practice.
- **Grinding** was the work of slave women and captives (Exod 11:5; Judg 16:21; Isa 47:2). "Let my wife grind for another" (31:10) is a curse of degradation, with a sexual sense in the next line. `[S: recalled]` *moderate–high*.
- **Kissing the hand to the heavenly bodies.** A gesture of worship (cf. 1 Kgs 19:18; Hos 13:2 for kissing idols). Astral cult was known and forbidden in Israel (Deut 4:19; 17:3; 2 Kgs 23:5; Jer 8:2). `[S: recalled]` *moderate–high*.
- **A written charge and a signature.** Legal disputes could be settled by documents, and a "mark" put to a statement was a signature. Job wants his opponent's written indictment in his hands, to wear it in public as a vindication. `[S: recalled]` *moderate*.
- **Slavery.** Ancient Near Eastern law treated slaves largely as property. A master who let his slaves bring a complaint against him (31:13), and grounded their rights in a common Maker (31:15), stood out. `[S: recalled]` *moderate*.

**What this changes.**
- 31:21 is not about violence but about **corrupting a court**: Job had the power to win against the orphan, and swore he never used it.
- 29:7–17 is not nostalgia for social status but a **record of judicial service**: the eye that saw him in court bore witness for him (29:11).

### Original Audience Reception

**Canonical audience.**
- **Section:** Ketuvim. The canonical reader knows the Torah and the Prophets and lives with the tension of promises not yet fully kept.
- **What the reader knows:** the law of the widow, the orphan and the stranger (Exod 22; Deut 24); the covenant curses (Deut 28); Cain (Gen 4); the Servant (Isa 50); the Psalter just read (Ps 22; 32; 38; 72; 102).
- **Register:** Psalms before, Proverbs after. A reader of Ps 72 has just prayed for a king who delivers the afflicted; a reader of Ps 32 has just heard the blessing of the man who stops covering his sin.

**The first hearers.** The canonical reader of the Writings knows exile: righteous people who suffered, a king's line that failed, prayers apparently unanswered (Lam 3:8). `[I]`

**Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances.**
- **Shocking:** that the man who lived like Ps 72's king could become Lam 3's man of affliction; that a righteous man could say to God, "You have become cruel to me" (30:21) and not be struck down. And the self-curses on his wife (31:10).
- **Surprising:** that the oath puts the slave on a level with his master before God (31:15); and that Job claims not sinlessness but an uncovered life (31:33).
- **Comforting:** that God sees and counts every step (31:4) — which is both a threat and a comfort; and that the lament is addressed to God, not only about him.
- **Disturbing:** that God does not answer at once (30:20); and that integrity did not protect.

**What we bring that they didn't.** A therapeutic reading of chapter 30 that misses its legal function. A suspicion of self-defence as pride, which can make us hear chapter 31 as boasting. A privatised idea of righteousness, which makes 31:13–23 sound like optional extras. `[I]`

| **What they felt** | The ruin of a life lived rightly, public shame, and the silence of God — "I cry out to You for help, but You do not answer me" (30:20) `[T]` |

**Implication for the sermon.** Let chapter 29 be heard as loss, not as boasting. Give chapter 30 its full darkness. Hear chapter 31 as an oath in court. Then show where the book takes the case: to the God who answers (38:1).

### Biblical-Theological Themes

**1. The king who delivers the afflicted.**
- **Where this passage sits:** Job 29 portrays a man who lived the royal ideal of Ps 72 in his own sphere. Job 30 shows that man brought down to the place of the afflicted.
- **Before:** Exod 22:20–26 (God hears the afflicted widow and orphan); Ps 10:14 (God the helper of the orphan); Ps 72 (the king does it for God).
- **After:** Isa 11:4; 61:1–3; Luke 4:18–21. Christ the King "anointed to preach the gospel to the afflicted", who became afflicted himself (Isa 53:4, 7).
- **Contribution:** it explains why chapter 29 sounds royal and why chapter 30's reversal is so total. *Moderate.*

**2. The cry of the righteous sufferer and God's answer.**
- **Where this passage sits:** the cry unanswered (30:20), the demand for an answer (31:35), and the answer given (38:1).
- **Before:** Exod 22:22, 26 (I will surely hear); Ps 22:3, 25; Lam 3:8; Hab 1:2.
- **After:** Matt 27:46; Heb 5:7 ("heard because of His piety"); Rev 6:10.
- **Contribution:** it keeps chapter 30 from being read as despair. It is the cry of faith that God hears in his time. *High* for the internal chain; *moderate* for the canonical field.

**3. Righteousness as clothing.**
- **Where this passage sits:** Job wore righteousness (29:14). His clothing is now distorted (30:18), and he clothed the naked (31:19–20).
- **Before:** Ps 132:9 (priests clothed with righteousness); Isa 59:17 (the LORD puts on righteousness); Isa 61:10; Zech 3:4–5 (the accused high priest re-clothed).
- **After:** Rom 13:14; Gal 3:27 (putting on Christ); Rev 19:8.
- **Contribution:** the righteousness Job wore was his own; the righteousness the gospel gives is put on from outside. *Moderate.*

**Confluence: climactic convergence.** The three themes meet in Christ. He is the King who delivers the afflicted. He became the afflicted one who cried and was not answered, and was heard. He clothes the unrighteous with a righteousness not their own. Job 29–31 holds the three apart; the gospel joins them. `[I]`

### Christological Reading

**Types of connection.**
- **Type.** The righteous king of chapter 29 and the righteous sufferer of chapter 30.
- **Contrast.** The oath of chapter 31, which only one man could swear without remainder.
- **Trajectory.** The demand for God's answer (31:35).

**How the passage points to Christ.**
1. **The King who delivers the afflicted** (29:12 = Ps 72:12; 29:14, 25 with Isa 61:2–3, 10). Jesus reads Isa 61:1–2 of himself (Luke 4:18–21), and Ps 72 is a royal psalm that the church has always read of Christ `[S: recalled]` for the reception. *Typology tests:* category (kingship) ✓; NT precedent (Isa 61 explicitly, Luke 4) ✓ for the kind; escalation (Job delivered some; Christ "will deliver the needy when he cries", Ps 72:12, without end) ✓; authorial pattern — the verbal contact with Ps 72:12 is exclusive, but whether the author of Job intended a royal type is not shown ✗. **Three of four: moderate.**
2. **The afflicted one who cries and is not answered** (30:20 with Ps 22:3; 30:9 with Lam 3:14; 30:10 with Isa 50:6). Christ was mocked and made a song (Matt 27:39–44), spat on (Matt 26:67; 27:30; Mark 14:65; 15:19), and cried "My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?" (Matt 27:46, Ps 22:2). *Tests:* category (the righteous sufferer / Servant) ✓; NT precedent (Ps 22 and Isa 50 applied to the passion) ✓; escalation (Christ's suffering is substitutionary and willing — "I gave my back", Isa 50:6) ✓; authorial pattern — the words are exclusive, design open ✗. **Moderate.** Claim audit 3 rated the Servant as a design behind Job 16–17 *low* `[S: audit]`; that verdict stands for design, not for the canonical reading.
3. **Contrast: the oath only Christ could swear.** Job swears an uncovered life, not a sinless one (31:33; cf. 13:26). Christ is the one "who committed no sin, nor was any deceit found in His mouth" (1 Pet 2:22, from Isa 53:9); "Which one of you convicts Me of sin?" (John 8:46). *High* (contrast).
4. **Trajectory: the answer and the Advocate.** Job demanded that God answer and produce the charge (31:35). God answered (38:1). In Christ, the record of debt "which was hostile to us" is nailed to the cross (Col 2:14). The written charge Job wanted to wear as a crown is cancelled for those in Christ. `[I]` *Moderate.*

**Trajectory.**
- **Longed for:** a hearing; vindication; a God who answers; the restoration of fellowship ("the friendship of God", 29:4).
- **Fulfilled:** God speaks (38–41; Heb 1:1–2); vindication given (42:7; Rom 8:33–34); Christ heard for his reverence (Heb 5:7); the friendship of God (John 15:15).
- **Still to come:** the full end of mourning (Isa 61:3; Rev 21:4); the poor finally delivered (Ps 72; Luke 6:20–21).

**Moralism check.**
- **The "be like X" temptation:** "Be a man of integrity like Job — make a covenant with your eyes, care for the poor." The ethic is real, but preached alone it crushes, since no hearer can swear chapter 31.
- **The gospel grounding:** God counts every step (31:4), and the only safe ground is the uncovered life that Ps 32 describes — sin confessed, and covered by God. That covering is given in Christ (Rom 4:6–8).
- **Christ as hero:** the King who delivers (29), the afflicted one who was not answered for us (30), and the only one who could swear 31 — and who then bore the curse of the oath for those who could not (Gal 3:13). `[I]`

**Confidence.** *Moderate* for the typology; *high* for the contrast; *moderate* for the trajectory.

### Difficult / Contested Verses

**30:1–8 — Job's contempt for the outcasts.**
- **Category:** ethical, pastoral.
- **The difficulty:** the champion of the poor (29:12–17) speaks with disdain of the poorest: their fathers were not fit to be with his dogs (30:1).
- **Common mishandlings:** skipping it, or excusing it entirely.
- **Rhetorical function:** it measures his fall — he is mocked by those he would once not have employed. It is an honour-culture complaint, not a policy.
- **What honest exegesis surfaces:** Job is not presented as sinless (42:6 is still to come), and this is his speech, not the narrator's.
- **Handling:** acknowledge it. It shows that even righteous people speak out of wounded pride.

**30:21 — "You have become cruel (לְאַכְזָר) to me."**
- **Category:** doctrinal, pastoral.
- **The difficulty:** Job accuses God of cruelty. The word is used of the cruel venom of asps (Deut 32:33) and of the daughter of Zion become "cruel like ostriches" (Lam 4:3); in Job it recurs of Leviathan, whom no one is "fierce" enough to rouse (41:2 [Eng 41:10]).
- **Rhetorical function:** the climax of the only address to God in the unit.
- **Options:** honest lament that God tolerates; or sin later corrected (40:8: "Will you condemn Me that you may be justified?").
- **What honest exegesis surfaces:** both. God says Job spoke rightly (42:7), and God also rebukes him for darkening counsel (38:2; 40:8).
- **Handling:** do not soften the line, and do not endorse its verdict. Let it stand as the cry of faith that 38:1 answers.

**30:24 — the crux.** The Hebrew is obscure and the Greek reads a death-wish. **Handling:** do not build on it. **Pastoral note:** if the line is read aloud, be aware that some hearers carry thoughts of ending their lives. Job's words are addressed to God and the book does not end with them; point to the God who answers, and to the help the church and others can give.

**31:1 — the covenant with the eyes.** *Pastoral.* Do not turn it into a moralistic checklist on lust. The ground is God's sight (31:4).

**31:9–12 — adultery and the curse on the wife.**
- **Category:** ethical.
- **The difficulty:** Job curses his wife for his own possible sin (31:10).
- **What honest exegesis surfaces:** it is the logic of talion in a patriarchal household: the penalty fits the crime against another man's wife. It is not commended. It also shows how seriously Job took the crime: "fire that consumes to Abaddon" (31:12).
- **Handling:** acknowledge that the curse falls on her, and do not dwell on it.

**31:13–15 — slaves.** *Ethical, apologetic.* Job owned slaves. **What honest exegesis surfaces:** he grounds their right to bring a case against him in a shared creation, which undercuts the institution's logic. **Handling:** address it briefly; it is a seed the Bible later grows (Job 31:15 → Mal 2:10; Eph 6:9; Philem 16).

**31:18 — who grew up, and whom did Job guide?** The pronouns are compressed. The ESV's supplied nouns are a fair reading. **Handling:** read the ESV and do not build on the pronouns.

**31:33 — "like Adam" or "as men do"?** Contested. **Handling:** tell the congregation the word can mean either. The point is the same either way: Job did not conceal. Ps 32:5 makes it.

**31:35–37 — demanding that God answer.**
- **Category:** doctrinal, pastoral.
- **The difficulty:** is this faith or presumption?
- **What honest exegesis surfaces:** God answers it (38:1), and God rebukes it (38:2; 40:2). Job's final words are a retraction (42:6).
- **Handling:** do not commend it as a model of prayer; do not condemn it as unbelief. It is the cry of a man who will deal only with God.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: Hearing chapter 29 as nostalgia or boasting

- **What it looks like:** "Job was proud of his good old days; he needed humbling."
- **Why it is wrong:** chapter 29 is testimony for the court (Genre), and its centre is justice done for those with no helper (29:11–17), grounded by two "for"s. The first thing Job misses is God (29:2–5). God himself called Job blameless (1:8; 2:3).
- **The corrective:** preach 29 as the record of a good life lost, and let the congregation feel the loss.

### Pitfall: Preaching chapter 31 as a checklist for Christian living

- **What it looks like:** a sermon series on "Job's ten commitments": eyes, marriage, employees, generosity, money …
- **Why it is wrong:** the chapter is an oath of clearance under self-curse (Genre). It is the case of a man calling God to court. Preached as a list, it becomes moralism, and no hearer can swear it.
- **The corrective:** let 31 show what integrity looks like, then let 31:33 (Ps 32:5) and 31:4 ("He sees my ways") drive the hearer to the God who covers sin, and to the One who alone could swear the oath whole.

### Pitfall: "Job was self-righteous, and that is why God rebuked him"

- **What it looks like:** "Chapter 31 is Job's pride; Elihu and God put him in his place."
- **Why it is wrong:** God says Job spoke what is right, and the friends did not (42:7). Job does not claim sinlessness (31:33; 13:26); he claims not to have concealed. God rebukes Job's words about God (38:2; 40:8), not his oath about his neighbour. "Righteous in his own eyes" (32:1) is the narrator reporting why the three stopped answering, not God's verdict.
- **The corrective:** keep God's two verdicts together (42:7; 40:8), and do not let Elihu's anger (32:2) become the preacher's.

### Pitfall: Building on the ESV at 31:33 or 30:11, 19

- **What it looks like:** silence about Adam at 31:33 because the ESV says "as others do"; or "God has loosed my cord" read as if Job named God.
- **Why it is wrong:** the ESV has made decisions the Hebrew leaves open (Tool 8). In chapter 30 Job does not name God until he turns to him (30:20).
- **The corrective:** say "the Hebrew can mean 'like Adam'"; and read 30:11, 19 as "He".

### Pitfall: Making 30:20 the last word on prayer

- **What it looks like:** "Sometimes God just doesn't answer" — and the sermon ends there; or the opposite, "Job's problem was that he demanded an answer".
- **Why it is wrong:** the book answers 30:20 at 38:1, and God hears the raven's young when they cry (38:41). The demand is both answered (38:1) and rebuked (38:2).
- **The corrective:** preach 30:20 as faith's cry in the dark, held open until the book answers it — and until Christ's cry of Ps 22:2 is answered in the resurrection (Heb 5:7).
