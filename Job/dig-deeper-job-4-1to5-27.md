## The Passage

*Text: BHS as exported from Logos, with the NASB95 beneath each verse. ᵃ marks a BHS apparatus note (see Textual Variants).*

**4:1** וַ֭יַּעַן אֱלִיפַ֥ז הַֽתֵּימָנִ֗י וַיֹּאמַֽר׃  
Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered,

**4:2** הֲנִסָּ֬ה דָבָ֣ר אֵלֶ֣יךָ תִּלְאֶ֑ה וַעְצֹ֥ר בְּ֝מִלִּ֗ין מִ֣י יוּכָֽל׃  
“If one ventures a word with you, will you become impatient? But who can refrain from speaking?

**4:3** הִ֭נֵּה יִסַּ֣רְתָּ רַבִּ֑ים וְיָדַ֖יִם רָפֹ֣ות תְּחַזֵּֽק׃  
“Behold you have admonished many, And you have strengthened weak hands.

**4:4** כֹּ֖ושֵׁל יְקִימ֣וּן מִלֶּ֑יךָ וּבִרְכַּ֖יִם כֹּרְעֹ֣ות תְּאַמֵּֽץ׃  
“Your words have helped the tottering to stand, And you have strengthened feeble knees.

**4:5** כִּ֤י עַתָּ֨ה׀ תָּבֹ֣וא אֵלֶ֣יךָ וַתֵּ֑לֶא תִּגַּ֥ע עָ֝דֶ֗יךָ וַתִּבָּהֵֽל׃  
“But now it has come to you, and you are impatient; It touches you, and you are dismayed.

**4:6** הֲלֹ֣א יִ֭רְאָתְךָ כִּסְלָתֶ֑ךָ תִּ֝קְוָתְךָ֗ᵃ וְתֹ֣םᵇ דְּרָכֶֽיךָ׃  
“Is not your fear of God your confidence, And the integrity of your ways your hope?

**4:7** זְכָר־נָ֗א מִ֤י ה֣וּא נָקִ֣י אָבָ֑ד וְ֝אֵיפֹ֗ה יְשָׁרִ֥ים נִכְחָֽדוּ׃  
“Remember now, who ever perished being innocent? Or where were the upright destroyed?

**4:8** כַּאֲשֶׁ֣ר רָ֭אִיתִי חֹ֣רְשֵׁי אָ֑וֶן וְזֹרְעֵ֖י עָמָ֣ל יִקְצְרֻֽהוּ׃  
“According to what I have seen, those who plow iniquity And those who sow trouble harvest it.

**4:9** מִנִּשְׁמַ֣ת אֱלֹ֣והַᵃ יֹאבֵ֑דוּ וּמֵר֖וּחַ אַפֹּ֣ו יִכְלֽוּ׃  
“By the breath of God they perish, And by the blast of His anger they come to an end.

**4:10** שַׁאֲגַ֣ת אַ֭רְיֵה וְקֹ֣ול שָׁ֑חַל וְשִׁנֵּ֖י כְפִירִ֣ים נִתָּֽעוּ׃  
“The roaring of the lion and the voice of the fierce lion, And the teeth of the young lions are broken.

**4:11** לַ֭יִשׁ אֹבֵ֣ד מִבְּלִי־טָ֑רֶף וּבְנֵ֥י לָ֝בִ֗יא יִתְפָּרָֽדוּ׃  
“The lion perishes for lack of prey, And the whelps of the lioness are scattered.

**4:12** וְ֭אֵלַי דָּבָ֣ר יְגֻנָּ֑ב וַתִּקַּ֥ח אָ֝זְנִ֗י שֵׁ֣מֶץ מֶֽנְהֽוּ׃  
“Now a word was brought to me stealthily, And my ear received a whisper of it.

**4:13** בִּ֭שְׂעִפִּים מֵחֶזְיֹנֹ֣ות לָ֑יְלָה בִּנְפֹ֥ל תַּ֝רְדֵּמָ֗ה עַל־אֲנָשִֽׁים׃  
“Amid disquieting thoughts from the visions of the night, When deep sleep falls on men,

**4:14** פַּ֣חַד קְ֭רָאַנִי וּרְעָדָ֑ה וְרֹ֖בᵃ עַצְמֹותַ֣י הִפְחִֽיד׃

**4:15** וְ֭רוּחַ עַל־פָּנַ֣י יַחֲלֹ֑ף תְּ֝סַמֵּ֗ר שַֽׂעֲרַ֥ת בְּשָׂרִֽי׃  
“Then a spirit passed by my face; The hair of my flesh bristled up.

**4:16** יַעֲמֹ֤ד׀ וְֽלֹא־אַכִּ֬יר מַרְאֵ֗הוּ תְּ֭מוּנָה לְנֶ֣גֶד עֵינָ֑י דְּמָמָ֖ה וָקֹ֣ול אֶשְׁמָֽע׃

**4:17** הַֽ֭אֱנֹושׁ מֵאֱלֹ֣והַᵃ יִצְדָּ֑ק אִ֥ם מֵ֝עֹשֵׂ֗הוּ יִטְהַר־גָּֽבֶר׃  
‘Can mankind be just before God? Can a man be pure before his Maker?

**4:18** הֵ֣ן בַּ֭עֲבָדָיו לֹ֣א יַאֲמִ֑ין וּ֝בְמַלְאָכָ֗יו יָשִׂ֥ים תָּהֳלָֽהᵃ׃  
‘He puts no trust even in His servants; And against His angels He charges error.

**4:19** אַ֤ף׀ שֹׁכְנֵ֬י בָֽתֵּי־חֹ֗מֶר אֲשֶׁר־בֶּעָפָ֥ר יְסֹודָ֑ם יְ֝דַכְּא֗וּם לִפְנֵיᵃ־עָֽשׁ׃  
‘How much more those who dwell in houses of clay, Whose foundation is in the dust, Who are crushed before the moth!

**4:20** מִבֹּ֣קֶר לָעֶ֣רֶב יֻכַּ֑תּוּ מִבְּלִ֥י מֵ֝שִׂ֗יםᵃ לָנֶ֥צַח יֹאבֵֽדוּ׃  
‘Between morning and evening they are broken in pieces; Unobserved, they perish forever.

**4:21** הֲלֹא־נִסַּ֣ע יִתְרָ֣ם בָּ֑םᵃ יָ֝מ֗וּתוּ וְלֹ֣א בְחָכְמָֽה׃  
‘Is not their tent-cord plucked up within them? They die, yet without wisdom.’

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims, each surfaced by several tools:

1. **Eliphaz speaks in Scripture's own words — the right words for the wrong man.** `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the misapplication
   - **Proverbs.** "How happy is the man whom God reproves (יוֹכִחֶנּוּ), so do not despise (תִּמְאָס) the discipline (מוּסַר) of the Almighty" (5:17) is Prov 3:11–12: "do not reject (תִּמְאָס) the discipline (מוּסַר) of the LORD or loathe His reproof (בְּתוֹכַחְתּוֹ)". מוּסָר with מאס occurs only at Prov 3:11; 15:32 and Job 5:17; only Job 5:17 adds יכח in the same verse (lemma). Proverbs 22 supplies two more lines: זרע ("sow") with קצר ("reap") and אָוֶן ("iniquity") meets only at Prov 22:8 and Job 4:8; דכא ("crush") with שַׁעַר ("gate") only at Prov 22:22 and Job 5:4 (lemma). אֱוִיל ("fool", 5:2, 3) is Proverbs' word and occurs nowhere else in Job.
   - **Isaiah.** "You have strengthened weak hands (יָדַיִם רָפוֹת) … feeble knees (בִּרְכַּיִם כֹּרְעוֹת)" (4:3–4) is Isa 35:3, which the NASB95 renders "Encourage the exhausted, and strengthen the feeble" — in the Hebrew, "strengthen weak hands (יָדַיִם רָפוֹת), and make firm stumbling (כֹּשְׁלוֹת) knees (בִּרְכַּיִם)". Job 4:3–4 has all four words, with כּוֹשֵׁל ("the tottering"); כשׁל with בֶּרֶךְ occurs only at Isa 35:3; Ps 109:24; Job 4:4 (lemma).
   - **Moses' song.** "He wounds (יִמְחַץ), and His hands also heal (תִּרְפֶּינָה)" (5:18): מחץ with רפא occurs only at Deut 32:39 and Job 5:18 (lemma; 9–10 report).
   - **The Psalter.** "And unrighteousness must shut its mouth (וְעֹלָתָה קָפְצָה פִּיהָ)" (5:16) is Ps 107:42 word for word ("and all unrighteousness shuts its mouth") (phrase search). The verse before in the psalm — "He sets the needy securely on high (וַיְשַׂגֵּב אֶבְיוֹן)" (Ps 107:41) — stands behind 5:11, 15 ("those who mourn are lifted (שָׂגְבוּ) to safety … the poor (אֶבְיוֹן)"). **Job will quote the verse before that** — Ps 107:40, "He pours contempt upon princes and makes them wander in a pathless waste" — word for word at 12:21, 24 (overview). Eliphaz takes the psalm's comfort; Job takes its judgement.
   - **The New Testament agrees with the words.** Paul cites 5:13 as Scripture ("it is written", 1 Cor 3:19; overview). Hebrews 12 quotes both Prov 3:11–12 (Heb 12:5–6) and Isa 35:3 (Heb 12:12), the pair Eliphaz joins (sweep). The words are true; the book's verdict is that Eliphaz "has not spoken of Me what is right" (42:7).
   - *Surfaced by:* Tool 11 (triads), Vocabulary, Original Audience, Move 4. *High on the words.*

2. **The night vision is told in the vocabulary of Sinai and Horeb — and its message contradicts what God has said of Job.** `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the design
   - **"A form was before my eyes … there was silence, then I heard a voice" (4:16).** Three words, three exclusive links (lemma co-occurrence):
     - מַרְאֶה ("appearance") with תְּמוּנָה ("form") — only Job 4:16 and Num 12:8, where God speaks with Moses "mouth to mouth, even openly … and he beholds the form (תְּמֻנַת) of the LORD".
     - תְּמוּנָה with קוֹל ("voice") — only Job 4:16 and Deut 4:12: "you heard the sound of words, but you saw no form — only a voice".
     - דְּמָמָה ("silence, stillness") with קוֹל — only Job 4:16 and 1 Kgs 19:12, Elijah at Horeb: "a sound of a gentle blowing" (קוֹל דְּמָמָה דַקָּה). 1 Kgs 19:11 also has the רוּחַ ("wind, spirit") and עמד ("stand"), as Job 4:15–16 does.
   - **The setting.** "When deep sleep (תַּרְדֵּמָה) falls on men" (4:13) — the sleep of Adam's side (Gen 2:21) and of Abram's covenant vision with its "terror and great darkness" (Gen 15:12). "The hair of my flesh bristled (תְּסַמֵּר … בְּשָׂרִי)" (4:15): סמר with בָּשָׂר occurs only here and at Ps 119:120, "My flesh trembles for fear of You" (lemma).
   - **The message.** "Can mankind be just before God? … He puts no trust even in His servants (בַּעֲבָדָיו)" (4:17–18). God has twice called Job "My servant" (עַבְדִּי, 1:8; 2:3), "blameless and upright", and will say it four times more (42:7–8). In the Greek the clash is sharper still: "or a man blameless (ἄμεμπτος) in his works?" (Swete 4:17) uses the word the Greek narrator and God use of Job at 1:1, 8 and 2:3.
   - **Its afterlife in the book.** Its question becomes the friends' creed (9:2; 15:14; 25:4; 33:12; overview and sweep). Job will say God terrifies him with such "visions" (חֶזְיֹנוֹת, 7:14). Its "whisper" (שֵׁמֶץ, 4:12) is answered by Job's "how faint a word (שֵׁמֶץ דָּבָר) we hear of Him!" (26:14), the only two occurrences (sweep, confirmed).
   - *Surfaced by:* Vocabulary, Tool 11, Translations (the Greek), Move 4, Difficult Verses. *High on the words; moderate–high on the design.* **Load-bearing and unaudited.**

3. **Eliphaz lays the covenant's judgements and healings — Hosea's and Deuteronomy's — over a man outside Israel who has not broken covenant.** `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the pattern (synthetic)
   - **Hosea, four times** (lemma co-occurrence):
     - "those who plow (חֹרְשֵׁי) iniquity … harvest it (יִקְצְרֻהוּ)" (4:8) — חרשׁ ("plough") with קצר ("reap") in a moral sense only at Hos 10:13, "You have plowed wickedness, you have reaped injustice";
     - the lion pair שַׁחַל + כְּפִיר (4:10) — only Hos 5:14, Ps 91:13 and here — with Hos 5:14's "and there will be none to deliver (וְאֵין מַצִּיל)" at Job 5:4;
     - "He inflicts pain, and gives relief (וְיֶחְבָּשׁ, "binds up"); He wounds, and His hands also heal" (5:18) — Hos 6:1, "He has torn us, but He will heal (וְיִרְפָּאֵנוּ) us; He has wounded us, but He will bandage (וְיַחְבְּשֵׁנוּ) us";
     - "you will be in league (בְרִיתֶךָ, "your covenant") with the stones of the field, and the beasts of the field (וְחַיַּת הַשָּׂדֶה) will be at peace with you" (5:23) — בְּרִית with חַיַּת הַשָּׂדֶה only at Hos 2:20 [Eng 2:18], where God abolishes "the bow, the sword (חֶרֶב) and war (מִלְחָמָה)" — the "war" and "sword" of Job 5:20.
   - **Deuteronomy's curses.** "They grope at noon (יְמַשְׁשׁוּ בַצָּהֳרָיִם) as in the night" (5:14): משׁשׁ with צָהֳרַיִם occurs only here and at Deut 28:29, "you will grope at noon … and there will be none to save you" (lemma). And the Song of Moses supplies both the "sparks" (בְנֵי־רֶשֶׁף, 5:7; רֶשֶׁף, "plague", Deut 32:24, with its "famine", רָעָב, as at Job 5:20) and the healing of 5:18 (Deut 32:39).
   - **Job answers in kind.** "The arrows (חִצֵּי) of the Almighty are within me, their poison (חֲמָתָם) my spirit drinks" (6:4) — the "arrows" (חִצַּי) and "venom" (חֲמַת) of Deut 32:23–24. "They grope (יְמַשְׁשׁוּ) in darkness (חֹשֶׁךְ)" (12:25) — משׁשׁ with חֹשֶׁךְ only at Exod 10:21 (the plague of darkness) and Job 5:14; 12:25 (lemma). Job makes God the lion (שַׁחַל, 10:16), as Hosea did (Hos 5:14).
   - *Surfaced by:* Tool 11, Vocabulary, Original Audience, Biblical Theology. *High on the words; moderate–high on the pattern.* **Unaudited.**

4. **"Who ever perished being innocent?" (4:7). Eliphaz answers Job's first word, and the canon answers Eliphaz.** `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the bearing
   - **"Perish."** Job's speech began, "Let the day perish (יֹאבַד) on which I was born" (3:3). Eliphaz's first argument turns on the same verb four times: "who ever perished (אָבָד) being innocent? … By the breath of God they perish (יֹאבֵדוּ) … The lion perishes (אֹבֵד) … they perish (יֹאבֵדוּ) forever" (4:7, 9, 11, 20) (lemma).
   - **"Touch."** "Now it has come to you … it touches (תִּגַּע) you, and you are dismayed" (4:5). "Even in seven evil will not touch (יִגַּע) you" (5:19). נגע is the prologue's verb: "touch all that he has … touch his bone and his flesh" (1:11; 2:5), and the wind that "struck" (וַיִּגַּע) the house (1:19). Job will answer, "the hand of God has touched (נָגְעָה) me" (19:21) (lemma).
   - **The canon's two voices.** The doctrine is Psalter and Proverbs: "the way of the wicked will perish" (Ps 1:6; also Prov 10:28; 11:10; 28:28 — צַדִּיק with אבד, lemma). But the same canon says the righteous perish: "The righteous man perishes (הַצַּדִּיק אָבָד), and no man takes it to heart" (Isa 57:1); "The godly person has perished (אָבַד) from the land, and there is no upright (יָשָׁר) person among men" (Mic 7:2) — יָשָׁר with אבד only at Mic 7:2 and Job 4:7 (lemma); and Qoheleth, with Eliphaz's own "I have seen" (רָאִיתִי, 4:8; 5:3): "I have seen (רָאִיתִי) everything … there is a righteous man who perishes (אֹבֵד) in his righteousness" (Eccl 7:15).
   - **The reader's knowledge.** The reader knows that Job is innocent (1:8; 2:3), that his children perished, and that God was "incited … to ruin him without cause" (2:3). Job will say it: God "mocks the despair of the innocent (נְקִיִּם)" (9:23) (נָקִי, lemma).
   - *Surfaced by:* Repetition, Vocabulary, Tool 11, Move 4, Narrator's Comment. *High.*

5. **The speech's promise comes true at the end of the book — but through Job's prayer for Eliphaz, not through Eliphaz's formula.** `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the irony
   - **"To which of the holy ones will you turn?" (5:1).** At the end God sends Eliphaz to Job: "go to My servant Job … and My servant Job will pray for you" (42:8). "The LORD restored the fortunes of Job when he prayed for his friends" (42:10). The one Eliphaz must turn to is the man he has rebuked. Elihu meanwhile offers the mediator Eliphaz ruled out: "an angel (מַלְאָךְ) as mediator, one out of a thousand" (33:23) — מַלְאָךְ occurs in Job only at 1:14; 4:18; 33:23 (lemma).
   - **"You will know also that your descendants will be many … You will come to the grave in full vigour" (5:25–26).** "Job lived … and saw his sons and his grandsons, four generations. And Job died, an old man and full of days" (42:16–17). Eliphaz's picture of the blessed end is the book's last verse — given after God has rebuked Eliphaz.
   - **"Behold this; we have investigated it (חֲקַרְנוּהָ)" (5:27).** Only God has "searched it out (חֲקָרָהּ)" — wisdom (28:27); the man of 5:9's "unsearchable things" (חֵקֶר) claims to have searched his doctrine out.
   - *Surfaced by:* Move 4, Narrator's Comment, Who Am I?. *High on the words; moderate on the irony as design.*

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book level (overview, confirmed).** The book tests a doctrine — that suffering proves guilt — in front of a reader who knows the premise is false in this case (overview, "Does suffering prove guilt?", citing 4:7). It exposes the friends' theology as speaking wrongly of God (42:7) and brings Job to a fear of God that wants God himself. Eliphaz's first speech is where the doctrine is first stated, at its most attractive. `[I]`

**Unit level.** 4:1–5:27 does four things. `[T]` / `[I]`
- **It states the friends' doctrine.** The innocent do not perish; those who sow trouble reap it (4:7–8); trouble does not sprout from the ground (5:6). `[T]`
- **It grounds the doctrine in revelation.** A night vision says that no mortal is righteous before God (4:12–21). `[T]`
- **It turns the doctrine into counsel.** "As for me, I would seek God" (5:8); accept God's discipline (5:17); then expect restoration (5:18–26). `[T]`
- **It plants the dialogue.** Almost every line will be answered by Job, quoted by Elihu, or overturned by God (Move 4). `[I]`

**Selection.** Eliphaz says nothing about:
- the specific sins Job might have committed (Eliphaz will name them only in 22:6–9);
- Job's children by name — but "his sons are far from safety … crushed in the gate" (5:4) is spoken to a man whose children have died (1:18–19);
- the possibility that Job is suffering innocently, except to deny it (4:7).

He never addresses God. Every second-person form is to Job (Original Language Observations). `[T]` for the absences; `[I]` for the effect.

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement.** `[T]`
- **The prologue** has shown God's verdict ("My servant Job … blameless and upright", 1:8; 2:3), the Accuser's two "touches" (1:11; 2:5), the deaths of Job's children by wind and sword (1:15, 17, 19), and three friends who sat seven days in silence (2:13).
- **Job (3)** has broken the silence by cursing his day: "Let the day perish" (3:3); "Why did I not die at birth?" (3:11); "what I fear (פַּחַד) comes upon me" (3:25); "trouble (עָמָל)" not hidden from his eyes (3:10); "my groanings (שַׁאֲגֹתַי, literally "my roarings") pour out like water" (3:24).

**Necessity answer.** 4–5 must come here and must be Eliphaz's. `[I]`
- **Job 3 needs an answer**, and Eliphaz gives it in Job's words: "perish" (אבד, 3:3 → 4:7–20); "trouble" (עָמָל, 3:10 → 4:8; 5:6–7); "born" (ילד, 3:3 → 5:7); "dread" (פַּחַד, 3:25 → 4:14); the roar (שְׁאָגָה, 3:24 → 4:10 — the only two in Job); the grave (קֶבֶר, 3:22 → 5:26) (lemma).
- **The dialogue needs its doctrine stated at its best**, before it hardens in Eliphaz's second and third speeches (15; 22) and in Bildad and Zophar. The first speech is courteous (4:2), personal (4:3–6), mystical (4:12–21) and ends in an idyll (5:17–26).
- **The senior friend must speak first.** God addresses Eliphaz at the end (42:7). `[T]`

**Implication.** Eliphaz's speech is the friends' case at its most persuasive, and therefore the most dangerous to preach uncritically. Its words are Scripture's words (Headline 1); its message is the book's foil. `[I]`

#### Surrounding context

- **Before:** Job's curse (3), whose words Eliphaz answers one by one.
- **After:** Job's first reply (6–7), which opens by answering 5:2 ("For anger (כַּעַשׂ) slays the foolish man") with "Oh that my grief (כַּעְשִׂי) were actually weighed" (6:2) — כַּעַשׂ occurs in Job only at 5:2; 6:2; 10:17; 17:7 (lemma) — and which answers Deut 32 with Deut 32 (6:4; Headline 3).
- **Section:** round one of the dialogue (4–14): Eliphaz, Job, Bildad, Job, Zophar, Job. This is its first speech.
- **Masoretic paragraphing:** one paragraph, 4:1–5:27, with a petuḥah after 3:26 and after 5:27 (Leningrad, as BHS prints it). `[T]`; `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`.

`[T]` for every marker (lemma and morphology).

**Stanza lengths.** 5, 5, 10, 7, 9, 10, 1 verses. The ESV paragraphs fall at 4:7, 4:12, 5:1, 5:8 and 5:17; the NIV84 likewise, and again at 5:27. `[T]`

**Device: an address in three parts framing two "I have seen" doctrines, a vision and a hymn.** `[T]` for the markers; `[I]` for the design.
- **Address to Job** opens (4:2–7), returns briefly (5:1), and closes (5:17–27). All 41 second-person forms in the unit fall in these verses (Original Language Observations).
- **The teaching in the third person** fills the middle: the doctrine (4:8–11), the vision (4:12–21), the fool (5:2–7), the hymn (5:8–16).
- **A frame of discipline.** "You have admonished (יִסַּרְתָּ) many" (4:3) and "the discipline (מוּסַר) of the Almighty" (5:17) are the same root, יסר, at the opening and the turn to the promise. Job taught others discipline; now he must accept it. `[T]` for the root (lemma 3256 / 4148); `[I]` for the frame. *Moderate–high.*
- **A frame of hope.** "your hope (תִּקְוָתְךָ)" (4:6) and "So the helpless has hope (תִּקְוָה)" (5:16) — the only two in the unit. `[T]`

**The hymn's grammar.** 5:9–13 runs in participles of divine action — עֹשֶׂה ("who does"), הַנֹּתֵן ("who gives"), שֹׁלֵחַ ("who sends"), מֵפֵר ("who frustrates"), לֹכֵד ("who captures") (WLC morphology) — the doxology form Job will sing back with dread at 9:5–10 (9–10 report). `[T]`

### 4. Linking Words

- **הֲ … ("If one ventures …?", 4:2).** A polite rhetorical opening: "If one ventures (הֲנִסָּה) a word with you, will you become impatient?" Eliphaz begins by asking permission he does not wait for. `[T]`
- **הִנֵּה / הֵן ("behold", 4:3, 18; 5:17, 27).** Four exclamations frame the speech: Job's past (4:3), the vision's charge (4:18), the beatitude (5:17), the conclusion (5:27). `[T]`
- **כִּי עַתָּה ("But now", 4:5).** A real "now": the turn from Job's past to his present. Contrast the unreal כִּי־עַתָּה of 13:19 and 14:16 (13–14 report). `[T]`
- **הֲלֹא ("Is not …?", 4:6, 21).** Two rhetorical questions expecting "yes" — Job's piety should be his confidence (4:6); mortals die without wisdom (4:21). `[T]`
- **זְכָר־נָא ("Remember now", 4:7).** The phrase occurs in Job only here and at 10:9, where Job says it to God: "Remember now that You have made me as clay" (phrase search). Elsewhere it is the plea of the sick Hezekiah — "Remember now, O LORD, I beseech You, how I have walked before You in truth and with a whole heart" (2 Kgs 20:3; Isa 38:3) — and occurs at 2 Sam 14:11; Mic 6:5; Neh 1:8. Eliphaz says to Job what Job will say to God. `[T]`
- **כַּאֲשֶׁר רָאִיתִי ("According to what I have seen", 4:8) and אֲנִי־רָאִיתִי ("I have seen", 5:3).** Eliphaz's evidence is experience. `[T]`
- **אַף ("How much more", 4:19).** The a-fortiori: if God does not trust his servants and angels, how much less those in houses of clay. `[T]`
- **כִּי ("for", 5:2, 6, 7, 18, 23).** The grounds of the argument. At 5:6–7 the pair כִּי לֹא … כִּי ("For affliction does not come from the dust … For man is born for trouble") sets the two halves of the doctrine side by side. `[T]`
- **אוּלָם אֲנִי ("But as for me", 5:8).** The phrase occurs only here and at 13:3, where Job begins his turn to God: "But I (אוּלָם אֲנִי) would speak to the Almighty" (phrase search). Eliphaz would "seek" God and "place my cause" before him; Job will "speak" and "argue". `[T]` for the data; `[I]` for the reply (Headline 1 of the 13–14 report). *High on the phrase.*
- **הִנֵּה אַשְׁרֵי ("Behold, how happy", 5:17).** The beatitude, the only אַשְׁרֵי in Job (lemma). `[T]`
- **וְיָדַעְתָּ ("You will know", 5:24, 25) and דַע ("know", 5:27).** The promise ends in knowledge: you will know your tent is secure, your descendants many — "Hear it, and know for yourself". `[T]`

### 5. Parallels

- **4:3–4 — synonymous, four lines.** Admonished many // strengthened weak hands; helped the tottering stand // strengthened feeble knees. The diction of Isa 35:3 (Tool 11). `[T]`
- **4:8 — synthetic, an agricultural proverb.** Plough iniquity, sow trouble, reap it. `[T]`
- **4:9 — synonymous.** "By the breath (נִשְׁמַת) of God they perish // by the blast (רוּחַ) of His anger (אַפּוֹ) they come to an end." נְשָׁמָה, רוּחַ and אַף meet in one verse only at Gen 7:22; 2 Sam 22:16; Ps 18:16; Job 4:9; 27:3 (lemma). At 27:3 Job swears "as long as … the breath of God is in my nostrils (בְּאַפִּי)" — the breath that Eliphaz says destroys is the breath that keeps Job alive. `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the reply.
- **4:10–11 — five words for lions in two verses.** אַרְיֵה, שַׁחַל, כְּפִירִים, לַיִשׁ, לָבִיא. Roar, voice, teeth broken; perishing for lack of prey; cubs scattered. The predator's power ends. `[T]` (lemma).
- **4:17 — synonymous.** "Can mankind (אֱנוֹשׁ) be just (יִצְדָּק) before God (אֱלוֹהַּ)? // Can a man (גֶּבֶר) be pure (יִטְהַר) before his Maker (עֹשֵׂהוּ)?" `[T]`
- **4:18–19 — a fortiori.** Servants and angels // those in houses of clay, founded in dust, crushed before the moth. `[T]`
- **5:6–7 — antithetical.** Affliction does not sprout from the ground // man is born for trouble, as sparks fly upward. `[T]`
- **5:9–16 — a hymn of reversals.** Rain on the earth (5:10); the lowly set on high (5:11); the crafty frustrated, the wise caught (5:12–13); darkness at noon for them (5:14); the poor saved (5:15); hope for the helpless, unrighteousness silenced (5:16). `[T]`
- **5:18 — synonymous, chiastic.** Inflicts pain // binds up; wounds // heals. `[T]`
- **5:19 — a graded numerical saying.** "From six troubles … even in seven". The form of Prov 6:16 ("There are six things which the LORD hates, yes, seven"); among the poetic books שֵׁשׁ with שֶׁבַע occurs only there and here (lemma; the other co-occurrences are numbers in narrative and lists). `[T]`
- **5:20–23 — the catalogue of deliverances.** Famine and war (5:20); the scourge of the tongue and violence (5:21); violence, famine, beasts (5:22); stones and beasts at peace (5:23). `[T]`

### 6. Narrator's Comment

**N/A for the speech itself** — 4:2–5:27 is character speech with no narrative intrusion. The only narrative words are the formula at 4:1 ("Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered"). `[T]`

**What the narrator has already said, and will say, stands over the speech.**
- **Before:** Job is "blameless, upright, fearing God and turning away from evil" (1:1); God calls him "My servant" twice (1:8; 2:3) and admits that he was ruined "without cause" (2:3); the friends came "to sympathize with him and comfort him" (2:11) and sat silent "for they saw that his pain was very great" (2:13). Eliphaz's "your fear of God" and "the integrity of your ways" (4:6) are the narrator's words for Job (Tool 7).
- **After:** "the LORD said to Eliphaz the Temanite, 'My wrath is kindled against you and against your two friends, because you have not spoken of Me what is right as My servant Job has'" (42:7). Of all the friends, only Eliphaz is named in God's verdict. `[T]` for the texts; `[I]` for their bearing on this speech.

### 7. Vocabulary

**(a) The prologue's words in Eliphaz's mouth.** `[T]` (lemma, WLC)
- **יִרְאָה ("fear of God") and תֹּם ("integrity", 4:6).** "Is not your fear of God your confidence, and the integrity of your ways your hope?" The narrator called Job "fearing God (וִירֵא אֱלֹהִים)" and "blameless (תָּם)" (1:1), and God said he "holds fast his integrity (בְּתֻמָּתוֹ)" (2:3; his wife repeats it, 2:9). יִרְאָה with תֹּם occurs in one verse only at 4:6 (sweep, confirmed); תֹּם occurs in Job only at 4:6 and 21:23. Eliphaz affirms Job's piety and turns it into a lever: if you really fear God, you have nothing to fear. *High.*
- **כִּסְלָה ("confidence", 4:6).** Two occurrences in the Hebrew Bible: here and Ps 85:9 [Eng 85:8], "let them not turn back to folly (לְכִסְלָה)" (lemma). The noun can mean "confidence" or "folly", as its relative כֶּסֶל can mean "loins, confidence" or "stupidity" (Job 8:14; 15:27; 31:24). Swete reads "folly": "Is not your fear in folly (ἐν ἀφροσύνῃ)?" (Translations). *Moderate–high* on a double edge.
- **נגע ("touch", 4:5; 5:19).** In Job at 1:11, 19; 2:5; 4:5; 5:19; 6:7; 19:21; 20:6 (lemma). The Accuser's verb (1:11; 2:5) and the wind's (1:19), Eliphaz's twice (Headline 4), and Job's last word on it: "the hand of God has touched (נָגְעָה) me" (19:21). *High.*
- **עֶבֶד ("servant", 4:18).** In Job at 1:8; 2:3; 3:19; 4:18; 7:2; 19:16; 31:13; 40:28; 42:7, 8 (lemma). Apart from the slave of 3:19 and 7:2 and the servants of 19:16 and 31:13, every occurrence is God's "My servant Job" — except 4:18, where the vision says God "puts no trust even in His servants". *High* on the data; the clash is `[I]`.
- **מַלְאָךְ ("messenger, angel", 4:18).** In Job only at 1:14 (the "messenger" who came to Job), 4:18 and 33:23 (Elihu's "angel as mediator") (lemma). *Moderate.*
- **נָקִי ("innocent", 4:7).** In Job at 4:7; 9:23; 17:8; 22:19, 30; 27:17 (lemma). Eliphaz's "who ever perished being innocent?" is answered by Job's "He mocks the despair of the innocent (נְקִיִּם)" (9:23). נָקִי with אבד occurs only at Job 4:7 and Jonah 1:14, the sailors' prayer: "do not let us perish … and do not put innocent blood on us" (lemma). *High.*
- **חטא ("sin; miss", 5:24).** "You will visit your abode and fear no loss (וְלֹא תֶחֱטָא)" — literally "and you will not miss". In Job at 1:5, 22; 2:10; 5:24; 7:20; 8:4; 10:14 and later (lemma). The prologue's "perhaps my sons have sinned" (1:5) and "Job did not sin" (1:22; 2:10) are the same verb. ESV "miss nothing" and NIV84 "find nothing missing" keep the root sense; the NASB95's "fear no loss" hides it. *Moderate* for a deliberate play.

- **אבד ("perish").** Job 3:3, "Let the day perish"; Eliphaz 4:7, 9, 11, 20 (Headline 4). *High.*
- **עָמָל ("trouble").** In Job at 3:10; 4:8; 5:6, 7; 7:3; 11:16; 15:35; 16:2 (lemma). Job: the womb did not "hide trouble from my eyes" (3:10). Eliphaz: those who "sow trouble" reap it (4:8); "trouble" does not sprout from the ground (5:6); "man is born for trouble" (5:7). Job: "sorry comforters (מְנַחֲמֵי עָמָל, literally "comforters of trouble") are you all" (16:2; sweep). Eliphaz's second speech gives the proverb whole: "They conceive mischief (עָמָל) and bring forth iniquity (אָוֶן)" (15:35) — הרה with עָמָל and אָוֶן only at Isa 59:4; Ps 7:15 and Job 15:35 (lemma). *High.*
- **ילד ("be born", 5:7).** "Man is born (יוּלָּד) for trouble." Job had cursed "the day on which I was to be born (אִוָּלֶד)" (3:3). The verb threads the book: 3:3; 5:7; 11:12; 14:1 ("born of woman"); 15:7 ("Were you the first man to be born?"); 15:14; 25:4; and God's "You know, for you were born then" (38:21) and "who has begotten (הוֹלִיד) the drops of dew?" (38:28) (lemma). *High.*
- **פַּחַד ("dread", 4:14).** Job: "what I fear (פַּחַד פָּחַדְתִּי) comes upon me" (3:25). Eliphaz: "Dread (פַּחַד) came upon me, and trembling" — in his vision. In Job at 3:25; 4:14; 13:11; 15:21; 21:9; 22:10; 25:2; 31:23; 39:16, 22 (lemma). *Moderate.*
- **שְׁאָגָה ("roar", 4:10).** In Job only at 3:24 ("my groanings (שַׁאֲגֹתַי) pour out like water") and 4:10 ("The roaring (שַׁאֲגַת) of the lion") (lemma; Job 3 report). Job roars in grief; Eliphaz says the roaring lion's teeth are broken. *Moderate–high.*
- **קֶבֶר ("grave", 5:26).** Job: those who long for death "rejoice when they find the grave" (3:22). Eliphaz: "You will come to the grave in full vigour" (5:26). In Job at 3:22; 5:26; 10:19; 17:1; 21:32 (lemma). *Moderate.*

- **חִזָּיוֹן ("vision", 4:13).** In Job at 4:13; 7:14; 20:8; 33:15 (lemma). Job: "You … terrify me by visions" (7:14). Zophar: "Even like a vision of the night he is chased away" (20:8). Elihu: "In a dream, a vision of the night" (33:15). *High.*
- **שֵׁמֶץ ("whisper", 4:12).** Only 4:12 and 26:14 (sweep, confirmed). *High on the data.*
- **שְׂעִפִּים ("disquieting thoughts", 4:13).** In Job at 4:13 and 20:2 (Zophar: "my disquieting thoughts make me respond") (lemma). *Moderate.*
- **רְעָדָה ("trembling", 4:14).** Six occurrences: Exod 15:15 (the nations at the Red Sea); Isa 33:14; Ps 2:11; 48:7; 55:6 [Eng 55:5]; Job 4:14 (lemma). Ps 55:6, "Fear and trembling come upon me", has the same scene of night terror. *Moderate.*
- **סמר ("bristle", 4:15).** Two occurrences: Job 4:15 and Ps 119:120 (lemma). *High on the data.*
- **מַרְאֶה, תְּמוּנָה, דְּמָמָה, קוֹל (4:16).** Headline 2. תְּמוּנָה occurs ten times: the Decalogue's ban on images (Exod 20:4; Deut 5:8), Deut 4:12, 15, 16, 23, 25 (Israel "saw no form" at Horeb, so must make no image), Num 12:8 (Moses "beholds the form of the LORD"), Ps 17:15 ("I will be satisfied with Your likeness when I awake") and Job 4:16 (lemma). דְּמָמָה occurs three times: 1 Kgs 19:12; Ps 107:29 (the storm stilled); Job 4:16. *High.*
- **תָּהֳלָה ("error", 4:18).** Only here (lemma); BHS proposes תִּפְלָה ("folly") or הַתָּלָה ("deception"). תִּפְלָה is the word of 1:22, "nor did he blame (תִּפְלָה) God". *Uncertain.*
- **עֹשֶׂה ("Maker", 4:17).** "Can a man be pure before his Maker (מֵעֹשֵׂהוּ)?" The participle of עשׂה as a title of God in Job: 4:17; 31:15 ("Did not He who made me (עֹשֵׂנִי) in the womb make him?"); 32:22 ("my Maker would soon take me away"); 35:10 ("Where is God my Maker?"); 40:19 (Behemoth's "maker") (WLC). Job's "Your hands fashioned and made (וַיַּעֲשׂוּנִי) me" (10:8) speaks to the same Maker. *Moderate.*
- **חֹמֶר ("clay") with עָפָר ("dust", 4:19).** In one verse only in Job: 4:19; 10:9; 27:16; 30:19 (lemma; 9–10 report). *High.*

**(d) Proverbs' vocabulary.** `[T]`
- **אֱוִיל ("fool", 5:2, 3)** — in Job only here (lemma); 19 times in Proverbs.
- **פֹּתֶה ("simple", 5:2)** — Proverbs' פֶּתִי, here as the verb's participle (lemma 6601).
- **כַּעַשׂ ("anger, vexation", 5:2)** — six times in Proverbs; in Job at 5:2; 6:2; 10:17; 17:7 (lemma). Job answers it at once (6:2).
- **עָרוּם ("shrewd", 5:12) and עָרְמָה ("shrewdness", 5:13)** — Gen 3:1's serpent (overview); Proverbs' "prudent" (Prov 12:16, 23).
- **תּוּשִׁיָּה ("success", 5:12)** — Prov 2:7; 3:21; 8:14; 18:1; in Job at 5:12; 6:13; 11:6; 12:16; 26:3; 30:22.
- **מוּסָר ("discipline", 5:17)** with מאס ("despise") — Prov 3:11; 15:32; Job 5:17 (Headline 1).
- Eliphaz is the dialogue's first speaker and its first proverb-speaker. `[T]` for the lemmas; `[I]` for the characterisation. *High.*

**(e) The hymn and the promise.** `[T]`
- **שָׁפָל ("lowly") with מָרוֹם ("height", 5:11).** Only Job 5:11 and Isa 57:15: "I dwell on a high (מָרוֹם) and holy place, and also with the contrite and lowly (שְׁפַל־רוּחַ) of spirit in order to revive the spirit of the lowly (שְׁפָלִים)" (lemma). *Moderate–high.*
- **קֹדְרִים ("those who mourn", 5:11).** קדר in Job at 5:11; 6:16; 30:28 (lemma). Job: "I go about mourning (קֹדֵר) without comfort" (30:28) — the mourner Eliphaz said God lifts to safety. *Moderate–high.*
- **מָטָר ("rain", 5:10).** In Job at 5:10; 28:26; 29:23; 36:27; 37:6; 38:28 (lemma). God: "Has the rain a father?" (38:28). *Moderate.*
- **שׂחק ("laugh", 5:22).** In Job at 5:22; 29:24; 30:1; 39:7, 18, 22; 40:20, 29; 41:21 [Eng 41:29] (lemma). Eliphaz: "You will laugh at violence and famine". Job: "those younger than I mock (שָׂחֲקוּ) me" (30:1). God: the wild donkey "scorns (יִשְׂחַק) the tumult of the city" (39:7), the ostrich "laughs at the horse" (39:18), the horse "laughs at fear" (39:22), Leviathan "laughs at the rattling of the javelin" (41:21). The laughter Eliphaz promised Job belongs, in God's speech, to the wild creatures. *Moderate–high.*
- **צֶאֱצָאִים ("offspring", 5:25).** In Job at 5:25; 21:8; 27:14; 31:8; otherwise only Isaiah (22:24; 34:1; 42:5; 44:3; 48:19; 61:9; 65:23) (lemma). Job: the wicked have "their offspring (וְצֶאֱצָאֵיהֶם) before their eyes" (21:8). *Moderate–high.*

- **גָּדִישׁ ("stack of grain", 5:26).** In Job only at 5:26 and 21:32, where the wicked man "is carried to the grave (לִקְבָרוֹת), and men will keep watch over his tomb (גָּדִישׁ)" (lemma; sweep, confirmed). Both verses have the grave and the stack. *High on the data.*
- **נָוֶה ("abode, fold", 5:3, 24).** "I cursed his abode (נָוֵהוּ)" (5:3); "you will visit your abode (נָוְךָ)" (5:24). Bildad: God will "restore your righteous estate (נְוַת)" (8:6). *Moderate.*
- **בְּרִית ("covenant", 5:23).** One of three in Job (5:23; 31:1; 40:28), none of them a covenant between God and a people (overview). Its only verbal partner in the Hebrew Bible is Hos 2:20 (Headline 3). *High.*

**(f) Names.**
- **Eliphaz the Temanite.** אֱלִיפַז occurs in Job at 2:11; 4:1; 15:1; 22:1; 42:7, 9, and elsewhere only as Esau's firstborn, father of Teman (Gen 36:4, 10–12, 15–16; 1 Chr 1:35–36) (lemma). תֵּימָן is Edom's heartland: Jer 49:7, 20; Ezek 25:13; Amos 1:12; Obad 9; Hab 3:3 (lemma). The canonical reader meets the name in Jeremiah's oracle on Edom: "Is there no longer any wisdom in Teman? Has good counsel been lost to the prudent? Has their wisdom decayed?" (Jer 49:7) — the only verse with תֵּימָן and חָכְמָה — and "the plan (עֲצַת) of the LORD … and His purposes (מַחְשְׁבוֹתָיו) … against the inhabitants of Teman", who "will make their pasture (נְוֵהֶם) desolate" (Jer 49:20) — the only verse with תֵּימָן, עֵצָה and מַחֲשָׁבָה (lemma). Eliphaz says God frustrates "the plotting (מַחְשְׁבוֹת) of the shrewd" and "the advice (עֲצַת) of the cunning" (5:12–13) and speaks of folds (5:3, 24). Obad 8–9 adds "destroy wise men from Edom … your mighty men will be dismayed, O Teman". `[T]` for the data; `[I]` for any irony intended — *moderate*. (The sweep's Unit 2 dealt with the name; the Jeremiah link is new.)
- **Divine names.** אֱלוֹהַּ (4:9, 17; 5:17), אֵל and אֱלֹהִים (5:8), שַׁדַּי (5:17), and "his Maker" (4:17). God is spoken of, never to. `[T]`

**Paronomasia check (words repeated three or more times).** אבד, עָמָל, ראה, ידע, עשׂה, בוא and the lion words were checked. Three sound- or sense-plays are worth recording:
- **שׁוֹט ("scourge", 5:21) and שׁוּט ("roam", 1:7; 2:2).** "You will be hidden from the scourge (בְּשׁוֹט) of the tongue." The claim audit confirmed the same sound-play at 9:23 with nuance: two lemmas, the same consonants `[S: audit]`. The reader knows the one whose tongue has roamed the earth and accused Job (1:7–11). *Moderate*; BHS also notes that the Greek, Syriac and Latin read מִשּׁוֹט and proposes the infinitive בְּשׁוּט.
- **כִּסְלָה ("confidence", 4:6) and כְּסִיל ("fool").** The sound of Proverbs' commonest word for "fool" in the word for Job's confidence; Ps 85:9 uses כִּסְלָה for "folly" and Swete renders "folly". *Moderate.*
- **חטא ("miss", 5:24) and חטא ("sin").** One verb, two senses (above). *Moderate.*

**What the table shows.**
- **The ESV lets the congregation hear the Scripture Eliphaz quotes better than the NASB95 does.** ESV 4:3–4 "strengthened the weak hands … made firm the feeble knees" matches ESV Isa 35:3 ("Strengthen the weak hands, and make firm the feeble knees"); the NASB95's Isa 35:3 ("Encourage the exhausted, and strengthen the feeble") hides it. ESV 4:8 "plow iniquity" matches ESV Hos 10:13 ("You have plowed iniquity"). ESV 5:18 "binds up" matches ESV Hos 6:1 ("he will bind us up"); the NASB95's "gives relief" breaks it. ESV 5:17 matches ESV Prov 3:11–12 ("despise … discipline … reproves"). For Headlines 1 and 3 the pulpit text is the better text.
- **ESV 4:17 "in the right before God"** is the phrase the ESV uses at 9:2 ("how can a man be in the right before God?") (9–10 report). The congregation can hear Job take up the vision's question.
- **ESV 5:2 "vexation"** is its word at 6:2 ("Oh that my vexation were weighed"); the NASB95 has "anger" and "grief". The ESV lets Job's first reply be heard answering Eliphaz.
- **ESV 5:12 "the crafty"** is its word at Gen 3:1 ("the serpent was more crafty"); NASB95's "the shrewd" breaks the link the overview records. ESV 5:13 "in their own craftiness" matches 1 Cor 3:19 in the ESV.
- **The NIV84 reads 4:17 as a comparison** — "Can a mortal be more righteous than God?" — taking מִן as "than". The Hebrew allows both; the parallel line ("pure before his Maker") and the later forms of the question — "how can a man be in the right with God (עִם־אֵל)?" (9:2; 25:4) — favour "before". `[I]`
- **Where no English version helps:**
  - 4:16's three Sinai–Horeb words: ESV "silence … a voice" against its own "the sound of a low whisper" at 1 Kgs 19:12.
  - 4:18 "His servants" against "My servant Job" (1:8; 2:3) — all versions keep "servant", so this one can be heard if the preacher points to it.
  - 5:16 against Ps 107:42: ESV "injustice shuts her mouth" against "all wickedness shuts its mouth". Supply it.

**Ancient versions check.** The modern versions agree on several renderings that carry weight — 4:6, 4:17, 5:17, 5:27 — so the check runs. The Old Greek has no asterisked lines in chapters 4–5 (Rahlfs export); Swete, triaged:
- **4:6 — a hostile rendering, and an edition difference.** Swete: "Is not your fear in folly (ἐν ἀφροσύνῃ), and your hope and the evil (κακία) of your way?" Rahlfs reads ἀκακία ("innocence") where Swete has κακία. Swete's 2:3 has the same ἀκακία of Job ("he still holds to his innocence"). *Category 1* and an edition fact: in Rahlfs, Eliphaz grants Job's innocence; in Swete, he charges evil. `[T]`, *as the two exports print them*.

### 9. Tone and Feel

**The register is pastoral wisdom: courteous, mystical, proverbial and finally lyrical — and every register is a way of telling Job that he is wrong.** `[T]` for the devices; `[I]` for the register.
- **4:2–6 — the gentle opening.** A question asking leave to speak; praise of Job's ministry to others; then the turn: "But now it has come to you, and you are impatient". The tone is a senior colleague's: kind, a little disappointed.
- **4:7–11 — doctrine as common sense.** "Remember now … As I have seen". The lions are a parable: the strongest predator's teeth are broken.
- **4:12–21 — the shiver.** A first-person account of a revelation, unique in the dialogue: a whisper stolen in the night, dread, trembling, hair standing on end, a form that cannot be made out, stillness, a voice. Eliphaz claims an authority beyond experience.
- **5:1–7 — the fool's ruin.** A brief, chilling vignette: the fool takes root, Eliphaz curses his house, his sons are crushed at the gate, the hungry eat his harvest. Spoken to a man whose sons are dead and whose goods are taken.
- **5:8–16 — doxology.** The hymn of reversals: rain on the earth, the lowly lifted, the crafty caught. Orthodox and beautiful.
- **5:17–26 — the idyll.** Blessing, healing, rescue from six troubles and seven, laughter at famine, peace with stones and beasts, a secure tent, many descendants, a grave in ripe age like a sheaf at harvest. The most beautiful promise in the dialogue.
- **5:27 — the seal.** "We have investigated it, and so it is."

**The soundtrack.** A pastor's visit that begins with a hand on the shoulder and ends with a closed file. **For the preacher:**
- Do not caricature Eliphaz. His speech is the friends' case at its finest, and most of its sentences are Scripture.
- Let the congregation feel 5:4 and 5:25 as Job would hear them.
- Do not read 4:12–21 as a straightforward revelation from God (Difficult Verses).

**Proverbs 3:11–12 → 5:17 (with Ps 94:12)** *(high; overview, confirmed)* `[T]`

*Move 1 — Source context.*
- A father's instruction (Prov 3:1–12) closes: "My son, do not reject (אַל־תִּמְאָס) the discipline (מוּסַר) of the LORD or loathe His reproof (בְּתוֹכַחְתּוֹ), for whom the LORD loves He reproves (יוֹכִיחַ), even as a father corrects the son in whom he delights" (3:11–12). Then: "How blessed (אַשְׁרֵי) is the man who finds wisdom" (3:13).

*The verbal data* (lemma, WLC):
- מוּסָר with מאס ("despise") — Prov 3:11; 15:32; Job 5:17. Job 5:17 alone adds יכח ("reprove") in the same verse; Prov 3:11–12 has all three across two verses, and אַשְׁרֵי at 3:13.

*Move 2 — Book usage.* Proverbs is Job's canonical neighbour and live in the overview (Prov 1:7; 3; 8). This unit adds Prov 22:8, 22 and 15:22 (below). **Eliphaz is the dialogue's Proverbs-speaker.** `[T]`

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT and onward.* Heb 12:5–6 quotes Prov 3:11–12 to sons who suffer: "God deals with you as with sons" (12:7). `[T]` (SBLGNT).

*What it adds.* Proverbs grounds discipline in a father's love for a son; Eliphaz keeps the reproof and drops "love", "son" and "father". Hebrews restores them. The word is true; as a diagnosis of Job it is false, because the reader knows his suffering is not correction for sin (1:8; 2:3). `[T]` for the omissions; `[I]` for the verdict.

**Isaiah 35:3 → 4:3–4 (and Heb 12:12)** *(high on the words; sweep, confirmed; direction open)* `[T]`

*Move 1 — Source context.* Isaiah 35 is the desert in bloom and the redeemed returning to Zion. 35:3–4: "Encourage the exhausted, and strengthen the feeble. Say to those with anxious heart, 'Take courage, fear not. Behold, your God will come with vengeance … He will save you.'" *The verbal data.* Job 4:3–4 has יָדַיִם רָפוֹת, חזק ("strengthen"), כּוֹשֵׁל ("tottering"), בִּרְכַּיִם and אמץ ("make firm"); כשׁל with בֶּרֶךְ occurs only at Isa 35:3; Ps 109:24; Job 4:4 (lemma).

*Move 2 — Book usage.* Isaiah is live in Job (Isa 41:20 at 12:9, overview; the Servant, Isa 11, Isa 26 and Isa 44 in the digs). `[T]`

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT and onward.* Heb 12:12 quotes Isa 35:3 (from its Greek) beside Prov 3:11–12 (Heb 12:5–6). Swete's Job 4:3–4 does not reproduce Isaiah's Greek, so Hebrews reached the pairing through Isaiah and Proverbs, not through Job (sweep). `[T]`

*What it adds.* Eliphaz grants that Job did Isaiah's work for others; now he must take Isaiah's medicine himself. But in Isaiah the weak are strengthened because "your God will come … He will save you" — a promise, not a rebuke. Eliphaz turns the prophet's comfort into a reproach. `[I]` *High on the words.*

**The Sinai–Horeb revelations — Num 12:6–8; Deut 4:12; 1 Kgs 19:11–12 → 4:12–16** *(high on the words; moderate–high on the design)* `[T]` / `[I]` (synthetic)

*Move 1 — Source context.*
- **Num 12:6–8.** God defends Moses against Miriam and Aaron: to a prophet "I shall make Myself known to him in a vision (בַּמַּרְאָה). I shall speak with him in a dream. Not so, with My servant (עַבְדִּי) Moses … With him I speak mouth to mouth, even openly (וּמַרְאֶה), and not in dark sayings, and he beholds the form (וּתְמֻנַת) of the LORD."
- **Deut 4:12, 15–16.** At Horeb "the LORD spoke to you from the midst of the fire; you heard the sound of words, but you saw no form (וּתְמוּנָה) — only a voice (קוֹל)". Therefore "watch yourselves carefully, since you did not see any form on the day the LORD spoke to you at Horeb … so that you do not act corruptly and make a graven image".
- **1 Kgs 19:11–12.** At Horeb Elijah is told, "Go forth and stand (וְעָמַדְתָּ) on the mountain before the LORD". A great wind (רוּחַ), an earthquake, a fire — "but the LORD was not in the fire; and after the fire a sound of a gentle blowing (קוֹל דְּמָמָה דַקָּה)".
- The register in all three is revelation at its highest — Moses, Israel, Elijah — with one shared caution: no form of God is to be seen or made, except by Moses.

*The verbal data* (lemma co-occurrence, WLC):
- **מַרְאֶה + תְּמוּנָה** — only Num 12:8 and Job 4:16.
- **תְּמוּנָה + קוֹל** — only Deut 4:12 and Job 4:16.
- **דְּמָמָה + קוֹל** — only 1 Kgs 19:12 and Job 4:16.
- **רוּחַ + עמד** — 1 Kgs 19:11 (and 1 Kgs 22:21, below); Job 4:15–16 has רוּחַ ("a spirit passed") and יַעֲמֹד ("it stood still").
- **The setting** — תַּרְדֵּמָה falling (Gen 2:21; Gen 15:12, with "terror and great darkness"); Num 12:6's dream and vision; Job 4:13's "visions of the night".

*Move 2 — Book usage.* Torah and the Former Prophets are present in Job through Genesis (overview), Exodus (earlier digs) and Deuteronomy 28 and 32 (earlier digs). This is the first contact with the revelation texts. It returns: Elihu repeats 4:13 at 33:15 to describe how God speaks "in a dream, a vision of the night"; and God himself answers Job out of a storm (סְעָרָה, 38:1), not in a whisper. `[T]` for the data; synthetic.

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* Deut 4 and Num 12 are a pair in the Torah: Israel saw no form; only Moses saw "the form of the LORD". 1 Kgs 19 restages Horeb for Elijah, and the LORD is not in the wind but in the stillness. Ps 17:15 hopes to see God's "form" (תְּמוּנָה) "when I awake". `[T]`

*What it adds.*
- Eliphaz describes his vision in the words of the canon's greatest revelations: a form (Moses), a voice without a form (Horeb), a stillness and a voice (Elijah). He claims something like a prophet's authority.
- But the content contradicts what God has said. God called Moses "My servant" (Num 12:7–8) and calls Job "My servant" (1:8; 2:3); the voice says God "puts no trust even in His servants" (4:18). God said Job was blameless (1:8; 2:3); the voice says no mortal is righteous before God (4:17; Swete's ἄμεμπτος).
- Deut 4 warned Israel that because they saw no form they must not make one. Eliphaz says "a form was before my eyes" — but "I could not discern its appearance" (4:16). The reader is left asking whose form it was (Difficult Verses).
- God's verdict on Eliphaz (42:7) is the book's verdict on the vision's use. `[I]` *Moderate–high.* **Load-bearing (Headline 2); unaudited.**

**Hosea — 2:20 [Eng 2:18]; 5:14; 6:1; 10:13 → 4:8, 10; 5:4, 18, 20–23** *(high on the words; moderate–high on the pattern; synthetic)* `[T]` / `[I]`

*Move 1 — Source context.*
- **Hos 5:12–6:3.** God against Ephraim and Judah: "I am like a moth to Ephraim and like rottenness to the house of Judah … I will be like a lion (כַשַּׁחַל) to Ephraim and like a young lion (וְכַכְּפִיר) to the house of Judah. I, even I, will tear to pieces and go away, I will carry away, and there will be none to deliver (וְאֵין מַצִּיל)" (5:12, 14). Then the call to return: "Come, let us return to the LORD. For He has torn us, but He will heal (וְיִרְפָּאֵנוּ) us; He has wounded us, but He will bandage (וְיַחְבְּשֵׁנוּ) us. He will revive us after two days; He will raise us up on the third day, that we may live before Him" (6:1–2).

- The register is covenant prosecution of a faithless Israel, with healing after repentance.

*The verbal data* (lemma co-occurrence, WLC):
- **חרשׁ + קצר** (4:8) — 1 Sam 8:12; Amos 9:13; Hos 10:13; Job 4:8. Moral sense only at Hos 10:13 and Job 4:8.
- **שַׁחַל + כְּפִיר** (4:10) — Hos 5:14; Ps 91:13; Job 4:10.
- **וְאֵין מַצִּיל** (5:4) — 11 verses (phrase search), including Hos 5:14, Isa 42:22 and Ps 50:22.
- **חבשׁ + רפא** (5:18) — Isa 30:26; Ezek 34:4; Hos 6:1; Ps 147:3; Job 5:18. With מחץ, Deut 32:39 (below).
- **בְּרִית + חַיַּת הַשָּׂדֶה** (5:23) — only Hos 2:20 and Job 5:23. Job 5:20 has מִלְחָמָה and חֶרֶב, Hos 2:20's sword and war.
- **רָקָב + עָשׁ** (13:28) — only Hos 5:12 and Job 13:28 (13–14 report).

*Move 2 — Book usage.* Hosea is new to the map. **It now touches Job in two speeches and five places:** Eliphaz at 4:8, 4:10, 5:4, 5:18 and 5:20–23, and Job at 13:28; and Job's "You hunt me like a lion (כַּשַּׁחַל)" (10:16) makes God the lion as Hos 5:14 does. `[T]` for the links; the pattern is synthetic, capped at moderate–high until audited.

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* Hosea's covenant of peace with the beasts (2:20) is developed in Ezek 34:25 ("I will make a covenant of peace with them and eliminate harmful beasts from the land") and rests on Lev 26:6. Hos 6:1–2's "He will raise us up on the third day" becomes, in the New Testament's reading of the Scriptures, part of the hope of resurrection "on the third day according to the Scriptures" (1 Cor 15:4) `[S]`. `[T]` for the Hebrew links.

*What it adds.*
- Eliphaz speaks the covenant prophet's language of judgement (the ploughed wickedness, the broken lion, no deliverer) and of healing (wounded and bound up; covenant with the beasts). In Hosea these words are spoken to a people who have broken covenant and must return.
- Job is outside Israel, and the reader knows he has not broken faith (1:8; 2:3). Eliphaz's doctrine fits Hosea's Israel; it does not fit Job. That is the book's quarrel with the friends in miniature: true theology misapplied.
- Job answers in the same register: if God is the lion, it is God who hunts him (10:16). `[I]` *Moderate–high.*

- מחץ + רפא — only Deut 32:39 and Job 5:18 (9–10 report).
- רֶשֶׁף — Deut 32:24; Hab 3:5; Ps 76:4; 78:48; Song 8:6; Job 5:7 (בְנֵי־רֶשֶׁף, "sparks"). Deut 32:24 joins it to רָעָב ("famine", Job 5:20) and to "the teeth (שֵׁן) of beasts" (Job 4:10's "teeth", 5:22's beasts).
- משׁשׁ + צָהֳרַיִם — only Deut 28:29 and Job 5:14.
- **Job's reply.** חֵץ ("arrow") with שַׁדַּי — only Job 6:4; Deut 32:23–24 has the arrows and the "venom" (חֵמָה), as 6:4 does ("their poison (חֲמָתָם) my spirit drinks"). משׁשׁ + חֹשֶׁךְ — only Exod 10:21 and Job 5:14; 12:25.

*Move 2 — Book usage.* Deuteronomy is live in Job: Deut 28 at 1:10, 2:7 and 3:25 (earlier digs), now 5:14; Deut 32:39 at 5:18 and 10:7. **With 5:7, 5:20 and 6:4, the Song of Moses touches the first exchange four or five times, from both sides.** And Exod 10:21, found at 3:4 (Job 3 report), returns at 12:25 with 5:14's verb. `[T]`

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* Deut 32:39 is taken up at Isa 43:13 (9–10 report) and Hos 6:1 (above). `[T]`

*What it adds.* Eliphaz uses the Song's healing (32:39) and, faintly, its plagues (32:24); Job replies with its arrows and poison (32:23–24) — as though to say, "You have quoted the healing; I am living the curse." The covenant song is about Israel's apostasy; neither speaker is in Israel, and Job has not apostatised. `[I]` *Moderate–high on the pattern.*

**Psalm 107:40–42 → 5:11, 15–16 (and 12:21, 24)** *(high, verbatim)* `[T]`

*Move 1 — Source context.* Psalm 107 calls the redeemed to thank God, in four scenes of rescue (107:4–32), then sings God's reversals: "He pours contempt upon princes and makes them wander in a pathless waste. *The verbal data.*
- עַוְלָה + קפץ + פֶּה — only Ps 107:42 and Job 5:16 (lemma); the clause is verbatim apart from כָל־ (phrase search).
- שׂגב + אֶבְיוֹן — Ps 107:41; Job 5:11 has שׂגב ("lifted to safety"), 5:15 has אֶבְיוֹן ("the poor").
- **Job's half.** Ps 107:40 is quoted at Job 12:21a ("He pours contempt on nobles") and 12:24b ("makes them wander in a pathless waste") — word for word (overview).

*Move 2 — Book usage.* Ps 107 is a live source in the overview (12:21, 24). **It is now used by both speakers, and they take adjacent verses**: Eliphaz the rescue of the needy (107:41–42), Job the humiliation of princes (107:40). `[T]`

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* Ps 107:40 is itself shared with Job 12; Ps 107:41's lifting of the needy belongs with Hannah's song (1 Sam 2:7–8) and Ps 113:7–8 (live at 1:21, earlier digs). `[T]` for the links.

*What it adds.* The pattern of the 9–10 dig recurs: a single text is split between Eliphaz and Job (there Deut 32:39; here Ps 107:40–42). Eliphaz sings the comfort; Job sings the judgement. Both are true readings of the psalm, and the book will not let either stand alone. `[I]` *High on the words; moderate–high on the pattern.*

*Addressee-differentiation.*

- **Who answers this speech?** Job answers almost every line (above). Elihu quotes it (33:15) and supplies the mediator it denies (33:23). God answers its rain (38:28), its laughter (39–41), its "searched out" (28:27, if 28 is the book's own voice) — and its speaker (42:7–9).

### 12. Genre

**Hebrew poetry: a disputation speech in the form of pastoral wisdom, with an embedded vision report, a hymn and a beatitude-promise.** `[T]`
- **Embedded forms.**
  - *An exhortation (4:2–6)*: praise of past conduct and a call to confidence.
  - *Sapiential observation (4:7–11; 5:2–7)*: "I have seen", proverbs, an animal parable.
  - *A vision report (4:12–21)*: setting, physical terror, the apparition, the oracle.
  - *A hymn in participles (5:9–16)*.
  - *A beatitude with a catalogue of blessings (5:17–26)*.
  - *A teacher's seal (5:27)*: "we have investigated it".
- **Reading rules.**
  - It is character speech, and God names its speaker in his rebuke (42:7). Report it as what Eliphaz says, never as the book's doctrine.
  - Most of its sentences are true in general (Paul cites 5:13; Hebrews uses its two texts); the error lies in their application to Job.
  - The vision is reported by Eliphaz; the narrator does not vouch for it.
- **What the genre does not license.**
  - It does not license "the innocent never perish" (4:7) as biblical teaching.
  - It does not license treating all suffering as divine discipline for sin (5:17).
  - It does not license night visions as authority over the narrator's and God's word. `[I]`

**Common copycat misreading.** "Eliphaz was a godly friend; let's follow his counsel." Most of his sentences are true, which is why the misreading is easy. The book shows that true sentences can make a false speech, and God says so (42:7). The comforter must know the case before he quotes the text. `[I]`

**Where the reader sits.** This side of the cross. `[I]`

**How it reframes the reading.** The Christian may not use Eliphaz's doctrine to diagnose suffering, because the cross falsifies its premise. But the Christian may use his texts, as Hebrews does, inside the Father's love. `[I]`

### 15. Who Am I?

| Speaker / figure | Function |
|---|---|
| **Eliphaz** | The **wise comforter** with the right texts and the wrong diagnosis. **Representative** of every pastor, friend or preacher who explains suffering too soon. **Not** an example to follow, though most of his sentences are true. Rebuked by name (42:7); restored through Job's prayer (42:8–10) |
| **Job** | Addressed and silent until 6:1. Praised for his past ministry (4:3–4) and told his piety should be his confidence (4:6). The reader knows he is "My servant" and blameless |
| **God** | Spoken of — the Maker, the destroyer of the wicked, the doer of wonders, the one who wounds and heals — but never addressed. Silent |
| **The spirit of the vision** | Unidentified. It stands, it cannot be made out, it speaks. Its message contradicts God's own word about Job (Difficult Verses) |
| **The fool** (5:2–5) | The type Eliphaz holds up as warning: rooted, cursed, his sons crushed, his harvest eaten — the very outline of Job's losses |
| **The reader** | Knows the prologue: God's trust in his servant, the Accuser's "touch", the children's deaths, the "without cause" (2:3). Hears every promise and every warning against that knowledge |

**The "Job-is-me" trap here cuts the other way.** The danger in this chapter is not that we identify too quickly with Job, but that we fail to identify with Eliphaz. Every hearer who has ever explained someone else's suffering to them is in his chair. `[I]`

### 16. So What?

**Stage 1 — the author's intended response.** To feel the persuasive force of the friends' case, and then to see it fail: to recognise that true doctrine, Scripture's own words and even religious experience can be misapplied to the suffering, and that God judges the misapplication (42:7). `[T]` / `[I]`

**Stage 2 — worldview, behaviour, motivation.**

| Domain | Application |
|---|---|
| **Worldview** | God does wound and heal (5:18), frustrate the crafty (5:12–13), lift the lowly (5:11) and discipline those he loves (5:17). But the righteous can perish (Isa 57:1; Eccl 7:15), and suffering is not proof of sin (1:8; 2:3; John 9:3). Both truths are in the canon; Eliphaz has only one |
| **Stop** | Diagnosing suffering from a doctrine. Quoting Eliphaz as the Bible's verdict on a sufferer. Claiming a private word ("I sensed the Lord saying …") as authority over the sufferer's own case and over God's word |
| **Start** | Sitting silent, as the friends did for seven days (2:13), before speaking. Using the true texts (Prov 3:11–12; Isa 35:3) as Hebrews does, inside the Father's love. Praying for the sufferer, and being willing to be prayed for by him (42:8) |
| **Motivation** | God trusts his Servant, and in Christ calls us his children; discipline, where it is discipline, is love (Heb 12:5–11). And God's verdict on the comforters is meant to make us humble comforters |

### Historical and Cultural Background

**Setting.** The story's world is patriarchal and outside Israel (overview). Eliphaz is a Temanite — of Edom, by the Genesis genealogy (Gen 36:11, 15) `[T]`; Edom had a reputation for wisdom (Jer 49:7; Obad 8) `[T]`. The composition date is `[S]` and not needed here.

**What the original audience knew.**
1. **The sage's appeal to experience.** "As I have seen" (4:8; 5:3) is the method of Proverbs and of Qoheleth (Prov 24:30–34; Eccl 7:15) `[T]`. Eliphaz's evidence is his observation; Qoheleth will observe the opposite.
2. **Night visions.** Revelation by dream or night vision is familiar in the Torah and Prophets (Gen 15:12; 28:12; Num 12:6; 1 Sam 3; Dan 7) `[T]`; it is also a known channel of false prophecy (Deut 13:1–5; Jer 23:25–32) `[T]`. Deut 13 tests a dream by its message, not by its vividness.
3. **Lions.** Lions were real in the Levant and the standard image of the violent oppressor (Ps 7:2; 17:12; 22:13, 21) `[T]`.
4. **The gate.** The city gate was the court (Deut 21:19; Ruth 4:1; Prov 22:22; Amos 5:12, 15) `[T]`. "Crushed in the gate" (5:4) means losing one's case — the fool's sons have no advocate.
5. **The covenant with the beasts.** Peace with wild animals and fertile, stone-free fields are blessings of covenant faithfulness (Lev 26:6; Hos 2:20; Ezek 34:25) `[T]`. Stones in the field were a farmer's curse (2 Kgs 3:19, 25; Isa 5:2) `[T]`; a "covenant with the stones" is a playful image of the land itself at peace with you `[I]`.
6. **Grain stacked in its season.** The sheaf brought to the threshing-floor at harvest (Exod 22:5; Judg 15:5 — the only other uses of גָּדִישׁ) `[T]`: a ripe death at the proper time.

**What this changes.** Eliphaz's speech would have sounded to its first hearers like the best of their own tradition: observation, revelation, proverb, hymn and covenant blessing. That is the book's point. The tradition is not wrong; its use on Job is. `[I]`

### Original Audience Reception

**Canonical audience.** Readers of the Writings who know the Torah's Horeb, Elijah's Horeb, Hosea's covenant prosecution, Moses' song, the Psalter and Proverbs — and who have just read the prologue. `[I]`

**What they would have heard.**
- **Their own Scriptures.** Isa 35:3, Prov 3:11–12, Deut 32:39, Ps 107:41–42, Hosea's words (Headlines 1 and 3). They would nod.
- **A revelation in the words of Moses and Elijah** — and a message that God trusts none of his servants, after God has twice boasted of "My servant Job". They would hesitate.
- **"His sons are far from safety … crushed"** (5:4) — spoken to a man whose sons died under a fallen house (1:19). They would wince.
- **"Who ever perished being innocent?"** — after reading that God was incited to ruin Job "without cause" (2:3). They would know the answer.

**Expectations created.**
- That Job will accept the counsel, or refute it. He refutes it (6–7), line by line (Move 4).
- That Eliphaz's vision will be tested. Job calls such visions God's terrors (7:14); Elihu re-uses its frame (33:15); God speaks out of the storm.
- That the promise of 5:18–26 will be tested. It comes true (42:10–17), but only after God rebukes Eliphaz and Job prays for him.

**What would have surprised them.** That God's verdict at the end singles out the speaker of this beautiful speech: "My wrath is kindled against you" (42:7). `[T]` for the text; `[I]` for the surprise.

### Biblical-Theological Themes

**1. True words, wrongly applied.** `[T]` / `[I]`
- Job 4–5 (Prov 3; Isa 35; Deut 32; Ps 107; Hosea) → 42:7 → 1 Cor 3:19 (Paul cites Eliphaz as Scripture) → Heb 12:5–12 (the same texts in the Father's love).
- **For the preacher:** Scripture's words can be quoted against Scripture's God. The question is never only "is it true?" but "is it true of this person, now?"

**2. Revelation and its tests.** `[T]`
- Num 12:6–8; Deut 4:12–16 (no form seen; no image made) → Deut 13:1–5 (dreams tested by their message) → 1 Kgs 19:11–12 (the LORD in the stillness) → 1 Kgs 22:19–23 (a deceiving spirit in the council) → Job 4:12–21 → Jer 23:25–32 → 1 John 4:1 ("test the spirits").
- Eliphaz's vision has every mark of authenticity and a message that contradicts what God has said (1:8; 2:3). Deut 13's test applies.

**3. Retribution and its limits.** `[T]`
- Ps 1:6; Prov 10:28; 11:10 (the wicked perish) — Job 4:7 — Isa 57:1; Mic 7:2; Eccl 7:15 (the righteous perish) → Luke 13:1–5 ("do you suppose that these Galileans were greater sinners?") → John 9:2–3 ("neither this man sinned, nor his parents") → Luke 23:41.
- The canon holds both; Eliphaz has only one.

**4. Discipline as sonship.** `[T]`
- Prov 3:11–12 → Ps 94:12 → Job 5:17 → Heb 12:5–11.
- Discipline is real and good. It is not the explanation of all suffering, and in Job it is not the explanation at all.

**5. God who wounds and heals.** `[T]`
- Deut 32:39 → Hos 6:1–2 → Job 5:18 → Isa 30:26 → Ps 147:3.
- In Hosea healing follows return and leads to being raised "on the third day". Eliphaz has the healing without the cross that grounds it.

**6. The covenant of peace with creation.** `[T]`
- Lev 26:6 → Hos 2:20 → Job 5:22–23 → Ezek 34:25 → Isa 11:6–9 → Job 38–41 (God's wild creatures, untamed) → Rom 8:19–21.
- Eliphaz promises Job peace with the beasts; God will show Job the beasts and their freedom, not their domestication.

### Christological Reading

*(For the OT passage: the canonical trajectory, offered as raw material for `/point-purpose`.)*

**1. "Who ever perished being innocent?" (4:7 → the cross).** `[I]`
- The book answers with Job: an innocent man who suffers (1:8; 2:3). The canon answers with Isa 57:1, Mic 7:2 and Eccl 7:15. The gospel answers with the cross: "This man has done nothing wrong" (Luke 23:41); "the righteous for the unrighteous" (1 Pet 3:18).
- **Category:** *contrast* — Eliphaz's doctrine is falsified finally at Golgotha. *Moderate–high.*

**2. The Servant God trusts (4:18 → Isa 42:1; Matt 3:17).** `[I]`
- The vision says God trusts none of his servants. God calls Job "My servant" (1:8; 2:3; 42:7–8), and in Isaiah has a Servant "in whom My soul delights" (Isa 42:1), whom the Father names at the Jordan (Matt 3:17, from Isa 42:1 and Ps 2:7).
- **Category:** *contrast and trajectory*. *Moderate.*

**3. The holy one to whom you turn (5:1 → 42:8 → Heb 7:25).** `[I]`
- Eliphaz: there is no holy one to answer you. At the end Eliphaz himself must turn to Job, who prays for him (42:8–10). The New Testament names the one who "always lives to make intercession" (Heb 7:25) and the "one mediator" (1 Tim 2:5; 9–10 report).
- **Category:** *type* (Job as intercessor; overview's servant type) and *trajectory*. *Moderate.*

**4. He wounds and binds up (5:18; Hos 6:1–2 → 1 Cor 15:4).** `[I]`
- Hosea's "He has torn us, but He will heal us … He will raise us up on the third day" stands behind Eliphaz's 5:18. The New Testament speaks of resurrection "on the third day according to the Scriptures" `[S]` for Hos 6:2 among them. The healing Eliphaz promised Job is grounded, for the Christian, in the one wounded for us: "by His scourging we are healed" (Isa 53:5; 1 Pet 2:24).
- **Category:** *trajectory*. *Moderate.*

**5. True wisdom (5:12–13 → 1 Cor 1–3).** `[T]` for the citation
- Paul cites 5:13 — "He is the one who catches the wise in their craftiness" (1 Cor 3:19) — in his argument that God's wisdom is Christ crucified, foolishness to the world (1 Cor 1:18–25). The text Eliphaz used against Job, Paul uses against the wisdom that rejects the cross. Eliphaz himself is "caught": his wisdom fails before God (42:7).
- **Category:** *trajectory and irony*. *Moderate–high.*

**The moralism check.** The trap is to preach Eliphaz as a bad friend and the congregation as good ones. The text puts us in his chair. The gospel is not "be a better comforter" but "there is a righteous Servant who suffered innocently, and an Intercessor who prays for the comforters who failed". `[I]`

### Difficult / Contested Verses

**4:7–8 — "Who ever perished being innocent? … those who sow trouble harvest it."**
- **Category:** weaponised (a proof-text for prosperity and for blaming sufferers).
- **Honest exegesis:** Eliphaz's claim, contradicted by the prologue (1:8; 2:3), by the canon (Isa 57:1; Mic 7:2; Eccl 7:15) and by God (42:7). Sowing and reaping is true in general (Prov 22:8; Hos 10:13; Gal 6:7), but it does not run backwards from suffering to sin.
- **Handling:** never quote 4:7 as Scripture's teaching without saying who said it and what God said about him.

**4:12–21 — the night vision.**
- **Category:** doctrinal and pastoral.
- **Honest exegesis:** reported by Eliphaz, not by the narrator. Its form borrows Moses' and Elijah's revelations (Headline 2); its content contradicts God's word about Job (4:18 against 1:8; 2:3). Its general truth — no mortal is pure before God — is biblical (Ps 143:2; Rom 3:20); its use is to silence Job. Some have argued that the spirit is not from God at all `[S]`; the text leaves it unidentified ("I could not discern its appearance", 4:16).
- **Handling:** do not preach 4:17 as a word from God without the frame. Apply Deut 13's test: what does it say about what God has said?

**5:4 — "His sons are far from safety … crushed in the gate."**
- **Category:** pastoral.
- **Honest exegesis:** a general picture of the fool's ruin, said to a man whose children have died (1:18–19). Bildad will make it explicit (8:4).
- **Handling:** name the cruelty of a true-sounding generality in the wrong ear.

**5:17 — "How happy is the man whom God reproves."**
- **Category:** doctrinal (true, misapplied).
- **Honest exegesis:** Prov 3:11–12, as Hebrews uses it (Heb 12:5–11). Discipline is a mark of sonship. But Job's suffering is not discipline for sin (1:8; 2:3; 42:7), and Eliphaz leaves out Proverbs' "love" and "son".
- **Handling:** preach the truth of discipline with Hebrews, and its misapplication with Job.

**5:19–26 — the promise.**
- **Category:** prosperity reading.
- **Honest exegesis:** a beautiful picture of the blessed life, conditional on accepting a false diagnosis. The book's ending gives Job much of it (42:10–17), but as gift, after God has rebuked Eliphaz and Job has prayed for him.
- **Handling:** do not promise 5:19–26 to the suffering as a formula. Point to the book's end as God's free restoration, and beyond it to the resurrection.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: Quoting Eliphaz as the Bible's verdict

- **What it looks like:** "The Bible says, 'Who ever perished being innocent? … those who sow trouble harvest it'" (4:7–8); or 5:17 on a get-well card to someone in crisis.
- **Why it's wrong:** God says Eliphaz "has not spoken of Me what is right" (42:7). His sentences are true in general and false as a diagnosis of Job.
- **The corrective:** always name the speaker and God's verdict. Use 5:17 as Hebrews does — inside the Father's love, for those whose suffering is discipline — not as an explanation of all suffering.

### Pitfall: Caricaturing Eliphaz

- **What it looks like:** "Job's friends were heartless religious bullies."
- **Why it's wrong:** this speech is courteous, pastoral, scriptural and beautiful. Its danger is that it is persuasive. A caricature lets the congregation off.
- **The corrective:** read 4:2–6 and 5:17–26 with their warmth. Then show where the speech fails — in its premise about Job, not in its tone.

### Pitfall: Treating the night vision as revelation from God

- **What it looks like:** "God revealed to Eliphaz that no man can be righteous before him" (4:17), preached as a direct word from God.
- **Why it's wrong:** the narrator never says God sent it. Its message contradicts God's own words about Job (1:8; 2:3), and God rebukes its reporter (42:7). Its borrowed Sinai–Horeb vocabulary is the reason to test it, not to trust it.
- **The corrective:** preach 4:12–21 as Eliphaz's claimed experience, tested by what God has said. The general truth (no mortal is pure before God) is found elsewhere in Scripture (Ps 143:2; Rom 3:23) and is not in dispute; its use against Job is.

### Pitfall: Promising 5:19–26 as a formula

- **What it looks like:** "Accept God's discipline, and he will restore your health, your family and your wealth."
- **Why it's wrong:** the promise rests on a false diagnosis. The book does restore Job (42:10–17) — but freely, after God rebukes Eliphaz and Job prays for him.
- **The corrective:** preach the restoration of 42 as grace, not as the payoff of a formula, and the Christian's final restoration as resurrection.

### Pitfall: Preaching "don't be like Eliphaz" without the mirror

- **What it looks like:** a sermon on how not to comfort, with the preacher outside the frame.
- **Why it's wrong:** Eliphaz is the senior, wise, Scripture-quoting friend. Preachers are his successors.
- **The corrective:** let 42:7–8 land on the pulpit. And let the congregation hear the hope of 42:8–10: the failed comforter is restored through the intercession of the man he wronged.
