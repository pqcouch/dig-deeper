## Scope

**One sermon on the whole book, built on two anchor texts.**
- **1:9, the question:** הַחִנָּם יָרֵא אִיּוֹב אֱלֹהִים ("Does Job fear God for no reason?").
- **42:7–8, the verdict:** לֹא דִבַּרְתֶּם אֵלַי נְכוֹנָה כְּעַבְדִּי אִיּוֹב ("you have not spoken of me what is right, as my servant Job has").

The overview proposed this pair for a single sermon on Job, and the series plan adopted it as the introduction.

**What this sermon is for.** It gives the congregation the book's shape before they spend seven sermons inside the prologue, the debate and the storm.
- **It carries the verdict.** It hands them God's verdict in advance, so that they hear Job's laments and the friends' arguments fairly. The book itself does this for its reader: the reader is told "without reason" at 2:3, before anyone on earth speaks. `[T]`
- **It sets the series question** that Sermons 1–9 each answer from their own text.
- **It stays off the units' ground.** Sermon 1 has the prologue, Sermon 8 the storm and Sermon 9 the epilogue. This sermon **names** the prologue's question, the storm and the restoration, but does not **expound** them.

**Readings.** Four short readings, one for each movement, rather than one long one. Read the first at the start and each of the others as its movement begins:
1. 1:8–11 — the question.
2. 13:3, 7–8 — speaking to God, and speaking for him.
3. 38:1–7 and 42:5 — the answer.
4. 42:7–10 — the verdict.

## Exegetical Basis

*This sermon's dig. The book-level tools were worked in the overview and the sweep and are not repeated. What follows is the load-bearing evidence, re-checked in the WLC for this sermon (run32), with one new mechanical test.*

### 1. One question, and the fear of God as keynote

- **The question.** The Accuser's question is about God's worth as much as Job's virtue: is God feared for himself, or for what he gives (1:9–11; 2:4–5)? `[T]`
- **The keynote.** The book's keynote is the fear of God.
  - It is in the first sentence: "fearing God and turning away from evil" (1:1).
  - It is in God's own mouth (1:8; 2:3).
  - It is the last line of the wisdom poem: "the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom; and to depart from evil is understanding" (28:28).
  - סוּר מֵרָע ("turning from evil") occurs in Job only at 1:1, 1:8, 2:3 and 28:28 (overview, WLC phrase search). `[T]`

### 2. The book's shape: heaven unseen, earth argued, heaven speaking, earth restored

The overview's arc map, confirmed by the claim audit `[S: audit]`:

| Verses | Movement | Voice |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 (3:1) | the question asked in heaven | prose |
| 3–31 | the human debate, with the wisdom poem at the hinge (28) | poetry |
| 32–37 | Elihu | poetry |
| 38:1–42:6 | the LORD answers from the storm | poetry |
| 42:7–17 | the verdict given on earth | prose |

- **The two pressures.** The book answers two pressures `[T]`:
  - Is piety disinterested? (1:9)
  - Does suffering prove guilt? The friends say it does: "who that was innocent ever perished?" (4:7, ESV); "Is not your evil abundant?" (22:5).
- **What the reader knows.** The reader knows from 2:3 that in this case the premise is false. `[T]`

### 3. Speaking to God, and speaking for him *(the finding this sermon turns on)*

The sweep's second headline claimed that Job speaks **to** God and that nobody else in the debate does, and that God's verdict turns on it. It flagged the claim for a mechanical check. That check was run for this sermon.

**(a) The mechanical test** (WLC morphology, run32).
- **The method.** Every verse in each speaker's speeches with a second-person singular form (verb, pronoun or suffix) was listed and read, to see whom it addresses.

| Speaker | Verses with a second-person singular form | Addressed to God in the speaker's own voice |
|---|---:|---:|
| Eliphaz (4–5; 15; 22) | 50 | 0 |
| Bildad (8; 18; 25) | 11 | 0 |
| Zophar (11; 20) | 13 | 0 |
| Elihu (32:6–37:24) | 41 | 0 |
| Job (positive control) | 66 | most, including 7:12–21; 10:2–18; 13:20–14:20; 30:20–23 |

- **Friends and Elihu.** Every second-person form in their speeches addresses Job, or stands inside quoted speech:
  - the plant's place, "I have never seen you" (8:18);
  - Job's words to God as Zophar reports them (11:4);
  - the wicked to God (22:17);
  - a model penitent (34:31–32);
  - a hypothetical challenger (36:23);
  - God to the snow (37:6).
- **Job.** In his speeches the second-person forms turn again and again to God. The control passes.
- **Result.** No friend, and not Elihu, ever addresses God in prayer. They speak about him, to Job. `[T]` *High.*

**(b) The friends speak "for" God.** Job asks them:
- הַלְאֵל תְּדַבְּרוּ עַוְלָה ("Will you speak falsely for God?", 13:7, ESV);
- אִם־לָאֵל תְּרִיבוּן ("Will you plead the case for God?", 13:8, ESV).

The preposition is לְ ("for, on behalf of"). Elihu says the same of himself: "I have yet something to say on God's behalf (לֶאֱלוֹהַּ)" (36:2). `[T]`

**(c) Job resolves to speak "to" God.** In the same chapter, four verses earlier: אוּלָם אֲנִי אֶל־שַׁדַּי אֲדַבֵּר ("But I would speak to the Almighty", 13:3, ESV). The construction is the verb דבר ("speak") with the preposition אֶל ("to"). `[T]`

**(d) God's verdict uses Job's construction.** לֹא דִבַּרְתֶּם אֵלַי נְכוֹנָה ("you have not spoken to me what is right", 42:7; again at 42:8).
- **The same words in the same verses.** In 42:7–9 the same verb with the same preposition means "to" three more times:
  - "after the LORD had spoken these words **to** Job" (42:7a);
  - "the LORD said **to** Eliphaz" (42:7a, with אָמַר);
  - "as the LORD had told **them**" (42:9).
- **Swete.** It renders ἐνώπιόν μου ("before me").
- **The English versions.** NASB95, ESV and NIV84 all have "of me".
- **Keep honest.** דבר אֶל can mean "concerning" (1 Sam 3:12, "all that I have spoken concerning his house"; Jer 40:16, "you are speaking falsely of Ishmael"). So "of me" is a defensible translation.
- **The case for "of me" at its strongest (Logos round 3, 5 Oct 2026).** Ortlund ("How Did Job Speak Rightly about God?", *Themelios* 43.3, 2018, pp. 352–53) raises three objections: if only the address counted, "what is right" would be idle; דבר אֶל can include the content of what is said; and אֶל and עַל overlap elsewhere. `[S: Ortlund]` Walton and Longman (*How to Read Job*, 2015, p. 63) read "to me" but restrict it to Job's last words (42:1–6). `[S]`
- **What the text says to each.**
  - **Content.** נְכוֹנָה ("what is right, true") names what is said: its other use of speech is Ps 5:10, "there is nothing reliable (נְכוֹנָה) in their mouth". Swete has ἀληθὲς οὐδέν ("nothing true"). So the verdict judges what was said as well as to whom. Ortlund's first point stands.
  - **Content within address.** Agreed, and consistent with "to me".
  - **The overlap.** Real elsewhere, but not attested in this book. Every other דבר + אֶל in Job marks the person addressed (2:13; 13:3; 40:27; 42:7a, 9), and Job never has דבר + עַל; speaking "for" God takes לְ (13:7–8; 36:2) (WLC lemma index).
  - **The restriction to 42:1–6** is not required by the construction, since Job addresses God throughout the dialogue (table (a)). It is one way of holding 38:2 with 42:7.
  - `[T]` for the data; `[I]` for the weighing.
- **Net.** "To me" keeps its weight in context, but read the verdict whole: what the friends said to God was nothing, and what they said about him was not right; what Job said to God was. Direction *and* content, not direction alone.
- **What favours "to me".** Context favours it: the verse's own usage, Job's 13:3 and the address pattern in (a). `[T]` for the data; *moderate–high* on the preposition's weight.
- **The finding does not depend on the preposition alone.** It rests on (a) and (b) together.

**(e) What "right" means.**
- נְכוֹנָה ("what is right") is the niphal participle of כון ("to be firm, established"): what is firm, true, fitting.
- **Four limits keep the verdict honest** `[T]`:
  - God rebukes Job: "Who is this that darkens counsel by words without knowledge?" (38:2).
  - God asks: "Will you condemn me that you may be in the right?" (40:8, ESV).
  - Job lays his hand on his mouth (40:4–5).
  - Job confesses: "I have uttered what I did not understand" (42:3, ESV).
- **So the verdict commends** the direction and honesty of Job's speech — held on to God, addressed to God, refusing to confess guilt he did not have — **not every sentence of it.** `[I]`

### 4. God answers — with himself

- **The demand met.** Job demanded an answer (13:22; 31:35). "Then the LORD answered Job out of the whirlwind" (38:1). `[T]`
- **What God does and does not say.** The speeches never mention the council, the Accuser or a reason. They answer chapter 3 in its own words — the womb and its doors (3:10–11 → 38:8), the eyelids of the dawn (3:9 → 41:18 [Heb 41:10]), Leviathan roused (3:8 → 41:10 [Heb 41:2]). These are sweep Headline 1, all verified by lemma, and most are the only occurrences in the Hebrew Bible. `[T]`
- **Job's response.** "I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees you" (42:5, ESV). `[T]`
- **What the book gives.** It gives Job God, not the explanation. It gives the reader the explanation and forbids the reader to use it on anyone else (overview, trap 3). `[I]`

### 5. The verdict, the prayer and the gift

- **"My servant Job."** God calls Job this four times in 42:7–8 (once in 42:7, three times in 42:8), as at 1:8 and 2:3. God's first estimate is his last verdict. `[T]`
- **The friends' "folly".** God will not deal with them "according to your folly (נְבָלָה)" (42:8). Job used the cognate word of his wife's counsel (הַנְּבָלוֹת, 2:10). `[T]`
- **Lifting the face.** Job warned the friends that God would "surely rebuke" them if they "show partiality" — literally "lift his face" (פָנָיו תִּשָּׂאוּן) — for God (13:8, 10). God now says "I will lift his face" (פָנָיו אֶשָּׂא, 42:8), and "the LORD lifted Job's face" (42:9). The ones who flattered God are judged; the one who argued with him is accepted. `[T]` (overview echo table, re-read in the WLC).
- **The restoration.** "The LORD restored the fortunes of Job when he had prayed for his friends" (42:10, ESV).
  - The restoration follows the verdict and the intercession.
  - The flocks are exactly doubled (1:3 → 42:12), but the children are not: seven sons and three daughters, as before (1:2 → 42:13).
  - "The LORD blessed the latter days of Job" (42:12). The word the Accuser used as "curse" (1:11; 2:5) closes the book as God's gift.
  - `[T]` for the data; the reading of the undoubled children is `[I]`.

### 6. Christological reading (from the overview, re-checked)

- **Trajectory — the mediator Job cannot find.** No arbiter (9:33) → a witness in heaven (16:19–21) → "I know that my Redeemer lives" (19:25). The New Testament names him: "one mediator … the man Christ Jesus, who gave himself as a ransom" (1 Tim 2:5–6). *High for the trajectory; low for any claim that Job foresaw Christ.*
- **Type — the servant who suffers "without cause" and intercedes.**
  - God's "my servant" and "without reason" (2:3); the prayer for those who wronged him (42:8–10).
  - Christ was "hated without a cause" (John 15:25).
  - He prayed for those who crucified him (Luke 23:34).
  - He "always lives to make intercession" (Heb 7:25).
  - *Moderate*: the type runs through the servant category; Isaiah is not quoted.
- **Contrast.** Job is righteous, not sinless (38:2; 40:8; 42:6). Christ "when he was reviled, did not revile in return" (1 Pet 2:23). *High.*
- **The gift.** God declares "right" the man who clung to him. In the gospel God justifies the ungodly as a gift (Rom 3:24; 4:5), because the true Servant earned the verdict. `[I]`

## Fallen Condition Focus

**We suspect that our faith is really a bargain; and when suffering comes we reach for an explanation, or turn away from God, or go quiet before him — because we do not believe he wants to be spoken to in our anguish, or that he is worth holding on to when he gives no reasons.**

---

## Teaching Point

**The book of Job asks whether anyone will fear God for nothing, and God himself gives the verdict: the servant who held on to him and poured out his anguish *to* him has spoken what is right, while the friends who defended him with a formula spoke folly — for God is worth fearing for himself, he answers with himself rather than with reasons, and in the end he gives his verdict and his blessing as a gift, the gift the true Servant has won for all who come to God through him.**

- **Subject:** When life falls apart, what does God count as right speech — and is he worth fearing for nothing?
- **Complement:** Honest speech held on to God and addressed to him, not explanations offered for him. And yes: he is worth it, because he gives himself, and in Christ he gives the verdict "my servant" as a gift.

**Built from:** Exegetical Basis findings 1, 3, 4 and 5, which converge. The overview's microscript and purpose are confirmed.

## Purpose Statement

**To give the congregation the key to the whole book before they enter it — and to move them from talking *about* God in suffering to talking *to* him.** Differentiated by hearer:

- **The suffering:** give them permission. Lament, protest and complaint addressed to God are the speech God calls right. The book is on the side of the sufferer who keeps talking to God.
- **The comforters** — which is most of us: warn them. The book condemns the orthodox explanation spoken over a sufferer. Commend the friends' first week of silence (2:13), and Job's closing prayer for the very men who wounded him (42:10).
- **The half-hearted:** press the Accuser's question on their own faith. Would it survive the loss of the gifts?
- **The sceptic:** show them a book that refuses cheap answers to suffering. Then show them the Mediator Job longed for — Christ, who was hated without a cause, prays for those who wrong him, and gives God's verdict as a gift.

**For the series:** set the question that every sermon will answer, and ask the congregation to read the book alongside the series.

## Proposed Structure

Four movements, following the book's own shape: heaven unseen → earth argued → heaven speaking → earth restored. The pace is brisk. This is a map, not a tour.

### 1. The question asked in heaven (chapters 1–2; read 1:8–11)

*"Does Job fear God for no reason?"*
- **The man, the council, the question** — which is about God's worth as much as Job's.
- **Two tests take everything.** Job worships, and does not curse.
- **Only the reader is shown heaven.** Job never is. Hand the congregation God's own words, "without reason" (2:3), and tell them to keep them in their pocket for the whole series.

*Function:* raise the FCF, and set the series question.

### 2. The argument on earth (chapters 3–37; read 13:3, 7–8)

*Three friends with a formula; a sufferer with a complaint.*
- **The friends' doctrine is simple and partly true:** the innocent do not perish (4:7). Paul quotes Eliphaz as Scripture (Job 5:13 in 1 Cor 3:19).
- **But they apply it as a verdict on Job,** and they speak about God, for God, to the sufferer: "Will you speak falsely for God?" (13:7).
- **Job refuses** both to curse God and to confess sins he did not commit.
- **Job keeps turning to God:** "But I would speak to the Almighty" (13:3). He longs for an arbiter (9:33), a witness in heaven (16:19) and a Redeemer (19:25).
- **The friends never pray. Not once.**

*Function:* expose the two ways of speaking. This is the heart of the sermon, and the unit sermons (3–7) will fill it in.

### 3. The answer from the storm (chapters 38:1–42:6; read 38:1–7 and 42:5)

*"Then the LORD answered Job out of the whirlwind."*
- **God comes.** That is what Job asked for, and what the friends said would not happen.
- **He gives no reasons.** No word about the council or the Accuser.
- **He gives himself,** the Creator who shuts in the sea, feeds the raven and rules Leviathan.
- **Job is humbled but not crushed:** "now my eye sees you" (42:5).

*Function:* the TP's middle clause. God answers with himself rather than with reasons. Name it; Sermon 8 will expound it.

### 4. The verdict on earth (chapter 42:7–17; read 42:7–10)

*"You have not spoken to me what is right, as my servant Job has."*
- **The verdict.** God's anger falls on the friends. Job is "my servant" four times. The man who argued with God is accepted; the men who flattered God are rebuked.
- **Job's prayer** for the friends who wounded him.
- **The restoration** as gift, not wage — the children not doubled. Bring it in only briefly; Sermon 9 will preach it.

*Landing — the Christ turn.* The Mediator Job longed for has come.
- **Christ is the true Servant.** He feared God for nothing when the Accuser offered him everything (Matt 4:8–10), and was hated without a cause (John 15:25).
- **He speaks to the Father for us** and intercedes for those who wronged him (Luke 23:34; Heb 7:25; Rom 8:33–34).
- **In him God gives his verdict as a gift** to all who come to God through him (Rom 3:24).

So the answer to the series question is yes: God is worth it, and he has proved it by giving himself. And the application: talk to him — in the worst of it, honestly — and pray for those who hurt you.

*Function:* press the PS, and send the congregation into the series with the verdict in hand.

## Keep Honest

1. **"Spoke what is right" does not bless every word Job said.**
   - God rebukes him (38:2; 40:8), he lays his hand on his mouth (40:4–5), and he confesses he spoke of what he did not understand (42:3).
   - The verdict commends the direction and honesty of his speech, not each sentence.
   - Do not make the sermon a licence for any complaint in any tone.
2. **Do not make the friends cartoon villains.**
   - Much of what they say is true in general. Paul quotes Eliphaz (1 Cor 3:19).
   - They came to comfort, and began well (2:11–13).
   - Their error was to apply true general statements as a verdict on Job's particular case, and to speak for God to the sufferer instead of speaking to God with him. That is the error the congregation is most likely to commit.
3. **Do not promise the explanation the book withholds.**
   - God never tells Job about the council, and the sermon must not tell sufferers which scene lies behind their pain.
   - "God is proving you" is the friends' move in a new form.
4. **Do not sell the ending.**
   - The restoration follows the verdict and Job's prayer, and it is God's free gift. It is the very thing the Accuser said God was buying.
   - The children are not doubled.
   - It is not a promise that every faithful sufferer will be restored in this life.
5. **Do not rest the whole sermon on one preposition.**
   - אֵלַי can mean "concerning", and every English version says "of me".
   - The case rests on the pattern — the friends never pray, they speak "for God" (13:7), and Job resolves to speak "to the Almighty" (13:3) — with the preposition as its seal.
   - Say "the Hebrew can be read 'to me' — and the whole book supports it", not "the Hebrew says".
   - The verdict also judges content: נְכוֹנָה is "what is right". Do not say that Job was right merely because he prayed.
6. **Leave Elihu alone here.** He is absent from the verdict (42:7 names Eliphaz "and your two friends"). His status is Sermon 7's question, and this sermon should not settle it.

## Christ-centred check

1. **"Be like Job", or "trust the Christ whom Job points to"?**
   - The TP makes God the subject: his worth, his answer and his verdict. It ends in the true Servant.
   - The application ("talk to him; pray for those who hurt you") flows from the gift of the verdict, not towards earning it. **Pass.**
2. **Is Christ the subject or merely an example?**
   - Christ is presented as the Mediator Job longed for and the Servant who intercedes and wins the verdict for others — not as a more patient Job. **Pass.**
3. **Is the good news grounded in Christ's saving work?**
   - Yes: the ransom (1 Tim 2:6), the intercession (Heb 7:25; Rom 8:34), and justification as a gift (Rom 3:24). **Pass.**
   - *Caution:* the servant type is moderate (Isaiah is not quoted in Job), and the mediator trajectory is canonical, not a claim about what Job knew. Present both as the canon's answer to Job's longing.

## Pulpit-text note (ESV)

Checked against the ESV export for Job 1, 4, 5, 9, 13, 19, 38 and 42.

**Where the ESV helps the plan.** Six notes, including the movement 2 hinge.
- **1:9 — "for no reason".** The series question is in the pew Bible.
- **13:3 — "But I would speak to the Almighty"**, and **13:7 — "Will you speak falsely for God?"**. Both prepositions are audible — "to the Almighty" against "for God". **Movement 2's hinge can be read straight from the ESV.**
- **9:33 — "There is no arbiter between us"**; **19:25 — "For I know that my Redeemer lives"**. The mediator trajectory is clear.
- **42:5 — "but now my eye sees you".**
- **42:11 — "showed him sympathy and comforted him for all the evil that the LORD had brought upon him".** This matches 2:11 ("to show him sympathy and comfort him") and "all this evil" (2:11). The comforters' purpose is fulfilled by others, audibly.
- **42:12 — "the LORD blessed the latter days of Job".** The book ends on "blessed".

**Where the congregation will not hear what the plan rests on.** Three notes.
- **42:7, 8 — "you have not spoken of me what is right".** The ESV, with every English version, has "of me". **Supply it:** "The words 'of me' can equally be translated 'to me' — and the same verse uses them that way twice: the LORD had spoken these words *to* Job, and said *to* Eliphaz. Job spoke to God. His friends only spoke about him." Then read 13:3 and 13:7 alongside, which the ESV does carry.
- **42:8 — "I will accept his prayer"** (ESV; also NIV84). The Hebrew is "I will lift his face", the idiom of 13:8, 10 ("show partiality"). The link from Job's warning to God's verdict is lost in the ESV. It is optional colour; if used, say it.
- **1:11; 2:5, 9 — "curse"** against 42:12 **"blessed"**. The Accuser's euphemism (Sermon 1) is hidden, so the ending on God's blessing cannot be heard as its answer without a word from the preacher. It is optional here; Sermon 1 carries it.

## How this sermon sets up the series

It gives the congregation three things to carry into Sermons 1–9:
- the **question** (1:9);
- the **verdict** (42:7–8);
- the **two ways of speaking** (to God, or for him).

Each unit sermon can then begin from where this one ends:
- **Sermon 1** shows the question asked.
- **Sermon 2** shows the first speech to God, a lament addressed to no one that becomes a lament to God.
- **Sermons 3–7** show the two ways of speaking side by side.
- **Sermon 8** shows the answer.
- **Sermon 9** shows the verdict at full length.

**A practical suggestion.** Give out a one-page map of the book (the five movements, the three friends, the verdict), or invite the congregation to read the book alongside the series. The optional "In Brief" task in the plan could supply it.
