# Job — Aide-Mémoire

*For holding the whole in mind while working the detail. From `book-overview-job` v1.0, the eighteen runs, claim audits 1–7 and the macro-synthesis v1.1. Counts are Hebrew (WLC).*

**In one sentence.** A blameless man is stripped of everything to test whether anyone fears God חִנָּם ("for nothing", 1:9); he will neither curse God nor accept his friends' verdict that suffering proves guilt, and he demands that God answer him; YHWH answers from the storm — not with the reason, but with himself — and the man who has now seen him (42:5) is declared to have spoken **what is right** (42:7), prays for the friends who did not, and is restored. **Pulse: asked in heaven → argued on earth → answered from the storm → judged on earth; and the judgement falls on what was said.**

**Proportions are the argument.** The poem runs 3:2–42:6, **1,023 of the book's 1,070 verses**, between prose frames of 36 and 11. **The friends fill 211 of them and God says they "have not spoken of Me what is right" (42:7)**; Job is rebuked for "words without knowledge" (38:2); Elihu's 165 verses are neither rebuked nor commended. **So most of the book is spoken by people God later says were wrong — keep the speaker attached to every line you quote.** And God's answer is 129 verses of creation tour with **no explanation anywhere in it.**

**The reader knows what no character ever learns.** הַשָּׂטָן stands **14 times in 11 verses, all inside 1:6–2:7, and never again.** Nobody in the book is told about the council. The whole rhetoric depends on that gap — and so does the third standing trap.

**Its own thesis sentences.**
**1:9** הַֽחִנָּם יָרֵא אִיּוֹב אֱלֹהִים — *"Does Job fear God for nothing?"* The question the book exists to answer, and it is about **God's worth** as much as Job's virtue.
**42:7, 42:8** לֹא דִבַּרְתֶּם אֵלַי נְכוֹנָה — *"you have not spoken of me what is right."* **Exactly these two verses in the whole Hebrew Bible.** The verdict, and it falls on speech.
**28:28** יִרְאַת אֲדֹנָי הִיא חָכְמָה — the poem's last word is the character of the first verse: **סוּר מֵרָע ("turning from evil") stands at 1:1, 1:8, 2:3 and 28:28 and nowhere else in the book.**

**Carry two structural facts.** **The divine names split with the frame** — יְהוָה in the prose and the storm headings (32× in 23 verses) **and once only in the dialogue, at 12:9**, inside four words shared verbatim with Isa 41:20; the disputants say אֵל, אֱלוֹהַּ (41× of 60 in the canon) and שַׁדַּי (31× of 48). **And the setting is deliberately outside Israel** — יִשְׂרָאֵל, מֹשֶׁה, יְרוּשָׁלַםִ, צִיּוֹן all **0×**; תּוֹרָה once, in Eliphaz's mouth; בְּרִית three times, never of God and a people.

> **Versification trap.** **Hebrew 40:25–32 = English 41:1–8, and Hebrew 41:1–26 = English 41:9–34.** Swete diverges again in 39–41. Check the neighbouring verse before calling a reference in that stretch wrong.

## Five movements

**1:1–2:13 · The question asked in heaven** (prose) — integrity by the narrator's word and God's; the Accuser's question; two strippings; seven days' silence.
**3:1–27:23 · The human debate** — a curse on his birth, then three rounds. **The third breaks down: Bildad gets six verses, Zophar none.**
**28:1–28 · Where is wisdom found?** — men can mine silver; wisdom's place is God's alone.
**29:1–42:6 · Three discourses and a meeting** — then-and-now and a sworn oath ("The words of Job are ended", 31:40); Elihu, ending in a storm; **YHWH answers from the whirlwind**; Job replies twice.
**42:7–17 · The verdict given on earth** (prose) — friends rebuked, Job intercedes, the comfort, the doubling, a patriarch's death.

**The device:** a prose frame round a poetic core, marked by genre, by the accentuation (poetic throughout 3:2–42:6, **Elihu's prose preface included**) and by the divine names. **Job's soliloquies at 3 and 29–31 bracket the dialogue, with 28 between them.** Heaven-unseen → earth-argued → heaven-speaking → earth-restored.

**Three judgements tested and not fully settled.** The third cycle: **breakdown-as-design, the person shifting at 27:13.** Chapter 28's speaker: **within Job's discourse, moderate.** **Elihu: still open**, and two measured claims about him have fallen.

**Compressing?** Six sermons that keep the book: 1–2 · 3 · 19 (built on 9:33 and 16:19) · 28 · 38:1–42:6 · 42:7–17 — but it loses the friends' doctrine as a foil, so **one sermon must name it.** A single sermon goes on 1:9 with 42:7–8. **Never preach a friend's speech standalone**; hold it against 42:7.

## Eight threads

**The book is made out of its own words.** **Eight of the eighteen runs found this independently.** Job hands Eliphaz's doxology back as terror (5:9 → 9:10); Eliphaz turns Job's "What is man?" against him (7:17 → 15:14); Elihu quotes Job's charge sheet (13:24, 27 → 33:10–11); Job quotes God and accepts the charge (38:2 → 42:3; 40:7 → 42:4). **And God answers Job in Job's own grammar: 9:20 is 40:8's construction stem for stem, in Job's mouth first.**

**The lawsuit — and the word withheld.** יכח stands **17 times in 15 verses** (of 59 in the canon), the verb צדק 17 of 41, מִשְׁפָּט 23, רִיב 12. Then the absence: **40:8 is the only verse carrying מִשְׁפָּט or any צדק word in the last 147 verses** — 79 justice-free verses before, 67 after. **God withholds the courtroom vocabulary through the whole first speech and releases it in one question.** And **מִשְׁפָּטִי, the identical form, is Job's at 27:2, 29:14, 34:5, 34:6 — and God's at 40:8.**

**The direction of speech, and the verdict falls on it.** Job stops speaking *about* God to the friends and starts speaking *to* God, charging them with speaking *for* him (13); by the third round nobody speaks to anyone. **Then 42:7–8, where the preposition is אֵלַי, "to me."** *Lament addressed to God is vindicated; orthodoxy spoken about God against a sufferer is condemned.* **But the preposition argument is this project's own reading — no commentator in the library rests on it. Present it that way.**

**"Answer" demanded, denied, granted.** From Eliphaz's taunt (5:1) through 13:22 and 14:15, against Job's own unanswered cries (19:7; 30:20), to the storm: **ענה stands five times in the frame of 38:1–40:5 and never once inside the creation tour.** *God grants the hearing and withholds the explanation, and the book treats that as sufficient.*

**The mediator Job cannot find.** מוֹכִיחַ ("umpire", 9:33 — and only 32:12 and 40:2 besides) → עֵד (16:19) → ערב (17:3) → גֹּאֵל (19:25) → Elihu's mediator and כֹּפֶר (33:23–24) → **and פָּלַל stands in exactly two verses of the book, 42:8 and 42:10, both intercession for someone else.** *There is still no umpire at the end: God simply pronounces, and the man who wanted one becomes one for his accusers.* **The preaching gain is in the gap.**

**Comfort, and the only comforting that works is wordless.** נָחַם in **exactly seven verses — 2:11, 7:13, 16:2, 21:34, 29:25, 42:6, 42:11.** **נוד + נָחַם + רָעָה + בּוֹא עָלָיו is unique in the whole Hebrew Bible to 2:11 and 42:11, with the agent of the evil withheld at the first (qal) and named at the second (hiphil, "the LORD").** *Three men came from three countries with a theology and failed; relatives arrived late with bread and succeeded, **explaining nothing** — and the narrator, who comments rarely, lets that stand.*

**"For nothing", and the fear of God.** חִנָּם through four verses and four mouths — the Accuser's charge (1:9), God's admission (2:3), Job's complaint (9:17), Eliphaz's accusation (22:6). **בָּרַךְ in eight verses, four of them meaning "curse" by euphemism (1:5, 11; 2:5, 9) — and 42:12 the only one where God is subject and it means blessing.** *The Accuser said Job would "bless" God to his face; the book ends with God blessing Job.*

**The New Testament quarries the whole cast.** **1 Cor 3:19 cites Eliphaz (5:13) with γέγραπται.** **ἀνεξιχνίαστος at Rom 11:33 stands in the Greek OT only at Job 5:9, 9:10 and 34:24 — Eliphaz, Job, Elihu — and Rom 11:35 renders 41:11 [Heb 41:3], God's own words**, making Rom 11:33–35 a Joban frame round a verbatim Isa 40:13. **Phil 1:19 reproduces Swete's 13:16 exactly.** *The NT quoting Eliphaz as Scripture is the strongest corrective to a flat "the friends were wrong": their sentences are often true; their **application** was false.*

*Also: both sides quarry the same Scriptures — מָה־אֱנוֹשׁ at Ps 8:5, Job 7:17 (Job) and 15:14 (Eliphaz); שֹׁפֵךְ בּוּז עַל־נְדִיבִים at Ps 107:40 and Job 12:21 only. **The instances hold; the pattern was tested and failed**, six more of the same shape falling out of one mechanical pass. Preach it as a reading of the dialogue, not a measured design.*

## Where Christ is

**No direct messianic prophecy here — do not manufacture one.** The weight is a trajectory, a type and a contrast.

**Trajectory — the mediator**, named in the NT: *"one mediator between God and men … who gave Himself as a ransom"* (1 Tim 2:5–6). **Keep honest: the identity of the גֹּאֵל at 19:25 is contested, NASB95's capital R is an interpretive choice, and Job's words are a longing inside his lawsuit, not a prediction he understood.** Canonical, not psychological.

**Type — the servant who suffers חִנָּם and intercedes.** God calls Job עַבְדִּי twice in the prologue and **four times in the epilogue**; admits he was ruined *without cause* (2:3); he is struck on the cheek (16:10) and spat on (30:10); **and the fortunes turn "when he prayed for his friends" (42:10).** Isa 53:12's shape is close and Isaiah is not quoted — *moderate*. Job's prayer spares three men; Christ's intercession saves a people.

**Contrast — Job is righteous, not sinless.** God rebukes his words (38:2) and asks whether he will condemn God to justify himself (40:8). **His righteousness includes accusing God** — against the one who *"while being reviled, did not revile in return"* (1 Pet 2:23).

**Lower confidence:** wisdom hidden and found (28:23 → Col 2:3) · walking on the sea (9:8, 11 → Mark 6:48) · the Greek already reading resurrection into 19:26 and 42:17a · **Rom 11:33–35 as a Joban doxology — read Naselli, *From Typology to Doxology*, first.**

**The gospel ground is free grace.** The Accuser asked whether anyone fears God for nothing. The book shows a man who does and a God who is worth it; the gospel supplies what Job lacked — the mediator, the ransom, and a verdict of "right" given to the ungodly as a gift.

## The standing traps

**Preaching the friends** — they are wrong not because every sentence is false (Paul cites 5:13 as Scripture) but because they apply true general sentences as a verdict on a particular case; bites at 4–5, 8, 11, 15, 18, 20, 22, 25 **and Elihu** · **"the patience of Job"** — 3–31 are the bulk, and the commendation follows twenty-nine chapters of protest; James gives *patience* to the prophets and *endurance* to Job in consecutive verses, though hold that lightly (the nouns stand together at Col 1:11; 2 Tim 3:10) · **supplying the explanation** — "Satan has asked for you"; **Job is never told the prologue and God's answer does not tell him**; the heavenly scene is given to the reader so the reader does *not* claim to know which scene lies behind another's suffering · **dualism** — the Accuser acts only under permission (1:12; 2:6), and the narrator says the evils were those *"the LORD had brought on him"* (42:11) · **hearing the storm as a put-down** — God *answers*, the thing Job asked for and the friends said would not come, then calls him "My servant" four times; preach the content of the tour, not merely that God spoke · **the ending as reward, or as proof the friends were right** — restoration comes **after** the verdict and *"when he prayed for his friends"*; **the livestock doubles and the children do not** · **the daughters' names** (42:14–15) — the literature reads them as paternal delight; **a wry reading is not attested, so do not imply one** · **building on the cruxes as though they were plain.**

## Handle with care

**42:6 — say what the congregation will hear, then what the Hebrew more likely means.** NASB95: *"I retract, and I repent in dust and ashes."* ESV and NIV84: *"I despise myself…"* **The better reading is "and I am comforted *concerning* dust and ashes"** — וְנִחַמְתִּי עַל־X stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible and the three in Jeremiah all mean "relent concerning"; אֶמְאַס is objectless, as at 7:16 and 36:5; the book's ash-heap idiom is בְּתוֹךְ (2:8), not עַל; **and BHS records no variant, so the crux is wholly construal.** Boda reaches this in print; Pohl notes the accentuation itself marks עַל as the object of both verbs. *"I despise myself" is heard very differently from "I retract", and both differ again from "I am comforted".*

**42:7 — one variant with a doctrinal consequence, and it cannot be resolved.** BHS records *mlt Mss* reading בְּעַבְדִּי for כְּעַבְדִּי, and **Swete agrees at 42:8: κατὰ τοῦ θεράποντός μου, "**against** my servant Job".** On that reading God says nothing about Job's speech being right **and the book's central tension with 38:2 disappears.** MT preferred at *moderate* as the harder reading. **The library has nothing on it and neither BHQ nor the Göttingen *Iob* is owned — disclose it as unresolved; do not settle it from the pulpit.**

**Two places where the Bible in the pew may say the opposite.** **9:33** — NASB95 and ESV follow the MT, *"There is no umpire between us"*; **NIV84 follows the other reading, "If only there were someone to arbitrate"** — a denial or a wish. **13:15**, the most-quoted verse in the book, **rests on a qere**: all three versions read *"Though He slay me, I will hope in Him"*, while **the ketiv reads "He will slay me; I have no hope"** (NIV84 margin), and Swete reads neither.

**Suffering at the bedside.** The prologue forbids the friends' error; **it does not license a new one.** Never tell a sufferer which heavenly scene lies behind their case — the book's whole design is that nobody in it ever learns.

---

*What remains open. **Elihu's status.** **And four readings cannot be settled from the owned library**: the suspended ʿayin at 38:13, 15; the retroversion at 38:34; גַּאֲוָה or גֵּוֹה at 41:15, **where the ESV has adopted the conjecture**; and the variant at 42:7–8. Each needs BHQ, the Göttingen *Iob* or a specialist text-critical commentary. **About forty queued items at or below moderate were left untested at Finalise** — if one is later promoted above moderate, it needs an audit first.*

*One method note for the pulpit. Across seven audits this book taught the same lesson every time: **the lemma-verified chains hold and the patterns built on them do not** — in the last audit the counts were right in sixteen cases of sixteen and the designs fell in fifteen. **A congregation can be told that two words stand together in only two verses of the Bible; that is checkable and true. They should not be told that a statistical shape proves authorial intent, because in this book it has not.***
