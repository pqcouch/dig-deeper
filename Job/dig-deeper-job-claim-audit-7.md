# Job — Dig-Deeper Claim Audit 7

*The Finalise audit. Testing the claims that would enter the Job Book Overview v1.0 above moderate, together with every design claim still capped and unbaselined — including the ones this project's own macro-synthesis raised five hours earlier. Run against the WLC, Swete and SBLGNT corpus; findings cite BHS and the Logos exports. Two blind auditors, run one after the other.*

**Run:** 16 November 2026. **Mode:** Claim Audit (dig-deeper Phase 0.5b).
**Scope:** 33 items — 17 single contacts (Part A) and 16 design, field and measured claims (Part B).
**Preceded by:** claim audits 1–6, the Logos rounds of 5, 7 and 16 November, and the macro-synthesis of 16 November.

## A note on what this audit is for

Audit 6 ran nine days into the Job work and discharged the backlog of items the Sermons 8–9 digs had raised. It did not sweep up the rest — and when that was put to me plainly, I conceded it: **about sixty items in the main queue had never been audited at all**, some of them filed in early October and carrying ratings of *moderate–high* or *high* that no one had tested. Under the standing trigger rule, **limb 3 requires an audit before any Finalise pass, and the audit must sweep up everything outstanding.** Audit 6 satisfied the trigger to run an audit. It did not satisfy limb 3.

This audit does, and it does so economically, on the Lamentations audit-7 model: **it tests what would enter the overview above moderate, and leaves at *moderate, marked untested* everything that enters at or below it.** An item rated *low–moderate* in the queue cannot be raised by an audit it never had; it can only be entered honestly as untested. So the test set is not "the sixty" but the subset of them whose ratings make a claim the overview would repeat.

**To that set this audit adds a case it would have been easy to leave out.** The macro-synthesis run earlier today raised one new cross-pericope design claim of its own — chapter 28 as the book's hinge — and capped it at moderate with no baseline, under the rule that the synthesis's claims face the bar it applies to others. **It is tested here, by an auditor who does not know the project proposed it.** Audit 6 broke four design claims of exactly that shape. There is no reason to think this one is safer because it is ours.

## Method

**Two auditors, fresh instances, run sequentially** — not in parallel, per the standing cost rule (Patrick, 2 October). Each received only the corpus, a written brief, and its claims **stated as proposals to break, with the proposed rating attached** so the rating itself was under attack and not merely the claim.

**Auditor A** took the single contacts: wording, distribution, rarity, rivals, the Greek. **Auditor B** took the designs, fields and measured claims, and was barred from A's work so that a design could not be propped up by a contact A had just weakened.

**Each brief carried the accumulated operating rules, every one of them learned from a past failure:** results go to stderr, so append `2>&1`; **never pipe a search through a fixed-length `head` or `tail`**, which silently dropped three verses in audit 6 and put a wrong count into a published Headline; **check the lemma number before trusting a nil**, because a bare Strong's number can conflate homograph entries the index keeps apart; **a nil from an ordered phrase search is not an absence**, since a prefixed waw, בְּ or כְּ defeats it; **a three-way co-occurrence can fail on part of speech or stem**; **book-name prefixes differ between editions**, so a cross-edition nil may be a prefix mismatch; and **the corpus uses Hebrew versification**, where English Job 41:34 is Hebrew 41:26.

**History of Interpretation was scored "not assessable from the corpus" throughout, as expected.** The corpus holds one Hebrew manuscript tradition; it can verify wording and counts and can never report what interpreters have held or how widely an apparatus feature is attested. **No verdict below incorporates any evidence about the history of interpretation** except where the Logos rounds already supplied it and the audit says so.

**Design claims were tested with a chance baseline**, as the standing rule requires: rarity ceilings at 50, 150, 400 and 800 verses; the Dice coefficient as the primary statistic; a **per-chapter control** (hold the chapter, vary the block) and a **design null** (what share of same-length windows have their own best partner scoring as high). A rank is quoted only where it holds at two settings of the parameters.

**Five verdicts:** Confirmed · Confirmed with nuance · Needs reframing · Uncertain — flag for research · Discard. Confidence is stated separately for the *wording* and for the *use or dependence* wherever those diverge, because audits 3 to 6 established that in this book the two come apart constantly: the contacts mostly stand and the patterns mostly fall.

## Part A — single contacts above moderate (auditor A)

*Seventeen proposals, every one of them filed between early October and mid-November with a rating of moderate–high or high, and never tested. Three survived attack intact. Seven were factually wrong as stated.*

**A1 — #8. גאל at 3:5 planting the Redeemer of 19:25. Confirmed with nuance.** גאל stands in **exactly two verses of Job, 3:5 and 19:25**, and the WLC files **both under the same homograph entry, "1350 a"** — the redemption root. The *defile* root is numbered separately as **1351** and occurs in nine verses, **none of them in Job**, so the corpus's morphology does not support reading 3:5 as "defile". BHS offers **no variant on יִגְאָלֻהוּ**. The rarity holds at both ends, which is unusual in this set. **But the earliest reader we can observe split them: the Greek has ἐκλάβοι at 3:5 and ὁ ἐκλύειν με μέλλων at 19:25 — different verbs, different roots.** And the index assignment is one morphology project's judgement on a disputed form; whether the lexica divide גאל I from גאל II here is not assessable from the corpus. *Lemma identity high; the sense at 3:5 moderate; the planting design **low–moderate**, down from the proposed moderate.*

**A2 — #18. Isa 49:4 behind Job 9:29. Needs reframing — and my own rescue of it was wrong.** I had broken the claim as filed (יגע + רִיק returns Isa 49:4, Isa 65:23, Jer 51:58, Hab 2:13, and **Job 9:29 is not among them**) and then rescued it on a better pair: יגע + הֶבֶל in **exactly two verses, Isa 49:4 and Job 9:29**. **The auditor broke the rescue.** In Isa 49:4 **הֶבֶל is not the complement of יגע**: colon A is לְרִיק יָגַעְתִּי, colon B is לְתֹהוּ וְהֶבֶל כֹּחִי כִלֵּיתִי. **The two-lemma co-occurrence is an artefact of bicolon parallelism, not a shared idiom.** And Job 9:29's הֶבֶל belongs to Job's own formula: **לָמָּה־זֶּה הֶבֶל + verb stands at Job 9:29 and Job 27:12 and nowhere else.** The Greek of Job 9:29 does not render labour at all (διὰ τί οὐκ ἀπέθανον). *Wording low–moderate; use **low**. If the overview wants a Servant link at Job 9, it must come from elsewhere.*

**A3 — #25. Isa 11:1 behind Job 14:7–9. Needs reframing; the Christological colour goes.** The data claim is right — **גֶּזַע stands in three verses (Isa 11:1, Isa 40:24, Job 14:8) and the pair גֶּזַע + שֹׁרֶשׁ in exactly two (Isa 11:1, Job 14:8)**. But four things break the dependence. **Isa 40:24, the third גֶּזַע verse the proposal omits, shares three elements with Job 14:8 where Isa 11:1 shares two** — שׁרשׁ, גֶּזַע *and* בָּאָרֶץ; the pair-exclusivity survives only because Isa 40:24 has שׁרשׁ as a **verb**, a different lemma. **Job's own tree is the better partner: שֹׁרֶשׁ + מַיִם + קָצִיר within two verses occurs in exactly two places in the canon and both are in Job — 14:8–9 and 29:19**, where all three stand in one verse. The two passages share **no verb and no sprout-word**: Isaiah has חֹטֶר, נֵצֶר, יצא, פרה; Job has כרת, חלף, יֹנֶקֶת, חדל, זקן, מות, פרח, נֶטַע. And **Isa 6:13 shows the felling-and-remnant motif inside Isaiah with wholly different vocabulary**. Two further objections to the Christological reading: **Job 14:10–12 concludes that man, unlike the tree, does *not* rise — the image is deployed against consolation** — and **the Greek flattens the pair, rendering both Isaianic nouns ῥίζα.** *Noun pair moderate–high, moderate once Isa 40:24 is admitted; design **low**.*

**A4 — #27. Jer 51 behind Job 14:12. DISCARD — and this one corrects me.** I had reported that the three-verse phrase claim "holds, with a spelling note". **The auditor established what the spelling is, and it destroys the claim.** Jeremiah reads **וְלֹא יָקִיצוּ at both 51:39 and 51:57 — the waw sits on the negative.** Job 14:12 reads a **bare לֹא יָקִיצוּ**, its waws on the flanking clauses. **So the plain pass was right and the three-verse claim is false: וְלֹא יָקִיצוּ stands at Jer 51:39 and 51:57 only, and what Job shares is two words that are not a distinctive formula.** קיץ stands in 22 verses, Job 14:12 its only occurrence in the book. Jeremiah's markers (שׁכר, שְׁנַת־עוֹלָם, נְאֻם יְהוָה) are absent from Job and Job's (שׁכב, קום, עַד־בִּלְתִּי שָׁמַיִם, עור) from Jeremiah; thematic coherence between a drunken-stupor oracle on Babylon's officials and a lament on mortality is nil. **And Jer 31:26 shares more with Job 14:12 (קיץ + שֵׁנָה) than the Babylon oracle's distinctive שְׁנַת־עוֹלָם does.** *Discarded as a Jeremiah-dependence claim. What is worth keeping is a reframe: **Job 14:12 denies in the hiphil what Dan 12:2 and Isa 26:19 affirm in the same stem.***

**A5 — #28. Isa 19:5 behind Job 14:11. Confirmed with nuance — and stronger than claimed.** The parallel is **the whole bicolon**, not one clause: Isa 19:5 וְנִשְּׁתוּ־מַיִם מֵהַיָּם ‖ וְנָהָר יֶחֱרַב וְיָבֵשׁ against Job 14:11 אָזְלוּ־מַיִם מִנִּי־יָם ‖ **וְנָהָר יֶחֱרַב וְיָבֵשׁ**, identical but for the first verb and the preposition's form. **And the three rivals do not reach it: Isa 42:15, Isa 44:27 and Nah 1:4 are all hiphil, all with YHWH as agent, none with the מַיִם/יָם colon.** The motif is Isaianic and wider than the proposal allows, but **the intransitive qal pairing of חרב and יבשׁ with a river is not a commonplace — it is these two verses.** *Wording **high**; dependence low–moderate, direction unassessable from two verses.*

> **A corpus defect found here, and it should be recorded in the texts README.** The **WLC index transposes the two verbs of Isa 19:5**, filing וְנִשְּׁתוּ under 3001 (יבשׁ) and וְיָבֵשׁ under 5405 (נשׁת) — 5405 stands in only three verses (Isa 19:5, Isa 41:17, Jer 51:30), which confirms the swap. **Any lemma search touching Isa 19:5 for either root will be right only by accident.** The phrase match is independent of the index and stands. Whether the same fault occurs elsewhere in Isaiah is unchecked.

**A6 — #29. Lev 26:36 behind Job 13:25. Confirmed with nuance; the covenant-curse reading downgraded.** עָלֶה + נדף co-occur in **exactly two verses, Lev 26:36 and Job 13:25**, both carrying the **identical niphal participle נִדָּף**; adding רדף leaves the same two. **But the proposal misses a serious rival, and it is in the same verse of Job.** Isa 41:2 has **כְּקַשׁ נִדָּף** — the same participle — and 41:3 has יִרְדְּפֵם; Job 13:25's second colon is וְאֶת־**קַשׁ** יָבֵשׁ **תִּרְדֹּף**. **נדף + קַשׁ co-occurs in exactly two verses: Isa 41:2 and Job 13:25.** So **Job 13:25 holds two stock similes and has one unique contact in each direction** — the Leviticus statistic is exactly right about the leaf half and silent about the stubble half. **Second objection: the roles invert.** In Lev 26:36 the leaf's *sound* pursues; in Job 13:25 God pursues and the leaf is pursued. *Wording moderate–high; use low–moderate.*

**A7 — #40. Jer 49:7 / Obad 8–9 and Eliphaz the Temanite. Needs reframing, and relocated to the Greek.** **The two ends of the pairing use different lemmas and no verse holds both.** תֵּימָן stands in 11 verses (Gen 36:11, 36:15, 36:42, Jer 49:7, 49:20, Ezek 25:13, Amos 1:12, Obad 1:9, Hab 3:3, 1 Chr 1:36, 1:53) — **none in Job**. תֵּימָנִי stands in 8 (Gen 36:34, Job 2:11, 4:1, 15:1, 22:1, 42:7, 42:9, 1 Chr 1:45) — **none in Jeremiah, Obadiah or any judgement oracle**. **A correction to the proposal's own text: תֵּימָן does not appear in Obad 1:8 at all** — there wisdom is destroyed from Edom and Mount Esau, and at 1:9 it is Teman's *warriors* who are shattered. **Only Jer 49:7 actually joins חָכְמָה to בְּתֵימָן.** There is no verbal contact with Job 42:7, and **Job never names אֱדוֹם or עֵשָׂו at all.** The genealogy runs the wrong way: Gen 36:11 makes Teman the *son* of Eliphaz. **But the Greek supplies the identification explicitly, and that is a real finding: Swete Job 2:11 reads Ἐλειφὰζ ὁ Θαιμανῶν βασιλεύς, and the LXX colophon at Job 42:17 places Job in Ausitis "on the borders of Idumea and Arabia", names him Ἰωβάβ son of Ζάρε "of the sons of Esau", reproduces Genesis 36's Edomite king-list, and calls the friend Ἐλειφὰς τῶν Ἠσαὺ υἱῶν, Θαιμανῶν βασιλεύς.** *Hebrew-level irony **low**; **LXX reception moderate** — a different claim, and it belongs in a different row.*

**A8 — #43. Deut 32:39 behind Job 10:7. Confirmed with nuance, with the warrant relocated — and this is the most instructive result in Part A.** The formula is identical at **Deut 32:39 and Isa 43:13**, and **Job 10:7's second-person וְאֵין מִיָּדְךָ מַצִּיל is unique in the Hebrew Bible**; the wider formula אֵין + יָד + נצל stands in seven verses. **Ruling on the wording: Deuteronomy cannot be singled out over Isa 43:13 — nothing in Job 10:7's words discriminates between them.** **But the proposal's second leg does discriminate, and the corpus settles it: מחץ + רפא co-occur in exactly two verses of the whole canon — Deut 32:39 (מָחַצְתִּי וַאֲנִי אֶרְפָּא) and Job 5:18 (יִמְחָץ ... תִּרְפֶּינָה) — and Isa 43:13 has neither root.** So **Eliphaz at 5:18 is demonstrably using Deut 32:39**, and the route to Deuteronomy for 10:7 is **contextual, not lexical**. *Wording **high** that Job turns a formula standing in both places; **moderate** that Deuteronomy is the verse in view on wording alone; **use moderate–high**, carried entirely by the מחץ+רפא uniqueness at 5:18.*

**A9 — #46. Jer 13:16 behind Job 3:9. Confirmed with nuance, against a live rival.** The four lemmas are **חשׁך, נֶשֶׁף, קוה, אוֹר**, and the first three co-occur in **exactly two verses, Jer 13:16 and Job 3:9**; adding אוֹר does not change the list. **Both have קוה + לְאוֹר in that order and in the piel — a genuine idiom match, not a coincidence of verse boundaries.** Job's חשׁך is qal against Jeremiah's hiphil. **But Isa 59:9–10 is a live rival the proposal does not name: Isa 59:9 has נְקַוֶּה לָאוֹר וְהִנֵּה־חֹשֶׁךְ and Isa 59:10 has נֶשֶׁף — three of the four lemmas plus the fourth one verse away, with a closer sense, since Job 3:9 is disappointment and Jer 13:16 an exhortation.** Job 30:26 is Job's own hope-and-disappointment formula. **And צַלְמָוֶת is worthless as corroboration: it stands in 17 verses, ten of them in Job, and it is not in 3:9 but two verses earlier at 3:5.** *Wording moderate–high; use low–moderate. The row must name Isa 59:9–10, and the צַלְמָוֶת element must be dropped.*

**A10 — #84. Ps 78:49 behind Job 20:23. Needs reframing; the exactness claim is false.** **יְשַׁלַּח־בּוֹ חֲרוֹן אַפּוֹ stands at Job 20:23 only — Ps 78:49 reads יְשַׁלַּח־בָּם**, 3mp against Job's 3ms. **חֲרוֹן אַפּוֹ stands in 13 verses and חרון + אף in one verse in 34** — a commonplace by any measure. שׁלח + חרון + אף stands in three verses, and **Jer 49:37 is the third, unmentioned by the proposal.** There is **no second point of contact between Job 20 and Psalm 78**; Ps 78 does use מטר, but of manna and quail. *Wording low–moderate; use **low**. What survives is narrower than claimed.*

**A11 — #85. 2 Kgs 1:12 behind Job 1:16. Needs reframing, three ways.** The plain-pass exclusivity is real — **אֵש אֱלֹהִים at 2 Kgs 1:12 and Job 1:16 only** — and the skeletal pass's six extra verses are all **אִישׁ אֱלֹהִים, "man of God"**, a false-hit cluster from the dropped yod. **Then it breaks.** (i) **The construct is a free variant inside its own pericope: 2 Kgs 1:10 and 1:14 have bare אֵשׁ**, so the marked form stands once in a scene that repeats the formula three times. (ii) **It is textually contested and the Greek is against it.** BHS at 2 Kgs 1:12 reads *"pc Mss א׳ ut 10 cf 𝔊"*, and **Swete has bare πῦρ at 4 Reigns 1:12 and bare Πῦρ at Job 1:16** — the single word creating the exclusivity is absent from both ends in Greek. (iii) **Job 1:16 is a composite of the whole divine-fire type-scene and 2 Kgs 1:12 supplies one element of five.** אֵשׁ + **נפל** + אכל stands in five verses including **1 Kgs 18:38**, which matches Job's verb of motion where **Kings has "came down"**; אֵשׁ + **בער** + אכל in ten including **Num 11:1**, which matches Job's second and third verbs exactly. The only set 2 Kgs 1 wins, אֵשׁ + שָׁמַיִם + אכל, it wins **as a chapter, not a verse**. *Construct moderate; "that verse specifically" **low**; use low–moderate.*

**A12 — #87. Ps 102:5–6 behind Job 3:24. Needs reframing — broken independently by the auditor and by me.** **אֲנָחָה + לֶחֶם in one verse stands at Job 3:24 only, in the whole canon.** In Psalm 102 **לַחְמִי is at v.5 and אַנְחָתִי at v.6 — two different verses.** The stated exclusivity is self-contradictory: there is no verse in the Psalter holding both. A motif parallel across two psalm verses is a different and much smaller thing. **And better rivals exist for each colon:** Ps 42:4 (דִמְעָתִי לֶחֶם) and Ps 80:6 (לֶחֶם דִּמְעָה) for the bread-and-grief motif, neither using אֲנָחָה; and **Ps 22:2 for Job 3:24's second colon**, שְׁאָגָה standing in Job at 3:24 and 4:10 and in Ps 22:2. *Wording **low**; use **low**. Psalm 102's moderate standing elsewhere as a Job source must not be lent to this row.*

**A13 — #103. Proverbs 8 at Job 26:10 and 28:26–27. The two halves must be rated apart, and the gap is wide. This one corrects me too.** I had broken the first half on the lexeme: **the verb חוג occurs once in the canon (Job 26:10) and the noun חוּג in three (Isa 40:22, Job 22:14, Prov 8:27)**, so Job 26:10 and Prov 8:27 share no lexeme. **The auditor showed that the inversion is the finding, not the flaw.** Job 26:10 has **חֹק** (noun) + **חָג** (verb) + עַל־פְּנֵי + מָיִם; Prov 8:27 has **בְּחוּקוֹ** (verb) + **חוּג** (noun) + עַל־פְּנֵי + תְהוֹם. **The same two root-families stand in both verses with the parts of speech inverted, followed by the identical prepositional phrase and a water-word — and outside these two verses no verse pairs a חוג-word with עַל־פְּנֵי.** Isa 40:22 and Job 22:14 are the circle of a different element, with neither a חקק root nor עַל־פְּנֵי. **A13a: Confirmed, wording high, use moderate.**

**The second half fails.** Job 28:26 and Prov 8:29 share **only חֹק, which stands in 124 verses**; the verbs differ, the elements differ, the state of חֹק differs. **Three rivals beat Job 28:26: ב + infinitive is Prov 8:27–29's own device six times over; Job 28:25 is the same template one verse earlier with a different noun; and חֹק + יָם co-occurs in exactly two verses — Jer 5:22 and Prov 8:29 — where Jer 5:22 shares with Prov 8:29 the verb שׂים, לַיָּם, חֹק *and* the non-transgression clause**, with Ps 148:6 a third member. **The sea-bound template belongs to a cluster from which Job 28:26 is absent.** *A13b: Needs reframing, low/low.* **Recommendation: carry the 26:10 row; drop the 28:26 row, or carry them at visibly different weights — a single rating across both misreports the stronger half as well as the weaker.**

**A14 — #156. The Greek of Job 29:12 and Psa 71:12. Needs reframing — and here the auditor overturned a finding of mine that I had thought the best of the day.** The overlap is substantial: **eight shared word-forms in order, the first four a contiguous identical run — πτωχὸν ἐκ χειρὸς δυνάστου.** I had noted that **ἐκ χειρὸς δυνάστου renders nothing in either Hebrew verse** and concluded that a shared, unmotivated Greek plus was strong evidence of reception, worth an upgrade to high. **Two breaks.** (i) **ἐκ χειρὸς δυνάστου stands in three Swete verses, not two: Psa 71:12, Job 29:12 and Job 5:15 — and at Job 5:15 it is a literal rendering of the Hebrew וּמִיַּד חָזָק.** So the phrase is already native to Job's translator from a verse where the Hebrew demanded it, and its reappearance at 29:12 needs no psalm. (ii) **The Hebrew lines are themselves near-twins** — same כִּי + deliverance verb + poor-noun + מְשַׁוֵּעַ + waw + second noun + negative + עֹזֵר לוֹ — **so most of the Greek agreement is the mechanical consequence of that**, which the proposal frames as a Greek-only phenomenon. **What genuinely survives is narrower and still interesting: in both verses the phrase stands where the Hebrew has מְשַׁוֵּעַ, which it does not translate** — two books' translators making the same non-literal substitution on the same rare participle in the same clause-type. Whether that is one translator reading another, a common rendering tradition or a *Vorlage* difference, the corpus cannot say. *Greek overlap high; the reception claim **low–moderate**. **All Greek here is Swete; Rahlfs is the citation of record and was not checked.***

**A15 — #183. Ezekiel 14 behind Job 42:11. It is not a three-contact cluster. Needs reframing as a whole, with one element discarded and one confirmed high.**

*Element 1 — the comfort construction. Needs reframing.* The correct list: **נחם + רָעָה + בּוֹא in one verse stands in four verses — Ezek 14:22, Job 2:11, Job 42:11, Lam 1:21** — so "only these two" is wrong at the lemma level, and **Lam 1:21 is a real distractor**. **The break is the stem: Ezek 14:22's נִחַמְתֶּם is niphal, Job 42:11's וַיְנַחֲמוּ and Job 2:11's לְנַחֲמוֹ are piel** — different stem, subject-type and sense at the one verb the claim turns on. **The surviving point, which the proposal deserves credit for: on בּוֹא, Job 42:11 agrees with Ezek 14:22 and not with Job 2:11** — both have the **hiphil with a named personal agent** (הֵבֵאתִי / הֵבִיא יְהוָה) where 2:11 has the agentless qal participle. **Exclusivity holds only at the full construction with בּוֹא's stem specified and נחם's unspecified**, and the row must say so or it overclaims. *Wording moderate; use low–moderate.*

*Element 2 — חִנָּם with the comfort root. **Discard.*** **חִנָּם + נחם in one verse stands at Ezek 14:23 only, in the whole canon.** In Job the two **never share a verse**: חִנָּם stands at 1:9, 2:3, 9:17, 22:6 with no נחם near any of them. *A book-level coincidence of two moderately common words; the proposal's "low" was generous.*

*Element 3 — "these three men". **Confirmed**, and undersold.* **The ordered phrase שְׁלֹשֶׁת הָאֲנָשִׁים הָאֵלֶּה stands in exactly four verses in the canon — Ezek 14:14, 14:16, 14:18 and Job 32:1** — and **Ezek 14:14 names אִיּוֹב in the very same verse as the phrase.** The lemma triple adds only 2 Sam 23:17 and 1 Chr 11:19, which are David's Three. *Wording **high**; use **moderate–high**. Availability, volume and recurrence all at once, on a phrase found nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible.*

**The cluster may enter the overview only as described above** — one strong contact, one partial, one deleted. A single moderate–high on "the cluster" would publish the dead element under the live one's rating.

**A16 — #194. גִּיחַ across the two divine speeches. - **נוד + רָעָה: exactly two verses of the Hebrew Bible**, Job 2:11 and 42:11
- **נוד + נָחַם + רָעָה: exactly two**, the same pair

### B16 — Chapter 28 as the book's hinge. DISCARD as a design.

*This was the macro-synthesis's own new claim, raised hours before the audit and capped at moderate with no baseline. It was given to an auditor who did not know whose it was, with instructions to test it hardest. It failed every test.*

**All three chains re-verified with homograph checks.** חָכְמָה + בִּינָה: exactly five Job verses (28:12, 28:20, 28:28, 38:36, 39:17) and sixteen canon-wide; **both lemmas have a single index entry, so the 998/8394 trap does not arise here.** שַׁחַץ: exactly Job 28:8 and 41:26, both בְּנֵי־שָׁחַץ. שֵׁמַע: exactly Job 28:22 and 42:5, **both under sub-entry "8088 b"**, so the pair is not a homograph artefact.

**Test A — chapter 28 fixed, partner varied over chapters.** Chapter **38 ranks 1st of 41 at all four ceilings** — a robust result. **But chapter 39 ranks 34th, 34th, 40th and 37th of 41, and chapter 40 ranks 15th to 32nd.**

**Test A2 — chapter 28 fixed, partner varied over 129-verse windows.** Windows scoring at or above the claimed pair: **23 %, 26 %, 33 %, 40 %.** The best window is **never** the divine speeches.

**Test B — divine speeches fixed, block varied.** Chapter 28 ranks **183rd, 176th, 183rd and 232nd of 897** windows, and **4th to 8th of 38 chapters — beaten at every ceiling by chapters 31, 9 and 3.**

**Test C — the design null.** Of 261 twenty-eight-verse windows, the share whose own best 129-verse partner scores at or above the claimed pair: **99 %, 98 %, 98 %, 98 %.**

**Test D — the chain shape.** Six lemmas have their Job occurrences wholly inside chapter 28 ∪ chapters 38–41 while touching both. **The control: chapter 30 has eight, chapters 31, 9 and 3 have six each. Chapter 28 ranks 2nd of 38 on the raw count and is not first per verse.**

**Three further findings cut against "hinge" specifically.**

1. **The one robust result is about Job 38, not the divine speeches.** A claim about "the two divine speeches" built on a statistic that holds for one of their four chapters and is **near-worst for another** is the exact failure the brief warned of.
2. **Dice(chapter 28, epilogue) is 0.0000 at both rare ceilings.** **The epilogue is chapter 28's worst partner, not an answering voice.**
3. **No chain spans two of the three claimed destinations, and one destination is misidentified by me.** חָכְמָה+בִּינָה reaches only the *first* speech; שַׁחַץ only the *second*; **שֵׁמַע reaches 42:5 — which stands in Job's closing reply at 42:1–6, not the epilogue at 42:7–17.** So the "four answers" are three single contacts landing in three different places, each once.

**And chain 1 is a within-book distributional claim, not a rare-wording claim.** Both are common wisdom nouns and their pairing is the standard wisdom-literature collocation, **seven of its sixteen canon verses falling in Proverbs.** That it occurs in Job only in chapter 28 and the first divine speech is a fact about where Job talks about wisdom.

**What the three chains do support, at their true strength:**

- **שַׁחַץ (28:8 / 41:26) is a genuine two-verse canon-exclusive contact and the strongest of the three** — a noun found nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible, in the same bound phrase, **denying at 28:8 what is asserted at 41:26: the proud beasts have not trodden the miner's path; Leviathan is king over them.** *That single contact is worth more than the hinge.*

## Gate lessons from audit 7

Four, and two are new.

**(i) A one-line queue summary loses the lexeme, and a summary naming the wrong word can make a sound finding look broken.** This happened three times today in opposite directions: **#18's summary named רִיק where Job has הֶבֶל** (and the better pair turned out to be an artefact anyway); **#103's summary named a shared word where the finding is a part-of-speech inversion**; and **#206's summary named two verses where the corpus has three, the omitted one being the strongest.** *New rule: a queue entry for a wording claim must carry the lemma numbers, not an English gloss.*

**(ii) A skeletal-pass hit may be a different word, and the forms must be inspected.** **אֵש אֱלֹהִים returns six extra verses on the skeletal pass and every one of them is אִישׁ אֱלֹהִים, "man of God"** — the yod drops and the two collapse. *This is the converse of the standing rule that a plain-pass nil is not an absence: a skeletal-pass hit is not a presence.*

**(iii) The cross-edition prefix trap fired for the second time in two audits.** The Swete files label Psalms **"Psa"** where the Hebrew labels it "Ps"; my first search for Psa 71:12 returned nothing and looked like a refutation of #156. Audit 6 had the same thing with "Eze" and "Ezek". *The prefix list belongs in the texts README.*

**(iv) Check the lemma number before trusting a nil — and the trap now has a worked example in both directions.** I searched **8394 (תְּבוּנָה)** for a claim about **998 (בִּינָה)** and briefly concluded a sound five-verse chain was fiction.
