## The Passage

*Text: BHS as exported from Logos, with the NASB95 beneath each verse. ᵃ ᵇ ᶜ mark BHS apparatus notes (see Textual Variants). Leningrad, as BHS prints it, has a petuḥah after 8:22 and after 10:22 and no marker between 9:35 and 10:1: the whole of 9:1–10:22 is one Masoretic paragraph.*

**9:1** וַיַּ֥עַן אִיֹּ֗וב וַיֹּאמַֽר׃  
Then Job answered,

**9:2** אָ֭מְנָם יָדַ֣עְתִּי כִי־כֵ֑ן וּמַה־יִּצְדַּ֖ק אֱנֹ֣ושׁ עִם־אֵֽל׃  
“In truth I know that this is so; But how can a man be in the right before God?

**9:3** אִם־יַ֭חְפֹּץ לָרִ֣יב עִמֹּ֑ו לֹֽא־יַ֝עֲנֶ֗נּוּ אַחַ֥ת מִנִּי־אָֽלֶף׃  
“If one wished to dispute with Him, He could not answer Him once in a thousand times.

**9:4** חֲכַ֣ם לֵ֭בָב וְאַמִּ֣יץ כֹּ֑חַ מִֽי־הִקְשָׁ֥ה אֵ֝לָ֗יו וַיִּשְׁלָֽם׃  
“Wise in heart and mighty in strength, Who has defied Him without harm?

**9:5** הַמַּעְתִּ֣יק הָ֭רִים וְלֹ֣א יָדָ֑עוּ אֲשֶׁ֖ר הֲפָכָ֣ם בְּאַפֹּֽו׃  
“It is God who removes the mountains, they know not how, When He overturns them in His anger;

**9:6** הַמַּרְגִּ֣יז אֶ֭רֶץ מִמְּקֹומָ֑הּ וְ֝עַמּוּדֶ֗יהָ יִתְפַלָּצֽוּןᵃ׃  
Who shakes the earth out of its place, And its pillars tremble;

**9:7** הָאֹמֵ֣ר לַ֭חֶרֶס וְלֹ֣א יִזְרָ֑ח וּבְעַ֖ד כֹּוכָבִ֣ים יַחְתֹּֽם׃  
Who commands the sun not to shine, And sets a seal upon the stars;

**9:8** נֹטֶ֣ה שָׁמַ֣יִם לְבַדֹּ֑ו וְ֝דֹורֵ֗ךְ עַל־בָּ֥מֳתֵי יָֽםᵃ׃  
Who alone stretches out the heavens And tramples down the waves of the sea;

**9:9** עֹֽשֶׂה־עָ֭שׁᵃ כְּסִ֥יל וְכִימָ֗ה וְחַדְרֵ֥י תֵמָֽן׃  
Who makes the Bear, Orion and the Pleiades, And the chambers of the south;

**9:10** עֹשֶׂ֣ה גְ֭דֹלֹות עַד־אֵ֣ין חֵ֑קֶר וְנִפְלָאֹ֗ות עַד־אֵ֥ין מִסְפָּֽר׃  
Who does great things, unfathomable, And wondrous works without number.

**9:11** הֵ֤ן יַעֲבֹ֣ר עָ֭לַי וְלֹ֣א אֶרְאֶ֑ה וְ֝יַחֲלֹ֗ףᵃ וְֽלֹא־אָבִ֥ין לֹֽו׃  
“Were He to pass by me, I would not see Him; Were He to move past me, I would not perceive Him.

**9:12** הֵ֣ן יַ֭חְתֹּףᵃ מִ֣י יְשִׁיבֶ֑נּוּ מִֽי־יֹאמַ֥ר אֵ֝לָ֗יו מַֽה־תַּעֲשֶֽׂה׃  
“Were He to snatch away, who could restrain Him? Who could say to Him, ‘What are You doing?’

**9:13** אֱ֭לֹוהַּ לֹא־יָשִׁ֣יב אַפֹּ֑ו תַּחְתָּ֥ו שָׁ֝חֲח֗וּ עֹ֣זְרֵי רָֽהַב׃  
“God will not turn back His anger; Beneath Him crouch the helpers of Rahab.

**9:14** אַ֭ף כִּֽי־אָנֹכִ֣י אֶֽעֱנֶ֑נּוּ אֶבְחֲרָ֖ה דְבָרַ֣י עִמֹּֽו׃  
“How then can I answer Him, And choose my words before Him?

**9:15** אֲשֶׁ֣ר אִם־צָ֭דַקְתִּי לֹ֣א אֶעֱנֶ֑ה לִ֝מְשֹׁפְטִ֗י אֶתְחַנָּֽן׃  
“For though I were right, I could not answer; I would have to implore the mercy of my judge.

**9:16** אִם־קָרָ֥אתִי וַֽיַּעֲנֵ֑נִיᵃ לֹֽא־אַ֝אֲמִ֗ין כִּֽי־יַאֲזִ֥ין קֹולִֽי׃  
“If I called and He answered me, I could not believe that He was listening to my voice.

**9:17** אֲשֶׁר־בִּשְׂעָרָ֥הᵃ יְשׁוּפֵ֑נִי וְהִרְבָּ֖ה פְצָעַ֣י חִנָּֽם׃  
“For He bruises me with a tempest And multiplies my wounds without cause.

**9:18** לֹֽא־יִ֭תְּנֵנִי הָשֵׁ֣ב רוּחִ֑י כִּ֥י יַ֝שְׂבִּעַ֗נִי מַמְּרֹרִֽים׃  
“He will not allow me to get my breath, But saturates me with bitterness.

**9:19** אִם־לְכֹ֣חַ אַמִּ֣יץ הִנֵּ֑ה וְאִם־לְ֝מִשְׁפָּ֗ט מִ֣י יֹועִידֵֽנִי׃  
“If it is a matter of power, behold, He is the strong one! And if it is a matter of justice, who can summon Him?

**9:20** אִם־אֶ֭צְדָּק פִּ֣י יַרְשִׁיעֵ֑נִי תָּֽם־אָ֝֗נִי וַֽיַּעְקְשֵֽׁנִי׃  
“Though I am righteous, my mouth will condemn me; Though I am guiltless, He will declare me guilty.

**9:21** תָּֽם־אָ֭נִי לֹֽא־אֵדַ֥ע נַפְשִׁ֗י אֶמְאַ֥ס חַיָּֽי׃  
“I am guiltless; I do not take notice of myself; I despise my life.

**9:22** אַחַ֗ת הִ֥יא עַל־כֵּ֥ן אָמַ֑רְתִּי תָּ֥ם וְ֝רָשָׁ֗ע ה֣וּא מְכַלֶּֽה׃  
“It is all one; therefore I say, ‘He destroys the guiltless and the wicked.’

**9:23** אִם־שֹׁ֭וט יָמִ֣ית פִּתְאֹ֑ם לְמַסַּ֖ת נְקִיִּ֣ם יִלְעָֽג׃  
“If the scourge kills suddenly, He mocks the despair of the innocent.

**9:24** אֶ֤רֶץ׀ נִתְּנָ֬ה בְֽיַד־רָשָׁ֗ע פְּנֵֽי־שֹׁפְטֶ֥יהָ יְכַסֶּ֑ה אִם־לֹ֖א אֵפֹ֣וא מִי־הֽוּא׃  
“The earth is given into the hand of the wicked; He covers the faces of its judges. If it is not He, then who is it?

**9:25** וְיָמַ֣יᵃ קַ֭לּוּ מִנִּי־רָ֑ץ בָּֽ֝רְח֗וּ לֹא־רָא֥וּ טֹובָֽה׃  
“Now my days are swifter than a runner; They flee away, they see no good.

**9:26** חָ֭לְפוּ עִם־אֳנִיֹּ֣ות אֵבֶ֑ה כְּ֝נֶ֗שֶׁר יָט֥וּשׂ עֲלֵי־אֹֽכֶל׃  
“They slip by like reed boats, Like an eagle that swoops on its prey.

**9:27** אִם־אָ֭מְרִיᵃ אֶשְׁכְּחָ֣ה שִׂיחִ֑י אֶעֶזְבָ֖ה פָנַ֣י וְאַבְלִֽיגָה׃  
“Though I say, ‘I will forget my complaint, I will leave off my sad countenance and be cheerful,’

**9:28** יָגֹ֥רְתִּי כָל־עַצְּבֹתָ֑י יָ֝דַ֗עְתִּי כִּי־לֹ֥א תְנַקֵּֽנִי׃  
I am afraid of all my pains, I know that You will not acquit me.

**9:29** אָנֹכִ֥יᵃ אֶרְשָׁ֑ע לָמָּה־זֶּ֝֗ה הֶ֣בֶל אִיגָֽע׃  
“I am accounted wicked, Why then should I toil in vain?

**9:30** אִם־הִתְרָחַ֥צְתִּי בְמֵו־ᵃ שָׁ֑לֶג וַ֝הֲזִכֹּ֗ותִי בְּבֹ֣ר כַּפָּֽי׃  
“If I should wash myself with snow And cleanse my hands with lye,

**9:31** אָ֭ז בַּשַּׁ֣חַתᵃ תִּטְבְּלֵ֑נִי וְ֝תִֽעֲב֗וּנִי שַׂלְמֹותָֽי׃  
Yet You would plunge me into the pit, And my own clothes would abhor me.

**9:32** כִּי־לֹא־אִ֣ישׁ כָּמֹ֣נִי אֶֽעֱנֶ֑נּוּ נָבֹ֥וא יַ֝חְדָּ֗ו בַּמִּשְׁפָּֽט׃  
“For He is not a man as I am that I may answer Him, That we may go to court together.

**9:33** לֹ֣אᵃ יֵשׁ־בֵּינֵ֣ינוּ מֹוכִ֑יחַ יָשֵׁ֖ת יָדֹ֣ו עַל־שְׁנֵֽינוּ׃  
“There is no umpire between us, Who may lay his hand upon us both.

**9:34** יָסֵ֣ר מֵעָלַ֣י שִׁבְטֹ֑ו וְ֝אֵמָתֹ֗ו אַֽל־תְּבַעֲתַֽנִּי׃  
“Let Him remove His rod from me, And let not dread of Him terrify me.

**9:35** אַֽ֭דַבְּרָהᵃ וְלֹ֣א אִירָאֶ֑נּוּ כִּ֥י לֹא־כֵ֥ן אָ֝נֹכִ֗י עִמָּדִֽי׃  
“Then I would speak and not fear Him; But I am not like that in myself.

**10:1** נָֽקְטָ֥הᵃ נַפְשִׁ֗י בְּחַ֫יָּ֥י אֶֽעֶזְבָ֣ה עָלַ֣י שִׂיחִ֑י אֲ֝דַבְּרָה֗ᵇ בְּמַ֣ר נַפְשִֽׁי׃  
“I loathe my own life; I will give full vent to my complaint; I will speak in the bitterness of my soul.

**10:2** אֹמַ֣ר אֶל־אֱ֭לֹוהַּ אַל־תַּרְשִׁיעֵ֑נִי הֹֽ֝ודִיעֵ֗נִי עַ֣ל מַה־תְּרִיבֵֽנִי׃  
“I will say to God, ‘Do not condemn me; Let me know why You contend with me.

**10:3** הֲטֹ֤וב לְךָ֨׀ כִּֽי־תַעֲשֹׁ֗ק כִּֽי־תִ֭מְאַס יְגִ֣יעַ כַּפֶּ֑יךָ וְעַל־עֲצַ֖ת רְשָׁעִ֣ים הֹופָֽעְתָּ׃  
‘Is it right for You indeed to oppress, To reject the labor of Your hands, And to look favorably on the schemes of the wicked?

**10:4** הַעֵינֵ֣י בָשָׂ֣ר לָ֑ךְ אִם־כִּרְאֹ֖ות אֱנֹ֣ושׁ תִּרְאֶֽה׃  
‘Have You eyes of flesh? Or do You see as a man sees?

**10:5** הֲכִימֵ֣י אֱנֹ֣ושׁ יָמֶ֑יךָ אִם־שְׁ֝נֹותֶ֗יךָ כִּ֣ימֵי גָֽבֶר׃  
‘Are Your days as the days of a mortal, Or Your years as man’s years,

**10:6** כִּֽי־תְבַקֵּ֥שׁ לַעֲוֹנִ֑י וּ֭לְחַטָּאתִ֥י תִדְרֹֽושׁ׃  
That You should seek for my guilt And search after my sin?

**10:7** עַֽל־דַּ֭עְתְּךָ כִּי־לֹ֣א אֶרְשָׁ֑ע וְאֵ֖ין מִיָּדְךָ֣ מַצִּֽיל׃  
‘According to Your knowledge I am indeed not guilty, Yet there is no deliverance from Your hand.

**10:8** יָדֶ֣יךָ עִ֭צְּבוּנִי וַֽיַּעֲשׂ֑וּנִי ᵃיַ֥חַד סָ֝בִ֗יבᵃ וַֽתְּבַלְּעֵֽנִי׃  
‘Your hands fashioned and made me altogether, And would You destroy me?

**10:9** זְכָר־נָ֭א כִּי־כַחֹ֣מֶר עֲשִׂיתָ֑נִי וְֽאֶל־עָפָ֥ר תְּשִׁיבֵֽנִי׃  
‘Remember now, that You have made me as clay; And would You turn me into dust again?

**10:10** הֲלֹ֣א כֶ֭חָלָב תַּתִּיכֵ֑נִי וְ֝כַגְּבִנָּ֗ה תַּקְפִּיאֵֽנִי׃  
‘Did You not pour me out like milk And curdle me like cheese;

**10:11** עֹ֣ור וּ֭בָשָׂר תַּלְבִּישֵׁ֑נִי וּֽבַעֲצָמֹ֥ות וְ֝גִידִ֗ים תְּסֹכְכֵֽנִי׃  
Clothe me with skin and flesh, And knit me together with bones and sinews?

**10:12** חַיִּ֣ים וָ֭חֶסֶד עָשִׂ֣יתָ עִמָּדִ֑י וּ֝פְקֻדָּתְךָ֗ שָֽׁמְרָ֥ה רוּחִֽי׃  
‘You have granted me life and lovingkindness; And Your care has preserved my spirit.

**10:13** וְ֭אֵלֶּה צָפַ֣נְתָּ בִלְבָבֶ֑ךָ יָ֝דַ֗עְתִּי כִּי־זֹ֥את עִמָּֽךְ׃  
‘Yet these things You have concealed in Your heart; I know that this is within You:

**10:14** אִם־חָטָ֥אתִי וּשְׁמַרְתָּ֑נִי וּ֝מֵעֲוֹנִ֗י לֹ֣א תְנַקֵּֽנִי׃  
If I sin, then You would take note of me, And would not acquit me of my guilt.

**10:15** אִם־רָשַׁ֡עְתִּי אַלְלַ֬י לִ֗י וְ֭צָדַקְתִּי לֹא־אֶשָּׂ֣א רֹאשִׁ֑י שְׂבַ֥ע קָ֝לֹ֗ון וּרְאֵ֥הᵃ עָנְיִֽי׃  
‘If I am wicked, woe to me! And if I am righteous, I dare not lift up my head. I am sated with disgrace and conscious of my misery.

**10:16** וְ֭יִגְאֶה כַּשַּׁ֣חַל תְּצוּדֵ֑נִי וְ֝תָשֹׁ֗ב תִּתְפַּלָּא־בִֽי׃  
‘Should my head be lifted up, You would hunt me like a lion; And again You would show Your power against me.

**10:17** תְּחַדֵּ֬שׁ עֵדֶ֨יךָ׀ נֶגְדִּ֗י וְתֶ֣רֶב כַּֽ֭עַשְׂךָ עִמָּדִ֑י חֲלִיפֹ֖ות וְצָבָ֣א עִמִּֽי׃  
‘You renew Your witnesses against me And increase Your anger toward me; Hardship after hardship is with me.

**10:18** וְלָ֣מָּה מֵ֭רֶחֶם הֹצֵאתָ֑נִי אֶ֝גְוַ֗ע וְעַ֣יִן לֹא־תִרְאֵֽנִי׃  
‘Why then have You brought me out of the womb? Would that I had died and no eye had seen me!

**10:19** כַּאֲשֶׁ֣ר לֹא־הָיִ֣יתִי אֶהְיֶ֑ה מִ֝בֶּ֗טֶן לַקֶּ֥בֶר אוּבָֽל׃  
‘I should have been as though I had not been, Carried from womb to tomb.’

**10:20** הֲלֹא־מְעַ֣ט ᵃיָמַ֣י יַחֲדָ֑לᵃ ᵇיְשִׁ֥יתᶜ מִ֝מֶּ֗נִּי וְאַבְלִ֥יגָה מְּעָֽט׃  
“Would He not let my few days alone? Withdraw from me that I may have a little cheer

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims, each surfaced by several tools:

1. **Job turns the friends' doctrine into a lawsuit he cannot win, and he does it in their own words.** `[T]`
   - **The opening question is Eliphaz's.** "How can a man be in the right (יִצְדַּק, "be in the right") before God?" (9:2) takes up Eliphaz's night-vision, "Can mankind be just (יִצְדָּק, "be just") before God?" (4:17). צדק ("be in the right") with אֱנוֹשׁ ("man, mortal") in one verse occurs only at 4:17; 9:2; 15:14; 25:4 and 33:12 (lemma). Eliphaz meant moral purity. Job means winning a case: the next verse is "If one wished to dispute (לָרִיב, "to dispute") with Him" (9:3).
   - **The answers are Bildad's words turned round.** Bildad said "God will not reject (יִמְאַס, "reject") a man of integrity (תָּם, "blameless")" (8:20). Job replies "I am guiltless (תָּם); … I despise (אֶמְאַס, "despise") my life" (9:21). תָּם with מאס ("reject, despise") in one verse occurs only at 8:20 and 9:21 in the Hebrew Bible (lemma). Bildad told him to "implore the compassion" (תִּתְחַנָּן, "implore compassion") of the Almighty (8:5). Job: even if right, "I would have to implore the mercy (אֶתְחַנָּן) of my judge" (9:15) — the same verb in the same stem.
   - **The courtroom vocabulary is dense.** In 56 verses, ענה ("answer") ×5 (9:3, 14, 15, 16, 32); the verb רשׁע ("condemn, be guilty") ×5 (9:20, 29; 10:2, 7, 15); צדק ("be in the right") ×4 (9:2, 15, 20; 10:15); רִיב ("dispute, contend") ×2 (9:3; 10:2); מִשְׁפָּט ("justice, court") ×2 (9:19, 32); תָּם ("blameless") ×3 (9:20, 21, 22); נקה ("acquit") ×2 (9:28; 10:14); and מוֹכִיחַ ("umpire", 9:33) (lemma).
   - *Surfaced by:* Vocabulary, Repetition, Context, Move 4. *High.*

2. **Job calls himself by God's word for him, and without knowing it he repeats God's own admission in heaven.** `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the effect
   - **"Blameless."** תָּם ("blameless") is the narrator's and God's word for Job (1:1, 8; 2:3). Job says it of himself twice and of God's victims once (9:20, 21, 22). Those are its only occurrences in Job besides the prologue and Bildad's 8:20 (lemma).
   - **"Without cause … swallow."** God told the Accuser, "you incited Me against him to ruin (לְבַלְּעוֹ, literally "swallow") him without cause (חִנָּם, "without cause")" (2:3). That is the only verse in the Hebrew Bible with both words (lemma). In this unit Job says, "He … multiplies my wounds without cause (חִנָּם)" (9:17) and "Your hands fashioned … me, and would You destroy (וַתְּבַלְּעֵנִי, "swallow") me?" (10:8).
   - **"Kept."** God told the Accuser, "only spare (שְׁמֹר, "keep") his life" (2:6). Job says to God, "Your care has preserved (שָׁמְרָה, "keep") my spirit" (10:12) — and two verses later turns the verb into surveillance: "If I sin, then You would take note (וּשְׁמַרְתָּנִי, "keep watch") of me" (10:14).
   - The reader hears Job describe, piece by piece, the scene in heaven he never saw.
   - *Surfaced by:* Move 4, Vocabulary, Narrator's Comment (by absence), Who Am I?. *High on the words.*

3. **The unit holds two creation hymns — the Maker of the stars and the Maker of Job — and Job cannot make them one God.** `[T]`
   - **9:5–10 is a hymn in seven participles** (WLC morphology): "who removes … who shakes … who commands … who alone stretches out … and tramples … who makes … who does". It is the grammar of praise with the content of dread. Its vocabulary is that of Amos's doxologies (Amos 4:13; 5:8–9) and of Isa 44:24 (Tool 11).
   - **10:8–12 is a hymn of Job's own making:** hands that fashioned, clay, poured milk, curdled cheese, skin and flesh, bones and sinews, "life and lovingkindness (חֶסֶד, "lovingkindness")" (10:12). חֶסֶד occurs in Job only at 6:14; 10:12; 37:13 (lemma).
   - **Job cannot hold them together.** The God who made him with care is the God who hunts him "like a lion" (10:16) and who "works wonders" against him (תִּתְפַּלָּא, 10:16) — the verb of the "wonders" of 9:10.
   - **God's speeches take up both hymns.** The Bear, Orion and the Pleiades of 9:9 return in the Hebrew Bible only at 38:31–32 (with two of them at Amos 5:8). Clay (10:9) returns at 38:14, the eagle (9:26) at 39:27, and the "wonders" (9:10) in Job's confession at 42:3 (all lemma).
   - *Surfaced by:* Structure, Parallels, Tone, Tool 11, Move 4. *High.*

4. **Chapter 9 speaks about God; chapter 10 speaks to him — and God's answer takes up the very words Job speaks to him.** `[T]`
   - **Address.** There is no second-person plural in 9:1–10:22: the friends are never addressed (control: chapter 6 has 19 forms). Chapter 9 has three second-person singular forms — one in a quoted question (9:12) and two in a sudden turn to God (9:28, 31). Chapter 10 has 41 (WLC morphology).
   - **The hinge.** "I would speak (אֲדַבְּרָה, "speak") and not fear Him" (9:35); "I will speak (אֲדַבְּרָה) in the bitterness of my soul. I will say to God (אֹמַר אֶל־אֱלוֹהַּ, "I will say to God") …" (10:1–2). אמר ("say") with אֶל ("to") and a name of God occurs in Job only here, of the speaker himself. Its companion is 13:3, "I would speak to (אֶל) the Almighty" (lemma co-occurrence).
   - **God's reply.** "Do not condemn me (אַל־תַּרְשִׁיעֵנִי, "do not condemn me")" (10:2) is answered by "Will you condemn Me (תַּרְשִׁיעֵנִי) that you may be justified?" (40:8). The form occurs **only** at 10:2 and 40:8 in the Hebrew Bible (phrase search, confirmed by lemma).
     - "Let me know (הוֹדִיעֵנִי, "make me know")" (10:2; again 13:23) returns as God's "I will ask you, and you instruct (וְהוֹדִיעֵנִי) Me" (38:3; 40:7), which Job quotes at 42:4.
     - 40:2 is the only verse in the Hebrew Bible with רִיב ("contend"), מוֹכִיחַ ("reprover, umpire") and ענה ("answer") together — the three lawsuit words of 9:3 and 9:33.
     - Job said no one could answer God "once (אַחַת, "one") in a thousand times" (9:3). He ends, "Once (אַחַת) I have spoken, and I will not answer (אֶעֱנֶה)" (40:5).
   - *Surfaced by:* Original Languages (the address test), Structure, Linking Words, Move 4. *High.*

5. **Job longs for someone to stand between him and God, and plants every later mediator text in the book.** `[T]` for the words; trajectory `[I]`
   - "He is not a man as I am that I may answer Him … There is no umpire (מוֹכִיחַ, "umpire") between us, who may lay his hand upon us both" (9:32–33). "Let Him remove His rod … let not dread (אֵמָתוֹ, "dread of him") of Him terrify (תְּבַעֲתַנִּי, "terrify") me" (9:34). "You renew Your witnesses (עֵדֶיךָ, "witnesses") against me" (10:17).
   - **What follows in the book:**
     - Job says 9:34 again, now to God: "let not the dread of You terrify me" (13:21). אֵמָה ("dread") with בעת ("terrify") occurs only at 9:34; 13:21; 33:7 (lemma).
     - The witnesses change sides: "my witness (עֵדִי) is in heaven" (16:19). עֵד ("witness") occurs in Job only at 10:17; 16:8; 16:19.
     - Elihu offers himself as the figure Job described. "Behold, I belong to God like you; I too have been formed out of the clay (מֵחֹמֶר, "out of the clay")" (33:6). "Behold, no fear of me (אֵמָתִי) should terrify (תְבַעֲתֶךָּ) you" (33:7). Two consecutive verses answer 10:9 and 9:34.
     - "One out of a thousand (מִנִּי־אָלֶף)" (33:23) takes up 9:3; the phrase occurs only at these two verses (sweep; reconfirmed).
     - And God gives the title מוֹכִיחַ ("reprover") to Job (40:2). The participle occurs in Job only at 9:33; 32:12; 40:2.
   - **The Greek.** Swete's 9:33 reads "would that there were our mediator (μεσίτης)", the only μεσίτης in Swete's LXX, and the New Testament's word for Christ (1 Tim 2:5).
   - *Surfaced by:* Vocabulary, Translations, Move 4, Christological Reading. *High on the words; the trajectory is canonical, not a claim about what Job foresaw.*

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book level (overview, confirmed).** The book shows a righteous man who, stripped of everything, will neither curse God nor buy relief by confessing sins he did not commit. It exposes the friends' tidy theology as speaking wrongly of God (42:7), and brings the sufferer and the reader to a fear of God that wants God himself, reached through God's self-disclosure rather than an explanation. The introductory sermon's reading of 42:7 — right speech is speech *to* God — is borne out here: 10:2 is the first time in the book that a speaker announces "I will say to God" (Headline 4). `[I]`

**Unit level.** Chapters 9–10 do four things. `[T]` / `[I]`
- **They answer Bildad, and behind him Eliphaz, by agreeing with them.** "In truth I know that this is so" (9:2) concedes the friends' premise. No mortal can win against God. Then Job turns the premise into an accusation: that is precisely the problem. `[T]` for the concession and the borrowed words; `[I]` for the turn.
- **They make the book's lawsuit explicit.** Until now Job has lamented (3) and complained (6–7). Here he imagines taking God to court, and finds the court impossible: God is judge and opponent at once (9:15, 19), too strong (9:19), unseen (9:11) and unanswerable (9:3, 14). `[T]`
- **They turn lament into address.** Chapter 3 spoke to no one (Job 3 report). Chapter 7 turned to God (7:7–21). Chapter 10 announces the turn and sustains it for seventeen verses (10:2–18). `[T]`
- **They plant the mediator.** The umpire (9:33), the one "not a man as I am" (9:32), the witnesses (10:17) — every later text of this kind in the book grows from here (Headline 5). `[I]`

**Selection.** Job says nothing here about:
- his children, his wealth or his wife;
- any sin he might have committed;
- the friends as persons — they are never addressed (Headline 4).

He speaks about God (9) and to God (10), and about himself only as God's creature, defendant and prey. `[T]` for the absences; `[I]` for the effect.

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement.** `[T]`
- **Eliphaz (4–5)** has set the terms. No mortal is righteous before God (4:17). God "does great and unsearchable things, wonders without number" (5:9). "He inflicts pain, and gives relief; He wounds, and His hands also heal" (5:18).
- **Job (6–7)** has turned from the friends to God: "Remember that my life is but breath" (7:7). He has asked why he is watched (7:12, 17–20) and begged to be let alone: "Leave me alone (חֲדַל מִמֶּנִּי, "leave me alone"), for my days are but a breath (הֶבֶל, "breath")" (7:16).
- **Bildad (8)** has defended God's justice: "Does God pervert justice?" (8:3). He has blamed the children (8:4) and promised that if Job is "pure and upright" (8:6) and implores God (8:5), God "will not reject a man of integrity" (8:20).

**Necessity answer.** 9–10 must come here, and must be Job's. `[I]`
- **Bildad's promise needs its answer.** "God will not reject a man of integrity (תָּם)" (8:20) is answered by "I am guiltless (תָּם) … He destroys the guiltless (תָּם) and the wicked" (9:21–22).
- **Eliphaz's two sentences need their answer.** "Can mankind be just before God?" (4:17) becomes a courtroom question (9:2). His "wonders without number" (5:9) is sung back as a hymn to terrifying power (9:10).
- **Job's plea to be let alone (7:16, 19) needs its sequel.** It comes at the end, "Would He not let my few days alone (יֶחְדָּל, "cease")?" (10:20). The verb חדל ("leave off, cease") with "my days" occurs in Job only at 7:16; 10:20; 14:6 (lemma).
- **The book needs its legal frame.** The book's lawsuit vocabulary — ריב ("contend"), מִשְׁפָּט ("court"), צדק ("be in the right"), יכח ("argue") — first gathers here. The mediator longing that runs to 16:19, 19:25 and 33:23 starts here.

**Implication.** 9–10 is the point where Job's problem becomes a problem of *access*: he has a case and no court, a Judge who is also his opponent, and no one to stand between. That is why the unit ends not with a verdict but with a plea to be left alone before death (10:20–22). A sermon that preaches 9:33 must preach it as the cry of a man shut out of court. `[I]`

#### Surrounding context

- **Before:** Bildad's first speech (8). Its "God will not reject a man of integrity" (8:20) and "He will yet fill your mouth with laughter" (8:21) are the words Job answers.
- **After:** Zophar (11), who says that God exacts less than Job's guilt deserves (11:6), and whose "if you would direct your heart" (11:13) prescribes repentance. Job's next reply (12–14) returns to the lawsuit ("I would speak to the Almighty", 13:3) and repeats 9:34 as a prayer (13:21).
- **Section:** round one of the dialogue (4–14): Eliphaz, Job, Bildad, Job, Zophar, Job. This is Job's second reply.
- **Masoretic paragraphing:** one paragraph, 9:1–10:22, with a petuḥah after 8:22 and after 10:22 (Leningrad, as BHS prints it). The chapter division at 10:1 is not a Masoretic break. `[T]`; `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`.

`[T]` for every marker (lemma and morphology).

**Stanza lengths.** 12, 11, 11, 7, 10, 5 verses. The ESV paragraphs fall at 9:13, 9:25, 10:1 and 10:18; the NASB95's at 9:13, 9:25, 10:1, 10:8 and 10:18. `[T]`

**Device: two halves, each ending in death.** `[T]` for the markers; `[I]` for the design.
- **Chapter 9** moves from God's power (I), to Job's helplessness (II), to his short days and the missing umpire (III). It ends with "I would speak and not fear Him" (9:35).
- **Chapter 10** moves from the decision to speak (IV), to God's making and unmaking of Job (V), to the womb and the land of darkness (VI).
- **The hinge** is אֲדַבְּרָה ("I would speak / I will speak"), the last word-group of 9:35 and the first act of 10:1.

**Two creation panels.** The Creator of the cosmos (9:5–10) and the Creator of Job (10:8–12) stand at matching points in the two halves: each follows an opening question (9:2–4; 10:2–7), and each is followed by the charge that the Creator uses his power against Job (9:11–24; 10:13–17). `[T]` for the positions; `[I]` for the correspondence. *Moderate–high.*

**Bookend with chapter 3.** 10:18–22 returns to chapter 3's wish and vocabulary: the womb (רֶחֶם, 3:11; 10:18), expiring (גוע, 3:11; 10:18), darkness and deep darkness (חֹשֶׁךְ וְצַלְמָוֶת, 3:5; 10:21 — the only two verses in Job's own speeches with both nouns before 12:22), and the verb יפע ("shine", 3:4; 10:3, 22). What chapter 3 said to no one, 10:18 says to God. `[T]` (lemma; Job 3 report, Headline 2).

### 4. Linking Words

- **אָמְנָם יָדַעְתִּי כִי־כֵן ("In truth I know that this is so", 9:2).** A concession. "This" refers back to the friends' claim, Bildad's (8:3, 20) and Eliphaz's (4:17). The וּ ("but") that follows turns the concession into the problem. `[T]`
- **אִם ("if"), a chain of hypotheticals.** 9:3, 15, 16, 19 (×2), 20, 23, 24, 27, 30; 10:14, 15. (At 10:4–5 אִם means "or", in paired questions.) The unit is built of imagined trials that all end in loss. "If I called and He answered me, I could not believe that He was listening" (9:16). "If I should wash myself with snow … You would plunge me into the pit" (9:30–31). `[T]`
- **הֵן ("behold", 9:11, 12).** "Behold, He passes by me … behold, He snatches away." The exclamation marks God's unseen, unstoppable action. `[T]`
- **אַף כִּי ("How much less / how then", 9:14).** The a-fortiori turn: if Rahab's helpers crouch (9:13), how could Job answer? `[T]`
- **אֲשֶׁר (9:15, 17).** Twice at the head of a line, with the force "for / since" (NASB95 "For"). `[T]`
- **עַל־כֵּן ("therefore", 9:22).** "It is all one; therefore I say, 'He destroys the guiltless and the wicked.'" The one "therefore" in the unit introduces its most shocking sentence. `[T]`
- **אִם־לֹא אֵפוֹא מִי־הוּא ("If it is not He, then who is it?", 9:24).** A conditional that closes the second stanza by refusing any other agent. The reader knows another name (1:12; 2:6–7). `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the irony.
- **כִּי ("for", 9:32, 35; 10:6, 7, 9).** At 9:32 it grounds the impossibility: "For He is not a man as I am." At 10:6–7 it supplies God's motive, as Job imagines it: "that You should seek for my guilt … although You know I am not guilty". `[T]`
- **וְ ("yet / and", 10:8b, 13).** "Your hands fashioned and made me … and would You destroy me?" (10:8); "Yet these things You have concealed in Your heart" (10:13). The two adversatives of chapter 10 set making against unmaking. `[T]`
- **וְלָמָּה ("Why then", 10:18).** One of two "why"s in the unit, with לָמָּה־זֶּה ("Why then …?", 9:29) (lemma). It resumes chapter 3's לָמָּה (3:11, 20), and it is the only one addressed to God. `[T]`

### 5. Parallels

- **9:5–10 — a hymn of seven participles in synonymous and synthetic lines.** Mountains removed and overturned (9:5); earth shaken and pillars trembling (9:6); sun and stars sealed (9:7); heavens stretched and sea trampled (9:8); the constellations and "the chambers of the south" (9:9); great things and wonders (9:10). The parallelism is praise; the verbs are violent. `[T]`
- **9:11 — synonymous.** "Were He to pass by (יַעֲבֹר, "pass by") me, I would not see Him; were He to move past (יַחֲלֹף, "move past") me, I would not perceive Him." Eliphaz used the second verb of the spirit that "passed (יַחֲלֹף) by my face" (4:15). The friend saw and heard; Job neither sees nor perceives. `[T]` for the verb; `[I]` for the contrast.
- **9:20 — antithetical within each line.** "Though I am righteous, my mouth will condemn me; though I am guiltless, He will declare me guilty." Righteousness and guilt stand side by side in both halves, with the verdict reversed each time. `[T]`
- **9:22 — the merism of the blameless and the wicked.** "He destroys the guiltless and the wicked (תָּם וְרָשָׁע)." Death as the great leveller, the same thought as 3:17–19. `[T]`
- **10:4–5 — synonymous questions.** "Have You eyes of flesh? Or do You see as a man sees? Are Your days as the days of a mortal (אֱנוֹשׁ, "mortal"), or Your years as man's (גֶּבֶר, "man") years?" `[T]`
- **10:10–11 — the making of a body, in two panels.** Milk poured, cheese curdled (10:10); skin and flesh for clothing, bones and sinews for weaving (10:11). The images move from conception to form. `[T]`
- **10:12 against 10:14 — the same verb, turned.** "Your care has preserved (שָׁמְרָה) my spirit" // "If I sin, then You would take note (וּשְׁמַרְתָּנִי) of me". The parallel is broken across two verses: care becomes surveillance. `[T]` for the verb; `[I]` for the effect.
- **10:21–22 — piled synonyms for darkness.** חֹשֶׁךְ ("darkness"), צַלְמָוֶת ("deep shadow") ×2, עֵיפָה ("utter gloom"), אֹפֶל ("darkness") ×2, and "without order" (לֹא סְדָרִים). The last line reverses itself: the land "shines (וַתֹּפַע) as the darkness". `[T]`

### 6. Narrator's Comment

**N/A for the speech itself** — 9:2–10:22 is character speech with no narrative intrusion. The only narrative words are the formula at 9:1 ("Then Job answered"). `[T]`

**What the narrator has already said stands over the speech.** He has called Job תָּם ("blameless", 1:1), and God has said it twice (1:8; 2:3). He has reported God's admission that Job was ruined "without cause" (2:3). He has recorded that Job "did not sin with his lips" (2:10). When Job says תָּם אָנִי ("I am blameless", 9:21), he is asserting what the narrator and God have already said, and the reader knows it. The book's verdict on this speech comes later: God rebukes Job's words "without knowledge" (38:2) and his condemning of God (40:8), and says Job spoke "what is right" (42:7). `[T]` for the texts; `[I]` for their bearing.

### 7. Vocabulary

**(a) The lawsuit words.** `[T]` (lemma, WLC)
- **צדק ("be in the right").** 9:2, 15, 20; 10:15. In 4:17 Eliphaz asked whether a mortal can be "just" before God, meaning morally pure. In 9:2 the same verb is forensic: to win a case, to be declared in the right. 9:15 makes the sense plain: "though I were right (צָדַקְתִּי), I could not answer". *High.*
- **רשׁע ("condemn", hiphil; "be guilty", qal).** 9:20 ("my mouth will condemn me", יַרְשִׁיעֵנִי); 9:29 ("I am accounted wicked", אֶרְשָׁע); 10:2 ("Do not condemn me", אַל־תַּרְשִׁיעֵנִי); 10:7 ("I am indeed not guilty", לֹא אֶרְשָׁע); 10:15 ("If I am wicked", רָשַׁעְתִּי). Five of the eleven occurrences of the verb in Job fall in this unit. The others are 15:6 (Eliphaz quoting 9:20), 32:3, 34:12, 17, 29 (Elihu) and 40:8 (God). *High.*
- **צדק with רשׁע ("justify / condemn").** The pair stands in one verse at 9:20; 10:15; 40:8 in Job, and elsewhere only at Deut 25:1, 1 Kgs 8:32 and Prov 17:15 (lemma co-occurrence). All three are the judge's duty: to declare the righteous in the right and the wicked guilty. Deut 25:1: "they justify the righteous and condemn the wicked". Prov 17:15: "He who justifies the wicked and he who condemns the righteous, both of them alike are an abomination to the LORD". Job charges God with the second. *High on the data; moderate on a deliberate echo.*
- **תָּם ("blameless, guiltless").** 9:20, 21, 22. NASB95 "guiltless"; the same word is "blameless" at 1:1, 8; 2:3 and "a man of integrity" at 8:20. The lemma occurs 14 times in the Hebrew Bible, seven of them in Job (1:1, 8; 2:3; 8:20; 9:20, 21, 22). Job is not boasting. He is holding to the word God used of him. *High.*
- **מוֹכִיחַ ("umpire", 9:33).** The hiphil participle of יכח ("argue, decide, reprove"): one who decides a case between two parties. In Job only at 9:33; 32:12; 40:2. At 40:2 God calls Job himself מוֹכִיחַ אֱלוֹהַּ ("he who reproves God"). *High.*
- **ענה ("answer").** 9:3, 14, 15, 16, 32. In a court, to answer is to make one's plea. Job cannot answer God (9:3, 14, 15, 32), and if God answered him he would not believe it (9:16). The claim audit notes that God is the subject of ענה with Job as object at 38:1; 40:1, 6 `[S: audit]`. *High.*

**(b) Words from the prologue, in Job's mouth.** `[T]`
- **חִנָּם ("without cause", 9:17).** In Job only at 1:9; 2:3; 9:17; 22:6 (lemma). The Accuser asked if Job served God חִנָּם (1:9). God said Job was ruined חִנָּם (2:3). Job now says his wounds are multiplied חִנָּם — the one person in the book who says what God said. *High.*
- **בלע ("swallow, destroy", 10:8).** 2:3 is the only verse in the Hebrew Bible where בלע and חִנָּם meet. The verb occurs in Job at 2:3; 7:19; 8:18; 10:8; 20:15, 18; 37:20. *High on the words.*
- **שׁמר ("keep, guard", 10:12, 14).** "Only spare (שְׁמֹר) his life" (2:6) is God's limit on the Accuser. Job: "Your care has preserved (שָׁמְרָה) my spirit" (10:12). שׁמר with נֶפֶשׁ ("life") stands in Job only at 2:6; שׁמר with רוּחַ ("spirit") only at 10:12. *Moderate–high* for a deliberate echo.

**(c) Creation and the body.** `[T]`
- **חֹמֶר ("clay", 10:9).** In Job at 4:19 (Eliphaz: "those who dwell in houses of clay, whose foundation is in the dust"); 10:9; 13:12; 27:16; 30:19; 33:6; 38:14. חֹמֶר with עָפָר ("dust") in one verse occurs only in Job: 4:19; 10:9; 27:16; 30:19. *High.*
- **סכך ("weave, knit together", 10:11).** תְּסֹכְכֵנִי ("You knit me together"). The WLC index files it under Strong's 5526, with different homograph letters for different senses: 3:23 ("whom God has hedged in") and 38:8 ("who enclosed the sea") are 5526 *a*; 10:11 is 5526 *f*; Ps 139:13 ("You wove me in my mother's womb") is 5526 *c* (lemma). So one root, and to the ear one word, with more than one sense. The lexicons divide it into "screen, cover" and "weave" `[S]`. *Moderate* for any wordplay between "hedged in" (3:23) and "knit" (10:11).
- **גִּיד ("sinew", 10:11).** Six occurrences in the Hebrew Bible. Skin, flesh and sinews meet in one verse only at Ezek 37:6, 8 and Job 10:11 (lemma co-occurrence). *High on the data.*
- **חֶסֶד ("lovingkindness", 10:12).** In Job only at 6:14; 10:12; 37:13. Here, and only here in Job, it is God's חֶסֶד towards Job, confessed in the middle of accusation. The idiom עשׂה חֶסֶד עִם ("show lovingkindness to") is the covenant idiom of Gen 24:12; Ruth 1:8; 2 Sam 9:1. `[T]` for the forms; *high.*
- **פְּקֻדָּה ("care, visitation", 10:12).** Only here in Job. Its verb פקד is the verb of 7:18, "that You examine (תִּפְקְדֶנּוּ) him every morning", the Ps 8 inversion (overview). *Moderate.*

**(d) Names and the proper-noun inventory.**
- **Inventory.** רַהַב ("Rahab", 9:13); עָשׁ ("the Bear"), כְּסִיל ("Orion"), כִּימָה ("the Pleiades") and חַדְרֵי תֵמָן ("the chambers of the south", 9:9); אֵל ("God", 9:2) and אֱלוֹהַּ ("God", 9:13; 10:2). No personal or place names.
- **Rahab.** The WLC files Job's two occurrences (9:13; 26:12) under a different lemma number from Isa 51:9, Ps 87:4 and Ps 89:11 (overview; sweep). Both were searched. It is the sea-monster of Isa 51:9, the power God defeated. *High.*
- **The constellations.** כִּימָה occurs three times in the Hebrew Bible (Amos 5:8; Job 9:9; 38:31), כְּסִיל four times (with Isa 13:10), עָשׁ twice (9:9; 38:32, spelled עַיִשׁ) (lemma). All three return together only in God's question at 38:31–32. *High.*

**Paronomasia check (words repeated three or more times).** ענה, רשׁע, צדק, תָּם, יוֹם ("day"), יָד ("hand"), עשׂה ("make") and שׁוב ("return") were checked. Two sound-plays are worth recording, and neither carries a finding alone.
- **עַצְּבֹתָי ("my pains", 9:28) and עִצְּבוּנִי ("fashioned me", 10:8).** Two different lemmas (6094 and 6087 *b*), identical in root letters, on either side of the chapter division. "I am afraid of all my pains" against "Your hands fashioned me". *Moderate*; a sound-play without independent corroboration (the hard rule applies). `[T]` for the forms.
- **שׁוֹט ("scourge", 9:23) and שׁוּט ("roam", 1:7; 2:2).** The claim audit confirmed the sound-play with nuance: two lemmas, the same consonants `[S: audit]`. *Moderate.*

### 9. Tone and Feel

**The register is a lawsuit conducted in lament, with a hymn sung through clenched teeth.** `[T]` for the devices; `[I]` for the register.
- **9:2–13 — the hymn of dread.** The grammar is doxology: seven participles of divine action, as in Amos's doxologies. The content is fear. Mountains are overturned "in His anger" (9:5), the sun is told not to rise (9:7), God passes by unseen and snatches unhindered (9:11–12), and Rahab's helpers crouch (9:13). Praise has become a description of what cannot be resisted.
- **9:14–24 — the imagined trial.** Short conditional clauses: "If I called … If it is a matter of power … If it is a matter of justice … Though I am righteous …" The voice gets harder until the sentence that no friend would dare: "He destroys the guiltless and the wicked … He mocks the despair of the innocent … If it is not He, then who is it?" (9:22–24).
- **9:25–35 — speed and futility.** Days like a runner, reed boats, a swooping eagle (9:25–26). Then the useless washing — snow, lye, the pit, clothes that abhor him (9:30–31). Then the longing: no umpire, and a wish for the rod to be lifted.
- **10:1–17 — tender and accusing at once.** Job speaks to God as a creature to his Maker. "Remember now, that You have made me as clay" (10:9). The images are intimate — milk, cheese, skin, knitting — and then turn. The God who "granted me life and lovingkindness" (10:12) is now a lion on the hunt (10:16).
- **10:18–22 — the dark.** The unit ends where chapter 3 ended, in a land "without order", where light shines as darkness. Six words for darkness in two verses (Tool 10).

**The soundtrack.** A courtroom with only one party present, and then a man speaking alone to someone he cannot see. **For the preacher:**
- Do not lift 9:5–10 out as a hymn of praise; it is praise with its teeth bared.
- Do not soften 9:22–24. The book includes them, God rebukes Job's words at 38:2 and 40:8, and God says Job spoke rightly at 42:7.
- Let chapter 10's tenderness be heard: this is a creature pleading with the hands that made him.

**Amos's doxologies — Amos 4:13; 5:8–9 → 9:5–10; 9:27; 10:20–22** *(high on the words; moderate–high on the design)* `[T]` / `[I]` (synthetic)

*Move 1 — Source context.*
- Amos breaks into his oracles of judgement three times with a hymn to the Creator (4:13; 5:8–9; 9:5–6).
- **4:13** follows "prepare to meet your God, O Israel" (4:12): "For behold, He who forms mountains and creates the wind … He who makes dawn into darkness (עֹשֵׂה שַׁחַר עֵיפָה) and treads on the high places of the earth (וְדֹרֵךְ עַל־בָּמֳתֵי אָרֶץ), the LORD God of hosts is His name."
- **5:8–9** interrupts a lament over Israel's injustice (5:7, 10): "He who made the Pleiades and Orion (עֹשֵׂה כִימָה וּכְסִיל) and changes deep darkness (צַלְמָוֶת) into morning … who calls for the waters of the sea … It is He who flashes forth (הַמַּבְלִיג, "flashes forth") with destruction upon the strong."
- The register is praise used as warning. The God who orders the cosmos is coming in judgement.

*The verbal data* (all lemma, WLC):
- עֵיפָה ("gloom") occurs twice in the Hebrew Bible: Amos 4:13 and Job 10:22.
- דרך ("tread") with בָּמָה ("high place") in one verse occurs at Amos 4:13; Mic 1:3; Deut 33:29; Hab 3:19; Job 9:8. Job's is the only one with "the sea" (בָּמֳתֵי יָם, "the waves of the sea").
- כִּימָה ("the Pleiades") occurs at Amos 5:8; Job 9:9; 38:31. כְּסִיל ("Orion") at Amos 5:8; Isa 13:10; Job 9:9; 38:31.
- בלג ("flash forth; be cheerful") occurs at Amos 5:9; Job 9:27; 10:20; Ps 39:14 [Eng 39:13]. In Amos it means "flash forth"; in Job and Ps 39 "be cheerful". It is one lemma with two senses.
- הפך ("overturn", Job 9:5; Amos 5:8, "changes"), צַלְמָוֶת ("deep darkness", Job 10:21–22; Amos 5:8), יָם ("sea", Job 9:8; Amos 5:8; 9:6) and שָׁמַיִם ("heavens", Job 9:8; Amos 9:6) are shared as well.

*Move 2 — Book usage.* The Job 3 report found Amos 5:8 behind 3:5–9 (צַלְמָוֶת into morning; day darkened into night). With 9:5–10 and 10:22, Amos's doxologies touch Job in two speeches and three places. **On the overview's rule (two or more uses), Amos's doxologies are now a live source.** They are not yet in the map. `[T]` for the links; the pattern is synthetic.

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* Amos's doxologies are themselves built on the Genesis creation vocabulary: light and darkness, day and night, the waters of the sea "on the face of the earth" (Amos 5:8; 9:6; Gen 1:2, 9). Job 3 had used Genesis 1 directly to un-create his day. Here Job uses the prophet's creation hymns, which already carried judgement. `[I]`

*What it adds.*
- Job's hymn is orthodox. Its words are a prophet's words about the Creator.
- What Job has done is to hear them as Amos meant them, as the praise of a God who comes in judgement, and to apply them to himself. Amos sang them over a guilty Israel; Job sings them over an innocent man (1:8; 2:3).
- The gloom Amos's God makes of the dawn (4:13) is the land of gloom Job goes to (10:22). Amos's God turns deep darkness into morning (5:8); Job goes where light shines as darkness (10:22). `[I]`
- *Confirms and extends* the sweep's Amos entry (Unit 6).

**Isaiah 44:24 → 9:8; 10:8–19** *(high on the phrase; moderate on the design)* `[T]` / `[I]`

*Move 1 — Source context.*
- Isa 44:21–28 closes the great polemic against idols (44:9–20).
- "Thus says the LORD, your Redeemer (גֹּאֲלֶךָ, "your Redeemer"), and the one who formed you from the womb (וְיֹצֶרְךָ מִבָּטֶן, "who formed you from the womb"), 'I, the LORD, am the maker of all things, stretching out the heavens by Myself (נֹטֶה שָׁמַיִם לְבַדִּי) and spreading out the earth all alone'" (44:24).
- It then runs on to Cyrus and the rebuilding of Jerusalem (44:26–28). The register is assurance: the Creator of the cosmos is the Maker and Redeemer of his people.

*The verbal data.*
- נטה ("stretch out") with שָׁמַיִם ("heavens") and לְבַד ("alone") in one verse occurs **only** at Isa 44:24 and Job 9:8 (lemma co-occurrence). The pair נטה + שָׁמַיִם occurs in 20 verses, most of them Isaiah's creation language (40:22; 42:5; 45:12; 51:13) and the Psalter's (Ps 104:2).
- Isa 44:24 also has "formed you from the womb (מִבָּטֶן)". Job 10:8–19 is Job's own forming in the womb, with בֶּטֶן ("womb") at 10:19.

*Move 2 — Book usage.* The first link to Isaiah 40–48 in the digs so far. The overview has Isa 41:20 at 12:9, the dialogue's only יְהוָה ("the LORD"). With 9:8 and 10:7 (next entry), chapters 40–45 of Isaiah touch Job 9–10 at three points. *Watch for more.* `[T]`

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* Isaiah joins in one verse what Job holds apart: the Maker of the heavens (9:5–10) and the Maker of the man in the womb (10:8–12). And he calls that Maker "your Redeemer". The word returns in Job only at 19:25, "I know that my Redeemer (גֹּאֲלִי) lives" (and at 3:5, where darkness "claims" the day; Job 3 report). `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the pattern.

*What it adds.* Job's two creation hymns are the two halves of Isa 44:24. In Isaiah they meet in a promise; in Job they come apart into terror (9) and bewilderment (10:8, "and would You destroy me?"). The canon has already said what Job cannot yet say: the one who stretched out the heavens alone and formed you in the womb is your Redeemer. Job reaches the word at 19:25. `[I]` *Moderate.* Direction of dependence open.

**Deuteronomy 32:39; Isaiah 43:13 → 10:7 (with 9:12, and Eliphaz's 5:18)** *(high on the phrase)* `[T]`

*Move 1 — Source context.*
- **Deut 32:39** is the climax of the Song of Moses. After Israel's idolatry and God's judgement, God speaks: "See now that I, I am He, and there is no god besides Me (עִמָּדִי, "with me"). It is I who put to death and give life. I have wounded and it is I who heal, and there is no one who can deliver from My hand (וְאֵין מִיָּדִי מַצִּיל)." The register is sovereign self-assertion, judgement turning to vindication (32:43).
- **Isa 43:13**: "Even from eternity I am He, and there is none who can deliver out of My hand (וְאֵין מִיָּדִי מַצִּיל). I act and who can reverse it (וּמִי יְשִׁיבֶנָּה)?" — the Redeemer of Israel, in a court scene where God's people are his witnesses (43:9–12).

*The verbal data.*
- The phrase וְאֵין מִיַּד־ … מַצִּיל ("there is none who delivers from [your / my] hand") occurs **only** at Deut 32:39; Isa 43:13; Job 10:7 (phrase search; lemma co-occurrence of אֵין, יָד and נצל gives these three with four others).
- "Who can turn Him back? (מִי יְשִׁיבֶנּוּ)" (9:12) matches Isa 43:13's "who can reverse it? (מִי יְשִׁיבֶנָּה)". מִי with the hiphil of שׁוב stands at Isa 14:27; 43:13; Jer 2:24; Job 9:12; 11:10; 23:13 (phrase search).
- **Eliphaz had used the other half of Deut 32:39.** מחץ ("wound") with רפא ("heal") in one verse occurs only at Deut 32:39 and Job 5:18, "He wounds, and His hands also heal" (lemma).

*Move 2 — Book usage.* Deuteronomy is already live in Job. Deut 28 stands behind 1:10, 2:7 and 3:25 (earlier digs). **Deut 32:39 is now used twice, by two speakers:** Eliphaz takes its comfort ("He wounds, and heals", 5:18); Job takes its closing clause, turned against himself ("there is no deliverance from Your hand", 10:7). `[T]`

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* Isa 43:13 is itself built on Deut 32:39 — "I am He", "none can deliver from My hand" (the direction of dependence between Deuteronomy's song and Isaiah is open `[S]`). Job 10:7 thus stands on a canonical line of the sovereign "I am He". `[I]`

*What it adds.* Eliphaz quoted Moses' song to promise healing. Job answers with the next line of the same verse, and hears it as a trap: there is no one to deliver from the hand that wounds. Both are true readings of Deut 32:39, and the book will not let either stand alone. `[I]` *Moderate–high.*

**Psalm 39 → 9:27, 34; 10:2, 14, 20–21 (and 7:16, 19; 14:6)** *(high on the words; a live source)* `[T]`

*Move 1 — Source context.*
- **The psalmist's silence and speech.** He resolved to keep silent before the wicked, "I will guard (אֶשְׁמְרָה) my ways that I may not sin (מֵחֲטוֹא)" (39:2 [Eng 39:1]), but the silence burned, and he spoke (39:3–4 [Eng 39:2–3]).
- **What he says.** "LORD, make me to know (הוֹדִיעֵנִי) my end and what is the extent of my days; let me know how transient (חָדֵל) I am" (39:5 [Eng 39:4]). "Surely every man at his best is a mere breath (הֶבֶל)" (39:6 [Eng 39:5]). "Remove (הָסֵר) Your plague from me (מֵעָלַי); because of the opposition of Your hand I am perishing" (39:11 [Eng 39:10]). *The verbal data* (lemma and phrase, WLC):
- **בְּטֶרֶם אֵלֵךְ ("before I go")** — only Ps 39:14 and Job 10:21 (phrase search).
- **וְאַבְלִיגָה ("that I may be cheerful")** — as a form, only Job 9:27; 10:20; Ps 39:14 (phrase search).
- **הוֹדִיעֵנִי ("make me know")** — Ps 39:5; Job 10:2 (with 13:23 הֹדִיעֵנִי); also Ps 25:4; 143:8; 1 Sam 28:15; Jer 11:18; Job 38:3; 40:7; 42:4.
- **סור + מֵעָלַי ("remove from me")** — Ps 39:11; Job 9:34 ("Let Him remove His rod from me", יָסֵר מֵעָלַי).
- **שׁמר with חטא ("guard / sin")** — Ps 39:2; Job 10:14; 2 Kgs 10:31 (lemma co-occurrence).
- **הֶבֶל ("breath, vanity")** — Ps 39:6, 7, 12 [Eng 39:5, 6, 11]; Job 7:16; 9:29.
- **שׁעה with מִן ("look away from")** — Ps 39:14; Job 7:19 ("Will You never turn Your gaze away from me?"); 14:6 ("Turn Your gaze from him").

*Move 2 — Book usage.* Job's earlier plea used the psalm's verbs: "Leave me alone (חֲדַל מִמֶּנִּי), for my days are but a breath (הֶבֶל)" (7:16); "Will You never turn Your gaze away from me (לֹא־תִשְׁעֶה מִמֶּנִּי)?" (7:19). This unit takes up the psalm's ending almost word for word (10:20–21). 14:6 will use it again. **Psalm 39 is live in Job: 7:16, 19; 9:27, 34; 10:2, 14, 20–21; 14:6.** It is not in the overview's map. `[T]`

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* Psalm 39 is a Davidic psalm in the Psalter's first book, Job's canonical neighbour. Its "mere breath" (הֶבֶל) returns in Ps 62:10 [Eng 62:9] and 144:4 (lemma). In Job the plea's verb שׁעה ("look away") occurs only at 7:19 and 14:6 (lemma). `[T]` for the lemmas.

*What it adds.*
- The psalmist asks to be told his end and to be left alone before he goes, and he asks it **of God**: "Hear my prayer, O LORD" (39:13 [Eng 39:12]).
- Job's ending of chapter 10 is the psalmist's ending. Its opening, "make me know" (10:2), is the psalmist's opening. Chapter 10 is Job praying a psalm of the righteous sufferer back to God, bitterly.
- The canonical reader, fresh from the Psalter, would recognise the prayer. `[I]` *High on the words; the reading is `[I]`.*

**Exodus 33:19–34:7 → 9:11; 9:15, 28; 10:12–15** *(moderate–high; synthetic)* `[T]` / `[I]`

*Move 1 — Source context.*
- After the golden calf, Moses asks to see God's glory.
- God says: "I Myself will make all My goodness pass (אַעֲבִיר, "make pass") before you … and will be gracious (וְחַנֹּתִי, "be gracious") to whom I will be gracious" (33:19). "You cannot see My face, for no man can see Me and live" (33:20). "While My glory is passing by (בַּעֲבֹר) … I will cover you (וְשַׂכֹּתִי) with My hand until I have passed by" (33:22). "I will take My hand away (וַהֲסִרֹתִי) and you shall see My back" (33:23).

- The register is revelation and mercy after judgement.

*The verbal data* (lemma, WLC):
- **עבר ("pass by")** — God passes by Moses (Exod 33:19, 22; 34:6); "Were He to pass by (יַעֲבֹר) me, I would not see Him" (Job 9:11).
- **נקה, piel, with עָוֹן** — in one verse only at Exod 34:7; Num 14:18 (which quotes it); Num 5:31; Job 10:14. With חֶסֶד as well, only Exod 34:7 and Num 14:18. Job 10:12–14 has חֶסֶד (10:12), עָוֹן (10:14) and "You would not acquit me (לֹא תְנַקֵּנִי)" (10:14), with עָוֹן and חַטָּאת ("sin") at 10:6.
- **חנן ("be gracious")** — Exod 33:19; Job 9:15 ("I would have to implore the mercy (אֶתְחַנָּן) of my judge").
- **סכך with כַּף ("cover with the hand")** — Exod 33:22; compare Job 10:11 (סכך, "knit") and 13:21 ("Remove Your hand (כַּפְּךָ) from me").
- **נשׂא ("bear, lift")** — "who forgives (bears) iniquity" (Exod 34:7); "I dare not lift up (אֶשָּׂא) my head" (Job 10:15).
- **Swete:** Exod 34:6 καὶ παρῆλθεν Κύριος ("and the Lord passed by"); Job 9:11 ἐὰν παρέλθῃ με ("if he passes by me").

*Move 2 — Book usage.* Exodus has not been in the overview. The Job 2 and Job 3 digs found the sixth and ninth plagues (Exod 9:8–10 at 2:12; Exod 10:21 at 3:4). This is the first link to the Sinai theophany. `[T]` Synthetic, and capped at moderate–high until audited.

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* Exod 34:6–7 is the most quoted self-description of God in the Hebrew Bible:
- Num 14:18;
- Joel 2:13;
- Jonah 4:2;
- Nah 1:3 (whose "will by no means acquit" is נקה again);
- Ps 86:15; 103:8; 145:8;
- Neh 9:17.

Job 10:12–14 stands in that line, but inverted. `[T]`: אֶרֶךְ אַפַּיִם ("slow to anger") with חַנּוּן ("gracious") stands in one verse at Exod 34:6; Joel 2:13; Jonah 4:2; Ps 86:15; 103:8; 145:8; Neh 9:17 (lemma co-occurrence); Num 14:18 and Nah 1:3 carry the "acquit" clause (נקה).

*What it adds.*
- Moses asked to see God, and God passed by and proclaimed his name: mercy, lovingkindness, forgiveness — and "He will by no means acquit the guilty".
- Job says God passes by unseen (9:11). He grants lovingkindness (10:12) and then will not acquit (10:14) — **a man he knows is not guilty** (10:7). Job has only the one clause of the Name that applies to the guilty, and it is being applied to him.
- The New Testament's Jesus walks on the sea and "intended to pass by (παρελθεῖν) them", then says "It is I (ἐγώ εἰμι)" (Mark 6:48–50, SBLGNT). That is the vocabulary of Job 9:8, 11 (Swete) and Exod 34:6 (Swete) together. See Christological Reading. `[I]` *Moderate–high.*

**Isaiah 49:4 → 9:29 (with 9:19, 22 and 10:18–19)** *(moderate–high)* `[T]` / `[I]`

*Move 2 — Book usage.* The overview's servant type rests on Isa 50:6 (the cheek and the spitting, at 16:10 and 30:10). This is the first verbal contact with the second Servant Song. `[T]`

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* Isa 49:4 and 50:8–9 belong together: the Servant who says "my justice is with the LORD" goes on to say "He who vindicates Me is near; who will contend with Me?" (50:8, NASB95). Both are the courtroom of Job 9 seen from the other side. `[I]`

*What it adds.* The Servant says what Job says, "I toiled in vain", and then what Job cannot say: "yet my justice is with the LORD". The servant category is already the overview's type for Job (God's "My servant", 1:8; 2:3; 42:7–8). Here the verbal contact runs to Isaiah's own Servant. `[I]` *Moderate–high on the words; the typological weight is carried under Christological Reading.*

### 12. Genre

**Hebrew poetry: a disputation speech that imagines a lawsuit, contains a hymn, and turns into a prayer of complaint.** `[T]`
- **Embedded forms.**
  - *A hymn in participles (9:5–10).* The form of Amos's doxologies and the Psalter's creation hymns.
  - *A trial scene, imagined (9:14–24, 32–33).* The vocabulary of the court: contend, answer, judge, summon, justify, condemn, acquit, umpire, witness.
  - *A complaint addressed to God (10:2–22).* The shape of the Psalter's complaints (Ps 39; 88): questions, "Remember" (10:9), a plea to be let alone.
- **Reading rules.**
  - It is character speech, and the speaker will be both rebuked (38:2; 40:8) and vindicated (42:7). Report it as what Job says, never as the book's doctrine.
  - It is poetry: God is not literally a lion (10:16), nor does he literally blindfold judges (9:24). The images carry the speaker's experience.
  - It is argument by agreement: Job concedes the friends' premise (9:2) and draws the opposite conclusion.
- **What the genre does not license.**
  - It does not teach that God destroys the blameless and the wicked indifferently (9:22).
  - It does not teach that God is unjust (9:24).
  - It reports them as the accusations of a man who will not let go of God. `[I]`

### 13. Copycat

| Speech act | Category | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Conceding that no one can win against God (9:2–4) | **Descriptive**; true as far as it goes | The friends say it; Job says it; God will say it differently (40:2) |
| Singing God's power as terror (9:5–13) | **Descriptive** | Honest, not a model hymn |
| Accusing God of injustice (9:22–24) | **Descriptive — not normative** | God rebukes it at 40:8 ("Will you condemn Me that you may be justified?") |
| Holding to his integrity (9:20–21) | **Normative pattern, with the Copycat caveat** | Job refuses to confess what he did not do. God affirms his integrity (2:3) and his speech (42:7). Not a licence for self-righteousness: Job is not claiming sinlessness (10:14, "If I sin …") |
| Longing for an umpire (9:33) | **Normative longing** | The longing the gospel meets (Christological Reading) |
| Speaking to God (10:2–22) | **Normative pattern** | "I will say to God" — the direction of speech the book vindicates (42:7; introductory backbone) |
| Wishing he had died at birth (10:18–19) | **Descriptive — a pastoral landmine** | As in chapter 3, now addressed to God. See Difficult Verses |

**Common copycat misreading.** "Job said God was unfair, so we may say anything we like." The book commends the direction of Job's speech, to God, and his refusal to lie about himself. It does not commend every sentence: God asks, "Will you condemn Me that you may be justified?" (40:8). `[I]`

**Where the reader sits.** This side of the cross. `[I]`
- **The umpire has come.** "There is one God, and one mediator (μεσίτης) also between God and men, the man Christ Jesus, who gave Himself as a ransom for all" (1 Tim 2:5–6). He is "not a man as I am" in that he is God, and yet a man as we are, able to lay his hand on both.
- **The court has a verdict.** "Who will bring a charge against God's elect? God is the one who justifies (δικαιῶν); who is the one who condemns (κατακρινῶν)? Christ Jesus is He who died … who also intercedes for us" (Rom 8:33–34). Job's "Do not condemn me" (10:2) has its answer.
- **The Maker has had "eyes of flesh".** "We do not have a high priest who cannot sympathize with our weaknesses, but One who has been tempted in all things as we are, yet without sin" (Heb 4:15). And "in the days of His flesh, He offered up both prayers and supplications with loud crying and tears to the One able to save Him from death, and He was heard because of His piety" (Heb 5:7).

**How it reframes the reading.** The Christian may not preach 9:33 as though no umpire had come. But the Christian must hear Job's cry as the need the umpire meets, and must not answer it as the friends did, with a doctrine. `[I]`

### 15. Who Am I?

| Speaker / figure | Function |
|---|---|
| **Job** | The **righteous sufferer** who cannot get a hearing. **Representative** of every believer who feels God as opponent. **Example** of speech held on to God and turned towards him, and of refusing to confess what one has not done. **Not** an example of the accusation in 9:22–24. His longing runs to Christ (Christological Reading) |
| **God** | Spoken of as the overwhelming Creator (9) and spoken to as the Maker who is also the hunter (10). Silent. When he speaks, he takes up these words (40:2, 5, 8) |
| **The umpire** (9:33) | Absent. The one Job describes and cannot find. The New Testament names him (1 Tim 2:5) |
| **The friends** | Present and unaddressed. Their doctrine is conceded (9:2) and turned. They will quote this speech back as evidence against Job (15:6; 25:4) |
| **The reader** | Knows the prologue: the "without cause" (2:3), the "hand" (1:12; 2:6), the "spare his life" (2:6). Hears the words Job does not know he is echoing |

**The "Job-is-me" trap here cuts two ways.** A congregation may identify too quickly ("God is unfair to me, too") and take the accusations as licence. Or it may distance itself ("I would never speak to God like that") and miss the prayer. The text invites the suffering to recognise themselves in the man shut out of court, and it invites everyone to recognise the need for an umpire. `[I]`

### 16. So What?

**Stage 1 — the author's intended response.** To feel, with Job, the impossibility of standing in the right before God by argument, and the need for someone to stand between. To hear that such a man may turn his complaint into speech to God. And to learn not to answer him as the friends will (15:6; 25:4). `[T]` / `[I]`

**Stage 2 — worldview, behaviour, motivation.**

| Domain | Application |
|---|---|
| **Worldview** | No one wins a case against God by argument (9:2–3); even Job concedes it. God is Creator both of the stars and of the child in the womb. The book holds together what Job cannot: the God who made us with care and the God who seems to hunt us. Both halves of Isa 44:24 are true, and they meet in the Redeemer |
| **Stop** | Quoting 9:22–24 as doctrine, or silencing it. Answering a sufferer's "Do not condemn me" with "your own mouth condemns you" (15:6). Treating the hymn of 9:5–10 as praise to lift out of its context |
| **Start** | Speaking to God, not only about him, even in accusation (10:2). Praying the Psalter's complaints, Ps 39 above all, as the church's vocabulary for this. Pointing the accused to the Mediator, not to their own record |
| **Motivation** | Not "be as honest as Job". God has answered the lawsuit Job could not bring: the Mediator has laid his hand on both, and "God is the one who justifies" (Rom 8:33) |

**Four audiences.**
- **For me:** Do I talk about God, or to him, when he seems against me? Where do I look for someone to stand between?
- **For a Christian friend:** When someone says "God is against me", listen first. Then do not argue God's case (13:7), but point them to the one who pleads theirs.
- **For the church:** Our prayers need Ps 39 and Job 10 as well as Ps 23. Our preaching needs 1 Tim 2:5 as the answer to Job 9:33, not a defence of God against the sufferer.
- **For an unbeliever:** The Bible admits the problem: "How can a man be in the right before God?" (9:2). It does not answer with a better argument. It answers with a Mediator.

**Prayer.**
- Thanks that God has provided the umpire Job could not find.
- Confession of the times we have answered the suffering as the friends did.
- A request for the courage to say "I will say to God".
- Mercy for those who feel God has become their opponent.

**Grammatical and syntactical features.**
- **Seven participles in a row (9:5–10).** הַמַּעְתִּיק ("who removes"), הַמַּרְגִּיז ("who shakes"), הָאֹמֵר ("who commands"), נֹטֶה ("who stretches out"), דּוֹרֵךְ ("and treads"), עֹשֶׂה ("who makes") ×2 (WLC morphology). The hymnic participle is the grammar of doxology — Amos 4:13; 5:8; Isa 44:24; Ps 104:2–4. `[T]`
- **The concessive conditionals (9:15, 16, 20, 30).** "If I were right … if I called … if I am righteous … if I washed". In each the protasis states a good case and the apodosis loses it. `[T]`
- **The shift of person.** Third person about God (9:2–27), a sudden second person (9:28, 31), third person again (9:32–35), then sustained second person (10:2–18). No second person plural anywhere. `[T]` (WLC morphology)
- **9:19 — "who will summon me?"** יוֹעִידֵנִי has a first-person suffix. The English versions, following the Greek and the Syriac, have "him". Read as it stands, Job voices God's own challenge (Jer 49:19; 50:44). `[T]` for the form; `[I]` for the reading.
- **9:33 — לֹא יֵשׁ ("there is not").** The negative of existence in Hebrew is normally אֵין. The WLC lemma index never has לֹא immediately before יֵשׁ; the only "לא/לו + יֵשׁ" sequences are Num 22:29, Job 9:33 and Job 16:4. The index itself tags 9:33 as לוּ ("would that") (see Textual Variants). `[T]`
- **10:20 — ketiv and qere.** Ketiv יֶחְדָּל יָשִׁית ("Will He not cease, let Him set …"), third person. Qere וַחֲדָל וְשִׁית ("then cease! and set …"), imperatives to God. The qere keeps the address of 10:2–18 going to the end; the ketiv lets it fall back to "He". ESV follows the qere; NASB95 has the ketiv's third person with an imperative. `[T]`

**Wordplay and sound.**
- **עַצְּבֹתָי / עִצְּבוּנִי ("my pains" / "fashioned me", 9:28; 10:8).** Two lemmas, one set of consonants, either side of the chapter division. *Moderate*; recorded, not relied on.
- **אֲדַבְּרָה … אֲדַבְּרָה ("I would speak … I will speak", 9:35; 10:1).** The hinge, in sound as in sense. `[T]`
- *Assonance of 9:21:* *tām-ʾānî lōʾ-ʾēdaʿ napšî ʾemʾas ḥayyāy* — three short clauses in the first person, ending in "my life". The claim, the confusion, the despair. `[T]` for the forms; `[I]` for the effect.

### Textual Variants

**Variation inside the Hebrew** (BHS apparatus for chapters 9–10, *as BHS prints it*):
- **9:33 לֹא ("not") or לוּ ("would that").**
  - *The issue:* "There is no umpire between us" or "Would that there were an umpire between us".
  - *Evidence:* BHS: *nonn Mss* ("some manuscripts"), the Greek and the Syriac read לוּ, cross-referring 16:4. Swete: εἴθε ("would that"). Grammar: לֹא is not otherwise used directly before יֵשׁ in the Hebrew Bible, while לוּ יֵשׁ ("would that there were") occurs at Num 22:29 and in Job's own mouth at 16:4 (WLC; positive control 518 → יֵשׁ returned 21). The WLC's own lemma index tags 9:33 as לוּ.
  - *Most likely sense:* the consonants are the same, since לוּא is sometimes spelled with א. The question is the vowels. The grammar favours the wish; the Masoretic vowels give the denial.
  - *Preaching implication:* real. The ESV and NASB95 give a denial; NIV84 a wish. Either way Job has no umpire. The difference is between despair ("there is none") and longing ("if only there were"). Say which the pulpit text has.

### Historical and Cultural Background

**Setting.** The story's world is patriarchal and outside Israel (overview). The composition date is `[S]` and not needed here.

**What the original audience knew.**
1. **The lawsuit.** Israel knew the procedure of the gate: two parties, a judge, witnesses, a verdict of "in the right" or "guilty" (Deut 25:1). The prophets used the form of God's lawsuit with Israel (רִיב, Mic 6:1–2; Isa 1:18). Job turns it round: the man brings the suit against God. `[T]` for the texts; `[I]` for the reversal.
2. **The "umpire".** In an ancient dispute, a third party of standing could hear both sides and "lay a hand" on each, to bring them to terms `[S]`. The gesture is otherwise unattested in this form; the phrase יָשֵׁת יָדוֹ עַל ("lay his hand on") is unique to 9:33 (phrase search). *Moderate.*
3. **The constellations.** The Bear, Orion and the Pleiades were the markers of the seasons in the ancient Near East; "the chambers of the south" are probably the southern sky `[S]`. Job and Amos name them as God's handiwork, not as powers. *Moderate.*
4. **Rahab and her helpers.** The chaos-monster whose defeat the Psalter and Isaiah celebrate (Isa 51:9; Ps 89:11 [Eng 89:10]). The "helpers" (9:13) may recall the monster's allies in older combat myths `[S]`. The canon uses her as a figure of powers subject to God. `[T]` for the canon.
5. **Snow and lye.** Snow-water and lye (בֹר, "potash") were the strongest cleansers known (Jer 2:22) `[S]`. Job imagines the most thorough washing possible, and it fails.
6. **Milk and cheese.** An ancient picture of conception and gestation: the seed poured like milk and set like cheese `[S]`. The same idea stands in Wisdom 7:2 `[S]`. *Moderate.*

**What this changes.** Job is not merely complaining. He is imagining, in the form his world knew, a case against God. He finds that the form fails, because the defendant is also the judge and has no equal. The longing for an umpire is a longing for the procedure itself to be possible. `[I]`

### Original Audience Reception

**Canonical audience.** Readers of the Writings who have the Torah and the Prophets behind them, who have just read the Psalter, and who live with the exile's question of whether God's people can be in the right before him (Isa 40:27; 49:4). `[I]`

**Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances.**
- **Shocking:** "He destroys the guiltless and the wicked … He mocks the despair of the innocent … If it is not He, then who is it?" (9:22–24). The canonical reader knows Deut 25:1 and Prov 17:15. Job charges God with what the law calls an abomination. `[T]`
- **Surprising:** he says it to God (10:2). The man who has just accused God does not walk away from him; he turns and speaks to him. `[T]`
- **Comforting:** the words are the Psalter's (Ps 39) and the Servant's (Isa 49:4). A believer may pray this. `[I]`
- **Disturbing:** there is no umpire (9:33), and the chapter ends in the land of darkness (10:22). `[T]`
- **Ironic:** the reader knows the "hand" into which the earth was given (1:12; 2:6) and the words "without cause" and "swallow" (2:3). Job does not. `[I]`

**What we bring that they didn't.**
- A court culture in which the judge is independent of the parties.
- A therapeutic instinct that hears the speech as "venting".
- A doctrinal instinct that hears it as blasphemy.
- For the Christian, knowledge of the Mediator — which can make 9:33 sound like a riddle already solved rather than a cry.

### Biblical-Theological Themes

**(1) The lawsuit and justification.**
- **Before:** the judge's duty (Deut 25:1); the judicial abomination (Prov 17:15); God's lawsuit with his people (Mic 6:1–2); Eliphaz's "Can mankind be just before God?" (4:17).
- **After:**
  - God's reply, "Will you condemn Me that you may be justified?" (40:8);
  - the Servant, "He who vindicates Me is near; who will contend with Me?" (Isa 50:8);
  - "God is the one who justifies; who is the one who condemns?" (Rom 8:33–34);
  - "justifies the ungodly" (Rom 4:5).
- **What it contributes:** Job states the problem the gospel solves: no one can be in the right before God by pleading. Job's answer is to hold to his integrity and cry for an umpire. The gospel's answer is a verdict given, not won.
- **Confidence:** high.

**(2) The Mediator.**
- **Before:** Moses standing between God and the people (Exod 32:30–32; 33:12–23).
- **After:**
  - within Job: 16:19–21; 19:25; 33:23;
  - "one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus" (1 Tim 2:5);
  - "Jesus, the mediator of a new covenant" (Heb 12:24);
  - "an Advocate with the Father" (1 John 2:1).
- **What it contributes:** 9:33 is the first cry in the book's mediator line. It names the two requirements that Christ meets: someone who can lay his hand on both God and man. It is also the first cry in the canon for such a figure in so many words.
- **Confidence:** high for the trajectory.

**(3) The Maker and the made.**
- **Before:** Gen 2:7; 3:19 (dust); Ps 139:13–16 (knit in the womb).
- **After:** Isa 44:24 ("your Redeemer, and the one who formed you from the womb"); Isa 45:9 (the clay and the potter); Rom 9:20–21; the new creation (2 Cor 5:17).
- **What it contributes:** Job asks why the Maker would unmake what he made so carefully (10:8–9). The canon's answer is that the Maker is also the Redeemer. Job reaches the word at 19:25.
- **Confidence:** moderate–high.

**Relation.** *Climactic convergence.* The Maker (3) is the Judge (1), and the only way to stand before him is through the Mediator (2). The three meet in Christ, the one through whom all things were made (John 1:3), who justifies (Rom 8:33–34) and who stands between (1 Tim 2:5). `[I]`

### Christological Reading

**Type of connection:** trajectory (the mediator); contrast (the self-cleansing that fails); and, through the Greek, a New Testament use of the vocabulary of 9:8–11.

**How the passage points to Christ.**
- **The umpire who lays his hand on both.**
  - Job wants someone who is not God's inferior and not Job's stranger: "He is not a man as I am … There is no umpire between us, who may lay his hand upon us both" (9:32–33).
  - The New Testament names "one mediator (μεσίτης) also between God and men, the man Christ Jesus, who gave Himself as a ransom for all" (1 Tim 2:5–6). Swete's 9:33 has the same noun, its only occurrence in the Greek Old Testament.
  - *Tests:*
    - category passes (covenant mediation);
    - NT precedent is lexical and strong for the category (1 Tim 2:5; Heb 8:6; 9:15; 12:24), though no NT text cites Job 9:33;
    - escalation passes (Christ is God and man, not merely a third party);
    - authorial pattern passes (the longing grows through 16:19; 19:25; 33:23).
  - *High for the trajectory; not a claim that Job foresaw Christ.*
- **"Have You eyes of flesh?" (10:4–5).**
  - Job asks it as an absurdity: God does not see as a man sees, and his days are not a man's days.
  - The incarnation makes the absurdity true: "the Word became flesh" (John 1:14). "We do not have a high priest who cannot sympathize with our weaknesses" (Heb 4:15). In the days of his flesh he "offered up both prayers and supplications with loud crying and tears" (Heb 5:7).
  - *Moderate* — a canonical reflection.
- **"Do not condemn me" (10:2) and the verdict given.**
  - Job pleads not to be condemned and fears his own mouth will condemn him (9:20).
  - "God is the one who justifies; who is the one who condemns? Christ Jesus is He who died … who also intercedes for us" (Rom 8:33–34).
  - *Trajectory; moderate–high* (Rom 8:33–34 draws on Isa 50:8–9 `[S]`, the Servant's own courtroom).
- **The one who walks on the sea and passes by.**
  - Job: God "tramples down the waves of the sea" (9:8) — Swete, "walking on the sea as on dry ground" — and "were He to pass by me, I would not see Him" (9:11), Swete "if He passes by (παρέλθῃ) me".
  - Exod 34:6: "the LORD passed by", Swete παρῆλθεν.
  - Mark 6:48–50: Jesus comes "walking on the sea; and He intended to pass by (παρελθεῖν) them … 'Take courage; it is I (ἐγώ εἰμι), do not be afraid.'"
  - What Job said only God does and no one sees, the disciples see Jesus do, and they hear the Name.
  - *Category 3 (Greek); moderate–high.* The Mark–Job link is noted by commentators `[S]`; the verbal data are `[T]`.
- **The Servant who toiled in vain.** "I have toiled in vain … yet surely the justice due to Me is with the LORD" (Isa 49:4). Job says the first half (9:29) and cannot say the second. The Servant says both, and is the one the New Testament names (Acts 13:47 quotes Isa 49:6). *Moderate.*
- **Washing that cannot cleanse, and washing that can.**
  - "If I should wash myself with snow … You would plunge me into the pit" (9:30–31). "Wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow" (Ps 51:9 [Eng 51:7]).
  - Job cannot make himself clean in God's sight. The New Testament answer is a washing given: "the blood of Jesus His Son cleanses us from all sin" (1 John 1:7).
  - *Contrast; moderate.* `[unverified — 1 John 1:7 quoted from recall]`
- **Into the darkness, and out of it.**
  - Job goes "to the land of darkness and deep shadow" (10:21).
  - Isaiah's people "who walk in darkness … who live in a dark land (צַלְמָוֶת)" see a great light (Isa 9:1 [Eng 9:2]). Matthew's Greek is "in the land and shadow of death (σκιᾷ θανάτου) … a light dawned" (Matt 4:16); Luke 1:79 speaks of "those who sit in darkness and the shadow of death".
  - *Trajectory; moderate.* Job's Greek does not have σκιὰ θανάτου at 10:21, so the link runs through the Hebrew pair.

**Trajectory.**
- **What the passage longs for:** an umpire; a hearing; to be declared in the right; the rod removed; to be left alone; light.
- **What Christ fulfils:** the Mediator who is God and man (1 Tim 2:5); the verdict "justified" given, not won (Rom 8:33); the high priest with eyes of flesh (Heb 4:15); the one who walks on the sea and says "It is I" (Mark 6:50); the light in the land of deep darkness (Matt 4:16).
- **What still remains:** the last removal of the rod and the end of night (Rev 21:4; 22:5).

**Moralism check.**
- **The "be like X" temptation:** "Be honest with God, like Job" as a technique. Or, "Don't be like Job, who accused God."
- **The gospel grounding:** the umpire Job lacked has come, and in him God is both the one who judges and the one who justifies.
- **Christ as hero:** not a better arguer with God, but the one who stands between and lays his hand on both.

**Confidence.** *High* on the mediator trajectory; *moderate* on the incarnation reflection, the washing contrast and the light; *moderate–high* on Mark 6:48–50 through the Greek.

### Difficult / Contested Verses

**9:22–24 — "He destroys the guiltless and the wicked … He mocks the despair of the innocent … If it is not He, then who is it?"**
- **Category:** doctrinal / ethical — an accusation of God.
- **The difficulty:** the Bible puts in a righteous man's mouth the claim that God is indifferent to innocence and laughs at the suffering of the innocent.
- **Rhetorical function in context:** the climax of the imagined trial (9:14–24). It is the logical end of Job's premise that suffering proves God's hostility. It is spoken about God, not yet to him.
- **Common mishandlings:**
  - quoting it as the Bible's teaching;
  - skipping it;
  - rebuking Job from the pulpit as though God had not answered him himself.
- **Honest exegesis:**
  - God answers the accusation in its own words: "Will you really annul My judgment? Will you condemn Me that you may be justified?" (40:8). He rebukes Job's words "without knowledge" (38:2).
  - Yet God says Job, not the friends, spoke what is right (42:7). The book holds both.
  - The reader knows that 9:24's "hand of the wicked" is not God's but the Accuser's, under permission (1:12; 2:6) — and that 42:11 still says the evil was what "the LORD had brought on him".
- **Handling:** read it aloud; name it as accusation; point to 40:8 and 42:7 together. Do not resolve the tension by blaming Satan (Unit 1 backbone, Keep Honest 2).

**9:33 — denial or wish?** See Textual Variants. **Handling:** say which the pulpit text has, and do not build on the difference.

**9:20 — "my mouth will condemn me".**
- **Category:** doctrinal.
- **The difficulty:** is Job confessing sin?
- **Honest exegesis:** no. The conditional is concessive: "though I am righteous, my mouth will condemn me". In a court where God is judge and opponent, even Job's true plea would be turned against him. 10:7 says it outright: "According to Your knowledge I am indeed not guilty". Job does not claim sinlessness ("If I sin", 10:14); he denies the charge that his suffering implies.
- **Handling:** do not preach 9:20 as Job's confession. Eliphaz will do that (15:6).

**10:8–12 — the making of the child in the womb.**
- **Category:** pastoral and weaponised — a text used on the value of unborn life.
- **Rhetorical function in context:** the premise of an accusation — why would God make me so carefully only to destroy me?
- **Honest exegesis:** the text does affirm God's intimate making of the unborn (with Ps 139:13–16), and that use is legitimate. But in Job 10 the point is the contradiction Job feels between God's care and God's hostility.
- **Handling:** preach the passage's point; if the verses are used for the other, say so and cite Ps 139 alongside.

**10:18–22 — "Would that I had died."**
- **Category:** pastoral, as in chapter 3.
- **Honest exegesis:** now addressed to God (10:18), and ending in a plea for a little respite before death (10:20), not for death. It is the psalmist's plea (Ps 39:14 [Eng 39:13]).
- **Handling:** as for 3:20–22 (Job 3 report). Name the longing; commend speaking it to God; know where help is.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: Lifting the hymn out of its context

- **What it looks like:** 9:8–10 used as a call to worship — "He alone stretches out the heavens … who does great things, unfathomable".
- **Why it's wrong:** the hymn is sung through clenched teeth. Its point in context is that such power cannot be resisted or called to account (9:3–4, 11–14). Job is not praising; he is despairing of a hearing.
- **The corrective:** if 9:5–10 is read, read 9:2–4 and 9:11–14 around it. The same words are praise in Amos 5:8 and Isa 44:24; in Job they are dread. Let the congregation hear the difference.

### Pitfall: Hearing Job confess his sin (9:20, 28–31)

- **What it looks like:** "Even Job admits, 'my mouth would condemn me' … 'I am accounted wicked' … no one can make himself clean."
- **Why it's wrong:** the conditionals are concessive: "though I am righteous …". 10:7 says, "According to Your knowledge I am indeed not guilty". God himself called Job blameless (1:8; 2:3). The one who turns 9:20 into a confession is Eliphaz (15:6), and God rebukes him (42:7).
- **The corrective:** preach 9:20–31 as the despair of an innocent man in a court he cannot win. The gospel truth that no one can cleanse himself (Ps 51) may be drawn from 9:30–31, but not as Job's confession of guilt.

### Pitfall: Quoting the accusation as doctrine — or silencing it

- **What it looks like:** either "God destroys the blameless and the wicked alike" (9:22) presented as biblical teaching, or the verses skipped as embarrassing.
- **Why it's wrong:** the book includes them, God answers them (40:8), and God commends Job's speech (42:7). Neither quoting nor skipping honours that.
- **The corrective:** read them, name them as Job's accusation, and hold 40:8 and 42:7 together.

### Pitfall: Preaching 9:33 as though no one had ever needed an umpire

- **What it looks like:** "Job said there was no umpire — but we have one! 1 Tim 2:5!" in the first two minutes, and the cry is never felt.
- **Why it's wrong:** the book makes the reader wait: 16:19, 19:25 and 33:23 grow the longing, and the answer is canonical, not in Job. The gospel lands only on those who have felt the need.
- **The corrective:** let the congregation stand where Job stands — accused, unheard, with no one between. Then name the Mediator. Also, say which reading the pulpit text gives: denial (ESV, NASB95) or wish (NIV84).

### Pitfall: Answering the speech as the friends did

- **What it looks like:** the sermon replies to Job with doctrine — "Job was wrong to say God is unjust" — and moves on.
- **Why it's wrong:** that is what Eliphaz and Bildad do. They quote this speech back as evidence against him (15:6; 25:4), and God condemns them (42:7). God's own reply comes from the storm and takes up Job's words (40:2, 5, 8), without the friends' formula.
- **The corrective:** answer as God did — with the Creator's self-disclosure and, for us, with the Mediator — and let Job's words stand as the words of a man who turned to God.
