## Scope

**Single sermon on 15:1–21:34: the second round of the dialogue.** The series plan fixes one sermon for this unit. It runs to six speeches and 186 verses, so the decision needs a reason.

**Why one sermon is the right decision.**
- **"Comfort" brackets the round.** Eliphaz opens with "Are the comforts of God too small for you?" (15:11, ESV). Job closes with "Keep listening to my words, and let this be your comfort" (21:2) and "How then will you comfort me with empty nothings?" (21:34, ESV). The noun תַּנְחוּמִים ("consolations") occurs in Job only at 15:11 and 21:2 (WLC, lemma). Job's "miserable comforters are you all" (16:2) and his last line (21:34) use the verb נחם ("comfort"), the friends' purpose in coming (2:11). `[T]`
- **The round has two stories told across one another.** The friends paint three portraits of "the wicked man" (15:20–35; 18:5–21; 20:5–29). In between, Job makes his two great appeals: the witness and surety (16–17) and the Redeemer (19). Then he answers the portraits (21). Split the round, and the contrast between accusation and confession is lost. `[T]` for the speeches; `[I]` for the contrast.
- **The conversation contracts across the round** (Headline 1).
  - **The friends' address to Job.** It falls from 40, 17 and 33 verses in round one to 29, 4 and 1 in round two (WLC morphology). The friends stop talking to Job and talk about the wicked man.
  - **Job's address to God.** It ends at 17:4. Chapter 19 has no second-person singular at all.
  - Only the whole round shows this. `[T]`

**The text marks the unit.** BHS sets setumot after 15:35, 17:16, 18:21 and 21:34, and a petuḥah after 20:29. It sets no marker after 19:29. The round ends where Eliphaz begins his third speech (22:1). `[T]`

**If the series is ever re-planned.** The fallback is the overview's three units: 15–17 ("My witness is in heaven", HIGH), 18–19 ("I know that my Redeemer lives", HIGH) and 20–21 ("Why do the wicked prosper?"). Each would then need to name the comfort bracket.

**Series context.** By this point the congregation has heard:
- the question (1:9), the verdict (42:7) and the "two ways of speaking", in the introductory sermon;
- the councils and the friends' seven silent days (2:13), in Sermon 1;
- the curse on the day, in Sermon 2;
- the first round, in Sermon 3. It ended on the longing "You would call, and I would answer you" (14:15) and the charge "you destroy the hope of man" (14:19, ESV).

Sermon 3 promised that the hope thread runs on: "where then is my hope?" (17:15, ESV); "my hope has he pulled up like a tree" (19:10, ESV). It also promised that the longing for a mediator grows: "There is no arbiter" (9:33) → "my witness is in heaven" (16:19) → "I know that my Redeemer lives" (19:25). This sermon keeps both promises. `[T]` for the words.

**Where the series question stands.** *"Is God worth it — even if he gives you nothing?"*
- **The friends' answer.** In this round the friends no longer offer Job his life back, as they did in round one (8:5–7; 11:15–19). They describe the wicked man's end in the colours of Job's own losses: fire, the tent, no survivor, no children, the skin devoured.
  - The commentators read this as a veiled accusation. Konkel–Longman (127), as the Research Assistant summarises them, say that Bildad describes the wicked in the exact terms of Job's situation. `[S]`
  - The losses are read as the verdict.
- **Job's answer.** With everything gone, and every friend and relative gone with it (19:13–19), Job confesses what he still has: "I know that my Redeemer lives". What he longs for is not his goods but to see God: "whom I shall see for myself, and my eyes shall behold, and not another" (19:27, ESV). `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the link to 1:9.
- **What it shows.** That is fearing God "for nothing" at its most exposed.

**Reading.** Do not read the round. Read two passages, about four minutes in all, and quote the rest in the movements.
- **16:18–17:3:** "O earth, cover not my blood" to "who is there who will put up security for me?" (the witness and the surety).
- **19:21–27:** "Have mercy on me, have mercy on me, O you my friends" to "My heart faints within me!" (the plea and the confession).

Quote 15:11 and 21:34 at the start, so the comfort bracket is in the congregation's ears before the readings.

## Fallen Condition Focus

**When our suffering is read as a verdict against us — by our circumstances, by the people who should comfort us, and by our own fears — we fear that no one in heaven will speak for us. And when others suffer, we reach for portraits and proverbs instead of pity.**

**Where it comes from.**
- **The first half** is the 16–17 dig's candidate FCF ("When our suffering seems to testify against us and those around us agree, we fear that heaven is empty of anyone who will speak for us — and that even God is against us"), shortened. The report rates it "anchored in the text": the two witnesses (16:8, 19), the comforters (16:2) and the surety (17:3).
- **The second half** is the consolidation report's So What: "Stop comforting with portraits of what happens to the wicked; quoting true proverbs as verdicts; talking about the sufferer instead of to him".
- The "we" is inferred. `[I]`

**What the first hearers felt.** The canon's own language of judgement turned on an innocent man.
- **The friends speak Scripture accurately.**
  - Bildad's "the light of the wicked is put out" (18:5, ESV) is the proverb of Prov 13:9 and 24:20.
  - Zophar's wicked man "will suck the poison of cobras" (20:16, ESV), in the words the Song of Moses uses of Israel's enemies (Deut 32:33). `[S: audit 3]`: *high (words)*.
  - Bildad's man with "no posterity or progeny" (18:19, ESV) uses the rare pair of Isaiah's taunt over Babylon's king (Isa 14:22). `[S: audit 3]`: *moderate*.
- **They apply it to Job.** A reader of the prologue knows that fire fell (1:16), that a house fell on the children (1:19), that one servant alone escaped each disaster (1:15–19), and that Job's skin was struck (2:7). That reader hears the portraits as Job's own story under another name. `[T]` for the prologue's words; `[I]` for the hearing.

**What they shared with us, and what differs.**
- **Shared:** the hostile "witness" of circumstance (16:8); comforters who explain instead of pitying (19:21–22); and the fear that no one will speak for us.
- **Differs:** we know the witness and advocate by name, "an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous" (1 John 2:1, ESV), and the guarantor: "This makes Jesus the guarantor of a better covenant" (Heb 7:22, ESV). We know that the Redeemer who "lives" said, "I died, and behold I am alive forevermore" (Rev 1:18, ESV). `[T]` for the texts.

## Teaching Point

**When suffering is read as a verdict, comfort does not come from "the comforts of God" the friends offered — portraits of the wicked man's end, drawn in the colours of Job's losses. It comes from the living Redeemer whom Job confesses in the middle of their accusations. He is the witness in heaven who argues a man's case with God (16:19–21), the surety God himself must give (17:3), and the Redeemer who lives and will stand at the last upon the dust (19:25). God has given that Redeemer in Christ, who was "numbered with the transgressors" and is "the living one". So the accused sufferer may hope in him, and the church may comfort with pity, not with portraits.**

- **Subject:** When suffering is read as a verdict against us, where does comfort come from?
- **Complement:** It does not come from a doctrine that reads God's judgement off a person's losses (15:11; 18:21; 21:34). It comes from the living Redeemer, who speaks for the accused with God and stands at the last (16:19; 17:3; 19:25). God gives him in Christ (Luke 22:37; Rev 1:17–18; 1 John 2:1).

A shorter form a hearer could carry home: *"When your life is read as a verdict, your Redeemer lives."*

**Built from** the consolidation report's convergent findings, with the two solo digs.
- **Headline 1:** the round contracts, and "comfort" brackets it.
- **Headline 2,** reframed by claim audit 3 and the Logos round: the portraits describe Job's own losses. The commentators read this as the friends' insinuation; the author's lexical design is not shown.
- **Headline 3:** each friend turns Job's own words against him. Zophar turns even the witness and the Redeemer: "The heavens will reveal his iniquity, and the earth will rise up against him" (20:27, ESV), against 16:19 and 19:25.
- **Headline 5:** the round's other story, the witness, the surety and the Redeemer, from the 16–17 and 19 digs.
- **The 19 dig's Headline 1:** "know". Bildad's last line consigns Job to "him who knows not God" (18:21, ESV). Job answers "know then" (19:6) and "I know that my Redeemer lives" (19:25).
- **Headline 4,** reframed: Job 21 answers the portraits with pointed rejoinders, and closes the comfort bracket.

**Why the TP makes God the subject.**
- **The easy versions put a human at the centre.** "Don't be like Job's friends" makes the sermon a lecture on bad counsel. "Have faith like Job" makes his confession a technique. `[I]`
- **The round's own centre is a confession about God.** "I know that my Redeemer lives" names who the Redeemer is ("lives", "the last", "God"), not how strongly Job believes. `[T]`
- **The good news is that the Redeemer lives and stands at the last.** It is not that comforters should be kinder, or that sufferers should be braver. God has given that Redeemer in Christ. `[I]`

## Purpose Statement

**To move hearers who feel their suffering has been read as a verdict to hope in the living Redeemer who speaks for them with God — and to move the church from comforting with explanations to comforting with pity, because Christ, counted among the wicked, is our advocate and lives.**

Differentiated by hearer:
- **The sufferer who feels accused** — by circumstances, by others or by their own conscience. Job's "witness" rose against him: "my leanness has risen up against me; it testifies to my face" (16:8, ESV). The Hebrew word for "leanness" otherwise means "lie" (Logos round 6), and it is a lying witness. Your circumstances are not God's verdict. "My witness is in heaven" (16:19). Christ is "an advocate with the Father" (1 John 2:1).
- **The one who has lost everyone.** "He has put my brothers far from me, and those who knew me are wholly estranged from me" (19:13, ESV). At the bottom of that list Job says "I know". The one who knows you is the Redeemer who lives, and you will see him "for yourself … and not another" (19:27).
- **The comforter — most of us.** Do not describe the end of the wicked to the bereaved. Do not quote true proverbs as verdicts. Job asked for two things: "Have mercy on me, have mercy on me, O you my friends" (19:21, ESV) and "Keep listening to my words, and let this be your comfort" (21:2, ESV). At the end it was "all his brothers and sisters and all who had known him before" who came, "and they showed him sympathy and comforted him" (42:11, ESV).
- **The sceptic,** who thinks believers must pretend the world is fair. Here the Bible itself asks, "Why do the wicked live, reach old age, and grow mighty in power?" (21:7, ESV). It sets that question beside its most famous confession of hope. The hope is not spoken from outside the worst. It is spoken from inside it.

The motive throughout is **the living Redeemer**: Christ counted among the wicked, raised, and interceding. It is not the moral that the friends were cruel, nor that Job was brave. `[I]`

## Candidate Big Question

**"When your life is read as a verdict against you, who will speak for you?"**

A longer form for the introduction: *"When everyone who knew you reads your suffering as proof that you deserved it, is there anyone in heaven on your side?"*

A form for the application to the church: *"When someone is suffering, will you hand them a portrait — or pity?"*

*Candidate.* The preacher settles the final wording.
- **How it applies the series question.** The series asks whether God is worth it even if he gives nothing (1:9). This sermon asks it at the point where Job has nothing, and even his losses are used against him. The round's answer is that he has a Redeemer, and wants to see God.
- **The answer from the round:** "My witness is in heaven … I know that my Redeemer lives."
- **The answer from the book:** God gives his verdict, "my servant Job" (42:7–8, ESV), and Job's brothers and sisters come and comfort him (42:11).

## Proposed Structure

Four movements and a landing. The structure follows the round's two stories, not the order of its six speeches.
- It begins with the friends' portraits (1).
- It moves to Job's two appeals: the witness and surety (2), then the Redeemer (3).
- It ends where the round ends, with Job answering the portraits and closing the comfort bracket (4).

Movement 1 gathers three speeches (15, 18 and 20). Do not preach them one by one (Keep Honest 1). The first four movements should take about four-fifths of the sermon.

### 1. "Are the comforts of God too small for you?" — the friends' portrait (15:1–35; 18:1–21; 20:1–29)

*The friends stop speaking to Job and paint the wicked man, in the colours of Job's losses.*
- **Eliphaz's offer (15:11).** "Are the comforts of God too small for you, or the word that deals gently with you?" (ESV). It is the round's first word of comfort, and the last offered to Job in the second person. `[T]`
- **The contraction.** Eliphaz still speaks to Job in 29 verses, Bildad in 4 and Zophar in 1. After that each friend speaks only of "the wicked man" (15:20; 18:5; 20:5, 29) (WLC morphology). `[T]` Say it plainly: by the end of the round, nobody is talking to Job.
- **The portrait, from the prologue's losses.**
  - **Fire.** The fire of 1:16 returns: "fire consumes the tents of bribery" (15:34, ESV); "a fire not fanned will devour him" (20:26, ESV).
  - **Children and survivor.** The lone survivors of 1:15–19 and the children of 1:18–19 return: "He has no posterity or progeny among his people, and no survivor where he used to live" (18:19, ESV).
  - **Skin.** The skin of 2:4–7 returns: "It consumes the parts of his skin" (18:13, ESV).
  - `[T]` for the verbal links. The commentators read the match as the friends' meaning: Konkel–Longman, Zuck and Andersen (Logos round 6) `[S]`. Claim audit 3 found that the words do not show the author built the portraits from the prologue `[S: audit 3]`. Preach it as the friends' insinuation, which a reader of chapters 1–2 cannot miss.
- **True Scripture as a verdict.**
  - Bildad: "the light of the wicked is put out … his lamp above him is put out" (18:5–6, ESV), the proverb of Prov 13:9 and 24:20. `[T]`
  - Zophar: "He will suck the poison of cobras" (20:16, ESV), the Song of Moses' "poison of cobras" (Deut 32:33), spoken of a people "whose vine comes from the vine of Sodom" (32:32). `[S: audit 3]`: *high (words)*.
  - Let the friends sound wise (Keep Honest 2).
- **The verdict pronounced (18:21; 20:29).** "Surely such are the dwellings of the unrighteous, such is the place of him who knows not God" (18:21, ESV). "This is the wicked man's portion from God" (20:29, ESV). `[T]`

*Hinge:* 15:11 against 18:21. "The comforts of God" are offered, and the place of "him who knows not God" is described. The friends' comfort has become a portrait of the damned.

*Function:* raise the FCF. Suffering is read as a verdict, and the comforters have stopped speaking to the sufferer. The congregation should recognise the friends' reflex in itself before it sees what is wrong with it.

### 2. "My witness is in heaven" — accused on earth, appealing to God against God (16:1–17:16)

*Job is accused by his own body and by his friends, and he appeals to a witness in heaven and asks God to stand surety for him with God.*
- **"Miserable comforters are you all" (16:2, ESV).** The verb of 2:11, "to comfort", turned. `[T]`
- **The hostile witness (16:8).** "And he has shriveled me up, which is a witness against me, and my leanness has risen up against me; it testifies to my face" (ESV).
  - עֵד ("witness") occurs in Job only at 10:17, 16:8 and 16:19 (WLC). `[T]`
  - The word for "leanness" (כַּחַשׁ) means "lie" in every other place it occurs (Hos 7:3; 10:13; 12:1; Nah 3:1; Ps 59:13). HALOT gives "lie" as an alternative here (Logos round 6). `[S]`
  - Job's wasted body testifies against him like a lying witness. The language is the court's: a witness who "rises up" against a man (Deut 19:16; Ps 27:12; 35:11). `[S: audit 3]`: *moderate–high* for the forensic reading.
- **God as assailant, in the words of Lamentations (16:9–16).**
  - "He has gnashed his teeth at me … He slashes open my kidneys and does not spare; he pours out my gall on the ground … I have sewed sackcloth upon my skin" (16:9, 13, 15, ESV).
  - Job speaks Jerusalem's lament as his own: "my bile is poured out to the ground" (Lam 2:11, ESV); "put on sackcloth" (Lam 2:10); "they gnash their teeth" (Lam 2:16). Delitzsch compares 16:13 with Lam 2:11 (Logos round 6). `[S: audit 3]`: *high (words)*; direction open.
  - Colour, not hinge: the congregation need only hear that Job prays the city's lament.
- **Innocent (16:17).** "Although there is no violence in my hands, and my prayer is pure" (ESV). `[T]`
- **The two witnesses (16:18–21).**
  - "O earth, cover not my blood" (16:18). Abel's blood cried from the ground (Gen 4:10).
  - "Even now, behold, my witness is in heaven, and he who testifies for me is on high … my eye pours out tears to God, that he would argue the case of a man with God" (16:19–21, ESV). `[T]`
  - The umpire Job said did not exist (9:33) now has a name, "witness", and a task: to "argue" (יכח), the verb of 9:33. `[T]`
- **The surety (17:3).** "Lay down a pledge for me with you; who is there who will put up security for me?" (ESV).
  - Job asks God to stand guarantor for him, with God.
  - Delitzsch: "God is conceived of as two persons … a judge who treats Job as one deserving of punishment … a bondsman who pledges himself for the innocence of the sufferer" (Logos round 6). `[S]`
  - The petition עָרְבֵנִי ("be my surety") is shared with Hezekiah's prayer (Isa 38:14) and Ps 119:122. `[S: audit 3]`: *moderate*.
- **Hope going down to the dust (17:13–16).** "Where then is my hope? Who will see my hope? Will it go down to the bars of Sheol? Shall we descend together into the dust?" (17:15–16, ESV). `[T]`

*Hinge:* 16:8 against 16:19. Two witnesses, one on earth, rising against him, and one in heaven, speaking for him. The ESV carries both.

*Function:* state the first half of the TP. The accused sufferer appeals past the verdict of circumstance to a witness in heaven, and even to God against God. Do not resolve the paradox; the book resolves it only at 38–42.

### 3. "I know that my Redeemer lives" — the confession from the bottom (19:1–29)

*Bildad consigns Job to "him who knows not God". Job, stripped of everyone, answers "I know".*
- **"How long?" (19:2–3).** "How long will you torment me and break me in pieces with words?" (19:2, ESV), answering Bildad's own "How long?" (18:2). `[T]`
- **"Know then" (19:6).** "Know then that God has put me in the wrong and closed his net about me" (ESV). Job answers Bildad's last word, "knows not God" (18:21), with Bildad's verb. ידע ("know") runs through the speech five times: 19:6, 13, 14, 25, 29 (WLC). `[T]`
- **The siege (19:7–12).** "Behold, I cry out, 'Violence!' but I am not answered … He has walled up my way, so that I cannot pass, and he has set darkness upon my paths … my hope has he pulled up like a tree" (19:7–8, 10, ESV).
  - The siege uses the words of Lam 3:7–9 ("He has walled me about … though I call and cry for help, he shuts out my prayer"). Schnittjer argues that Lamentations is the source and Job the borrower (Logos round 6) `[S]`. Claim audit 3 rates the core contact *high*. `[S: audit 3]`
  - The cry "Violence!" is Habakkuk's (Hab 1:2). `[S: audit 3]`: *moderate–high (words)*.
  - The hope thread from Sermon 3 runs on: the tree that would sprout again (14:7) is uprooted (19:10). `[T]`
- **Everyone gone (19:13–20).** Brothers, acquaintances, relatives, guests, maidservants, his servant, his wife, "the children of my own mother" and "even young children" (19:13–18, ESV). "All my intimate friends abhor me … My bones stick to my skin and to my flesh, and I have escaped by the skin of my teeth" (19:19–20, ESV). `[T]`
- **"Have mercy on me" (19:21–22).** "Have mercy on me, have mercy on me, O you my friends, for the hand of God has touched me! Why do you, like God, pursue me?" (ESV). Job pleads with his friends for pity and is pursued instead. `[T]`
- **Written for ever (19:23–24).** "Oh that my words were written! Oh that they were inscribed in a book! Oh that with an iron pen and lead they were engraved in the rock forever!" (ESV). The words echo Isa 30:8, "write it … inscribe it in a book … a witness forever". Barnes notes the link. `[S: audit 3]`: *high (words)*.
  - Job's wish is granted by the book that records it, and we are reading it. `[I]`
- **The confession (19:25–27).** "For I know that my Redeemer lives, and at the last he will stand upon the earth. And after my skin has been thus destroyed, yet in my flesh I shall see God, whom I shall see for myself, and my eyes shall behold, and not another. My heart faints within me!" (ESV).
  - **"Lives."** The Redeemer lives, while Job is dying. `[T]`
  - **"At the last."** אַחֲרוֹן ("last") stands with גֹּאֵל ("Redeemer") elsewhere only at Isa 44:6: "Thus says the LORD … and his Redeemer … 'I am the first and I am the last'". Delitzsch reads Job's word by Isa 44:6, 48:12 and 41:4, the divine title (Logos round 6). `[S: audit 3]`: *moderate–high (words)*; the Redeemer's identity is a reading `[S]`.
  - **"Upon the dust."** The Hebrew is עַל־עָפָר ("upon the dust"), the place where Job said he would lie (17:16) and where the dead lie (20:11; 21:26). The Redeemer stands over the grave. `[T]` See the pulpit note: the ESV's "earth" hides it.
  - **"Not another."** The stranger turned: זוּר ("be a stranger") ran through the list of the estranged (19:13, 15). At the end the God Job will see is not a stranger to him (19:27, וְלֹא־זָר). `[T]`
- **The warning (19:28–29).** "Be afraid of the sword … that you may know there is a judgment" (19:29, ESV). The fifth "know". `[T]`

*Hinge:* 18:21 against 19:25. "Him who knows not God" against "I know that my Redeemer lives". The ESV carries both "knows" and "know".

*Function:* state the centre of the TP. The confession is made at the bottom, not the top. It rests on who the Redeemer is, not on how Job feels. Keep 19:13–22 attached to it (Keep Honest 4).

### 4. "How often?" — Job answers the portraits (21:1–34)

*Job takes the friends' portraits one by one, sets the world as it is beside them, and closes the comfort bracket.*
- **"Let this be your comfort" (21:2).** "Keep listening to my words, and let this be your comfort" (ESV). The comfort Job asks for is listening. `[T]`
- **The question (21:7).** "Why do the wicked live, reach old age, and grow mighty in power?" (ESV). `[T]`
- **The portraits answered.**
  - **Children:** Bildad's man has "no posterity" (18:19), and Job's wicked have "offspring … established in their presence" (21:8, ESV).
  - **The rod:** "no rod of God is upon them" (21:9).
  - **Music:** timbrel and lyre, and they "go down to Sheol" (21:12–13).
  - `[T]` for each link.
- **"In peace" (21:13).** "They spend their days in prosperity, and in peace they go down to Sheol" (ESV).
  - Zophar had said "the joy of the godless [is] but for a moment" (20:5, ESV). Job's reply uses the same consonants, רֶגַע, read "in peace" (HALOT, BDB and the Greek; Logos round 6). `[S]`
  - Optional colour, and only in one sentence: Zophar gave the wicked a moment of joy, and Job sees them go down to the grave in peace.
- **The lamp (21:17).** "How often is it that the lamp of the wicked is put out?" (ESV).
  - It is Bildad's line (18:5–6) and the proverb's exact words (Prov 13:9; 24:20), turned into a question. `[T]`
  - Job does not say "never". He says "how often?" — not always, not on schedule. `[I]`
- **The grave (21:26, 32).** "They lie down alike in the dust" (21:26, ESV). The wicked man "is carried to the grave, watch is kept over his tomb" (21:32, ESV).
  - Eliphaz had promised the righteous man, "You shall come to your grave in ripe old age, like a sheaf gathered up in its season" (5:26, ESV).
  - The word for that sheaf, גָּדִישׁ ("stack of grain"), is the word for the wicked man's "tomb" here. It occurs in Job only at 5:26 and 21:32, and with "grave" only there (WLC; Logos round 6). The honoured end Eliphaz promised the righteous, Job sees given to the wicked. `[T]` *Moderate–high.*
- **"For you say" (21:28)** and **"empty nothings" (21:34).** "For you say, 'Where is the house of the prince?'" (ESV). "How then will you comfort me with empty nothings? There is nothing left of your answers but falsehood" (21:34, ESV). `[T]`

*Hinge:* 21:2 against 21:34, with 15:11 behind them. "Let this be your comfort" (listening) against "comfort me with empty nothings" (the portraits). The bracket closes on the word "falsehood", which God will confirm (42:7). `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the link to 42:7.

*Function:* dismantle the formula without replacing it with cynicism. Let the honest question stand ("Why do the wicked live?") beside the confession of movement 3. Hold two verses side by side: "They lie down alike in the dust" (21:26) and "at the last he will stand upon the dust" (19:25).

### Landing: the Redeemer who stood upon the dust (beyond the round)

*The round ends on "falsehood". The book ends with God's verdict, Job's prayer and true comfort. The canon ends with the Redeemer who lives.*
- **The verdict.** "My anger burns against you and against your two friends, for you have not spoken of me what is right, as my servant Job has" (42:7, ESV). The portrait of the wicked man was false. `[T]`
- **The prayer.** "The LORD restored the fortunes of Job, when he had prayed for his friends" (42:10, ESV). The man the friends painted as the wicked man prays for them. `[T]`
- **True comfort.** "Then came to him all his brothers and sisters and all who had known him before, and ate bread with him in his house. And they showed him sympathy and comforted him" (42:11, ESV). The brothers who were put "far from me" (19:13) come back, and those who "knew" him return (19:13 → 42:11, the only verses in Job with "brothers" and "those who know"; WLC). The comfort the friends never gave, the family gives. `[T]`
- **The Christ turn.** The Redeemer Job longed for has stood on the earth.
  - **He was counted with the wicked.** "This Scripture must be fulfilled in me: 'And he was numbered with the transgressors'" (Luke 22:37, ESV, citing Isa 53:12). The innocent one was painted as the wicked man, and died a criminal's death. Jesus applies the words to himself. Job does not prophesy him, and the Servant link in Job's own words is low `[S: audit 3]`. `[T]` for Luke; `[I]` for the comparison.
  - **He refused the friends' logic.** "Who sinned, this man or his parents?" — "It was not that this man sinned, or his parents" (John 9:2–3, ESV). Of those on whom the tower in Siloam fell: "do you think that they were worse offenders …? No, I tell you" (Luke 13:4–5, ESV). A building fell on Job's children (1:19). `[T]`
  - **He prayed for his accusers.** "Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do" (Luke 23:34, ESV), as Job prayed for his friends (42:10). `[I]`
  - **He lives.** "I am the first and the last, and the living one. I died, and behold I am alive forevermore" (Rev 1:17–18, ESV). The Redeemer who "lives", and is "the last" (19:25; Isa 44:6), has stood upon the dust and risen from it. `[I]` for the connection; *high on the trajectory* (overview).
  - **He speaks for us.** "We have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous" (1 John 2:1, ESV). "He always lives to make intercession" (Heb 7:25), as "the guarantor of a better covenant" (Heb 7:22, ESV): the witness in heaven, and the surety Job asked God to give. `[T]` for the texts.

So when your life is read as a verdict against you, your Redeemer lives. He has heard the false portrait and the true lament, and he speaks for you with God. And because he lives, the church can be what Job's friends were not: the brothers and sisters who come, sit, eat, listen and comfort.

*Function:* press the PS. End with the Redeemer, and then with comfort that listens. Do not end with a lecture on bad comforters. The church's application flows from Christ's advocacy and does not replace it.

## Keep Honest

1. **One sermon, not six speeches, and not three portraits and three rebuttals.**
   - The round's meaning is in its movement: the conversation contracting, the portraits growing, the appeals rising, and Job answering. A tour of "Eliphaz said … Job replied …" loses it (15–21 dig, Pitfall 1).
   - Gather the three portraits in one movement. Choose the verses each movement needs. The hinges are the skeleton: 15:11 with 18:21; 16:8 with 16:19; 18:21 with 19:25; 21:2 with 21:34. Everything else is optional.
2. **Quote the friends with their speaker, and let them sound wise.**
   - **Never offer the portraits as promises or warnings from God.** "The light of the wicked is put out" (18:5) and "the joy of the godless [is] but for a moment" (20:5) are true as proverbs (Prov 13:9; 24:20). They are false as verdicts on Job (42:7). Job answers them with "How often?" (21:17).
   - **Do not caricature the friends either.** They speak Proverbs, the Song of Moses and Isaiah accurately. Their error is application and pitilessness, and every congregation contains people who reason as they do.
3. **Handle the children with care: 18:19, 20:10 and 21:19.**
   - "He has no posterity or progeny" (18:19) is aimed at a father whose children are dead.
   - Job rejects the transfer of guilt to children (21:19).
   - Name the cruelty plainly, and be aware of who is in the room. This is the round's pastoral landmine, as 8:4 was in round one.
4. **Keep 19:25–27 attached to 19:13–22, and do not overstate it.**
   - **Do not lift the confession off the list.** It comes after everyone has left him and after a plea for pity that goes unheard. It ends in longing ("My heart faints within me!"), not repose (19 dig, Pitfall 1).
   - **Do not build bodily resurrection on מִבְּשָׂרִי ("from my flesh") alone, and do not deny any hope beyond death.** The Hebrew allows "in my flesh" (ESV text) and "without my flesh". Lange takes the second (Logos round 6) `[S]`. Preach what is clear: a living Redeemer, after Job's death, and Job seeing God himself. Let the canon supply the body (1 Cor 15).
   - **A trajectory, not a prediction.** Job does not foresee Jesus. He confesses a Redeemer whom the canon names. The identity of the גֹּאֵל in Job's mouth is contested: God himself (Delitzsch, Andersen, Lange), or a third party. The plan follows the God-himself reading, with Isa 44:6 behind it, and says so `[S]`.
   - **Chapter 19 is not a prayer.** It has no second-person singular. Job speaks of God, to the friends and to future readers. Do not present 19:25 as Job addressing God.
5. **"The wicked always prosper": not the conclusion, and do not answer 21:26 too fast.**
   - **Job's question is "how often?", not "never".** And he disowns "the counsel of the wicked" (21:16) even while he describes their prosperity.
   - **Hold 21:26 beside 19:25 and let the canon finish the thought.** "Until I went into the sanctuary of God; then I discerned their end" (Ps 73:17, ESV). Do not let Job 21 become the Bible's realism against naive faith, and leave it there.
6. **Hold each finding at the strength it has been measured at** (claim audit 3; Logos round 6).
   - **May bear weight:**
     - the contraction and the comfort bracket (counts in the Hebrew);
     - the five "know"s;
     - the two witnesses (עֵד only 10:17; 16:8, 19);
     - the confession's words;
     - 21:17's quotation of the proverb.
   - **Colour, verified on the words** (claim audit 3; Schnittjer for Lam 3, Delitzsch for Lam 2):
     - Lamentations at 16:9–16 and 19:7–8 (*high*);
     - Isa 30:8 at 19:23–24 (*high*);
     - Isa 44:6 at 19:25 (*moderate–high*);
     - Deut 32:33 at 20:16 (*high*);
     - Hab 1:2 at 19:7 (*moderate–high*);
     - 5:26 → 21:32 (*moderate–high*).
     Use them as colour, and never make one a hinge.
   - **Do not claim:**
     - **that the friends' portraits were designed from the prologue:** preach them as the friends' insinuation;
     - **that Job 21 answers "clause by clause":** it answers by pointed rejoinders;
     - **that the Servant pattern runs through Job 16** (*low*): use Luke 22:37 as Jesus' own words, not as Job's;
     - **that Isa 26:19 is quoted** (*low–moderate*): canonical reflection only;
     - **that Ps 22 is a source** (*low*);
     - **that Sodom stands behind 1:16 or 20:29** (dropped);
     - **any window rank.**

## Christ-centred check

1. **"Be like Job", or "trust the Christ whom Job points to"?**
   - The TP's subject is the source of comfort, and its complement makes the Redeemer the actor: he speaks, stands guarantor, lives and stands at the last.
   - Job is not held up as a model of faith to copy. His confession shows where comfort is found.
   - The friends are not held up only as a warning. Their failure shows a doctrine put in the Redeemer's place.
   - The application to the church (comfort with pity) flows from Christ's advocacy, not from a standard to meet. **Pass.**
2. **Is Christ the subject or merely an example?**
   - The landing presents Christ as the one counted with the wicked, the Advocate, the guarantor and the living one. He is not a better sufferer to imitate. His prayer for his accusers (Luke 23:34) is presented as what he did, not as a technique. **Pass.**
3. **Is the good news grounded in Christ's saving work?**
   - Yes: his death among transgressors (Luke 22:37), his resurrection ("I died, and behold I am alive forevermore", Rev 1:18), and his intercession (Heb 7:25; 1 John 2:1–2, "the propitiation for our sins"). **Pass.**
   - *Caution:* no New Testament text cites Job 15–21. The links run through the categories the round raises: the witness (16:19), the surety (17:3), the Redeemer who lives (19:25), and the innocent counted wicked. Present them as the canon's answer to Job's longing, not as the meaning of his words.

## Pulpit-text note (ESV)

Checked against the ESV export for Job 1–2, 5, 15–21 and 42, and for the parallels (Ps 73:17; Prov 13:9; 24:20; Lam 2:10–16; Lam 3:7–9). The New Testament quotations are checked against the ESV exports for Luke, John, Hebrews, 1 John and Revelation.

**Where the ESV helps the plan.**
- **The comfort bracket — the round's frame.** "The comforts of God" (15:11); "miserable comforters" (16:2); "let this be your comfort" (21:2); "How then will you comfort me" (21:34); "and comforted him" (42:11). **Every link is audible.**
- **"Know" — movement 3's hinge.** "Him who knows not God" (18:21) against "know then" (19:6), "those who knew me" (19:13), "I know that my Redeemer lives" (19:25) and "that you may know there is a judgment" (19:29). Four of the five are audible; 19:14 has "close friends" (Hebrew "those known to me").
- **The two witnesses — movement 2's hinge.** "Which is a witness against me … it testifies to my face" (16:8) against "my witness is in heaven, and he who testifies for me is on high" (16:19). **Audible.** The ESV's 16:21, "that he would argue the case of a man with God", makes the witness the one who argues. That suits the plan.
- **17:3, the surety.** "Lay down a pledge for me with you." The "with you" (Hebrew עִמָּךְ) that Delitzsch makes the point is kept.
- **The lamp.** "The light of the wicked is put out … his lamp above him is put out" (18:5–6) against "How often is it that the lamp of the wicked is put out?" (21:17). Prov 13:9 and 24:20: "the lamp of the wicked will be put out". **Audible across all three.**
- **The siege and Lamentations.** "He has walled up my way" (19:8) against "He has walled me about" (Lam 3:7). "He pours out my gall on the ground" (16:13) against "my bile is poured out to the ground" (Lam 2:11). Close enough to be heard if the parallels are read.
- **19:23–24 against Isa 30:8.** "Inscribed in a book … forever". Audible if Isaiah is read.
- **"Have mercy on me"** (19:21; NASB95 "Pity me") — the plea is audible either way.

**Where the congregation will not hear what the plan rests on.**
- **19:25, "upon the earth" — movement 3 and the landing.** The ESV has "at the last he will stand upon the earth". The Hebrew is עַל־עָפָר ("upon the dust"), the word of 17:16 ("into the dust") and 21:26 ("in the dust"), the place of the dead. The NASB95 also has "on the earth"; the NIV84 has "upon the earth", with a margin "upon my grave".
  - **Supply it:** "The word Job uses is 'dust' — the dust he expected to lie in. The Redeemer stands over the grave."
  - The landing's "stood upon the dust" depends on this sentence.
- **19:26, "in my flesh".** The ESV text takes one side of a disputed preposition. The NASB95 has "from my flesh", and the NIV84 "in my flesh" with a margin "apart from my flesh". Do not build on "in" (Keep Honest 4).
- **19:27, "My heart faints within me!"** The Hebrew is "my kidneys". It is the only other כִּלְיָה ("kidney") in Job besides 16:13, "He slashes open my kidneys". The kidneys God slashed are the kidneys that long for him. Optional colour; supply it if used.
- **21:13, "in peace".** This is the lexicon's reading (HALOT, BDB) and the Greek's. **Do not correct it.** The play on Zophar's "but for a moment" (20:5) is in the Hebrew only (both are רֶגַע). The NASB95 has "suddenly".
- **21:32 against 5:26 — "tomb" and "sheaf".** The ESV's "a sheaf gathered up" (5:26) and "his tomb" (21:32) translate one word, גָּדִישׁ. **Supply it** if the link is used: "The word for Eliphaz's sheaf is the word for the wicked man's tomb."
- **16:8, "my leanness".** The word otherwise means "lie". Supply it if the "lying witness" is used: "The word for 'leanness' is the word the prophets use for 'lies'."
- **20:23, "into his body".** The ESV follows HALOT ("flesh, body"). The NASB95's "while he is eating" is not what the congregation will hear. The plan does not use the verse.
- **20:27 against 16:19 and 19:25.** Zophar's "The heavens will reveal his iniquity, and the earth will rise up against him" turns Job's witness in heaven and his Redeemer who will "stand". The ESV's "rise up" and "stand" hide that the Hebrew verb is the same (קום). By accident, the ESV's "earth" at 19:25 lets "earth" be heard in both verses. Optional colour.
- **19:6, "God has put me in the wrong".** The NASB95 has "God has wronged me". The ESV is softer, but the charge is still clear. No change needed.

**For the NIV84, if the sermon is preached elsewhere.**
- **It carries the comfort bracket** ("God's consolations" at 15:11; "miserable comforters" at 16:2; "the consolation you give me" at 21:2; "console me" at 21:34).
- **It weakens 17:3:** "Give me, O God, the pledge you demand". The "with you" is lost.
- **At 16:20 it follows a conjecture:** "My intercessor is my friend". Say so if it is read.
- **At 18:15 it adopts BHS's "fire" conjecture:** "Fire resides in his tent".
- **At 19:25 it has "Redeemer" with the margin "defender", and "upon the earth" with "upon my grave".** Its margin supplies the dust note.
- **At 21:13 it has "in peace"** (margin "in an instant").
