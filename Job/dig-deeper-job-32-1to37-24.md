### Prose introduction and Elihu's first speech, to the friends (32:1–22)

*Petuḥah (פ) after 32:1 and after 32:5 in BHS.*

**32:1** וַֽיִּשְׁבְּת֡וּ שְׁלֹ֤שֶׁת הָאֲנָשִׁ֣ים הָ֭אֵלֶּהᵃ מֵעֲנֹ֣ות אֶת־אִיֹּ֑וב כִּ֤י ה֖וּא צַדִּ֣יק בְּעֵינָֽיוᵇ׃ פ  
Then these three men ceased answering Job, because he was righteous in his own eyes.

**32:2** וַיִּ֤חַר אַ֨ף׀ אֱלִיה֣וּא בֶן־בַּרַכְאֵ֣ל הַבּוּזִי֮ מִמִּשְׁפַּ֪חַ֫ת רָ֥ם בְּ֭אִיֹּוב חָרָ֣ה אַפֹּ֑ו עַֽל־צַדְּקֹ֥ו נַ֝פְשֹׁ֗ו מֵאֱלֹהִֽים׃  
But the anger of Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite, of the family of Ram burned; against Job his anger burned because he justified himself before God.

**32:3** וּבִשְׁלֹ֣שֶׁת רֵעָיו֮ חָרָ֪ה אַ֫פֹּ֥ו עַ֤ל אֲשֶׁ֣ר לֹא־מָצְא֣וּ מַעֲנֶ֑ה וַ֝יַּרְשִׁ֗יעוּ אֶת־אִיֹּֽובᵃ׃  
And his anger burned against his three friends because they had found no answer, and yet had condemned Job.

**32:4** וֶֽאֱלִיה֗וּ חִכָּ֣ה אֶת־אִ֭יֹּוב בִּדְבָרִ֑ים כִּ֤י זְֽקֵנִים־הֵ֖מָּה מִמֶּ֣נּוּ לְיָמִֽים׃  
Now Elihu had waited to speak to Job because they were years older than he.

**32:5** וַיַּ֤רְא אֱלִיה֗וּא כִּ֘י אֵ֤ין מַעֲנֶ֗ה בְּ֭פִי שְׁלֹ֥שֶׁת הָאֲנָשִׁ֗ים וַיִּ֥חַר אַפֹּֽו׃ פ  
And when Elihu saw that there was no answer in the mouth of the three men his anger burned.

**32:6** וַיַּ֤עַן׀ אֱלִיה֖וּא בֶן־בַּֽרַכְאֵ֥ל הַבּוּזִ֗י וַיֹּ֫אמַ֥ר צָ֘עִ֤יר אֲנִ֣י לְ֭יָמִים וְאַתֶּ֣ם יְשִׁישִׁ֑ים עַל־כֵּ֖ן זָחַ֥לְתִּי וָֽאִירָ֓א׀ מֵחַוֹּ֖ת דֵּעִ֣י אֶתְכֶֽם׃  
So Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite spoke out and said, / I am young in years and you are old; / Therefore I was shy and afraid to tell you what I think.

**32:7** אָ֭מַרְתִּי יָמִ֣ים יְדַבֵּ֑רוּ וְרֹ֥ב שָׁ֝נִ֗ים יֹדִ֥יעוּ חָכְמָֽה׃  
I thought age should speak, / And increased years should teach wisdom.

**32:8** אָ֭כֵן רֽוּחַ־הִ֣יא בֶאֱנֹ֑ושׁ וְנִשְׁמַ֖ת שַׁדַּ֣י תְּבִינֵֽם׃  
But it is a spirit in man, / And the breath of the Almighty gives them understanding.

**32:9** לֹֽא־רַבִּ֥יםᵃ יֶחְכָּ֑מוּ וּ֝זְקֵנִ֗ים יָבִ֥ינוּ מִשְׁפָּֽט׃  
The abundant in years may not be wise, / Nor may elders understand justice.

**32:10** לָכֵ֣ן אָ֭מַרְתִּי שִׁמְעָהᵃ־לִּ֑י ᵇאֲחַוֶּ֖ה דֵּעִ֣י אַף־אָֽנִיᵇ׃  
So I say, 'Listen to me, / I too will tell what I think.'

**32:11** הֵ֤ן הֹוחַ֨לְתִּי לְֽדִבְרֵיכֶ֗ם אָ֭זִיןᵃ עַד־תְּב֥וּנֹֽתֵיכֶ֑םᵇ עַֽד־תַּחְקְר֥וּן מִלִּֽין׃  
Behold, I waited for your words, / I listened to your reasonings, / While you pondered what to say.

**32:12** וְעָֽדֵיכֶ֗ם אֶתְבֹּ֫ונָ֥ן וְהִנֵּ֤ה אֵ֣ין לְאִיֹּ֣וב מֹוכִ֑יחַ עֹונֶ֖ה אֲמָרָ֣יו מִכֶּֽם׃  
I even paid close attention to you; / Indeed, there was no one who refuted Job, / Not one of you who answered his words.

**32:13** פֶּן־תֹּ֣֭אמְרוּ מָצָ֣אנוּ חָכְמָ֑ה אֵ֖ל יִדְּפֶ֣נּוּᵃ לֹא־אִֽישׁ׃  
Do not say, / 'We have found wisdom; / God will rout him, not man.'

**32:14** וְלֹא־עָרַ֣ךְ אֵלַ֣י מִלִּ֑ין וּ֝בְאִמְרֵיכֶ֗ם לֹ֣א אֲשִׁיבֶֽנּוּ׃  
For he has not arranged his words against me, / Nor will I reply to him with your arguments.

**32:15** חַ֭תּוּ לֹא־עָ֣נוּ עֹ֑וד הֶעְתִּ֖יקוּ מֵהֶ֣ם מִלִּֽים׃  
They are dismayed, they no longer answer; / Words have failed them.

**32:16** וְ֭הֹוחַלְתִּי כִּי־לֹ֣א יְדַבֵּ֑רוּ כִּ֥י עָ֝מְד֗וּ לֹא־עָ֥נוּ עֹֽוד׃  
Shall I wait, because they do not speak, / Because they stop and no longer answer?

**32:17** אַעֲנֶ֣הᵃ אַף־אֲנִ֣י חֶלְקִ֑י אֲחַוֶּ֖ה דֵעִ֣י אַף־אָֽנִי׃  
I too will answer my share, / I also will tell my opinion.

**32:18** כִּ֭י מָלֵ֣תִיᵃ מִלִּ֑ים הֱ֝צִיקַ֗תְנִי ר֣וּחַ בִּטְנִֽי׃  
For I am full of words; / The spirit within me constrains me.

**32:19** הִנֵּֽה־בִטְנִ֗י כְּיַ֥יִן לֹא־יִפָּתֵ֑חַ כְּאֹבֹ֥ות חֲ֝דָשִׁ֗ים יִבָּקֵֽעַ׃  
Behold, my belly is like unvented wine, / Like new wineskins it is about to burst.

**32:20** אֲדַבְּרָ֥ה וְיִֽרְוַֽח־לִ֑י אֶפְתַּ֖ח שְׂפָתַ֣י וְאֶֽעֱנֶֽה׃  
Let me speak that I may get relief; / Let me open my lips and answer.

**32:21** אַל־נָ֭א אֶשָּׂ֣א פְנֵי־אִ֑ישׁ וְאֶל־אָ֝דָ֗ם לֹ֣א אֲכַנֶּֽה׃  
Let me now be partial to no one, / Nor flatter any man.

**32:22** כִּ֤י לֹ֣א יָדַ֣עְתִּי אֲכַנֶּ֑ה כִּ֝מְעַ֗ט יִשָּׂאֵ֥נִי עֹשֵֽׂנִי׃  
For I do not know how to flatter, / Else my Maker would soon take me away.

### The first speech, to Job (33:1–33)

*Setumah (ס) after 33:33 in BHS.*

**33:1** וְֽאוּלָ֗ם שְׁמַֽע־נָ֣א אִיֹּ֣וב מִלָּ֑י וְֽכָל־דְּבָרַ֥י הַאֲזִֽינָה׃  
However now, Job, please hear my speech, / And listen to all my words.

**33:2** הִנֵּה־נָ֭א פָּתַ֣חְתִּי פִ֑י דִּבְּרָ֖ה לְשֹׁונִ֣י בְחִכִּֽי׃  
Behold now, I open my mouth, / My tongue in my mouth speaks.

**33:3** יֹֽשֶׁר־לִבִּ֥י אֲמָרָ֑י וְדַ֥עַת שְׂ֝פָתַ֗י בָּר֥וּר מִלֵּֽלוּ׃  
My words are from the uprightness of my heart, / And my lips speak knowledge sincerely.

**33:4** רֽוּחַ־אֵ֥ל עָשָׂ֑תְנִי וְנִשְׁמַ֖ת שַׁדַּ֣י תְּחַיֵּֽנִי׃  
The Spirit of God has made me, / And the breath of the Almighty gives me life.

**33:5** אִם־תּוּכַ֥ל הֲשִׁיבֵ֑נִי עֶרְכָ֥ה לְ֝פָנַ֗י הִתְיַצָּֽבָה׃  
Refute me if you can; / Array yourselves before me, take your stand.

**33:6** הֵן־אֲנִ֣י כְפִ֣יךָ לָאֵ֑ל מֵ֝חֹ֗מֶר קֹרַ֥צְתִּי גַם־אָֽנִי׃  
Behold, I belong to God like you; / I too have been formed out of the clay.

**33:7** הִנֵּ֣ה אֵ֭מָתִי לֹ֣א תְבַעֲתֶ֑ךָּ וְ֝אַכְפִּ֗י עָלֶ֥יךָ לֹא־יִכְבָּֽד׃  
Behold, no fear of me should terrify you, / Nor should my pressure weigh heavily on you.

**33:8** אַ֭ךְ אָמַ֣רְתָּ בְאָזְנָ֑י וְקֹ֖ול מִלִּ֣ין אֶשְׁמָֽע׃  
Surely you have spoken in my hearing, / And I have heard the sound of your words:

**33:9** זַ֥ךְ אֲנִ֗י בְּֽלִ֫י פָ֥שַׁע חַ֥ף אָנֹכִ֑י וְלֹ֖א עָוֹ֣ן לִֽי׃  
'I am pure, without transgression; / I am innocent and there is no guilt in me.

**33:10** הֵ֣ן תְּ֭נוּאֹות עָלַ֣י יִמְצָ֑א יַחְשְׁבֵ֖נִי לְאֹויֵ֣ב לֹֽו׃  
'Behold, He invents pretexts against me; / He counts me as His enemy.

**33:11** יָשֵׂ֣םᵃ בַּסַּ֣ד רַגְלָ֑י יִ֝שְׁמֹ֗ר כָּל־אָרְחֹתָֽי׃  
'He puts my feet in the stocks; / He watches all my paths.'

**33:12** הֶן־זֹ֣את לֹא־צָדַ֣קְתָּ אֶעֱנֶ֑ךָּ כִּֽי־יִרְבֶּ֥ה אֱ֝לֹ֗והַᵃ מֵאֱנֹֽושׁ׃  
Behold, let me tell you, you are not right in this, / For God is greater than man.

**33:13** מַ֭דּוּעַ אֵלָ֣יו רִיבֹ֑ותָ כִּ֥י כָל־דְּ֝בָרָ֗יו לֹ֣א־יַעֲנֶֽה׃  
Why do you complain against Him / That He does not give an account of all His doings?

**33:14** כִּֽי־בְאַחַ֥ת יְדַבֶּר־אֵ֑ל וּ֝בִשְׁתַּ֗יִם לֹ֣א יְשׁוּרֶֽנָּה׃  
Indeed God speaks once, / Or twice, yet no one notices it.

**33:15** בַּחֲלֹ֤ום׀ חֶזְיֹ֬וןᵃ לַ֗יְלָה בִּנְפֹ֣ל תַּ֭רְדֵּמָה עַל־אֲנָשִׁ֑ים בִּ֝תְנוּמֹ֗ות עֲלֵ֣י מִשְׁכָּֽב׃  
In a dream, a vision of the night, / When sound sleep falls on men, / While they slumber in their beds,

**33:16** אָ֣ז יִ֭גְלֶה אֹ֣זֶן אֲנָשִׁ֑ים וּבְמֹ֖סָרָ֣םᵃ יַחְתֹּֽםᵇ׃  
Then He opens the ears of men, / And seals their instruction,

**33:17** לְ֭הָסִיר אָדָ֣ם מַעֲשֶׂ֑הᵃ וְגֵוָ֖ה מִגֶּ֣בֶר יְכַסֶּֽהᵇ׃  
That He may turn man aside from his conduct, / And keep man from pride;

**33:18** יַחְשֹׂ֣ךְ נַ֭פְשֹׁו מִנִּי־שָׁ֑חַת וְ֝חַיָּתֹ֗ו מֵעֲבֹ֥ר בַּשָּֽׁלַח׃  
He keeps back his soul from the pit, / And his life from passing over into Sheol.

**33:19** וְהוּכַ֣ח בְּ֭מַכְאֹוב עַל־מִשְׁכָּבֹ֑ו וְרֹ֖יבᵃ עֲצָמָ֣יו אֵתָֽן׃  
Man is also chastened with pain on his bed, / And with unceasing complaint in his bones;

**33:20** וְזִֽהֲמַ֣תּוּ חַיָּתֹ֣ו לָ֑חֶם וְ֝נַפְשֹׁ֗ו מַאֲכַ֥ל תַּאֲוָֽה׃  
So that his life loathes bread, / And his soul favorite food.

**33:21** יִ֣כֶל בְּשָׂרֹ֣ו מֵרֹ֑אִי וְשֻׁפּ֥יּᵃ עַ֝צְמֹותָ֗יו לֹ֣א רֻאּֽוּ׃  
His flesh wastes away from sight, / And his bones which were not seen stick out.

**33:22** וַתִּקְרַ֣ב לַשַּׁ֣חַת נַפְשֹׁ֑ו וְ֝חַיָּתֹ֗ו לַֽמְמִתִֽיםᵃ׃  
Then his soul draws near to the pit, / And his life to those who bring death.

**33:23** אִם־יֵ֤שׁ עָלָ֨יו׀ מַלְאָ֗ךְ מֵלִ֗יץ אֶחָ֥ד מִנִּי־אָ֑לֶף לְהַגִּ֖יד לְאָדָ֣ם יָשְׁרֹֽו׃  
If there is an angel as mediator for him, / One out of a thousand, / To remind a man what is right for him,

**33:24** וַיְחֻנֶּ֗נּוּ וַיֹּ֗אמֶר פְּ֭דָעֵהוּᵃ מֵרֶ֥דֶת שָׁ֗חַת מָצָ֥אתִי כֹֽפֶר׃  
Then let him be gracious to him, and say, / 'Deliver him from going down to the pit, / I have found a ransom';

**33:25** רֻֽטֲפַ֣שׁᵃ בְּשָׂרֹ֣ו מִנֹּ֑עַר יָ֝שׁ֗וּב לִימֵ֥י עֲלוּמָֽיו׃  
Let his flesh become fresher than in youth, / Let him return to the days of his youthful vigor;

**33:26** יֶעְתַּ֤ר אֶל־אֱלֹ֨והַּ׀ וַיִּרְצֵ֗הוּ וַיַּ֣רְא פָּ֭נָיו בִּתְרוּעָ֑ה וַיָּ֥שֶׁב לֶ֝אֱנֹ֗ושׁ צִדְקָתֹֽו׃  
Then he will pray to God, and He will accept him, / That he may see His face with joy, / And He may restore His righteousness to man.

**33:27** יָשֹׁ֤רᵃ׀ עַל־אֲנָשִׁ֗ים וַיֹּ֗אמֶר חָ֭טָאתִי וְיָשָׁ֥ר הֶעֱוֵ֗יתִי וְלֹא־שָׁ֥וָה לִֽי׃  
He will sing to men and say, / 'I have sinned and perverted what is right, / And it is not proper for me.

**33:28** פָּדָ֣ה נַ֭פְשֹׁיᵃ מֵעֲבֹ֣ר בַּשָּׁ֑חַתᵇ וְ֝חַיָּתֹיᶜ בָּאֹ֥ור תִּרְאֶֽה׃  
'He has redeemed my soul from going to the pit, / And my life shall see the light.'

**33:29** הֶן־כָּל־אֵ֭לֶּה יִפְעַל־אֵ֑ל פַּעֲמַ֖יִם שָׁלֹ֣ושׁ עִם־גָּֽבֶר׃  
Behold, God does all these oftentimes with men,

**33:30** לְהָשִׁ֣יב נַ֭פְשֹׁו מִנִּי־שָׁ֑חַת לֵ֝אֹ֗ורᵃ בְּאֹ֣ור הַֽחַיִּיםᵇ׃  
To bring back his soul from the pit, / That he may be enlightened with the light of life.

**33:31** הַקְשֵׁ֖ב אִיֹּ֥וב שְֽׁמַֽע־לִ֑י הַ֝חֲרֵ֗שׁ וְאָנֹכִ֥י אֲדַבֵּֽר׃  
Pay attention, O Job, listen to me; / Keep silent, and let me speak.

**33:32** אִם־יֵשׁ־מִלִּ֥ין הֲשִׁיבֵ֑נִי דַּ֝בֵּ֗ר כִּֽי־חָפַ֥צְתִּי צַדְּקֶֽךָּ׃  
Then if you have anything to say, answer me; / Speak, for I desire to justify you.

**33:33** אִם־אַ֭יִן אַתָּ֥ה שְֽׁמַֽע־לִ֑י הַ֝חֲרֵ֗שׁ וַאֲאַלֶּפְךָ֥ חָכְמָֽה׃ ס  
If not, listen to me; / Keep silent, and I will teach you wisdom.

### The second speech (34:1–37)

*Setumah (ס) after 34:37 in BHS.*

**34:1** וַיַּ֥עַן אֱלִיה֗וּא וַיֹּאמַֽר׃  
Then Elihu continued and said,

**34:2** שִׁמְע֣וּ חֲכָמִ֣ים מִלָּ֑י וְ֝יֹדְעִ֗ים הַאֲזִ֥ינוּ לִֽי׃  
Hear my words, you wise men, / And listen to me, you who know.

**34:3** כִּי־אֹ֭זֶן מִלִּ֣ין תִּבְחָ֑ן וְ֝חֵ֗ךְ יִטְעַ֥ם לֶאֱכֹֽלᵃ׃  
For the ear tests words / As the palate tastes food.

**34:4** מִשְׁפָּ֥ט נִבְחֲרָה־לָּ֑נוּ נֵדְעָ֖ה בֵינֵ֣ינוּ מַה־טֹּֽוב׃  
Let us choose for ourselves what is right; / Let us know among ourselves what is good.

**34:5** כִּֽי־אָ֭מַר אִיֹּ֣וב צָדַ֑קְתִּי וְ֝אֵ֗ל הֵסִ֥יר מִשְׁפָּטִֽי׃  
For Job has said, 'I am righteous, / But God has taken away my right;

**34:6** עַל־מִשְׁפָּטִ֥י אֲכַזֵּ֑ב אָנ֖וּשׁ חִצִּ֣י בְלִי־פָֽשַׁע׃  
Should I lie concerning my right? / My wound is incurable, though I am without transgression.'

**34:7** מִי־גֶ֥בֶר כְּאִיֹּ֑וב יִֽשְׁתֶּה־לַּ֥עַג כַּמָּֽיִם׃  
What man is like Job, / Who drinks up derision like water,

**34:8** וְאָרַ֣ח לְ֭חֶבְרָה עִם־פֹּ֣עֲלֵי אָ֑וֶן וְ֝לָלֶ֗כֶת עִם־אַנְשֵׁי־רֶֽשַׁע׃  
Who goes in company with the workers of iniquity, / And walks with wicked men?

**34:9** כִּֽי־אָ֭מַר לֹ֣א יִסְכָּן־גָּ֑בֶר בִּ֝רְצֹתֹ֗ו עִם־אֱלֹהִֽים׃  
For he has said, 'It profits a man nothing / When he is pleased with God.'

**34:10** לָכֵ֤ן׀ אַ֥נֲשֵׁ֥י לֵבָ֗ב שִׁמְע֫וּ לִ֥י חָלִ֖לָה לָאֵ֥ל מֵרֶ֗שַׁע וְשַׁדַּ֥י מֵעָֽוֶל׃  
Therefore, listen to me, you men of understanding. / Far be it from God to do wickedness, / And from the Almighty to do wrong.

**34:11** כִּ֤י פֹ֣עַלᵃ אָ֭דָם יְשַׁלֶּם־לֹ֑ו וּֽכְאֹ֥רַח אִ֝֗ישׁ יַמְצִאֶֽנּוּ׃  
For He pays a man according to his work, / And makes him find it according to his way.

**34:12** אַף־אָמְנָ֗ם אֵ֥ל לֹֽא־יַרְשִׁ֑יעַ וְ֝שַׁדַּ֗י לֹֽא־יְעַוֵּ֥ת מִשְׁפָּֽט׃  
Surely, God will not act wickedly, / And the Almighty will not pervert justice.

**34:13** מִֽי־פָקַ֣ד עָלָ֣יו אָ֑רְצָהᵃ וּמִ֥י שָׂ֝֗ם תֵּבֵ֥ל כֻּלָּֽהּ׃  
Who gave Him authority over the earth? / And who has laid on Him the whole world?

**34:14** אִם־יָשִׂ֣ים אֵלָ֣יו לִבֹּ֑ו רוּחֹ֥ו וְ֝נִשְׁמָתֹ֗ו אֵלָ֥יו יֶאֱסֹֽף׃  
If He should determine to do so, / If He should gather to Himself His spirit and His breath,

**34:15** יִגְוַ֣ע כָּל־בָּשָׂ֣ר יָ֑חַד וְ֝אָדָ֗ם עַל־עָפָ֥ר יָשֽׁוּב׃  
All flesh would perish together, / And man would return to dust.

**34:16** וְאִם־בִּ֥ינָהᵃ שִׁמְעָה־זֹּ֑את הַ֝אֲזִ֗ינָה לְקֹ֣ול מִלָּֽי׃  
But if you have understanding, hear this; / Listen to the sound of my words.

**34:17** הַאַ֬ף שֹׂונֵ֣א מִשְׁפָּ֣ט יַחֲבֹ֑ושׁ וְאִם־צַדִּ֖יק כַּבִּ֣יר תַּרְשִֽׁיעַᵃ׃  
Shall one who hates justice rule? / And will you condemn the righteous mighty One,

**34:18** הַאֲמֹ֣רᵃ לְמֶ֣לֶךְ בְּלִיָּ֑עַל רָ֝שָׁ֗ע אֶל־נְדִיבִֽים׃  
Who says to a king, 'Worthless one,' / To nobles, 'Wicked ones';

**34:19** אֲשֶׁ֤ר לֹֽא־נָשָׂ֨א׀ פְּנֵ֥י שָׂרִ֗ים וְלֹ֣א נִכַּר־שֹׁ֖ועַ לִפְנֵי־דָ֑ל כִּֽי־מַעֲשֵׂ֖ה יָדָ֣יו כֻּלָּֽם׃  
Who shows no partiality to princes / Nor regards the rich above the poor, / For they all are the work of His hands?

**34:20** רֶ֤גַע׀ יָמֻתוּ֮ וַחֲצֹ֪ותᵃ לָ֥יְלָה ᵇיְגֹעֲשׁ֣וּ עָ֣םᵇ וְיַעֲבֹ֑רוּ וְיָסִ֥ירוּᶜ אַ֝בִּ֗ירᵈ לֹ֣א בְיָֽד׃  
In a moment they die, and at midnight / People are shaken and pass away, / And the mighty are taken away without a hand.

**34:21** כִּי־עֵ֭ינָיו עַל־דַּרְכֵי־אִ֑ישׁ וְֽכָל־צְעָדָ֥יו יִרְאֶֽה׃  
For His eyes are upon the ways of a man, / And He sees all his steps.

**34:22** אֵֽין־חֹ֖שֶׁךְ וְאֵ֣ין צַלְמָ֑וֶת לְהִסָּ֥תֶר שָׁ֝֗ם פֹּ֣עֲלֵי אָֽוֶן׃  
There is no darkness or deep shadow / Where the workers of iniquity may hide themselves.

**34:23** כִּ֤י לֹ֣א עַל־אִ֭ישׁ יָשִׂ֣ים עֹ֑ודᵃ לַהֲלֹ֥ךְ אֶל־אֵ֝֗ל בַּמִּשְׁפָּֽט׃  
For He does not need to consider a man further, / That he should go before God in judgment.

**34:24** יָרֹ֣עַ כַּבִּירִ֣ים לֹא־חֵ֑קֶר וַיַּעֲמֵ֖דᵃ אֲחֵרִ֣ים תַּחְתָּֽם׃  
He breaks in pieces mighty men without inquiry, / And sets others in their place.

**34:25** לָכֵ֗ן יַ֭כִּיר מַעְבָּֽדֵיהֶ֑ם וְהָ֥פַךְ לַ֝֗יְלָה וְיִדַּכָּֽאוּ׃  
Therefore He knows their works, / And He overthrows them in the night, / And they are crushed.

**34:26** תַּֽחַת־רְשָׁעִ֥ים סְפָקָ֗םᵃ בִּמְקֹ֥ום רֹאִֽים׃  
He strikes them like the wicked / In a public place,

**34:27** אֲשֶׁ֣ר עַל־כֵּ֭ן סָ֣רוּ מֵֽאַחֲרָ֑יו וְכָל־דְּ֝רָכָ֗יו לֹ֣א הִשְׂכִּֽילוּ׃  
Because they turned aside from following Him, / And had no regard for any of His ways;

**34:28** לְהָבִ֣יא עָ֭לָיוᵃ צַֽעֲקַת־דָּ֑ל וְצַעֲקַ֖ת עֲנִיִּ֣ים יִשְׁמָֽע׃  
So that they caused the cry of the poor to come to Him, / And that He might hear the cry of the afflicted—

**34:29** וְה֤וּא יַשְׁקִ֨טᵃ׀ וּמִ֥י יַרְשִׁ֗עַ וְיַסְתֵּ֣ר פָּ֭נִים וּמִ֣י יְשׁוּרֶ֑נּוּ וְעַל־גֹּ֖וי וְעַל־אָדָ֣ם יָֽחַד׃  
When He keeps quiet, who then can condemn? / And when He hides His face, who then can behold Him, / That is, in regard to both nation and man?—

**34:30** מִ֭מְּלֹךְ אָדָ֥ם חָנֵ֗ף מִמֹּ֥קְשֵׁי עָֽם׃  
So that godless men would not rule / Nor be snares of the people.

**34:31** כִּֽי־אֶל־אֵ֭ל הֶאָמַ֥ר נָשָׂ֗אתִי לֹ֣א אֶחְבֹּֽל׃  
For has anyone said to God, / 'I have borne chastisement; / I will not offend anymore;

**34:32** בִּלְעֲדֵ֣י אֶ֭חֱזֶה אַתָּ֣ה הֹרֵ֑נִי אִֽם־עָ֥וֶל פָּ֝עַ֗לְתִּי לֹ֣א אֹסִֽיף׃  
Teach me what I do not see; / If I have done iniquity, / I will not do it again'?

**34:33** הַֽמֵעִמְּךָ֬ יְשַׁלְמֶ֨נָּהᵃ כִּֽי־מָאַ֗סְתָּ כִּי־אַתָּ֣ה תִבְחַ֣ר וְלֹא־אָ֑נִי וּֽמַה־יָדַ֥עְתָּᵇ דַבֵּֽר׃  
Shall He recompense on your terms, because you have rejected it? / For you must choose, and not I; / Therefore declare what you know.

**34:34** אַנְשֵׁ֣י לֵ֭בָב יֹ֣אמְרוּ לִ֑י וְגֶ֥בֶר חָ֝כָ֗ם שֹׁמֵ֥עַֽ לִֽי׃  
Men of understanding will say to me, / And a wise man who hears me,

**34:35** אִ֭יֹּוב לֹא־בְדַ֣עַת יְדַבֵּ֑ר וּ֝דְבָרָ֗יו לֹ֣א בְהַשְׂכֵּֽיל׃  
'Job speaks without knowledge, / And his words are without wisdom.

**34:36** אָבִ֗י יִבָּחֵ֣ן אִיֹּ֣וב עַד־נֶ֑צַח עַל־תְּ֝שֻׁבֹ֗ת בְּאַנְשֵׁיᵃ־אָֽוֶן׃  
'Job ought to be tried to the limit, / Because he answers like wicked men.

**34:37** כִּ֥י יֹ֘סִ֤יף עַֽל־חַטָּאתֹ֣ו פֶ֭שַׁע בֵּינֵ֣ינוּ יִסְפֹּ֑וקᵃ וְיֶ֖רֶב אֲמָרָ֣יו לָאֵֽל׃ ס  
'For he adds rebellion to his sin; / He claps his hands among us, / And multiplies his words against God.'

### The third speech (35:1–16)

*Petuḥah (פ) after 35:16 in BHS.*

**35:1** וַיַּ֥עַן אֱלִיה֗וּ וַיֹּאמַֽר׃  
Then Elihu continued and said,

**35:2** הֲ֭זֹאת חָשַׁ֣בְתָּ לְמִשְׁפָּ֑ט אָ֝מַ֗רְתָּ צִדְקִ֥י מֵאֵֽל׃  
Do you think this is according to justice? / Do you say, 'My righteousness is more than God's'?

**35:3** כִּֽי־תֹ֭אמַר מַה־יִּסְכָּן־לָ֑ךְ מָֽה־אֹ֝עִ֗יל מֵֽחַטָּאתִֽי׃  
For you say, 'What advantage will it be to You? / What profit will I have, more than if I had sinned?'

**35:4** אֲ֭נִי אֲשִֽׁיבְךָ֣ מִלִּ֑ין וְֽאֶת־רֵעֶ֥יךָ עִמָּֽךְ׃  
I will answer you, / And your friends with you.

**35:5** הַבֵּ֣ט שָׁמַ֣יִם וּרְאֵ֑ה וְשׁ֥וּר שְׁ֝חָקִ֗ים גָּבְה֥וּ מִמֶּֽךָּ׃  
Look at the heavens and see; / And behold the clouds—they are higher than you.

**35:6** אִם־חָ֭טָאתָ מַה־תִּפְעָל־בֹּ֑ו וְרַבּ֥וּ פְ֝שָׁעֶ֗יךָ מַה־תַּעֲשֶׂה־לֹּֽו׃  
If you have sinned, what do you accomplish against Him? / And if your transgressions are many, what do you do to Him?

**35:7** אִם־צָ֭דַקְתָּ מַה־תִּתֶּן־לֹ֑ו אֹ֥ו מַה־מִיָּדְךָ֥ יִקָּֽח׃  
If you are righteous, what do you give to Him, / Or what does He receive from your hand?

**35:8** לְאִישׁ־כָּמֹ֥וךָ רִשְׁעֶ֑ךָ וּלְבֶן־אָ֝דָ֗ם צִדְקָתֶֽךָ׃  
Your wickedness is for a man like yourself, / And your righteousness is for a son of man.

**35:9** מֵ֭רֹב עֲשׁוּקִ֣יםᵃ יַזְעִ֑יקוּ יְשַׁוְּע֖וּ מִזְּרֹ֣ועַ רַבִּֽים׃  
Because of the multitude of oppressions they cry out; / They cry for help because of the arm of the mighty.

**35:10** וְֽלֹא־אָמַ֗ר אַ֭יֵּה אֱלֹ֣והַּ עֹשָׂ֑י נֹתֵ֖ן זְמִרֹ֣ות בַּלָּֽיְלָה׃  
But no one says, 'Where is God my Maker, / Who gives songs in the night,

**35:11** מַ֭לְּפֵנוּᵃ מִבַּהֲמֹ֣ות אָ֑רֶץ וּמֵעֹ֖וף הַשָּׁמַ֣יִם יְחַכְּמֵֽנוּ׃  
Who teaches us more than the beasts of the earth / And makes us wiser than the birds of the heavens?'

**35:12** שָׁ֣םᵃ יִ֭צְעֲקוּ וְלֹ֣א יַעֲנֶ֑ה מִ֝פְּנֵ֗י גְּאֹ֣ון רָעִֽים׃  
There they cry out, but He does not answer / Because of the pride of evil men.

**35:13** אַךְ־שָׁ֭וְא לֹא־יִשְׁמַ֥ע׀ אֵ֑ל וְ֝שַׁדַּ֗י לֹ֣א יְשׁוּרֶֽנָּה׃  
Surely God will not listen to an empty cry, / Nor will the Almighty regard it.

**35:14** אַ֣ף כִּֽי־תֹ֖אמַר לֹ֣א תְשׁוּרֶ֑נּוּ דִּ֥ין לְ֝פָנָ֗יו וּתְחֹ֥ולֵֽלᵃ לֹֽו׃  
How much less when you say you do not behold Him, / The case is before Him, and you must wait for Him!

**35:15** וְעַתָּ֗ה כִּי־אַ֭יִן פָּקַ֣ד אַפֹּ֑ו וְלֹֽא־יָדַ֖ע בַּפַּ֣שׁᵃ מְאֹֽד׃  
And now, because He has not visited in His anger, / Nor has He acknowledged transgression well,

**35:16** וְ֭אִיֹּוב הֶ֣בֶל יִפְצֶה־פִּ֑יהוּ בִּבְלִי־דַ֝֗עַת מִלִּ֥ין יַכְבִּֽרᵃ׃ פ  
So Job opens his mouth emptily; / He multiplies words without knowledge.

### The fourth speech, part one (36:1–33)

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims that emerged across several tools:

1. **Elihu enters as the one who will answer, at the point where everyone has stopped answering — and no one answers him.** `[T]`
   - **"Answer" is chapter 32's leading word.** The verb עָנָה ("answer") and the noun מַעֲנֶה ("answer, reply") occur 9 times in its 22 verses (32:1, 3, 5, 6, 12, 15, 16, 17, 20). That is 0.41 a verse. **No other chapter of Job comes close:** the next densest is chapter 9 at 0.17, and the whole book has 62 (WLC). The chapter opens "these three men ceased answering Job" (32:1) and ends "let me open my lips and answer" (32:20).
   - **Elihu answers when no one has spoken.** "Then Elihu answered (וַיַּעַן) and said" (34:1; 35:1) follows his own speeches; Job has said nothing. (The NASB95 renders both as "continued", which hides the oddity; the ESV keeps "answered".)
   - **He demands an answer and a hearing.** "Answer me (הֲשִׁיבֵנִי) if you can" (33:5); "if you have anything to say, answer me" (33:32). Five times he says "hear me" (שְׁמַע־לִי and its forms: 32:10; 33:31, 33; 34:10, 34). Job had cried, "Oh that I had one to hear me (שֹׁמֵעַ לִי)!" (31:35). **Elihu offers himself not as a hearer but as a speaker to be heard.**
   - **Job never replies.** God then "answered (וַיַּעַן) Job out of the whirlwind" (38:1).

   Surfaced by: Repetition (with a book-wide baseline), Structure, Linking Words, Tool 8, Move 4. *High.*

2. **Elihu promised new words and used old ones.** `[T]`
   - **The promise:** "I will not answer him with your speeches (בְאִמְרֵיכֶם)" (32:14).
   - **Job's words, quoted back at him:**
     - "He counts me as his enemy" (33:10, from 13:24); "He puts my feet in the stocks; he watches all my paths" (33:11, from 13:27) — each pair of words occurs only in these two verses of Job `[S: prior dig]`;
     - "God has taken away my right (הֵסִיר מִשְׁפָּטִי)" (34:5, from 27:2, only these two) `[S: prior dig]`;
     - "the ear tests words" (34:3, from 12:11) `[S: overview]`;
     - "If I have sinned (חָטָאתִי), what have I done (אֶפְעַל) to You?" (7:20) becomes "If you have sinned, what do you accomplish (תִּפְעָל) against Him?" (35:6). Sin with that verb occurs only in these two verses;
     - **both nouns of Job's complaint against the friends,** "How then will you comfort me with vanity (הֶבֶל), for your answers (וּתְשׁוּבֹתֵיכֶם) remain full of falsehood?" (21:34), **are turned on Job.** He "answers (תְּשֻׁבֹת) like wicked men" (34:36), and "Job opens his mouth emptily (הֶבֶל)" (35:16). That noun for "answers" occurs in Job only at 21:34 and 34:36.
   - **The friends' words, Eliphaz's above all:**
     - **Eliphaz:**
       - "when deep sleep falls on men (בִּנְפֹל תַּרְדֵּמָה עַל־אֲנָשִׁים)" (4:13 → 33:15), the whole phrase only in these two verses `[S: prior dig]`;
       - "drinks … like water" (15:16 → 34:7, only these two in Job) `[S: prior dig]`;
       - "profit" (סכן, 22:2 → 34:9; 35:3);
       - "if you are righteous" (22:3 → 35:7);
       - "see the heavens … how high" (22:12 → 35:5);
       - "pray" (עתר, 22:27 → 33:26, the only two in Job);
       - his hymn's "unsearchable … great things … wonders" (5:9, which Job repeated at 9:10), split across 34:24; 36:26 and 37:5.
     - **Bildad:** "Does God pervert justice (יְעַוֵּת מִשְׁפָּט)?" (8:3 → 34:12, the phrase only in these two verses) `[S: prior dig]`; "God will not reject (לֹא יִמְאַס)" (8:20 → 36:5).
     - **Zophar:** "Can you find out the Almighty?" (11:7 → 37:23; find with "the Almighty" only in these two verses).

   **Elihu answers Job in Eliphaz's vocabulary more than anyone's — the friend at whom God's anger burns (42:7).** Surfaced by: Move 4, Vocabulary, Repetition, Linking Words. *High for every chain; the observation that Elihu recycles is* `[I]`, *high.*

3. **What Elihu adds is a reading of suffering as God's speech — warning, discipline and rescue — with a mediator and a ransom; and his model penitent speaks the words Job will say to God.** `[T]`
   - **God speaks through the night and through pain.** "God speaks once (בְאַחַת), or twice (וּבִשְׁתַּיִם)" (33:14): in a dream (33:15–18) and on the sickbed (33:19–22). Job had complained, "You frighten me with dreams and terrify me by visions" (7:14); Elihu makes the dreams a warning to "turn man aside from his conduct" (33:17). God "opens the ear (יִגְלֶה אֹזֶן)" (33:16; 36:10, 15, the only three in Job).
   - **The mediator, "one out of a thousand".** "If there is an angel as mediator (מַלְאָךְ מֵלִיץ) for him, one out of a thousand (אֶחָד מִנִּי־אָלֶף) … 'Deliver him from going down to the pit, I have found a ransom (כֹפֶר)'" (33:23–24).
     - The phrase answers Job's "he could not answer Him once in a thousand (אַחַת מִנִּי־אָלֶף)" (9:3); the pair "one" and "thousand" occurs in Job only in these two verses.
     - Elihu also takes up 9:3's "He does not answer (לֹא־יַעֲנֶה)" at 33:13.
     - מֵלִיץ ("mediator, interpreter") takes up Job's מְלִיצַי ("my scoffers, my interpreters", 16:20), the only other use in Job `[S: prior dig]`.
   - **Affliction as instruction.** "He delivers the afflicted (עָנִי) by their affliction (בְעָנְיוֹ), and opens their ear by oppression" (36:15). Job's chapters 29–30 turned on that root (29:12; 30:11, 16, 27); Elihu makes affliction God's means of speaking. And his last line about God uses the verb of 30:11 ("He has afflicted me", וַיְעַנֵּנִי): "justice and abundant righteousness He will not afflict (לֹא יְעַנֶּה)" (37:23). Those are the only two uses of that verb in Job.
   - **The penitent's script.** Elihu's penitent says, "I have borne chastisement; I will not offend anymore; teach me (הֹרֵנִי) what I do not see; if I have done iniquity, I will not do it again (לֹא אֹסִיף)" (34:31–32). Job will say to God, "Once (אַחַת) I have spoken, and I will not answer; or twice (וּשְׁתַּיִם), and I will add no more (לֹא אוֹסִיף)" (40:5).
     - "One", "two" and "speak" stand together only at Ps 62:12 ("Once God has spoken; twice I have heard this"), Job 33:14 and Job 40:5, apart from two unrelated lists (Num 17:21; Dan 11:27).
     - Job's "I will add no more" is the penitent's line of 34:32. **Job speaks Elihu's words — to God, after God has spoken, not to Elihu.**
   - **The Greek has none of the mediator.** Swete and Rahlfs read 33:23 as "if there be a thousand death-bringing angels, not one of them shall wound him". **The mediator and the ransom are Hebrew-only.**

   Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Original Languages, Move 4, Tool 11 (Ps 62; Ps 56:14), Christological Reading. *High for the words and the internal chains; moderate for Ps 62:12 as a source.*

4. **Elihu's last speech leads straight into God's: he asks God's questions, uses God's thunder and confesses that the Almighty cannot be found — just before the Almighty comes.** `[T]`
   - **The thunder:** "God thunders (יַרְעֵם) with His voice (בְּקוֹלוֹ)" (37:4–5) → "Or can you thunder (תַרְעֵם) with a voice (וּבְקוֹל) like His?" (40:9). Thunder, voice and God stand together only at Ps 29:3, Job 37:5 and Job 40:9.
   - **"Make known":** Job asked God to "make me know (הוֹדִיעֵנִי)" (10:2; 13:23). Elihu asks Job, "Teach us (הוֹדִיעֵנוּ) what we shall say to Him" (37:19). God says, "I will ask you, and you instruct Me (וְהוֹדִיעֵנִי)" (38:3; 40:7), and Job quotes it back (42:4).
   - **The questions:** "Do you know (הֲתֵדַע)…?" (37:15, 16) is the form God's speech takes (38:33; 39:1–2) `[S: prior dig]`.
   - **The wonders:** "wonders (נִפְלָאוֹת) … that we cannot know (וְלֹא נֵדָע)" (37:5) → "things too wonderful (נִפְלָאוֹת) for me, which I did not know (וְלֹא אֵדָע)" (42:3). "Wonders" with "not know" occurs only in these two verses of Job. 37:14's "wonders" → 42:3 is `[S: prior dig]`.
   - **The one who cannot be found:** "The Almighty (שַׁדַּי) — we cannot find Him (לֹא מְצָאנֻהוּ)" (37:23). It answers Zophar's "Can you find out the limit of the Almighty?" (11:7) and Job's "Oh that I knew where I might find Him!" (23:3) `[S: prior dig]` for 23:3. The next verse but one is 38:1.
   - **But the storm is not distinctive by vocabulary.** Against the weather section of God's first speech (38:1–38), chapter 37 ranks 4th of 35 chapters of Job by shared rare words (8th when commoner words are admitted). Job's own hymn (ch. 26) and his curse (ch. 3) rank as high or higher. So **the overview's "the storm he describes is the storm God speaks from" rests on placement and on a few words, not on saturation**, and the storm words differ (סוּפָה, "storm", 37:9; סְעָרָה, "whirlwind", 38:1).

   Surfaced by: Move 4, Vocabulary, a chance baseline, Tool 11 (Ps 29). *High for the chains; moderate for the storm as a designed lead-in.*

5. **The text gives Elihu's status both ways, and does not settle it.** `[T]` for each datum; `[I]` for the weighing.
   - **For Elihu:**
     - His anger at the three friends (חרה אף, "anger burned", 32:3, 5) anticipates God's: "My wrath is kindled (חָרָה אַפִּי) against you and against your two friends" (42:7). In Job that phrase occurs only at 19:11 (Job's complaint that God is angry with him), 32:2–5 (four times) and 42:7 `[S: prior dig]`.
     - His charge "words without knowledge" (35:16: בִּבְלִי־דַעַת מִלִּין) is God's opening line: "Who is this that darkens counsel by words without knowledge (בְמִלִּין בְּלִי־דָעַת)?" (38:2). The words occur together only in these two verses.
     - His "Will you condemn (תַּרְשִׁיעַ) the righteous mighty One?" (34:17) is God's "Will you condemn Me (תַּרְשִׁיעֵנִי) that you may be justified?" (40:8).
   - **Against him:**
     - His anger at Job (32:2) is not God's: God says Job spoke "what is right" (42:7).
     - He wishes Job "tested (יִבָּחֵן) to the end, because he answers like wicked men" (34:36). Job had trusted that test: "when He has tried me (בְּחָנַנִי), I shall come forth as gold" (23:10).
     - He claims for himself, "one who is perfect in knowledge (תְּמִים דֵּעוֹת) is with you" (36:4) — the title he then gives God, "Him who is perfect in knowledge (תְּמִים דֵּעִים)" (37:16). דֵּעַ ("knowledge, opinion"), the noun of 37:16, **occurs five times in the Hebrew Bible, all in Elihu's mouth** (32:6, 10, 17; 36:3; 37:16).
   - **And then silence:** God's verdict names "Eliphaz … and your two friends" (42:7). Elihu is neither rebuked nor commended, and is never mentioned again.
   - **The Greek tradition shows two things.** Its translator abbreviated Elihu more than any other part of the book: counting the words that stand between Origen's signs in the Rahlfs export, about 27 % of Elihu's Greek is supplied material, against about 11 % in the dialogue (3–31) and 10 % in God's speeches (38–42). And Swete's 38:1 opens, "After Elihu had ceased speaking, the Lord said to Job out of the whirlwind" `[S: prior dig]`.

   Surfaced by: Move 4, Vocabulary, Narrator's Comment, Versions, Difficult Verses. *High for the data; the weighing is open.*

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**The book.** With the overview: the book asks whether anyone fears God חִנָּם ("for nothing", 1:9), refuses the friends' verdict that suffering proves guilt, and brings God to answer, not with a reason but with himself. `[S]` (overview microscript, which notes that Elihu is "the movement the microscript serves least").

**The unit.** Job has rested his case (31:35–40). The three friends have "ceased answering" (32:1). A fourth man, unannounced, younger, and named with a genealogy, speaks for six chapters, and no one replies. `[T]` **The unit's function is to show what one more human voice can and cannot do before God speaks.** `[I]`
- **What it can do:** say true things about God's justice (34:10–12), his greatness (36:22–26) and his storm (36:27–37:13); name the possibility of a mediator and a ransom (33:23–24); and call Job to "stand and consider the wonders of God" (37:14).
- **What it cannot do:** answer Job. Elihu tells Job what he has said (33:8–11; 34:5–9; 35:2–3, 14), but he cannot say why Job suffers. He falls back on the friends' categories: God "pays a man according to his work" (34:11), and Job "answers like wicked men" (34:36). His last word is that the Almighty cannot be found (37:23).

**What the unit contributes to 1:9.** The Accuser's question was whether Job feared God for nothing. Elihu ends: "Therefore men fear Him (יְרֵאוּהוּ); He does not regard any who are wise of heart" (37:24). **The unit closes on the book's keynote, the fear of God, as the poem of chapter 28 did** (28:28) — and with the same paradox: the wisdom that matters is not the wisdom of the wise. `[T]` for the words (ירא, "fear", with חָכָם, "wise"); `[I]` for the bracket.

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement.** `[T]`
- **Job has signed his case and demanded an answer:** "Behold, here is my signature; let the Almighty answer me!" (31:35); "The words of Job are ended" (31:40).
- **The friends have nothing left.** Bildad's last speech was six verses, Zophar has none, and now "these three men ceased answering Job" (32:1).
- **The question the dialogue raised and could not settle is the mediator's:** "There is no umpire between us" (9:33); "my witness is in heaven" (16:19); "my Redeemer lives" (19:25).

**Necessity answer.** Elihu exists here for three reasons, all grounded in the text.
1. **The human answer must be shown exhausted from a new angle.** The three failed as elders (32:6–9). Elihu speaks as the young man with "the breath of the Almighty" (32:8; 33:4), and he too ends where they did (34:36–37). `[I]`
2. **The words of 38–42 need a bridge.** Elihu introduces the storm (36:27–37:13), the questions in the second person ("Do you know…?", 37:15–18), the "words without knowledge" (35:16 → 38:2) and the penitent's "I will add no more" (34:32 → 40:5). God's speech then takes these up — without acknowledging Elihu. `[T]` for the links; `[I]` for "bridge".
3. **Job's demand must go unanswered by any man.** Elihu's "answer me" (33:5, 32) meets silence; only God's "answer" is answered (40:3–5; 42:1–6). `[I]`

**Why not elsewhere?** Not before 31: the debate had to run its course. Not after 38: once God has spoken, a human voice would be an anticlimax, and God's verdict (42:7) would have to deal with him. **Placed here, Elihu is the last word before the Word — and the book leaves him unjudged.** `[I]`

**Implication for this passage.** Elihu is neither the book's answer nor a mere interruption. He is the best a fourth human voice can do: true about God, wrong about Job, and silenced by the voice he describes. This frames how the sermon should treat him (Pitfalls).

#### Immediate context

- **Before:** 29–31, Job's closing statement and oath, ending "The words of Job are ended" (31:40).
- **After:** 38:1, "Then the LORD answered Job out of the whirlwind." No reply to Elihu intervenes.
- **Section:** the overview's Movement IV, "three discourses and a meeting" `[S]`.

| **34:1–37** | **Speech 2, to "you wise men"** | Job quoted (34:5–6, 9); God's justice and rule (34:10–30); the penitent's words (34:31–32); "Job speaks without knowledge" (34:35–37). Ends at a setumah |

| **36:1–33** | **Speech 4a, to Job** | "Yet more to be said in God's behalf" (36:2); affliction as instruction (36:5–15); warning (36:16–21); God the teacher (36:22–26); rain and lightning (36:27–33). No marker |

**The speech formulas.** `[T]`
- 32:6 "And Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite answered (וַיַּעַן) and said";
- 34:1 and 35:1 "Then Elihu answered (וַיַּעַן) and said";
- 36:1 "Then Elihu continued (וַיֹּסֶף) and said".

The last formula is the verb of Job's own "again took up his discourse" (27:1; 29:1), without the word "discourse" (מָשָׁל). **Elihu's four speeches are introduced as Job's last speeches were, as a man "continuing" when no one has answered.** `[I]`

**The address test.** Second-person forms counted by verse in the WLC morphology: `[T]`
- **Ch. 32:** plural in 5 verses (32:6, 11–14), to the friends;
- **Ch. 33:** singular in 11 verses — Job by name (33:1, 31);
- **Ch. 34:** plural at 34:2, 10 ("you wise men", "you men of understanding"), singular at 34:16–17, 33;
- **Ch. 35:** singular in 8 verses;
- **Ch. 36:** singular in 9;
- **Ch. 37:** singular in 7, by name at 37:14.

Elihu names Job as addressee three times (33:1, 31; 37:14). The friends never addressed Job by name in the debate; Job's name occurs in chapters 4–31 only in the narrator's formulas and at 31:40 `[S: prior dig]`. Elihu himself uses the name nine times — as addressee at 33:1, 31 and 37:14, and in the third person at 32:12; 34:5, 7, 35, 36 and 35:16 (WLC). The narrator has it at 32:1–4.

**Device: an inclusio of "answer" and "fear".** The unit opens with the friends who cannot answer (32:1, 3, 5) and closes with the Almighty who cannot be found, and with fear (37:23–24). `[I]`

**Paragraphing.** BHS (Leningrad) has petuḥot after 32:1, 32:5, 35:16 and 37:24, and setumot after 33:33 and 34:37. There is no marker between chapters 36 and 37, which stand as one speech. The markers coincide with the four speech-ends. `[T]`, *as BHS prints them*; spread across other manuscripts `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`. Nothing rests on a marker alone.

### 4. Linking Words

- **כִּי ("because") in the narrator's prose (32:1–5).** Each "because" gives a reason for anger or silence:
  - "because he was righteous in his own eyes" (32:1);
  - "because he justified himself before God" (32:2, עַל, "on account of");
  - "because they had found no answer" (32:3);
  - "because they were years older" (32:4).

  The narrator explains everything except whether Elihu is right. `[T]`
- **לָכֵן ("therefore") — Elihu's conclusions** (32:10; 34:10, 25; 37:24). The last is the speech's last word: "Therefore men fear Him." `[T]`
- **הֵן / הִנֵּה ("behold") — Elihu's demand for attention** (32:11, 12, 19; 33:2, 6, 7, 10, 12, 29; 36:5, 22, 26, 30). `[T]`
- **אִם ("if") in the discipline sequence** (33:23; 36:8, 11, 12): "If there is an angel …"; "if they are bound … if they hear … if they do not hear". The conditional shape is Elihu's theology: suffering is a fork in the road, not a verdict. `[I]`
- **אַף כִּי ("how much less", 35:14).** "How much less when you say you do not behold Him!" — an *a fortiori* turning Job's complaint into a reason God does not listen. `[T]`
- **וְעַתָּה ("and now", 35:15; 37:21).** Elihu's two "and now"s against Job's three in chapter 30 (30:1, 9, 16). `[T]`

### 5. Parallels

Selected parallels that clarify meaning: `[T]`
- **32:7, 9:** "days should speak, and increased years should teach wisdom … the abundant in years may not be wise" — synonymous, then antithetical; the elders' claim stated and refuted.
- **33:4:** "The Spirit (רוּחַ) of God has made me, and the breath (וְנִשְׁמַת) of the Almighty gives me life." The pair רוּחַ / נְשָׁמָה ("spirit / breath") recurs at 32:8 and 34:14. It is the pair of Job 27:3 ("my breath is in me, and the spirit of God is in my nostrils") `[S: prior dig]`.
- **36:15:** "He delivers the afflicted (עָנִי) in their affliction (בְעָנְיוֹ) // and opens their ear (אָזְנָם) in time of oppression (בַלַּחַץ)" — affliction and oppression as the means by which God speaks.
- **37:13:** "Whether for correction (לְשֵׁבֶט, lit. 'for a rod'), or for His land, or for lovingkindness (לְחֶסֶד)" — a three-way list; Elihu's only חֶסֶד ("lovingkindness").
- **37:24:** "Therefore men fear (יְרֵאוּהוּ) Him; He does not regard (לֹא־יִרְאֶה) any who are wise of heart." The verbs יָרֵא ("fear") and רָאָה ("see, regard") sound alike: *yĕrēʾûhû* and *yirʾeh* — a closing paronomasia. *Moderate.*

### 6. Narrator's Comment

**32:1–5 is the narrator's only intervention between 3:2 and 42:6 apart from the speech formulas and 31:40c.** `[T]` What he says:
- **"These three men ceased answering Job, because he was righteous in his own eyes (בְּעֵינָיו)"** (32:1). Is this the narrator's verdict, or a report of why they stopped? The sentence explains the friends' silence; it does not endorse their view. One manuscript, the Greek, Symmachus and the Syriac read "in their eyes" (Textual Variants).
- **Elihu's anger (חרה, "burn") four times in four verses** (32:2 twice, 3, 5), at Job and at the friends. **Four statements of anger, and no statement that he was right.** `[T]`
- **The reasons:**
  - Job "justified himself (צַדְּקוֹ נַפְשׁוֹ) before God / rather than God (מֵאֱלֹהִים)" (32:2). The construction is Eliphaz's question in the night vision, "Can mankind be just before God (מֵאֱלוֹהַּ)?" (4:17). The verb "be just" with "from" and a divine name occurs in Job only at 4:17 and 32:2 (35:2 has the noun: "my righteousness … from God"). *High.*
  - The friends "had found no answer, and yet had condemned Job (וַיַּרְשִׁיעוּ אֶת־אִיּוֹב)" (32:3). The Masoretic tradition records this as one of the scribes' corrections: the original is said to have read "and condemned God" (BHS: *Tiq soph*, "correction of the scribes"). On that reading, by failing to answer Job the friends had in effect condemned God (Textual Variants). `[S]` for the tradition.
- **Elihu's genealogy:** "Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite, of the family of Ram" (32:2). **No other speaker in the book receives one** `[S: prior dig]`. See Tool 7.


### 7. Vocabulary

**Proper-noun inventory.** `[T]`
- **People:**
  - אִיּוֹב ("Job");
  - אֱלִיהוּא / אֱלִיהוּ ("Elihu", "He is my God"; both spellings: 32:2, 4, 5, 6; 34:1; 35:1; 36:1);
  - בַּרַכְאֵל ("Barachel", "God blesses", 32:2, 6 only);
  - the three friends, unnamed.
- **Gentilic:** הַבּוּזִי ("the Buzite", 32:2, 6 only).
- **Clan:** רָם ("Ram", 32:2).
- **Divine names:** אֵל ("God"), אֱלוֹהַּ ("God"), אֱלֹהִים ("God", 32:2; 34:9), שַׁדַּי ("the Almighty"). The covenant name is absent, as throughout the dialogue.
- **Places:** none. **Numbers:** "one … two" (33:14), "twice, three times" (33:29), "one out of a thousand" (33:23).

**Names with canonical freight.**
- **Buz (load-the-story rule applied).**
  - **The source:** Gen 22:20–21 lists the sons of Nahor, Abraham's brother: "Uz his firstborn and Buz his brother" (עוּץ בְּכֹרוֹ וְאֶת־בּוּז אָחִיו).
  - **In Job:** Job is of "the land of Uz" (1:1); Elihu is "the Buzite". **The book's two named men from Nahor's line are the firstborn's land and the younger brother's people — and Elihu insists he is the younger (32:4, 6).** `[T]` for the names; `[I]` for the pairing.
  - **Elsewhere:** Buz appears with Dedan and Tema among the Arabian peoples who drink the cup of wrath (Jer 25:23) `[S: recalled]`.
  - *Moderate.*
- **Barachel ("God blesses").** The name carries the book's double-edged verb בֵּרַךְ ("bless", used for "curse" at 1:5, 11; 2:5, 9, and for God's blessing at 42:12). `[T]` for the root; *low–moderate* as a pun the text exploits, since the narrator never plays on it.
- **Elihu ("He is my God").**
  - **The bearers:** the name is borne elsewhere by Samuel's ancestor (1 Sam 1:1), by a man of Manasseh who joined David (1 Chr 12:21), by a gatekeeper (1 Chr 26:7) and by David's brother (1 Chr 27:18) (WLC).
  - **In Job:** it is the only speaker's name in the book with a divine element in a Hebrew form.

**Ancient versions check.** The NASB95, ESV and KJV agree on several weight-bearing renderings, so the Greek was checked. Each divergence is classified under the three-way triage:
- **33:23–24 — category 2 (or the translator's paraphrase); a real split.** Swete and Rahlfs: "If there be a thousand death-bringing angels, not one of them shall wound him; if he resolves in his heart to turn to the Lord …". **No mediator, no ransom.** *Moderate* for which text is prior; the Hebrew is the harder reading. **Preaching implication:** the mediator and ransom are Masoretic-text findings, and the Christological use of 33:23–24 cannot appeal to the Greek. The sweep's verdict, "Hebrew-only", is confirmed `[S: prior dig]`.
- **32:1 — category 2.** "Righteous in his own eyes" (MT) against "righteous before them" (Greek; also Symmachus and the Syriac, per BHS). See Textual Variants.
- **34:36 — category 2 or paraphrase.** The Greek has "Learn, Job, do not give again an answer like the fools" for "Job ought to be tried to the limit". The Greek softens Elihu.
- **37:24 — category 2 or paraphrase.** The Greek reads "the wise in heart will fear him" — the opposite of the Masoretic "He does not regard any who are wise of heart". The NIV84 text ("for does he not have regard for all the wise in heart?") is closer to the Greek, with the Masoretic sense in its margin.
- **38:1 — reception (category 2).** Swete opens God's speech, "After Elihu had ceased speaking" `[S: prior dig]`. The Greek tradition ties Elihu to the theophany.
- **The extent of the Greek.** Counting the words between Origen's asterisk and metobelus in the Rahlfs export: about 27 % of Elihu's Greek is supplied material (from Theodotion), against about 11 % in chapters 3–31 and 10 % in 38–42. By chapter: 32, 19 %; 33, 12 %; 34, 26 %; 35, 29 %; 36, 45 %; 37, 35 %. `[T]` *as the Rahlfs export prints the signs*. The count is my own and approximate, since the signs do not always close cleanly. **The Old Greek translator abbreviated Elihu more heavily than any other part of the book, and the storm hymn (36–37) most of all.**

**Pulpit divergence note.** The pulpit text is the ESV. **Five places need a word from the pulpit:**
- **37:24** — "wise in their own conceit" resolves a paradox the Hebrew keeps;
- **33:14** — read "once … twice", to hear 40:5;
- **33:16** — "terrifies" follows the Greek;
- **35:2** — the ESV softens Elihu's charge;
- **36:19** — "cry for help" is a choice, and a good one for the sermon.

At 34:1 and 35:1 the ESV is the better guide. The NIV84, if used elsewhere, agrees with the ESV at 32:2 ("rather than God") and gives the scribal tradition at 32:3 in its margin ("and so had condemned God").

### 9. Tone and Feel

**32: impatient, self-conscious, almost comic.** He was "shy and afraid" (32:6), "waited" (32:4, 11, 16), and is now "full of words … like new wineskins about to burst" (32:18–19). The image of fermenting wine that must vent is vivid and slightly absurd. The four statements of anger in the prose (32:2–5) set the tone before he speaks. `[T]` for the devices; the comic register is `[I]`, and readers differ `[S: recalled]`.

**33: earnest, pastoral, even tender.** "Behold, no fear of me should terrify you" (33:7) — a deliberate answer to Job's "let not the dread of You terrify me" (13:21; 9:34). The terror word with the dread word stands together only at 9:34, 13:21 and 33:7 (WLC). The sickbed and the angel (33:19–28) are the warmest passage in Elihu. `[T]`

**34: prosecutorial and cold.** Elihu addresses "you wise men" about Job in the third person (34:2–9, 34–37). "What man is like Job, who drinks up derision like water?" (34:7). The speech ends on a wish that Job be "tried to the limit" (34:36). `[T]`

**35: argumentative.** It is a sequence of "if … what?" questions (35:6–7) and a dismissal: "Job opens his mouth emptily" (35:16). `[T]`

**36–37: lyrical and awe-struck.** Elihu moves from teaching (36:5–21) to doxology (36:22–26), into a storm that is gathering as he speaks: "At this also my heart trembles and leaps from its place. Listen closely to the thunder of His voice" (37:1–2). `[T]` **Soundtrack:** a single voice, insistent and slightly shrill, which grows into a description of thunder — and is then drowned out by it.

**For the preacher.** The tone moves from self-importance to awe. Let the sermon move with it. Do not mock Elihu (he says true things), and do not make him the hero (he is angry at the man God calls "My servant").

### 11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

**The book's live sources** (overview; claim audits 2–4): Genesis 1–4 and the patriarchal narratives; Deuteronomy 32; Isaiah; Lamentations 2–3; the Psalter (8; 88; 102; 107). **Canonical neighbours:** Psalms before Job, Proverbs after, in the BHS order. Ezekiel stands before Job in that order, and names Job (Ezek 14:14, 20) `[S: overview]`.

**Candidates were found independently first.** The passage's own distinctive words and word-pairs were searched across the whole Hebrew Bible before the sweep's Unit 14 was opened. A scan of rare shared word-pairs was run against each chapter as a sweep for anything missed; its ranks are not used as evidence. **New to this run:** Ezek 14:14, 16, 18 at 32:1; Ps 62:12–13 at 33:14 and 34:11; Ps 56:14 at 33:30; Ps 104:29 at 34:14–15; Ps 29:3 at 37:4–5; Num 14:34 at 33:10; Isa 29:14 at 37:14, 24; Exod 22:22 at 34:28; Ps 23:5 at 36:16; Prov 3:11–12 as the discipline field. **From the sweep, not re-found:** Ps 49:8 at 33:24 `[S: prior dig]`.

#### The high-confidence contacts — full triad

**Ezek 14:14, 16, 18 → Job 32:1** *(high for the words; moderate for design)*

*Source context:* in Ezekiel's oracle against a land that sins, God says that even if "these three men (שְׁלֹשֶׁת הָאֲנָשִׁים הָאֵלֶּה), Noah, Daniel and Job were in its midst, by their own righteousness (בְצִדְקָתָם) they could only deliver themselves" (Ezek 14:14). The refrain returns at 14:16, 18 and 20: "they could not deliver either their son or their daughter". It is a verdict on Jerusalem, set against the most righteous men the prophet can name. Its register is uncompromising judgement, with a remnant at the end (14:22–23).

*Book usage:* Ezekiel is not a live source by quotation, but Ezek 14 already touches Job. נחם ("comfort") with רָעָה ("calamity") and "come / bring" stands together only at Ezek 14:22, Job 2:11, Job 42:11 and Lam 1:21 (WLC; queue #21, unaudited). **This run adds the phrase "these three men", which occurs in the Hebrew Bible only at Ezek 14:14, 16, 18 and Job 32:1** (the plain-and-skeletal phrase search; Ezek 14:18 spells it differently). Job 32:5 has "the three men".

*OT-to-OT:* Ezek 14 is itself a reading of the Noah, Daniel and Job traditions. Job is one of Ezekiel's three, cited as righteous (צְדָקָה). In Job 32:1 "these three men" are the friends, who stop answering Job "because he was righteous (צַדִּיק) in his own eyes". `[T]`

*What it adds:* **for the canonical reader, who has met Ezekiel first, "these three men" has carried Job's name.** Here the phrase turns: the three are now his accusers, and his righteousness is called his own opinion. Ezekiel's rule is that a righteous man "could not deliver either their son or their daughter", but only himself. At 42:8–10 Job's prayer delivers the three men. **The book ends by exceeding the rule Ezekiel stated with Job's name in it.** `[I]` *High on the phrase; moderate for design* (the phrase is natural Hebrew; the coincidence of "these three men", Job and righteousness in one verse is not). **Planted for 42:8–10.**

**Ps 62:12–13 [Eng 62:11–12] → Job 33:14; 34:11; and Job 40:5** *(moderate–high)*

*Source context:* Ps 62 is a psalm of waiting for God alone ("My soul waits in silence for God only", 62:2 [Eng 62:1]). It ends: "Once God has spoken (אַחַת דִּבֶּר אֱלֹהִים); twice I have heard this (שְׁתַּיִם־זוּ שָׁמָעְתִּי): that power belongs to God; and lovingkindness is Yours, O Lord, for You recompense (תְשַׁלֵּם) a man (לְאִישׁ) according to his work" (62:12–13 [Eng 62:11–12]). The two verses are the psalm's closing confession: God's power and his steadfast love, with judgement by works.

*Book usage:* the Psalter is live in Job (Ps 8, 88, 102, 107) `[S: audit]`. No earlier use of Ps 62 in Job has been found. "One", "two" and "speak" stand together only at Ps 62:12, Job 33:14 and Job 40:5 among related texts (Num 17:21 and Dan 11:27 are lists). "Repay (שלם, piel)" with "a man (אִישׁ)" in the Psalms occurs only at Ps 62:13; Job 34:11 has the verb with "man (אָדָם … אִישׁ)" and the noun "work" (פֹעַל).

*OT-to-OT:* the pairing of power and repayment is the retribution confession of the wisdom tradition (Prov 24:12, "will He not render to man according to his work?") `[S: recalled]` for the Proverbs wording.

*What it adds:*
- **Elihu splits the psalm's close across two speeches:** "God speaks once, or twice" (33:14), and "He pays a man according to his work" (34:11). It is the same move Job made with Ps 107:40 (12:21, 24) `[S: audit]`.
- **Then Job takes up the psalm's numbers himself:** "Once I have spoken, and I will not answer; twice, and I will add no more" (40:5).
- **The psalm the friends and Elihu use for retribution, Job uses for silence before God.** And the psalm's own centre is the line no one in Job quotes: "lovingkindness (חֶסֶד) is Yours" (62:13). `[I]`
- *Moderate–high* for 33:14 / 40:5 with Ps 62:12; *moderate* for 34:11.

**Ps 104:29 → Job 34:14–15** *(high)*

*Source context:* in the creation psalm, all creatures look to God for food. Then: "You hide Your face, they are dismayed; You take away their spirit (רוּחָם), they expire (יִגְוָעוּן) and return (יְשׁוּבוּן) to their dust (עֲפָרָם)" (104:29). Then: "You send forth Your Spirit, they are created" (104:30). The register is wonder at dependence.

*Book usage:* no earlier use of Ps 104 in Job is recorded in the overview. The sea and Leviathan of Ps 104:26 stand behind the overview's sea-monster row `[S: overview]`. "Expire", "dust" and "return" stand together only at Ps 104:29 and Job 34:15 in the Hebrew Bible. Job 34:14 has "spirit" and "breath".

*OT-to-OT:* both rest on Gen 2:7 and 3:19 (breath; "to dust you shall return"). Job has the dust-and-return pair at 10:9 and here `[S: overview]`. Eccl 12:7 takes the same pair to its end.

*What it adds:* Elihu's argument is the psalm's. **If God withdrew his breath, all flesh would perish together; so the one who rules everything cannot be unjust** (34:13–17). It is a sound argument from creation, and the LORD will make a version of it himself (38–41). It does not answer Job's case. `[I]`

**Ps 29:3 → Job 37:4–5 → Job 40:9** *(high for the words)*

*Source context:* "The voice of the LORD (קוֹל יְהוָה) is upon the waters; the God of glory thunders (אֵל־הַכָּבוֹד הִרְעִים)" (29:3). It is the first of the psalm's seven "voice of the LORD" lines, which end, "The LORD sat as King at the flood … The LORD will give strength to His people; the LORD will bless His people with peace" (29:10–11). Its register is the doxology of the storm.

*Book usage:* not used earlier in Job. "Thunder" (רעם, verb), "voice" and "God" (אֵל) stand together only at Ps 29:3, Job 37:5 and Job 40:9. The verb also occurs at Job 37:4.

*OT-to-OT:* the storm-voice tradition runs through 1 Sam 7:10; 2 Sam 22:14 = Ps 18:14 (the LORD thundered from heaven) and Exod 19:16–19 (thunder and voice at Sinai) `[S: recalled]` for Exod 19's vocabulary.

*What it adds:* Elihu speaks the psalm's doxology over Job: "God thunders with His voice wondrously" (37:5). **Then God asks Job the question in the psalm's words:** "Have you an arm like God, or can you thunder with a voice like His?" (40:9). **Elihu describes the thunder; God answers in it.** *High* for the chain 37:4–5 → 40:9.

**Ps 56:14 [Eng 56:13] → Job 33:28, 30** *(moderate–high)*

*Source context:* "For You have delivered my soul from death … so that I may walk before God in the light of the living (בְּאוֹר הַחַיִּים)" (56:14). It is the vow of thanks that closes a psalm of trust under attack.

*Book usage:* not used earlier in Job. **The phrase "the light of life (בְּאוֹר הַחַיִּים)" occurs only at Ps 56:14 and Job 33:30** (phrase search, plain and skeletal). Job 33:28, from the healed man's own mouth (ketiv, "my soul … my life"), is "He has redeemed my soul from going to the pit, and my life shall see the light".

*OT-to-OT:* the rescue-from-the-pit thanksgiving is the shape of Ps 30:4 [Eng 30:3] and Ps 107:17–22 `[S: recalled]` for Ps 30.

*What it adds:* **Elihu's restored sufferer sings the thanksgiving psalm.** "He will sing to men and say, 'I have sinned …'" (33:27). Elihu's sickbed scene ends where the Psalter's thanksgivings end. **Translation note:** the NASB95 renders the one Hebrew phrase "the light of the living" at Ps 56:13 and "the light of life" at Job 33:30, hiding the contact; the ESV has "the light of life" in both.

#### Moderate-confidence allusions — Move 1 only

- **Num 14:34 → Job 33:10** *(moderate)*. תְּנוּאָה ("opposition, pretext") occurs only at Num 14:34 and Job 33:10. In Numbers God tells the wilderness generation, "you will know My opposition" — forty years for forty days. ### 12. Genre

Hebrew poetry, introduced by narrative prose (32:1–5). The overview notes that the Masoretic accentuation treats the prose too as poetry (dehi 4×, ole 2×) `[S: overview]`. Within the poetry:
- **32:6–22: an apology for speaking** — the speaker's credentials, his claim to inspiration and his impatience.
- **33–35: disputation speeches.** Elihu quotes the opponent and refutes him, and calls witnesses ("you wise men", 34:2).
- **36:22–37:13: a hymn of the storm,** in the tradition of Ps 29 and of the theophanies.
- **37:14–20: a series of rhetorical questions in the second person** — the form of the divine speeches.

**Reading rule:** Elihu is a character, not the narrator. His true statements about God are true, and his verdicts on Job are his own. God neither confirms nor denies them. `[I]`

### 13. Copycat

Non-narrative, but Elihu is a character, so label his acts:
- **32:4, 6–7 — waiting for his elders to speak:** *descriptive*. The custom is honoured, then broken.
- **32:18–20 — speaking because he must:** *descriptive*. He presents it as inspiration (32:8, 18); the book does not confirm it.
- **33:7 — speaking to a sufferer without threat:** *normative pattern* (cf. Gal 6:1).
- **33:23–30 — God's way of rescuing through a mediator:** *teaching*, Christologically fulfilled (Christological Reading); not a template for counselling.
- **34:7–9, 34–37 — characterising a sufferer as one who keeps company with the wicked:** *negative example*. It is the friends' error, which God rebukes (42:7).
- **36:22–37:24 — directing attention to God's works:** *normative pattern* (Ps 19:1; Rom 1:20), and the pattern God himself follows (38–41).

### 14. Bible Timeline

**Canonical position (Phase 0.55).** Job stands in the **Ketuvim**, second, between Psalms and Proverbs in the BHS order. `[T]` In this unit the neighbours do real work:
- **Psalms before:** Ps 62:12–13 (33:14; 34:11), Ps 104:29 (34:14–15), Ps 29:3 (37:5), Ps 56:14 (33:30), Ps 49:8 (33:24).
- **Proverbs after:** the discipline vocabulary (Prov 3:11–12; 6:23; 15:31–32).
- **Ezekiel, earlier in the BHS order,** names Job as one of "these three men" (Ezek 14:14) — and Job 32:1 is the only other use of that phrase.

The direction of dependence is open in every case.

**Where the story sits.** The patriarchal world outside Israel (overview) `[S]`. Elihu's name, and perhaps his clan (Ram), sound Israelite, but the text does not say so. `[I]`

**Where we stand.**
- **The mediator Elihu imagines — "one out of a thousand", with a ransom — has come.** "There is one God, and one mediator also between God and men, the man Christ Jesus, who gave Himself as a ransom for all" (1 Tim 2:5–6) `[S: recalled]` for the wording.
- **The God Elihu says cannot be found has been found, because he came** (John 1:18). `[I]`

### 15. Who Am I?

- **Elihu: a foil, and a warning to the earnest.** He is the young believer who is sure he can fix the old men's failure, and who says true things in the wrong spirit. Most preachers are more like Elihu than like Job. `[I]`
- **Job: the silent hearer.** He takes no part here. The sufferer in the congregation may need to hear that silence before such speech is not defeat.
- **The angel-mediator (33:23): a type pointing to Christ**, with escalation (Christological Reading).
- **The restored sufferer of 33:26–28: the believer who confesses and is restored** — "I have sinned … He has redeemed my soul" (33:27–28). Here we may rightly identify, so long as we do not make every sufferer a sinner being disciplined.

### 16. So What?

**Stage 1 — the author's intended response.** The reader is meant to:
- feel the weight of one more human answer that cannot answer;
- notice what is true in it — God speaks in pain; a mediator ransoms; God's justice cannot be perverted; creation displays his majesty;
- notice what is wrong — the anger, the presumption to know, the verdict on Job;
- and be brought, with Job, to the threshold of the LORD's speech (37:23 → 38:1).

**Stage 2 — worldview.**
- **God does speak through suffering, but the sufferer's affliction is not a public verdict.** Elihu is right that God "opens the ear by oppression" (36:15), and wrong to treat Job as one who "drinks up derision like water" (34:7).
- **A true theology can be spoken in a false spirit.** Elihu's anger (×4) is noted by the narrator and never endorsed.
- **The God who cannot be found comes himself** (37:23 → 38:1).

**Stage 2 — behaviour.**
- *Stop:* rushing in where others have failed, "full of words" (32:18); speaking about a sufferer in the third person to an audience (34:2–9); wishing a sufferer "tested to the limit" (34:36).
- *Start:* waiting before speaking (32:4, 11); speaking without threat (33:7); pointing sufferers to the mediator and the ransom (33:23–24); considering God's works with them (37:14).

**Motivation.**
- **God is greater than man** (33:12).
- **He does not pervert justice** (34:12).
- **He delivers the afflicted** (36:15).
- **He has found the ransom** (33:24) — in Christ, who gave himself (Mark 10:45).

**Four audiences.**
- **For me:** am I an Elihu — earnest, angry, sure, and quick to correct the suffering?
- **For a Christian friend:** a believer who has been "corrected" by someone like Elihu, and needs to hear that God did not endorse his verdict.
- **For the church:** do we let a sufferer stay silent, or do we fill the silence?
- **For the unbeliever:** Elihu's question, "Where is God my Maker?" (35:10), and his confession, "The Almighty — we cannot find Him" (37:23). The gospel says God has come looking for us.

**Prayer.**
- Sorry that I speak too quickly, and in anger, to those who suffer.
- Thank you that you have found the ransom, and that you came when we could not find you.
- Please give me Elihu's awe without his anger, and Job's patience to wait for your voice.

**Grammatical and syntactical features.**
- **32:2 — the infinitive with a suffix,** צַדְּקוֹ נַפְשׁוֹ ("his justifying himself"), with מִן ("from / more than") and God. The preposition can be comparative ("rather than") or locative ("before"). Both English versions are possible (Tool 8). *High.*
- **33:9 — Elihu's quotation of Job is a composite, not a citation.** "I am pure (זַךְ), without transgression; I am innocent (חַף) and there is no guilt in me." Job never said this in these words. The closest are 9:21 ("I am guiltless"), 10:7 ("I am indeed not guilty"), 16:17 ("my prayer is pure", זַכָּה) and Zophar's report, "you say, 'My teaching is pure (זַךְ)'" (11:4). In Job זַךְ occurs at 8:6 (Bildad), 11:4 (Zophar), 16:17 (Job) and 33:9 (Elihu). **Elihu quotes Job as Zophar heard him.** *High* for the words.

### Textual Variants

Classified under the three-way triage; only category 2 and the Hebrew tradition's own variation belong here.

**32:1 — "righteous in his own eyes" (בְּעֵינָיו) or "in their eyes" (בְּעֵינֵיהֶם).**
- **Evidence:** BHS: one manuscript, the Greek ("righteous before them"), Symmachus and the Syriac read "in their eyes".
- **Weighing:** on the minority reading the friends stopped because they were persuaded — which 32:3 ("and yet had condemned Job") contradicts. The Masoretic reading coheres with 32:2 ("he justified himself").
- **Position:** keep the MT. *Moderate–high.* **Preaching:** the narrator reports why the friends stopped; he does not endorse their view.

**32:3 — the scribal correction: "condemned Job" or "condemned God".**
- **Evidence:** BHS marks the line *Tiq soph* — one of the corrections the Masoretic tradition attributes to the scribes. The note says the original read הָאֱלֹהִים ("God") where the text has אִיּוֹב ("Job"). The NIV84 margin gives it: "an ancient Hebrew scribal tradition: Job, and so had condemned God". The Greek has "Job". `[S]` for the tradition.
- **Weighing:** on the tradition's reading, by finding no answer the friends had in effect condemned God. That is close to God's verdict, "you have not spoken of Me what is right" (42:7). The scribes' corrections are a list preserved by the tradition, and modern scholars differ on whether they record real earlier readings `[S: recalled]`.
- **Position:** read the MT, and tell the congregation what the tradition records. *Moderate.*

**33:16 — "seals" (MT) or "terrifies" (Greek).** BHS notes that the Greek ἐξεφόβησεν reflects יְחִתֵּם ("terrifies"). **The ESV follows it; the NASB95 keeps the MT.** Both fit; the MT links to 37:7, the Greek to 7:14. *Uncertain which is prior.*

### Historical and Cultural Background

**Setting.** The same patriarchal-style world outside Israel. Buz is a people of the Arabian margin, named with Dedan and Tema (Jer 25:23). `[S: recalled]` for the location.

**What the original audience knew.**
- **Age and the right to speak.** Wisdom belonged to the old (12:12; 15:10). For a young man to wait until his elders had spoken (32:4, 6) was proper; to speak after them was bold, and needed justifying (32:8–9). `[S: recalled]` *high.*
- **Wineskins.** New wine ferments and swells; a new skin without a vent bursts (32:19; cf. Matt 9:17). `[S: recalled]` *high.*
- **The ransom (כֹּפֶר).** In Israel's law a ransom was the price paid for a forfeited life (Exod 21:30; 30:12; Num 35:31). It is not paid for a murderer (Num 35:31). In Ps 49:8 no man can pay it for another. `[S: recalled]` *high* for the legal sense.
- **The intercessor (מֵלִיץ).** An interpreter stood between parties who could not speak to each other directly (Gen 42:23); an envoy spoke for a king (2 Chr 32:31). `[T]` for the texts.
- **Dreams and night visions** as a channel of divine warning (Gen 20:3; 31:24; 41:1–32). `[S: recalled]` *high.*
- **The storm god.** Neighbouring religions credited thunder, rain and lightning to Baal or Hadad. The Psalms and Job credit them to the LORD (Ps 29). `[S: recalled]` *high.*

**What this changes.**
- 33:23–24 is legal language. The mediator is not a comforting presence but an advocate who argues a case and names a price.
- 32:4–9 is not youthful arrogance alone. It is a claim that the Spirit, not age, gives understanding — a claim the Bible elsewhere affirms (Joel 2:28) and the book leaves untested.

### Original Audience Reception

**Canonical audience.**
- **Section:** Ketuvim. The reader knows the Torah and the Prophets — Ezekiel's "these three men" among them — and has just read the Psalter.
- **What the reader knows:** the law of ransom (Exod 21; Num 35); the Psalter's thanksgivings and storm psalms (Ps 29; 56; 62; 104); and the prologue, which Elihu does not know.
- **Register:** the reader hears Elihu with the prologue in mind and knows what he cannot: that God called Job blameless (1:8; 2:3).

**Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances.**
- **Shocking:** a fourth speaker no one announced. And that the narrator says four times he was angry.
- **Surprising:** that he quotes Job and the friends so freely after promising new words. And that the angel-mediator appears in the mouth of a young man.
- **Comforting:** "no fear of me should terrify you" (33:7); "He delivers the afflicted by their affliction" (36:15); "I have found a ransom" (33:24).
- **Disturbing:** "Job ought to be tried to the limit" (34:36) — said of a man the reader knows God calls blameless.

**What we bring that they didn't.** A suspicion of youth that may make us dismiss Elihu. A therapeutic reading that hears only comfort in 33:19–30. And a ready identification of Elihu with the preacher, which can make us defend him too quickly. `[I]`

### Biblical-Theological Themes

**1. The mediator and the ransom.**
- **Where this passage sits:** the fourth figure in Job's search for a go-between: the umpire (9:33), the witness (16:19), the Redeemer (19:25), the angel-mediator who finds a ransom (33:23–24) `[S: overview]`.
- **Before:** Exod 21:30; 30:12; Num 35:31 (ransom for a life); Ps 49:8 (no man can pay it); Gen 42:23 (the interpreter).
- **After:** Mark 10:45 ("a ransom for many"); 1 Tim 2:5–6 ("one mediator … who gave Himself as a ransom"); Heb 9:15.
- **Contribution:** 33:23–24 names the two things the gospel joins in one person: the go-between who speaks for us, and the price that redeems. *High* for the trajectory; Hebrew-only.

**2. God speaks — in pain, in the night, in the storm.**
- **Where this passage sits:** Elihu's thesis that God speaks "once, twice" (33:14) through dream, sickness and storm. It is answered, and exceeded, by God speaking directly (38:1).
- **Before:** Ps 62:12; Ps 29; Prov 3:11–12.
- **After:** Heb 1:1–2 ("God, after He spoke long ago … in many portions and in many ways, in these last days has spoken to us in His Son"); Heb 12:5–11 (discipline).
- **Contribution:** it keeps both truths: God does speak through affliction, and his last word is not affliction but himself. *Moderate–high.*

**3. The search for God and the God who comes.**
- **Where this passage sits:** "Can you find out the Almighty?" (11:7); "Oh that I knew where I might find Him" (23:3); "the Almighty — we cannot find Him" (37:23); "Then the LORD answered Job" (38:1).
- **After:** John 1:18; Luke 19:10 ("the Son of Man has come to seek").
- **Contribution:** the unit's last word sets up the book's turn. *High* for the internal chain.

**Confluence: climactic convergence.** The three themes meet in the incarnation. In Christ God speaks (theme 2), comes to be found (theme 3), and is himself the mediator and the ransom (theme 1). Elihu holds them apart; the gospel joins them. `[I]`

### Christological Reading

**Types of connection.**
- **Type with escalation:** the angel-mediator of 33:23–24.
- **Trajectory:** the God who cannot be found (37:23), and who comes (38:1).
- **Contrast:** Elihu, the self-appointed spokesman "perfect in knowledge" (36:4), against Christ, the Word who is "the radiance of His glory" (Heb 1:3).

**How the passage points to Christ.**
1. **The mediator who finds the ransom** (33:23–24). *Typology tests:*
   - category (mediation, ransom) ✓;
   - NT precedent (1 Tim 2:5–6; Mark 10:45 — the same two categories joined) ✓;
   - escalation (Elihu's mediator is "one out of a thousand", an angel who declares a ransom found; Christ is the one mediator who is himself the ransom) ✓;
   - authorial pattern — the text raises the expectation in the book's mediator sequence (9:33; 16:19; 19:25) and leaves it unresolved ✓.

   **Four of four: high** for the trajectory, on the Hebrew text. The Greek lacks it. The overview rates the mediator trajectory high `[S: overview]`; the sweep rates this point moderate–high `[S: prior dig]`.
2. **The restored man's confession** (33:26–28) — "I have sinned … He has redeemed my soul from going to the pit, and my life shall see the light" — is the shape of the gospel's restoration: confession, redemption, light (1 John 1:7–9). *Moderate* (canonical reflection).
3. **"The Almighty — we cannot find Him"** (37:23) → "No one has seen God at any time; the only begotten God … has explained Him" (John 1:18). *Moderate* (trajectory).
4. **Contrast.** Elihu claims, "one perfect in knowledge is with you" (36:4). The one in whom "are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (Col 2:3) did not claim it in anger, and spoke gently to the bruised (Isa 42:3). *Moderate.*

**Trajectory.**
- **Longed for:** a go-between who can speak for a man to God and find a ransom; a God who can be found.
- **Fulfilled:** Christ the mediator and ransom; God made known in the Son.
- **Still to come:** the full sight of God (Job 19:26; 1 John 3:2).

**Moralism check.**
- **The "be like X" temptation:** "Don't be like Elihu" (a lesson in tact), or "be like Elihu" (speak boldly for God).
- **The gospel grounding:** the unit's best word is not Elihu's manner but his angel: "Deliver him … I have found a ransom" (33:24). That ransom is given in Christ.
- **Christ as hero:** the mediator Elihu imagined, the ransom he could only announce, and the God who comes when we cannot find him.

**Confidence.** *High* for the mediator trajectory (on the Hebrew); *moderate* for the rest.

### Difficult / Contested Verses

**Elihu's status — the explicit question the series plan asks for.**
- **Category:** interpretive, homiletic.
- **The difficulty:** six chapters from a speaker never introduced before 32:2, never answered by Job, and never mentioned by God.
- **Rhetorical function:** he stands between Job's demand for an answer (31:35) and God's answer (38:1).
- **The main interpretive options** `[S: recalled]` for the history of interpretation:
  - **(a) A later insertion** that interrupts the book's design. The text has no witness against it: the Greek abbreviates him, but has him.
  - **(b) A fourth failed friend** — a young, angry repeater of the friends' doctrine.
  - **(c) The book's true answer, or a prophet** who prepares for God — Swete's 38:1 reads him that way.
  - **(d) A deliberately ambiguous voice:** half right, a test of the reader's discernment.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:**
  - **The data for (b):** his anger at Job (32:2); his recycling of the friends' words (Headline 2); his wish that Job be tested to the end (34:36); his claim to perfect knowledge (36:4); God's silence about him (42:7).
  - **The data for (c):** his verdict on the friends anticipates God's (42:7); his "words without knowledge" is God's (38:2); his question "will you condemn …?" is God's (40:8); his storm, questions and "make known" lead into God's speech (Headline 4); the penitent's words he scripts are spoken by Job (40:5).
  - **The data for (d):** both sets of data at once; and Job's silence, which neither concedes nor refutes.
  - **Against (a):** the internal links run in both directions — Elihu quotes 31:35 and 9:3, and God's speech takes up 35:16 and 34:17. That is not decisive, since an inserted speech could quote both sides `[I]`.
- **Suggested handling for the preacher:** **name the question and choose (d)**, with its reasons. Preach what Elihu gets right, mark where he goes wrong, and let God's silence about him stand. Do not caricature him as a windbag, and do not canonise him as the book's answer. Either error flattens the text.

**32:3 — "condemned Job" or "condemned God"?** See Textual Variants. **Handling:** mention the tradition; build nothing on it.

**33:19–22 — suffering as discipline.**
- **Category:** pastoral.
- **The difficulty:** read alone, Elihu's sickbed reads as "you are ill because God is correcting you".
- **What honest exegesis surfaces:** Elihu offers it as one of God's ways (33:14, 29: "God does all these oftentimes"), not as the explanation of every illness. But he does apply it to Job (34:36–37).
- **Handling:** affirm that God can use affliction to save; refuse the inference that every sufferer is being disciplined for sin. The prologue forbids it (1:8; 2:3).

**34:7–9, 36–37 — Job "drinks up derision … walks with wicked men … answers like wicked men".**
- **Category:** pastoral.
- **What honest exegesis surfaces:** God says otherwise (42:7–8).
- **Handling:** read these lines as Elihu's verdict, not the Bible's.

**36:14 — "among the cult prostitutes".** NASB95 and ESV "cult prostitutes"; NIV84 "male prostitutes of the shrines" `[S: prior dig]`. **Handling:** a description of a shameful early death; not a topic for the sermon.

**36:18–20 — the crux.** The meaning is uncertain (NIV84 note). **Handling:** do not build on it.

**37:24 — "He does not regard any who are wise of heart".** See Tool 8. **Handling:** read it as the fear of God set above human wisdom (28:28), and tell the congregation the ESV's "in their own conceit" is an interpretation.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: Skipping Elihu, or preaching him as a windbag

- **What it looks like:** "Six chapters of hot air from a young man no one asked" — and straight on to 38.
- **Why it is wrong:** Elihu names the mediator and the ransom (33:23–24), says true things about God's justice and greatness, and leads into God's speech (Headline 4). God's first line takes up his charge (38:2).
- **The corrective:** preach what he gets right — and what he gets wrong.

### Pitfall: Preaching Elihu as the book's answer

- **What it looks like:** "Elihu finally gets it right: suffering is God's discipline to save you."
- **Why it is wrong:**
  - He is angry with the man God calls "My servant";
  - he wishes Job "tried to the limit" (34:36), says he "answers like wicked men", and claims "perfect knowledge" (36:4);
  - God never endorses him;
  - and the prologue forbids reading Job's suffering as discipline for sin.
- **The corrective:** Elihu is a true voice in a wrong spirit, not the book's answer. The answer is God himself (38:1).

### Pitfall: Turning 33:19–30 into "every illness is God's correction"

- **What it looks like:** a pastoral application that tells the sick to look for the sin God is correcting.
- **Why it is wrong:** Elihu offers it as one of God's ways (33:29), and the book's frame denies that it explains Job.
- **The corrective:** God can save through affliction (36:15); affliction is not a verdict (1:8; 42:7).

### Pitfall: Building on the ESV at 37:24 or 33:14

- **What it looks like:** "He does not regard any who are wise in their own conceit" preached as the text; or "God speaks in one way, and in two" preached without hearing 40:5.
- **Why it is wrong:** the ESV has resolved 37:24's paradox, and obscured the numbers of 33:14.
- **The corrective:** read "wise of heart" and "once … twice", and point the congregation to 28:28 and 40:5.

### Pitfall: Preaching the mediator of 33:23 without the Hebrew–Greek note — or with a claim Job foresaw Christ

- **What it looks like:** "Here Elihu prophesies Christ the mediator."
- **Why it is wrong:** the angel is "one out of a thousand", not God himself, and the Greek lacks the passage altogether. The connection is a canonical trajectory, not Elihu's prediction.
- **The corrective:** preach the trajectory: Elihu imagines a go-between who finds a ransom; the gospel gives the one mediator who is himself the ransom (1 Tim 2:5–6).
