The Passage

1:1 אִ֛ישׁ הָיָ֥ה בְאֶֽרֶץ־ע֖וּץ אִיּ֣וֹב שְׁמ֑וֹ וְהָיָ֣ה ׀ הָאִ֣ישׁ הַה֗וּא תָּ֧ם וְיָשָׁ֛ר וִירֵ֥א אֱלֹהִ֖ים וְסָ֥ר מֵרָֽע׃

There was a man in the land of Uz whose name was Job; and that man was blameless, upright, fearing God and turning away from evil.

1:2 וַיִּוָּ֥לְדוּ ל֛וֹ שִׁבְעָ֥ה בָנִ֖ים וְשָׁל֥וֹשׁ בָּנֽוֹת׃

Seven sons and three daughters were born to him.

1:3 וַיְהִ֣י מִ֠קְנֵהוּ שִֽׁבְעַ֨ת אַלְפֵי־צֹ֜אן וּשְׁלֹ֧שֶׁת אַלְפֵ֣י גְמַלִּ֗ים וַחֲמֵ֨שׁ מֵא֤וֹת צֶֽמֶד־בָּקָר֙ וַחֲמֵ֣שׁ מֵא֣וֹת אֲתוֹנ֔וֹת וַעֲבֻדָּ֖ה רַבָּ֣ה מְאֹ֑ד וַיְהִי֙ הָאִ֣ישׁ הַה֔וּא גָּד֖וֹל מִכָּל־בְּנֵי־קֶֽדֶם׃

His possessions also were 7,000 sheep, 3,000 camels, 500 yoke of oxen, 500 female donkeys, and very many servants; and that man was the greatest of all the men of the east.

1:4 וְהָלְכ֤וּ בָנָיו֙ וְעָשׂ֣וּ מִשְׁתֶּ֔ה בֵּ֖ית אִ֣ישׁ יוֹמ֑וֹ וְשָׁלְח֗וּ וְקָרְאוּ֙ לִשְׁלֹ֣שֶׁת אחיתיהם לֶאֱכֹ֥ל וְלִשְׁתּ֖וֹת עִמָּהֶֽם׃

His sons used to go and hold a feast in the house of each one on his day, and they would send and invite their three sisters to eat and drink with them.

1:5 וַיְהִ֡י כִּ֣י הִקִּיפֽוּ֩ יְמֵ֨י הַמִּשְׁתֶּ֜ה וַיִּשְׁלַ֧ח אִיּ֣וֹב וַֽיְקַדְּשֵׁ֗ם וְהִשְׁכִּ֣ים בַּבֹּקֶר֮ וְהֶעֱלָ֣ה עֹלוֹת֮ מִסְפַּ֣ר כֻּלָּם֒ כִּ֚י אָמַ֣ר אִיּ֔וֹב אוּלַי֙ חָטְא֣וּ בָנַ֔י וּבֵרֲכ֥וּ אֱלֹהִ֖ים בִּלְבָבָ֑ם כָּ֛כָה יַעֲשֶׂ֥ה אִיּ֖וֹב כָּל־הַיָּמִֽים׃

When the days of feasting had completed their cycle, Job would send and consecrate them, rising up early in the morning and offering burnt offerings according to the number of them all; for Job said, “Perhaps my sons have sinned and cursed God in their hearts.” Thus Job did continually.

1:6 וַיְהִ֣י הַיּ֔וֹם וַיָּבֹ֨אוּ֙ בְּנֵ֣י הָאֱלֹהִ֔ים לְהִתְיַצֵּ֖ב עַל־יְהוָ֑ה וַיָּב֥וֹא גַֽם־הַשָּׂטָ֖ן בְּתוֹכָֽם׃

Now there was a day when the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan also came among them.

1:7 וַיֹּ֧אמֶר יְהוָ֛ה אֶל־הַשָּׂטָ֖ן מֵאַ֣יִן תָּבֹ֑א וַיַּ֨עַן הַשָּׂטָ֤ן אֶת־יְהוָה֙ וַיֹּאמַ֔ר מִשּׁ֣וּט בָּאָ֔רֶץ וּמֵֽהִתְהַלֵּ֖ךְ בָּֽהּ׃

The LORD said to Satan, “From where do you come?” Then Satan answered the LORD and said, “From roaming about on the earth and walking around on it.”

1:8 וַיֹּ֤אמֶר יְהוָה֙ אֶל־הַשָּׂטָ֔ן הֲשַׂ֥מְתָּ לִבְּךָ֖ עַל־עַבְדִּ֣י אִיּ֑וֹב כִּ֣י אֵ֤ין כָּמֹ֨הוּ֙ בָּאָ֔רֶץ אִ֣ישׁ תָּ֧ם וְיָשָׁ֛ר יְרֵ֥א אֱלֹהִ֖ים וְסָ֥ר מֵרָֽע׃

The LORD said to Satan, “Have you considered My servant Job? For there is no one like him on the earth, a blameless and upright man, fearing God and turning away from evil.”

1:9 וַיַּ֧עַן הַשָּׂטָ֛ן אֶת־יְהוָ֖ה וַיֹּאמַ֑ר הַֽחִנָּ֔ם יָרֵ֥א אִיּ֖וֹב אֱלֹהִֽים׃

Then Satan answered the LORD, “Does Job fear God for nothing?

1:10 הֲלֹֽא־את שַׂ֣כְתָּ בַעֲד֧וֹ וּבְעַד־בֵּית֛וֹ וּבְעַ֥ד כָּל־אֲשֶׁר־ל֖וֹ מִסָּבִ֑יב מַעֲשֵׂ֤ה יָדָיו֙ בֵּרַ֔כְתָּ וּמִקְנֵ֖הוּ פָּרַ֥ץ בָּאָֽרֶץ׃

“Have You not made a hedge about him and his house and all that he has, on every side? You have blessed the work of his hands, and his possessions have increased in the land.

1:11 וְאוּלָם֙ שְֽׁלַֽח־נָ֣א יָֽדְךָ֔ וְגַ֖ע בְּכָל־אֲשֶׁר־ל֑וֹ אִם־לֹ֥א עַל־פָּנֶ֖יךָ יְבָרֲכֶֽךָּ׃

“But put forth Your hand now and touch all that he has; he will surely curse You to Your face.”

1:12 וַיֹּ֨אמֶר יְהוָ֜ה אֶל־הַשָּׂטָ֗ן הִנֵּ֤ה כָל־אֲשֶׁר־לוֹ֙ בְּיָדֶ֔ךָ רַ֣ק אֵלָ֔יו אַל־תִּשְׁלַ֖ח יָדֶ֑ךָ וַיֵּצֵא֙ הַשָּׂטָ֔ן מֵעִ֖ם פְּנֵ֥י יְהוָֽה׃

Then the LORD said to Satan, “Behold, all that he has is in your power, only do not put forth your hand on him.” So Satan departed from the presence of the LORD.

1:13 וַיְהִ֖י הַיּ֑וֹם וּבָנָ֨יו וּבְנֹתָ֤יו אֹֽכְלִים֙ וְשֹׁתִ֣ים יַ֔יִן בְּבֵ֖ית אֲחִיהֶ֥ם הַבְּכֽוֹר׃

Now on the day when his sons and his daughters were eating and drinking wine in their oldest brother’s house,

1:14 וּמַלְאָ֛ךְ בָּ֥א אֶל־אִיּ֖וֹב וַיֹּאמַ֑ר הַבָּקָר֙ הָי֣וּ חֹֽרְשׁ֔וֹת וְהָאֲתֹנ֖וֹת רֹע֥וֹת עַל־יְדֵיהֶֽם׃

a messenger came to Job and said, “The oxen were plowing and the donkeys feeding beside them,

1:15 וַתִּפֹּ֤ל שְׁבָא֙ וַתִּקָּחֵ֔ם וְאֶת־הַנְּעָרִ֖ים הִכּ֣וּ לְפִי־חָ֑רֶב וָֽאִמָּ֨לְטָ֧ה רַק־אֲנִ֛י לְבַדִּ֖י לְהַגִּ֥יד לָֽךְ׃

and the Sabeans attacked and took them. They also slew the servants with the edge of the sword, and I alone have escaped to tell you.”

1:16 ע֣וֹד ׀ זֶ֣ה מְדַבֵּ֗ר וְזֶה֮ בָּ֣א וַיֹּאמַר֒ אֵ֣שׁ אֱלֹהִ֗ים נָֽפְלָה֙ מִן־הַשָּׁמַ֔יִם וַתִּבְעַ֥ר בַּצֹּ֛אן וּבַנְּעָרִ֖ים וַתֹּאכְלֵ֑ם וָאִמָּ֨לְטָ֧ה רַק־אֲנִ֛י לְבַדִּ֖י לְהַגִּ֥יד לָֽךְ׃

While he was still speaking, another also came and said, “The fire of God fell from heaven and burned up the sheep and the servants and consumed them, and I alone have escaped to tell you.”

1:17 ע֣וֹד ׀ זֶ֣ה מְדַבֵּ֗ר וְזֶה֮ בָּ֣א וַיֹּאמַר֒ כַּשְׂדִּ֞ים שָׂ֣מוּ ׀ שְׁלֹשָׁ֣ה רָאשִׁ֗ים וַֽיִּפְשְׁט֤וּ עַל־הַגְּמַלִּים֙ וַיִּקָּח֔וּם וְאֶת־הַנְּעָרִ֖ים הִכּ֣וּ לְפִי־חָ֑רֶב וָאִמָּ֨לְטָ֧ה רַק־אֲנִ֛י לְבַדִּ֖י לְהַגִּ֥יד לָֽךְ׃

While he was still speaking, another also came and said, “The Chaldeans formed three bands and made a raid on the camels and took them and slew the servants with the edge of the sword, and I alone have escaped to tell you.”

1:18 עַ֚ד זֶ֣ה מְדַבֵּ֔ר וְזֶ֖ה בָּ֣א וַיֹּאמַ֑ר בָּנֶ֨יךָ וּבְנוֹתֶ֤יךָ אֹֽכְלִים֙ וְשֹׁתִ֣ים יַ֔יִן בְּבֵ֖ית אֲחִיהֶ֥ם הַבְּכֽוֹר׃

While he was still speaking, another also came and said, “Your sons and your daughters were eating and drinking wine in their oldest brother’s house,

1:19 וְהִנֵּה֩ ר֨וּחַ גְּדוֹלָ֜ה בָּ֣אָה ׀ מֵעֵ֣בֶר הַמִּדְבָּ֗ר וַיִּגַּע֙ בְּאַרְבַּע֙ פִּנּ֣וֹת הַבַּ֔יִת וַיִּפֹּ֥ל עַל־הַנְּעָרִ֖ים וַיָּמ֑וּתוּ וָאִמָּ֨לְטָ֧ה רַק־אֲנִ֛י לְבַדִּ֖י לְהַגִּ֥יד לָֽךְ׃

and behold, a great wind came from across the wilderness and struck the four corners of the house, and it fell on the young people and they died, and I alone have escaped to tell you.”

1:20 וַיָּ֤קָם אִיּוֹב֙ וַיִּקְרַ֣ע אֶת־מְעִל֔וֹ וַיָּ֖גָז אֶת־רֹאשׁ֑וֹ וַיִּפֹּ֥ל אַ֖רְצָה וַיִּשְׁתָּֽחוּ׃

Then Job arose and tore his robe and shaved his head, and he fell to the ground and worshiped.

1:21 וַיֹּאמֶר֩ עָרֹ֨ם יצתי מִבֶּ֣טֶן אִמִּ֗י וְעָרֹם֙ אָשׁ֣וּב שָׁ֔מָה יְהוָ֣ה נָתַ֔ן וַיהוָ֖ה לָקָ֑ח יְהִ֛י שֵׁ֥ם יְהוָ֖ה מְבֹרָֽךְ׃

He said, / “Naked I came from my mother’s womb, / And naked I shall return there. / The LORD gave and the LORD has taken away. / Blessed be the name of the LORD.”

1:22 בְּכָל־זֹ֖את לֹא־חָטָ֣א אִיּ֑וֹב וְלֹא־נָתַ֥ן תִּפְלָ֖ה לֵאלֹהִֽים׃

Through all this Job did not sin nor did he blame God.

2:1 וַיְהִ֣י הַיּ֔וֹם וַיָּבֹ֨אוּ֙ בְּנֵ֣י הָֽאֱלֹהִ֔ים לְהִתְיַצֵּ֖ב עַל־יְהוָ֑ה וַיָּב֤וֹא גַֽם־הַשָּׂטָן֙ בְּתֹכָ֔ם לְהִתְיַצֵּ֖ב עַל־יְהוָֽה׃

Again there was a day when the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan also came among them to present himself before the LORD.

2:2 וַיֹּ֤אמֶר יְהוָה֙ אֶל־הַשָּׂטָ֔ן אֵ֥י מִזֶּ֖ה תָּבֹ֑א וַיַּ֨עַן הַשָּׂטָ֤ן אֶת־יְהוָה֙ וַיֹּאמַ֔ר מִשֻּׁ֣ט בָּאָ֔רֶץ וּמֵהִתְהַלֵּ֖ךְ בָּֽהּ׃

The LORD said to Satan, “Where have you come from?” Then Satan answered the LORD and said, “From roaming about on the earth and walking around on it.”

2:3 וַיֹּ֨אמֶר יְהוָ֜ה אֶל־הַשָּׂטָ֗ן הֲשַׂ֣מְתָּ לִבְּךָ֮ אֶל־עַבְדִּ֣י אִיּוֹב֒ כִּי֩ אֵ֨ין כָּמֹ֜הוּ בָּאָ֗רֶץ אִ֣ישׁ תָּ֧ם וְיָשָׁ֛ר יְרֵ֥א אֱלֹהִ֖ים וְסָ֣ר מֵרָ֑ע וְעֹדֶ֨נּוּ֙ מַחֲזִ֣יק בְּתֻמָּת֔וֹ וַתְּסִיתֵ֥נִי ב֖וֹ לְבַלְּע֥וֹ חִנָּֽם׃

The LORD said to Satan, “Have you considered My servant Job? For there is no one like him on the earth, a blameless and upright man fearing God and turning away from evil. And he still holds fast his integrity, although you incited Me against him to ruin him without cause.”

2:4 וַיַּ֧עַן הַשָּׂטָ֛ן אֶת־יְהוָ֖ה וַיֹּאמַ֑ר ע֣וֹר בְּעַד־ע֗וֹר וְכֹל֙ אֲשֶׁ֣ר לָאִ֔ישׁ יִתֵּ֖ן בְּעַ֥ד נַפְשֽׁוֹ׃

Satan answered the LORD and said, “Skin for skin! Yes, all that a man has he will give for his life.

2:5 אוּלָם֙ שְֽׁלַֽח־נָ֣א יָֽדְךָ֔ וְגַ֥ע אֶל־עַצְמ֖וֹ וְאֶל־בְּשָׂר֑וֹ אִם־לֹ֥א אֶל־פָּנֶ֖יךָ יְבָרֲכֶֽךָּ׃

“However, put forth Your hand now, and touch his bone and his flesh; he will curse You to Your face.”

2:6 וַיֹּ֧אמֶר יְהוָ֛ה אֶל־הַשָּׂטָ֖ן הִנּ֣וֹ בְיָדֶ֑ךָ אַ֖ךְ אֶת־נַפְשׁ֥וֹ שְׁמֹֽר׃

So the LORD said to Satan, “Behold, he is in your power, only spare his life.”

2:7 וַיֵּצֵא֙ הַשָּׂטָ֔ן מֵאֵ֖ת פְּנֵ֣י יְהוָ֑ה וַיַּ֤ךְ אֶת־אִיּוֹב֙ בִּשְׁחִ֣ין רָ֔ע מִכַּ֥ף רַגְל֖וֹ עד קָדְקֳדֽוֹ׃

Then Satan went out from the presence of the LORD and smote Job with sore boils from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head.

2:8 וַיִּֽקַּֽח־ל֣וֹ חֶ֔רֶשׂ לְהִתְגָּרֵ֖ד בּ֑וֹ וְה֖וּא יֹשֵׁ֥ב בְּתוֹךְ־הָאֵֽפֶר׃

And he took a potsherd to scrape himself while he was sitting among the ashes.

2:9 וַתֹּ֤אמֶר לוֹ֙ אִשְׁתּ֔וֹ עֹדְךָ֖ מַחֲזִ֣יק בְּתֻמָּתֶ֑ךָ בָּרֵ֥ךְ אֱלֹהִ֖ים וָמֻֽת׃

Then his wife said to him, “Do you still hold fast your integrity? Curse God and die!”

Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims, each surfaced by several tools:

  1. The prologue is one question tested twice, and God answers it with the Accuser’s own word. [T]
    • The Accuser asks הַחִנָּם יָרֵא אִיּוֹב אֱלֹהִים (“Does Job fear God for nothing?”, 1:9).
    • After the first test God says, “you incited Me against him to ruin him without cause (חִנָּם)” (2:3). The word is the same.
    • The second test goes deeper. First “all that he has”, then “his bone and his flesh” (1:11 → 2:5). God’s limit narrows from רַק אֵלָיו (“only … on him”, 1:12) to אַךְ אֶת־נַפְשׁוֹ (“only … his life”, 2:6).
    • Both tests end with the same verdict, בְּכָל־זֹאת לֹא־חָטָא אִיּוֹב (“Through all this Job did not sin”, 1:22; 2:10) — the phrase occurs only in these two verses (phrase search).
    • Surfaced by: Structure, Repetition, Vocabulary, Narrator’s Comment, Move 4. High.
  2. “Bless” carries the plot, and the narrator’s only “curse” falls on Job’s day. [T] new, at unit level
    • ברך occurs six times in the unit (1:5, 10, 11, 21; 2:5, 9, lemma). Four times it is the polite substitute for “curse”: the sons’ feared sin (1:5), the Accuser’s two predictions (1:11; 2:5), and the wife’s advice (2:9). Twice it means “bless”: God blessed Job’s work (1:10), and Job blesses God’s name (1:21).
    • The real word for “curse”, קלל, appears only once in the whole unit. At 3:1 Job “cursed his day” — his day, not God.
    • The Accuser’s prediction — that Job would “bless” God to his face — is answered twice with a refusal. The unit ends on the one curse it allows: a curse on a day.
    • Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Repetition, Translations, Narrator’s Comment. High.
  3. The prologue is five scenes, alternating earth and heaven, and the reader alone sees heaven. [T]
    • The scenes run: 1:1–5 (earth) | 1:6–12 (heaven) | 1:13–22 (earth) | 2:1–6 (heaven) | 2:7–3:1 (earth) [S: audit].
    • The scene openings are marked by וַיְהִי הַיּוֹם (“Now there was a day”) at 1:6, 1:13 and 2:1 — the only three occurrences in Job. The fifth scene has no such marker. Instead, “Satan went out from the presence of the LORD and smote Job” (2:7): the Accuser himself crosses from heaven to earth.
    • new: The covenant name יְהוָה (“the LORD”) occurs eighteen times in twelve verses of the unit (lemma). Every occurrence is in a heaven scene or at the threshold as the Accuser leaves it (1:12; 2:7), except one verse: Job’s own 1:21, where the name stands three times. On earth everyone else says אֱלֹהִים (“God”): the messenger (1:16), the narrator (1:22), the wife (2:9), and Job himself the second time (2:10).
    • Surfaced by: Structure, Narrator’s Comment, Repetition, Genre. High.
  4. The words of the Torah’s promises and curses, and of Abraham’s test, lie on the surface of the text. [T] (synthetic)
    • “You have blessed the work of his hands” (1:10) is Deuteronomy’s covenant-blessing formula. ברך with מַעֲשֵׂה יָד occurs only at Deut 2:7; 14:29; 15:10; 16:15; 24:19; 28:12; Isa 19:25; Job 1:10.
    • “Sore boils from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head” (2:7) is Deuteronomy’s curse. שְׁחִין with קָדְקֹד occurs only at Deut 28:35 and Job 2:7.
    • Deut 28 is used at both ends of the covenant, in the Accuser’s charge and in the Accuser’s blow.
    • The test itself is told in Abraham’s words. “Rising early” with “burnt offering” occurs in one verse only at Gen 22:3 and Job 1:5. “Do not put forth your hand” occurs only at Gen 22:12 and Job 1:12.
    • Surfaced by: Tool 11 (Moves 1–3), Vocabulary, Original Audience. High on the words; moderate on the design.
  5. Three responses to loss, and the book commends two. [T] / [I]
    • Job grieves and worships: five verbs, then “Blessed be the name of the LORD” (1:20–21). His second answer receives both good and evil from God (2:10).
    • The wife repeats God’s words — “Do you still hold fast your integrity?” (2:9 = 2:3; חזק with תֻּמָּה occurs only in these two verses) — and draws the Accuser’s conclusion: “Curse God and die.”
    • The friends come “to sympathize … and comfort”, weep, and sit “seven days and seven nights with no one speaking a word to him, for they saw that his pain was very great” (2:11–13). Job later says their silence would have been their wisdom (13:5).
    • The unit ends when that silence breaks, and it is Job who breaks it (3:1).
    • Surfaced by: Structure, Copycat, Who Am I?, Tone, Move 4. High.

The Sixteen Tools

1. Author’s Purpose

Book level. The overview’s purpose is confirmed. The book shows a righteous man who, stripped of everything, will neither curse God nor buy relief by confessing sins he did not commit. It exposes the friends’ theology, and brings the sufferer and the reader to a fear of God that wants God himself, reached through God’s self-disclosure rather than an explanation. [I]

Unit level. The prologue does four things. [T]

  • It gives the verdict before the trial. Job’s character is stated in the same four terms by the narrator (1:1) and by God (1:8; 2:3).
  • It poses the question. “Does Job fear God for nothing?” (1:9). This is a question about God’s worth as well as Job’s integrity.
  • It answers the question twice, in deed and word. “The LORD gave and the LORD has taken away. Blessed be the name of the LORD” (1:21). “Shall we indeed accept good from God and not accept adversity?” (2:10).
  • It stages the dialogue. It supplies the friends and their silence, and lets Job break it with a curse on his day (3:1). The reader knows what the speakers will never learn: God has said “without cause” (2:3).

Selection. The narrator gives the Accuser no reply to God’s “without cause” (2:3–4). He gives Job no reason, then or later. He gives the wife one line and no name. He gives no verdict on the children’s feasts. The catastrophe takes four verses, while Job’s response and the friends’ silence are drawn out. [T]

2. Context

Positional Necessity Check

Preceding movement. In the BHS order the reader comes to Job straight from the Psalter, which promises blessing to the man who fears the LORD. - “How blessed is the man who fears the LORD … wealth and riches are in his house” (Ps 112:1, 3). - “Thus shall the man be blessed who fears the LORD” — with “the fruit of your hands” (Hebrew יְגִיעַ, “labour”) and sons “around your table” (Ps 128:2–4). - “The counsel of the wicked (עֲצַת רְשָׁעִים)” (Ps 1:1) is a phrase shared only with Job (10:3; 21:16; 22:18). [T] for the words.

Job 1 opens on exactly such a man. The unit puts the Psalter’s promise to the test. [I]

Necessity answer. The prologue must come first, and must be whole, for three reasons. [I] - The reader must be given heaven’s knowledge before the speakers argue without it. The five-scene alternation is the device that does this. - Both tests must be complete — possessions (ch. 1) and body (ch. 2) — before the Accuser’s theory is disproved. Only then can the dialogue move to the second question: does suffering prove guilt? - The friends must arrive, and fall silent, so that the silence can break into the lament (3:1).

Implication. Preaching 1:1–3:1 as one unit is not a compromise: the prologue’s meaning lies in the doubling. What is repeated shows the pattern; what changes shows the escalation. One sermon should hold heaven and earth, test and test, silence and scream.

4. Linking Words

  • כִּי (“for”, 1:5). Job’s sacrifices rest on a fear, not a fact: “for Job said, ‘Perhaps my sons have sinned.’” [T]
  • הֲלֹא … וְאוּלָם (“Have You not…? But…”, 1:10–11) and אוּלָם (“However”, 2:5). The Accuser argues in two steps both times. [T]
  • אִם־לֹא (1:11; 2:5). An oath with its curse unspoken, sworn twice. The two oaths are the only occurrences of עַל־פָּנֶיךָ / אֶל־פָּנֶיךָ (“to Your face”) in the unit. [T]
  • רַק (1:12, 15, 16, 17, 19) and אַךְ (2:6). God’s limits. רַק also runs through the survivors’ “I alone (רַק־אֲנִי)”. [T]
  • וְעֹדֶנּוּ … וַתְּסִיתֵנִי (“and he still … although you incited Me”, 2:3) → עֹדְךָ (“do you still…?”, 2:9). God’s boast becomes the wife’s reproach. [T]
  • בְּכָל־זֹאת (“through all this”, 1:22; 2:10). Each test is gathered into a verdict. [T]
  • כִּי רָאוּ (“for they saw”, 2:13). The narrator gives the reason for the friends’ silence. [T]
  • אַחֲרֵי־כֵן (“afterward”, 3:1). The only temporal “afterward” in the unit. It ends the prose and opens the poetry. [T]

5. Parallels

Three poetic sayings inside the prose:

  • 1:21 — two antithetical pairs (came / return; gave / taken) and a single closing line of blessing. The shape is poetic, though the accents are prose. [T]
  • 2:4 — the Accuser’s proverb, “Skin for skin! Yes, all that a man has he will give for his life.” The second line explains the first. [T]
  • 2:10 — “Shall we indeed accept good from God and not accept adversity [evil]?” — antithetical, with קבל (“accept”) repeated in both halves. [T]

The job of 1:21 is taken over by 2:10. Where 1:21 blessed the name, 2:10 generalises: God gives both good and evil. new: Apart from his private reasoning about his sons (1:5, “for Job said”), the two sayings are Job’s only words before 3:1. Between them, he moves from YHWH to הָאֱלֹהִים. [T]

6. Narrator’s Comment

  • 1:1 — the verdict before the story. [T]
  • 1:5 — the habit: “Thus Job did continually.” [T]
  • 1:22 — “Through all this Job did not sin nor did he blame God (תִּפְלָה).” It answers Job’s fear for his sons: “perhaps my sons have sinned” (1:5) → “Job did not sin” (1:22). It also approves Job’s “the LORD has taken away”, even though the narrator knows the Accuser’s hand was in it. [T]
  • 2:10 — “In all this Job did not sin with his lips.” It is narrower than 1:22, and it omits the תִּפְלָה clause. Rabbinic tradition read a hint of the heart in it (b. B. Bat. 16a) [S]. The next word the narrator gives Job is “opened his mouth” (3:1). new: The unit’s sin-vocabulary runs from the sons’ possible sin “in their hearts” (1:5), through “did not sin” (1:22), to “did not sin with his lips” (2:10). חטא (“sin”) occurs only at those three verses.   • 2:11, 13 — the friends’ purpose and the reason for their silence, both sympathetic. [T]
  • 3:1 — the hinge: “Afterward Job opened his mouth and cursed his day.” [T]

What the narrator withholds. He never tells Job about the council, and he never tells the reader why God accepted the wager beyond the Accuser’s question. [T]

9. Tone and Feel

The unit’s emotional arc in five scenes.

  1. Serene (1:1–5). A storyteller’s portrait, with round numbers and habitual verbs.
  2. Ceremonial (1:6–12). A court in set phrases, and the wager.
  3. A cataract (1:13–19), then worship (1:20–22). Four blows, each cut off by the next — “while he was still speaking”. Then five verbs of grief and a blessing.
  4. Ceremonial again, and chilling (2:1–6). The council almost word for word, so that the new sentences land: “without cause”, “skin for skin”.
  5. Brutal, domestic, tender, silent (2:7–3:1).
    • A single blow from sole to crown.
    • A potsherd and ashes.
    • A three-word question, a three-word command, a steady reply.
    • Friends travelling, weeping, throwing dust.
    • Seven days of silence.
    • A mouth opened to curse.

The soundtrack. A chorale; a court theme; four drumbeats and the chorale sung back; the court theme again, in a minor key; one blow; two voices; a long rest; and a scream. For the preacher: a single sermon on 1:1–3:1 must pace itself so that the silence of 2:13 is felt. Don’t spend the whole time on chapter 1. The unit’s final note is not the triumph of 2:10 but the rest before 3:1. [T] for the devices; [I] for the register.

12. Genre

Hebrew prose narrative — the prose frame of a poetic disputation. [T] (prose accentuation throughout 1:1–3:1 in the WLC; the poetic accents from 3:2.)

  • Stylised narrative. Five scenes, doubled councils, a four-messenger sequence, round numbers, formulaic verdicts. This is a storyteller’s patterning. Whether it reports history or composes a frame for a wisdom disputation is [S]; Ezekiel and James name Job as a real exemplar (Ezek 14:14, 20; Jas 5:11). The reading rules do not change.
  • Reading rules for the unit.
    • The narrator’s statements are authoritative (1:1, 5, 22; 2:10, 13).
    • The speakers’ statements are not: the messengers’ “fire of God”, the Accuser’s theory, and the wife’s counsel.
    • The heaven scenes are disclosure to the reader, not explanation to the sufferer.
  • Embedded forms.
    • two court scenes (the divine council type-scene: compare 1 Kgs 22:19–23);
    • a messenger-report sequence;
    • mourning rites;
    • three short poetic sayings (1:21; 2:4; 2:10);
    • a comforters’ visit;
    • a transition formula to poetry (3:1).
  • The genre of the whole unit is a frame, not a self-contained tale. It poses the question the poetry argues (why does the righteous suffer?), and gives the answer the poetry never hears (2:3). That is why 3:1 belongs to it. [I]

Preaching Pitfalls

Pitfall: Preaching only chapter 1, or only 1:20–22

  • What it looks like: a sermon that ends with “blessed be the name of the LORD”, as though the prologue were a success story.
  • Why it’s wrong: the prologue is built on doubling. The second test goes deeper (the body), the verdict narrows (“with his lips”), and the unit ends not in triumph but in seven days of silence and a curse on his day (3:1).
  • The corrective: preach the unit as one movement. Give the second council and the silence their weight, and end with 3:1.

Pitfall: “The patience of Job”

  • What it looks like: Job as the model of composure — accept, don’t question.
  • Why it’s wrong: the torn robe precedes the blessing (1:20); chapter 3 follows it. Jas 5:11’s word is ὑπομονή (“endurance”), and God says Job spoke rightly (42:7) after the laments.
  • The corrective: endurance that grieves, worships and laments — all addressed to God.

Pitfall: Dualism — “Satan did it, not God”

  • What it looks like: protecting God by blaming the Accuser.
  • Why it’s wrong: God opens the conversation (1:8; 2:3), sets the limits (1:12; 2:6), and says “you incited Me against him to ruin him without cause” (2:3). Job ascribes the loss to the LORD and to God (1:21; 2:10), and the narrator rules both no sin.
  • The corrective: hold both. The Accuser acts; God permits, limits and owns it. The text does not resolve the tension, and the preacher must not either.

Pitfall: “God is proving you, as he proved Job”

  • What it looks like: the council as a pastoral key to the hearer’s suffering.
  • Why it’s wrong: the reader is shown the council, not Job. No sufferer is told which scene lies behind their grief. The friends make the equivalent move, and are condemned (42:7).
  • The corrective: the council teaches that there is a scene we cannot see — and forbids us to claim we can see it.

Pitfall: Villainising Job’s wife

  • What it looks like: “Satan’s tool”, “the devil’s helpmeet”.
  • Why it’s wrong: she speaks one line, has lost the same children, and the text gives no verdict on her. Job rebukes her words and includes her in “we” (2:10).
  • The corrective: preach her counsel as the voice of despair many hearers know.

Pitfall: Hurrying past the silence

  • What it looks like: 2:11–13 treated as a bridge to the speeches, or the friends painted as villains from the start.
  • Why it’s wrong: the narrator gives them good motives (2:11) and a sympathetic inside view (2:13). Their silence is the book’s one moment of true comfort, and Job says so (13:5).
  • The corrective: let the silence be the unit’s application for the church.

Pitfall: Prosperity or transaction

  • What it looks like: the hedge (1:10) as the believer’s normal portion, or 42:10–17 promised to the faithful sufferer.
  • Why it’s wrong: the Accuser’s theory is exactly that piety is paid. The book refutes it. The children are not doubled (42:13).
  • The corrective: blessings are gifts, not wages; God is worth fearing for nothing.
