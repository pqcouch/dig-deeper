## Group A — single contacts asserted in recent work. Verify each independently.

**A1.** The lemmas for "glean" and "leave" stand together in one verse in exactly three verses of the Hebrew Bible: Leviticus 19:10, Leviticus 23:22, Ruth 2:16.

**A2.** Leviticus 19:10 and Leviticus 23:22 carry an identical clause meaning "you shall leave them for the poor and the sojourner".

**A3.** The lemma for "sheaf" (not the dry measure) stands in exactly eight verses of the canon: Deuteronomy 24:19, Job 24:10, Leviticus 23:10, 11, 12, 15, Ruth 2:7, Ruth 2:15. The dry measure of Exodus 16 is a different lemma and stands in six verses, all in Exodus 16.

**A4.** "Roasted grain" stands in exactly five verses: Leviticus 23:14, 1 Samuel 17:17, 1 Samuel 25:18, 2 Samuel 17:28, Ruth 2:14.

**A5.** The book of Ruth contains none of the following, and each absence survives a positive control: "widow"; "sojourner" (the noun); "orphan"; "poor"; "forget"; the "edge" of a field. Further, the verb "to sojourn" occurs in Ruth exactly once, at 1:1.

**A6.** The lemmas for "recognise" (hiphil of nkr) and "foreign" stand together in exactly one verse of the Hebrew Bible: Ruth 2:10.

**A7.** "Be satisfied" and "have left over" stand together in one verse in exactly two verses of the canon: 2 Chronicles 31:10 and Ruth 2:14.

**A8.** "Wing" and "take refuge" stand together in one verse in exactly five verses: Psalm 36:8, 57:2, 61:5, 91:4, Ruth 2:12. Of these, Psalm 91:4 alone shares Ruth 2:12's ordered phrase "under his wings", and the match is skeletal rather than plain because the spelling differs.

**A9.** The piel verb "repay" and the adjective "full" (two different lemmas of the same root) stand together in exactly one verse of the canon: Ruth 2:12.

**A10.** "Wages" stands in exactly four verses: Genesis 29:15, 31:7, 31:41, Ruth 2:12.

**A11.** "Happen" with its cognate noun "chance" stands in exactly three verses: Ecclesiastes 2:14, 2:15, Ruth 2:3. The noun alone stands in eight verses, five of them in Ecclesiastes.

**A12.** Three words in Ruth 2:14–16 are hapax legomena — each occurring in one verse of the Hebrew Bible: the verb at 2:14 meaning "held out / heaped up", and at 2:16 the verb meaning "pull out" and the noun meaning "bundles".

**A13.** "Bitter" (hiphil) with the divine name Shaddai stands together in one verse in exactly two verses: Job 27:2 and Ruth 1:20.

**A14.** Shaddai with the verb "answer" stands together in exactly three verses: Job 31:35, Job 40:2, Ruth 1:21. Shaddai stands in 48 verses of the canon, 31 of them in Job.

**A15.** All three occurrences of the consonantal root ʿnh in Ruth (1:21, 2:6, 2:11) are filed by the index under the same dictionary entry, the one meaning "answer".

**A16.** "Full" and "empty" stand together in one verse in exactly one verse of the canon: Ruth 1:21. The word "empty" stands in sixteen verses, and in Ruth only at 1:21 and 3:17.

**A17.** The verb "be stirred" at Ruth 1:19 stands in exactly five verses of the canon: 1 Samuel 4:5, 1 Kings 1:45, Micah 2:12, Psalm 55:3, Ruth 1:19.

**A18.** "Conception" stands by lemma in exactly three verses: Genesis 3:16, Hosea 9:11, Ruth 4:13. The full spelling stands at Hosea 9:11 and Ruth 4:13; Genesis 3:16 has a shorter form.

**A19.** The lemma "child" stands in exactly two verses of Ruth: 1:5 and 4:16.

**A20.** The verb "bear/give birth" stands in ten verses of Ruth, nine of them in chapter 4; the tenth is 1:12.

**A21.** The root šwb stands in thirteen verses of Ruth, eleven of them in chapter 1, and its last occurrence in the book is at 4:15. The form at 1:21 and the form at 4:15 are both hiphil of that root.

**A22.** The verb form *tiqreʾnah* occurs in Ruth exactly four times: 1:20, 1:21, and twice at 4:17.

**A23.** Four pairings each stand in exactly one verse of the Hebrew Bible: (a) hiphil "cause to cease" with "redeemer"; (b) "sustain" with "old age"; (c) "good/better" with "seven" and "son"; (d) "bosom" with "set/put".

**A24.** "Love" occurs in Ruth exactly once, at 4:15. "Love" with "seven" stands together in one verse in exactly four verses: Genesis 29:18, 29:20, 29:30, Ruth 4:15.

**A25.** "Daughter-in-law" stands in seven verses of Ruth, which is more occurrences than in any other book of the canon.

**A26.** The feminine form of the participle of ʾmn meaning "nurse" occurs in the Hebrew Bible only at Ruth 4:16; every other occurrence of that participle in the nursing or guardian sense is masculine.

**A27.** "Nurse" (ʾmn) with "bosom" stands together in exactly three verses: Numbers 11:12, Micah 7:5, Ruth 4:16 — and at Micah 7:5 the root carries the sense "trust", not "nurse".

**A28.** The lemmas "redeemer", "call" and "name" stand together in one verse in exactly four verses: Genesis 48:16, Isaiah 43:1, Isaiah 54:5, Ruth 4:14. The ordered phrase "and his name shall be called" stands in seven verses on a plain pass, and Genesis 48:16 is **not** among them.

**A29.** As the corpus prints it, the book of Ruth carries exactly one paragraph marker, after 4:17.

**A30.** Ruth 1:5 and Ruth 4:14 use **different** verbs for "was left" and "has not left". There is no shared lemma between them for that idea.
