### 8.2 — Single contacts: the five that failed, and why

Every one is the project's named error class: *a count made by lemma search described in words the search cannot see.*

- **V4 — "לֶחֶם stands in two verses of Ruth, and the giver differs: YHWH at 1:6, Boaz at 2:14."** **The count is exact. The second half is false.** **Ruth 2:14 contains no verb of giving at all** — its verbs are אמר, נגש, אכל, טבל, ישב, צבט, אכל, שבע, יתר, and the bread is governed by **וְאָכַלְתְּ מִן־הַלֶּחֶם**, "eat *of* the bread", partitive. The one thing Boaz hands over, **וַיִּצְבָּט־לָהּ**, has **קָלִי** (roasted grain) as its object, not לֶחֶם. **Better form, and sounder:** the two acts are not parallel donations but a **sequence** — at 1:6 YHWH *gives* (נתן) bread to עַמּוֹ in Judah, which Naomi only *hears* of; at 2:14 Boaz *invites Ruth to eat* from bread already there. **The echo-table row is not raised. It is reworded.**
- **V2 (second half) — "nine places within four verses."** An artefact of `find.py near`'s greedy windowing, and **the breakdown convicted itself: 1 + 5 + 1 + 1 + 2 = ten against a stated nine**, because the tool gives four Leviticus 25 windows and the claim said five. **Better form, tool-independent: גָּאַל and קָנָה stand within four verses of one another in exactly five chapters of the Hebrew Bible — Exodus 15, Leviticus 25, Leviticus 27, Psalm 74, Ruth 4 — with zero cross-chapter contacts anywhere in the canon.**
- **Shaddai — "five of the six Genesis occurrences attach the name to progeny."** **Three do, in the same verse** (28:3, 35:11, 49:25). Gen 17:1 needs 17:6 and Gen 48:3 needs 48:4. **Better reading, and better than the claim:** **Gen 43:14 does not lack the theme, it inverts it** — כַּאֲשֶׁר שָׁכֹלְתִּי שָׁכָלְתִּי, the name invoked over bereavement of children to get sons back.
- **"רָעָב בָּאָרֶץ in nine verses."** Unreproducible: 6 pointed, 10 consonantal, 38 by bare co-occurrence. Nine arises on one unstated normalisation. **The ordered clause וַיְהִי רָעָב בָּאָרֶץ at exactly three verses — Gen 12:10, Gen 26:1, Ruth 1:1 — is confirmed by three independent passes and is the figure to use.** *(Gen 42:5 carries the identical lemma sequence and is excluded on form — perfect הָיָה, articular רעב, construct בְּאֶרֶץ. A consonant-only re-derivation returns four and looks like a corpus fault.)*
- **V5 (second half) — עָשָׂה with חַיִל.** The raw co-occurrence is **25 verses**, of which **eleven are plausibly the idiom** "do valiantly / amass wealth". **This corrects the project's own R2, which gave six.** And it carries a live trap: **Ruth 3:11 is in the 25 and is not the idiom** — there אֶעֱשֶׂה־לָּךְ governs "for you" and חַיִל belongs to אֵשֶׁת חַיִל. **Better form: the idiom stands in eleven verses, and Prov 31:29 and Ruth 4:11 are the only two whose subject is a woman or women.**

### 8.3 — Single contacts: the five that held, with their nuances

- **V1 — עַם־זוּ in exactly three verses (Exod 15:13, 15:16, Isa 43:21), with the two Exodus instances completed by גָּאָלְתָּ and קָנִיתָ, both HVqp2ms.** Confirmed exactly, by normalised surface search and by lemma-slot adjacency. **Tighter than claimed:** Isa 43:21 continues the template with יָצַרְתִּי (1cs), so all three are *formula + qal perfect of a verb of acquiring or forming*. **And the syntax is not unique to the Song: Ps 74:2 runs both verbs as 2ms qal perfects in one verse, people as object, order reversed.**
- **V6 — the three negatives confirmed.** זֶרַע once in Ruth (4:12); דֶּרֶךְ once (1:7); עֹרֶף absent, against a 32-verse positive control. **Caveat that must travel with the third:** Orpah (עָרְפָּה) stands at 1:4 and 1:14 under lemma 6204, and a common-noun nil **cannot reach a name-pun claim.** The negative refutes a Leitwort reading; it does not refute Verrett's.
- **V3 (distribution) — בֹּעַז, 24 occurrences in 22 verses: eighteen in Ruth, four outside (1 Kgs 7:21, 2 Chr 3:17, 1 Chr 2:11, 1 Chr 2:12).** Confirmed exactly. **One lemma, man and pillar undistinguished, no homograph sub-entries** — so no count of "Boaz the man" can be taken straight from the lemma. Both pillar verses name the **left/northern** pillar.
- **יָדַע with שַׁעַר in exactly four verses (Josh 2:5, Amos 5:12, Prov 31:23, Ruth 3:11); Prov 31:31 has שַׁעַר and no יָדַע.** Confirmed exactly. **Nuance that forbids "verbatim":** the two run in opposite voice and number — Prov 31:23 **niphal** נוֹדָע with **plural** בַּשְּׁעָרִים, the *husband* known *in* the gates; Ruth 3:11 **active participle** יוֹדֵעַ with **singular** כָּל־שַׁעַר as *subject*, the gate knowing the *woman*. **Ruth inverts the Proverbs line**, which strengthens the contact and changes what it means.
- **אֵשֶׁת חַיִל in exactly three verses; חַיִל in exactly three verses of Ruth (2:1, 3:11, 4:11).** Confirmed exactly. **Not graphically identical:** maqqef-bound and verse-initial in Prov 12:4 and 31:10, two free accent units in a subordinate clause at Ruth 3:11.

### 8.4 — Design claims: none survived

**D-A — Ruth 1 repeats the exodus · Discard.**
Against the whole of Ruth: **Exod 1 = 0.000, Exod 13 = 0.000, Exod 14 = 0.000** — not one rare lemma pair. Exod 12 = 0.039. The best Exodus chapter anywhere is **Exod 5 at 0.217**, which forms no part of the proposal and loses 40% of its figure on dropping הָלַךְ. **Two of the three lexical anchors are not weak but wrong: Exod 1:22 does not contain יֶלֶד** (its word is הַיִּלּוֹד, lemma 3209), **and Exod 13:17–21 does not contain בַּדֶּרֶךְ at all** (13:17 and 13:18 are construct, 13:21 articular; the *ba‑* forms in Exodus are at 4:24, 18:8, 23:20, 33:3).
**Recovered from underneath: כִּי־פָקַד יְהוָה stands in exactly four verses of the Hebrew Bible — Exod 4:31, 1 Sam 2:21, Zech 10:3, Ruth 1:6** — and Exod 4:31 and Ruth 1:6 are the two where the object is the afflicted people as a collective. **In both, what follows the visitation is a departure.** This is a clean, rare, checkable allusion at the book's hinge, and the typology was burying it. *Moderate–high on the phrase; the literature verdict of L7 stands — commentators treat פָּקַד as covenantal idiom and none argues deliberate exodus evocation.*

**D-F — Exodus 15 as a source for Ruth (Embry's cluster) · Discard as an Exodus 15 claim; reframe as a Leviticus 25 claim, where it is strong.**
**Exod 15 against Ruth 4 — the target the claim needs — is 0.000. Zero rare pairs, rank 229, in the tied-zero floor.** Against the whole of Ruth: 0.037, one pair, one distinct partner verse, and dropping חֶסֶד takes it to 0.000. Reversed, **193 chapters of the canon beat Ruth's best figure against Exodus 15.**
**The promiscuity defence is removed by measurement.** Exodus 15 is *not* a verse-dense poem that scores against everything: 67.6% of chapters return zero against it, and its top partner is **Num 33 at 0.571** — the itinerary that genuinely re-narrates Marah and Elim. It finds a real partner when it has one. It does not find Ruth.
Element by element: **Marah falsified at the lemma level** — Ruth 1:20's מָרָא is lemma **4755, a canon hapax**, and Exod 15:23's מָרָה is lemma **4785**; two entries, not one. **חֶסֶד, נַחֲלָה and Moab are furniture** (245, 222 and 181 occurrences). **The verb pair points away from Exodus 15: Lev 25 against the whole of Ruth scores 0.218 with 12 pairs over 9 distinct partner verses, carried by exactly גָּאַל and קָנָה — six times Exodus 15's figure, on precisely the verbs the element claims.**
**Recovered from underneath: חֶסֶד with גָּאַל in one verse stands in exactly four verses — Exod 15:13, Isa 54:8, Ps 103:4, Ruth 2:20.** **The right verse was identified for the wrong reason.** If anything ties Ruth to Exod 15:13 it is **Ruth 2:20**, where redemption and covenant loyalty arrive in one breath and the redemption plot is born — not a character's name sharing three consonants with the next clause.
**Consequence for Embry.** His evidence does not support his conclusion. It supports **Ruth 4 being shaped by Leviticus 25**, which needs no Song of the Sea to explain it.

**D-B — the eighteen-row Genesis 38 table · Discard.**
**Ruth 3 against Genesis 38 = 0.000, rank 708 — and rows 8 to 12 of the eighteen are all drawn from Ruth 3.** Against Gen 38, **Judges 14 (0.400) and 2 Samuel 13 (0.282) both outrank every chapter of Ruth**, as does **Deuteronomy 25 (0.263)**. **Drop-one is fatal: remove the single lemma כַּלָּה ("daughter-in-law") and Gen 38 ↔ Ruth collapses from 0.167 to 0.033** — one lemma carrying the whole apparent partnership, and a lemma any story about a woman and her in-laws must use.
**Two rows refuted precisely.** *"The same reason is given for the refusal":* **יָבַם stands in exactly three verses — Gen 38:8, Deut 25:5, Deut 25:7 — and never in Ruth**, while **גָּאַל in Deuteronomy stands only at 19:6 and 19:12, of the blood-avenger.** Disjoint vocabularies, disjoint institutions; the stated reasons share one word, and it is "not". *"The purpose is to raise up offspring for a dead brother":* **Gen 38:8 is הָקֵם זֶרַע — raise up *seed*, and Ruth is not among the 13 verses carrying קוּם + זֶרַע. Ruth 4:5 and 4:10 are לְהָקִים שֵׁם־הַמֵּת — raise up the *name* — which is Deuteronomy 25:7's formula.** **Ruth 4 quotes Deuteronomy, not Genesis 38.**
**And a constructed comparator shows how cheap the rows are:** applying the proposal's own latitude to **Gen 19:30–38 (Lot's daughters)** against Genesis 38 yields roughly **9 or 10 of the 18 row-types** from a narrative nobody proposes as a source — and it **beats Ruth lexically on the "raise up seed" row**, since Gen 19:32 and 19:34 share זֶרַע with Gen 38:8 and Ruth does not. *(This half is the auditor's judgement, not a figure, and is flagged as such.)*
**Recovered from underneath, and it is the find of the round: Ezekiel 16:8–9 carries both of Ruth 3's signature gestures in consecutive verses.** **פָּרַשׂ with כָּנָף of a person stands in exactly two verses — Ezek 16:8 and Ruth 3:9** (nine of its twelve canonical instances are cherubim or an eagle); **רָחַץ with סוּךְ stands in exactly three — 2 Sam 12:20, Ezek 16:9, Ruth 3:3.** Ruth 3:3's vocabulary shares **not one lexeme** with Gen 38:14. The table was pointing at the wrong book. *The overview already carries the כָּנָף half at row 13; the wash-and-anoint half joining it in the adjacent verse is new.*
**What survives of the table: row 18.** Ruth 4:12 names Perez, Tamar and Judah, and it is the single verse where Ruth 4 touches Gen 38 at all.

**D-C — Ruth 2 and Ruth 3 as two food panels · Discard.**
**גָּאַל / גֹּאֵל by chapter: ch. 1 — 0 · ch. 2 — 1 · ch. 3 — 7 · ch. 4 — 13.** **Chapter 3 carries seven occurrences of the redeemer root against chapter 2's one.** The family problem is not reserved for chapter 4; chapter 3 is about almost nothing else. **לֶחֶם does not occur in chapter 3 at all.** Chapter 3's barley is a *token sent to Naomi* with the instruction at 3:17 — not a meal.
**Chapter 3 is framed twice over:** **מִי־אַתְּ** at 3:9 and 3:16 (an inclusio in a phrase standing in five verses of the canon), and **יָטַב**, whose three Ruth occurrences are all in chapter 3 — 3:1, 3:7, 3:10 — and nowhere else in the book.
**And the proposal's first matching element is backwards.** At **2:2 Ruth proposes to Naomi**; at **3:1 Naomi proposes to Ruth**. **The initiative reverses** — a chiastic inversion, which is a better observation than the parallel it is offered as, and incompatible with "two panels of the same story".
**Better description: an ascending pair** — the same staging used twice to move from food to family, **with 2:20 as the pivot that plants the second theme inside the first.**

**D-D — Boaz = בְּעָזְּךָ · Discard as stated.**
**בֹּעַז is lemma 1162, tagged HNp at all 24 occurrences, and is never analysed with a `b/` prefix** — the index takes the initial bet as the first of three root consonants. **בְּעָזְּךָ at Exod 15:13 is `b/5797`, morph HR/Ncmsc/Sp2ms** — three morphemes. **Not the same lexeme and not cognate. עֹז occurs zero times in Ruth.** **The pointing separates them at the first syllable: bō-ʿaz against bə-ʿoz-zᵉkā** — so even "resonates phonetically" overstates it. **Maximum defensible form:** *unpointed, the name is identical in its consonants to בְּעָז־, "in the strength of", and a reader of an unpointed text could see the one in the other.* A footnote, not a sermon point.
**And the pillar is a datum, not a gloss.** 1 Kgs 7:21 and 2 Chr 3:17 do name the left-hand pillar בֹּעַז — **but the canon nowhere glosses either Jachin or Boaz.** It cannot be made to do the work of an etymology.

**D-E — Obed as a new-David figure · Discard both halves.**
עוֹבֵד stands in **three verses of Ruth, all in the closing genealogical material — 4:17, 4:21, 4:22.** **He is named, begotten, and begets. He is never the subject of a verb of action, never speaks, is never spoken to, and nothing is said of him in his own right anywhere in the canon.** In 4:13–16 he is not even called Obed: he is בֵּן at 4:13 and **הַיֶּלֶד at 4:16**, the book's second and last use of יֶלֶד, answering 1:5. **The text does not make Obed a new David; it makes David the point of Obed.**
**"The birth of Obed answers exile" is not a claim the text makes.** The book's stated problem is famine, bereavement and a line about to end; its stated resolution is a גֹּאֵל and a name not cut off (4:10, 4:14). Exile is a frame a reader brings from the book's canonical position.
**And the food reading inverts its own grammar.** **כַּלְכֵּל takes שֵׂיבָתֵךְ, "your grey head", as its object: the child will *sustain* Naomi's old age. The child is the provider, not the provision** — cast in the role Joseph and the ravens occupy (Gen 45:11, 47:12, 50:21; 1 Kgs 17:4, 17:9). **מֵשִׁיב נֶפֶשׁ's nearest formal parallel is Lam 1:16, מְנַחֵם מֵשִׁיב נַפְשִׁי, where the restorer is a human comforter**; only two of the 35 שׁוּב + נֶפֶשׁ verses pair it with food vocabulary. *(Note also that the subject of 4:15 carries from גֹּאֵל at 4:14, so it predicates of the unnamed redeemer-child two verses before the name Obed exists.)*
**Recovered from underneath:** Ruth 4:15–16 answers 1:5 and 1:21 by vocabulary the book controls itself — **יֶלֶד at 1:5 and 4:16, and empty at 1:21 against כַּלְכֵּל at 4:15.** An internal inclusio, textually secure, needing neither Obed-as-David nor exile nor a food metaphor.

### 8.5 — The best case against these verdicts, recorded

Auditor 2 was asked for the strongest argument against its own lowest rating and gave one worth keeping:

> **The scanner is structurally blind to the only thing a typology claims.** A typology asserts shared *shape* — sequence, role, plot function — none of which produces a rare lemma pair inside a single verse. A writer re-using a pattern reaches for his own vocabulary, so **heavy verbal overlap would falsify a typology, not confirm it.** Of course Exodus 1 scores zero against Ruth 1: one narrates with taskmasters and a river, the other with barley and a gate.

**Why it was still rejected, and the reasoning should govern future rounds.** First and decisively: **two of the three lexical anchors offered are not weak but factually wrong** — Exod 1:22 has no יֶלֶד, Exod 13:17–21 has no בַּדֶּרֶךְ — and a design argued from citations that do not survive a check cannot then be credited on the strength of its shape. Second, the defence as offered is **unfalsifiable**: it predicts the same zero whether the design is there or not. Third, **the good contact does not need the design and is damaged by it** — bundled into an eighteen-point typology with two broken anchors, Ruth 1:6 ~ Exod 4:31 will be discarded along with them.

**The standing form of the lesson:** *the pattern may be the frame a writer worked in, and the measurements cannot say; the phrase is the thing he wrote, and that is what can be handed over.*

### 8.8 — Method faults and corpus traps added by this round

- **`find.py near` confines its window to a single chapter** (`chap(r) == chap(order[i])`), so a contact straddling a chapter boundary returns a silent low count. Not in `_texts/README.md`; it should be.
- **`find.py near`'s windowing is greedy and skips forward by the window length**, so its output count is a property of the algorithm, not the text. **Never report "N places" from it.** Report chapters, or verse-pairs, or maximal clusters — and say which.
- **Lemma 3557 (כּוּל) conflates two senses** — "contain, measure" and the pilpel "sustain, provide" — under one Strong's number with no lettered sub-entry, separable only by stem in the morph field. A provisioning search on 3557 silently collects 1 Kgs 8:27 and Isa 40:12.
- **The README's 25-verse benchmark for קְדוֹשׁ יִשְׂרָאֵל is a word-boundary figure.** Substring gives 30, lemma adjacency 32. The README should say which search it answers; an auditor lost a pass believing its control had failed.
- **A lemma nil cannot refute a name-pun**, because proper nouns carry their own lemma. State the scope of every negative finding.
- **`contacts.py`'s homograph fix is confirmed present and its cache name bumped to `contacts-v2hg-`**, so the stale-pickle trap is defused for that filename. Calibration reproduces at 8.786 and 7.182.
- **Housekeeping:** auditor 2 left a helper script at `/Users/pqc/auditwork/chk.py`, outside the corpus folder. It adds proper-noun exclusion and drop-one to the scan, which the stock tool does not do. Keep or delete.

### 8.10 — Colophon, Part 8

Two blind auditors, per the standing rule.
