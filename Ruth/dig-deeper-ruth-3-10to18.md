## Headline Findings

1. **One question frames the whole threshing-floor scene, and every English version destroys the frame.** The two words מִי־אַתְּ ("who are you?") stand at **3:9**, in Boaz's mouth in the dark, and at **3:16**, in Naomi's mouth at dawn — **the book's only two occurrences** `[T]`. The exact phrase stands in five verses of the Hebrew Bible: Gen 24:23; 24:47; Isa 51:12; Ruth 3:9; Ruth 3:16 `[T]`. **All three English versions render it "Who are you?" at 3:9 and then paraphrase it seven verses later** — "How did it go" (NASB95, NIV84), "How did you fare" (ESV) `[T]`. Rahlfs renders it literally (Τίς εἶ, θύγατερ); **Swete omits the question altogether** `[T]`. Surfaced by: Structure, Vocabulary, Translations, Narrator's Comment, Move 4. *High confidence* on the data; *moderate–high* that the repetition is deliberate. **New to this run.**

2. **At 3:15 the Masoretic text says Boaz went to the city, and both literal versions say Ruth did.** MT reads וַיָּבֹא הָעִיר — **masculine**, "and *he* came to the city" `[T]`. BHS records *mlt Mss* 𝔖 𝔙 reading וַתָּבֹא, "and *she* came" `[T]`; the Greek is gender-neutral and settles nothing `[T]`. **The NASB95 and the ESV both read "she"; the NIV84 reads "he"** `[T]`. MT is the harder reading, and it is what sets up 4:1, where Boaz is already at the gate. Surfaced by: Structure, Translations, Textual Variants, Move 4. *High* on the readings; *moderate–high* for MT. **The two literal versions follow a secondary reading here and the dynamic one follows MT — the reverse of the usual pattern.**

3. **3:11 names the venue of chapter 4 before the reader knows there will be a scene there, and no version in any language carries it.** שַׁעַר ("gate") stands in **four verses of Ruth — 3:11, 4:1, 4:10, 4:11** `[T]`; 3:11 reads כָּל־שַׁעַר עַמִּי, "all the gate of my people". **All three English versions delete the gate at 3:11 and keep it at 4:1, 4:10 and 4:11** `[T]`. And **both Greek editions read πᾶσα φυλὴ λαοῦ μου, "all the tribe of my people"** `[T]` — the reading the claim audit queued as **R22** and never tested. **This run classifies it as category 1, translation loss, not a variant**: שַׁעַר → שֵׁבֶט requires two palaeographically unnatural substitutions, and the modern versions independently make the same move the Greek made. Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Translations, Textual Variants, Quotation/Allusion, Move 4. *High* on the chain; *moderate–high* on the classification.

4. **Boaz's blessing at 3:10 is the control case for the 2:20 crux, and it corrects yesterday's framing.** The construction **בָּרוּךְ / בְּרוּכָה / בְּרוּכִים + a nominal + לַיהוָה** stands in **six verses of the Hebrew Bible — Judg 17:2; 1 Sam 15:13; 1 Sam 23:21; Ps 115:15; Ruth 2:20; Ruth 3:10** `[T]`. **Two of the six are in Ruth.** At 3:10 the referent is unambiguous: Ruth is blessed and YHWH is the source. **So the blessing formula at 2:20 is settled — "may he be blessed by YHWH" — and only the following אֲשֶׁר clause is open** `[I]`. The 2:17–23 dig reported the masculine phrase as "unique in the canon", which is true of that exact wording and misleading about the construction; **the correction strengthens its text-critical conclusion** and narrows its literary one. Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Translations, Textual Variants, Move 4. *High* on the six-verse family; *moderate–high* on the consequence for 2:20.

5. **"Sit still, my daughter" is answered by six sittings at the gate.** 3:18 opens with שְׁבִי בִתִּי, Naomi's imperative to Ruth `[T]`. יָשַׁב then occurs **six times in 4:1–4** — וַיֵּשֶׁב, שְׁבָה, וַיֵּשֵׁב (4:1), שְׁבוּ, וַיֵּשֵׁבוּ (4:2), הַיֹּשְׁבִים (4:4) `[T]`. **One imperative to a waiting woman, then six sittings in a court.** Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Repetition, Move 4, So What?. *High confidence* on the count; *moderate–high* on the reading. **New to this run.**

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book level.** Taken from the overview rather than re-derived: to show a God who is spoken about but never speaks refilling an emptied household through ordinary acts of kindness and costly redemption, and to give Israel David as the fruit `[I: overview]`.

**Passage level.** This unit's contribution is to convert a request into a legal obligation, and then to introduce the obstacle that makes the obligation cost something. Ruth asked at 3:9 on the ground of a status — כִּי גֹאֵל אָתָּה, "for you are a redeemer" `[T]`. Boaz's answer does three things in four verses: he **accepts the characterisation** (3:12, "I am a redeemer"), he **binds himself by oath** (3:13, חַי־יְהוָה), and he **names a prior claimant** (3:12, "there is a redeemer nearer than I") `[T]`.

The third is the author's real business. **Had Boaz simply agreed, the book would end in chapter 3 with a marriage and no redemption of the field** `[I: sweep, confirmed]`. The nearer redeemer exists so that the transaction must be done in public, at cost, before witnesses — which is chapter 4, and which the unit announces in advance by naming the gate at 3:11 `[T]`.

A second contribution, and the one the sweep did not see. The unit is built on **not knowing and then knowing**. יָדַע ("to know") occurs **eight times in eight verses of Ruth, five of them in chapter 3 — and three of those five are here**: all the gate *knows* Ruth's character (3:11), she rose before anyone could *recognise* another (3:14), let it not be *known* that the woman came (3:14), and Naomi tells her to sit until she *knows* how the matter falls (3:18) `[T]`. That is four knowings in a unit of nine verses, in a scene conducted in the dark. **Reputation is public, the night is not, and the outcome is not yet knowable** — and the author's arrangement of the root says so `[I]`. *Moderate–high confidence.*

**Choice and pacing.** Boaz's four verses of speech get the detail; the journey home gets a clause; the whole of the following day — the gate scene Boaz is walking towards — is withheld until chapter 4 `[T]`. The unit ends on an instruction to wait, and makes the reader wait with her.

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement.** 3:1–9 has produced a request that cannot be answered informally. Naomi's plan ended with "and *he* will tell you what to do" (3:4); **Ruth departed from it and spoke first** (3:9) — a departure the 3:1–9 dig established from the structure of instruction against execution `[S: 3:1–9 dig]`. What she said was not a request for kindness but an invocation of a legal standing: *spread your wing over your maidservant, for you are a redeemer* `[T]`.

So the preceding movement has put a man under a claim, at night, with no witnesses, on the initiative of a foreign widow. **Three things therefore have to happen before the book can continue, and this unit does all three** `[I]`: the claim must be accepted or refused; the acceptance must be made binding in a setting where nothing can be proved; and the public process must be set in motion.

And the movement reaches further back. **2:20 introduced the category and conceded a rival in the same breath** — Naomi's partitive מִגֹּאֲלֵנוּ, "he is *of* our redeemers" `[T]`, which the 2:17–23 dig identified as making room for someone nearer `[S: 2:17–23 dig]`. **3:12 is where the room gets filled.** The obstacle is not introduced out of nowhere at the end of chapter 3; it was conceded by Naomi's grammar in chapter 2 and is now named.

**Necessity answer — why here, and not elsewhere.** Three reasons the text supplies.

First, **the oath has to be sworn where it cannot be enforced.** 3:13's חַי־יְהוָה is the book's only oath `[T]`, and it is sworn in the dark to a woman with no standing to sue. Place the same promise at the gate and it is a contract; place it here and it is character. The author has chosen the second, and 3:18 makes the point explicit: Naomi's confidence rests on what kind of man he is, not on what he has signed `[I]`.

Second, **the gate must be named before it is used.** 3:11's "all the gate of my people" is the first of the book's four occurrences of שַׁעַר `[T]`, and it is spoken in the least public place in the book. The venue of the public settlement is named in the one scene that must stay secret `[I]`. *Moderate–high confidence*: the chain is `[T]` and the irony is a short inference.

Third, **the unit must end with waiting, because chapter 4 belongs to the men.** 3:18's שְׁבִי ("sit") is answered by six sittings in 4:1–4 `[T]`. The book has spent three chapters with two women acting; the resolution is transacted by men at a gate, and this verse is where the women are moved from agency to waiting `[I]`.

**Implication for the passage.** The unit is not the happy half of the threshing-floor scene. It is the hinge at which a private initiative becomes a public process, and the preacher's weight should fall on what makes the hinge turn: an oath with no witnesses, and an obstacle named by the man who could have concealed it.

#### General contextual notes

**Immediately before:** 3:9, Ruth's request, and behind it the whole night scene from 3:6. **Immediately after:** 4:1, Boaz at the gate — which on the Masoretic reading of 3:15 is where he was already heading `[T]`.

**Section:** the back half of the arc map's "Threshing floor" movement (3:1–18) `[S: overview]`.

**The unit boundary is contested, and the contest is worth stating.** The overview divides the chapter 3:1–9 / 3:10–18. **The 3:1–9 solo dig concluded that the tighter literary unit is 3:1–13 and recommended preaching 3:1–9 while reading through v.13** `[S: 3:1–9 dig]`. This run agrees with that judgement on the text's own seams. The chapter's clear breaks are at 3:6 (she goes down), 3:8 (midnight), **3:14 (dawn, and she rises)** and **3:16 (she arrives home)** `[T]`; there is no seam of any kind between 3:9 and 3:10, which are question and answer in a single exchange `[T]`. **And the WLC carries no paragraph marker anywhere in the chapter** — one petuchah in the whole book, after 4:17 `[T]`, re-confirmed here. So on the literary evidence the divisions are 3:1–13 and 3:14–18, and the preaching unit 3:10–18 begins in the middle of a conversation.

`[unchecked — apparatus spread]` on the paragraphing, as in the two previous runs: the corpus holds one manuscript tradition and prints paragraph division without variants, so this is a statement about Leningrad. Capped at *moderate*, named by witness, barred from the overview until a library pass on the spread has run. Paragraph markers are a reading tradition, not authorial `[S]`.

**What this costs, and what to do about it.** Preached as 3:10–18, the unit opens on Boaz's "blessed are you" without the request it answers, and the congregation has to be told what Ruth said. Preached as 3:14–18 it is too thin to carry a sermon. **The practical recommendation, which follows the 3:1–9 dig rather than overturning it: preach 3:1–13 as one unit and 3:14–18 as the coda, or preach 3:10–18 while reading from v.6.** Either way the exegesis in this report applies; only the pulpit division changes. Recorded as a Book-Overview Tension.

**Read out of context?** Verse 11 is, mildly: "a woman of excellence" is quoted as a character reference with the gate deleted and the Proverbs phrase supplied from elsewhere. And 3:18 is quoted as a devotional maxim about waiting on God, when it is Naomi's assessment of a particular man's temperament `[T]`.

### 3. Structure

Seven movements, with the pivot marked twice by the same particle.

| Verse | Movement | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | **The blessing** | Boaz answers the request with a blessing and a verdict on her motive |
| 11 | **The promise** | וְעַתָּה (1): "I will do for you all that you ask" — and the ground: her public reputation |
| 12 | **The obstacle** | וְעַתָּה (2): "there is a redeemer nearer than I" |
| 13 | **The oath** | The procedure set out as a conditional, sealed by the book's only oath |
| 14 | **Dawn and discretion** | She lies till morning, rises before recognition, and he orders secrecy |
| 15 | **The measure and the sending** | The cloak, six of barley, and — on MT — his departure for the city |
| 16–18 | **The report and the wait** | Naomi's question, Ruth's report, and the instruction to sit |

**Device: a doubled וְעַתָּה, and a dawn that splits the unit.**

**וְעַתָּה ("and now") stands at the head of both v.11 and v.12** `[T]`. Hebrew uses it to mark a turn from what precedes to what follows; using it twice in consecutive verses marks **two turns**, and the second undoes part of the first `[I]`. The promise of v.11 is unconditional in form — *all that you say I will do for you* — and v.12's second וְעַתָּה immediately qualifies it. **The structure is promise-then-complication, and the author has marked it with the same word twice rather than with an adversative** `[T]`. *Moderate–high confidence*; the sweep noticed the doubling and this run confirms it.

**The dawn at v.14 is the unit's real seam** (Tool 2). Everything before it is speech in the dark; everything after it is action in the light, and the two halves are joined by a verse that contains both — she lay until morning, she rose before recognition `[T]`.

**One structural observation the sweep did not have.** The unit's three speeches to Ruth all begin by naming her בִּתִּי ("my daughter") — Boaz at 3:10 and 3:11, Naomi at 3:16 and 3:18 `[T]`. Four times in nine verses, from two different speakers, in a unit where nobody uses her name at all. **Ruth's name does not occur in these nine verses** `[T]`; she is "my daughter" to both of them and הָאִשָּׁה ("the woman") to Boaz when he speaks about her to no one in particular at 3:14 `[T]`. *High* on the data; *moderate* on the reading, which is that the unit treats her as a dependent in two households at once.

### 4. Linking Words

Narrative with a long speech in it, so the connectors matter more than in most units.

| Marker | Verse | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **לְבִלְתִּי** ("in not, by not") | 10 | The ground of Boaz's verdict: her kindness is greater *in that* she did not go after the young men `[T]`. A negative purpose/result particle, and it is what makes 3:10 a judgement about motive rather than a compliment |
| **אִם … וְאִם** ("whether… or") | 10 | "whether poor or rich" — a merism closing the field of alternatives `[T]` |
| **וְעַתָּה** ×2 | 11, 12 | The structural pivot, twice (Tool 3) `[T]` |
| **כִּי … כִּי** | 11 | Two causal clauses stacked: *for* all the gate knows *that* you are a woman of valour `[T]`. The first is the reason she need not fear; the second is its content |
| **כִּי אָמְנָם כִּי (אִם)** | 12 | The unit's knottiest syntax, and the site of a ketiv (Textual Variants). As the consonants stand: "and now, for truly, for if I am a redeemer…" `[T]` |
| **וְגַם** ("and moreover") | 12 | Additive, introducing the obstacle — not an adversative. Boaz does not say "but"; he says "and also" `[T]`. The English versions all supply a contrast ("however", "Yet", "Although") |
| **אִם … וְאִם־לֹא** ("if… and if not") | 13 | The procedure as a two-branch conditional, with the second branch carrying the oath `[T]` |
| **עַד / בְּטֶרֶם** | 13, 14, 18 | Three temporal boundaries: until morning (13, 14), before recognition (14), until you know (18) `[T]` |
| **כִּי לֹא … כִּי־אִם** | 18 | Naomi's double clause: *for* the man will not rest *until* he has finished the matter `[T]`. The idiom כִּי־אִם after a negative is "but rather / until", and it is the unit's last word on Boaz's character |

→ **The logical spine, and the thing a sermon should carry:** *blessing* → *and now, an unconditional promise* → *and now, a prior claim* → *therefore a conditional procedure, under oath* → *secrecy* → *a measure sent* → *therefore wait, because of what he is like*. The one place the Hebrew refuses a contrast where English supplies one is v.12's וְגַם — Boaz adds the obstacle rather than opposing it to his promise `[T]`, and the preacher who reads "however" has been handed a reluctance the Hebrew does not express.

### 6. Narrator's Comment

**The narrator makes one intrusion in this unit, and it is a small one of a kind he uses nowhere else.**

The unit is almost entirely speech and reported action. But at 3:14 the narrator reports Boaz's words without naming him — **וַיֹּאמֶר אַל־יִוָּדַע**, "and he said, let it not be known" `[T]` — and the sentence has no addressee. Boaz is not speaking to Ruth (he gives her instructions in the next verse, separately introduced), and no one else is present. **The clause reads as a resolve rather than a conversation**, and both Greek editions felt the difficulty: they supply the subject, "and Boaz said" `[T]`, which BHS records (𝔊 𝔙 add Βοος) `[T]`. The Syriac supplies a different subject again — "and she said" `[T]`, per BHS. **Three witnesses, three attempts to attach the sentence to somebody.**

This is as close as the book comes to reporting an inner intention in this unit, and it is reported as speech `[I]`. *Moderate confidence*, and it is worth the preacher's notice because the concealment is Boaz's own decision, taken at dawn, with no one asking him for it.

**What the narrator still withholds.** He passes no verdict on the night, on Naomi's plan, on Ruth's departure from it, or on Boaz's secrecy `[T]`. The pattern that makes this admissible as a finding is the one established in the previous two runs: the same narrator reports an inner state at 3:7, explains an obsolete custom at 4:7, and attributes a divine act to YHWH in his own voice at 4:13 `[T]`. He can speak; here he does not. *Moderate* on the significance (the ceiling for an absence claim); *high* on the cited instances.

The claim audit's discard of a "narratorial cluster" as a design claim (R28) stands and is not reopened; **and its specific correction applies directly here** — 3:10 is Boaz's direct speech, not a narratorial aside `[S: audit, R28]`.

### 7. Vocabulary

#### Proper-noun inventory (run before triage)

**Persons:** none named. **Ruth's name does not occur in this unit** `[T]`; Boaz's does not either. Both are named only by relationship or by the definite noun — בִּתִּי ("my daughter", ×4), הָאִישׁ ("the man", 3:16, 3:18), הָאִשָּׁה ("the woman", 3:14), חֲמוֹתָהּ / חֲמוֹתֵךְ ("her/your mother-in-law", 3:16, 3:17) `[T]`. **Places:** הָעִיר ("the city", 3:15), הַגֹּרֶן ("the threshing floor", 3:14), and **שַׁעַר** ("gate", 3:11), which is a place-word doing structural work. **Numbers:** שֵׁשׁ ("six", 3:15, 3:17).

Triage: **שַׁעַר** gets the full check below and is cross-reported to Tool 11. **"Six"** gets one line: the claim audit baselined the book's numerals and found two is its commonest, with **six** tying ten at two occurrences — both of them in this unit `[S: audit, R1; T for the figures]`. **Recorded as a distribution; there is no numerical design in this book and it may not be preached as one.** No personal or place name in the unit carries an outstanding word (curse, vow, oath or prophecy) that it activates; the check returns nil — **with the single qualification that this unit contains an oath of its own**, treated below.

#### (a) שַׁעַר ("gate") at 3:11 — the venue named in the dark

**שַׁעַר stands in four verses of Ruth: 3:11, 4:1, 4:10, 4:11** `[T]`. *High confidence.* The first is here; the other three are the gate scene and its witnesses.

3:11 reads כִּי יוֹדֵעַ כָּל־שַׁעַר עַמִּי — literally "for all the gate of my people knows" `[T]`. The gate is the civic assembly by metonymy: the place where elders sat, witnesses gathered and transactions were ratified `[S]`, which is exactly what 4:1–11 shows `[T]`.

**Three consequences, in descending confidence.**

**(i) The chain is real and load-bearing.** Boaz, in the most private place in the book, grounds his promise in the opinion of the one public institution that will have to ratify it `[I]`. *High* on the chain, *moderate–high* on the reading.

**(ii) No version in any language carries it.** NASB95 "all my people in the city"; ESV "all my fellow townsmen"; NIV84 "All my fellow townsmen" `[T]`. **All three render שַׁעַר at 4:1, 4:10 and 4:11 as "gate"** `[T]`. So every English congregation hears the gate three times in chapter 4 and never hears it anticipated. The preacher has to supply the link by hand.

**(iii) The Greek did the same thing, and that settles R22.** Both Greek editions read πᾶσα φυλὴ λαοῦ μου, "all the **tribe** of my people" `[T]`, and **BHS records nothing at 3:11 on this point** — its only note there is a few manuscripts and four versions adding אֵלַי, "to me" `[T]`. The claim audit queued this as **R22**, "the Greek reading that looks like שֵׁבֶט, which BHS does not record", and left it untested pending a library question `[S: audit]`.

**Classified: category 1 of the triage — translation loss, not a substantive variant.** *Moderate–high confidence.* Three reasons. **(a)** A *Vorlage* reading שֵׁבֶט for שַׁעַר requires two substitutions (ע → ב, ר → ט), neither of them palaeographically natural in any Hebrew script. **(b)** The metonymy "all the gate of my people" has no Greek equivalent, and a translator needing a word for a civic body would reach for one — φυλή is a reasonable, if loose, choice. **(c)** Decisively, **three modern English versions independently make the same move 2,200 years later**, each replacing the gate with a word for the townspeople `[T]`. When ancient and modern translators converge on dissolving a metonymy, the convergence is evidence about the idiom's difficulty, not about the Hebrew text.

**So BHS's silence is correct**, and R22 can be closed on the classification question: there is nothing text-critical to record. What a library pass would still add is whether any scholar argues for a שֵׁבֶט *Vorlage*; that is worth asking, but the corpus has taken the question as far as it needs to go for preaching. Reported to the queue.

#### (b) אֵשֶׁת חַיִל at 3:11, and the חַיִל chain

**אֵשֶׁת חַיִל stands as an ordered phrase in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: Prov 12:4; Prov 31:10; Ruth 3:11** `[T]`, plain and skeletal. *High confidence.*

**And חַיִל stands in three verses of Ruth, one per principal: 2:1 (Boaz, אִישׁ גִּבּוֹר חַיִל), 3:11 (Ruth, אֵשֶׁת חַיִל), 4:11 (the household, וַעֲשֵׂה־חָיִל)** `[T]`. *High confidence.* The 2:1–16 dig recorded the translation consequence and it holds here: **the ESV keeps the link and loses the wealth; the NASB95 keeps the wealth and loses the link** `[S: 2:1–16 dig]`. At 3:11 specifically, NASB95 "a woman of excellence" and ESV "a worthy woman" and NIV84 "a woman of noble character" `[T]` — none of which a hearer connects to 2:1's "a man of great wealth" (NASB95) or "a worthy man" (ESV).

**On the Proverbs relation, the audit's verdict is accepted and not re-derived.** The relation is **Prov 31:10–31 ↔ Ruth 3**, at *moderate–high* — Ruth 3 ranks first of 832 chapters against the acrostic at two rarity settings, and scores zero or one against eight comparable Proverbs blocks `[S: audit, R2]`. **The canonical adjacency is withdrawn outright**: *Baba Batra* 14b places Ruth first among the Writings, the head-of-the-Writings position antedates the move into the Megillot, and Campbell suggests the Leningrad placement may itself have been *prompted* by this very phrase — which would make the lexical link the cause of the adjacency rather than evidence of design `[S: overview v1.4, Logos L1]`. **It may not be preached.**

**The relation's sharpest member is in this verse**, and this run reproduces it independently: **יָדַע ("know") with שַׁעַר ("gate") in one verse stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible — Josh 2:5; Amos 5:12; Prov 31:23; Ruth 3:11** `[T]`. Prov 31:23 is "her husband is known in the gates"; Ruth 3:11 is "all the gate of my people knows". *Moderate–high*, the audit's rating (N1), confirmed `[S: audit, N1]`.

#### (c) גָּאַל — six occurrences in two verses

**גָּאַל/גֹּאֵל occurs 21 times in 10 verses of Ruth, none before 2:20** `[T]`. **Six of the twenty-one are in this unit: two at 3:12 and four at 3:13** `[T]`, verified occurrence by occurrence in the index. *High confidence.*

The four at 3:13 are worth setting out, because the saturation is the point:

> אִם־**יִגְאָלֵךְ** טוֹב **יִגְאָל** וְאִם־לֹא יַחְפֹּץ **לְגָאֳלֵךְ** **וּגְאַלְתִּיךְ** אָנֹכִי
> "if he will **redeem you**, good, let him **redeem**; and if he does not wish **to redeem you**, then **I will redeem you**"

Four forms of one root in one verse, across three persons and two aspects `[T]`. **This is the densest concentration of the book's second keyword anywhere in the book** `[T]`, and it falls in the verse where the obligation is assumed under oath. *High confidence* on the count.

**Translation note, cross-reported to Tool 8:** the NASB95 and ESV keep all four; **the NIV84 reduces the fourth to "I will do it"** `[T]`, so one congregation in three hears the word three times rather than four at the moment it matters most.

#### (d) רֵיקָם ("empty-handed") at 3:17 — and the obligation it usually carries

**רֵיקָם stands in two verses of Ruth, 1:21 and 3:17** `[T]`, and in **sixteen verses of the Hebrew Bible** `[T]`. *High confidence.* The reversal is the sweep's finding 4 and the overview's echo-table row, and it holds: Naomi said YHWH brought her back רֵיקָם; Boaz says Ruth must not go to her mother-in-law רֵיקָם `[T]`.

**What this run adds is the word's canonical company**, which has a shape. Six of the sixteen are commands or complaints about **not sending a dependent away empty**: Gen 31:42 (Jacob to Laban, "you would have sent me away empty"), Exod 3:21 ("you shall not go out empty"), Exod 23:15 and 34:20 and Deut 16:16 ("none shall appear before me empty"), and **Deut 15:13** — "when you send him out free from you, you shall not send him away empty-handed", of the released Hebrew slave `[T]`. *High* on the distribution.

So the word Boaz uses belongs to a family of **obligations on the stronger party at the point of release** `[I]`. He is under no such obligation — nothing in the night's transaction requires him to send barley — and he uses the word that elsewhere states the duty. *Moderate confidence* on the reading, and it is a better route into 3:17 than the arithmetic of the measures.

#### (e) The unnamed measure at 3:15, and the unknown garment

**Two things in this verse the text does not tell us, and the versions supply both.**

**שֵׁשׁ־שְׂעֹרִים** — "six of barley", with **no unit of measure named** `[T]`. The ordered phrase stands in **Ruth 3:15 alone** in the Hebrew Bible `[T]`. Both Greek editions likewise have ἓξ κριθῶν, "six of barley", with no unit `[T]`. **All three English versions supply "measures"** `[T]`. So the quantity is genuinely unknown, and the sweep was right to say a solo dig should note it rather than compute it `[S: sweep]`. *High confidence* that the unit is unstated.

**The contrast with the previous unit is exact and worth preaching.** At 2:17 the narrator names the measure — כְּאֵיפָה, "about an ephah" `[T]`, a phrase unique in the canon `[S: 2:17–23 dig]`. At 3:15 he declines to. **The public, daylight gleaning is quantified; the night-time gift is not** `[I]`. *Moderate confidence*, and it is the kind of observation only available once both units have been worked.

**הַמִּטְפַּחַת** — the garment Ruth holds out. **The word stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: Isa 3:22 and Ruth 3:15** `[T]`, and the Isaiah instance is a plural in a catalogue of twenty-one items of women's finery that YHWH will strip away `[T]`. *High confidence* on the count.

**So the canon gives almost nothing to go on, and the renderings diverge accordingly:** NASB95 "cloak", ESV "garment", NIV84 "shawl"; Greek περίζωμα, "girdle, apron" `[T]`. **This is a real limit on how much weight the detail can bear**, and it matters because the threshing-floor details are the overview's third preaching trap `[S: overview]`. A preacher who builds on the kind of garment it was is building on a two-verse word whose only rival is a list. *Recommend a lexicon check*, and expect it to be inconclusive.

#### (f) יָדַע — the unit's leading root

**יָדַע occurs eight times in eight verses of Ruth: 2:1, 2:11, 3:3, 3:4, 3:11, 3:14, 3:18, 4:4** `[T]`. **Five are in chapter 3 and three of those five are here** `[T]`. *High confidence.*

The 3:1–9 dig identified the root as the chapter's leading root and recorded the distribution with the wordplay reading capped at *moderate* under the hard rules `[S: 3:1–9 dig]`. That stands. **What this unit contributes is the root's reversal inside nine verses**:

| Verse | Form | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 3:11 | יוֹדֵעַ | all the gate **knows** her character — public knowledge, and the ground of the promise |
| 3:14 | יַכִּיר | before one could **recognise** another — the dark, and the condition of the secrecy |
| 3:14 | אַל־יִוָּדַע | let it **not be known** — the one thing that must stay hidden |
| 3:18 | תֵּדְעִין | until you **know** how the matter falls — the one thing not yet knowable |

Note that 3:14's יַכִּיר is from **נָכַר**, not יָדַע — a different root, and the report keeps them apart `[T]`. נָכַר stands in three verses of Ruth (2:10, 2:19, 3:14), and the 2:17–23 dig established that chain `[S: 2:17–23 dig]`. **So the unit runs two "knowing" roots at once**, and 3:14 carries one of each. *High* on the distributions; *moderate–high* on the reading, which is that the unit is organised around what is known publicly, what must not be known, and what cannot yet be known.

#### (g) יָשַׁב at 3:18, answered six times at the gate

**3:18's first word is שְׁבִי, "sit"** — an imperative to Ruth `[T]`. **יָשַׁב then occurs six times in 4:1–4**: וַיֵּשֶׁב, שְׁבָה, וַיֵּשֵׁב (4:1), שְׁבוּ, וַיֵּשֵׁבוּ (4:2), הַיֹּשְׁבִים (4:4) `[T]`, verified occurrence by occurrence. *High confidence.*

**One imperative to a woman who must wait, then six sittings by men conducting a legal process.** The verb that means "wait at home" in 3:18 means "convene a court" in 4:1–2 `[I]`. *Moderate–high confidence* on the reading; the counts are certain. See Tool 16: this is the unit's most useful corrective to a sermon on passivity.

The English versions all render 3:18 "Wait, my daughter" `[T]` — a reasonable sense-rendering, and one that removes the link entirely, since all three then render 4:1–2 with "sat down" `[T]`.

#### (h) Boaz's blessing at 3:10, and what it does to the 2:20 crux

**בְּרוּכָה אַתְּ לַיהוָה** — "blessed are you to YHWH" `[T]`.

The construction, searched across the WLC with final letter-forms folded and a positive control run first (Declaration): **בָּרוּךְ / בְּרוּכָה / בְּרוּכִים followed within two words by לַיהוָה stands in six verses of the Hebrew Bible — Judg 17:2; 1 Sam 15:13; 1 Sam 23:21; Ps 115:15; Ruth 2:20; Ruth 3:10** `[T]`. *High confidence.* **Two of the six are in Ruth, and they are the book's two blessings pronounced on a person** `[T]`.

**This corrects the 2:17–23 dig's framing and strengthens its conclusion.** That run reported בָּרוּךְ הוּא לַיהוָה as "unique in the canon", which is true of the exact masculine wording and gives a misleading impression of the construction, which is a six-verse family `[S: 2:17–23 dig]`. The correction cuts two ways:

- **It strengthens the text-critical result.** The 2:17–23 dig argued that MT at 2:20 is original and that the variant (one Hebrew manuscript and the Syriac reading יהוה, assimilating to Gen 24:27's בָּרוּךְ יְהוָה) is secondary `[S: 2:17–23 dig]`. **That argument is now stronger, not weaker**: the book demonstrably uses this construction, so 2:20 is the masculine member of a pair with 3:10 rather than an isolated oddity, and assimilation away from a construction the author uses twice is the obvious direction of change `[I]`. *Moderate–high.*
- **It narrows the literary result.** At 3:10 there is no following relative clause and the sense is unambiguous: Ruth is blessed and YHWH is the source `[T]`. **So the formula itself is settled — at 2:20 it means "may he be blessed by YHWH" — and the ambiguity at 2:20 lies entirely in the אֲשֶׁר clause that follows it**, which 3:10 has no counterpart to and cannot adjudicate `[I]`. *Moderate–high.* The 2:17–23 dig's crux stands, but it is a narrower crux than that report implied.

**One further item for the queue, raised and not pressed.** **Judg 17:2 is one of the six** — Micah's mother's "blessed be my son to YHWH" `[T]` — and the 1:1–5 dig established that Ruth 1:1 reproduces a clause from **Judg 17:8** `[S: 1:1–5 dig]`. So a second contact point with Judges 17 has turned up. **The claim audit withdrew the Judges 17 block on distributional grounds (R19) and reduced it to the single verbal contact at 1:1** `[S: audit, R19]`, and **this report does not revive it**: a shared member of a six-verse idiom family is weak evidence, and two contacts with a chapter are what the audit already found insufficient. Rated *low–moderate*, logged for the audit to weigh, not claimed.

#### (i) חֶסֶד at 3:10 — the last of three

**חֶסֶד stands in three verses of Ruth — 1:8, 2:20, 3:10 — and all three are in characters' speech, never the narrator's** `[T]`. *High confidence*; the overview's finding, confirmed in the previous run and again here `[S: overview]`.

3:10 is the last, and the only one in which a character evaluates somebody else's חֶסֶד as better than their own earlier one: הֵיטַבְתְּ חַסְדֵּךְ הָאַחֲרוֹן מִן־הָרִאשׁוֹן `[T]`. The verb is **יָטַב**, which stands in three verses of Ruth, **all three in chapter 3 — 3:1, 3:7, 3:10** `[T]`: that it may be *well* with you (Naomi's purpose), his heart was *merry* (Boaz at supper), you have made your kindness *better* (Boaz's verdict). *High* on the count; the 3:1–9 dig's finding, confirmed, with the third member falling here `[S: 3:1–9 dig]`. **Both English versions break all three; Rahlfs keeps it** `[S: 3:1–9 dig]`.

**What "the first kindness" was, the text does not say** `[T]`. The natural referents are Ruth's loyalty to Naomi (1:16–17) or her work in the field for the household; the clause's own ground, supplied by לְבִלְתִּי, is that she did not go after the young men `[T]`. So Boaz reads her night-time approach as an act of kindness **to the dead man's name**, not to himself `[I]` — the sweep's point, and it is the correct reading of the particle. *Moderate–high.*

### 8. Translations

The pulpit text is the **ESV**; the study text is the **NASB95**. **This is the richest unit in the book for divergence, and in three places the versions choose against the Masoretic text or against each other.**

#### Pulpit divergence note

**(i) 3:16 — the same two Hebrew words, rendered two ways seven verses apart, by all three versions.**

| Verse | Hebrew | NASB95 | ESV | NIV84 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3:9 | מִי־אָתּ | "Who are you?" | "Who are you?" | "Who are you?" |
| 3:16 | מִי־אַתְּ בִּתִּי | "**How did it go**, my daughter?" | "**How did you fare**, my daughter?" | "**How did it go**, my daughter?" |

`[T]` for all six readings. *High confidence.*

**What the congregation will hear.** Nothing of the frame. The question Boaz asks a stranger in the dark and the question Naomi asks her daughter-in-law at dawn are **the same two words in Hebrew** `[T]`, and in English they are two different sentences. The paraphrase is defensible — Naomi plainly knows who Ruth is, and some idiomatic force is likely `[I]` — but it is a decision, and it is the decision that removes the unit's most striking literary feature. **Supply it from the pulpit:** *Naomi asks exactly what Boaz asked — "who are you?" — and the question has a different point now, because what Ruth is has changed in the course of one night.*

Note what the ancient witnesses did with the same difficulty: **Rahlfs renders it literally (Τίς εἶ, θύγατερ); Swete omits it altogether, leaving only "Daughter"** `[T]`. **BHS records nothing at 3:16** `[T]`. So the reading was felt as hard early, and one Greek witness solved it by deletion. That is good evidence it is original (Textual Variants).

**(ii) 3:15 — who went to the city.**

| Witness | Reading |
|---|---|
| **MT** | וַיָּבֹא הָעִיר — **"and *he* came to the city"** (3ms) `[T]` |
| BHS apparatus | *mlt Mss* 𝔖 𝔙: וַתָּבֹא — "and **she** came" `[T]` |
| Swete, Rahlfs | εἰσῆλθεν — 3sg, **no gender**; settles nothing `[T]` |
| **NASB95** | "Then **she** went into the city" `[T]` |
| **ESV** | "Then **she** went into the city" `[T]` |
| **NIV84** | "Then **he** went back to town" `[T]` |

*High confidence* on all six. **The two literal versions follow a secondary reading against MT; the dynamic version follows MT.** That is the reverse of the pattern this book's digs have otherwise found, and it is worth the preacher knowing.

**What hangs on it.** On MT, the chapter's last action before Naomi's speech is **Boaz walking into town** — and 4:1 then opens with him already at the gate `[T]`. Naomi's "the man will not rest until he has settled it today" (3:18) is then a correct reading of a man who has already set off `[I]`. On the variant, the last action is Ruth walking home, 3:16's "and she came to her mother-in-law" repeats it, and 3:18 is a character assessment with nothing visible behind it. **MT is the more economical and the harder reading** (see Textual Variants), and it gives the sermon a better last beat.

**(iii) 3:11 — the gate.** All three versions delete שַׁעַר here and keep it at 4:1, 4:10, 4:11 `[T]`. See Tool 7a. **The anticipation is lost in English and the fulfilment is kept** — the single most supplyable gap in the unit.

**(iv) 3:12 — "redeemer", and the ketiv.** ESV "I am a redeemer… there is a redeemer nearer than I"; NASB95 "I am a close relative… a relative closer than I"; NIV84 "I am near of kin… a kinsman-redeemer nearer than I" `[T]`. **The NASB95 loses גֹּאֵל entirely at 3:12, as it did at 2:20** `[T]`. And on the ketiv (Textual Variants): **only the NIV84's "Although it is true that…" reflects the conditional/concessive force of the written אִם**; the ESV and NASB95 both follow the qere `[T]`.

**(v) 3:13 — the fourfold redeem.** NASB95 and ESV keep all four occurrences; **NIV84 renders the fourth "I will do it"** `[T]`.

**(vi) 3:17 — a word read but not written.** The Masoretes read אֵלַי ("to me") after אָמַר, though **no such word stands in the consonantal text** — a *qere wela ketiv* `[T]`, and BHS records the Greek, Syriac and Targum supporting it `[T]`. **The ESV supplies it ("for he said to me"); the NASB95 does not ("for he said"); the NIV84 avoids the question ("saying")** `[T]`. Nothing turns on it exegetically, and it is a neat illustration for a congregation of what a qere is.

**Where they agree and nothing turns on it.** "Six measures of barley" in all three (supplying a unit the Hebrew and Greek both lack); "as the LORD lives" in all three; "do not fear" in all three; "Wait, my daughter" in all three for 3:18's שְׁבִי.

**Two of these deserve more than a table row.**

**3:18 and the "word that falls".** נָפַל with דָּבָר in one verse stands in **seventeen verses of the Hebrew Bible** `[T]`, and in **five of them it is the formula of a promise that did not fail** — לֹא־נָפַל דָּבָר and its kin at **Josh 21:45; Josh 23:14; 1 Sam 3:19; 1 Kgs 8:56; 2 Kgs 10:10**, every one of them negated `[T]`. *High confidence* on the distribution.

**Ruth 3:18 uses the idiom's verb in an open question with no negative** — אֵיךְ יִפֹּל דָּבָר, "how the matter will fall" `[T]`. **Both Greek editions supply the negative the formula normally carries** `[T]`. That is a translator hearing a stock phrase and completing it, which is category 1, not a variant — and BHS's silence is right. **But the Hebrew's choice is a finding in its own right:** Naomi uses the vocabulary of a promise's fulfilment or failure, in a question, about a matter whose outcome nobody yet knows `[I]`. *Moderate confidence*, and all three English versions render it "how the matter turns out" / "what happens", losing the verb `[T]`.

**No translation-tradition split is flagged in this unit.** The one item that would qualify — 3:15's he/she — is a split between the **Hebrew manuscript tradition plus two versions** on one side and **MT** on the other, with the Greek neutral; it is reported as a category-2 variant rather than as an ancient-versus-modern split, because the moderns are themselves divided.

**No KJV comparison is offered**; no KJV export is in the corpus and the report does not quote a version it has not verified.

### 9. Tone and Feel

**Register: relief and suspense, held in the same unit, with a seam of real anxiety between them.**

The unit opens warm — a blessing, "my daughter", an unconditional promise — and then does three things that chill it: names a prior claimant (12), swears an oath that might not be needed (13), and orders that nobody must know (14) `[T]`. It ends on an instruction to sit still and an assessment of a man's temperament, with the outcome unknown `[T]`.

What carries it:

- **The doubled וְעַתָּה** (Tool 3). The second one is where the tone turns, and it turns without an adversative `[T]`.
- **The oath.** חַי־יְהוָה is a standard formula — it stands in **43 verses of the Hebrew Bible** `[T]`, so it is not rare and the report does not claim it is. What is notable is that **this book, which contains no divine speech and exactly one divine act in the narrator's own voice (4:13), contains exactly one oath by YHWH, and it is sworn here** `[T]`. *High* on the counts; *moderate–high* on the significance.
- **The darkness, and the hour.** "Lie down until morning" (13), "until morning" (14), "before one could recognise another" (14) `[T]` — three time-markers in two verses, all of them about the light not yet being up.
- **The secrecy.** אַל־יִוָּדַע, "let it not be known" (14) `[T]`, with no addressee (Tool 6). The sentence is the unit's one cold moment.
- **The silence of the gift.** Six of barley, measured and loaded, with no word to Ruth about what it means; **she has to supply the interpretation herself at 3:17**, and she does — it is a message to Naomi `[T]`.
- **Naomi's last line.** Two clauses, both about Boaz, neither about God `[T]`. The book's most confident sentence about a human being.

**Soundtrack.** Low and unresolved. The warmth at v.10 is real, and by v.18 the music has not finished; something is still running. If anything, a held chord rather than a cadence.

**The preacher's note.** The temptation is to preach this as the resolution of the threshing-floor crisis, because the pastoral danger of 3:1–9 is over by v.10. **It is not a resolution; it is a referral.** The emotional architecture should end where the text ends — with a woman sitting down and a man walking into town.

**Proverbs 31:10–31 → Ruth 3:11** *(high confidence on the phrase; moderate–high on the relation; nil on the adjacency)* `[T]`

*Move 1 — Source context, read.* Prov 31:10–31 is the acrostic that closes the book, opening אֵשֶׁת־חַיִל מִי יִמְצָא ("a woman of valour, who can find?") and running twenty-two lines on her industry, provision, trade and standing. Its function in its own passage is to embody, in a woman managing a household, the wisdom the whole book has commended `[T]`. **Prov 31:23 is the line that matters here**: "her husband is **known** in the **gates**" `[T]`.

*The contacts, measured.* **אֵשֶׁת חַיִל: three verses — Prov 12:4; Prov 31:10; Ruth 3:11** `[T]`. **יָדַע with שַׁעַר in one verse: four verses — Josh 2:5; Amos 5:12; Prov 31:23; Ruth 3:11** `[T]`. Both reproduced in this run.

*Move 2 — Book usage.* Proverbs touches Ruth at one chapter and effectively one verse. The audit's baseline put **Ruth 3 first of 832 chapters against the acrostic** at two rarity settings, with Ruth 1, 2 and 4 scoring zero at the tighter setting, and against eight comparable Proverbs blocks Ruth 3 scores zero against six and one against the other two `[S: audit, R2]`. So the relation is chapter-to-chapter, not book-to-book, and **this verse is where it lands**.

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* No pre-existing conversation is claimed. Prov 31:23 and Ruth 3:11 both use the gate for civic standing, which is ordinary Hebrew idiom; what is not ordinary is the co-occurrence with יָדַע, which is four verses `[T]`.

*What it adds.* **Boaz describes Ruth in the phrase Proverbs uses of its ideal, and does it in a sentence that also shares the acrostic's gate-and-knowing collocation** `[T]`. The reading worth preaching is that the verdict is *public* — he is not paying her a compliment, he is reporting what the assembly already thinks `[I]`. *Moderate–high.*

**And the canonical argument is withdrawn.** The sweep and earlier drafts treated Ruth's position after Proverbs in BHS as doing interpretive work. It cannot: *Baba Batra* 14b puts Ruth **first** among the Writings; the head-of-the-Writings position antedates the move into the Megillot, which Webb dates sixth to ninth century AD; and Campbell suggests the Leningrad placement may have been **prompted by this very phrase** — making the lexical link the cause of the adjacency rather than evidence of design `[S: overview v1.4, Logos L1]`. **Rate the relation moderate–high; rate the adjacency nil; do not preach it.**

**The "word that did not fall" formula → Ruth 3:18** *(high confidence on the distribution; moderate on the reading)* `[T]` for the data, `[I]` for the reading

*Move 1 — Source contexts, read.* The formula appears at the great moments of accounting for God's promises. **Josh 21:45**: "not one word failed of all the good that YHWH had spoken to the house of Israel" — the summary of the land's allotment. **Josh 23:14**: Joshua's farewell, the same claim in the first person. **1 Sam 3:19**: of Samuel, "YHWH was with him and let none of his words fall to the ground" — the establishment of a prophet. **1 Kgs 8:56**: Solomon's dedication blessing, "not one word has failed of all his good promise". **2 Kgs 10:10**: Jehu, "nothing of the word of YHWH shall fall to the earth" `[T]`. **Every one is negated, and every one is about a word that stood** `[T]`.

*The distribution.* נָפַל with דָּבָר in one verse: **seventeen verses**; the five above are the fulfilment formula `[T]`.

*Move 2 — Book usage.* No earlier use in Ruth; דָּבָר occurs in three verses of the book, two of them in 3:18 `[T]`. A minimal Move 2: this is the only place the book reaches for the idiom.

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* Joshua 21:45 and 23:14 are in deliberate conversation with each other (the same formula at the allotment and at the farewell), and 1 Kgs 8:56 reaches back to both `[T]`. Ruth does not have to put them in conversation; the formula is already a chain by the time it reaches her.

*What it adds, and the honest limit.* **Naomi asks, in the vocabulary of promise-fulfilment, how a matter will fall — and leaves off the negative that the formula carries in all five of its canonical homes** `[T]`. Read with the chain, she is asking whether this word will stand; read without it, she is asking how things will turn out. **The lexeme is common and the collocation is not rare enough to force the reading** — seventeen verses is idiom territory — so the finding is reported at *moderate* and no allusion is claimed. What makes it worth reporting at all is that **both Greek editions supplied the missing negative** `[T]`, which shows an ancient reader hearing the formula where a modern translation does not.

### 12. Genre

**Hebrew prose narrative**, and within it **a reported negotiation** — the only passage in the book where a legal procedure is set out prospectively, as a conditional, before it is carried out.

Two reading rules earn their place.

**First, a conditional is not a promise.** 3:13's structure is *if he redeems, good; and if not, I will* `[T]`. Boaz commits himself to an outcome only on the second branch, and the first branch is the one he expects to be tested first. **A sermon that reads v.13 as "Boaz promised to marry Ruth" has read past the syntax.**

**Second, characters' assessments are theirs.** Naomi's closing verdict — "the man will not rest until he has finished the matter today" (3:18) `[T]` — is a character's reading of another character, and the narrative vindicates it in chapter 4. **That it turns out right does not make it the narrator's doctrine**, any more than 1:21 was; and the same discipline that refuses to make 2:20 a doctrinal proposition applies here.

Brief and uncontested.

### 14. Bible Timeline

**Where the passage sits.** The judges' period `[T]`, at the end of the barley harvest — the threshing floor of 3:2 follows 2:23's close of the harvest `[T]`. The institutions in play are redemption of land and person by the nearest kinsman, and the civic assembly at the gate `[S]`.

**Where the book sits — canonical position.** Ruth stands in the **Ketuvim**, fourth in the sequence BHS prints `[T]`; English Bibles and the Greek tradition place it between Judges and 1 Samuel `[S]`. **The Proverbs-adjacency argument is withdrawn and may not be preached** (Tool 11) `[S: Logos L1]`. Codex Leningradensis itself puts Chronicles first among the Writings, though Ruth keeps the same neighbours either way `[S]`.

**What the position contributes here, and it is more than in most units.** This is the unit in which Ruth is called an אֵשֶׁת חַיִל, and that phrase is the one point at which Ruth and Proverbs demonstrably touch `[T]`. **A reader of the Writings has Proverbs in his ears** `[S]` — not because of the sequence, which is late, but because the two books are canonical neighbours in the same section with the same register `[S: overview]`. The relation is real; what the reader may not do is build on the order.

**Canonical and compositional order.** Unchanged: the events fall in the judges' period `[T]`; the Hebrew canon files the book among the Writings `[T]`; the English and Greek canons after Judges `[S]`. The direction of dependence between Ruth 4:18–22 and 1 Chr 2:5–15 is open `[T]`. Both reported, neither preferred.

**Where the reader sits.** This side of the cross. What bears on this unit: an oath sworn in private and performed in public, by a man who had a prior claimant to defer to and deferred to him. **The redeemer the word גֹּאֵל is waiting for has no prior claimant and no obstacle** `[I]`, which is the difference the typology turns on (Christological Reading).

**How that reframes it.** It stops the unit being read as suspense about whether Boaz is willing. He is willing by v.11 and under oath by v.13 `[T]`. The suspense is procedural, and procedure is the thing the gospel's redemption does not have to negotiate.

### 15. Who Am I?

- **Boaz** is the type here, and the category is **redeemer** — named by Ruth at 3:9, accepted by him at 3:12, and sworn to at 3:13 `[T]`. The typology is worked in the next section and it passes all four tests at the category level.
- **Ruth** is the one with a claim she cannot enforce. She has invoked a status, received an oath, and been sent home to wait `[T]`. **The honest identification for most hearers is with her position rather than her character**: holding a promise from someone with the standing to keep it, with the outcome out of your hands `[I]`.
- **Naomi** is the one who reads the situation correctly and can do nothing about it either. Her role here is interpretive, not active.
- **The nearer redeemer** is nobody yet. Reading ourselves into him — "the one with the right who would not pay" — is available and is a chapter 4 question, not this unit's.
- **The misidentification to name.** "Be the Boaz in someone's life" takes the unit's typological figure and converts him into an example at precisely the point where the text makes him a redeemer under oath `[I]`. The imitable things he does here are narrower and better (Tool 13).

### 16. So What?

**Stage 1 — the response the author seeks.** To see a man accept a claim he was not obliged to accept, disclose an obstacle he could have concealed, bind himself by oath where nothing could be enforced — and then to wait with the two women while the matter is settled somewhere else, by somebody else, today `[I]`.

**Stage 2 — worldview, then behaviour.**

| Domain | Content |
|---|---|
| **Worldview** | Obligations are kept by character before they are kept by procedure. Boaz's oath is unenforceable, and the book's only oath `[T]`; Naomi's confidence at 3:18 rests on what he is like, not on what he has signed `[T]`. That is a claim about how God's purposes actually move in this book: through people whose word holds when nothing compels it |
| **Behaviour — start** | **Disclosing the prior claim.** The imitable act in the unit is 3:12 — naming the person with a better right at the moment you have been invited to act on your own `[T]`. And interpreting a kindness correctly and passing it on, as Ruth does at 3:17 |
| **Behaviour — stop** | Treating waiting as either passivity or anxiety. 3:18's שְׁבִי is answered by six sittings in a court (Tool 7g): **the woman sits because the process has started, not because nothing is happening** `[I]` |
| **Motivation** | Not "wait and God will act" — the verse does not mention God. The ground Naomi gives is the character of a particular man who is already walking into town `[T]`. For the Christian reader, that is the pattern and not the point: the one whose word holds has already finished the matter |

|---|---|
| **For me** | The test in v.12 is the one I would fail: having been handed an opportunity, do I mention the person with the better claim? Boaz volunteers a fact that could cost him everything he has just been offered |
| **For a Christian friend** | For someone in a waiting season: the text's own counsel is Naomi's, and it is not "trust the process" but "trust the man" — which for us means the one who has sworn and does not need an oath. Waiting here is not inaction; it is the posture of someone whose case is being heard |
| **For the church** | A congregation hears v.15 (the generous gift) and misses v.12 (the honest disclosure). The second is harder and more imitable, and it is what makes the first credible |
| **For an unbeliever** | The book's hinge is a promise made where it could not be enforced, by a man who told the truth against his own interest. Whatever else Christianity claims, this is the kind of character it says God works through — and the kind of promise it says God has made |

**Prayer in response.**

- Thank you for a redeemer with no prior claimant and no obstacle to negotiate.
- Sorry for the opportunities I have taken without mentioning the better claim.
- Help me to sit down while the matter is being settled, without pretending that is easy.
- And make our word hold where nothing compels it.

#### Grammatical and syntactical features

- **The doubled וְעַתָּה** at vv.11 and 12 `[T]`, marking two turns where English supplies one turn and one contrast (Tool 4).
- **וְגַם, not an adversative,** at v.12 `[T]`. Boaz *adds* the nearer redeemer. All three English versions render a contrast ("however", "Yet", "Although"), which imports a reluctance the Hebrew does not express.
- **The emphatic אָנֹכִי twice** (3:12, 3:13) `[T]`, neither required by its verb. "**I** am a redeemer"; "**I** will redeem you."
- **A two-branch conditional with unequal halves** (3:13): three words for the nearer redeemer's option, nine for Boaz's, the oath on the longer side `[T]`.
- **לְבִלְתִּי + infinitive** (3:10), a negative complement giving the ground of the verdict `[T]` — the particle that makes v.10 an assessment of motive rather than a compliment.
- **Three ketivs in two verses.** 3:12's אם and 3:14's מרגלתו and בטרום all stand **unpointed** in the WLC `[T]` — three of the book's eleven unpointed words, in a unit of nine verses. Ruth is the fourth densest book in the canon for unpointed forms, at about 10.6 % of its verses `[S: _texts README; overview]`, and this unit is denser again.
- **A qere with no ketiv at 3:17** — the Masoretes read אֵלַי ("to me") where the consonantal text has no such word `[T]`. The ESV supplies it; the NASB95 does not.
- **A verbless clause carrying the unit's claim:** כִּי אֵשֶׁת חַיִל אָתְּ (3:11), "for a woman of valour *are* you" `[T]`, with the pronoun last — the emphatic position.

#### Wordplay and sound-features in the original

- **No paronomasia claim is made in this unit.** The repetition check fires on גָּאַל, בִּתִּי and בֹּקֶר; none has a sound-partner in the unit's register, and the hard rules are not met for any sound-based claim. **Both the consonant-reversal and the taxonomy-activation checks were run and both return nil.**
- The one place sound may be doing work is **3:13's fourfold גאל** (*yigʾālēk… yigʾāl… ləgāʾălēk… ûgəʾaltîk*), where the root is repeated rather than played on `[T]`. That is repetition, reported at Tool 10, not paronomasia.

#### Recommended verification

- **The sense of מִטְפַּחַת** (3:15). Two verses in the canon; the lexica will have more than the corpus can, and a preacher tempted by the garment should know how little is establishable. **Expect the answer to be inconclusive**, which is itself the useful result.
- **Whether anyone argues a שֵׁבֶט *Vorlage* at 3:11** (R22). This run classifies the Greek's φυλή as translation loss at *moderate–high*, which is as far as the corpus goes. A library pass would confirm or contest the classification; it is no longer urgent.
- **The force of כִּי אָמְנָם כִּי אִם at 3:12** with the ketiv standing. The syntax is awkward on any reading and the grammars will have views; the exegetical stake is small (Textual Variants).

### Textual Variants

**Eleven apparatus items in nine verses — the densest cluster in the book** `[T]`. All are set out with evidence, positions and confidences in Tool 8's ancient-versions table, which is where the triage was run. **Two are category 1 and belong to Tools 7 and 8** (the gate at 3:11; the supplied negative at 3:18); **there is no category 3 item in this unit.** The category-2 items worth more than a table row are three.

#### 3:15 — who went to the city

- **The issue:** MT וַיָּבֹא הָעִיר, "and **he** came to the city" (3ms); *mlt Mss* with the Syriac and Vulgate read וַתָּבֹא, "and **she** came" `[T]`. The Greek's εἰσῆλθεν is gender-neutral `[T]`.
- **Manuscript evidence:** BHS apparatus at 3:15ᵇ, "mlt Mss 𝔖 𝔙 וַתָּבֹא". Many Hebrew manuscripts, so not a negligible reading.
- **Most likely original: MT.** Three reasons. **(a)** MT is the harder reading: Ruth is the subject of the two preceding clauses, so continuing with her is the natural scribal and translational move and the reverse is not. **(b)** MT loses nothing — 3:16 supplies Ruth's arrival separately, with וַתָּבוֹא `[T]`, so the variant makes 3:15b and 3:16a say the same thing twice. **(c)** MT sets up 4:1, where Boaz is at the gate, and it makes 3:18's "the man will not rest… today" a correct reading of someone already in motion `[T]`. *Moderate–high confidence.*
- **Preaching implication: real.** On MT the chapter ends with Boaz striding into town; the NASB95 and ESV congregation hears Ruth walking home instead (Tool 8). **Say which you are reading and why.**
- **Confidence:** *moderate–high.*

#### 3:12 — the ketiv אִם

- **The issue:** the consonantal text reads וְעַתָּה כִּי אָמְנָם כִּי **אם** גֹאֵל אָנֹכִי; the word אם stands **unpointed**, and the qere omits it `[T]`. With it: "and now, for truly, *if* I am a redeemer" — or, taking כִּי אִם as a unit, something concessive. Without it: "and now, truly I am a redeemer."
- **Manuscript evidence:** BHS at 3:12ᵃ, "mlt Mss ut Q" — many manuscripts read with the qere. **Both Greek editions follow the qere**: καὶ ὅτι ἀληθῶς ἀγχιστεὺς ἐγώ εἰμι, "and that truly I am a redeemer" `[T]`.
- **Most likely original:** the qere's sense, on the weight of the versions and of many manuscripts; but **the consonants are the consonants**, and the harder text has אם. The honest position is that **the ketiv is probably a dittograph or an early intrusion and the clause means "truly I am a redeemer"** — which is how the ESV and NASB95 read it, while the NIV84's "Although it is true that…" reflects the written form `[T]`. *Moderate confidence.*
- **Preaching implication:** small but not nil. On the qere Boaz affirms his standing flatly; on the ketiv he hedges it. The sequel (3:13's oath) favours the flat affirmation `[I]`.
- **Confidence:** *moderate.*

#### 3:14 — the subject nobody could supply

- **The issue:** וַיֹּאמֶר אַל־יִוָּדַע, "and he said, let it not be known", with **no subject named and no addressee** `[T]`. **𝔊 and 𝔙 supply "Boaz"; 𝔖 reads "and *she* said"** `[T]`.
- **Manuscript evidence:** BHS at 3:14ᶜ.
- **Most likely original: MT**, precisely because it is the reading that creates the difficulty three witnesses then solve in two different ways. *Moderate–high confidence.*
- **Preaching implication:** the secrecy is Boaz's own resolve, taken at dawn, unprompted (Tool 6) — and the versions' attempts to attach the sentence to a speaker show how unusual the construction is.
- **Confidence:** *moderate–high.*

#### A standing observation across three units, recorded and capped

In each of the three units dug on 14–15 November, **the Masoretic reading has been the harder one and the divergent witnesses have smoothed it**: the ambiguity at 2:20 resolved by one Hebrew manuscript and the Syriac; the question/answer mismatch at 2:19 levelled by the Greek; the subjectless resolve at 3:14 supplied by three witnesses in two directions; the difficult "who are you?" at 3:16 deleted by Swete; the open "how the word will fall" at 3:18 negated by the Greek.

**That is an observation, not a finding, and it is capped accordingly.** It spans three units and no baseline has been run — nobody has asked at what rate the Masoretic reading is the harder one in a comparable narrative, and "the harder reading is original" is a text-critical canon being applied here rather than a result being derived. **Flagged for the macro-synthesis, where cross-pericope claims belong, and queued. It may not be preached as a feature of the book.**

**Related and separate: the homograph distribution.** Three cruxes in three consecutive units turn on pointing rather than corruption — 1:21 עָנָה, 2:7 שִׁבְתָּהּ, 2:23 יָשַׁב/שׁוּב `[S: 2:17–23 dig]`. **This unit adds no homograph crux**, which is itself worth recording: the run of three does not continue, and a distribution that stops is weaker evidence than one that does not. Queued, unbaselined, not a pattern.

### Historical and Cultural Background

#### Setting

- **Time period (narrated):** the judges' era `[T]`, at the end of the barley harvest and so in late spring `[S]`, *moderate confidence*. The threshing floor is in use, which places the scene within days of 2:23's close of the harvest `[I]`.
- **Location:** the threshing floor outside Bethlehem for vv.10–15, and the town for vv.15–18 `[T]`. The gate named at 3:11 is the town's, and the walk is short `[S]`.
- **Institutions in play:** redemption of land and person by the nearest kinsman; the assembly of elders at the gate; the oath by the divine name `[S]`.

#### What the original audience knew without being told

Six items, each bearing on these nine verses.

1. **What "all the gate of my people" meant.** The gate was the town's court, market and council chamber — where elders sat, witnesses were mustered and transactions were ratified `[S]`, *moderate–high confidence*, and 4:1–11 shows it in operation `[T]`. **The first hearers did not need the metonymy explained**, which is why the narrator can use it in a bedroom scene and expect the anticipation to land.
2. **That a redeemer's right was ordered by proximity, and that the nearer had first refusal.** Lev 25:25's גֹאֲלוֹ הַקָּרֹב אֵלָיו ("his redeemer who is near to him") `[T]` establishes the principle; Boaz's deference at 3:12 presupposes an order of precedence the hearer knew `[S]`, *moderate–high*.
3. **That an oath by YHWH was legally and socially binding, with no further instrument required.** חַי־יְהוָה is the standard formula in 43 verses `[T]`; swearing it committed the swearer irrevocably `[S]`. **This is the item a modern reader most undervalues** — v.13 is not a reassurance, it is the transaction.
4. **Why dawn mattered.** A woman found leaving a threshing floor at daybreak would be assumed to have been there for one reason `[S]`, *moderate–high*. The hearers supplied the risk; 3:14's secrecy is Boaz managing a reputational hazard he names only obliquely `[I]`.
5. **What a threshing floor was for, and that it was not a dwelling.** An open, hard-packed area where the harvest was beaten and winnowed, and where the owner or his men slept during the threshing to guard the heap `[S]`, *moderate*. **The 3:1–9 dig recorded that the cultic-irregularity background for threshing floors was withdrawn as overstated by the library pass** (Logos L11) `[S: audit, L11]`, and this run does not revive it.
6. **That six of something could be carried, and that the gift was a message.** The text gives no unit `[T]`, and the first hearers would have judged the quantity by seeing it loaded onto a cloak `[I]`. **Ruth's own interpretation at 3:17 is the one the text endorses**: it was sent so that she would not arrive empty `[T]`.

#### What this changes about how we read

- **Item 3 relocates the unit's centre of gravity.** A modern reader reads v.13 as an expression of willingness and v.11 as the promise. The first hearers heard the oath as the binding act, which is why Naomi can be as confident as she is at v.18 `[I]`.
- **Items 1 and 4 together explain the unit's shape.** The two things Boaz does that look incidental — naming the gate, ordering secrecy — are the two things the original audience would have recognised as managing the gap between a night-time agreement and a daylight institution `[I]`. *Moderate–high.*

### Original Audience Reception

#### Canonical audience

- **Canonical section:** **Ketuvim**. The reader of the Writings arrives having read the Torah and the whole of the Prophets through to Malachi — post-exilic, knowing David, knowing how the monarchy ended, living with promises unresolved `[S]`.
- **What the canonical reader knows that bears on this unit:** the redemption provisions of Leviticus 25 `[T]`; the manumission law of Deuteronomy 15 with its "not empty-handed" `[T]`; the formula of promises that did not fall (Josh 21:45; 23:14; 1 Sam 3:19; 1 Kgs 8:56; 2 Kgs 10:10) `[T]`; **and the Proverbs acrostic, as a canonical neighbour in the same section** `[S]`. That last is the one place in the book where the canonical register does identifiable work.
- **Canonical register:** Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Lamentations, Esther, Daniel, Ezra–Nehemiah, Chronicles `[S]`. **Proverbs is live here and nowhere else in the book**, at one chapter and effectively one verse `[S: audit, R2]`. **The sequence is not available as an argument** `[S: Logos L1]`.

#### The first hearers

- **Who they were:** Israelites who knew the gate, the redemption order, the force of an oath and the hazards of a threshing floor at dawn. The narrative-world audience is the household; the compositional audience is contested; the canonical audience is the reader of the Writings `[S: overview]`, and interpretation defaults to the last.
- **Their situation:** living with promises partly kept, and with a legal system they knew to be better on paper than in practice `[S]`.
- **What they brought:** the six items above, and an expectation that a man invited to act in his own interest at night would act in it.

#### Where the passage fits their world

Three things they would have heard that we do not naturally pick up:

1. **The oath as the decisive moment** (item 3). We hear a reassurance; they heard an irrevocable commitment.
2. **The gate at 3:11 as an advance notice.** A hearer who knew what the gate was would hear v.11 and know where this was going `[I]`. *Moderate confidence* — the chain is certain, the ancient ear's reaction is an inference.
3. **The disclosure at 3:12 as costly and surprising.** Boaz volunteers the existence of a man with a better claim at the moment he could simply have acted `[T]`. **That is the unit's moral centre for a hearer who knew the redemption order**, and it is the part a modern sermon most often skips `[I]`.

#### Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances

- **Shocking:** nothing in the events; the danger has passed by v.10. **What would have struck them is v.12** — the voluntary disclosure against interest.
- **Surprising:** that the promise comes before the complication, and that both are introduced by the same particle `[T]`; and that Boaz, not Ruth, is the one who raises the legal difficulty.
- **Comforting:** the oath, and v.11's "do not fear" — the only time anyone in the book tells anyone not to be afraid `[T]`.
- **Disturbing:** v.14's secrecy, which concedes that what has happened could be construed badly `[T]`; and v.18's open question, which is the unit's last word on the outcome.
- **Familiar:** the oath formula, the redemption order, the gate, the measuring out of grain. Most of the unit is institutional furniture, and the two unusual things are the subjectless resolve at 3:14 and the repeated question at 3:16.

#### What we bring that they didn't

- **A romantic frame.** We read 3:10–13 as a proposal accepted. The text gives a legal undertaking with a prior claimant attached — the overview's first preaching trap `[S: overview]`.
- **A devotional reading of waiting.** 3:18 becomes "wait on the Lord", when the verse's ground is a man's character and its answer is a court convening `[T]`.
- **Impatience with the ketivs.** A modern reader wants 3:12 settled; the Masoretes preserved both the written and the read form.
- **No feel for an oath.** Our promises are backed by instruments. Theirs, here, was backed by nothing but the name invoked.

#### Implication for the sermon

Recovering item 3 — the oath as the binding act, not a reassurance — moves the sermon's weight from Boaz's warmth at v.10 to his commitment at v.13 and his honesty at v.12. And recovering item 1 means the gate at v.11 can be preached as what it is: the venue named in the dark, where everything will be settled in the light. **Both are supplyable from the pulpit in a sentence each, and neither survives any English translation.**

### Biblical-Theological Themes

Two themes, selected by textual signal and purpose connection; one named and declined.

#### (1) Redemption, and the order of claims

- **Where this passage sits:** at the point where the redeemer category, introduced at 2:20, is accepted by the man it names and immediately qualified by a prior claim `[T]`. **Six of the book's twenty-one occurrences of גָּאַל are in these two verses** `[T]`.
- **What comes before:** Lev 25:25's "his redeemer who is near to him", with its order of precedence `[T]`; the whole institution of kinship-redemption of land and person `[S]`; and Naomi's partitive at 2:20, which conceded a rival `[T]`.
- **What comes after:** the gate scene, where the nearer redeemer declines and the cost becomes public (4:1–8) `[T]`; the women's blessing at 4:14, "blessed be YHWH who has not left you without a redeemer today" `[T]`; and the New Testament's use of redemption language for Christ's work, reasoning from Israel's rescue as the archetype `[S]`.
- **What this contributes:** **the obstacle is the theme's content here, not an interruption of it.** Redemption in this book is not simply willingness; it is willingness inside an order of claims that has to be cleared publicly and at cost. That is what makes the category fit Christ by escalation rather than by resemblance (Christological Reading). **Confidence:** *high* that the theme is textually signalled; *moderate–high* on the reading of the obstacle.

#### (2) Oath, word and fulfilment

- **Where this passage sits:** at the book's only oath `[T]`, sworn where it cannot be enforced, and closed by a question using the vocabulary of promises that did or did not fall `[T]`.
- **What comes before:** the covenant formula חַי־יְהוָה across 43 verses `[T]`; and the five-verse formula of the word that did not fall — Josh 21:45; 23:14; 1 Sam 3:19; 1 Kgs 8:56; 2 Kgs 10:10 `[T]`, each a reckoning that God's word had stood.
- **What comes after:** Boaz performing at the gate what he swore on the floor (4:9–10) `[T]`; and the canon's long development of God's word as the thing that does not fall, into the New Testament's claim that in Christ every promise is Yes `[S]`.
- **What this contributes:** **the unit sets a human oath inside the canon's vocabulary for divine faithfulness, and leaves the outcome a question.** Naomi asks אֵיךְ יִפֹּל דָּבָר, "how the word will fall", and both Greek editions, hearing the formula, supplied the negative it carries everywhere else `[T]`. The book's answer comes at 4:13, where the only act the narrator attributes to YHWH in his own voice occurs `[T]`. **Confidence:** *high* on the distributions; *moderate* on the reading, since נָפַל with דָּבָר is seventeen verses and does not force it.

#### How the two relate

**(b) Mutual illumination.** Not climactic convergence: the two do not meet in a single clause here. **Each explains why the other takes the form it does.** The redemption theme needs an oath because the procedure cannot be completed on the floor — the nearer claimant has to be dealt with first, and nothing can be done about that until morning. And the oath theme needs the redemption order, because an unenforceable promise is only interesting when there is a real obstacle it might not survive. **Remove the nearer redeemer and the oath is decoration; remove the oath and the obstacle is merely a delay.**

#### Declined

**Marriage and divine-human union.** The threshing-floor scene invites it, and the overview's second preaching trap warns against using 1:16–17 as a marriage text while its third warns against allegorising chapter 3's details `[S: overview]`. **The unit's own vocabulary is legal, not nuptial**: גָּאַל six times, שַׁעַר, אָמְנָם, the two-branch conditional, the oath `[T]`. No marriage word occurs in these nine verses `[T]`. Not entered.

### Christological Reading

#### Type of Christological connection

**Typology through an established category, and contrast.** The category is **redeemer**, and the 3:1–9 dig established that it passes all four tests at *high confidence* — unusually, and because the book itself does the work: the category is named at 2:20, the root occurs 21 times, the climax is structured as a redemption transaction, and the book ends on David `[S: 3:1–9 dig]`. That verdict is accepted and not re-derived.

**What this unit adds is the *contrast* leg, and it is sharper here than anywhere else in the book.**

The four tests, scored for this unit's contribution:

1. **Theological-category test — passes.** גֹּאֵל is an institution with legal content `[T]`.
2. **NT-precedent test — passes.** Redemption language is applied to Christ's work directly and repeatedly `[S]`.
3. **Escalation test — passes, and this unit is where the escalation becomes visible.** Boaz accepts the claim, swears to it, **and must then defer to a man with a better right** `[T]`. The antitype has no prior claimant, no competing right, and no procedure to clear `[S]`. **The obstacle is what the escalation escalates past.**
4. **Authorial-pattern test — passes.** The root's distribution builds deliberately from 2:20 to 4:14, and this unit is its densest point `[T]`.

**All four pass: *high confidence* typology at the category level**, with this unit contributing the contrast.

#### How the passage points to Christ

- **By the honesty of v.12, which is not a Christological feature but a human one.** Boaz discloses the prior claim against his own interest `[T]`. This is the unit's imitable act (Tool 13), and it is *not* where the typology lies — a point worth making, because the two are easily confused.
- **By the oath at v.13, which is where the typology does lie.** A redeemer binds himself by the divine name to a redemption he cannot yet perform, in a setting where nothing could compel him `[T]`. The escalation: the greater Redeemer's commitment needed no oath, and the New Testament says as much about a God who swore by himself because there was no one greater `[S]`. *Moderate–high* on the connection; the escalation is the point and the resemblance is the weaker half.
- **By the obstacle, which the gospel does not have.** The whole suspense of chapters 3–4 is procedural: someone else has first refusal `[T]`. **There is no nearer redeemer to Christ**, and the contrast is the clearest Christological content in the unit `[I]`. *Moderate–high.*
- **By the sending-not-empty at v.17.** Boaz puts himself under the duty the law lays on the stronger party at a release (Tool 7d) `[I]`. *Moderate*, and it is a trajectory observation rather than a type.

#### Trajectory

- **What the passage anticipates:** a redemption that is willing, sworn, and not yet secured — and a settlement at a public place, at cost, before witnesses.
- **What Christ fulfils:** the category, escalated past the kinship system that defines it and past the order of claims that complicates it `[S]`.
- **What still remains:** the waiting. 3:18's posture — sitting while a matter is settled elsewhere, today — is the Christian's own, and the "today" of the text has become the "already" of the gospel `[I]`. *Moderate*, offered as canonical reflection.

#### Moralism check

- **The "be like X" temptation here is unusually strong and unusually specific:** "be a Boaz" — generous, protective, discreet. **The text's own imitable act is narrower and harder** (v.12's disclosure), and the generosity at v.15 is the part sermons take.
- **And there is a second temptation, aimed at the other character:** "wait like Ruth". 3:18 is Naomi's instruction, its ground is Boaz's temperament, and its answer is a court convening (Tool 7g). **Preached as a devotional posture it becomes a counsel of passivity the text does not give.**
- **The gospel grounding:** the oath holds because of what the swearer is, and the book's confidence at 3:18 rests there `[T]`. For the reader, the ground is a Redeemer whose word needs no oath and whose claim no one precedes.
- **Christ as hero, not just example:** in this unit the hero is a man under oath to a redemption he has not yet secured. **The right sermon makes the category the subject** — what a redeemer is, and what it costs — rather than making Boaz an exemplar of good behaviour at a threshing floor.

**Confidence:** *high* on the typology at the category level (inherited from the 3:1–9 dig and re-grounded here); *moderate–high* on the contrast reading, which is this unit's own contribution.

### Difficult / Contested Verses

Four live issues.

#### 3:10 — "you have not gone after young men"

- **Category:** ethical / pastoral; and the unit's most mishandled verse.
- **The difficulty:** read as a compliment on Ruth's sexual restraint, which both flatters and misses the clause's grammar.
- **Common mishandlings:** making it a commendation of chastity in general; or, in the other direction, hearing it as Boaz's relief that he has no younger rival.
- **Rhetorical function in context:** it is the **ground** of a verdict. The clause is governed by לְבִלְתִּי and the verdict is about **חֶסֶד** — "you have made your last kindness better than the first *in that* you did not go after the young men" `[T]`.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the kindness is to the dead man's name and the household, not to Boaz `[T]` — the sweep's reading, and the particle supports it. Ruth's approach to an older kinsman with a redemption claim was the costlier and less obvious route, and Boaz says so `[I]`. *Moderate–high.*
- **Suggested handling:** preach it as a verdict on loyalty, not on sexual conduct, and let the particle do the work.

#### 3:12 and the nearer redeemer — why does Boaz not simply act?

- **Category:** doctrinal / apologetic.
- **The difficulty:** a modern hearer reads the deferral as evasion, or wonders why a man with the means does not simply marry her.
- **Common mishandlings:** treating v.12 as reluctance (the English versions' "however" and "Although" encourage it — Tool 4); or skipping the nearer redeemer as a plot device.
- **Rhetorical function in context:** v.12 is an **addition**, marked by וְגַם, not a contrast `[T]`. Boaz is supplying a fact, not raising an objection.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the redemption order was a legal reality with a prior claimant (Lev 25:25's "near") `[T]`, and Boaz's disclosure is against his own interest at the moment he has been invited to act `[T]`. **This is the unit's moral centre, not its complication.** *Moderate–high.*
- **Suggested handling:** make v.12 the sermon's point of honesty rather than its obstacle. Then the gate scene reads as a man clearing a claim rather than winning a contest.

#### 3:14 — the secrecy

- **Category:** pastoral / apologetic.
- **The difficulty:** "let it not be known that the woman came to the threshing floor" `[T]` concedes that what happened could be construed badly, and a hearer may ask whether something is being hidden.
- **Common mishandlings:** reading the secrecy as evidence of guilt; or explaining it away as mere discretion.
- **Rhetorical function in context:** it is a **resolve with no addressee** — the unit's one quasi-narratorial moment, and three ancient witnesses tried to attach it to a speaker (Tool 6, Textual Variants) `[T]`.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the text has already given Boaz's own verdict on the night at 3:10 `[T]`, and the 3:1–9 dig showed that the narrator keeps the forbidden Deuteronomic idiom and this scene's vocabulary **lexically apart** — גָּלָה with כָּנָף stands in exactly two verses and both are the incest prohibition, while Ruth uncovers the מַרְגְּלֹת and asks Boaz to *spread* his כָּנָף `[S: 3:1–9 dig]`. **So the secrecy protects a reputation, not a secret.** *Moderate–high.*
- **Suggested handling:** say plainly what the risk was, cite 3:10 as the text's own verdict, and move on. Do not dwell.

#### 3:18 — "wait, my daughter"

- **Category:** weaponised / pastoral.
- **The difficulty:** the verse is quoted as a general counsel to wait on God, and used to tell people in difficult situations to do nothing.
- **Common mishandlings:** "wait on the Lord" preaching from a verse that does not mention God; and its mirror image, treating the waiting as mere passivity.
- **Rhetorical function in context:** Naomi's **assessment of a man**, with two clauses, both about Boaz `[T]`.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** **the ground of the waiting is stated and it is not divine providence but human character** — "for the man will not rest until he has finished the matter today" `[T]`. And the waiting is answered immediately: **six occurrences of יָשַׁב in 4:1–4** `[T]`. **She sits because a process has begun, not because nothing is happening.** *High* on the counts; *moderate–high* on the reading.
- **Suggested handling:** preach the ground Naomi gives. For the Christian reader the character in question is finally Christ's — but get there by way of the text's own argument rather than substituting God for Boaz in verse 18.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: preaching 3:18 as "wait on the Lord"

- **What it looks like:** "Naomi tells Ruth what God tells us all — be still, and let him work."
- **Why it's wrong:** the verse does not mention God `[T]`. Its stated ground is a man's temperament — "for the man will not rest until he has finished the matter today" — and its answer is a court convening: **six occurrences of "sit" in 4:1–4 against one imperative here** `[T]`. **She sits because the process has started.**
- **The corrective:** preach the ground Naomi actually gives, then make the move to Christ by argument rather than by substitution. The Christian's waiting is not "nothing is happening"; it is "the matter is being settled, today, by someone whose word holds."

### Pitfall: making v.12 Boaz's reluctance

- **What it looks like:** "Boaz wanted to help, *but* there was a problem…" — following the English versions' "however", "Yet", "Although".
- **Why it's wrong:** the Hebrew is **וְגַם**, "and moreover" `[T]`. Boaz *adds* the nearer redeemer; he does not oppose it to his promise. **The disclosure is voluntary and against his own interest**, made at the moment he has been invited to act on his own.
- **The corrective:** make v.12 the unit's point of honesty. It is the imitable act here, and it is harder than the generosity at v.15 that sermons usually take.

### Pitfall: "a woman of excellence" as a character reference

- **What it looks like:** a sermon on Ruth's virtues, with Proverbs 31 read alongside and the canonical order doing the work.
- **Why it's wrong:** two things. **The verdict is public, not private** — Boaz says *all the gate of my people knows* `[T]`, and he is reporting the assembly's view, not offering his own. **And the canonical adjacency is withdrawn**: *Baba Batra* 14b puts Ruth first among the Writings, the Megillot arrangement is centuries later, and Campbell suggests the Leningrad order may have been prompted by this very phrase `[S: Logos L1]`. **The relation to Prov 31:10–31 is real and measured; the sequence may not be preached.**
- **The corrective:** preach the phrase and the gate together — the one place where her reputation and the venue of her vindication stand in the same sentence. Use Proverbs 31 as a canonical neighbour, not as the previous page.

### Pitfall: building on the garment, or the six measures

- **What it looks like:** "the six measures signify the six days of labour before the Sabbath rest"; or a sermon on the kind of cloak it was and what it symbolised.
- **Why it's wrong:** **the Hebrew names no unit of measure** `[T]` and all three English versions supply one; and **מִטְפַּחַת stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible**, the other a plural in a list of finery `[T]`, so the sense is not establishable. The overview's third preaching trap bites hardest exactly here `[S: overview]`.
- **The corrective:** preach what Ruth says the barley meant — it was sent so she would not go home empty (3:17) `[T]` — and let the measure and the cloth alone.

### Pitfall: treating the oath as a reassurance

- **What it looks like:** "Boaz comforts her: as the LORD lives, I'll take care of it."
- **Why it's wrong:** חַי־יְהוָה is the binding formula, and in this book it occurs once `[T]`. **It is the transaction, not the bedside manner** — which is why Naomi can be as confident as she is six verses later.
- **The corrective:** say what an oath by the divine name did. Then the contrast with a Redeemer who needed no oath lands by itself.

### Pitfall: preaching this as the resolution of chapter 3

- **What it looks like:** a sermon that ends warmly at v.15 or v.17 and treats the threshing-floor crisis as over.
- **Why it's wrong:** the unit's last verse is a question — "until you know how the matter falls" — using the verb of promises that stand or fail, in a canon where that formula is always negated and here is not `[T]`. **Nothing is secured.** On the Masoretic reading of 3:15 the last action is Boaz walking into town to do something not yet done `[T]`.
- **The corrective:** end where the text ends. A woman sitting down, a man walking into town, and a matter to be settled today.
