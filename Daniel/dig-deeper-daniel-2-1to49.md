## The Passage

The WLC text is given with its vowels and without its accents, for legibility; the paragraph markers פ (petuchah, "open") and ס (setumah, "closed") are printed where Leningrad has them. The NASB95 follows each verse. **The Hebrew runs to the word אֲרָמִית ("in Aramaic") in 2:4; everything after it is Aramaic.**

**1** וּבִשְׁנַת שְׁתַּיִם לְמַלְכוּת נְבֻכַדְנֶצַּר חָלַם נְבֻכַדְנֶצַּר חֲלֹמוֹת וַתִּתְפָּעֶם רוּחוֹ וּשְׁנָתוֹ נִהְיְתָה עָלָיו׃

> Now in the second year of the reign of Nebuchadnezzar, Nebuchadnezzar had dreams; and his spirit was troubled and his sleep left him.

**2** וַיֹּאמֶר הַמֶּלֶךְ לִקְרֹא לַחַרְטֻמִּים וְלָאַשָּׁפִים וְלַמְכַשְּׁפִים וְלַכַּשְׂדִּים לְהַגִּיד לַמֶּלֶךְ חֲלֹמֹתָיו וַיָּבֹאוּ וַיַּעַמְדוּ לִפְנֵי הַמֶּלֶךְ׃

> Then the king gave orders to call in the magicians, the conjurers, the sorcerers and the Chaldeans to tell the king his dreams. So they came in and stood before the king.

**3** וַיֹּאמֶר לָהֶם הַמֶּלֶךְ חֲלוֹם חָלָמְתִּי וַתִּפָּעֶם רוּחִי לָדַעַת אֶת־הַחֲלוֹם׃

> The king said to them, “I had a dream and my spirit is anxious to understand the dream.”

**4** וַיְדַבְּרוּ הַכַּשְׂדִּים לַמֶּלֶךְ אֲרָמִית מַלְכָּא לְעָלְמִין חֱיִי אֱמַר חֶלְמָא לעבדיך וּפִשְׁרָא נְחַוֵּא׃

> Then the Chaldeans spoke to the king in Aramaic: “O king, live forever! Tell the dream to your servants, and we will declare the interpretation.”

**5** עָנֵה מַלְכָּא וְאָמַר לכשדיא מִלְּתָא מִנִּי אַזְדָּא הֵן לָא תְהוֹדְעוּנַּנִי חֶלְמָא וּפִשְׁרֵהּ הַדָּמִין תִּתְעַבְדוּן וּבָתֵּיכוֹן נְוָלִי יִתְּשָׂמוּן׃

> The king replied to the Chaldeans, “The command from me is firm: if you do not make known to me the dream and its interpretation, you will be torn limb from limb and your houses will be made a rubbish heap.

**6** וְהֵן חֶלְמָא וּפִשְׁרֵהּ תְּהַחֲוֺן מַתְּנָן וּנְבִזְבָּה וִיקָר שַׂגִּיא תְּקַבְּלוּן מִן־קֳדָמָי לָהֵן חֶלְמָא וּפִשְׁרֵהּ הַחֲוֺנִי׃

> “But if you declare the dream and its interpretation, you will receive from me gifts and a reward and great honor; therefore declare to me the dream and its interpretation.”

**7** עֲנוֹ תִנְיָנוּת וְאָמְרִין מַלְכָּא חֶלְמָא יֵאמַר לְעַבְדוֹהִי וּפִשְׁרָה נְהַחֲוֵה׃

> They answered a second time and said, “Let the king tell the dream to his servants, and we will declare the interpretation.”

**8** עָנֵה מַלְכָּא וְאָמַר מִן־יַצִּיב יָדַע אֲנָה דִּי עִדָּנָא אַנְתּוּן זָבְנִין כָּל־קֳבֵל דִּי חֲזֵיתוֹן דִּי אַזְדָּא מִנִּי מִלְּתָא׃

> The king replied, “I know for certain that you are bargaining for time, inasmuch as you have seen that the command from me is firm,

**9** דִּי הֵן־חֶלְמָא לָא תְהוֹדְעֻנַּנִי חֲדָה־הִיא דָתְכוֹן וּמִלָּה כִדְבָה וּשְׁחִיתָה הזמנתון לְמֵאמַר קָדָמַי עַד דִּי עִדָּנָא יִשְׁתַּנֵּא לָהֵן חֶלְמָא אֱמַרוּ לִי וְאִנְדַּע דִּי פִשְׁרֵהּ תְּהַחֲוֻנַּנִי׃

> that if you do not make the dream known to me, there is only one decree for you. For you have agreed together to speak lying and corrupt words before me until the situation is changed; therefore tell me the dream, that I may know that you can declare to me its interpretation.”

**10** עֲנוֹ כשדיא קֳדָם־מַלְכָּא וְאָמְרִין לָא־אִיתַי אֲנָשׁ עַל־יַבֶּשְׁתָּא דִּי מִלַּת מַלְכָּא יוּכַל לְהַחֲוָיָה כָּל־קֳבֵל דִּי כָּל־מֶלֶךְ רַב וְשַׁלִּיט מִלָּה כִדְנָה לָא שְׁאֵל לְכָל־חַרְטֹּם וְאָשַׁף וְכַשְׂדָּי׃

> The Chaldeans answered the king and said, “There is not a man on earth who could declare the matter for the king, inasmuch as no great king or ruler has ever asked anything like this of any magician, conjurer or Chaldean.

**11** וּמִלְּתָא דִי־מַלְכָּה שָׁאֵל יַקִּירָה וְאָחֳרָן לָא אִיתַי דִּי יְחַוִּנַּהּ קֳדָם מַלְכָּא לָהֵן אֱלָהִין דִּי מְדָרְהוֹן עִם־בִּשְׂרָא לָא אִיתוֹהִי׃

> “Moreover, the thing which the king demands is difficult, and there is no one else who could declare it to the king except gods, whose dwelling place is not with mortal flesh.”

**12** כָּל־קֳבֵל דְּנָה מַלְכָּא בְּנַס וּקְצַף שַׂגִּיא וַאֲמַר לְהוֹבָדָה לְכֹל חַכִּימֵי בָבֶל׃

> Because of this the king became indignant and very furious and gave orders to destroy all the wise men of Babylon.

**13** וְדָתָא נֶפְקַת וְחַכִּימַיָּא מִתְקַטְּלִין וּבְעוֹ דָּנִיֵּאל וְחַבְרוֹהִי לְהִתְקְטָלָה׃ פ

> So the decree went forth that the wise men should be slain; and they looked for Daniel and his friends to kill them.

**14** בֵּאדַיִן דָּנִיֵּאל הֲתִיב עֵטָא וּטְעֵם לְאַרְיוֹךְ רַב־טַבָּחַיָּא דִּי מַלְכָּא דִּי נְפַק לְקַטָּלָה לְחַכִּימֵי בָּבֶל׃

> Then Daniel replied with discretion and discernment to Arioch, the captain of the king’s bodyguard, who had gone forth to slay the wise men of Babylon;

**15** עָנֵה וְאָמַר לְאַרְיוֹךְ שַׁלִּיטָא דִי־מַלְכָּא עַל־מָה דָתָא מְהַחְצְפָה מִן־קֳדָם מַלְכָּא אֱדַיִן מִלְּתָא הוֹדַע אַרְיוֹךְ לְדָנִיֵּאל׃

> he said to Arioch, the king’s commander, “For what reason is the decree from the king so urgent?” Then Arioch informed Daniel about the matter.

**16** וְדָנִיֵּאל עַל וּבְעָה מִן־מַלְכָּא דִּי זְמָן יִנְתֵּן־לֵהּ וּפִשְׁרָא לְהַחֲוָיָה לְמַלְכָּא׃ פ

> So Daniel went in and requested of the king that he would give him time, in order that he might declare the interpretation to the king.

**17** אֱדַיִן דָּנִיֵּאל לְבַיְתֵהּ אֲזַל וְלַחֲנַנְיָה מִישָׁאֵל וַעֲזַרְיָה חַבְרוֹהִי מִלְּתָא הוֹדַע׃

> Then Daniel went to his house and informed his friends, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah, about the matter,

**18** וְרַחֲמִין לְמִבְעֵא מִן־קֳדָם אֱלָהּ שְׁמַיָּא עַל־רָזָה דְּנָה דִּי לָא יְהֹבְדוּן דָּנִיֵּאל וְחַבְרוֹהִי עִם־שְׁאָר חַכִּימֵי בָבֶל׃

> so that they might request compassion from the God of heaven concerning this mystery, so that Daniel and his friends would not be destroyed with the rest of the wise men of Babylon.

**19** אֱדַיִן לְדָנִיֵּאל בְּחֶזְוָא דִי־לֵילְיָא רָזָה גֲלִי אֱדַיִן דָּנִיֵּאל בָּרִךְ לֶאֱלָהּ שְׁמַיָּא׃

> Then the mystery was revealed to Daniel in a night vision. Then Daniel blessed the God of heaven;

**20** עָנֵה דָנִיֵּאל וְאָמַר לֶהֱוֵא שְׁמֵהּ דִּי־אֱלָהָא מְבָרַךְ מִן־עָלְמָא וְעַד־עָלְמָא דִּי חָכְמְתָא וּגְבוּרְתָא דִּי לֵהּ־הִיא׃

> Daniel said, “Let the name of God be blessed forever and ever, For wisdom and power belong to Him.

**21** וְהוּא מְהַשְׁנֵא עִדָּנַיָּא וְזִמְנַיָּא מְהַעְדֵּה מַלְכִין וּמְהָקֵים מַלְכִין יָהֵב חָכְמְתָא לְחַכִּימִין וּמַנְדְּעָא לְיָדְעֵי בִינָה׃

> “It is He who changes the times and the epochs; He removes kings and establishes kings; He gives wisdom to wise men And knowledge to men of understanding.

**22** הוּא גָּלֵא עַמִּיקָתָא וּמְסַתְּרָתָא יָדַע מָה בַחֲשׁוֹכָא ונהירא עִמֵּהּ שְׁרֵא׃

> “It is He who reveals the profound and hidden things; He knows what is in the darkness, And the light dwells with Him.

**23** לָךְ ׀ אֱלָהּ אֲבָהָתִי מְהוֹדֵא וּמְשַׁבַּח אֲנָה דִּי חָכְמְתָא וּגְבוּרְתָא יְהַבְתְּ לִי וּכְעַן הוֹדַעְתַּנִי דִּי־בְעֵינָא מִנָּךְ דִּי־מִלַּת מַלְכָּא הוֹדַעְתֶּנָא׃

> “To You, O God of my fathers, I give thanks and praise, For You have given me wisdom and power; Even now You have made known to me what we requested of You, For You have made known to us the king’s matter.”

**24** כָּל־קֳבֵל דְּנָה דָּנִיֵּאל עַל עַל־אַרְיוֹךְ דִּי מַנִּי מַלְכָּא לְהוֹבָדָה לְחַכִּימֵי בָבֶל אֲזַל ׀ וְכֵן אֲמַר־לֵהּ לְחַכִּימֵי בָבֶל אַל־תְּהוֹבֵד הַעֵלְנִי קֳדָם מַלְכָּא וּפִשְׁרָא לְמַלְכָּא אֲחַוֵּא׃ ס

> Therefore, Daniel went in to Arioch, whom the king had appointed to destroy the wise men of Babylon; he went and spoke to him as follows: “Do not destroy the wise men of Babylon! Take me into the king’s presence, and I will declare the interpretation to the king.”

**25** אֱדַיִן אַרְיוֹךְ בְּהִתְבְּהָלָה הַנְעֵל לְדָנִיֵּאל קֳדָם מַלְכָּא וְכֵן אֲמַר־לֵהּ דִּי־הַשְׁכַּחַת גְּבַר מִן־בְּנֵי גָלוּתָא דִּי יְהוּד דִּי פִשְׁרָא לְמַלְכָּא יְהוֹדַע׃

> Then Arioch hurriedly brought Daniel into the king’s presence and spoke to him as follows: “I have found a man among the exiles from Judah who can make the interpretation known to the king!”

**26** עָנֵה מַלְכָּא וְאָמַר לְדָנִיֵּאל דִּי שְׁמֵהּ בֵּלְטְשַׁאצַּר האיתיך כָּהֵל לְהוֹדָעֻתַנִי חֶלְמָא דִי־חֲזֵית וּפִשְׁרֵהּ׃

> The king said to Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, “Are you able to make known to me the dream which I have seen and its interpretation?”

**27** עָנֵה דָנִיֵּאל קֳדָם מַלְכָּא וְאָמַר רָזָה דִּי־מַלְכָּא שָׁאֵל לָא חַכִּימִין אָשְׁפִין חַרְטֻמִּין גָּזְרִין יָכְלִין לְהַחֲוָיָה לְמַלְכָּא׃

> Daniel answered before the king and said, “As for the mystery about which the king has inquired, neither wise men, conjurers, magicians nor diviners are able to declare it to the king.

**28** בְּרַם אִיתַי אֱלָהּ בִּשְׁמַיָּא גָּלֵא רָזִין וְהוֹדַע לְמַלְכָּא נְבוּכַדְנֶצַּר מָה דִּי לֶהֱוֵא בְּאַחֲרִית יוֹמַיָּא חֶלְמָךְ וְחֶזְוֵי רֵאשָׁךְ עַל־מִשְׁכְּבָךְ דְּנָה הוּא׃ פ

> “However, there is a God in heaven who reveals mysteries, and He has made known to King Nebuchadnezzar what will take place in the latter days. This was your dream and the visions in your mind while on your bed.

**29** אַנְתְּה מַלְכָּא רַעְיוֹנָךְ עַל־מִשְׁכְּבָךְ סְלִקוּ מָה דִּי לֶהֱוֵא אַחֲרֵי דְנָה וְגָלֵא רָזַיָּא הוֹדְעָךְ מָה־דִי לֶהֱוֵא׃

> “As for you, O king, while on your bed your thoughts turned to what would take place in the future; and He who reveals mysteries has made known to you what will take place.

**30** וַאֲנָה לָא בְחָכְמָה דִּי־אִיתַי בִּי מִן־כָּל־חַיַּיָּא רָזָא דְנָה גֱּלִי לִי לָהֵן עַל־דִּבְרַת דִּי פִשְׁרָא לְמַלְכָּא יְהוֹדְעוּן וְרַעְיוֹנֵי לִבְבָךְ תִּנְדַּע׃

> “But as for me, this mystery has not been revealed to me for any wisdom residing in me more than in any other living man, but for the purpose of making the interpretation known to the king, and that you may understand the thoughts of your mind.

**31** אַנְתְּה מַלְכָּא חָזֵה הֲוַיְתָ וַאֲלוּ צְלֵם חַד שַׂגִּיא צַלְמָא דִּכֵּן רַב וְזִיוֵהּ יַתִּיר קָאֵם לְקָבְלָךְ וְרֵוֵהּ דְּחִיל׃

> “You, O king, were looking and behold, there was a single great statue; that statue, which was large and of extraordinary splendor, was standing in front of you, and its appearance was awesome.

**32** הוּא צַלְמָא רֵאשֵׁהּ דִּי־דְהַב טָב חֲדוֹהִי וּדְרָעוֹהִי דִּי כְסַף מְעוֹהִי וְיַרְכָתֵהּ דִּי נְחָשׁ׃

> “The head of that statue was made of fine gold, its breast and its arms of silver, its belly and its thighs of bronze,

**33** שָׁקוֹהִי דִּי פַרְזֶל רַגְלוֹהִי מנהון דִּי פַרְזֶל ומנהון דִּי חֲסַף׃

> its legs of iron, its feet partly of iron and partly of clay.

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims that emerged across several tools:

1. **The chapter's question is "who can make known?", and its answer is "there is a God in heaven".** The Chaldeans say לָא־אִיתַי אֲנָשׁ עַל־יַבֶּשְׁתָּא ("there is not a man on earth", 2:10) who can do it, except gods "whose dwelling is not with flesh" (2:11); Daniel answers בְּרַם אִיתַי אֱלָהּ בִּשְׁמַיָּא ("however, there is a God in heaven", 2:28). The same word, אִיתַי ("there is"), stands at 2:10, 2:11 and 2:28, verified by lemma (WLC). The vocabulary of the demand carries the whole chapter: יְדַע ("to know", and in its causative "to make known") 17 times, פְּשַׁר ("interpretation") 13 times, חֲוָא ("to declare") 10 times — in Daniel 2 alone (WLC, by lemma). `[T]` Surfaced by: Tool 3 (structure), Tool 10 (repetition), Tool 7, Tool 1. *High confidence.*

2. **Daniel's doxology (2:20–23) is the chapter's key, and each line of it is answered later in the chapter.** He praises the God who "changes times" (2:21; the Chaldeans had stalled "until the time is changed", 2:9 — the same verb שְׁנָה and noun עִדָּן at both), "removes kings and sets up kings" (2:21; the kingdoms "arise" and God "sets up" the last, 2:39, 44 — the same verb קוּם), "gives wisdom" (2:21, 23; "not by any wisdom in me", 2:30) and "reveals the deep and hidden things" (2:22; "a God in heaven who reveals mysteries", 2:28, 29, 47). Every link verified by lemma (WLC). `[T]` for the links; `[I]` that the doxology functions as the chapter's hermeneutic. Surfaced by: Tool 3, Tool 5, Tool 10, Tool 11 (Move 4). *High confidence on the links; moderate–high on the design.*

3. **The kingdom God sets up is not one more kingdom in the series but the end of the series — and the text names only one of the four.** Only Babylon is identified: אַנְתְּה־הוּא רֵאשָׁה דִּי דַהֲבָא ("you are the head of gold", 2:38). The rest are "another kingdom", "a third", "a fourth" (2:39–40). The NASB95's editorial headings "Medo-Persia and Greece" and "Rome" (above 2:39 and 2:40) are the translators' interpretation, not the text. What the text does insist on is that the stone was cut out דִּי־לָא בִידַיִן ("without hands", 2:34, 45), struck the image at its feet, and turned the whole of it to chaff at once — כַחֲדָה ("all together", 2:35) — before becoming a mountain that "filled the whole earth". `[T]` Surfaced by: Tool 3, Tool 7, Tool 8, Tool 14, Difficult Verses. *High confidence on what is named and what is not.*

4. **Daniel is drawn as a second Joseph, but the chapter also plants Daniel 3.** The Joseph contacts are rare and specific: a king's spirit "troubled" by a dream (פָּעַם + רוּחַ, 2:1, 3 // Gen 41:8 — four verses in the Hebrew Bible); magicians summoned to a dream (Gen 41:8 and Dan 2:2 only); and the confession "it is not in me" (Gen 41:16 // Dan 2:30). Then the chapter ends with the king falling on his face and doing סְגִד ("homage, worship") to Daniel (2:46) — and in ch. 3 the same king makes a צְלֵם ("image") of gold and orders everyone to fall and סְגִד ("worship") it, eleven times. The image (צְלֵם) is the same word in both chapters. `[T]` Surfaced by: Tool 11 (Moves 1, 2, 4), Tool 7, Tool 8, Tool 13. *High confidence.*

5. **The New Testament reads Daniel 2 through its Greek, and the Greek it reads is Theodotion's.** ἃ δεῖ γενέσθαι ("what must take place", Theodotion 2:28, 29, 45) opens and closes Revelation (Rev 1:1; 4:1, with μετὰ ταῦτα, "after these things", as at Theodotion 2:29; 22:6, with πιστοὶ καὶ ἀληθινοί, "faithful and true", as at Theodotion 2:45); and Luke 20:18 says of the stone ἐφ' ὃν δ' ἂν πέσῃ, λικμήσει αὐτόν ("on whomever it falls, it will scatter him like chaff") with λικμήσει, Theodotion's verb at 2:44. `[T]` for the Greek wording (SBLGNT; Theodotion export); `[I]` for the allusions. Surfaced by: Tool 8 (ancient versions), Tool 11, Christological Reading. *High confidence.*

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book-level** `[S: overview]`: to show that the God of heaven gives and removes every kingdom on earth, so that his people can serve inside the empire without worshipping it, and suffer under it without despairing of it.

**Passage-level** `[I]`: Daniel 2 is the book's first statement, to and before a pagan king, of the whole programme: *every* human kingdom, beginning with the greatest the reader knows, is given by "the God of heaven" (2:37), is temporary and declining (2:39–43), and will be ended all at once by a kingdom God himself sets up (2:44). The chapter establishes this as **revealed knowledge**: the empire's own wise men confess it cannot be known by human means (2:10–11), and Daniel confesses that he does not know it by his own wisdom either (2:30). The passage therefore does two things at once: it tells the exile *what* history is (a series given and ended by God) and *how* that is known (by revelation to the humble, not by the techniques of the court).

**What the narrator chose to include** `[T]`: the narration slows sharply for two moments — the escalating exchange between king and Chaldeans (2:4–11, three rounds of speech) and Daniel's doxology (2:20–23) — and races through others: the night of revelation is one clause (2:19a), and Daniel's promotion one verse (2:48). The chapter spends its words on *the impossibility of knowing* and *the praise of the God who makes known*.

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement.** Daniel 1 ends with three things in place. God has "given" (נָתַן) the four youths מַדָּע וְהַשְׂכֵּל ("knowledge and intelligence"), and Daniel הֵבִין בְּכָל־חָזוֹן וַחֲלֹמוֹת ("understood all kinds of visions and dreams", 1:17) — a claim the reader has been told but has not yet seen. The king has found them "ten times better than all the magicians and conjurers" (1:20). And the opening verses of the book have stated, without explanation, that "the Lord gave Jehoiakim … into his hand" (1:2). `[T]`

**Necessity answer.** Daniel 2 exists here because ch. 1 has made three claims that only a test can prove: (a) that Daniel understands *dreams* — ch. 2 is the first dream; (b) that the four are better than the court's magicians — ch. 2 sets the whole guild against an impossible demand and condemns them all; (c) that "the Lord gave" Jerusalem's king into Nebuchadnezzar's hand — ch. 2 tells Nebuchadnezzar to his face that "the God of heaven has given you the kingdom" (2:37). The verb of 1:2 becomes the theology of 2:37–38. `[T]` for the links; `[I]` for the necessity.

**Implication.** Daniel 2 is not an exciting story that happens to follow ch. 1; it is the *proof* of ch. 1. And because it opens the Aramaic, it also stands at the head of the book's public section — the material addressed, in the empire's language, to "all the peoples" (cf. 3:4; 4:1 [Heb 3:31]; 6:25 [Heb 6:26]) `[S: overview]`. The first thing said in that language to the empire's king is that his kingdom is borrowed and will end.

**Revisit after Tool 11.** Move 2 sharpens the positional answer: the book's first Genesis contact (1:2, Shinar) and its second (1:10, the "dejected" faces of Gen 40:6) are followed here by the densest Joseph cluster in the book. Ch. 1 set up a Joseph *situation* (a young exile trained at a foreign court); ch. 2 runs the Joseph *plot* (a king's troubling dream, the failure of the court's experts, the exile who says "not I, but God", and his promotion). `[I]`

#### Context

**Immediately before:** 1:1–21, in Hebrew — the exile, the renaming, the food test, the gift of understanding, and the frame "until the first year of Cyrus" (1:21).

**Immediately after:** 3:1–30 — the same king makes an image of gold and demands that everyone fall down and worship it. The reader has just heard Daniel tell him "you are the head of gold" (2:38); the next thing the king does is make an image that is gold from head to foot. `[T]` for the sequence; `[I]` for the irony, which the text does not state.

**Section:** the first of the Aramaic court narratives (2:4b–6:28) and the head of the proposed concentric arrangement 2 // 7 (four kingdoms ended by a God-given kingdom), whose lexical support the overview found weakest of the three pairs `[S: overview]`.

**Dating within the book.** "In the second year of the reign of Nebuchadnezzar" (2:1) sits awkwardly with the three years of training in 1:5, 18; see *Difficult / Contested Verses*.

### 3. Structure

Stripped of chapter and verse divisions, the chapter falls at its scene changes and speech boundaries. The Leningrad paragraph markers (as the WLC prints them) fall at 2:13 (פ), 2:16 (פ), 2:24 (ס), 2:28 (פ), 2:45 (פ) and 2:49 (פ) `[T]` — a reading tradition, not authorial; its spread across other manuscripts has not been checked `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`.

| Verses | Section | Function |
|--------|---------|----------|
| 2:1–13 | **The impossible demand** | Dream; the court summoned; the switch to Aramaic (2:4); three rounds of demand and refusal; the decree to destroy "all the wise men of Babylon" (2:12) |
| 2:14–16 | **Daniel intervenes** | "Discretion and discernment" (2:14); a request for time (2:16) |
| 2:17–23 | **Prayer, revelation, doxology** | Companions asked to seek "compassion from the God of heaven" (2:18); the mystery revealed "in a night vision" (2:19); the hymn (2:20–23) |

| 2:31–45 | **The dream and its interpretation** | The image (2:31–33); the stone (2:34–35); the kingdoms (2:36–43); the kingdom God sets up (2:44–45) |
| 2:46–49 | **The king's response** | Homage and offering (2:46); confession (2:47); promotion (2:48–49) |

**Device: an inclusio, and a centre.** `[T]`
- *Inclusio.* The chapter's crisis is the order לְהוֹבָדָה לְכֹל חַכִּימֵי בָבֶל ("to destroy all the wise men of Babylon", 2:12); its resolution is Daniel set רַב־סִגְנִין עַל כָּל־חַכִּימֵי בָבֶל ("chief prefect over all the wise men of Babylon", 2:48). The phrase חַכִּימֵי בָבֶל ("wise men of Babylon") stands at 2:12, 14, 18, 24 (twice) and 48 — six times in the chapter, at the hinges of the plot (WLC). The wise men condemned at the start are saved by, and placed under, the exile at the end.
- *Centre.* Daniel's hymn (2:20–23) sits at the turning point between need and answer, and each of its lines is answered later (Headline Finding 2; Tool 5).

**The two-part movement of the revelation scene.** Daniel says what he does *not* rely on twice and what he *does* rely on once: "not … wise men, conjurers, magicians nor diviners" (2:27) — "however, there is a God in heaven" (2:28) — "not … for any wisdom residing in me" (2:30). The positive statement is framed by the two negatives. `[T]`

**The dream report and its interpretation mirror each other** `[T]`: image (2:31–33) → stone strikes (2:34) → all crushed together, the stone becomes a mountain (2:35) // Babylon (2:37–38) → three kingdoms (2:39–43) → the God-given kingdom crushes all (2:44), the stone recalled (2:45). The interpretation runs the dream in the same order and ends by quoting it.

### 4. Linking Words

Aramaic narrative uses few logical connectors, but four carry the chapter:

- **כָּל־קֳבֵל דְּנָה ("because of this", 2:12, 24)** — the same connector turns the plot twice: *because of* the Chaldeans' refusal, the king orders the wise men destroyed (2:12); *because of* the revelation, Daniel goes to Arioch to stop it (2:24). →therefore→ in both places. `[T]`
- **בְּרַם ("however, but", 2:28)** — the hinge of the chapter: "the wise men … cannot declare it … *however*, there is a God in heaven". ←but← `[T]`
- **לָהֵן ("except, but", 2:11, 30)** — the Chaldeans' "*except* gods whose dwelling is not with flesh" (2:11) is answered by Daniel's "not for any wisdom in me, *but* for the purpose of making the interpretation known to the king" (2:30). Both use the same word. `[T]`
- **דִּי ("that, so that, because")** at 2:20 — "blessed be the name of God … *for* (דִּי) wisdom and power are his": the doxology is grounded, not merely exclaimed. ←for←
- **וּבְיוֹמֵיהוֹן דִּי מַלְכַיָּא אִנּוּן ("and in the days of those kings", 2:44)** — the temporal link that places God's kingdom *within* the time of the human kingdoms, not after the last of them. `[T]` `[I]` that this is load-bearing for the "already" of the kingdom (see Christological Reading).

### 5. Parallels

**The doxology (2:20–23) is poetry, and its parallels interpret each other** `[T]`:

> 20 *Blessed be the name of God for ever and ever* — *for wisdom and might are his.*
> 21 *He changes times and seasons* — *removes kings and sets up kings;*
> *gives wisdom to the wise* — *and knowledge to those who know understanding.*
> 22 *He reveals the deep and hidden things* — *knows what is in the darkness* — *and the light dwells with him.*
> 23 *To you, God of my fathers, I give thanks and praise,* — *for you have given me wisdom and might,* …

Three things follow:
- **The pair "wisdom and might" (חָכְמְתָא וּגְבוּרְתָא) frames the hymn** (2:20 and 2:23): what belongs to God (2:20) is what God has *given* Daniel (2:23). The hymn begins with God's possession and ends with Daniel's receipt.
- **The two halves of 2:21 are the two halves of the dream**: "changes times … removes kings and sets up kings" is the image and the stone; "gives wisdom … knowledge" is Daniel's interpretation. The hymn anticipates *both* the content and the means of the revelation.
- **2:22 is antithetical-synthetic**: "darkness" against "light", with God the knower of the one and the dwelling of the other — the dream is in the dark; God is light.

**The pairs in the interpretation** `[T]`: "partly iron, partly clay" and "iron mixed with clay" recur six times in 2:33–43 in slightly varied forms (2:33; 41 twice; 42; 43 twice) — the near-repetition is the device; the last form, "iron does not mix with clay" (2:43), states what the repetition has been building to.

### 6. Narrator's Comment

Narrative, so the tool applies. The narrator intrudes rarely, and each intrusion matters:

- **2:4: "Then the Chaldeans spoke to the king in Aramaic."** The word אֲרָמִית ("in Aramaic") is the narrator's, and the language of the book changes immediately after it. Whether the word is an authorial notice or a later scribal note marking the switch is debated (the BHS apparatus marks it "probably added"); either way it is the only place where the book *names* its language. `[T]` for the word and the switch; `[S]` for the apparatus note.
- **2:19: "Then the mystery was revealed to Daniel in a night vision."** The narrator, not Daniel, reports the revelation, before Daniel praises. The source of the knowledge is established before Daniel speaks.
- **2:46: the king's homage and offering are reported without comment.** The narrator neither approves nor condemns Daniel's acceptance, and Daniel says nothing. The silence is itself an interpretive datum (see *Difficult / Contested Verses*).

### 7. Vocabulary

**רָז ("mystery") — 2:18, 19, 27, 28, 29, 30, 47 (twice)** `[T]`
Eight occurrences in this chapter, and **only nine in the whole Hebrew Bible** — the ninth is 4:9 [Heb 4:6] (WLC, by lemma). The word is the chapter's own: before it the king had a "dream" (חֵלֶם); once Daniel and his friends pray about it, it becomes a רָז, a secret hidden *by God* and disclosed *by God* (2:18–19). Its plural in Daniel's confession, גָּלֵא רָזִין ("revealer of mysteries", 2:28), is repeated in the king's confession, וְגָלֵה רָזִין ("a revealer of mysteries", 2:47). The word is generally taken as a Persian loanword `[S]`. Theodotion renders it μυστήριον ("mystery") throughout, and that is the word Paul uses for God's hidden-then-revealed purpose (Rom 16:25; 1 Cor 2:7; Col 2:2) — see Tool 11. *High confidence on the counts; moderate on the loanword.*

**גְּלָה ("to reveal") — 2:19, 22, 28, 29, 30, 47 (twice)** and **גָּלוּ ("exile") — 2:25** `[T]` / `[I]`
The Aramaic verb "to reveal" occurs seven times in the chapter; outside Daniel 2 the same lemma occurs only twice in the Hebrew Bible, at Ezra 4:10 and 5:12, where it means **"to deport"** (WLC). Arioch presents Daniel as גְּבַר מִן־בְּנֵי גָלוּתָא דִּי יְהוּד ("a man among the exiles of Judah", 2:25) — the noun from the same root. The one through whom the mystery is *revealed* is one of the *exiled*. The shared root is lexical fact (*high confidence*); whether the narrator intends the play is not stated (`[I]`, *moderate confidence*). If intended, it is the chapter's theology in a pun: God uncovers his purpose through those whom the empire has uncovered and carried away.

**פְּשַׁר ("interpretation") — 13 occurrences in 12 verses** `[T]`
The noun the Chaldeans promise (2:4, 7) and the king demands (2:5, 6, 9). The demand turns on the *pair* חֶלְמָא וּפִשְׁרֵהּ ("the dream and its interpretation", 2:5, 6 twice, 9, 26) — the king will not accept an interpretation without the dream, because only someone who can tell the dream can be trusted with its meaning (2:9). Daniel's last word ends with the same pair, now certified: וְיַצִּיב חֶלְמָא וּמְהֵימַן פִּשְׁרֵהּ ("the dream is true and its interpretation trustworthy", 2:45). *High confidence.*

**צְלֵם ("statue, image") — 2:31 (twice), 32, 34, 35** `[T]`
The Aramaic word is used only in Daniel 2 and 3 (17 occurrences in 15 verses, WLC); its Hebrew cognate צֶלֶם is the word of Gen 1:26–27 ("in our image") and Gen 9:6, and also of idols (Num 33:52; 2 Kgs 11:18; Ezek 7:20; Amos 5:26). **Canonical history (Tool 7c):** the first occurrence in the canon is humanity made in God's image; the word's other career is the man-made image. In ch. 2 a human king is shown, in his own dream, as a צְלֵם made of metals; in ch. 3 he makes one. *Moderate confidence that the Gen 1 freight is in view — the word is cross-language and the image is a dream-symbol, not an idol, in ch. 2.* `[I]`

**אֱלָהּ שְׁמַיָּא ("the God of heaven") — 2:18, 19, 37, 44; with "in heaven", 2:28** `[T]`
The Aramaic pair "God" + "heaven" stands in fifteen verses of the Hebrew Bible: five in this chapter, two later in Daniel (5:21, 23), eight in the Persian documents of Ezra 5–7, and Jer 10:11 (where it is the gods who did *not* make heaven) (WLC). It is the title a pagan court can recognise. It is the title Daniel uses to the pagan king, and the narrator uses for Daniel's prayer. `[I]` it is chosen as the bridge between the God of Israel and the empire's own categories. *High confidence on the distribution.*

**מִנְחָה ("offering") and נִיחֹחִין ("fragrant offerings, incense") — 2:46** `[T]`
Both Aramaic words occur in only two verses each in the Hebrew Bible: here, and in the Persian decrees for the temple of "the God of heaven" (Ezra 6:10 נִיחוֹחִין; Ezra 7:17 מִנְחָה) (WLC). The king orders for Daniel the vocabulary of temple sacrifice. See *Difficult / Contested Verses*.

**Proper-noun inventory (Tool 7c, Question 0).** Nebuchadnezzar, the Chaldeans (2:2, 4, 5, 10), Babylon, Arioch, Daniel / Belteshazzar (2:26), Hananiah, Mishael, Azariah (2:17), Shadrach, Meshach, Abed-nego (2:49), Judah (2:25); numbers: "the second year" (2:1), a fourth kingdom (2:40). Routine: Babylon, Judah, the Hebrew and Babylonian names of the four (already freighted in ch. 1). **Candidates:**

- **Arioch (אַרְיוֹךְ), "captain of the king's bodyguard" (2:14).** The name occurs in **only two places in the Hebrew Bible**: here (2:14, 15, 24, 25) and Gen 14:1, 9, where Arioch king of Ellasar is one of four eastern kings allied with **Amraphel king of Shinar** (WLC; the index files the two bearers apart). Load-the-story (Gen 14 re-read): four kings from the east, Shinar among them, subdue the cities of the plain and carry off Lot; Abram defeats them; Melchizedek, priest of אֵל עֶלְיוֹן ("God Most High"), blesses Abram by the God "possessor of heaven and earth" (Gen 14:18–19). Daniel's book has already sent the temple vessels to "the land of Shinar" (1:2), and will later make "the Most High" its title for God (3:26; 4:17 [Heb 4:14]). *Name link: high (exclusive). Intended allusion: uncertain — consider verifying.* `[I]`
- **רַב־טַבָּחַיָּא ("chief of the executioners / bodyguard", 2:14).** Two Hebrew equivalents carry canonical freight: שַׂר הַטַּבָּחִים, the title of **Potiphar** (Gen 37:36; 39:1; 40:3, 4; 41:10, 12 — six verses), and רַב־טַבָּחִים, the title of **Nebuzaradan**, who burned the temple (2 Kgs 25:8–20; Jer 39–52 — 24 verses) (WLC). The Aramaic matches Nebuzaradan's *form* and Potiphar's *story*. *Moderate confidence* that either is in view; cross-language. `[I]`
- **"The second year" (2:1)** — see *Difficult / Contested Verses*.

### 8. Translations

Five divergences between the study text (NASB95) and the pulpit text (ESV) bear on findings, and in four of them **the ESV is closer to the Aramaic**:

| Verse | NASB95 | ESV | What it bears on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2:9 | "until the situation is changed" | "till the times change" | The Aramaic is עַד דִּי עִדָּנָא יִשְׁתַּנֵּא ("until the time changes"). The ESV keeps the echo with 2:21 ("He changes times"); the NASB95 hides it |
| 2:21 | "changes the times and the epochs" | "changes times and seasons" | Both keep "change"; the ESV's "times" matches its 2:9 |
| 2:28 | "the visions in your mind" | "the visions of your head" | The Aramaic is חֶזְוֵי רֵאשָׁךְ ("the visions of your head"); the king who sees visions "of his head" is told "you are the head of gold" (2:38). The ESV keeps the word, the NASB95 does not |
| 2:31–35 | "statue" | "image" | The same word צְלֵם is "image" in ch. 3 in *both* versions. **Only the ESV lets the hearer connect "the image" of ch. 2 with "the image" of ch. 3** |
| 2:43 | "they will combine with one another in the seed of men" | "they will mix with one another in marriage" | The Aramaic is בִּזְרַע אֲנָשָׁא ("in the seed of men"). Here the **NASB95** is literal and the ESV has made an interpretive decision (intermarriage between royal houses) on the congregation's behalf |

**Two further notes.** (a) The NASB95 prints editorial headings "Medo-Persia and Greece" above 2:39 and "Rome" above 2:40. They are not part of the text. If the preacher reads from the NASB95 or a study Bible with similar headings, he is reading an identification the text does not make (see Preaching Pitfalls). (b) 2:46: NASB95 "did homage", ESV "paid homage", for סְגִד, which both versions render "worship" eleven times in ch. 3. **Neither version lets the hearer hear that the king does to Daniel in 2:46 what he will demand for his image in ch. 3.** `[T]`

**KJV as historic witness** (from recall — the KJV is not in the corpus). KJV 2:46 "worshipped Daniel" keeps the verb; KJV 2:31 "a great image" keeps the noun. For this chapter the KJV's literalism carries both echoes the modern study text loses.

**Ancient versions check** (both Greek texts, from the Logos exports):

- **2:34 "a stone was cut out [from a mountain] without hands."** The Aramaic of 2:34 has no "from a mountain"; 2:45 does (מִטּוּרָא, "from the mountain"). **Both** the Old Greek and Theodotion read ἐξ ὄρους ("from a mountain") at 2:34, and the BHS apparatus suggests inserting it. **Triage: category 2 (substantive variant)** — but most simply explained as the Greek harmonising 2:34 to 2:45. *Position: the shorter Aramaic is likelier original; moderate confidence.* The effect for preaching is nil: the mountain-origin is in the text at 2:45 either way.
- **2:35 "became a great mountain and filled the whole earth."** Theodotion agrees with the Aramaic (ἐπλήρωσεν, "filled"); the **Old Greek reads ἐπάταξε πᾶσαν τὴν γῆν ("struck the whole earth")**. **Triage: category 2**, almost certainly the OG translator's assimilation to the stone that "struck" the image in the same verse (ὁ πατάξας). *Position: the Aramaic "filled"; high confidence.* The difference matters theologically — a kingdom that *fills* the earth is not the same picture as one that *strikes* it.
- **2:28–29, 45 "what will be in the latter days / after this."** Theodotion renders ἃ δεῖ γενέσθαι ("what must take place") three times; the Old Greek has the same phrase at 2:28–29 and τὰ ἐσόμενα ("the things to come") with ἐσήμανε ("he made known by sign") at 2:45. **Triage: category 3 (the NT's own text)** — Revelation's opening and closing formulae take up both Greek renderings (Tool 11). The Greek δεῖ ("must") adds a note of necessity the Aramaic "what will be" does not state, and it is the Greek the NT reads.
- **2:44 "it will crush and put an end to all these kingdoms."** Theodotion λεπτυνεῖ καὶ λικμήσει ("it will grind to powder and winnow"); the Old Greek πατάξει καὶ ἀφανίσει ("strike and destroy"). **Triage: category 3** — Luke 20:18 takes λικμήσει from Theodotion. The winnowing verb also catches up the chaff image of 2:35.
- **2:47.** The Old Greek adds μόνος ("alone") to "the revealer of hidden mysteries". A Greek plus, category 2, no Aramaic support; not preachable from the Hebrew-based text.
- **2:1 "the second year."** Both Greek texts read "second" (δευτέρῳ). The BHS apparatus's "twelfth" is a modern conjecture with no ancient witness.

**Pulpit divergence note:** The ESV is the pulpit text. On this chapter it serves the preacher well: it keeps "image" (2:31–35) as ch. 3 does, "the times change" (2:9) as at 2:21, and "the visions of your head" (2:28). It **closes one question the Aramaic leaves open** — "mix with one another in marriage" (2:43) — and, like the NASB95, it **levels סְגִד in 2:46 to "paid homage"**. The congregation will hear the chapter's internal echoes better than a NASB95 reader would, but will not hear the 2:46 → 3:5 link unless the preacher supplies it.

### 9. Tone and Feel

**Three registers, in sequence.** `[T]` for the devices; `[I]` for the effect.

1. **Dread with a streak of farce (2:1–13).** A sleepless king, a demand no one can meet, threats of dismemberment and houses made dunghills (2:5), and a court guild that answers the same thing twice (2:4, 7) while the king accuses it of stalling for time (2:8). The Chaldeans' final defence — "no great king or ruler has ever asked anything like this" (2:10) — is the protest of experts who have met a problem their expertise cannot touch. The satire is quieter than in ch. 3, but it is there: the whole "wisdom" of Babylon is reduced to begging for the answer to be given to it first.
2. **Hush and praise (2:17–23).** The pace drops. Companions, a request for רַחֲמִין ("compassion", 2:18), a night, a revelation, and then the only poetry in the chapter. Soundtrack: a single voice, then a choir.
3. **Majesty and finality (2:31–45).** The dream report is written for awe: a single great image, "large and of extraordinary splendour … and its appearance awesome" (2:31) — and then a stone, the image struck at its weakest point, all five materials pulverised "all at the same time", chaff on a summer threshing-floor, blown away "so that not a trace of them was found" (2:35). The cadence of the list of metals (2:35, 45) is a drum-roll of destruction. Soundtrack: full orchestra, ending in silence.

**For the preacher:** the chapter's emotional architecture is fear → prayer → wonder. A sermon that goes straight to the image and the four kingdoms skips the fear and the prayer, and the wonder will not land.

**Two findings from the table** `[T]`:
- **"Crush" is shared by the fourth kingdom and God's kingdom.** The fourth kingdom crushes (תַּדִּק, 2:40); God's kingdom crushes (תַּדִּק, 2:44) — the same form. The last human kingdom and the divine kingdom are described with the same verb; what distinguishes them is that one is crushed and the other endures (2:44). The verb returns for the fourth beast in ch. 7 (7:7, 19, 23, verified by lemma). *High confidence.*
- **"For ever" runs from the court's greeting to God's kingdom.** The Chaldeans' formula מַלְכָּא לְעָלְמִין חֱיִי ("O king, live for ever", 2:4) is the first Aramaic sentence of the book. By 2:44 the only thing in the chapter that is לְעָלְמִין ("for ever") is the kingdom God sets up. *High confidence on the words; `[I]` on the irony.*

**Near-repetition** `[T]`: the list of materials changes order. The dream gives the image top-down (gold, silver, bronze, iron, clay, 2:32–33); its destruction bottom-up (iron, clay, bronze, silver, gold, 2:35); the interpretation's recap mixes them (iron, bronze, clay, silver, gold, 2:45). The bottom-up order of 2:35 follows the stone's strike at the feet.

### 11. Quotation/Allusion

**The book's confirmed live sources before this passage** `[S: overview]`, with this chapter's additions in **bold**: Genesis (1:2 Shinar; 1:10 Gen 40:6) — **Gen 40–41 heavily here**; Jeremiah (named at 9:2; Jer 27:6 at 2:38); Isaiah and Deuteronomy (live in chs 8–12). Daniel 2 is in the Writings, and its canonical neighbours (Esther, Ezra–Nehemiah) are in the background of its court-and-decree diction (Tool 7, "the God of heaven").

**Independent candidate-finding.** Before the overview's candidates were checked, the chapter's own distinctive vocabulary was searched — in the Hebrew of 2:1–4a directly; in the Aramaic by reading the Hebrew passages that share its concepts, since the lemma index cannot cross the language boundary. That search found four contacts the overview's map did not list (Exod 7:11; Isa 41:15–16; Deut 29:28 [Eng 29:29]; Deut 10:17) and confirmed two it did (Gen 41; Jer 27).

---

**Genesis 40–41 → Daniel 2:1–3, 2:16–30, 2:46–49** *(high confidence — multiple rare contacts plus a shared plot)* `[T]` for the contacts; `[I]` for the design

*The contacts* (Hebrew contacts by lemma, WLC; the rest cross-language, by reading):
- **A king's spirit troubled by a dream**: פָּעַם ("to be troubled") + רוּחַ ("spirit") in one verse — **four verses in the Hebrew Bible**: Gen 41:8, Judg 13:25, Dan 2:1, 2:3. Judges 13:25 is the Spirit stirring Samson, a different sense, so the closest rival is none.
- **Magicians summoned to a king's dream**: חַרְטֹם ("magician") + חֲלוֹם ("dream") in one verse — **Gen 41:8 and Dan 2:2 only**.
- **"It is not in me"**: Joseph's בִּלְעָדָי ("it is not in me; God will give Pharaoh an answer", Gen 41:16) and Daniel's "not for any wisdom residing in me" (2:30).
- **"God has made known to Pharaoh what he is about to do"** (Gen 41:25, 28; and 41:39, "since God has made known (הוֹדִיעַ) all this to you") and "the great God has made known (הוֹדַע) to the king what will take place" (2:45; cf. 2:28–29). Same root in the two languages.
- **The exile found and brought in haste**: Pharaoh's servants "hurried him out of the dungeon" (Gen 41:14); Arioch "hurriedly brought Daniel" and announces "I have found a man" (2:25) — cf. Pharaoh's "can we find a man like this?" (Gen 41:38).
- **Promotion and robes**: "I have set you over all the land of Egypt" (Gen 41:41) // "he made him ruler over the whole province of Babylon" (2:48); the gold chain of Gen 41:42 reappears at 5:29 `[S: overview]`.

*Source context (Move 1).* Genesis 40–41 is the turn of the Joseph story from prison to palace. Two dreams of Pharaoh's officers (ch. 40), then two of Pharaoh (41:1–7); the magicians and wise men of Egypt cannot interpret (41:8); the cupbearer remembers the Hebrew in prison (41:9–13); Joseph is brought in, disclaims the power ("it is not in me"), and interprets: seven years of plenty, seven of famine, and the doubling of the dream means "the matter is determined by God, and God will quickly bring it about" (41:32). Pharaoh confesses that the spirit of God is in Joseph, and sets him over Egypt. The function of the episode in Genesis: God's purpose for Abraham's family (the preservation of life, 50:20) runs *through* a foreign court, by means of a foreign king's dream, interpreted by a disclaiming exile.

*Book usage (Move 2).* Daniel has already used Genesis twice: "the land of Shinar" (1:2, Gen 10:10; 11:2) and the "dejected" faces of Gen 40:6 at 1:10 (זֹעֲפִים, the only two places the word is used of faces) `[S: overview]`. The book moves *through* the Joseph story in order: the prison chapter (Gen 40) at Dan 1, the palace chapter (Gen 41) at Dan 2, and the robe and gold chain of Gen 41:42 at Dan 5:29. **Daniel is following Genesis 40–41's own sequence across its first five chapters.** `[I]`, *moderate–high*; a synthetic claim, every constituent verified, but the design is a proposal for the claim audit.

*OT-to-OT (Move 3).* The magician-contest has a second canonical instance, and Dan 2:2 echoes it more exactly than Genesis does: **חַרְטֹם ("magician") + מְכַשֵּׁף ("sorcerer") stand together in two verses only — Exod 7:11 and Dan 2:2** (WLC). Exodus 7–9 is the court contest in which Egypt's magicians match Moses and then fail ("this is the finger of God", 8:19 [Heb 8:15]). Genesis 41 and Exodus 7 are already in canonical conversation — both Egyptian courts, both with חַרְטֻמִּים, one ending in a Hebrew's elevation and the other in the confession of God's finger. Dan 2:2 draws on *both* lists.

*What it adds.* The reader of the Writings knows that God's purpose for his people has been carried before through a foreign court, by an exile who said "not I". Daniel 2 presents the exile in Babylon as the same kind of moment — and with the Exodus overtone, the court's magicians are not merely unable but *outmatched*. Daniel is not a hero of wisdom; he is the next Joseph, through whom the God who "made known" to Pharaoh now makes known to Nebuchadnezzar. **The preacher who misses Joseph will preach Daniel's competence; the preacher who hears Joseph will preach God's providence.**

**Jeremiah 27:5–7 → Daniel 2:37–39** *(moderate–high confidence — cross-language, conceptual and close)* `[I]`

*Source context (Move 1).* Jeremiah 27, in Zedekiah's reign, is the yoke oracle: Jeremiah wears a yoke and tells the kings of Edom, Moab, Ammon, Tyre and Sidon to submit to Babylon. The LORD's claim: "I have made the earth, the men and the beasts … and I give it to whom it seems right to me" (27:5); "now I have given all these lands into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, my servant, and I have given him also the wild animals of the field to serve him" (27:6); "all the nations shall serve him and his son and his grandson, **until the time of his own land comes**" (27:7). The function: Babylon's empire is a gift, and it has an expiry date.

*Book usage (Move 2).* Jeremiah is named at 9:2 (the seventy years); the overview links Jer 27:6 to 2:38 `[S: overview]`. This dig extends the link to 27:5 and 27:7: **all three verses are present in Daniel 2.** 27:5 ("I give it to whom it seems right to me") is the theology of 2:21 and of the book's refrain (4:17, 25, 32 [Heb 4:14, 22, 29]); 27:6 (the beasts of the field given into his hand) is 2:38 almost clause by clause; 27:7 ("until the time of his own land comes") is 2:39 ("after you there will arise another kingdom").

*OT-to-OT (Move 3).* Jeremiah 27 is itself a reworking of creation language (27:5, "I made the earth … by my great power and outstretched arm") and of Gen 1:28's dominion over the animals — the dominion God gave the first man is now, temporarily, given to a pagan king. Daniel's "the beasts of the field and the birds of the sky" (2:38) has Gen 1:26, 28 behind Jeremiah behind it. *Moderate confidence on the Gen 1 layer.* `[I]`

*What it adds.* Daniel's "you are the head of gold" is not flattery. It is Jeremiah's word to Judah's kings repeated to Babylon's: the kingdom is God's gift, the dominion is borrowed, and it ends when "the time of his own land comes". The exile hearing Daniel 2 hears Jeremiah confirmed from inside the empire's throne-room.

**Isaiah 41:15–16 → Daniel 2:35** *(moderate–high confidence — cross-language, four elements in sequence)* `[I]`

*The contact.* Isaiah 41:15–16: "I have made you a new, sharp threshing sledge … you will thresh the mountains and **crush** (תָּדֹק) them, and make the hills like **chaff** (מֹץ); you will winnow them, and the **wind** (רוּחַ) will **carry** them **away** (תִּשָּׂאֵם)". Daniel 2:35: the materials were **crushed** (דָּקוּ) and became like **chaff** (עוּר) of the summer threshing-floors, and the **wind** (רוּחָא) **carried** them **away** (נְשָׂא). Crush, chaff, wind, carry away — the same four actions, in the same order, in cognate words. *Rarity:* in the Hebrew Bible "crush" + "chaff" stand together in one verse only (Isa 41:15), and "chaff" + "wind" + "carry" within one verse of each other only at Isa 41:15–16 (WLC). The chaff-and-wind image alone is common (Ps 1:4; 35:5; Isa 17:13; Hos 13:3), so **the rival is the image's wider currency; the four-element sequence has no rival.**

*Source context (Move 1).* Isaiah 41 is the first trial-speech against the nations' gods (41:1–7, 21–29), set around "one from the east" whom God stirs up (41:2, 25 — commonly identified with Cyrus `[S]`, who is named at 44:28; 45:1). Between the trial scenes comes the oracle to "you worm Jacob" (41:14): Israel, helpless, is made by God into a threshing sledge that pulverises mountains. Immediately after (41:21–24): the idols are challenged to "declare the things that are to come" and cannot.

*Book usage (Move 2).* Isaiah is a live source in the visions (10:23, 25; 26:19; 53:11; 66:24) `[S: overview]`; this is the first contact in the tales. It stands in the chapter that *opens* the Aramaic, and the vision chapters that close the book also lean on Isaiah — the book's Isaiah use is front and back.

*OT-to-OT (Move 3).* Isaiah 41's neighbouring verses (41:21–24) are the challenge to the gods to "declare what is to come", which no god can meet — exactly the Chaldeans' failure (2:10–11) and Daniel's answer (2:28). The crush-and-chaff oracle and the "declare what is to come" trial-speech are already joined in Isaiah 41; Daniel 2 has both.

*What it adds.* In Isaiah the threshing is done *by Israel*, made strong by God, against the mountains; in Daniel the threshing is done by a stone "without hands", which itself *becomes* a mountain. Read with Isaiah behind it, the stone is not an abstract force but God's own instrument for his people against the empires — which the book will confirm when the kingdom is "given to the people of the saints of the Highest One" (7:27). `[I]`, *moderate*.

**Deuteronomy 29:29 [Heb 29:28] → Daniel 2:22, 28** *(moderate confidence — conceptual, cross-language; Move 1)*

"The **secret** things (הַנִּסְתָּרֹת) belong to the LORD our God, but the things **revealed** (הַנִּגְלֹת) belong to us and to our sons for ever" (Deut 29:29 [Heb 29:28]). Daniel 2:22: God "reveals (גָּלֵא) the profound and **hidden** things (מְסַתְּרָתָא)". The Hebrew pair סָתַר ("to hide") + גָּלָה ("to reveal") stands in five verses (Deut 29:28 [Eng 29:29]; 1 Sam 20:2; Isa 16:3; Ezek 39:23; Prov 27:5) (WLC). *Source context:* the end of the covenant renewal in Moab, after the curses; the secret things are God's, the revealed things are Israel's *to do* ("that we may observe all the words of this law"). The chapter around it is the one Daniel's prayer cites as "the curse … written in the law of Moses" (9:11 ← Deut 29:20–21 [Heb 29:19–20]) `[S: overview]`. *What it adds:* Deuteronomy keeps the hidden things with God; Daniel 2 has God disclose them — to a pagan king, through an exile who is living under the curse of Deut 29. *Moderate confidence.* `[I]`

**Deuteronomy 10:17 → Daniel 2:47** *(moderate–high confidence — cross-language, near-formula)*

"The LORD your God is **God of gods and Lord of lords**, the great, the mighty and the awesome God" (אֱלֹהֵי הָאֱלֹהִים וַאֲדֹנֵי הָאֲדֹנִים, Deut 10:17; cf. Ps 136:2–3). Nebuchadnezzar: אֱלָהּ אֱלָהִין וּמָרֵא מַלְכִין ("a God of gods and a Lord of kings", 2:47). *Source context:* Moses' summary of what the LORD requires (10:12–22), grounded in who he is — the God of gods, who "shows no partiality", "loves the stranger". *What it adds:* the pagan king's confession is the Torah's confession with one word changed — "Lord of *kings*" for "Lord of *lords*" — fitted to a chapter about kings. And the confession is incomplete: it names Daniel's God as supreme among gods, which is not yet the confession of the only God (cf. 3:29; 4:34–37). `[I]` *Moderate–high.*

**Isaiah 2:2 (// Micah 4:1) → Daniel 2:28, 35, 44** *(moderate confidence — conceptual; Move 1)*

"In the latter days (בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים) the mountain of the house of the LORD will be established as the chief of the mountains … and all the nations will stream to it" (Isa 2:2). Daniel 2: "what will take place in the latter days" (בְּאַחֲרִית יוֹמַיָּא, 2:28), a stone that "became a great mountain and filled the whole earth" (2:35), a kingdom set up by God (2:44). "In the latter days" is idiom (thirteen verses in the Hebrew Bible, WLC) and cannot carry the link alone; the combination of *latter days + a mountain + universal dominion* is what makes the echo. *Source context:* Isaiah's vision of Zion exalted, the nations coming to learn torah, swords beaten into ploughshares. *What it adds:* the stone-mountain is Zion's mountain — the kingdom God sets up is the reign that Isaiah saw from Zion. `[I]`, *moderate*.

**Psalm 2:9 → Daniel 2:40–43** *(moderate confidence — conceptual, cross-language; Move 1)*

"You shall **break** them with a rod of **iron**; you shall shatter them like a **potter's** vessel" (תְּרֹעֵם בְּשֵׁבֶט בַּרְזֶל כִּכְלִי יוֹצֵר, Ps 2:9). The Hebrew triple "break" + "iron" + "potter" stands in that verse alone (WLC). Daniel 2: the fourth kingdom is iron that **breaks** (מְרָעַע, 2:40, the cognate verb) and **potter's** clay (חֲסַף דִּי־פֶחָר, 2:41). *Source context:* the nations rage against the LORD and his anointed; God installs his king on Zion and gives him the nations as inheritance, to break with an iron rod. *What it adds — an inversion:* in Psalm 2 the Messiah wields the iron and the nations are the pottery; in Daniel 2 the empire *is* iron and pottery, and is broken by God's kingdom. If the allusion is intended, the kingdom of 2:44 is the anointed king's kingdom of Psalm 2. *Moderate confidence — the image-cluster is suggestive, the words are cross-language.* `[I]`

**New Testament use** *(category 3 — the NT's own text; Tool 8)*

- **Revelation 1:1; 4:1; 22:6 ← Theodotion 2:28–29, 45.** ἃ δεῖ γενέσθαι ("what must take place") opens Revelation (1:1, "which God gave him to show to his servants, what must soon take place; and he made it known by sign (ἐσήμανεν)" — with the Old Greek's verb at 2:45), introduces the throne vision ("I will show you what must take place after these things", 4:1 — Theodotion 2:29 has ἃ δεῖ γενέσθαι μετὰ ταῦτα, "what must take place after these things") and closes it ("these words are faithful and true … to show his servants what must soon take place", 22:6 — Theodotion 2:45: "what must take place after these things, and the dream is true, and its interpretation faithful"). The Greek of all three NT verses was checked in the SBLGNT, and of Daniel in the Theodotion and Old Greek exports. **Revelation frames its whole prophecy in the words of Daniel 2.** Revelation's one change is significant: Daniel's "after this" / "in the latter days" becomes "soon" (ἐν τάχει, 1:1; 22:6). `[T]` wording; `[I]` allusion; *high confidence*.
- **Luke 20:17–18 ← Ps 118:22 + Theodotion 2:44.** The rejected stone of Ps 118 is joined to the crushing stone of Daniel 2: "everyone who falls on that stone will be broken to pieces; but on whomever it falls, it will scatter him like dust (λικμήσει)". *High confidence.* (Matt 21:44 has the same saying; the verse is bracketed in some editions — a variant to note if Matthew is preached.)
- **1 Cor 2:7–10; Col 2:2–3 ← Theodotion 2:18–23** *(moderate confidence)*. Paul speaks of "God's wisdom in a mystery (μυστηρίῳ), the hidden (ἀποκεκρυμμένην) … which none of the rulers of this age understood … God revealed (ἀπεκάλυψεν) it to us through the Spirit … the depths (τὰ βάθη) of God" (1 Cor 2:7–10); and of "the mystery of God, Christ, in whom are hidden (ἀπόκρυφοι) all the treasures of wisdom (σοφίας) and knowledge" (Col 2:2–3). Theodotion's Daniel 2 has μυστήριον, σοφία, σύνεσις, ἀποκαλύπτει, βαθέα and ἀπόκρυφα in four verses (2:18–22). The vocabulary is shared; the scene — wisdom hidden from rulers, revealed by God — is Daniel 2's. *Moderate: the words are individually common enough in Paul that the cluster, not any one word, carries the case.*

**Move 4 — internal echo**

*Within the chapter* (all verified by lemma, WLC):
- **2:9 → 2:21** — *answers*. The Chaldeans stall "until the time changes" (עִדָּנָא יִשְׁתַּנֵּא); Daniel praises the God who "changes the times" (מְהַשְׁנֵא עִדָּנַיָּא). Only God changes the times. *High.*
- **2:10–11 → 2:28** — *answers*. "There is not (לָא אִיתַי) a man on earth … except gods whose dwelling is not with flesh" → "however, there is (אִיתַי) a God in heaven". *High.*
- **2:12 → 2:48** — *reverses*. "All the wise men of Babylon" condemned → set under Daniel. *High.*
- **2:28 → 2:38** — *replants*. "The visions of your head" (חֶזְוֵי רֵאשָׁךְ) → "you are the head (רֵאשָׁה) of gold". *Moderate (the word is common; the placement is pointed).*

*Answers to earlier material:*
- **1:2 → 2:37–38** — *answers*. "The Lord gave (נָתַן) Jehoiakim … into his hand" → "the God of heaven has given (יְהַב) you the kingdom … has given them into your hand". The Hebrew verb of the prologue becomes the Aramaic verb of the chapter; "into his hand" (בְּיָדוֹ) becomes "into your hand" (בִּידָךְ). *High.*
- **1:17, 20 → 2:19–30** — *fulfils*. Daniel "understood all kinds of visions and dreams" (1:17); the four were "ten times better than all the magicians and conjurers" (1:20) → the first dream, and the whole guild failed. *High.*
- **1:4 → 2:14** — *replants*. The youths chosen as מַשְׂכִּילִים ("showing intelligence", 1:4) → Daniel answers Arioch עֵטָא וּטְעֵם ("with discretion and discernment", 2:14). *Moderate.*

*Planted for later:*
- **2:31–35 → 3:1–18** — *planted*. The צְלֵם ("image") of the dream → the צְלֵם of gold the king makes and commands everyone to worship (12 occurrences in ch. 3). The king who is "the head of gold" makes an image that is all gold. *High on the words; `[I]` on the irony.*
- **2:46 → 3:5–15** — *planted*. The king falls (נְפַל) and does homage (סְגִד) to Daniel → the king commands everyone to fall (נְפַל) and worship (סְגִד) the image; the pair stands at 3:5, 6, 7, 10, 11, 15. The man who prostrated himself in ch. 2 makes prostration the law in ch. 3. *High.*
- **2:21 → 7:25** — *planted*. God "changes times" (2:21) → the horn "will intend to change times and law" (7:25) `[S: overview]`, verified. *High.*
- **2:40 → 7:7, 19, 23** — *planted*. The fourth kingdom "crushes" (דְּקַק) → the fourth beast "crushes" (7:7, 19, 23). *High; the strongest single verbal support for the 2 // 7 pairing.*
- **2:44 → 4:3, 34; 6:26; 7:14, 27 [Heb 3:33; 4:31; 6:27]** — *planted*. "A kingdom which will never be destroyed (לָא תִתְחַבַּל)" → the confessions of two kings and the gift to the son of man and the saints `[S: overview]`, verified. *High.*
- **2:28 → 10:14** — *planted*. "In the latter days" → the last vision `[S: overview]`. *High on the words; the phrase is idiom (Tool 11, Isa 2:2).*
- **2:34, 45 → 8:25** — *planted*. "Without hands" → the last king "broken without hand" (בְּאֶפֶס יָד) `[S: overview]`. *High, cross-language.*

*Opening material checked:* yes — all of ch. 1 was held against the chapter. *Addressee-differentiation:* the book opens naming four youths and a guild of magicians and conjurers (1:6, 20); ch. 2 differentiates them — Daniel receives the revelation, the three pray (2:17–18) and are promoted with him (2:49), and the guild is saved by him.

### 12. Genre

**Court narrative with an embedded dream-report, an embedded hymn, and an embedded interpretation.** `[T]` The host genre is narrative (scene, speech, action, narrator); 2:20–23 is poetry; 2:31–45 is symbolic dream with authoritative interpretation — the first appearance in the book of the mode that dominates chs 7–12. The overview's book-level structure (tales 1–6, visions 7–12) can obscure this: **Daniel 2 is a vision inside a tale.** The reading rules of both apply. As narrative, it happened, and its characters are not all models (Tool 13). As symbolic vision, the image is "vision not video": the metals are kingdoms, not a forecast of metallurgy, and the text supplies its own key for some symbols (the head = Nebuchadnezzar, 2:38; the stone = a God-given kingdom, 2:44–45) and *not* for others (the second, third and fourth kingdoms; the toes; "the seed of men", 2:43). **Where the text supplies a key, preach it with confidence; where it does not, hold the identification as interpretation.**

### 13. Copycat

| Character / action | Label | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Nebuchadnezzar's demand and decree (2:5, 12) | Negative | Tyranny; the narrator does not endorse it |
| The Chaldeans' stalling (2:4–11) | Descriptive / negative | Honest about their limits (2:10–11) but no answer |
| Daniel's "discretion and discernment" with Arioch (2:14) | Normative pattern | Commended by the narrator's own words עֵטָא וּטְעֵם ("counsel and discretion") — a man under sentence who speaks wisely to the executioner |
| Daniel's request for time (2:16) | Descriptive | Not a method for guidance; a bold step taken before he had an answer |
| Daniel seeks prayer from his companions (2:17–18) | **Normative pattern** | Prayer for "compassion" (רַחֲמִין) in a crisis is the canon's normal pattern (Neh 1; Ezra 9; Dan 9) |
| The revelation in a night vision (2:19) | **Unique event** | Not a promise that God will reveal dreams on request |
| Daniel's praise *before* he acts (2:19–23) | Normative pattern | He blesses God before he goes to the king |
| Daniel's "not by any wisdom in me" (2:30) | Normative pattern, with Christological weight | Cf. Joseph (Gen 41:16); see Tool 15 |
| Daniel's intercession for the condemned wise men (2:24) | Normative pattern | "Do not destroy the wise men of Babylon" — he saves the pagan guild that could not save itself |
| Nebuchadnezzar's homage and offering (2:46) | Descriptive / contested | See *Difficult / Contested Verses* |
| Daniel's request for his friends (2:49) | Descriptive | Loyalty; sets up ch. 3 |

**The common copycat error** is to take 2:19 as a pattern ("pray, and God will show you the answer"). The narrative presents it as a unique act of revelation, not a technique. The prayer of 2:17–18 *is* a pattern; the revelation is a gift.

### 14. Bible Timeline

**Narrative time** `[T]` / `[S]`: "the second year of the reign of Nebuchadnezzar" (2:1) — early in the exile, within the reign of the king who would destroy Jerusalem (the destruction itself is not narrated in Daniel). The temple still stands in Jerusalem when this dream is told (1:2 has only "some of the vessels" taken), as far as the book's own chronology runs `[I]`.

**Canonical time.** Ketuvim, after Esther, before Ezra–Nehemiah. The reader of the Writings already knows what Daniel 2 forecasts for the first kingdom: Babylon falls (Dan 5), and the "first year of Cyrus" (Dan 1:21; Ezra 1:1; 2 Chr 36:22) opens the return. That reader is living *inside* the series of kingdoms the dream describes — after the head of gold, before the stone. **The canonical reader is a reader in the middle of the image.** `[I]`

**Where we are.** On the far side of the incarnation, death, resurrection and ascension of the one who said "the kingdom of God has come near" (Mark 1:15) and who identified himself with the stone (Luke 20:17–18). The stone has been cut out; the mountain has not yet filled the earth. Daniel 2:44 places God's kingdom "in the days of those kings" — that is, *during*, not after, the human kingdoms — which is the shape of the NT's already-and-not-yet. `[I]`

**Timeline-driven misreading to avoid.** Reading 2:41–43 as a forecast of modern political alliances (the European Union, a "revived Roman Empire"). The text locates the fourth kingdom in a series that begins with Nebuchadnezzar and is ended by the kingdom God sets up "in the days of those kings"; it does not leap across millennia, and the NT places the decisive strike of the stone in the coming of Christ.

**Canonical and compositional order.** The book presents the dream as given in Nebuchadnezzar's second year; most critical scholarship dates the book's final form to the second century BC `[S]`. On the first view the image is prediction; on the second, history up to the writer's day with the stone still future. **The text claims the first and does not argue for it**; the date question is reported, not resolved, here (see the overview's Presenting Situation).

### 15. Who Am I?

| Figure | Function |
|---|---|
| **Daniel** | Primarily a **type**: the righteous exile who mediates God's revelation to a pagan king and saves those condemned with him — in the pattern of Joseph, and pointing beyond both to Christ (see Christological Reading). Secondarily an **example** of prayer, praise, and humility before God (2:17–23, 30), which the NT commends in kind (Heb 11:33 lists the faithful who "shut the mouths of lions", 6:22) |
| **Hananiah, Mishael, Azariah** | **Examples** of the praying community (2:17–18). The reader belongs here — among those who ask "the God of heaven" for compassion in a crisis they cannot solve |
| **The wise men of Babylon** | **Representative of humanity**: the best of human knowledge, confessing its limit (2:10–11), condemned, and saved by another's intercession (2:24). The congregation is closer to them than to Daniel |
| **Nebuchadnezzar** | **Foil and recipient**: the most powerful man on earth, told his power is borrowed (2:37), who confesses more than he understands (2:47). The unbeliever in the congregation hears his own position here |

**The "Daniel-is-me" error** is the standard one: "be a Daniel in your workplace — and God will promote you". The chapter's emphasis falls elsewhere: Daniel disclaims his own wisdom (2:30), and the hero of the chapter is the God who reveals (2:28, 47). The congregation is more often the wise men who cannot know and who live because another prayed and spoke for them.

### 16. So What?

**Author's intended response (Stage 1).** To trust the God of heaven as the One who gives and ends every human kingdom, and so to live faithfully inside the present kingdom — serving it, praying in it, speaking truthfully to its powers — without either despairing of it or worshipping it; and to receive knowledge of God's purposes as revelation, with praise and humility, not as human achievement. `[I]`

**Worldview (Stage 2).**
- *History is a series God gives and ends.* The metals fall in value from gold to clay even as the fourth kingdom grows in strength (2:39–40); every one of them, the gold included, ends as "chaff" (2:35). The stone's kingdom alone is "for ever" (2:44).
- *Ultimate reality is known only by revelation.* The world's expertise is real but cannot reach the deep things (2:10–11, 27). This is not anti-intellectualism — Daniel is the most educated man in the chapter (1:4, 17) — but a statement of where knowledge of God's purposes comes from.
- *God's kingdom is already at work "in the days of those kings"* (2:44), not only after them.

**Behaviour.**
- *Stop:* treating political power as ultimate — fearing it as if it could not end, or courting it as if it could save; treating knowledge of God's purposes as a matter of technique or expertise; presenting spiritual insight as one's own.
- *Start:* praying together in a crisis before acting (2:17–18); praising God *before* the problem is solved in public (2:19–23); speaking wisely and courageously to those in power (2:14, 24, 36–45); interceding for those who are not God's people but share our danger (2:24).

**Motivation (gospel ground).** Not "be wise like Daniel", but: the stone has been cut out without hands; the kingdom has been set up in Christ "in the days of those kings"; the revealer of mysteries has made known the mystery of God, which is Christ (Col 2:2). The church lives inside the image's last days in the confidence that the outcome has been told in advance — "the dream is true and its interpretation trustworthy" (2:45).

**Four audiences.**
- *For me:* Which human power or system am I treating as permanent, whether by fear or by hope? Do I praise God for what he reveals, or treat it as my own insight?
- *For a Christian friend:* For a believer working inside a powerful secular institution — Daniel 2 is the picture of faithful service, prayerful dependence and truthful speech there.
- *For the church community:* The church is the praying company of 2:17–18 — a small group of exiles asking "the God of heaven" for compassion — and the bearer of a word to the powers (2:36–45).
- *For an unbeliever:* Nebuchadnezzar's position: the most capable person in the room, confronted with a word he could not have found for himself, and confessing more than he yet understands (2:47). The question the chapter puts is whether the "God of gods and Lord of kings" will be worshipped, or only admired.

**Prayer.** Blessed be your name, God of heaven, for ever and ever; wisdom and power are yours. You change times and seasons; you remove kings and set up kings. Give us wisdom we do not have, show us what we cannot find, and keep us faithful in the days of these kingdoms until your kingdom fills the earth.

**The switch of language (2:4).** The Hebrew narrative ends on the word אֲרָמִית ("in Aramaic"), and the first Aramaic words are the court's greeting מַלְכָּא לְעָלְמִין חֱיִי ("O king, live for ever!"). The book changes language at the moment the empire speaks. Of Daniel 2's 49 verses, 2:1–3 and the first five words of 2:4 are Hebrew; the rest is Aramaic (WLC morphology). `[T]` *High confidence.* The Hebrew/Aramaic boundary is one of the book's two great structural seams `[S: overview]`; here it is also a dramatic device: the reader hears the empire in its own tongue.

**Sound-play in 2:1** `[T]` / `[I]`: וּבִשְׁנַת שְׁתַּיִם … וּשְׁנָתוֹ נִהְיְתָה עָלָיו ("in the second *year* … and his *sleep* left him", *bišnat … ûšnātô*). "Year" (שָׁנָה) and "sleep" (שֵׁנָה) are different words that sound alike; the verse opens and closes on them. *Moderate confidence that the play is intended* — it is the kind of thing a Hebrew narrator does, but it carries no argument.

**נִהְיְתָה (2:1), "his sleep left him / was gone"** `[T]`: the niphal of הָיָה in an unusual sense. The BHS apparatus compares 8:27, where Daniel says נִהְיֵיתִי ("I was exhausted") after his own vision. *Internal: the king's sleepless night (2:1) and Daniel's collapse (8:27) are the two reactions in the book to a revelation the recipient cannot bear — moderate confidence.*

### Textual Variants

Category 2 only (substantive variants). See also Tool 8.

- **2:4 אֲרָמִית ("in Aramaic").** The BHS apparatus marks the word "probably added" — a scribal notice of the change of language rather than part of the narrative. No ancient version omits it as far as the exports show (Theodotion and the Old Greek both have Συριστί, "in Syriac/Aramaic") `[T]` for the Greek. *Position: the word stands in every witness consulted; whether it began as a marginal note is a conjecture. Moderate confidence that it is original to the text as transmitted; it does not affect preaching.*
- **2:34 "from a mountain".** Present in both Greek texts, absent from the Aramaic; BHS suggests inserting it. *Position: a harmonisation to 2:45 in the Greek; the Aramaic is likelier original. Moderate confidence.*
- **2:35 "filled" (Aramaic, Theodotion) / "struck" (Old Greek).** *Position: "filled"; high confidence.*
- **2:40, 41, 42.** The BHS apparatus notes words in 2:40 absent from the Old Greek, Theodotion and the Vulgate, part of 2:41 absent from the Old Greek, and judges 2:42 "probably added". These are editorial judgements about a repetitive passage; no theological point in this report turns on them. `[T]` for the apparatus notes; *uncertain — consider verifying* in a commentary that works the Aramaic.

**Apparatus features (gate item e4).** The paragraph markers cited in Tool 3 (פ at 2:13, 16, 28, 45, 49; ס at 2:24) and the ketiv/qere at 2:9 are Leningrad's, as the WLC and BHS print them; their spread across other manuscripts has not been checked `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`. No finding in this report rests on them alone.

### Historical and Cultural Background

**Setting.**
- **Time period:** the court of Nebuchadnezzar II (r. 605–562 BC), early in the reign as the text dates it `[S]` for the regnal dates.
- **Location:** Babylon, the royal court.
- **Political context:** Judah a vassal, its first deportees (including the royal youths of ch. 1) at court; Jerusalem not yet destroyed on the book's own chronology `[I]`.

**What the original audience knew.**
1. **Dream interpretation was a professional discipline in Mesopotamia**, with written omen-handbooks and a priestly-scholarly class trained to use them `[S]`. The king's demand to be told the dream itself (2:5, 9) removes the handbooks: the experts can interpret a reported dream, not discover an unreported one. The demand is a test of whether the experts have access to the gods or only to their manuals — which is exactly what the Chaldeans concede (2:10–11). `[I]`
2. **"Chaldeans"** (כַּשְׂדִּים) is both an ethnic term (the dynasty and people of Babylon, 5:30) and, in Daniel, the name of a class of court scholars (2:2, 4, 5, 10) `[T]` for both uses in the book; `[S]` for the historical background of the second.
3. **Composite statues and the ages of metals.** A sequence of world-ages in descending metals is known from Greek literature (Hesiod's *Works and Days*) and from Persian traditions `[S]`. The original audience may have recognised the form; Daniel's use of it differs in ending not with a cycle but with a stone and a mountain `[I]`.
4. **"O king, live for ever"** was a standard court address; the Hebrew Bible has it of David (1 Kgs 1:31) and on Nehemiah's lips to Artaxerxes (Neh 2:3, הַמֶּלֶךְ לְעוֹלָם יִחְיֶה) `[T]` for the Hebrew texts.
5. **"The God of heaven"** was a title intelligible across the Persian-period empire — it is the title of the Persian decrees in Ezra (Tool 7) `[T]` for the distribution; `[S]` for its wider use.
6. **Dismemberment and houses made a dunghill** (2:5; cf. 3:29; Ezra 6:11) were attested royal punishments `[S]`; the threat is not hyperbole.

**What this changes about how we read.** The king's demand is not madness but a test of the experts' claim to divine access; and their failure is a confession that the empire's religion cannot reach the God who rules it. Daniel's answer is therefore not a better technique but a different source: "there is a God in heaven who reveals mysteries" (2:28).

### Original Audience Reception

**Canonical audience.**
- **Canonical section:** Ketuvim. The reader arrives knowing the Torah and the Prophets — Joseph in Pharaoh's court, Moses before Egypt's magicians, Jeremiah's yoke and seventy years, Isaiah's trial of the gods.
- **What the canonical reader knows:** that Babylon fell (Dan 5; Isa 47; Jer 50–51), that Cyrus allowed a return (Ezra 1), and that no Davidic king reigns. The reader stands *after the head of gold and before the stone*.
- **Canonical register:** Esther before, Ezra–Nehemiah after — Jews in foreign courts, Persian decrees, prayer in crisis.

**The first hearers.**
- **Who they were:** Jews living under foreign empire, whether in Babylon, Persia, or a later Hellenistic kingdom `[I]` (the date of composition is contested, `[S]`).
- **Their situation:** a small people, without a king, ruled by the latest of a series of empires, many of whose members served those empires in office.
- **What they brought:** Jeremiah's word that the exile was God's doing and would end; the question whether God still ruled history now that his house and king were gone.

**Where the passage fits their world.** It answered their deepest question before they asked it: *the God of Israel is the God of heaven, and he is not absent from the empire — he is the one who gave it, and he has already told its king how it ends.*

**Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances.**
- **Shocking:** a pagan king falling on his face before a Jewish exile and ordering sacrifice to him (2:46).
- **Surprising:** that the revelation is given to the pagan king *first*, in his own dream, and only interpreted by the Jew. God speaks to the empire directly.
- **Comforting:** every empire, including the one over them, is temporary and in God's hand; the God-given kingdom will "never be destroyed" and "will not be left for another people" (2:44).
- **Disturbing:** the kingdoms *succeed one another* — the end is not soon on the dream's own terms (four kingdoms, the fourth long and divided). Patience is required.

**What we bring that they didn't.** Modern confidence in democratic institutions as self-correcting and permanent; a reflex to read prophecy as a coded forecast of our own century; and a privatised faith that would not think to speak to a head of state about his kingdom.

### Biblical-Theological Themes

**1. The kingdom of God.** Daniel 2:44 is the Old Testament's most explicit statement that God will "set up a kingdom which will never be destroyed" and which replaces all others. It draws on God's rule over creation (Jer 27:5; Gen 1:28 behind 2:38), on Zion's mountain (Isa 2:2), and on the anointed king of Psalm 2 (moderate); it is taken up in Dan 7:14, 27, and in Jesus' announcement of the kingdom (Mark 1:15) and his self-identification with the stone (Luke 20:17–18). Its NT shape is the shape of 2:44: set up "in the days of those kings" — present within history, consummated beyond it (Rev 11:15). `[I]`

**2. Revelation and wisdom.** The God who "reveals mysteries" (2:28, 47) and gives wisdom (2:21, 23) stands against the wisdom of the nations, which cannot reach him (2:10–11, 27). The theme runs from Joseph (Gen 41:16) and the magicians of Egypt (Exod 8:19 [Heb 8:15]) through Isaiah's trial of the gods (Isa 41:21–24) to Paul's "God's wisdom in a mystery … which none of the rulers of this age understood" (1 Cor 2:7–8) and Christ "in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (Col 2:3). `[I]`

**3. The nations and their idols.** The image of ch. 2 becomes the idol of ch. 3; the human king's dominion is a borrowed version of the dominion given to humanity as God's image (Gen 1:26–28) — a theme the book develops in the king made beast-like and restored (4:16, 34) and in the beasts and the son of man (7:4, 13–14) `[S: overview]`. *Moderate confidence on the image-of-God thread.*

### Christological Reading

**Type of connection:** **direct prophecy** (the kingdom of 2:44, which the NT treats as fulfilled in Christ); **typology** (Daniel as revealer-mediator in the Joseph pattern); **trajectory** (revelation and wisdom).

**How the passage points to Christ.**
- **The stone and the kingdom.** The NT authorises the link explicitly: Luke 20:17–18 joins the rejected cornerstone (Ps 118:22) to the stone that crushes, in Theodotion's wording. The kingdom that "will never be destroyed" and fills the earth is the kingdom Jesus announces and embodies. *Prophecy; NT-precedent test passed. High confidence.*
- **"Without hands."** The stone is not of human making (2:34, 45). The kingdom is God's act, not the climax of human politics. NT resonances: the temple "not made with hands" (Mark 14:58; Heb 9:11, 24) — a related idea in different words `[I]`, *moderate*.
- **Daniel as mediator of revelation** (typology). Daniel stands between God and the king, receives the mystery, disclaims his own wisdom, and by his word saves those under sentence of death (2:24). Four tests: *theological category* — prophet / mediator of revelation: passes; *NT precedent* — the NT makes the same kind of connection with Joseph-like figures and calls Christ the revealer of the mystery (Col 2:2; Rom 16:25–26): passes in kind, not explicitly; *escalation* — Christ does not merely receive and pass on the mystery, he *is* the mystery revealed (Col 2:2), and he saves the condemned not by interpreting but by dying: passes; *authorial pattern* — the text draws the Joseph pattern deliberately (Tool 11): passes. *Typology passes three to four tests; moderate–high.*
- **The revealer of mysteries.** Revelation frames its whole prophecy in Daniel 2's words ("what must take place", Rev 1:1; 4:1; 22:6), and gives the revelation to Jesus Christ to show to his servants (Rev 1:1). The God who "made known to the king what will take place" (2:45) now makes it known through his Son. *Trajectory; high confidence on the wording.*

**Trajectory.**
- **What the passage anticipates:** a kingdom God himself sets up, which ends every human kingdom and fills the earth.
- **What Christ fulfils:** the kingdom inaugurated in his coming, death and resurrection — "set up in the days of those kings", within Roman rule (Mark 1:15; Luke 20:18).
- **What remains:** the stone has struck; the mountain has not yet filled the earth (Rev 11:15). Already and not yet.

**Moralism check.**
- **The "be like X" temptation:** "Be a Daniel at work: pray hard, stay humble, and God will promote you."
- **The gospel grounding:** the chapter's hero is the God who reveals (2:28, 47), and Daniel says so (2:30). The comfort of the chapter is not Daniel's success but God's kingdom.
- **Christ as hero, not just example:** Christ is the stone that ends the kingdoms, and the one in whom the mystery is revealed. Daniel's prayer and humility follow from that, as the believer's do.

**Confidence:** prophecy (2:44 → the kingdom in Christ): *high*; Revelation's framing: *high*; Daniel as type: *moderate–high*; "without hands" → "not made with hands": *moderate*.

### Difficult / Contested Verses

**2:1 — "the second year" and the three years of training (1:5, 18).** *Issue:* on the face of it, Daniel and his friends were still in training in Nebuchadnezzar's second year. *Options* `[S]`: (a) Babylonian accession-year reckoning makes "three years" span parts of three calendar years, so that training ended in the king's second year; (b) the friends were already counted among the wise men during training (2:13 says they were sought out to be killed with them, not that they had been consulted); (c) a textual error ("twelfth" — the BHS conjecture, with no ancient support; Tool 8). *Recommendation:* name the issue only if asked; option (a) is widely held and reasonable. *Moderate confidence on (a).*

**2:39–43 — the identity of the kingdoms.** *Issue:* the text names only Babylon (2:38). *Options* `[S]`: (a) Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, Rome — the traditional Jewish and Christian identification, which puts the stone's strike in the Roman period and makes Christ's coming its natural fulfilment; (b) Babylon, Media, Persia, Greece — widely held in critical scholarship, which reads the divided fourth kingdom as the Ptolemaic and Seleucid kingdoms and the "mixing in the seed of men" (2:43) as their dynastic marriages. *Data the book supplies:* ch. 8 treats Media and Persia as a single ram with two horns (8:20) `[T]`, which counts against (b)'s separate Median kingdom; "Darius the Mede" (5:31 [Heb 6:1]) and the "law of the Medes and Persians" (6:8) can be read either way `[I]`. *Recommendation:* preach what the text names and what all readings agree it does — one God-given kingdom ending all others; give the identification as interpretation. **Do not let the NASB95's headings decide it.**

**2:46 — the king's homage and offering to Daniel.** *Issue:* Nebuchadnezzar "fell on his face and did homage (סְגִד) to Daniel, and gave orders to present to him an offering and fragrant incense" — temple vocabulary (Tool 7) — and Daniel is not recorded as refusing. Contrast Peter (Acts 10:25–26) and Paul and Barnabas (Acts 14:13–15). *Options* `[S]` / `[I]`: (a) the king honours Daniel as the representative of his God, and 2:47 makes clear that the confession is directed to "your God"; (b) the narrator records the king's pagan reaction without endorsing it, and Daniel's silence is not consent (the narrative frequently leaves character responses unrecorded); (c) the scene is deliberately troubling, planting the question of worship that ch. 3 will force. *Recommendation:* (a) and (c) together fit the chapter's flow: the king's first response to revelation is to worship the human messenger, and the next chapter shows where that instinct leads. *Moderate confidence.*

**2:30, 47 — a pagan's confession.** Nebuchadnezzar's "your God is a God of gods" (2:47) is not conversion: in the next chapter he makes an idol. Preachers should not present 2:47 as the king's faith. `[T]` for the sequence.

**Pastoral landmine.** The chapter's promise is about God's kingdom, not about the believer's safety or promotion. Daniel and his friends were promoted (2:48–49); in the visions the wise "fall by sword and flame" (11:33) `[S: overview]`. Do not preach 2:48 as the normal outcome of faithfulness.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: The prophecy chart

- **What it looks like:** a sermon built on a diagram of four empires, with Rome's "iron and clay" mapped onto modern politics and the stone's strike predicted for our century.
- **Why it's wrong:** the text names only Babylon (2:38); it locates God's kingdom "in the days of those kings" (2:44); and the NT places the stone's strike in Christ (Luke 20:18). Identifications that the text does not make — including the NASB95's printed headings "Medo-Persia and Greece" and "Rome" — are interpretation.
- **The corrective:** preach what is keyed (Babylon; the stone = the God-given kingdom) with confidence, and hold the rest as interpretation. Put the weight where the text puts it: one kingdom that ends all the others.

### Pitfall: "Dare to be a Daniel" — the competence sermon

- **What it looks like:** "Daniel was wise, prayerful and brave; be like him at work, and God will promote you."
- **Why it's wrong:** Daniel's own words deny it (2:30), and the narrator gives the revelation to God (2:19) before Daniel speaks. The Joseph pattern (Tool 11) makes the same point: "it is not in me".
- **The corrective:** preach the God who reveals mysteries; let Daniel's prayer and humility follow as the response of one who has received.

### Pitfall: Starting at 2:31

- **What it looks like:** a sermon on "the statue and the stone" that begins with the dream report.
- **Why it's wrong:** the chapter spends its first thirty verses on the impossibility of knowing and on prayer and praise; the doxology (2:20–23) is the key to the dream (Headline Finding 2). Without 2:1–30 the dream is a puzzle; with it, it is an answer.
- **The corrective:** let the congregation feel the threat of 2:12–13 and hear the hymn of 2:20–23 before the image appears.

### Pitfall: Presenting 2:46–47 as the king's conversion

- **What it looks like:** "Nebuchadnezzar fell down and confessed Daniel's God — the power of a faithful witness."
- **Why it's wrong:** he worships the messenger (2:46) and calls Daniel's God supreme among gods (2:47); in the next chapter he makes an idol of gold. The chapter plants ch. 3.
- **The corrective:** read 2:46–47 as an incomplete response to revelation — real, but not yet faith — and let ch. 3 show where it goes.

### Pitfall: Missing the image

- **What it looks like:** a series that treats chs 2 and 3 as unrelated stories.
- **Why it's wrong:** the same word צְלֵם ("image") and the same verbs "fall" and "worship" join them (Tool 11 Move 4). The pulpit text helps: the ESV says "image" in both chapters. The NASB95 says "statue" in ch. 2.
- **The corrective:** preach ch. 3 with ch. 2 in view: the head of gold makes an image all of gold.
