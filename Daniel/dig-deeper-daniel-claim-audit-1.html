Claim Audit 1: Daniel

Passages audited: Daniel 1:1–2, 9–20; 2:1–49; 3–7 (the Aramaic arrangement); 8:17–26; 9:4–5, 27; 10:5–6, 14; 11:27–37; 12:2–7, together with the claims of the book overview about the shape of the whole book and its place in the canon. Date: 1 November 2026 Version: 1.0 Purpose: To test, before the overview is finalised and before any further dig, the intertextual claims and design claims made by the Daniel book overview (v0.1.0) and by the Daniel 2 dig. There are twenty-two claims: sixteen single contacts and six claims about design. Primary texts: WLC Hebrew and Aramaic (counted by lemma unless stated); Swete’s LXX for observing the Old Greek and Theodotion; the Rahlfs exports cited where a finding rests on Greek wording; SBLGNT for the New Testament. Every count below names its edition. Study text: NASB95. Pulpit text: ESV.

What this audit is

How it was run. Two blind auditors worked from the primary texts alone. Each had a brief, a list of claims, and the corpus. Neither saw the overview, the dig, any commentary or the other auditor’s report.

  • Auditor A tested the sixteen single-contact claims (A1–A16): one source text behind one place in Daniel.
  • Auditor B tested the six design claims (B1–B6): patterns across the book, and its place in the canon. Each claim had to face a chance baseline.

Each auditor was told to try to break each claim, not to defend it. Anything an auditor drew from memory of scholarship is tagged [S] and carries no verdict on its own.

Reproduction. After the reports came back, the decisive results were re-run in the main session against the same corpus. Every one tested came out as the auditor reported:

  • Auditor A: the results for A1, A4, A5, A6, A7, A9, A10, A11, A12, A13, A14 and A15.
  • Auditor B: the Joseph contacts at 1:9 and 2:1–3 and the “bank of the Nile” at 12:5; the “year one of [king]” idiom; the seven-word match between Dan 9:4 and Neh 1:5; the deliverance words shared by chapters 3 and 6; “change” with both time-words at 2:21 and 7:25; and the late books in Jonah’s density scan.

The doxology baseline (B6) was re-run independently with a slightly different list of words set aside. It gave the same direction as Auditor B: the hymn’s words recur elsewhere in chapter 2 at 62 per cent, against a mean of 69 per cent for the chapter’s four-verse stretches, and 39 of the 46 stretches equal or exceed it.

Not re-run. Three of Auditor B’s statistical tests were accepted without being re-run, as in earlier audits:

  • the concentric-pairing baseline across 745 six-chapter blocks;
  • the permutation test across 924 ways of splitting the book;
  • the rank-order shuffle test for the Joseph sequence.

They are reported as Auditor B’s.

What is not decided here. The audit tests whether a contact is in the text, how rare it is, and whether a rival explains it as well. It cannot settle the direction of dependence between late books, or the date of Daniel. Where a verdict touches either, it says so.

Method findings

These findings apply to more than one claim.

1. Rare pairs are cheap in Daniel. Auditor A measured how often a pair of content words occurs together in one verse of Hebrew Daniel and in exactly one other verse of the Bible (WLC). The average Hebrew verse of Daniel has about three such “exclusive pairs” (mean 3.0, median 2), and 78 per cent of verses have at least one. A single “only two verses” pair is therefore weak evidence on its own. It counts only when it is:

  • a genuinely rare word;
  • part of a cluster in one passage;
  • a repeated phrase or clause; or
  • supported by context and by early reception.

Words found in only two verses of the whole Bible, one of them in Hebrew Daniel, are genuinely scarce. There are seven:

  • Dan 10:4 and Gen 2:14, “Hiddekel”;
  • Dan 10:5 and Jer 10:9, “Uphaz”;
  • Dan 10:6 and Ezek 1:7, “burnished”;
  • Dan 10:17 and 1 Chr 13:12, “how”;
  • Dan 11:24 and Ps 68:31, “scatter”;
  • Dan 12:2 and Isa 66:24, “abhorrence”;
  • Dan 12:3 and Ezek 8:2, “brightness”.

2. Register inflates the late books. Set against each book’s size, the partner books of Daniel’s exclusive pairs come out as follows (WLC):

Partner book Times the chance expectation Why
Esther 7.5 Register
2 Chronicles 2.0 Register
Isaiah 1.7 Literary use
Ezekiel 1.7 Literary use
Nehemiah 1.6 Register
Jeremiah 1.2 —
Genesis 0.8 (under-represented) —
Psalms 0.8 (under-represented) —

The surplus in Esther and Chronicles comes from the shared court and archive vocabulary of late biblical Hebrew. The surplus in Isaiah and Ezekiel is the real literary signal. Any claim resting on a contact with Esther, Ezra–Nehemiah or Chronicles has to clear the register explanation first.

3. The lemma index sometimes splits one word into two entries. At Ezek 9:11 the participle “clothed” is filed under a different entry from the other forms of לָבַשׁ (“to clothe”), so a lemma search finds 12 verses with “clothed” and “linen” where a reading finds 13. The name Arioch is also split into two entries, one for each book. A lemma count is a floor until the hits have been read.

4. Lemma searches on common words give false positives. A search for “God” followed by “God” returns 16 verses, almost all of them “God, my God” or “the God of …”. The exact form אֱלֹהֵי הָאֱלֹהִים (“God of gods”) occurs only twice. Where a claim rests on a common word, the surface form must be read.

5. Contacts across the two languages are tested only by reading. The Aramaic words have their own lemma entries. No search can link an Aramaic word in Daniel to its Hebrew cognate elsewhere. Every Hebrew–Aramaic contact below (A9 (d), A13, A14, A15, B5, and part of B1) was read side by side and is reported as read.

6. The Greek translators are early readers of these links. At several points a Greek translator made a link explicit that the Hebrew or Aramaic leaves implicit:

  • Theodotion adds “yet” at 8:17, following Hab 2:3 (A6).
  • The Old Greek supplies “Lord of lords” at 4:34, following Deut 10:17 (A15).
  • Theodotion’s refrain “to whomever it seems good” in ch. 4 follows Jeremiah 27:5 in Greek (A14).
  • Theodotion’s “coming forth” at 11:30 follows Num 24:24 in Greek (A5).

This gives the history-of-interpretation criterion a footing in the texts, not in memory alone.

Single contacts (Auditor A)

A1: Isa 26:19 behind Dan 12:2

Source: Intertextual Map; live, high. Claim: Daniel’s “many of those who sleep in the dust of the ground will awake” takes up Isaiah’s “you who lie in the dust, awake”.

Results.

  • קִיץ (“to awake”) with עָפָר (“dust”) in one verse: Isa 26:19 and Dan 12:2 only (WLC). The search returns Isa 26:19 as a positive control.
  • In Auditor A’s baseline, this is the only exclusive-pair contact Dan 12:2 has anywhere in the Bible.
  • None of the rivals supplies awaking from dust:
    • Job 14:12 has “awake” with “lie down”, but no dust, and it is a denial.
    • Ps 22:30 has the dust but no waking.
  • An unnamed secondary echo. The verb יָשֵׁן (“to sleep”) with “awake” and עוֹלָם (“everlasting”) occurs at Jer 51:39 and 51:57 only: Babylon’s princes “will sleep a perpetual sleep and not wake”. Dan 12:2 uses the related adjective יָשֵׁן (“sleeper”) with “awake” and “everlasting”, and that combination occurs at Dan 12:2 alone. So Daniel’s sleepers who wake reverse Babylon’s everlasting sleep. This adds to Isaiah and does not compete with it, because only Isaiah supplies the dust.
  • Greek. Theodotion’s “will be roused” shares its root with the Greek of Isa 26:19 (“will be raised”). The Old Greek’s “will rise” is the first verb of the Greek of Isa 26:19 (Rahlfs; Swete).

A2: Isa 66:24 behind Dan 12:2

Source: Intertextual Map; live, high. Claim: Daniel’s “to everlasting contempt” takes up Isaiah’s last word, “an abhorrence to all mankind”.

Results.

  • דֵּרָאוֹן (“abhorrence”): Isa 66:24 and Dan 12:2 only (WLC). It is one of the seven words Hebrew Daniel shares with a single other verse.
  • The sense is transformed. In Isaiah the rebels’ corpses are seen, unburied and unconsumed. In Daniel people rise to shame. The noun and the “everlasting” outcome are the same; the subject changes.
  • The other half of the line is common idiom. חֶרְפָּה (“reproach”) with “everlasting” occurs at Jer 23:40; 49:13; Ps 78:66; Dan 12:2. Theodotion’s “everlasting reproach” at 12:2 is word for word the Greek of Jer 23:40.
  • Neither Greek Daniel marks the Isaiah link. The Old Greek has “dispersion and shame”; Theodotion has “shame” (Rahlfs).
  • A1 and A2 together. Two places in one other book, far apart, are not rare in themselves: 23 per cent of Hebrew Daniel’s verses show the pattern. What is unusual at 12:2 is the grade of the evidence: one rare word and one exclusive pair, with nothing competing. Drop either contact and Isaiah is still the only candidate for the other. The two contacts reinforce each other, and each would be enough on its own.

A3: Isa 52:13; 53:11 behind Dan 11:33, 35; 12:3, 10

Source: Intertextual Map; live, high. Claim: The מַשְׂכִּילִים (“those who have insight”) take up the Servant’s two marks: he will have insight, and he will make the many righteous.

Results.

  • צָדַק (“to be righteous”) with רַבִּים (“the many”) in one verse: Isa 53:11 and Dan 12:3 only (WLC).
  • The lemma search does not separate the verb’s stems, so the forms were read. Both are causative:
    • יַצְדִּיק (“he will make righteous”) at Isa 53:11;
    • מַצְדִּיקֵי (“those who make righteous”) at Dan 12:3.
    The causative stem occurs in 12 verses, and only these two have “the many”. The stem check strengthens the claim.
  • The fair test is the passage, not the verse. The Servant song (Isa 52:13–53:12) is the only text outside Daniel where three things come together:
    • the causative of שָׂכַל (“to have insight”), which opens the song at 52:13;
    • the causative of “make righteous”;
    • “the many” (52:14, 15; 53:11, 12).
  • Unnamed:
    • Dan 1:4 uses the same participle for the exiled youths, “showing intelligence” (NASB95). The book’s opening already plants the group its close names.
    • Isa 52:15 has בִּין (“to understand”) with “many”; Dan 11:33 has “they will make the many understand”.
  • Greek. The Greek of Isa 52:13 reads “my servant will understand”, and both Greek Daniels at 12:3 have “those who understand” (Rahlfs). Here the Greek tradition keeps the link.

A4: Isa 10:23, 25 behind Dan 9:27 and 11:36

Source: Intertextual Map; live, high. Claim: Isaiah’s “a complete destruction, one that is decreed” and “the indignation will be finished” are reapplied to the last king. The overview’s wording implies that 11:36 has both phrases.

Results.

  • The fixed phrase. The noun כָּלָה (“complete destruction”) with חָרַץ (“to decree”) occurs at Isa 10:23; 28:22; Dan 9:27 (WLC). It is the same fixed phrase in all three. A repeated phrase is stronger evidence than an exclusive pair.
  • The verb with “indignation”. The verb כָּלָה (“to be finished”) with זַעַם (“indignation”) occurs at Isa 10:25; Ezek 22:31; Dan 11:36. Only Isa 10:25 is a real parallel: Ezekiel uses another stem, with indignation as the object.
  • Correction: Dan 11:36 has the verb, not the noun. It has “until the indignation is finished” together with נֶחֱרָצָה (“decreed”). Daniel blends the two lines of Isaiah: the decree word comes from the fixed phrase, and the verb from 10:25. The overview’s “holds both” should go.
  • For 11:36, Isaiah 10 is clearly primary. The reflexive of גָּדַל (“to magnify oneself”) occurs only at Isa 10:15; Ezek 38:23; Dan 11:36, 37 (WLC). The arrogant king speaks with the voice of Isaiah’s boasting Assyrian axe.
  • For 9:27, Isaiah 10 and Isaiah 28 are co-sources and cannot be ranked.
    • For Isaiah 10: “decree” within three verses of “overflowing” finds Isa 10:22–23 and Dan 9:26–27.
    • For Isaiah 28: it has “overflowing” four times, and a covenant made and annulled (28:15, 18). That fits Dan 9:27’s covenant made strong with “the many”.

A5: Num 24:24 (and 24:14) behind Dan 11:30 (and 10:14)

Source: Intertextual Map; high (Kittim); live (provisional: one strong contact, one idiom). Claim: Balaam’s “ships of Kittim” stand behind Dan 11:30, and his “in the latter days” behind 10:14.

Results.

  • צִי (“ship”) with כִּתִּים (“Kittim”): Num 24:24 and Dan 11:30 only (WLC). “Ship” is itself a rare word, found in four verses.
  • The second contact is not bare idiom. “In the latter days” occurs in 13 verses. Among them, only Num 24:14 and Dan 10:14 add לְעַמְּךָ (“to your people”), and both are built the same way: “what will befall your people in the latter days”.
  • Both phrases introduce a revelation that ends with the Kittim (Num 24:24; Dan 11:30). So the claim rests on two matching brackets of one oracle, not on one contact plus one idiom.
  • Greek (Rahlfs):
    • The Old Greek of 11:30 reads “Romans will come”, an early identification of the oracle [S].
    • Theodotion’s “coming forth” follows the Greek of Num 24:24, “one shall come forth from the hand of the Kitians” (Swete).

A6: Hab 2:3 behind Dan 8:17, 19; 10:14; 11:27, 35

Source: Intertextual Map; live, high. Claim: Habakkuk’s “the vision is yet for the appointed time; it hastens toward the end” supplies Daniel’s vocabulary of the end.

Results.

  • חָזוֹן (“vision”) with קֵץ (“end”): Hab 2:3 and Dan 8:17 only (WLC).

  • The ordered phrase “for the vision is yet”: Hab 2:3 and Dan 10:14 only, both with and without vowels.

  • A repeated clause. “Yet” with “end” and “appointed time” occurs at Hab 2:3; Dan 11:27; 11:35 only, and the clauses line up:

    Verse Hebrew Gloss
    Hab 2:3 כִּי עוֹד חָזוֹן לַמּוֹעֵד “for the vision is yet for the appointed time”
    Dan 11:27 כִּי עוֹד קֵץ לַמּוֹעֵד “for the end is yet for the appointed time”
    Dan 11:35 כִּי עוֹד לַמּוֹעֵד “for it is yet for the appointed time”

    This is the strongest kind of evidence in the whole set.

  • Unnamed: Hab 2:3’s “it will not lie” and the “lie” spoken at Dan 11:27 fall in the same verse as the repeated clause.

  • Ezekiel 12 is a co-source, not a rival. “Vision” with “days” and “many” occurs at Ezek 12:27 and Dan 8:26 only. Ezekiel 12 therefore stands behind Dan 8:26 (“it pertains to many days”). It does not compete with Habakkuk at 8:17.

  • “Time of the end”, in that word order, is Daniel’s own phrase (8:17; 11:35, 40; 12:4, 9).

  • Greek. Theodotion at 8:17 adds “yet”, which is not in the Hebrew of that verse but is in the Greek of Hab 2:3. Theodotion read Habakkuk into Daniel.

A7: Ezekiel 1; 9–10 behind Dan 10:5–6; 12:6–7 (and 7:9–10)

Source: Intertextual Map; live, high. Claim: The heavenly figure of the last vision is drawn in Ezekiel’s colours; the throne of 7:9–10 shares Ezekiel’s wheels (across the two languages).

Results.

  • קָלָל (“burnished”): Ezek 1:7 and Dan 10:6 only (WLC). The simile is the same in both: כְּעֵין נְחֹשֶׁת קָלָל (“like the gleam of burnished bronze”).
  • Correction: “clothed” with “linen” occurs in 13 verses, not 12. Ezek 9:11 is missed by the lemma search, because its participle is filed under another entry (Method finding 3).
  • Leviticus 16 is background, not a rival.

    • Ezekiel and Daniel use the plural בַּדִּים (“linen garments”) with “clothed”.
    • The plural occurs only at Ezek 9:2, 3, 11; 10:2, 6, 7 and Dan 10:5; 12:6, 7.
    • “A man clothed in linen” occurs in Ezekiel 9–10 and Daniel alone.
    • The density scan ranks Ezekiel 9 first and Ezekiel 1 second of 851 chapters for Daniel 10.
  • More contacts in 10:6:
    • תַּרְשִׁישׁ (“beryl”) in the simile “like the gleam of beryl”: Ezek 1:16; 10:9; Dan 10:6 only.
    • לַפִּיד (“torch”) with “fire” and בָּרָק (“lightning”): Ezek 1:13 and Dan 10:6 only.

  • 7:9–10, read across the languages. Daniel’s “its wheels were a burning fire” answers the Hebrew גַּלְגַּל (“wheel”) with “fire”, which occurs at Ezek 10:2 and 10:6 only. The thrones answer Ezek 1:26; 10:1. The river of fire and the white garment have no match in Ezekiel. Theodotion keeps the wheels; the Old Greek drops them (Rahlfs).

A8: Deut 32:40 behind Dan 12:7

Source: Intertextual Map; live, high. Claim: The Song of Moses’ divine oath, “I lift up My hand to heaven, and say, as I live forever”, is taken up in the angel’s oath.

Results.

  • “Heaven” with “living” and “for ever”: Deut 32:40 and Dan 12:7 only (WLC).
  • The oath by life is common: swearing by “life” occurs in 14 verses. It is the raised hand together with heaven and “living for ever” that only these two verses share.
  • On the verb, Gen 14:22 is closer. Deut 32:40 has נָשָׂא יָד (“lift the hand”). Dan 12:7 has רוּם (“to raise”) with “hand”, and the verb רוּם with “hand” occurs at Gen 14:22: “I have raised my hand to the LORD … possessor of heaven and earth”.
  • The speaker is reversed. In Deuteronomy God swears by his own life; in Daniel an angel swears by God’s.
  • Greek. The Greek of Deut 32:40 reads “I will swear by my right hand”, supplying the right hand and the oath that the Hebrew of Deuteronomy lacks and Daniel has (Swete). Rev 10:5–6 follows Daniel 12:7 (SBLGNT).
  • Within Daniel, “by Him who lives forever” answers the Aramaic title of God at 4:34 (cross-language, read).

A9: Genesis 40–41 behind Daniel 1–2 (and 5:29), with Exodus 7:11

Source: Intertextual Map; Joseph live, high. Exod 7:11 was proposed by the Daniel 2 dig at moderate–high. Claim: Daniel is drawn as a second Joseph: a dejected face, a troubled spirit, magicians summoned for a dream, “it is not in me”, a man with the spirit of God, a gold chain on the neck. Exod 7:11 is a second, Mosaic layer behind 2:2.

Results.

  • (a) The troubled spirit. פָּעַם (“to be troubled”) with רוּחַ (“spirit”) occurs at Gen 41:8; Judg 13:25; Dan 2:1; 2:3 (WLC). Gen 41:8 and Dan 2:3 share the identical form, וַתִּפָּעֶם רוּחוֹ (“and his spirit was troubled”; “my spirit” at Dan 2:3). Judges 13:25 is a different sense.
  • (b) The magicians. חַרְטֹם (“magician”) with חֲלוֹם (“dream”): Gen 41:8 and Dan 2:2 only. The word is an Egyptian loanword [S]. Daniel’s Hebrew can only have it from Genesis or Exodus.
  • (c) The dejected faces. זֹעֲפִים (“dejected”) of faces: Gen 40:6 and Dan 1:10 only. The court-official vocabulary around it (שַׂר, “chief”, with סָרִיס, “official, eunuch”) is widespread court register. The density scan ranks Esther above Genesis 40 for Daniel 1. That vocabulary is not Joseph allusion.
  • (d) Read across the languages.
    • “Not in me” (Gen 41:16 / Dan 2:30): the sense matches; the wording does not.
    • “God has made known”: only Gen 41:39 uses the cognate of Daniel’s verb.
    • “In whom is the spirit of God” (Gen 41:38) is matched more exactly at Dan 4:8–9; 5:11, 14 (“in whom is a spirit of the holy gods”) than at 2:25 (“I have found a man”).
    • Gen 41:42 against Dan 5:29, “clothed … the gold chain on his neck”, is the closest parallel across the two languages. Hebrew “neck” with “gold” occurs only at Gen 41:42 and Judg 8:26 (camels’ ornaments).
  • (e) Exodus 7:11. חַרְטֹם (“magician”) with מְכַשֵּׁף (“sorcerer”): Exod 7:11 and Dan 2:2 only. But Exod 7:11 also has חֲכָמִים (“wise men”), as Gen 41:8 does, and the core term is the same Egyptian word. The Exodus contact is a list of diviners. It adds the motif of failed rival experts, which Genesis 41 already supplies.
  • The court-tale objection. The general setting is court-tale stock (Esther in vocabulary, Ahiqar in plot [S]). The specific chain is not: troubled spirit, magicians summoned for a dream, God making known, a man with the spirit of God, a gold chain on the neck. Esther supplies none of it.

Source: Structural Arc Map, the paragraph on concentric arrangement (moderate, after a baseline in the overview run). Note also the dig skill’s structure reference, which states the arrangement as fact. Claim: Chapters 2–7 are arranged in pairs around a centre.

Results: the exclusive items.

  • “Sent His angel” (the Aramaic verb with מַלְאַךְ, “angel”): Dan 3:28 and 6:22 only (WLC). Theodotion keeps the match (Rahlfs).
  • שֵׁיזִב (“to deliver”): nine occurrences in seven verses, all in chapters 3 and 6, and nowhere else in the canon.
  • The ruling refrain. “Ruler” with “kingdom” and “wishes” occurs at 4:17, 25, 32 and 5:21, exactly four. But 5:21 is Daniel retelling chapter 4 to Belshazzar. The link is made by the story quoting itself, not by the arrangement.
  • דְּקַק (“to crush”) is used of the fourth kingdom and the fourth beast, as stated. It is not exclusive to 2 and 7: the lions crush the accusers’ bones in 6:24.

    • “prospered” closes both chapters (3:30; 6:28);
    • the royal letter opens “to all the peoples … may your peace abound”;
    • “signs and wonders” appear in the doxologies.

B5: Daniel follows Genesis 40–41 in sequence across chapters 1–5

Source: Daniel 2 dig, Open Questions item 1 (moderate–high; design not yet baselined). Claim: Daniel follows the Joseph story in order: the prison chapter (Gen 40) at Dan 1, the palace chapter (Gen 41) at Dan 2, and the robe and chain (Gen 41:42) at Dan 5:29.

Results.

  • The three named points hold.
    • Dan 5:29 against Gen 41:42 was read across the languages.
    • In Hebrew, “clothe” with “gold” and “neck” occurs at Gen 41:42 only.
    • The Aramaic words for “purple” and “chain” occur only at Dan 5:7, 16, 29.
    • Theodotion’s 5:29 is closer to the Greek of Gen 41:42 than the Aramaic is to the Hebrew: “put the gold chain round his neck” (Rahlfs).
  • But the chapter-by-chapter mapping is too neat. Dan 1 already draws on all three Joseph chapters:
    • Gen 39:21 at 1:9;
    • Gen 40:6–7 at 1:10;
    • Gen 41:2–4 at 1:15;
    • Gen 41:8 at 1:20.
    Genesis 40 reappears after Dan 1 only in the formulaic language of reporting dreams.
  • The order across the whole set. Thirteen defensible Joseph contacts in Dan 1–5 fall in Genesis order with a strong rank correlation: Kendall’s tau 0.77, from 63 pairs in order against 8 out of order. In 20,000 random shuffles of the Genesis side, 4 matched it. On the claim’s own three points, the chance of the right order is one in six, which proves nothing by itself. It is the fuller inventory that makes the order striking.
  • But the order may be the plot’s, not the author’s reading. Gen 39–41 is a single rise: favour in confinement, interpretation, summons, the king’s dream, recognition of “the spirit of God”, investiture. Any story of a foreign courtier’s rise will touch those moments in that order.
  • Esther shows this. Mordecai is robed and led out with a proclamation (Esth 6:11), and later appears in royal purple with a crown of gold (8:15). These come at the same point of a different story. “Clothe” with a herald’s proclamation repeats the pattern of Gen 41:42–43 at Esth 6:11. Dan 5:29 is likewise followed by a proclamation.

B6: Daniel 2:20–23 (the doxology) as the key of Daniel 2

Source: Daniel 2 dig, Headline Finding 2: high on the links, moderate–high on the design. Claim: Each line of the doxology is answered later in the chapter, so the hymn is the chapter’s key. The dig also cited “all the wise men of Babylon” (2:12 → 2:48) as a frame.

Proposed skill amendments

These are drafted for the dig-deeper skill and the book-overview skill. None has been applied.

  1. An exclusive pair needs a baseline before it carries weight (Claim Audit Mode). Proposed text:

    Before rating a contact on a single “only two verses” pair, measure what an ordinary verse of the same book yields. In Hebrew Daniel the average verse has about three such pairs. A lone exclusive pair is moderate at most, unless it is a genuinely rare word, part of a cluster, a repeated phrase or clause, or confirmed by early reception.

  2. The register test for late books (Phase 0.55 and the claim-audit format). Proposed text:

    When a book’s density scan or exclusive pairs are dominated by Esther, Ezra–Nehemiah or Chronicles, run the same scan on a late book placed far away in the canon (Jonah, Ecclesiastes) before reading the pattern as canonical design. In the BHS order the late narrative books are adjacent, so design and register cannot be separated from inside the cluster.

  3. A lemma count is a floor (Phase 0.2). Proposed text:

    The index can file one form of a word under a separate entry (at Ezek 9:11, “clothed” is filed apart from the other forms), and it splits some names by book. Read the hits of any count on which a verdict turns, and search the neighbouring forms before writing “only”.

  4. Test the words the target text actually has (Claim Audit Mode). Proposed text:

    A search that includes a word absent from the target verse (as with “temple” at Dan 1:2) cannot show dependence. Build every rarity search only from words both texts share.

  5. Greek reception is evidence (the original-languages extension and Tool 11). Proposed text:

    Where a Greek translator renders a passage in the words of its proposed source (Theodotion’s “yet” at Dan 8:17 from Hab 2:3; “to whomever it seems good” from Jeremiah), record it under History of Interpretation as a text-based witness, tagged [T] for the wording and [I] for the inference.

  6. Bring the structure reference into line (the dig-deeper skill’s structure reference). Replace the statement that “Daniel 2–7 is a six-chapter chiasm; the centre (chs 4–5)” with:

    A proposed concentric arrangement of Daniel 2–7. Its inner pairs, 3 // 6 and 4 // 5, are strong; its outer pair, 2 // 7, rests on theme. It beats a chance baseline only at about one in fifteen. Hold it at moderate.

  7. A hymn is not a key by default (Tool 3). Proposed text:

    Before calling a hymn, prayer or speech the “key” of its chapter, measure how often its vocabulary recurs in the chapter against the chapter’s other stretches of the same length. Programmatic theology and lexical centrality are different claims.
