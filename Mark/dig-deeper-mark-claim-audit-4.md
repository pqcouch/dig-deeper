# Claim Audit 4: Mark — the Galilee Inventory (Schneck's List)

**Claims audited.** Ten Isaiah allusions in Mark 2–8 from Schneck's list, as Watts quotes it (round-2 Logos extracts). None of the ten was in the Mark overview's inventory, though one was anticipated in a "cf." note.
**Date.** 9 November 2026.
**Purpose.** The hypothesis test found Isaiah thin in the Galilee chapters (about 1 locus per 100 verses), and claim audit 2 found Isaiah's spread across Mark significantly front-weighted. Round 2 of the Logos questions showed that the inventory behind those findings had never tested Schneck's Galilee entries. This audit tests them and then recounts.
**Texts.**

- Mark: SBLGNT.
- Greek OT: Swete; Rahlfs Isaiah, one verse per line; Swete's Isaiah apparatus.
- Hebrew: the WLC, by lemma.
- The BHS apparatus.
- There is no Targum in the corpus.

**Companion.** Claim audit 3.

---

## How This Audit Was Run

- **One blind auditor** took all ten claims as single contacts. It could not see the hypothesis test, the earlier audits, the Logos records or Watts. It was told the list was published, and that "a published list is a claim like any other".
- **The recount** that follows is arithmetic on the audited ratings, done here. It uses the same method and the same movement boundaries as the hypothesis test, so that the two can be compared.

**Checking the checker.** These decisive findings were re-run here and reproduced:

- **Tombs in the Greek.** ἐν τοῖς μνήμασιν ("in the tombs") stands in Swete only at Isa 65:4, Deut 9:22 (the place name "Graves of Craving") and Odes 4:19.
- **Fettered tomb-dwellers.** πεπεδημένους ("fettered") with τάφοις ("tombs") stands in Swete only at Ps 67:7.
- **Fasting and the bridegroom.** "Bridegroom" within three verses of "fast" occurs in Swete only at Joel 2:15–16.
- **"Taken away".** Mark's ἀπαρθῇ ("be taken away", 2:20) does not occur in Swete.
- **Rahlfs Isa 43:25** has no ἁμαρτίας ("sins").
- **Rahlfs Isa 55:1** has πίετε ("drink") where Swete and the Hebrew have "eat".
- **John 12:40** renders Isa 6:10 with ἐπώρωσεν αὐτῶν τὴν καρδίαν ("he hardened their heart"), Mark's verb at 6:52 and 8:17.
- **Isa 65:4** is the only WLC verse with "tomb" + "swine" (also found by audit 3's auditor).

**Headline.**

- **Three of the ten survive at moderate or above,** and one of them is genuinely new: Isa 65:1–7 at 5:1–20.
- **Two are the hardening texts (6:52; 8:17).** These are Isa 6:9–10 again, picked up from its quotation at 4:12. They were "cf." material in the overview, and now count as loci of their own.
- **Four are discarded or beaten by rivals elsewhere:** Joel, Numbers, 2 Kings, Exodus, 1 Kings and Ezekiel.
- **The list overstates Isaiah, as published lists of allusions usually do.** But not by everything: the three that survive are enough to change the shape of the count.

## Claims Audited

### 1. 2:7 ← Isa 43:25–26

- **No shared verb.** Isaiah has מָחָה / ἐξαλείφω ("wipe out"); Mark has ἀφίημι ("forgive").
- **"Sins" is not secure in Isaiah's Greek.** It stands in Swete only. Rahlfs, Sinaiticus, Alexandrinus and Marchalianus lack it.
- **"God alone" is the Shema** (Deut 6:4; Mark 12:29, 32).
- **Isaiah's own uses of Mark's verb** are 33:24 and 55:7.

**Uncertain;** conceptual only.

### 2. 2:16–20 ← Isa 58:2–7

- **Shared:** "fast" and "day".
- **Isa 58's sequence loosely parallels Mark 2:1–3:6:** release; bread for the hungry; the house; the sabbath (58:13).
- **But Isa 58 has no bridegroom.** Joel 2:15–16 is the only text in either Swete or the WLC with fast + bridegroom, and the scanner ranks Joel 2 at median 7 against Mark 2:15–22.

**Uncertain–moderate** as background. Joel 2:15–16 is a new candidate for the bridegroom.

### 3. 2:20 ← Isa 58:8

- **The only shared word is τότε ("then").**
- **The moods are opposite:** light and healing in Isaiah, loss and mourning in Mark.
- **Mark's ἀπαρθῇ ("be taken away") is absent from Swete.**

**Discard.**

### 4. 4:24 ← Targum Isa 27:8

- **Hebrew:** בְּסַּאסְּאָה, a contested hapax, possibly a pun on "measure".
- **Greek:** no measure at all. It reads "contending … he will send them away".
- **The measure-for-measure idiom has no OT parallel.**
- **The Targum is not in the corpus,** so the claim cannot be tested here.

**Uncertain.** A Logos question if wanted.

### 5. 5:1–20 ← Isa 65:1–7 — the strongest of the ten

- **The phrase.** Mark 5:3, 5 has **ἐν τοῖς μνήμασιν** ("in the tombs"), word for word with Swete Isa 65:4. Apart from a place name and the Odes, that is the only place it occurs in Swete.
- **The pairing is unique in both languages.** "Tomb" with "swine" occurs only at Isa 65:4, in the WLC and in Swete.
- **The Greek adds "demons".** Isa 65:3 has τοῖς δαιμονίοις ("to the demons"); the Hebrew has "on the bricks".
- **The Hebrew adds "sit".** Isa 65:4 has יֹשְׁבִים ("sit, dwell"); Mark has "dwelling" (5:3) and "sitting" (5:15).
- **More contacts.**
  - Isa 65:5 Greek "I am clean" against Mark's "unclean spirit".
  - Isa 65:7 "on the mountains" against Mark 5:5, 11.
  - Isa 65:1 "a nation that did not call on my name", where Mark's scene is set on Gentile ground.
- **Weak points.**
  - Mark's word for swine (χοῖρος) is not Isaiah's (ὕειος).
  - In Isaiah people eat pork; in Mark demons enter the herd.
- **A secondary Greek contact:** Ps 67:7 LXX, "leading out the fettered … those dwelling in tombs", for Mark 5:3–4's dwelling and fetters. That is Greek only: the Hebrew has "a parched land".
- **The scanner's low rank for Isa 65 is a stem fault.** It missed μνήμασιν and ὀρέων, so it does not count against the link.

**Moderate.** Audit 3's auditor reached the same verdict on the same passage independently. History of Interpretation: Schneck and Watts (round 2).

### 6. 6:34–44 ← Isa 55:1–3

- **Isaiah contributes only "buy".** That is ordinary famine language, and in Mark it is the disciples who propose buying.
- **The real sources lie elsewhere:**
  - Num 27:17, "like sheep that have no shepherd", with Joshua (Ἰησοῦς) named next. This is already in the overview at high.
  - 2 Kgs 4:42–44, "give to the people and let them eat". It is the only Swete verse with "give" + "eat" in that form.
  - Ezek 34.

**Uncertain** for Isaiah.

### 7. 6:39–40 ← Isa 25:6

- **No shared word in any edition.**
- **No eating in the Greek of 25:6,** and no mountain in Mark: the setting is a deserted place.
- **The groups of hundreds and fifties point elsewhere:** Exod 18:21, 25, and 1 Kgs 18:4, 13, where a hundred men are fed with bread by fifties.

**Discard.**

### 8. 6:52 ← Isa 6:9–10

- **Shared:** συνίημι ("understand") with καρδία ("heart"). Of the Swete verses that join them, Isa 6:10 is the only one where the heart fails to understand.
- **The verb differs.** Mark's πωρόω ("harden") is not the LXX's παχύνω ("make fat"). But John 12:40 quotes Isa 6:10 with πωρόω, so Mark's verb was a current Greek rendering of that verse.
- **Mark has already quoted Isa 6:9–10, at 4:12.**
- **Exodus's hardening at the sea** has "harden" but not "understand".

**Moderate,** through 4:12.

### 9. 8:17b ← Isa 6:9–10

- **Heart and understanding come from Isa 6:9–10, through 4:12.** In a random-passage test, Mark 8:17–18 is first of 671 two-verse windows for words shared with Isa 6:9–10.
- **8:18 ("having eyes … having ears") is Ezekiel's form:** Ezek 12:2, with Jer 5:21. The overview already holds that.

**Moderate–high** combined; Isaiah alone **moderate**.

### 10. 8:25 ← Isa 42:6–7

- **Mark 8:25 has no "open" verb.** The claim's "eyes opened" is not Mark's wording.
- **Isa 35:5 has as good a claim as 42:7.** "Open" with eyes with blind occurs in Swete only at those two verses.
- **Isa 42:18 has Mark's ἀναβλέπω** ("look up, see again", 8:24).
- **Mal 3:23 (Swete 4:5)** has ἀποκαταστήσει ("he will restore"). Mark 8:25 has ἀπεκατέστη ("was restored"), and Mark 9:12 uses the same verb of Elijah. It is unaudited.

**Uncertain–moderate** as listed. The whole Isaianic blind-sight cluster (35:5; 42:16–20) stays **moderate** (audit 2).

## The Recount: Isaiah's Spread After Audits 2–4

**Method.** The same as the hypothesis test:

- loci rated moderate or above;
- the overview's six movements: Prologue 13 verses; Galilee 292; Way 117; Jerusalem 113; Passion 118; Tomb 8;
- one locus per Mark place.

**What changes.** These are added, all audited at moderate or above:

| Added locus | Movement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1:14–15, the herald complex (Isa 40:9; 52:7; 61:1) | Galilee | Audit 2 |
| 5:1–20 (Isa 65:3–5) | Galilee | Audits 3 and 4 |
| 6:52 (Isa 6:9–10) | Galilee | Audit 4 |
| 8:17 (Isa 6:9–10) | Galilee | Audit 4 |
| The healings of the blind framing the Way section (Isa 35:5; 40:3–4; 42:16–18) | Way | Audit 2 |

Nothing in the earlier count falls below moderate. The *Ephphatha* reframing (audit 3) does not remove 7:32–37, which stays high on other grounds.

**Result.**

| | P | G | W | J | Pa | T | Total | Per 100 verses (G, W, J, Pa) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Isaiah, hypothesis test | 3 | 3 | 2 | 4 | 3 | 0 | 15 | 1.0, 1.7, 3.5, 2.5 |
| **Isaiah, after audits 2–4** | 3 | **7** | **3** | 4 | 3 | 0 | **20** | **2.4, 2.6, 3.5, 2.5** |

*P = Prologue, G = Galilee, W = the Way, J = Jerusalem, Pa = the Passion, T = the Tomb.*

**The test against length.** Each distribution was tested against the movements' lengths (a χ² goodness of fit, p by simulation, 50,000 runs):

- **Isaiah before:** p ≈ 0.003. Significantly out of proportion, as audit 2 found.
- **Isaiah after:** p ≈ 0.015. Still out of proportion.
- **Isaiah after, with the prologue left out:** p ≈ 0.98. **Outside the prologue, Isaiah is spread almost exactly in proportion to the length of each movement:** 2.4, 2.6, 3.5 and 2.5 loci per 100 verses.

**What this means.**

- **The departure from an even spread is entirely the prologue:** 3 loci in 13 verses.
- **"Thin in Galilee" was an artefact** of an inventory that had not tested Schneck's entries. With the three survivors and the herald complex, Galilee has the same density as the Way and the Passion.
- **So Isaiah's profile in Mark is now:** concentrated at the opening, where the reader is told how to read; then present at a steady rate through every movement of the narrative except the empty tomb.

**The comparison with other books is not fair after this audit.** Only Isaiah has been searched for again in Galilee. The Psalms, Exodus and Daniel were not. So the recount can say how Isaiah is spread. It cannot say that Isaiah is spread more widely than any other source.

For the record, Exodus with its one audited addition (the vocabulary of 10:45, in the Way) stands at 10 loci, and fits length too (p ≈ 0.18). A fair comparison would need the same targeted search for the other books.

## Effect on the Hypothesis

| Form | Before audits 3–4 | After |
|---|---|---|
| **Frame** | Holds, high; front-weighting significant | **Holds, high.** The front-weighting is now located exactly: it is the prologue, and nothing else |
| **Substrate** | Holds in part, moderate: most-used, joint widest with Exodus, not evenly spread | **Strengthened, moderate.** 20 loci. Outside the prologue Isaiah is spread in proportion to the narrative (p ≈ 0.98). The claim that it is "not evenly spread" is withdrawn for the narrative after 1:13. Still not shown to exceed the other books, which were not re-searched |
| **Architecture** | Open, uncertain–moderate | **Unchanged.** An even spread is evidence that Isaiah is present throughout, not that it supplies the plan. If anything it tells against a three-stage Isaianic design, whose stages would be expected to differ in density. That is `[I]`, and worth putting to Watts's scheme |

The overall picture is now more definite. Mark names Isaiah at the start, concentrates Isaiah in his prologue, and then keeps Isaiah in play at a steady rate to the end of the passion. That is the behaviour of a book read *through* Isaiah, which is the frame form of your hypothesis, with the substrate form now better supported than before. Whether Isaiah also supplies the *plan* remains open.
