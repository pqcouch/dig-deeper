# Claim Audit 2: Mark — the Isaiah Hypothesis

**Claims audited.** Six claims from `Mark/dig-deeper-mark-isaiah-hypothesis-test.md` (6 November 2026): four new Isaiah allusion candidates, the "way" design claim, and the claim that Isaiah is Mark's most widely and evenly spread source. They fall at Mark 1:14–15; 3:27; 7:32–11:1; 8:22–10:52; and the book as a whole.
**Date.** 6 November 2026.
**Purpose.** To test these claims before any of them enters the Mark overview or a sermon. All six were produced by the same author in one session, and the test report said so and asked for this audit.
**Texts.** Mark from the SBLGNT with the MorphGNT index, counted in 1:1–16:8. Matthew and Luke from the SBLGNT. The Greek OT from Swete (observation) and the Rahlfs–Hanhart Isaiah export (citation). The Hebrew from the WLC, searched by lemma. The BHS Isaiah apparatus and the NA28 Mark apparatus exports.
**Consumes.** The hypothesis test, the Mark overview v0.2 (Intertextual Map and Structural Arc Map only), and `Mark/dig-deeper-mark-claim-audit.md` (audit 1) for method.

## How This Audit Was Run

**Two blind auditors**, following your standing rule: one for single contacts, one for field and design claims.

- **Auditor A (single contacts).** Claims 1, 3 and 4: Isa 42:16, Isa 52:7 and Isa 49 / 53:12.
- **Auditor B (field and design).** Claims 2, 5 and 6: the Isa 35 sequence, the "way" design, and Isaiah's spread.
- **What they were given.** Each auditor received the bare claim and the proposer's rating, and nothing else. Neither could open the hypothesis test, the sweep, audit 1 or the 1:1–13 dig. Auditor B was allowed the overview's two maps, which Claim 6 is built from.
- **What they were told to do.** Try to break each claim, working only from the corpus on your MacBook. Run baselines, a random-passage test, a search for rival sources, and positive controls. Report under the Hays criteria, or a counting test for the field claims.
- **Secondary sources.** None were opened, so History of Interpretation reads "Not checked" throughout.

**Checking the checkers.** The auditors' decisive findings were then re-run here. These reproduced exactly:

- Swete Jer 38:8 (Hebrew Jer 31:8) reads ἐν ἑορτῇ φάσεκ ("at the feast of Passover") where the Hebrew has "the blind and the lame".
- Swete Isa 42:18 reads οἱ τυφλοί, ἀναβλέψατε ("you blind, look up").
- Rahlfs has τὰ σκολιὰ εἰς εὐθεῖαν ("the crooked into straight") twice in Isaiah, at 40:4 and 42:16.
- ἐν τῇ ὁδῷ ("on the way") stands in six verses of Mark: 8:3, and five inside 8:27–10:52 (8:27; 9:33; 9:34; 10:32; 10:52) (SBLGNT).
- In the WLC, עִוֵּר ("blind") and דֶּרֶךְ ("way") stand within three verses of each other in eight places, including Jer 31:8–9.
- בשׂר ("bring good news") and מָשַׁל ("rule") stand within two verses of each other only at Isa 40:9–10 and 52:5–7 (WLC).
- Lam 4:19 is the only Swete verse with ἤγγικεν ("has drawn near"), πληρόω ("fulfil") and καιρός ("time").
- גִּבּוֹר ("mighty man") and מַלְקוֹחַ ("prey") share a verse only at Isa 49:24, 25 (WLC).
- Luke 11:22 has τὰ σκῦλα αὐτοῦ διαδίδωσιν ("distributes his spoils") where Mark 3:27 has τὰ σκεύη ("his goods") (SBLGNT).
- אֹזֶן ("ear") and פָּתַח ("open") share a verse in seven places in the WLC. Isa 35:5 is the only one about the deaf.
- Exod 23:20 in Swete continues ἵνα φυλάξῃ σε ἐν τῇ ὁδῷ ("to guard you on the way").
- The noun λύτρον ("ransom") occurs in Swete Isaiah only at 45:13. The verb occurs at 35:9; 41:14; 43:14; 44:22, 24; 52:3, with the noun λύτρωσις ("redemption") at 63:4.

**One figure reproduced with a smaller count.** Auditor B reported that 33 of 117 Mark lemmas of middling frequency cluster in a single movement as strongly as ὁδός does. Re-run here by verse, it is **28 of the 109 lemmas found in 10–30 verses**. The conclusion is the same: about a quarter of such words cluster that strongly somewhere. The difference is in how occurrences were counted. Seven of them cluster in the Way section, including ἐπερωτάω ("ask") and παιδίον ("child").

**Not re-run.** Two of Auditor B's findings were not re-run here:
- The Matthew and Luke parallel count outside the Way frames (14 of 16 kept). It rests on the auditor's own identification of the parallels.
- The motif-order shuffle test for Isa 35 (p ≈ 0.10). It rests on the auditor's coding of the motifs.

Both are reported as the auditor's.

**One apparatus check.** At Mark 7:35 the NA28 apparatus records variant forms of the verb: ἠνοίγησαν, διηνοίγησαν, διηνοίχθησαν, ἠνοίχθησαν ("were opened"). Every reading is a form of ἀνοίγω or διανοίγω ("to open"). So Auditor B's Hebrew point at 7:34–35 stands whichever reading is adopted `[T]`.

Parts of claims 5 and 6 were confirmed or discarded separately; see each claim.

**Headline.** None of the six claims survives as worded. What survives is smaller, and most of it is better grounded than what it replaces.

1. **The "blind on the way" lead is a cluster, not a verse.** It rests on Isa 42:16–18 for the wording and Isa 35:5–8 for the theme, and it is stronger in the Greek than in the Hebrew. "The only text where God leads the blind on a way" holds verse by verse but fails across two verses, because of Jer 31:8–9 in the Hebrew.
2. **Isa 35 stands behind 7:32–37 and no further.** That link is strengthened: in the Hebrew of Isa 35:5 the deaf man's ears are "opened", which is Mark's *Ephphatha*. The proposed sequence from Isa 35 to the arrival at Jerusalem fails.
3. **The "way" is Markan design, but its wording is Exodus's.** Matthew and Luke drop "on the way" at Mark's frames, while keeping ὁδός elsewhere. But the phrase ἐν τῇ ὁδῷ ("on the way") is the continuation of Exod 23:20, the very verse Mark 1:2 begins with, not Isa 40:3. Only Mark's heading puts it under Isaiah.
4. **Isaiah is the most-used source and, with Exodus, the most widely spread. It is not the most evenly spread.** Measured against the movements' lengths, Isaiah is significantly front-weighted, concentrated in the prologue. That supports the *frame* form of the hypothesis and withdraws a claim the *substrate* form had leaned on.

**Two auditors disagreed.** The auditors were blind to each other.

- **Auditor A** preferred Isa 35:5–8 to Isa 42:16 for the healings of the blind, on theme: "eyes opened", and a way that ends at Zion.
- **Auditor B** found that Isa 35 has no detectable verbal signal beyond 7:37. In the contact scan its median rank is 215 against 10:46–52, while Isa 42's is 28. Auditor B preferred Isa 42 on wording: ἀναβλέπω ("to see again"), Mark's own verb at 8:24 and 10:51–52.
- **Adjudication.** Both are right about different criteria. The wording points to Isa 42:16–18, the theme to Isa 35, and neither alone carries the frame. Claim 1's reframing below states it as a cluster for that reason `[I]`.

## Claims Audited

### Claim 1: Isa 42:16 behind the blind-framed Way section (8:22–26; 10:46–52)

**Source:** hypothesis test, Test 4.2. Proposed: moderate.
**Claim:** Isa 42:16 is the only text, in Hebrew or Greek, where God leads the blind on a way. It takes up Isa 40:3–4, and it stands behind the Way section's frame of two healings of the blind, especially 10:52.

**Evidence.**

*Mark* (SBLGNT): ἠκολούθει αὐτῷ ἐν τῇ ὁδῷ ("he was following him on the way", 10:52). τυφλός ("blind") occurs only at 8:22–23 and 10:46–51 `[T]`.

*Isa 42:16:*
- **Greek:** ἄξω τυφλοὺς ἐν ὁδῷ ᾗ οὐκ ἔγνωσαν ("I will lead the blind on a way they did not know"), the same in Swete and Rahlfs. Rahlfs ends τὰ σκολιὰ εἰς εὐθεῖαν ("the crooked into straight"), word for word Isa 40:4 `[T]`.
- **Hebrew:** וְהוֹלַכְתִּי עִוְרִים בְּדֶרֶךְ (WLC). BHS marks the first לֹא יָדָעוּ ("they did not know") as a probable addition.

*The reuse of 40:3–4 is mainly a Greek fact.*
- **In the Greek:** ὁδός ("way"), τρίβος ("path"), σκολιά ("crooked") and εὐθεῖα ("straight") are all shared. ὁδός + τρίβος + σκολι- in one verse occurs in Swete only at Isa 42:16.
- **In the Hebrew:** only דֶּרֶךְ ("way") and מִישׁוֹר ("level ground") are shared. 40:3 has מְסִלָּה ("highway"), not 42:16's נְתִיבָה ("path"), and 40:4 has עָקֹב, not מַעֲקַשִּׁים (both "crooked").
- **Mark sides with the Greek:** Mark 1:3 follows the Greek of 40:3 `[T]`.

*New, Greek only.* Isa 42:18 reads οἱ τυφλοί, ἀναβλέψατε ("you blind, look up"), with ἀναβλέπω ("to look up, see again"), Mark's verb at 8:24 and 10:51–52. Blind + way + ἀναβλέπω within three verses occurs in Swete only at Isa 42:16–18. The Hebrew of 42:18 is הַבִּיטוּ ("look"), without the "again" `[T]`.

**Baseline.**
- **Within one verse.** "Blind" + "way" occurs only at Deut 27:18; 28:29; Isa 42:16. This holds in Swete and in the WLC alike.
- **Within three verses.** Swete adds Isa 35:5–8; 59:8–10; Ps 145:8–9 (Hebrew Ps 146). The WLC adds eight places in all, including **Jer 31:8–9**, where God brings back "the blind and the lame" and leads them "by a straight way".
- **Ordinary vocabulary.** ὁδός + τρίβος together is ordinary poetic pairing (33 Swete verses), so that pair proves nothing on its own.

**Rivals.**
- **Isa 35:5–8**, on theme: eyes opened, a holy way, and the destination Zion (35:10).
- **Jer 31:8–9**, in the Hebrew only.
- **Isa 42:6–7**, opening the eyes of the blind and taking by the hand. Mark's taking by the hand is habitual (1:31; 5:41; 9:27).

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Isaiah is quoted at 1:2–3; Isa 42:1 and Isa 35 are already live in Mark |
| Volume | Weak–Possible | Only τυφλός and ὁδός are shared, with ἀναβλέπω from 42:18 in the Greek. No phrase is shared |
| Recurrence | Possible | Isa 40 and 42 recur in Mark (1:3; 1:11), but no other wording of 42:16 does |
| Thematic Coherence | Possible | "A way they did not know" suits disciples who do not understand. But 42:16 leads the blind without healing them, and has no destination |
| Historical Plausibility | Strong | An Isaianic new exodus is explicit in Mark's opening |
| History of Interpretation | Not checked | — |
| Satisfaction | Possible | It illuminates the section, but no better than Isa 35 |

**Verdict:** Needs reframing.

- **Moderate:** for an Isaianic "blind on the way" cluster — Isa 35:5–8 (theme), Isa 40:3–4 (the way announced), Isa 42:16–18 (the wording, Greek-led) — behind the frame at 8:22–26 and 10:46–52.
- **Uncertain–moderate:** for Isa 42:16 as a source on its own.

**Reasoning:** "The only text" holds verse by verse and fails across two verses. The reuse of 40:3–4 is mainly Greek. The best new support, ἀναβλέπω at 42:18, is also Greek. The cluster is better grounded than the single verse.

**Book-overview action:** No row yet. If one is added, it should read "Isa 35:5–8; 40:3–4; 42:16–18 (Greek-led cluster) | 8:22–26; 10:46–52 | moderate", with Jer 31:8–9 (Hebrew) named as a rival.

### Claim 2: Isa 35:5–10 as the sequence behind Mark 7:32–11:1

**Source:** hypothesis test, Test 4.3. Proposed: uncertain–moderate.
**Claim:** Isa 35's order maps a stretch of Mark: deaf and mute healed, then blind, the way, the redeemed, and Zion. Mark's version runs deaf-mute (7:32–37), blind (8:22–26; 10:46–52), the way (8:27–10:52), ransom (10:45), and Jerusalem (11:1).

**Evidence.**

*The 7:32–37 link is secure, and strengthened.*
- μογιλάλος ("speaking with difficulty") occurs in all of Swete only at Isa 35:6, and in the NT only at Mark 7:32 `[T]`.
- In the Hebrew, Isa 35:5 has the ears of the deaf **opened**: וְאָזְנֵי חֵרְשִׁים תִּפָּתַחְנָה. The LXX has "will hear".
- Mark has Εφφαθα … Διανοίχθητι ("be opened", 7:34) and the ears "opened" (7:35).
- אֹזֶן ("ear") + פָּתַח ("open") share a verse in seven WLC places, and Isa 35:5 is the only one about the deaf `[T]`. The NA28 variants at 7:35 are all forms of "open", so the point stands.
- This is a new case of Mark standing nearer the Hebrew than the Greek, the overview's seventh after the one audit 1 rejected. Triage category 3: the NT's own text, reached through the Hebrew.

*Beyond 7:37 the cluster is weak.*
- τυφλός ("blind") and ὁδός ("way") are common words.
- Isa 35:9 has λελυτρωμένοι ("the redeemed"), and Mark 10:45 has the noun λύτρον ("ransom"). They share a root, not a word.
- In the Hebrew, 35:9 has גְּאוּלִים ("the redeemed") and 35:10 has פְדוּיֵי יְהוָה ("the LORD's ransomed"). The LXX renders the second as συνηγμένοι ("gathered").
- Mark never uses Σιών ("Zion"). A search for it returns hits in Hebrews, 1 Peter and Revelation, so the nil in Mark is real.
- Isa 35 has no holy way in Mark, and its desert and water imagery is absent.

*The order breaks.*
- Isaiah has blind before deaf; Mark has deaf before blind.
- Jerusalem is named at 10:32–33, before the ransom saying.
- Bartimaeus comes after the ransom saying.

**Baseline and tests** (Auditor B).
- **The contact scan.** Isa 35 ranks first against 7:31–37 in four of six runs. Against 8:22–26 its median rank is 55; against 10:46–52, 215.
- **The order test.** A shuffle of Mark's motifs matches Isa 35's order as well as Mark does about one time in ten (p ≈ 0.10). On the coarse version of the claim, about three times in ten (p ≈ 0.29).
- **A rival with the same order.** Jer 31:8–12 in the Hebrew runs blind and lame, a straight way, ransomed and redeemed (פדה, גאל), then Zion. Its Greek loses the blind (Swete Jer 38:8).

**Rivals.**
- **For 10:45, Exodus.** Exod 21:30 and 30:12 have δίδωμι … λύτρα τῆς ψυχῆς αὐτοῦ ("give … a ransom for his life"). That is verbally closer to δοῦναι τὴν ψυχὴν αὐτοῦ λύτρον ("to give his life as a ransom") than anything in Isa 35 or Isa 53.
- **The noun λύτρον in Isaiah** occurs only at 45:13 (Swete) `[T]`.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Isaiah is named at 1:2 and 7:6 |
| Volume | Strong at 7:32–37; Weak beyond | One word unique to Isa 35:6, and the Hebrew "opened" ears. After that, common words and one cognate |
| Recurrence | Weak | No wording of Isa 35 recurs after 7:37 |
| Thematic Coherence | Possible | A return along the way fits a journey. But the Way section's core, that the Son of Man must suffer, is not in Isa 35 |
| Historical Plausibility | Possible | — |
| History of Interpretation | Not checked | — |
| Satisfaction | Weak | Rivals explain the later stages as well or better |

**Verdict:** Needs reframing.

- **High:** Isa 35:5–6 behind 7:32–37, strengthened by the Hebrew "opened" ears.
- **Uncertain:** Isa 35 as a map of 7:32–11:1.

**Reasoning:** The proof rests on one rare word at the start of the stretch. Every later stage shares vocabulary as much with Isa 42, 43 and 51, Jer 31 (Hebrew), or Exodus as with Isa 35. The order is broken in Mark and does not beat chance.

**Book-overview action:**
- Keep the Isa 35:5–6 row at *high*.
- Add the Hebrew "opened" note, and add 7:34–35 to the "nearer the Hebrew" list as a seventh case.
- Add no sequence row.

### Claim 3: Isa 52:7 behind Mark 1:14–15

**Source:** hypothesis test, Test 4.4. Proposed: moderate.
**Claim:** Isa 52:7 stands behind "proclaiming the gospel of God … the kingdom of God has come near". It joins one who brings good news with "your God reigns", and the proposer said that combination shares a verse only there in the Hebrew.

**Evidence.**
- **Mark 1:14–15 (SBLGNT):** κηρύσσων τὸ εὐαγγέλιον τοῦ θεοῦ … ἤγγικεν ἡ βασιλεία τοῦ θεοῦ.
- **Isa 52:7:**
  - Swete and Rahlfs agree: εὐαγγελιζομένου … Βασιλεύσει σου ὁ θεός ("bringing good news … your God will reign").
  - WLC: מְבַשֵּׂר … מָלַךְ אֱלֹהָיִךְ ("one bringing good news … your God reigns").
  - BHS has no note at the verse.
- **Mark shares almost no wording with it.** Mark has the noun εὐαγγέλιον ("gospel"), his own habitual term (7× in Mark). Isaiah has the participle. Mark has the noun βασιλεία ("kingdom"); the LXX has the verb.

**Baseline.**
- **The proposer's rarity claim holds.** "Bring good news" + "reign" share a verse only at Isa 52:7, in the WLC and in Swete alike. The positive control passed.
- **The second half of 1:15 is stock LXX idiom.** ἤγγικεν ("has drawn near") + καιρός ("time"): 8 verses of Swete. ἐγγίζω + βασιλεία: none.
- **The contact scan.** Isa 52 is absent from the top ten in all runs. Isa 40 and Isa 61 are present in several.

**Rivals.**
- **Isa 40:9–10.** The herald of good news twice, and "his arm rules". It is the only other place where the "good news" root comes within two verses of the "rule" root (WLC), and it is the chapter Mark quotes at 1:3.
- **Isa 61:1.** κηρύσσω ("proclaim") + εὐαγγελίζομαι ("bring good news") in one verse. Elsewhere in Swete this pair occurs only in Psalms of Solomon 11:2, which already reads Isa 40 and 52 together.
- **Ps 96:2, 10** (Swete 95). Proclaim salvation; "the LORD reigns".
- **Lam 4:19 and Dan 7:22, for "the time".**
  - Lam 4:19 is the only Swete verse with ἤγγικεν ("has drawn near"), πληρόω ("fulfil") and καιρός ("time"). It is a verse of doom.
  - Dan 7:22 has ὁ καιρὸς ἔφθασεν καὶ τὴν βασιλείαν κατέσχον οἱ ἅγιοι ("the time arrived and the saints took possession of the kingdom"), in both Greek texts.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Isaiah is quoted at 1:2–3 |
| Volume | Weak | One root in a different form, and the kingdom idea in a different word class |
| Recurrence | Possible | The words recur in Mark, but as his own vocabulary |
| Thematic Coherence | Strong | A herald announcing God's reign is exactly 1:14–15 |
| Historical Plausibility | Strong | Psalms of Solomon 11 shows the Isaianic herald reused before Mark |
| History of Interpretation | Not checked | — |
| Satisfaction | Possible | It explains "gospel" with "kingdom", but not "the time is fulfilled" |

**Verdict:** Needs reframing.

- **Moderate:** for an Isaianic herald complex — Isa 40:9–10; 52:7; 61:1 — with 52:7 the anchor for God's reign.
- **Uncertain–moderate:** for Isa 52:7 as a single source.

**Reasoning:** The rare pairing is real in both texts, but Mark shares almost no wording with it. The herald complex is better supported than any one verse, and it begins in the chapter Mark has just quoted.

**Book-overview action:** No row yet. If one is added: "Isa 40:9–10; 52:7; 61:1 (herald complex) | 1:14–15 | moderate", with Dan 7:22 flagged as a candidate for "the time … the kingdom".

### Claim 4: Isa 49:24–25 / Isa 53:12 behind Mark 3:27

**Source:** hypothesis test, Test 4.4. Proposed: uncertain.
**Claim:** Binding the strong man and plundering his house recalls Isa 49:24–25, prey taken from the mighty, and/or Isa 53:12, where the Servant "will divide the spoil of the strong".

**Evidence.**
- **Mark 3:27 (SBLGNT):** τοῦ ἰσχυροῦ … τὰ σκεύη αὐτοῦ διαρπάσαι ἐὰν μὴ πρῶτον τὸν ἰσχυρὸν δήσῃ ("the strong man's … to plunder his goods unless he first binds the strong man").
- **Isa 49:24–25, Greek** (Swete and Rahlfs): παρὰ γίγαντος σκῦλα … παρὰ ἰσχύοντος ("spoils from a giant … from the one who is strong"). Only the ἰσχυ- root ("strong") is shared. There is no binding, no house and no σκεῦος ("goods").
- **Isa 49:24–25, Hebrew:** מִגִּבּוֹר מַלְקוֹחַ ("prey from the mighty man"). BHS reads עָרִיץ ("tyrant") at 49:24 with 1QIsaᵃ, the Syriac and the Vulgate.
- **Isa 53:12, Greek:** τῶν ἰσχυρῶν μεριεῖ σκῦλα ("he will divide the spoils of the strong"). But σκῦλα ("spoils") is not Mark's σκεύη ("goods").
- **Luke 11:22 is the closer form.** It has τὰ σκῦλα αὐτοῦ διαδίδωσιν ("distributes his spoils"). If Isa 53:12 stands behind the saying, it shows in Luke, not in Mark.
- **Inside Mark.** ἰσχυρός ("strong") occurs only at 1:7, ὁ ἰσχυρότερος ("the stronger one"), and at 3:27. That chain is Mark's own `[T]`.

**Baseline.**
- **Greek (Swete).** ἰσχυ- + διαρπάζω ("plunder"): none. διαρπάζω + σκεῦος ("goods"): Nah 2:10 only.
- **Hebrew (WLC).** גִּבּוֹר ("mighty man") + מַלְקוֹחַ ("prey"): Isa 49:24, 25 only. No Isaiah text pairs a strong one with binding.

**Rivals.**
- **For the binding**, which the saying hinges on: Tob 3:17; 8:3 (Raphael binds the demon) and 1 Enoch 10:4, 12; 13:1 ("Bind Azazel").
- **Jer 50:33–34** (Swete 27:33–34): the captors hold the people fast, and "their Redeemer is strong" (ὁ λυτρούμενος αὐτοὺς ἰσχυρός).

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Isa 53 is live in Mark |
| Volume | Weak | No phrase is shared; Mark's key words are absent |
| Recurrence | Possible | Isa 53 recurs in Mark; Isa 49 does not |
| Thematic Coherence | Possible | Rescue of captives fits the exorcisms, but Isaiah has no binding |
| Historical Plausibility | Possible | The binding traditions fit at least as well |
| History of Interpretation | Not checked | — |
| Satisfaction | Weak | It explains the plunder, not the binding |

**Verdict:** Uncertain — flag for research.

- **Isa 49:24–25:** uncertain–moderate on concept, through the Hebrew.
- **Isa 53:12:** not to be cited for Mark 3:27 on lexical grounds.

**Reasoning:** Mark shares no phrase with either text, and the binding comes from outside Isaiah.

**Book-overview action:** None. Keep it as a flagged candidate, named with Jer 50:33–34 and Tob 8 / 1 Enoch 10.

### Claim 5: The "way" (ὁδός) as Markan design anchored in Isa 40:3

**Source:** hypothesis test, Test 4.1. Proposed: (a)–(c) high as data; (d) moderate.

The claim has four parts, tested separately.

| Part | Claim | Result | Verdict |
|----|----------|---------------------|--------|
| (a) | 7 of Mark's 16 uses of ὁδός fall in 8:22–10:52, which is 117 of 661 verses (p ≈ 0.014) | **Arithmetic correct** (SBLGNT). It is p ≈ 0.018 by words, and about 0.04 if 9:33–34 is counted as one place. **But it is not evidence of design on its own:** 28 of the 109 lemmas found in 10–30 verses of Mark cluster as strongly in some movement, seven of them in the Way section (re-run here). Words cluster where their subject is | **Confirmed with nuance**, as data only |
| (b) | At the Way-section parallels, Matthew and Luke keep "on the way" once in ten (Matt 20:17) | **Confirmed.** 10:46 was wrongly left out: both keep παρὰ τὴν ὁδόν ("by the road", Matt 20:30; Luke 18:35). **Strengthened by a control** (Auditor B; not re-run here): at the other parallels they keep ὁδός 14 times in 16. So they drop the word specifically at Mark's frames (Fisher test p ≈ 0.0002) | **Confirmed**, and stronger than proposed |
| (c) | Mark's only scriptural anchor for the word is 1:2–3 | **True for quotations, but incomplete.** Exod 23:20 in Swete, the verse 1:2 begins with, goes on ἵνα φυλάξῃ σε **ἐν τῇ ὁδῷ** ("to guard you *on the way*"). That is the Way section's own framing phrase. Isa 40:3 has τὴν ὁδὸν κυρίου ("the way of the Lord"). Mark 12:14 adds τὴν ὁδὸν τοῦ θεοῦ ("the way of God") | **Confirmed with nuance** |
| (d) | So the Way section is designed as Isaiah's "way of the Lord" | **The journey plot does not explain the cluster.** Travel words do not cluster in the Way section: ἀναβαίνω ("go up") p = 0.52, ὑπάγω ("go") p = 0.55, Ἱεροσόλυμα p = 0.58 (Auditor B). The design is in Mark's framing phrase. **But the phrase is Exodus's wording, not Isaiah's.** Mark never says "the way of the Lord" in chapters 8–10. Only the heading at 1:2 ties the composite to Isaiah | **Needs reframing** |

**Verdict on (d):**

### Claim 6: Isaiah is the most widely and most evenly spread OT source in Mark

**Source:** hypothesis test, Test 2. Proposed: moderate.

**The proposer's counting was inconsistent** (Auditor B). Rows shared between two sources were credited only to the first-named. Zech 14:4 was counted once, although it lists three places. The cup-of-wrath row was treated unevenly. The auditor could reproduce Isaiah's 15 loci and its concentration score of 0.21 only by guessing these choices.

**The benchmark was misread.** The hypothesis test compared Isaiah's concentration score (Herfindahl index) with 0.29, the score of a distribution exactly proportional to the movements' lengths, and read "below 0.29" as "evenly spread". It is not.

- A score below 0.29 means Isaiah is *over*-represented in the short movements.
- For 15 loci placed at random in proportion to length, the *expected* score is about 0.34.
- Tested against length directly, Isaiah's distribution is **significantly out of proportion** (p ≈ 0.01–0.03 under most counts). It has three loci in the 13-verse prologue, about ten times its share, and is thin in Galilee.
- Exodus never departs from a length-proportional spread (p ≈ 0.43–0.68).

**Robustness** (Auditor B's recount; "Mvts" = movements touched; "H" = concentration score, lower is more spread):

| Counting choice | Isaiah loci / Mvts / H | Most loci | Widest | Fits length best |
|----------|------|-----|------|-----|
| Shared rows to the first source | 19 / 5 / 0.22 | Isaiah | Isaiah = Exodus | Exodus |
| Shared rows to every source | 19 / 5 / 0.22 | Isaiah | Isaiah = Psalms = Exodus | Exodus |
| One per row | 14 / 5 / 0.22 | Isaiah | three-way tie | Exodus |
| Each psalm a separate source | 19 / 5 / 0.22 | Isaiah (lead grows) | Isaiah = Exodus | Exodus |
| Galilee/Way boundary at 8:26 or 8:30 | unchanged | Isaiah | Isaiah = Exodus | Exodus |
| Shared loci dropped | 13 / 5 / 0.22 | Isaiah | Isaiah | Isaiah |
| High-confidence loci only | 8 / 4 / 0.31 | **Psalms (9)** | **Exodus (6 / 5)** | Exodus |

**A circularity, too.** Two of Isaiah's three prologue loci (1:10; 1:11) rest on Hebrew-only contacts. The overview had already given Isaiah a raised prior as a live source, so part of the count leans on itself.

**Verdict:** Needs reframing.

| Part | Verdict | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| "Most-used source" | **Confirmed with nuance** | moderate. It fails on high-confidence loci only, where the Psalms lead 9–8 |
| "Most widely spread" | **Joint only**, with Exodus and often with the Psalms | moderate. It fails on high-confidence loci |
| "Most evenly spread" | **Discard as worded** | — |

**Replacement wording** (from Auditor B, adopted here): *Isaiah is Mark's most frequently used source and, with Exodus, the most widely distributed: present in every movement but the tomb, and weighted towards the prologue.*

**Bearing on the hypothesis.** This cuts in two directions.

- **It weakens the substrate form.** The hypothesis test's "most evenly spread" is withdrawn.
- **It strengthens the frame form.** Isaiah's over-representation in the prologue is significant against length, which is what a book that names Isaiah as its lens would show.

**Book-overview action:** Replace the Isaiah row's "the most-used source in Mark" with the replacement wording above.

## Tool Problems Found

1. **The contact scanner's stem matching misses and mis-credits words.** It failed to find ἀναβλέπω ("look up") and δέω ("bind") in Swete, and it missed ἤγγικεν at Lam 4:19. It credited false friends: ὀδόντες ("teeth") as ὁδός ("way"), βασιλεύς ("king") as βασιλεία ("kingdom"), and εὐθεῖα ("straight") as εὐθύς ("immediately"). It also fails with an error on a Mark passage that crosses a chapter boundary. Both auditors searched by hand where it mattered, and quoted no rank that did not hold across its runs.
2. **The SBLGNT lemma index files δεῖ ("it is necessary") under δέω ("bind").** Any count of "bind" in Mark taken from the index is inflated. The true verses are 3:27; 5:3–4; 6:17; 11:2, 4; 15:1, 7.
3. **The verse-display routine aligns Jeremiah by number, not by text.** For Jer 31 it prints Swete's Jer 31, which is an oracle on Moab; the matching Greek is at Swete Jer 38.
4. **The Rahlfs Isaiah export is running paragraphs, not one verse per line.** Rahlfs readings can be checked by string, but Rahlfs counts cannot be run.

These were flagged here; three of the four were fixed the same evening — see the Addendum.

## Method Lessons (candidate skill amendments, drafted not applied)

- **A clustering p-value needs a field baseline.** A word concentrated in one section at p ≈ 0.01 is unremarkable if a quarter of words of similar frequency are concentrated somewhere as strongly. The sweep rule already asks for a chance baseline for design claims. This run shows it applies to a single word's distribution too, and that the test is one command.
- **A concentration index is read against its simulated expectation, not against a length-proportional figure.** The hypothesis test's "0.21 against 0.29" was misread as evenness. The honest test is goodness of fit to section length.
- **A design claim about a phrase must check the whole verse of every text quoted alongside it.** Exod 23:20's ἐν τῇ ὁδῷ was missed because the check stopped where Mark's quotation stopped.

## Addendum: Tools Fixed and the Scan Re-run (6 November 2026)

Three of the four tool problems above were fixed the same evening. No verdict in this audit changes, but one adjudication gains weight.

**What was fixed.**

1. **The contact scanner's stem matching** (problem 1). Backed up beside it, dated 6 November.
   - Short noun and adjective stems are now matched as a stem plus a declension ending. ὁδός ("way") now finds ὁδῷ and no longer catches ὀδόντες ("teeth"). βασιλεία ("kingdom") no longer catches βασιλεύς ("king").
   - Verb stems now allow for the future and aorist consonant changes (ἀναβλέψατε, "look up"), vowel augments (ἤγγικεν, "has drawn near"), internal augments (ἀνέβλεψεν, "he looked up"), and reduplication (λελυτρωμένοι, "the redeemed").
   - δέω ("bind") is given its own forms.
   - Spans across chapters (8:22–10:52) now run.
   - The two positive controls still pass:
     - Heb 1:1–14 ranks Ps 44 LXX first (median 1), with Ps 101 LXX in the top ten.
     - Col 1:15–20 ranks Ps 88 LXX first, by median, in five of the six runs; it was first in all six before. That documented control should now read "first in five of six".
2. **The SBLGNT index fault** (problem 2) is worked around inside the scanner. Words filed under δέω that are really δεῖ ("it is necessary") or δέομαι ("ask") are re-assigned. Adverbial εὐθύς ("immediately"), which the index tags as an adjective, is no longer matched against εὐθεῖα ("straight"). The index itself is unchanged.
3. **The Rahlfs Isaiah export** (problem 4) is now also split into one verse per line, beside the export as `01-Isaiah-LXX-verses.txt`. The export itself is unchanged and remains the copy of record.
