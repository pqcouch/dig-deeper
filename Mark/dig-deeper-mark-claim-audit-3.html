Claim Audit 3: Mark — Single Contacts and Field Claims from the Isaiah Work

Claims audited. Thirteen claims from the Isaiah hypothesis work and the two Logos rounds (mark-logos-answers-round-1-assessment, mark-logos-answers-round-2-assessment):

  • nine single contacts, at Mark 1:15; 3:27; 3:28–30; 4:12; 6:50; 7:34–35; 8:22–10:52; 10:45;
  • four field and design claims, at 1:10; 8:27–10:52; 4:35–5:43; 16:8.

Date. 9 November 2026. Purpose. To clear the queue before any of these enters the Mark overview or a sermon. Several came from Watts, and some from this work’s own assessments. Both kinds are tested the same way. Texts.

  • Mark: SBLGNT, counted in 1:1–16:8.
  • Greek OT: Swete, accent-stripped, every stem hit read.
  • Rahlfs Isaiah, one verse per line, with Swete’s Isaiah apparatus.
  • Hebrew: the WLC, by lemma.
  • The BHS and NA28 apparatus exports.

Companion. Claim audit 4 (the Galilee inventory) was run at the same time.

How This Audit Was Run

Two blind auditors, per your standing rule. Auditor A took the nine single contacts; Auditor B took the four field and design claims.

  • Each auditor was given the bare claims and the proposer’s ratings.
  • Neither could open the hypothesis test, the earlier audits, the Logos records, Watts, or the Isaiah reports.
  • They were told to break the claims from the corpus alone. History of Interpretation was “Not checked” for both; the Logos rounds had filled it for some claims, and that is added below where it applies.

Checking the checkers. These decisive findings were re-run here and reproduced:

  • Isa 35:5 in Swete has ἀνοιχθήσονται (“will be opened”), of the eyes, in the same verse as the ears.
  • Isa 42:20 opens the ears in both languages: the Hebrew פָּקוֹחַ אָזְנַיִם, the Greek ἠνοιγμένα τὰ ὦτα.
  • Rahlfs Isa 56:1 reads ἤγγισεν (“drew near”). Swete’s apparatus gives ἤγγισεν for A and Q, so the perfect ἤγγικεν (“has drawn near”) is Vaticanus’s reading.
  • “Give” + “ransom” (נָתַן + כֹּפֶר) in one verse, in the WLC: Exod 21:30; 30:12; Isa 43:3; Ps 49:8.
  • Rahlfs Isa 43:4 has ἀνθρώπους πολλούς (“many men”); Swete does not.
  • Exod 14:13 in Swete has Θαρσεῖτε (“take heart”) at the sea.
  • Isa 22:14 has οὐκ ἀφεθήσεται (“it will not be forgiven”).
  • Ps 51:13 has “your Holy Spirit”, so “his Holy Spirit” stands only at Isa 63:10, 11. An earlier assessment listed Ps 51:13 loosely; this corrects it.
  • Dan 10:7 (Theodotion) reads ἔκστασις μεγάλη … ἔφυγον ἐν φόβῳ (“great astonishment … they fled in fear”).
  • Isa 36:22 and 37:1 render קָרַע (“tear”) with σχίζω (“tear”), of clothes.
  • Isa 65:4 is the only WLC verse with קֶבֶר + חֲזִיר (“tomb” + “swine”).
  • Lev 15:25 is the only Swete verse with ῥύσει αἵματος (“a flow of blood”).

Headline. The audit cut down this work’s own claims as hard as Watts’s.

  • The “own finding” from round 2, Ephphatha, falls. The Greek of Isa 35:5 already has “will be opened” in the same verse, and Greek Isaiah opens ears three times besides (42:20; 48:8; 50:5). So the Hebrew is not needed to explain Mark’s “opened”. The point survives only as a note: the Hebrew of 35:5 has “opened” of the ears, but Mark need not have read it there.
  • Two of Watts’s extensions fall: Isa 63:17 at 4:12, and the four-episode design of 4:35–5:43.
  • One of Watts’s extensions stands, and two auditors found it independently: Isa 65:3–5 behind the Gerasene demoniac (see audit 4, claim 5).

What survives at moderate or above:

  • 10:45 as a combination: Exodus vocabulary, Isa 53’s “many”, Isa 43:3–4, and the Ps 49 / Mark 8:37 foil.
  • 5:1–20 from Isa 65:3–5.
  • The 1:10 block reframed (uncertain–moderate, unchanged in rating).

Claims Audited — Single Contacts (Auditor A)

A1. Hebrew Isa 35:5 behind Ephphatha (7:34–35)

Finding. The claim was that Mark’s “be opened” follows the Hebrew of Isa 35:5 (ears “opened”), against the Greek (“will hear”). It fails on three grounds.

  • The Greek of 35:5 already has the verb. In Swete and Rahlfs, 35:5 reads τότε ἀνοιχθήσονται ὀφθαλμοὶ τυφλῶν (“then the eyes of the blind will be opened”). A reader of the Greek finds “opened” in the very verse.
  • Greek Isaiah opens ears elsewhere. ἀνοίγω (“open”) with ὦτα (“ears”) stands at Isa 42:20; 48:8; 50:5, and at 1 Kgs 8:52. The earlier claim, that “opened ears” is an idiom of the Hebrew Isaiah alone, is true of the Hebrew and irrelevant to the argument, because the Greek has it too.
  • The Aramaic proves nothing. “Be opened” is the ordinary Aramaic word for the act, so a shared root shows only that Jesus spoke Aramaic.

Mark’s noun at 7:35 is ἀκοαί (“hearing”). If anything, that picks up the Greek’s ἀκούσονται (“will hear”).

Verdict: Needs reframing. Uncertain.

Action:

  • Do not add 7:34–35 to the overview’s list of places where Mark stands nearer the Hebrew.
  • Withdraw “this work’s own finding” from the round-2 assessment.
  • The Isa 35:5–6 link at 7:32–37 stays high, on μογιλάλος (“speaking with difficulty”) and 7:37.

A2. Isa 56:1 behind ἤγγικεν (“has drawn near”, 1:15)

Finding.

  • The form is a Vaticanus reading. Rahlfs, Alexandrinus and Marchalianus read ἤγγισεν. The Hebrew has the adjective קְרוֹבָה (“near”), not a verb.
  • Closer wording elsewhere. Lam 4:19 (Swete; Hebrew 4:18) is the only verse with “drawn near” + “time” + “fulfilled”, in both languages. But it is a doom text.
  • The good-news-of-God’s-reign half is Isa 52:7 (audit 2).
  • Another rival. The NT-to-LXX scanner ranks Jonah 3 (proclaim, believe, repent) at median 2.

Verdict: Needs reframing. Uncertain.

Action: Present 1:14–15 as a cluster:

  • Isa 52:7, with 40:9 and 61:1 (the herald complex, moderate);
  • Dan 7:22 for “the time … the kingdom” (A6, uncertain);
  • Lam 4:18–19 for the closest wording, which reverses a doom formula.

Drop any argument from the form ἤγγικεν.

A3. The ransom saying (10:45)

Finding.

  • λύτρον (“ransom”) is Torah vocabulary, but Exodus has the transaction the other way round. There money ransoms the life; in Mark the life is the ransom.
  • The shape of the saying is Isa 53:
    • “many” (53:11–12, Greek and Hebrew);
    • “his life” (53:12 Greek; 53:10 Hebrew, אָשָׁם נַפְשׁוֹ, “his life a guilt offering”);
    • “serving many” (53:11 Greek, εὖ δουλεύοντα πολλοῖς).
  • Isa 43:3–4 is a new rival the earlier work missed.
    • It is the only WLC passage with “give” + “ransom” + “in place of” (תַּחַת, Mark’s ἀντί), with “your life” following.
    • Rahlfs 43:4 has “many men” (ἀνθρώπους πολλούς).
  • Mark 8:37 is the foil inside Mark. It reads “what can a man give in exchange for his life?” — Ps 49’s thought in Exodus’s syntax. So 10:45 answers 8:37.

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. Moderate for the combination. On the auditor’s reading Isa 53 has the better claim, against the round-1 assessment’s emphasis on Exodus.

Reconciliation. Both points stand. The wording “give … a ransom for his life” is Exodus’s, and the round-1 assessment was right that the Assistant’s “Isa 53 is closer in wording” was wrong on the noun. The shape (his own life, for many, by a servant) is Isa 53. Read with Isa 43:3–4 and 8:37, the saying combines them.

A4. Isa 41:10; 43:1–5 behind “it is I; do not fear” (6:50)

Finding.

  • The pairing is not Isaiah’s own. “Fear not” with a first-person pronoun occurs in 16 WLC verses: Genesis 6, Isaiah 4, Jeremiah 4. In the Greek, ἐγώ εἰμι next to “fear not” occurs at Gen 26:24; 46:3; 50:19; Jer 1:8 and elsewhere.
  • Isaiah 43 is distinctive. It joins “fear not” (43:1, 5), passing through the waters (43:2) and the absolute “I am he” (אֲנִי הוּא, 43:10, 13). The absolute “I am he” is Isaian, plus Deut 32:39.
  • Exod 14:13 supplies θαρσεῖτε. It is the only Swete verse with “take heart” at the sea.

Verdict: Needs reframing. Uncertain–moderate, as Isa 43:1–13 with Exod 14:13, beside the overview’s existing Exod 33–34 and Job 9 rows.

A5. Jer 31:8–9 as a rival at the healings of the blind

Finding.

  • A real tie in the Hebrew. Blind + lame + way within three verses occurs only at Isa 35:5–8 and Jer 31:8–9.
  • But the Greek of Jeremiah loses the blind. It reads “at the feast of Passover”.
  • Mark has no lame man here.
  • Mark’s verb ἀναβλέπω (“look up, see again”) matches Greek Isaiah only: Isa 42:18; 61:1.

Verdict: Discard as a rival. Footnote at most.

A6. Dan 7:22 behind 1:15

Finding. Daniel holds 8 of the 9 canonical Swete verses that join καιρός (“time”) and βασιλεία (“kingdom”), and Dan 7 is live in Mark (13:26; 14:62). But only those two common words are shared with Dan 7:22.

Verdict: Uncertain — flag for research. Conceptual, beside Isa 52:7.

A7. Tobit 8 / 1 Enoch 10 at 3:27

Finding. No single text supplies Mark’s wording.

  • δέω (“bind”) a spirit: only in Tobit (3:17; 8:3) and 1 Enoch (10:4; 13:1; 22:11).
  • The logic of taking the prey from the mighty: Isa 49:24–25, most clearly in the Hebrew. גִּבּוֹר (“mighty”) + מַלְקוֹחַ (“prey”) occurs only there.
  • “Goods” + “plunder”: only Nah 2:10.
  • Watts and France (round 2) agree there is no verbal link to Isaiah.

Verdict: Needs reframing. Uncertain, as Isaiah’s frame plus the binding motif. History of Interpretation: strong (round 2).

A8. Isa 63:10 behind 3:28–30

Finding.

  • Only one phrase is shared. “His Holy Spirit” stands only at Isa 63:10, 11. Mark shares no verb: Isaiah has “grieve” or “provoke”, Mark “blaspheme” and “forgiveness”.
  • The unforgivable sin is closer elsewhere:
    • Num 15:25–31: forgivable sins against the high-handed sinner, with παροξύνει (“provokes”) at 15:30, the same verb as Isa 63:10 in the Greek;
    • Isa 22:14: οὐκ ἀφεθήσεται (“it will not be forgiven”);
    • Wis 1:5–6: the holy spirit and the blasphemer.

Verdict: Uncertain — flag for research. Uncertain, down from uncertain–moderate. Group it with Num 15:30–31 and Isa 22:14 as a cluster for the unforgivable sin, and do not preach Isa 63:10 as the source.

A9. Isa 63:17 behind 4:12

Finding. Mark 4:12 has no “heart” and no hardening verb. Isa 6:10, which 4:12 quotes, accounts for everything. Mark’s own hardening words (πωρόω, σκληροκαρδία) are not Isa 63:17’s.

Verdict: Discard.

Claims Audited — Field and Design (Auditor B)

B1. Isa 63:11–14 behind 1:10, reopened

Finding.

  • The Vaticanus route is fragile. “Sea” is the uncorrected first hand only. Both of Vaticanus’s own correctors and the other great codices read “land”. It should not carry the case.
  • The case is better made without it, across the whole block 63:11–19:
    • the Hebrew 63:11, “sea” and the Holy Spirit set among them;
    • the Greek 63:12, “water”, in every witness;
    • the Greek 63:14, the Spirit came down, in every witness. It is the only Swete verse with the Lord’s Spirit as subject of καταβαίνω (“come down”);
    • the Hebrew 63:19, “tear” and “come down”.
  • Audit 1’s objection is corrected. “In the Hebrew of 63:14 it is cattle that go down” was overstated. תֵרֵד (“goes down”), רוּחַ (“spirit”) and בְּהֵמָה (“cattle”) are all feminine. Only the accents tie the verb to the cattle, so the consonants allow the Greek’s construal [T].
  • The verbs. Isaiah’s verb is causative, “bring up”. But its Hebrew root (עָלָה) is the one Mark’s ἀναβαίνω (“come up”) normally renders.
  • The wilderness follows in both. Isa 63:13–14 goes on, the Spirit “led them through the wilderness”, as Mark 1:12 goes on, the Spirit “drives him out into the wilderness”.
  • The baseline. The cluster of spirit + come down + come up + water is not rare: 4–7 places in Swete; in the WLC Isa 63, 2 Sam 22 / Ps 18, Ps 107, Prov 30. But Isa 63 is the only one with an exodus setting and the divine Spirit descending.

Verdict: Needs reframing. Uncertain–moderate, unchanged in rating, on a sounder basis. History of Interpretation: strong (Watts, round 2).

Action:

  • Extend the overview’s Isa 63:19 row at 1:10 to “Isa 63:11–19 (Hebrew 63:11, 19; Greek 63:12–14)”.
  • Add the note “→ 1:12, the Spirit and the wilderness”.
  • Mention Vaticanus’s first hand only as a curiosity that was corrected.

B2. Isaiah’s “fear not” behind 16:8

Finding.

  • The fact about Isaiah is real. אַל + a second-person form of יָרֵא (“do not fear”): 13 verses in Isaiah, a rate of 10.1 per 1,000 verses against a canon mean of 3.2 (probability about 0.0004). It is the highest of any book over 250 verses.
  • But nothing at 16:8 points to Isaiah.
    • Mark’s fear rate matches Luke’s.
    • τρόμος and ἔκστασις (“trembling” and “astonishment”) never stand together in Swete, and Isaiah has no ἔκστασις at all.
    • Mark’s own sequence explains the ending: “do not be alarmed” (16:6), then fear (16:8), as at 4:40–41, 5:15, 5:33 and 9:32.
  • The stronger rival is Dan 10 (Theodotion).
    • 10:7 has astonishment, flight and fear in one verse;
    • 10:5 has the man clothed in linen;
    • 10:12, 19 have “fear not”.

Verdict: Uncertain — flag for research. Low for 16:8.

Action:

  • Keep “Isaiah is the ‘fear not’ book” as a fact about Isaiah.
  • Record Watts’s reading as Watts’s (round 1), not as a link.
  • Add Dan 10 (Theodotion) as a candidate for 16:5–8, unaudited.

B3. Exod 23:20’s omitted clause as the source of “on the way”

Finding.

  • The “Way” framing is real. Five of the six uses of ἐν τῷ ὁδῷ fall within 8:27–10:52 (chance probability about 0.0008).
  • But the mechanism fails on three counts:
    • Mark 1:2 is not cut short. It is spliced: Malachi’s clause replaces Exodus’s continuation.
    • ἐν τῇ ὁδῷ is a stock idiom: 71 verses in Swete.
    • Mark cuts quotations short routinely: 14:27; 15:34; 4:12.
  • Isa 42:16 (“I will lead the blind on a way”) is the sharper match at 10:52.

Verdict: Needs reframing; the mechanism is discarded. Low.

Action:

  • Link the Way section’s “on the way” to the way of 1:2–3 as a whole.
  • Drop “the narrative supplies the omitted clause”.
  • History of Interpretation: Kelber and Swartley (round 1) read the phrase through Exodus. This audit finds that reading possible as theme, not as source.

B4. Mark 4:35–5:43 built on Isa 63:7–65:25

Finding. Only one of the four episodes has distinctive shared wording.

  • The demoniac (5:1–20) and Isa 65:3–5: strong.
    • Tombs + swine in one verse occurs in the WLC only at Isa 65:4.
    • The Greek adds “to the demons” (65:3).
    • Mark’s “dwelling among the tombs” is nearer the Hebrew “sit” (יֹשְׁבִים) than the Greek “sleep”.
  • The storm (4:35–41): Jonah 1 is far closer. “They feared a great fear” (Jonah 1:10) is already in the overview at high.
  • The woman (5:25–34): ῥύσει αἵματος (“a flow of blood”) is Lev 15:25, uniquely.
  • The girl (5:35–43): 1 Kgs 17 and 2 Kgs 4 are closer than Isa 65:20.
  • The block test is tailored. The motifs were chosen after the fact, and Isa 63–65 meets them in senses that do not match Mark’s episodes. A control passage, Mark 6:30–56, also finds four such blocks.

Verdict: The design is discarded. Isa 65:3–5 at 5:1–20 is kept at moderate. Audit 4’s auditor reached the same verdict independently.

Tool Note

Both auditors found that the NT-to-LXX scanner does not discriminate on long narrative passages, where Tobit and 2 Maccabees rise to the top. It also failed its positive control on Mark 1:9–11: Ps 2 and Isa 42 ranked low. And it still misses some augmented forms (κατέβη, ἔφυγον). Its ranks were not used for any verdict here.

Proposal (not applied):

  • The skill’s guidance should say the scanner is for passages of up to about 15 verses.
  • A Mark 1:9–11 run should be added to its positive controls, as a known failure to be fixed.
