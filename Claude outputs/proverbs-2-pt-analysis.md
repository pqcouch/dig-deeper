# Proverbs 2 — PT Analysis and Revision Notes

**Sermon 3 in the Proverbs series** · pulpit text NIV84 · checked against the NIV84 Logos exports in `_texts/` (every Scripture quotation now follows them word for word) and against `dig-deeper-proverbs-2` and `point-purpose-proverbs-2` (v3, 18 September), whose `[T]` corpus checks stand behind every count the sermon makes.

## 1. Opening summary

The exegesis behind this draft is excellent, and the sermon is built on the right foundation: the chapter’s own grammar (three “ifs” → “then” → “for” → “then” → two rescues), with 2:6 as its centre. But the draft is the point-purpose plan read aloud rather than a sermon preached from it. Its first page announces the Purpose Statement to the congregation (“my hope is that… I will have persuaded you… to move out of either doing nothing… into asking”) and lists the differentiated applications before a verse has been opened. The body is full of exegete’s findings (“the only two occurrences”, “the only occurrence of the word… in Proverbs”, “the consensus is…”), and some of them carry no pastoral weight. Then it stops at Philippians 2 with no conclusion, no “us now”, and no answer to the big question. The direction is right; it needs a sermon shape around it.

## 2. What is working well

- **Cooper — the author’s choices, not the events behind them.** One long sentence; the two “then you will understand” clauses (vv.5, 9); “store up” in v.1 and v.7; “right and just and fair” shared only by 1:3 and 2:9; the matched rescues of vv.12 and 16; the rare verb at v.22. These are all things the author has *done*, and a careful reader can see them.
- **Davies — the purpose heard rightly.** The draft has found the chapter’s purpose: to move people out of both passivity and self-reliance into *asking*. Landing on the middle condition (v.3) and using James 1:5 is exactly right.
- **The anti-moralism engine.** The draft keeps the order clear: “the effort isn’t in order to make Him give.” It also refuses to quote v.6 on its own. That is the sermon’s load-bearing sentence, and it is already there.
- **Pastoral care.** Defusing v.19 with 28:13 and refusing to read vv.21–22 as a promise of a comfortable life both protect real people.
- **Styles — Christ reached from the text.** 1 Cor 1:30 answers the *specific* problem v.7 creates (wisdom stored “for the upright”). Col 2:3 gives v.4’s digging a destination, and 2 Cor 4:6 picks up v.5’s “knowledge of God”. Together they make a trajectory, not a bouncing ball.

## 3. Where the framework is not fully applied

**(a) The Purpose Statement is preached instead of achieved** *(harmful).* Davies’ “so what” belongs at the end, *landed*, not announced at the start. Telling a congregation what you hope to persuade them of, and then listing their four categories, turns the introduction into a briefing. *Revision:* the introduction raises the Question (two ways we go wrong — earning or waiting, and neither asks). The four groups (hard-working, waiting, door-shut, not-yet-Christian) are moved to the conclusion, where they now land on people.

**(b) No “them then”** *(missed opportunity).* The son of 2:1 is never pictured, and the most important context, 1:28’s closed door, arrives halfway through as a technical note. Keep Honest 3 in the point-purpose asks for 1:28 to be read *first*. *Revision:* a father at the door of the family home, a son who has just heard Wisdom say “they will call to me but I will not answer”, and the question “is it ever too late?”. Chapter 2 becomes the father’s answer, and the son returns in the conclusion.

**(c) No conclusion, no “us now”** *(harmful).* The draft ends on Philippians 2:12–13, a supporting text, without returning to the big question or landing the call. *Revision:* a short conclusion, “So, how would we know?”, answers the big question (“not by checking ourselves; by receiving”), lands the four applications, brings back the illustration, and ends on v.6.

**(d) Findings that do no work.** Davies’ test is whether every part drives towards the one purpose. Some statistics do (“store up” ×2 → the treasure was hidden for him before he looked). Others don’t: the sole occurrence of “faithful ones” (v.8) goes nowhere, and the point-purpose itself warns against building on its plural. *Revision:* counts kept only where they carry the argument. The v.8 note is replaced by “five things God does in three verses”, the gate’s own anti-moralism point.

**(e) The Christ-landing needs the problem first.** The anti-moralism gate warns: “If the sermon does not name that problem, the landing will feel bolted on.” The draft states the answer without the problem. *Revision:* a new opening to the final section: v.7 and v.21 store the treasure and the home “for the upright”. Who is upright? Not me. Then 1 Cor 1:30.

**(g) Pulpit-text check.** Every argument in the revision now works in the NIV84 as read aloud: “store up / in store” (vv.1, 7), “call… find” (1:28 / 2:3, 5), “For the LORD gives wisdom” (v.6), “right and just and fair” (1:3 / 2:9), “hidden… treasure” (2:4 / Col 2:3). Two points depend on the Hebrew, and both are told rather than assumed: the shared opening of vv.12 and 16, and the rare verb that links v.22 with Deut 28:63.

## 4. The deepest missed opportunity

**The relief of the gift.** The point-purpose says this is “the week the question gets its most direct answer, and the congregation should feel the relief of it”. The draft *states* the gift (“the LORD gives wisdom”, twice, in the first paragraph) but never lets the congregation *feel* it. There is no picture, no closed door before the open one, no problem before the answer. The revision builds the relief in three steps: a door shut (1:28) → a door opened (“call… find”) → a door that would shut again on the not-upright (vv.7, 21), opened in Christ (1 Cor 1:30).

## 5. Summary judgement

This is the work of a careful exegete who has heard the text’s purpose rightly. The gap is homiletical, not theological: the research has not yet been turned into a sermon. The PT framework opens up three things here: picture the first hearer, keep only the findings that drive the one purpose, and save the application for the end, where it can land on real people.

## 6. Seven-second sermon plan (Davies)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Question?** | *Is wisdom something I must earn, or something I simply wait for? Either way I end up proud or lazy, and either way I don’t ask.* (Them then: a son at his father’s door who has just heard Wisdom say “they will call to me but I will not answer” (1:28). Is it ever too late?) |
| **Answer!** | *The LORD gives wisdom — so the search for it is worth everything and earns nothing. The search is real (receive, call out, dig), but it rests on a treasure God stored up before we started looking. That treasure is finally given, in Christ, to people who were not upright.* |
| **So what…** | *Search harder, claim less — and ask. Keep God’s words, cry out to him, dig, and find the treasure hidden in Christ.* |

**Sermon structure produced by the plan**

1. **Us now:** the treasure hunt; the series question and recap; two ways we go wrong (earning / waiting), neither of which asks.
2. **Them then:** the son at the door; 1:28’s closed door; “one long sentence”.
3. **Body** — *Three ifs* (vv.1–4: receive, call out, dig; land on the middle one; James 1:5) → *Then… for* (vv.5–8: the treasure is knowing God; “for” as the hinge; 1:28 reversed; “store up” ×2; five things God does) → *Then again* (vv.9–11: 1:3’s three words returned; God first, then people) → *Two rescues and a home* (vv.12–22: two voices; v.19 defused with 28:13; the covenant land; Heb 11:16) → *The treasure has a name* (the problem of “the upright”; 1 Cor 1:30; Col 2:3; 2 Cor 4:6; Phil 2:12–13).
4. **Them then:** the son doesn’t have to beat every voice in the city; the LORD has stored up what he needs.
5. **Us now:** the big question answered; the hard-working, the waiting, the door-shut, the not-yet-Christian; treasure-hunt callback; “For the LORD gives wisdom.”

## 7. The illustration

**Chosen: a treasure hunt set for a child.** It fits the chapter at four points, and each one carries part of the argument:

- **The search is real.** The child runs, reads clues, turns cushions over: vv.1–4.
- **The prize was bought and hidden before the hunt began.** This is v.7’s “holds in store”, and v.6’s “for”. The effort receives the gift; it doesn’t earn it.
- **Every clue was written by someone on the child’s side.** The father’s words (v.1), and the God who guards and protects (v.8).
- **When stuck, the child shouts “Dad!”** This is v.3, the middle condition the sermon lands on.

**The callback** (conclusion): the four points are repeated, and the last becomes “the child who gets stuck and shouts for help isn’t failing the hunt — that’s exactly how it’s meant to work.” The illustration then gives way to the gospel: “the One who hid the treasure is the One who gave his own Son, so that people who were not upright could find it.” Col 2:3’s “hidden… treasures” ties it to Christ without forcing it.

*It is written as an invitation (“Have you ever set up a treasure hunt…?”), not as a personal story. If you have a real one, perhaps from grandchildren, it will be stronger told as your own.* A possible alternative is a metal detectorist digging for silver (v.4). It is vivid, but it carries only the effort and not the gift, so it could not bear the closing.

## 8. Vocabulary (aimed at a 16-year-old)

- **Removed:** *self-certify, strenuous, inversion, consensus, grammatical weight, proverbial generalisation, covenant sanction, terminates, trajectory, relocated, vertical/horizontal, effortful, escalating, recipient, preceding*.
- **Explained where the NIV84 uses them:** *adulteress* (“a married woman who has broken faith with her husband”), *covenant* (“the binding agreement God made with Israel”), *store up* (“like putting something precious in a safe”).
- **Hebrew:** none is read aloud; each point is made in English (“In Hebrew they begin with exactly the same words”).
- The harder words left (*discretion, seductive, redemption*) are all in the Scripture readings.

## 10. Publication summary

*Is wisdom something we earn, or something we wait for? Proverbs 2 says neither. A father urges his son to receive, to cry out and to dig for wisdom as for hidden treasure, and then gives the reason the search will succeed: “For the LORD gives wisdom.” The search is real, but it rests on a gift stored up before we began. And the treasure kept for the upright is given, in Christ, to people who were not upright.*
