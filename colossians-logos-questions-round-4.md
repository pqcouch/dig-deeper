## 1. The Commentator's Own Words

**1.1 Colossians 1:20, "to Himself" — Dunn**

- **Ask:** How does Dunn (NIGTC) take εἰς αὐτόν in Colossians 1:20 ("to reconcile all things to him/himself")? Does he refer it to God or to Christ, and how does he relate it to εἰς αὐτόν in 1:16? Please quote his discussion.
- **Why:** round 3's summary had Dunn reading "to him", meaning Christ, but in a garbled form ("Christ serves as the subject"). The 1:15–20 dig (v1.5) now leans slightly to the Son, partly on Dunn. His own words decide whether he can be cited.
- **Watch for:** the summary's confusion between the *subject* of reconciling and the one reconciled *to*. Paste Dunn's text if the answer is a summary again.

**1.2 Colossians 1:19–20 — Pao, in English**

- **Ask:** In Pao's *Colossians and Philemon* (ZECNT, 2012), what does he say about the subject of εὐδόκησεν in 1:19 and about εἰς αὐτόν in 1:20? Give the page numbers in the English edition.
- **Why:** round 3 cited Pao on 1:20 from the Spanish translation (*Colosenses y Filemón*, 2021, p. 113). The dig needs the English page, and confirmation that the English says the same.
- **Watch for:** the Spanish edition again. Limit the scope to the English ZECNT volume if necessary.

**1.3 Colossians 2:23 — Brown**

- **Ask:** How does Brown (*Logos Research Commentaries*) explain the last clause of Colossians 2:23, "not of any value against the indulgence of the flesh" (οὐκ ἐν τιμῇ τινι πρὸς πλησμονὴν τῆς σαρκός)? Which readings does he list, and who holds each?
- **Why:** asked in round 3 and not returned. Brown surveys the field, and the 2:16–23 dig (v1.2) now names holders only through Dunn and Pao.
- **Watch for:** Brown's surveys have misfiled named commentators twice (Wright at 1:24; Dunn at 2:18). Check each name against what that commentator actually says, where you own him.

**1.4 Colossians 2:23 — πρός = "against"**

- **Ask (scope: My Library):** Do any lexicons or grammars in my library give examples of πρός with the accusative meaning "against" in the sense of a remedy or check (e.g. "a medicine against a disease")? Please quote the examples.
- **Why:** Dunn, Pao and Wright all read "no value", but none illustrates "against" (round 3, 1.1). Dunn points to Lightfoot (pp. 204–5) for "to check, prevent". BDAG is not in your library, so this asks whether anything you own supplies examples.
- **Watch for:** NT examples where πρός means "against" in the sense of *hostility* (e.g. Eph 6:12) rather than *remedy*. They are not the same use.

## 2. The State of a Debate — Your Own Commentaries

**2.1 Colossians 2:16 and the weekly Sabbath**

- **Ask:** In my Colossians commentaries, how do Dunn, Pao, Wright, Witherington, Bird and the *ESV Expository Commentary* take "a Sabbath day" (σαββάτων) in Colossians 2:16 — the weekly Sabbath, special sabbaths, or both? What evidence does each give?
- **Why:** round 3's answer cited only Seventh-day Adventist scholars (Bacchiocchi, Gane, du Preez). The 2:16–23 dig reads the triad as including the weekly Sabbath (moderate–high), and the preacher should know the commentators' view before the pulpit (overview, Trap 3).
- **Watch for:** confessional conclusions about the Lord's Day presented as exegesis of the verse. Note whether anyone uses the Hos 2:11 / Ezek 45:17 triad.

**2.2 Authorship, place and date**

- **Ask:** In my Colossians commentaries, what do Dunn, Wright, Pao, Witherington, Bird and Osborne conclude about who wrote Colossians, where Paul (or the writer) was, and when? Give each one's main reason.
- **Why:** round 3 answered from introductions and textbooks, not the commentaries. The overview's `[S]` tags need named holders from your library before Finalise. Dunn's position (Timothy writing during Paul's lifetime, with his approval) is known only from round 2.
- **Watch for:** stylistic arguments presented as settled; and the travel distances quoted in round 3 (Caesarea "350 miles", Rome "900 miles" from Colossae), which looked wrong. Do not take any distance on trust.

**2.3 Colossians 1:15–20 as a "hymn"**

- **Ask:** In my Colossians commentaries, do Dunn, Wright, Pao and Witherington treat Colossians 1:15–20 as a pre-Pauline hymn that the author adapted, or as written for this letter? What evidence does each give, and does anyone say that "the church" (1:18) or "through the blood of His cross" (1:20) was added?
- **Why:** round 3 found holders only outside your library (MacDonald; the *Dictionary of Paul and His Letters*, Bevere and Frederick). The overview tags the hymn as a form-critical reconstruction `[S]`.
- **Watch for:** "additions" that rest only on the reconstruction. The 1:15–20 dig finds both phrases bound into the strophe structure.

## 4. Has Anyone Seen This Allusion? (for claim audit 3)

These collect the history-of-interpretation criterion for claim audit 3 before it runs. Ask only the first three if time is short.

**4.1 "Fearing the Lord" at 3:22 (C42)**

- **Ask:** Which commentators on Colossians 3:22 ("fearing the Lord") connect the phrase with the Old Testament "fear of the LORD", and do any comment on its being directed to Christ as Lord?
- **Why:** C42, moderate–high. The phrase is a common Septuagint formula, and Col 3:22 is the only NT verse with the verb + τὸν κύριον. The variant θεόν (KJV "fearing God") matters here.
- **Watch for:** comment on the variant, and whether the commentator reads "the Lord" as God or Christ.

**4.2 The inheritance at 3:24 and 1:12 (C40, C43)**

- **Ask:** Do any commentators connect "the reward of the inheritance" (3:24) with "the inheritance of the saints in light" (1:12), and either verse with the division of the land by lot in Joshua 18–19?
- **Why:** C40 (moderate–high) rests on 1:12 κλῆρος → 3:24 κληρονομία — slaves among the heirs. C43 (low–moderate) is the Joshua allotment field that Dunn and Pao bring to 1:12 and 2:18.
- **Watch for:** the point that slaves had no legal right to inherit. Note who makes it and on what evidence.

**4.3 2 Chronicles 19:6–7 and Deuteronomy 10:17 behind 3:22–4:1 (C35)**

- **Ask:** Do any commentators on Colossians 3:22–4:1 refer to 2 Chronicles 19:6–7 (Jehoshaphat's judges: "you do not judge for man but for the LORD … no injustice or partiality") or Deuteronomy 10:17 ("who shows no partiality") behind "no partiality" (3:25) and "a Master in heaven" (4:1)?
- **Why:** C35, low–moderate. 2 Chr 19:7 is the one Swete verse with fear + Lord + ἀδικ- + πρόσωπον.
- **Watch for:** Rom 2:11 and Eph 6:9 offered instead. Record them, but they are not the OT claim.

**4.4 Isaiah 1:13–14 behind 2:16, 23 (C32)**

- **Ask:** Do any commentators connect the "festival, new moon, Sabbath" of Colossians 2:16 with Isaiah 1:13–14, where God rejects Israel's new moons, sabbaths and appointed feasts?
- **Why:** C32, moderate. The Greek of Isa 1:14 also has πλησμονή ("surfeit"), the word of Col 2:23, but the Hebrew has "burden" — the contact exists only in the Greek.
- **Watch for:** an answer that cites only Hos 2:11 or Ezek 45:17, which the dig already has.

**4.5 Isaiah 29:14 behind 2:2–3 (C30)** — *low priority*

- **Ask:** Do any commentators connect "the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (2:3) with Isaiah 29:14 ("the wisdom of their wise men will perish") or with Paul's use of that verse at 1 Corinthians 1:19?
- **Why:** C30, moderate, proposed by claim audit 2 as an inversion. Round 3 found no commentator linking Isa 29:14 to 2:23.

## 5. Colossians 3:18–4:1 — the Three Cruxes

**5.1 3:24, δουλεύετε — indicative or imperative?**

- **Ask:** In Colossians 3:24, is τῷ κυρίῳ Χριστῷ δουλεύετε ("you serve the Lord Christ") taken as a statement or a command by Dunn, Pao, Wright and Witherington, and what reasons do they give?
- **Why:** an open crux in the 3:18–4:1 dig. The NA28 has no γάρ; the majority text's γάρ (KJV "for ye serve") makes it a statement. The dig recalls the Vulgate as *servite* (imperative), unchecked.
- **Watch for:** an answer settled by the KJV's γάρ without mentioning that it is a variant.

**5.2 3:25 — whose wrongdoing?**

- **Ask:** In Colossians 3:25 ("he who does wrong will receive the consequences of the wrong"), do the commentators apply the warning to slaves, to masters, or to both, and why?
- **Why:** the dig finds that "no partiality" faces both ways in Colossians, while Eph 6:9 places it with masters (Headline 4). The commentators' reading tests that finding.
- **Watch for:** Philemon 18 ("if he has wronged you", ἀδικέω) brought in to make it Onesimus. Note who does this.

**5.3 4:1, ἰσότης — "fairness" or "equality"?**

- **Ask:** In Colossians 4:1, how do the commentators take τὴν ἰσότητα ("what is fair" / "equality") — fair treatment within the relationship, or a sense of equality between master and slave? What usage do they cite?
- **Why:** an open crux in the dig; the ESV's "justly and fairly" loses the noun. Paul's only other use is 2 Cor 8:13–14, where it means "equality".
- **Watch for:** Philo's or the Stoics' use of ἰσότης cited without a reference.

## 6. Philo (1:15–20 dig, Open Question 7)

**6.1 Philo's "firstborn" and "image"**

- **Ask (scope: My Library):** Which resources in my library quote the Greek of Philo, *On the Confusion of Tongues* (*De confusione linguarum*) §§146–147 and *On Husbandry* (*De agricultura*) §51? Give the Greek words Philo uses at those places for "firstborn" and for "image", and the page of each resource that quotes them.
- **Why:** you have Philo in English only. The dig needs to confirm that Philo's word is πρωτόγονος, not Colossians' πρωτότοκος, and that round 2's "section 28" was a chapter number.
- **Watch for:** Greek given with no resource that actually prints it (discard it); πρωτόγονος treated as πρωτότοκος; Yonge's chapter numbers in place of section numbers. Dunn on 1:15 is the likeliest owned source.
