# 2 John & 3 John — Dig-Deeper Macro-Synthesis

**Version 2.0 · 7 August 2026** *(supersedes v1.0, 25 July 2026)*

*What the two full solo `/dig-deeper` runs — `dig-deeper-2-john` and `dig-deeper-3-john` (7 August 2026) — taken together with the earlier combined sweep, jointly demonstrate about the book: the cross-letter arguments invisible at the level of a single verse but unmistakable once both letters are laid side by side. Companion to the 2 John & 3 John Book Overview (v0.3.0). Text-first; confidence-flagged.*

***

## How to read this document

Each macro-argument below recurs across **several movements of the two letters** — that is what makes it a claim about the book rather than a note on a verse. The arguments are **emergent** from the runs' Convergent Findings, Book-Overview Tensions, Headline Findings and Christological Readings; they are not imposed on them.

**Confidence key:** *NT-authorised* (a NT text makes the link) = **high**; *converging text-first* findings reached independently in more than one run = **strong**; real but looser trajectories = **moderate**.

**Small-corpus declaration (unchanged from v1.0, and still the governing caveat).** This is a two-letter book of twenty-eight verses. The toolkit's normal **≥3-pericope gate** cannot be applied as written, because the book has only two pericopes at solo-dig level. It is therefore applied at the level of the letters' **movements** — 2Jn 1–3 / 4–6 / 7–11 / 12–13; 3Jn 1–4 / 5–8 / 9–10 / 11–12 / 13–15 — and **every argument below is anchored across ≥3 of them**. This is a declared deviation with its reason stated, not a quiet relaxation of the gate. Nothing here rests on a single movement or a single secondary source.

**What changed at v2.0.** v1.0 was built on the combined sweep, which worked both letters at *proportional* depth. Both letters now have full solo digs, and the difference is substantial: the sweep's central lexical claim (a "receive/welcome" mirror) turns out to have been imprecise, and four load-bearing threads the sweep did not see have surfaced. Three arguments are sharpened, one is upgraded in confidence, one is corrected, and two are new. Each change is marked **[v2.0]** where it occurs.

## 1. The book is one ruling on the threshold — the two-door structure

**Claim.** 2 John and 3 John are not two topics (caution; welcome) but one argument about a single act — receiving a travelling teacher. The book stages three doors: the door **rightly shut** on the deceiver (2Jn 10), the door that must stay **open** to the brothers (3Jn 5–8), and the door **wrongly shut** by Diotrephes (3Jn 10). The identical act is obedience in one movement and sin in another, and the sole discriminator is the confession of Christ come in the flesh.

**Converging evidence.**

- **[v2.0] The λαμβάνω chain is the diptych's weld, and it runs through five verses in three movements.** 2Jn 4 *we received* (ἐλάβομεν) a commandment · 2Jn 8 you may *receive* (ἀπολάβητε) a full reward · 2Jn 10 *do not receive* (μὴ λαμβάνετε) him · 3Jn 7 *taking* (λαμβάνοντες) nothing from the Gentiles · 3Jn 8 we ought to *take up* (ὑπολαμβάνειν) such people. (§ 2Jn 4–6; § 2Jn 7–11; § 3Jn 5–8) `[T]` Greek verified word-by-word in the Logos Exegetical Guide across both runs.
- The ἐξῆλθον weld: deceivers "have gone out (ἐξῆλθον) into the world" (2Jn 7) ↔ brothers "have gone out (ἐξῆλθον) for the Name" (3Jn 7) — same aorist, antithetical errands. (§ 2Jn 7–11; § 3Jn 5–8) `[T]`
- Diotrephes' twin refusals use a *third* verb: ἐπιδέχεται at 3Jn 9 (he does not receive **us**) and 3Jn 10 (he does not receive **the brothers**). (§ 3Jn 9–10) `[T]`
- Positional necessity, established independently in both runs: 2 John's rule is **purely negative** — it legislates only for the traveller who does *not* bring the teaching — and 3 John supplies the missing positive obligation. (§ 2Jn 7–11; § 3Jn 5–8) `[I]`

**Confidence: high** on the lexical welds (three independent verb-chains, all verified in the Greek); **strong** on the three-door reading built from them. *(v1.0 rated this "strong"; the verification of the welds raises it.)*

**[v2.0] Correction to the sweep and to Overview v0.2.0.** The sweep and the overview described the 2Jn 10 ↔ 3Jn 10 relation as a *mirror* in the receive/welcome vocabulary, listing λαμβάνω, δέχομαι and ἐπιδέχομαι as one field. The solo digs show the situation is more precise and more interesting: **2 John 10's λαμβάνω is answered at 3 John 8, not at 3 John 10.** 3Jn 9–10 mirrors 2Jn 10 at the level of the *act*, in a different verb. So there are two distinct relations, and they should be recorded separately:

| Relation | Verses | Kind | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lexical resolution** — the prohibition answered in its own verb | 2Jn 10 μὴ λαμβάνετε → 3Jn 8 ὑπολαμβάνειν | same root, opposite polarity | high |
| **Conceptual mirror** — the same act, opposite verdicts | 2Jn 10 → 3Jn 9–10 ἐπιδέχεται | different verb, same deed | high |

This is variant-proof: the alternative reading at 3Jn 8 (ἀπολαμβάνειν) is also a λαμβάνω compound, so the resolution stands on either text.

**Payoff.** Preach the two letters as one message, or at minimum frame each with its twin. The sermon question is not "when should we be careful?" and separately "when should we be welcoming?" but "what makes the *same* open door faithful here and faithless there?" And the bridge is in the text: 3 John answers 2 John's prohibition in 2 John's own word.

## 2. "Truth" is the keyword, and it develops from confessed to embodied

**Claim.** *Alētheia* is the book's spine (eleven occurrences), and it does not merely repeat — it develops. In 2 John truth is **doctrinal**: confessed, abided in, and located entirely in the letter's frame. In 3 John it is **embodied**: walked in, testified to, co-worked *with*, and finally a witness in its own right. The book refuses to let truth's two faces come apart — which is exactly the error each half guards against.

**Converging evidence.**

- **[v2.0] The distribution is the evidence, and it is startling.** In **2 John**, ἀλήθεια occurs five times and **all five fall in vv. 1–4**; the word does not appear once in vv. 7–13. Nor does ἀγάπη — love occurs four times, all in vv. 1–6. The half of 2 John that gets preached as the harsh half contains neither word. (§ 2Jn 1–3; § 2Jn 4–6; § 2Jn 7–11) `[T]`
- In **3 John**, ἀλήθεια occurs six times and they are **spread from v. 1 to v. 12** — greeting, joy report, support ethic, commendation. (§ 3Jn 1–4; § 3Jn 5–8; § 3Jn 11–12) `[T]`
- The grammatical shift carries the development: one becomes a fellow worker **with** the truth (3Jn 8, dative συνεργοὶ τῇ ἀληθείᾳ) and Demetrius is attested **by the truth itself** (3Jn 12, ὑπὸ αὐτῆς τῆς ἀληθείας, genitive of agency). Truth has become a colleague and a witness. (§ 3Jn 5–8; § 3Jn 11–12) `[T]`

**Confidence: strong** (a vocabulary thread traced independently in both runs, with counts verified against the Greek).

**Payoff.** Let "truth" be the through-line of the series, and preach both faces. But note the sharper homiletical consequence of the distribution: **2 John states its terms and then spends them.** The doorway ruling is not the moment truth-and-love are suspended; it is the moment they are cashed. A sermon that begins at 2Jn 7 preaches the letter with its vocabulary removed.

## 3. Whom you receive, you partner — the participation ethic

**Claim.** Hospitality in this book is never a neutral courtesy; it is fellowship in a work. To greet the deceiver is to *share* (κοινωνεῖ) in his wicked works (2Jn 11); to support the brothers is to become a *fellow worker* (συνεργοί) with the truth (3Jn 8); and Diotrephes' refusal is the withholding of that partnership from those who deserve it (3Jn 9–10). The threshold is where a community either co-labours with the truth or co-labours with the lie.

**Converging evidence.**

- 2Jn 11: κοινωνεῖ τοῖς ἔργοις αὐτοῦ τοῖς πονηροῖς — greeting = sharing in evil works. (§ 2Jn 7–11) `[T]`
- 3Jn 8: συνεργοὶ … τῇ ἀληθείᾳ — hosting = co-working with the truth. (§ 3Jn 5–8) `[T]`
- 3Jn 9–10: Diotrephes withholds welcome and expels those who extend it — the anti-partnership. (§ 3Jn 9–10) `[T]`
- **[v2.0]** The 3 John run supplies the economics that make this concrete rather than metaphorical: προπέμπω (3Jn 6) is the technical term for a missionary send-off **with provisions**, and ὀφείλομεν (3Jn 8) is a word of *debt*. (§ 3Jn 5–8) `[T]`/`[S]`

**Confidence: strong** on the theme; **moderate** on the lexical link (κοινωνέω and συνεργός are different roots serving one idea — unchanged from v1.0).

**Payoff.** This dismantles the instinct that hospitality, endorsement or funding is "just being nice." Who a church platforms and funds is a theological act with the church's own name attached. And the 3 John side of it is a bill, not a sentiment.

## 4. Love is defined in 2 John and enacted in 3 John — a distributed interpretive key

**Claim.** The book seeds its definition of love early and cashes it late. 2 John *states* that love is "walking according to his commandments" (2Jn 6) and draws love's boundary (2Jn 10–11); 3 John *shows* love as costly, funded welcome to strangers (3Jn 5–8). The abstract command gets its case study.

**Converging evidence.**

- 2Jn 5–6: love = walking in the commandments; the old command "from the beginning." (§ 2Jn 4–6) `[T]`
- 2Jn 10–11: love has a boundary — it does not underwrite the denial of the incarnation. (§ 2Jn 7–11) `[T]`
- 3Jn 5–8: love in action — προπέμπω, hospitality to ξένοι. **[v2.0]** And the 3 John run establishes from the Greek that the brothers **are** the strangers (καὶ τοῦτο ξένους, a double accusative, not a second group): Gaius is funding fellow-believers he has never met. (§ 3Jn 5–8) `[T]`/`[S: logos]`
- **[v2.0]** The 2 John run adds the connector that binds the two halves of the definition: the ὅτι of 2Jn 7 is *causal* (Logos morphology), so the deceivers are given as the **reason for the love-command**. Internal love is the letter's answer to external deception. (§ 2Jn 4–6; § 2Jn 7–11) `[T]`

**Confidence: strong** (the definition and its enactment are text-explicit; the seeded-then-cashed reading now has the causal ὅτι supporting it).

**Payoff.** Neither letter's account of love is complete alone. And the ὅτι is preachable in its own right: the elder grounds "love one another" in the fact that liars are abroad — which is not where most sermons on 2 John go.

## 5. The book runs on the Fourth Gospel's reception-and-shepherd language

**Claim.** The ethic of the door is not free-floating: it is governed throughout by Johannine Gospel idiom. Christ "coming in the flesh" (2Jn 7) is the **criterion** at the closed door; "the Name" (3Jn 7) is the **ground** at the open door; and beneath both lies the Gospel's settled language of a comer received or refused — and, at the letter's very end, of a shepherd who calls his own by name.

**Converging evidence.**

- **[v2.0] The reception cluster (2 John).** The 2 John run derived, from the letter's own two verbs (ἔρχομαι, λαμβάνω), a four-point Gospel cluster: **John 1:11–12** (he came, his own did not receive), **John 5:43** ("I have come in my Father's name and you do not receive me; if another comes in his own name, him you will receive"), **John 12:48** (rejecting me and not receiving my words), **John 13:20** (receiving the sent one is receiving me). 2Jn 10 is the church's obedient inversion of John 5:43's indictment. **Keener** (*The Gospel of John*, Baker, 2012, 399) notes this cluster's connection to 2 John 10 explicitly. (§ 2Jn 7–11) `[T]` verbs / `[S: logos]` attestation
- **[v2.0] ὁ ἐρχόμενος.** 2Jn 7's *present* participle ἐρχόμενον (against 1 Jn 4:2's perfect ἐληλυθότα) borrows the Gospel's title "the one who comes" (**Thielman**, *Theology of the NT*, 559); the incarnation is confessed as a standing category, not a past datum. (§ 2Jn 7–11) `[S: logos]`
- **[v2.0] The shepherd link (3 John).** **κατ' ὄνομα occurs in only two places in the Greek New Testament: John 10:3 and 3 John 15.** The Good Shepherd "calls his own sheep by name"; Gaius is told to greet the friends by name. **Robertson** makes the connection. And John 10 is the discourse that immediately follows John 9, where the man born blind is **cast out** (ἐξέβαλον) — the verb Diotrephes wears at 3Jn 10. (§ 3Jn 9–10; § 3Jn 13–15) `[T]` phrase / `[S: logos]` attestation
- **[v2.0] The friends and the colophon.** 3Jn 15's οἱ φίλοι draws on John 15:13–15 (**Myers**, *Reading John and 1, 2, 3 John*, 304–305); 3Jn 12's attestation formula is near-verbatim John 21:24, the Gospel's own authenticating colophon. (§ 3Jn 11–12; § 3Jn 13–15) `[T]`/`[S: logos]`

**Confidence: high.** Multiple NT-authorised links, several with named commentary attestation, converging from both runs independently. *(v1.0 rated this "high (NT) / strong" on John 13:20 alone; the base is now five Gospel loci across four movements.)*

**Payoff.** This is the moralism-breaker, and it now has a second half. Preached without it the book becomes "guard doctrine" and "be hospitable." Preached with it, the doorway is where **Christ himself is received or refused**, and the closing instruction of the whole book — greet the friends by name — hands a layman the Shepherd's own practice. Christ is the hero on the threshold; Gaius is the instrument.

## 6. Discernment without paranoia — and the elder models the restraint himself

**Claim.** Held together, the two letters guard opposite failures: 2 John against the **gullible welcome of error**, 3 John against the **censorious refusal of truth**. Diotrephes is precisely the over-correction that a 2 John read alone could license. **[v2.0] And the decisive evidence is not the pairing but the elder's own conduct: he does not apply his own rule to Diotrephes.**

**Converging evidence.**

- 2Jn 7–11: the danger of receiving the deceiver — vigilance commanded, with three imperatives. (§ 2Jn 7–11) `[T]`
- 3Jn 9–10: the danger of refusing the brothers — vigilance turned to tyranny. The letter's **only** diagnosis of Diotrephes is φιλοπρωτεύων, "likes to put himself first." **No doctrinal charge is laid anywhere.** (§ 3Jn 9–10) `[T]`
- **[v2.0] The non-application.** The man who wrote *"do not receive him into a house, and do not say 'rejoice' to him"* is confronted by a man who has refused to receive **him**, refused the brothers, and expelled the willing — and his entire response is *"if I come (ἐὰν ἔλθω, a **contingent** third-class condition), I will call attention to (ὑπομνήσω, 'remind') what he is doing."* No sentence, no "antichrist," no use of the rule. Compare 2Jn 10's **first-class** condition (εἰ + indicative: the deceiver's arrival is assumed). The rule's own author honours the rule's scope, because Diotrephes has failed no christological test. (§ 2Jn 7–11; § 3Jn 9–10; § 3Jn 11–12) `[T]`/`[I]`

**Confidence: strong.** *(v1.0 rated this "moderate — an inference from the pairing." The non-application is direct textual warrant from the author's own conduct, and it lifts the argument out of inference.)* **[v2.0] Upgraded.**

**Payoff.** This is the book's most useful pastoral word and the single strongest answer in Scripture to the weaponisation of 2Jn 10–11. The same vigilance produces the faithful elder and the tyrant; only its object differs. And the apostle himself shows where the line falls — by declining to cross it when he had every provocation and the authority to do so.

## 7. Christ named, then assumed — the book's Christology moves from confession to shorthand

**Claim.** **[v2.0 — new]** 2 John requires the reader to *say* who Jesus is; 3 John assumes it so completely that it never says it at all. The move is not a slackening but a deepening: the confession 2 John demands at the door is what makes 3 John's shorthand intelligible.

**Converging evidence.**

- **2 John names him three times** — "Jesus Christ the Father's Son" (v. 3), "Jesus Christ coming in the flesh" (v. 7), "the teaching of Christ" (v. 9) — and the naming is *the test*: what the deceivers will not confess is precisely the name-plus-predicate. (§ 2Jn 1–3; § 2Jn 7–11) `[T]`
- **3 John never names Jesus or Christ. It is the only book in the New Testament that does not.** Its one reference is "the Name" (3Jn 7, ὑπὲρ τοῦ ὀνόματος, absolute and articular) — the designation that presumes no further specification is needed. (§ 3Jn 5–8) `[T]` The absence is an **admissible pattern-break**: the established pattern (every other NT book, including this letter's own twin) is citable, so the absence is noticeable rather than guessed.
- The letter then closes by requiring that everyone *else* be named: ἀσπάζου τοὺς φίλους **κατ' ὄνομα** (3Jn 15). One Name too well known to say; a congregation of names each worth saying. (§ 3Jn 13–15) `[T]`/`[I]`

**Confidence: moderate.** The data are certain and verified; the *development* reading (confessed → assumed) is an inference, and an absence-argument never carries more than moderate weight even when admissible.

**Payoff.** A two-part series can let this shape it: part one asks the congregation to say who Jesus is and shows what hangs on it; part two shows a church so settled in that confession that it can get on with the work, calling him only "the Name." Do not preach 3 John's silence as indifference — it is the silence of assumption, and 2 John is what earns it.

## 8. Method — an OT-silent book, Gospel-saturated, and unusually damaged in English

**Claim.** **[v2.0 — corrected and extended]** Two methodological findings the sweep produced and the solo digs revised.

**(a) The book cites no Old Testament — but it is not OT-innocent.** v1.0 recorded a flat "zero OT citations," and that remains true of *quotation*. The 3 John run found one genuine dependence: **Deuteronomy 19:15's two-or-three-witnesses rule is invoked at 3Jn 12** (Demetrius attested by everyone, by the truth itself, and by "we"), and the Johannine corpus cites that rule explicitly at **John 8:17** — so the dependence is real, mediated through the Gospel, and corpus-attested (**Brendsel**, in Beale & Gladd, *Connecting Scripture NT*, 488). The accurate statement is therefore: *the book quotes no OT text, and its one Deuteronomic dependence reaches it through John.*

**(b) The load-bearing threads are broken by every standard English version.** This is not a quibble; it is a finding about how the book must be preached.

| Weld | Greek | What English does |
|---|---|---|
| The joy-thread (2Jn) | ἐχάρην · χαίρειν ×2 · χαρά | ESV: "rejoiced" / "greeting" / "greets" / "joy" — four unrelated words |
| The receive-chain | ἐλάβομεν · ἀπολάβητε · λαμβάνετε · λαμβάνοντες · ὑπολαμβάνειν | rendered by five different English verbs |
| Diotrephes' twin refusal | ἐπιδέχεται (3Jn 9, 10) | ESV/NASB interpret v. 9 ("does not acknowledge our authority") and translate v. 10 — the pair vanishes; the KJV and the Latin keep it |
| The request | καλῶς ποιήσεις + ptc. (3Jn 6) | rendered as a statement of approval; it is the papyri's "please" (**Yarbrough**, BECNT, 371) |
| The door | εἰς οἰκίαν (2Jn 10), anarthrous | "into **your** house" — the possessive is supplied |

**Converging evidence.** Every row above was found in one run and confirmed against the Greek in the Logos Exegetical Guide; the joy-thread, the receive-chain and the οἰκίαν point come from the 2 John run (§ 2Jn 4–6; § 2Jn 7–11; § 2Jn 12–13), the ἐπιδέχομαι and καλῶς ποιήσεις points from the 3 John run (§ 3Jn 5–8; § 3Jn 9–10). `[T]`/`[S: logos]`

**Confidence: high** (facts about what the runs found, all verified).

**Payoff.** For the preacher: **this book rewards reading a verse aloud in Greek gloss more than most.** Four of the eight arguments above depend on a repetition the congregation cannot hear in the ESV. That is not an argument against the ESV; it is an argument for saying, once or twice in each sermon, "the word underneath is the same word."

For the overview-builder: do not hunt OT proof-texts here, and lean the passage runs on Move 4 (internal echo) and on the Gospel of John — which is exactly what both solo digs found themselves doing.

## Candidate threads — not yet macro

Recorded so a later pass knows they were considered and declined.

- **The pagan-theophoric names.** Both men named around Gaius bear the names of pagan deities (Diotrephes, "nourished by Zeus"; Demetrius, "belonging to Demeter"), and the letter does not comment. Anchored in one movement only (§ 3Jn 9–12) and resting on etymology the text does not exploit. *Not macro; do not preach as design.*
- **The φιλοπρωτεύων / φίλοι stem-echo** (3Jn 9 ↔ 15). One movement-pair, and φιλ- is far too common a stem to bear allusive weight. *Declined.*
- **The ἀγαπητέ section-markers and the gap before the Diotrephes block** (3Jn 2, 5, 11 with nothing at 9–10). A real structural observation, but internal to one letter. *Retained in the 3 John run; not macro.*
- **The "not with paper/pen and ink … face to face" closings** (2Jn 12; 3Jn 13–14). Genuine and high-confidence, but it is an epistolary frame rather than an argument — it proves the letters are a pair, which Argument 1 already establishes on stronger ground.

## What this means for the series as a whole

Two letters that look, apart, like a caution and a courtesy are together a single sustained argument about the most ordinary of church acts — who you let in, back, and send. The cumulative case: **the doorway is a doctrinal place.** Truth is confessed there (2 John) and embodied there (3 John); love is defined there and enacted there; Christ himself is received or refused there, in those he sends.

What the solo digs add to that case is a spine and a safeguard. The **spine** is a verb: λαμβάνω runs from "we received a commandment" through "do not receive him" to "we ought to take them up," so that the second letter answers the first in the first letter's own word. The **safeguard** is the elder's own restraint: holding a published rule for shutting doors, and provoked by a man who has shut one against him, he declines to use it — because the rule was never about people who annoy us. Between the spine and the safeguard the book steers its course, and it keeps its balance only because the confession of Christ come in the flesh, not the mere fact of a stranger at the door, is what decides.

Preach the pair. Let *truth* carry the series. Say the Greek word aloud where the English has hidden it. And end where the book ends — with a Name too well known to need saying, and a room full of people who each need naming.
