Claim Audit 7: Lamentations

Passages audited: 3:52–59, 4:9, 4:11 and 4:17, and poem 4 as a whole. A short Finalise audit of the eight items that the book overview would otherwise carry above moderate without an audit. All but one were found by claim audit 6 or Logos round 3 and are now queued as L159–L194. Date: 21 October 2026 · Version: 1.0 Purpose: queue trigger 3 requires an audit before Finalise. The other 70 queued items carry no headline and will enter the overview at no more than moderate, marked untested. This audit decides whether these eight may go higher. Method: two blind auditors, run one after the other (the two-auditor rule of 16 October 2026). Auditor A took the five single contacts. Auditor B took the formula and the two partner-level claims, with block tests against random equal-length blocks and window ranks at several settings. Neither saw the digs, audit 6, the Logos assessments or the other’s report. The main session reproduced the decisive results.

Primary texts: WLC Hebrew with its lemma index (every count); Swete LXX (observation); the Rahlfs LXX and BHS apparatus for Lamentations (Logos exports, the citation of record). References follow the Hebrew versification, with the English in brackets at the first mention where it differs.

Warrant tags: [T] the text itself · [I] an inference from it · [S] a secondary source · [S: audit] a verdict of this or an earlier audit.

The items

1. The formula “I said, I am cut off — You heard”

Claim tested. Lam 3:54–56 belongs to one formula, “I said, I am cut off [from before Your eyes] … You heard (my voice)”, with Jonah 2:5 and Ps 31:23.

What held. [T]

  • A fair search was run. “I said” (אָמַרְתִּי, 80 instances) followed by any niphal perfect in the first person singular gives exactly three verses: Jonah 2:5 נִגְרַשְׁתִּי מִנֶּגֶד עֵינֶיךָ (“I am driven away from before Your eyes”), Ps 31:23 נִגְרַזְתִּי מִנֶּגֶד עֵינֶיךָ (“I am cut off from before Your eyes”), and Lam 3:54 נִגְזָרְתִּי (“I am cut off”).
  • All three have “hear” and “voice” within two verses: Jonah 2:3 (“You heard my voice”); Ps 31:23 (“You heard the voice of my supplications”); Lam 3:56 (“You have heard my voice”). Of the 80 instances of “I said”, only seven have both words that near, so three out of three would arise by chance about seven times in ten thousand (Auditor B).
  • The morphology alone is weaker: a first-person niphal perfect follows any first-person perfect in about 1 % of cases (16 of 1,455), against 3 of 80 after “I said”.

What did not.

  • “Three roots differing by one letter” (the round 3 addendum) is wrong. Jonah’s גרשׁ and Ps 31’s גרז differ by one letter; Ps 31’s גרז and Lam’s גזר differ by the order of two letters (metathesis). The addendum’s advice to “keep metathesis out of it” is withdrawn: the Ps 31 / Lam link does involve it.
  • Jonah has no “waters over the head”. Jonah 2:6 has waters “to the soul” and weeds wrapped around the head. The waters + head + “I said” cluster is chance-level (22–26 % of comparable word sets have a single partner).
  • Jonah 2 and Ps 31 share no rare word sets with 3:52–58 at any setting. Their link is the formula, not the vocabulary.
  • Greek. ἀπῶσμαι (“I am thrust away”) renders גרשׁ in Jonah 2:5 and גזר in Lam 3:54. The Greek identity is a translation choice, shared, not a sign of shared Hebrew.

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. Overview rating: moderate–high for the three-member formula. State it as a formula — “I said, I am driven away / cut off … You heard” — with Jonah 2:5 and Ps 31:23 nearly word for word and Lam 3:54 the short form. Drop “one letter”, “waters over the head” and the Greek as evidence.

2. Ps 88:6–7 at 3:54–55

What held. [T] Ps 88 is not a member of the formula: Ps 88:6 is third person plural (“they are cut off from Your hand”), with no “I said” and no hearing. But it is a separate partner at the level of the words:

  • “the lowest pit” (בּוֹר תַּחְתִּיּוֹת): Ps 88:7 and Lam 3:55 only;
  • the same root for “cut off” (גזר) as Lam 3:54;
  • Ps 88 ranks first among all windows for 3:52–58 at the strict settings, a result that 0.07–0.7 % of random blocks reach (Auditor B).

Ps 69 leads at the broader settings, as a rival.

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. Overview rating: moderate–high, as a word-level partner beside the formula.

3. Ps 35:22 at 3:59

What held. [T] “You have seen, O LORD” — the lengthened perfect רָאִיתָה with the divine name next to it — stands only in Ps 35:22 and Lam 3:59, and the Greek keeps it (εἶδες / ἴδες Κύριε).

What did not.

  • An overclaim. Lam 3:52–53 has no “net”, and its pit word differs from Ps 35:7’s. Ps 35:7’s “without cause … net … pit” is not a contact.
  • “Without cause” + “enemy” is shared with Ps 69:5 (audit 6).
  • Rank. Ps 119:154–161 outranks Ps 35 at every window setting against 3:52–66 (first, against 28th, at the strictest), on plead, cause, redeem, “without cause” and pursue. Audit 6 had already named Ps 119:154 as co-equal at 3:58.

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. Overview rating: moderate. Ps 35:22 is one exact phrase; Ps 119:153–161 is the stronger psalm partner of 3:52–66 by vocabulary. The John 15:25 route stays as audit 6 left it (moderate), since John’s wording fits several psalms.

4. Jer 14:18 at 4:9

What held. [T] “Slain” (חָלָל) + “famine” (רָעָב) in one verse occurs only at Jer 14:18 and Lam 4:9; in Swete τραυματίαι … λιμοῦ only the same two. Jer 14 is the top window partner of 4:9–16 at two settings, and 4:9’s best partner verse. Audit 4 had already rated Jer 14:18 at 4:9 [S: audit 4].

What did not. The 4:13–15 half. Jer 14:18’s “go about” (סחר) is not Lam’s “wander” (נוע; Jer 14:10 has נוע); the window rank for 4:13–16 is unstable, and Ezek 22:24–27 is an equal rival there. Jer 14:17 belongs with Lam 2:18 and 3:48–49, not with poem 4.

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. Overview rating: moderate–high for Jer 14:18 at 4:9; moderate for 4:13–15. Both auditors agree that if the overview names one partner for 4:9, it should be Jer 14:18.

5. Jer 14 as the leading chapter partner of 4:1–16

Claim tested. Audit 6 had found Jer 14 first on block tests of exclusive word triples against 4:1–16.

What did not hold. [T]

  • The lead appears only when triples are counted, and it comes from Jer 14:16–19 (61 of 80 sets; 116 of 223). With word pairs Jer 14 ranks 26th and 71st, a result that 11–16 % of random blocks reach — chance level.
  • Control. Jer 14 is a stronger partner of Lam 2:1–16 (pairs: rank 4, reached by 0.5 % of random blocks) than of poem 4, and it also partners Lam 1, 3 and 5. Poem 4 is not special.

Verdict: Needs reframing. Overview rating: low–moderate for “the leading partner of poem 4”. Jer 14 belongs in the overview as a partner of the whole book — which the overview already says under L1 [S: audit 1], with Schnittjer, Lalleman and Salters for the history [S: Logos 2, 3] — and at 4:9 as item 4.

6. Deut 32 as a partner of poem 4

What held. [T]

  • Deut 32 is the most stable chapter partner of 4:1–16: chapter rank 3rd or 4th at every window setting tried.
  • On block tests against all of poem 4, Deut 32:1–43 scores as high as only 0.5–4 % of random equal-length blocks at three of four settings (it fails the strictest).
  • Controls: Deut 28:15–68, a known partner of the book, is weaker; Deut 31 and Deut 33 score nothing.
  • It is specific to poem 4. Against Lam 2, 3 and 5 Deut 32 is at chance; against Lam 1, marginal.

The constituents (items 7 and 8, with one more):

  • 4:9 ~ Deut 32:13 (item 7);
  • 4:11 ~ Deut 32:22 (item 8, overclaimed);
  • 4:3 ~ Deut 32:33, which both auditors found independently. “Cruel” (אַכְזָר, four verses in the Hebrew Bible) with the 4:3 ketiv / “serpents” (two index entries) stands only at Lam 4:3 and Deut 32:33, and the Greek pair δράκοντες + ἀνίατος only there (and in the Odes’ copy of the Song).

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. Overview rating: moderate–high for the Song of Moses as a partner of poem 4.

On 4:3. Audit 6 rated it low, because the qere and the index read “jackals”, the verse sits in the jackal–ostrich idiom, and δράκοντες also renders “jackals” (Jer 9:10; Mic 1:8). HALOT and BDB read “jackals” [S: Logos 3]. The auditors’ point is that the ketiv’s spelling, the word “cruel” and the Greek all meet at Deut 32:33, and that Deut 32 is already a proven partner of the poem. Main-session verdict: low–moderate, ketiv-dependent — the consonants allow the echo and the Greek translator’s wording allows it, but the qere reading does not need it. In the overview, give it as “possible, on the written text”.

7. Deut 32:13 at 4:9

What held. “Produce of the field” (תְּנוּבֹת שָׂדָי) as a phrase stands only in Deut 32:13 and Lam 4:9, and the Greek keeps it (γενήματα ἀγρῶν). [T]

What did not.

  • Ezek 36:30 has the noun “produce” with “field” and “famine” (the noun in five verses), and ranks 2nd for 4:9, against Deut 32:13’s 13th.
  • The spelling שָׂדָי is an ordinary poetic form (13 verses), not evidence.
  • BHS marks this half of 4:9 as corrupt [unchecked — apparatus spread].

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. Overview rating: moderate. A real phrase contact within a proven partner (item 6), but Jer 14:18 is the lead partner for 4:9 and Ezek 36:30 a rival.

8. Deut 32:22 at 4:11

What did not hold. [T] “Foundation + fire, in one verse only Deut 32:22 and Lam 4:11” is true only because two different foundation nouns were pooled (מוֹסָד-type and יְסוֹד). At a verse either side, Ps 18:8–9 = 2 Sam 22:8–9 join, where אַף is “nostrils”. The verb clause of 4:11 (“kindled a fire … it devoured”) is the prophets’ oracle formula (Jer 17:27; Amos 1:14 and the refrain), and the Greek follows the Amos refrain (θεμέλια).

Verdict: Needs reframing. Overview rating: low–moderate. Present 4:11 as the prophets’ formula against cities turned on Zion, with Zeph 3:8 its best partner [S: audit 5] and Deut 32:22 a shared image.

9. Isa 30:5–7 at 4:17

What held. The help words + “vapour” within three verses occur only at Isa 30:5, 30:7 and Lam 4:17. [T]

What did not.

  • The pair’s exclusivity is what chance supplies for words of these frequencies.
  • Counting the other “vain” words, Ps 60:13 = Ps 108:13 (“Give us help (עֶזְרָת) against the adversary, for vain is the salvation of man”) joins: the same help noun as Lam 4:17, with “salvation” (cf. 4:17 “a nation that could not save”). The Greek singles them out: βοήθεια + μάταιος in Swete only Ps 59:13; 107:13 [Heb 60:13; 108:13] and Lam 4:17.
  • Against 4:17 alone, Isa 30 has no distinctive window rank (Auditor A); audit 6 found Isa 30 strong against 4:17–20 as a whole.

Verdict: Needs reframing. Overview rating: moderate for “vain help” at 4:17, with Isa 30:5–7 and Ps 60:13 = 108:13 co-equal (the Greek favours the psalm). Isa 30 as a partner of poem 4 keeps audit 6’s moderate–high, resting on 4:2 (the potter’s jar) and 4:19 (the swift pursuers), both exclusive in Hebrew and Greek; 4:17 adds to it at moderate.

Critical assessment — the gaps in this audit

  1. Two auditors, not three. The single contacts and the partner claims were each tested once. Where the two overlapped (Deut 32:33 at 4:3; Deut 32:22 at 4:11; Jer 14:18 at 4:9), they agreed.
  2. History of interpretation is from Logos round 3 only, and some of it from works Patrick does not own (Lessing; Dobbs-Allsopp; Sasson) [S, not owned — unverified].
  3. The formula’s chance rate rests on 80 instances of one verb. It is a strong result for a small sample; a wider search (any verb of saying) was not run.
  4. BHS at 4:9 (a corrupt half-verse) bears on item 7 and was not read in Logos.
  5. Blindness. The auditors were barred from every earlier folder. Auditor B kept its scripts in the session scratchpad, which also held older working files from earlier runs (among them a copy of the overview); there is no sign they were opened, but the scratchpad was not on the forbidden list.
