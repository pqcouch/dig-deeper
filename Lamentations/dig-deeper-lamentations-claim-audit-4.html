What this audit tested, and how

Three blind auditors, run one after another. None of them saw the 2:1–22 dig, the earlier audits or each other’s reports. Each received a neutral brief, the claims restated, the staged corpus and the same lemma-index searches, and was told to try to break the claims.

Auditor Claims Kind
A L40, L41, L42, L43, L44 The claims that carry the 2:1–22 headlines. L43 and L44 are design claims (a “field” and a “cluster”), each tested against a chance baseline
B L45, L46, L47, L48, L49, L50, L51, L54 Single contacts at Lam 2 in the Psalms and the Prophets
C L52, L53, L34, L35, L36, L37, L38, L39 The remaining Lam 2 contacts and the six items left from audit 3. L35 is a design claim

Method for each claim:

  1. Check the wording on both sides in Hebrew and Greek.
  2. Count how rare the shared lemma set is — in how many verses of the Hebrew Bible (WLC) all the shared words occur together — searching both entries wherever the lemma index splits a word-family (the brief named five such splits from earlier runs).
  3. Test the selection effect: a set chosen from the overlap of two texts will often be exclusive to them by construction. Where possible, run a fairer test that does not pick the words in advance.
  4. Search for rival sources.
  5. Run a chance baseline for any design claim, and report every rank under at least two settings.
  6. Score the seven Hays criteria. “History of interpretation” is “not checked” throughout, because no commentary was consulted.
  7. Give a verdict and a recommended confidence.

Positive controls were run before every nil or “only” result: every exclusivity search returned the Lamentations verse itself; the matcher was checked on חֶסֶד (“lovingkindness”) in Ruth (1:8; 2:20; 3:10) and on חמל (“spare”) in Lamentations (2:2, 17, 21; 3:43, finding 2:2 through its ketiv). Ezek 9:5’s two ketivs were confirmed by lemma: a search on the read wording finds nothing, the written wording finds the verse.

The three baselines the dig relied on were reproduced independently by all three auditors:

  • Per verse. For every verse of Lamentations, the best partner verse elsewhere, weighted by rarity: median 13.6, top tenth 19.6, maximum 32.8 (WLC; words in up to about 1,150 verses). At a stricter setting the median is 12.0 and the top tenth 18.0; at a looser one 14.0 and 19.7. A control block of the same size (Nahum, Habakkuk and Zephaniah, 156 verses) scores higher: median 16.2, top tenth 23.3. Known verbatim parallels elsewhere (Mic 4 ~ Isa 2; Obadiah ~ Jer 49) score 40–76.
  • By chapter. The rare-pair scan of 858 chapters for Lam 2 reproduces at three settings.
  • The Psalter. The 1,136 twelve-verse windows reproduce exactly, when tied windows share a rank. Auditor B added a third ceiling, a rarity-weighted score at each ceiling, and controls (Lam 1, Lam 3, Jer 14, Ezek 7 as targets).

The main session’s gate. Twenty-four spot checks from the auditors’ lists were reproduced, among them:

Claims Audited

The 2:1–22 headline claims (Auditor A)

L40 — 2 Chr 36:15–21 as a partner of Lam 2, not merely the narrative of the event

Claim (dig v1.0, Headline 4; Tool 11 triad). Lam 2:21 and 2 Chr 36:17 share six lemmas found together in no other verse — הרג (“kill”), בָּחוּר (“young man”), חֶרֶב (“sword”), חמל (“spare”) with the negative, בְּתוּלָה (“virgin”), זָקֵן (“old man”). It is the joint-strongest verse contact in the book; 2 Chr 36 ranks 2nd of 858 chapters for Lam 2; and 36:15, 16, 19, 21 add further points.

Findings.

  • Wording. Verified in both verses. The agents differ: “You killed” (Lam 2:21, God addressed); “he killed” (2 Chr 36:17, the king of the Chaldeans, whom God “brought up” and into whose hand “He gave them all”). [T]
  • Rarity. All six together: Lam 2:21 and 2 Chr 36:17 only. Every five- and four-word subset is confined to the two, except killing + young man + virgin + old man, which adds Ezek 9:6.
  • The selection effect is real but does not dissolve the contact. For 95 of Lamentations’ 154 verses the best partner’s shared words occur in no third verse, so exclusivity alone proves little. What is not manufactured is the size of the set: only two verses in Lamentations share six or more content words with a single partner — Lam 2:18 with Jer 14:17 (seven) and Lam 2:21 with 2 Chr 36:17 (six). The score of 32.8 ranks 1st or 2nd of 154 at three settings. It is an outlier for Lamentations, but below the range of verbatim borrowing (40–76).
  • Half the six words are a stock list. Young man + virgin + old man occur together in six verses (Jer 31:13; 51:22; Ezek 9:6; Ps 148:12; Lam 2:21; 2 Chr 36:17). Lamentations’ fuller four-person list, with נַעַר (“youth”), occurs elsewhere only at Jer 51:22 and Ps 148:12; 2 Chr 36:17 lacks “youth”.
  • The distinctive core is “killed … did not spare”: הרג + חמל in five verses (Hab 1:17; Zech 11:5; Lam 2:21; 3:43; 2 Chr 36:17) — and Lamentations repeats it itself at 3:43. Add the killing in the sanctuary (Ezek 9:6; Lam 2:20; 2 Chr 36:17 only).
  • The chapter rank is not separate evidence. 12 of 2 Chr 36’s 15 rare pairs with Lam 2 come from 36:17; the other three from 36:19. Without 36:17 the chapter would fall far down the list.
  • Supports. 36:15 → 36:17 (compassion, then none) holds: they are the only two verses of Chronicles with חמל. 36:16 מַרְפֵּא (“remedy”) is weak: a different word from Lam 2:13’s רפא (“heal”), in 15 verses; Jer 6:14 and 8:11 (heal + breach) are better partners for 2:13. 36:19 (wall, palaces, destroy, precious things) is moderate — its “precious things” are vessels, Lam 2:4’s are people, and Lam 1:10 is the closer use. 36:21 names the seventy years by Jeremiah, not Lam 2:17’s “word from days of old”: thematic only.
  • Direction. None of the six words, nor the sanctuary or the remedy, is in the Kings or Jer 52 account of the fall: 2 Chr 36:15–17 is the Chronicler’s own wording, not borrowed from Kings. He names Jeremiah twice (36:12, 21) and knows a written collection of laments (35:25, on Josiah). Availability only; priority is not settled [T]/[I].
  • Greek. Swete 2 Chr 36:17 breaks the list — “he did not spare Zedekiah; their virgins they did not pity; their elders they led away”. The Greek weakens the link; φείδομαι (“spare”) and ῥομφαία (“sword”) survive.

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. Confidence: moderate–high for a genuine verbal partnership between Lam 2:20–21 and 2 Chr 36:15–17; low on direction.

Change: cite the six-word match with its stock half named; make “killed … did not spare” and the sanctuary the core; drop the chapter rank as independent evidence and the “no remedy” point; note that Greek Chronicles loses the list.

L41 — Ezek 9:5–8 as a partner of Lam 2:4, 20–22

Findings.

  • Numbers. Every claimed count reproduces, including through Ezek 9:5’s ketivs. Ezek 9 ranks 10th of 858 (9th and 4th at other settings).
  • Mostly the same evidence as L40. All four rare pairs that place Ezek 9 come from 9:6, and three of them are 2 Chr 36:17’s.
  • Independent of Chronicles: killing + women (נָשִׁים) + sanctuary within one verse either side — Ezek 9:6 and Lam 2:20–21 only. 2 Chr 36:17 has no women.
  • Ezek 9:5 is Ezekiel’s refrain: חוס (“pity”) + חמל (“spare”) in eight verses, six of them Ezekiel’s. חוס never occurs in Lamentations.
  • Ezek 9:8 is Ezekiel’s stock phrase (pour + wrath in 18 verses, 11 in Ezekiel). Lam 2:4’s real partner is Lam 4:11: pour + wrath + fire in those two verses only.
  • Homograph. In Ezek 9:6 חלל means “begin”, not “profane”.
  • Greek keeps the list (“old man and young man and virgin … kill”); the sanctuary words differ.

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance for Ezek 9:6 ~ Lam 2:20–21; Needs reframing for the rest. Confidence: moderate. Drop Ezek 9:5 and 9:8 as supports; cite the women + kill + sanctuary set as the evidence independent of Chronicles.

L42 — “Lam 2:17 inverts Hannah’s song (1 Sam 2:1)”

Findings.

  • Grammar. Lam 2:17 וַיְשַׂמַּח עָלַיִךְ אוֹיֵב הֵרִים קֶרֶן צָרָיִךְ (“He made the enemy rejoice over you; He exalted the horn of your adversaries”): causative verbs, God the subject. 1 Sam 2:1: Hannah’s horn is exalted and she rejoices over her enemies — simple verbs, the speaker’s own horn. Ps 89:43 [Eng 89:42]: “You exalted the right hand of his adversaries; You made all his enemies rejoice” — the same grammar and the same agent as Lam 2:17.
  • Rarity. Horn + exalt + rejoice + enemy: 1 Sam 2:1 and Lam 2:17 only (confirmed, at one verse and ±2). Exalt + adversary + rejoice + enemy: Ps 89:43 and Lam 2:17 only [S: audit]. Horn + exalt + adversary: Lam 2:17 alone — no other text exalts the adversary’s horn.
  • Fair test. Every four-word set drawable from any verse of Lamentations, from words in 50–300 verses (618 sets): only 3 occur in any other verse. Lam 2:17 is the only verse of the 154 with such a set shared exclusively with one other verse — and it has two partners, 1 Sam 2:1 and Ps 89:43. The contact is not chance; the test cannot choose between the two.
  • Ps 89 is the wider partner. Horn + exalt is idiom (12 verses). Ps 89 alone supplies all four words (horn exalted at 89:18, 25; foe exalted and enemies rejoicing at 89:43), and its lament runs alongside Lam 2:1–7: נאר (“spurn”) only at Ps 89:40 and Lam 2:7; reject + spurn within ±1 only Ps 89:39–40 and Lam 2:7; “profaned … to the ground” (89:40 ~ 2:2); strongholds (89:41 ~ 2:2, 5); right hand (89:43 ~ 2:3–4); “all who pass along the way” (89:42 ~ 2:15); “throne … to the ground” (89:45 ~ 2:1). Auditor B found Ps 89:39–50 the Psalter’s best window for Lam 2 at four of six settings.
  • Lam 2:3 and Ps 75 mis-stated in the brief. Lam 2:3 has no רום (“exalt”). “Cut off” + “horn” is in four verses, with Jer 48:25 (“the horn of Moab has been cut off”) an equal rival. The stronger tie for 2:3 is Ps 74:11 (L45).
  • Greek preserves both links; the causatives of Ps 88:43 (Greek numbering) match Lam 2:17 exactly.

Verdict: Needs reframing. Confidence: moderate–high for the language and for Ps 89 as its partner; low–moderate for Hannah. Put Ps 89 first; Hannah’s song is a second echo a reader may catch, and “inversion” is a reading the text does not mark.

L44 — “Ezekiel’s temple-judgement oracles are a live cluster for Lam 2”

Findings.

  • Singles. Ezek 7:26 ~ 2:9 (vision + prophet + torah, two verses only) and Ezek 22:28 ~ 2:14 (falsehood + whitewash, two verses only; Ezek 13 is an alternative home of תָּפֵל) — strong, as audited.
  • Ezek 22:26 ~ 2:6. “Do violence” + sabbath + priest: Ezek 22:26 and Lam 2:6 only, confirmed — but Zeph 3:4 (“her priests have done violence to the law”) is a rival: “do violence” + priest occurs only at Ezek 22:26, Zeph 3:4 and Lam 2:6. In both prophets the violence is the priests’ sin; in Lam 2:6 it is God’s act on his own booth. Lost in Greek. Moderate.
  • Ezek 22:31 is Ezekiel’s formula (21:36 [Eng 21:31] is near-identical; Zeph 3:8 is similar), its words spread across three verses of Lam 2. Weak.
  • Ezek 24:21 ~ 2:4. “Delight” + “eye” in five verses; the Greek ἐπιθυμήματα ὀφθαλμῶν is identical in Ezek 24:21 and Lam 2:4. Moderate. “Fall by the sword” is common (63 verses).
  • Baseline. Ezekiel, Jeremiah and Isaiah are of similar size. Exclusive three-word contacts with Lam 2: 35, 34 and 29. Best four chapters chosen after the fact: 12, 17 and 12. Contacts in Lamentations’ top tenth: 2, 5 and 4. The four named Ezekiel chapters yield seven exclusive contacts; Isa 10 alone yields six.
  • Missed. Zeph 3:3–8 runs parallel to Ezek 22:25–31 (princes, prophets, priests, the poured-out indignation) and is itself a partner of Lamentations [S: audit 3] (Zeph 3 as a ranking).

Verdict: Needs reframing. Confidence: high for 7:26 and 22:28 singly; moderate for 22:26 and 24:21; low for a “cluster”. Ezekiel is not above the baseline of a long prophet.

Psalms and Prophets at Lam 2 (Auditor B)

L45 — Ps 74 at Lam 2:3, 6–7, 9

  • Confirmed: “draw back” (hiphil) + “right hand” — Ps 74:11 and Lam 2:3 only, both hiphil; Lam 2:8 adds “He did not draw back His hand”, so 2:3 and 2:8 together carry both nouns of Ps 74:11. Reject + sanctuary + appointed place within ±10 — Ps 74:1–10 and Lam 2:7 only. In a fair test using every rare word of Lam 2:7, Ps 74 is its second-best partner chapter of 605. The Psalter ranks (3rd and 64th) reproduce.
  • Errors in the claim: at Lam 2:2 “to the ground” closes the half-line with “brought down”, and “profaned” opens the next clause: the two words are adjacent, not one phrase (only Ps 74:7 and Ps 89:40 have the phrase). “No prophet” is idiom (12 verses); for Lam 2:9 Ezek 7:26 is the partner.
  • Greek: “turn back” + “right hand” occurs in Swete only at Ps 73:11 [Heb 74:11], Lam 2:3 and Prov 4:27.
  • Missed: Ps 89 is an equal or stronger Psalter partner (L42). In the fair test for 2:3, Ps 89 ranks 11th, Ps 74 44th.

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. Moderate–high for 2:3 and 2:7; low for 2:2 and 2:9.

L46 — Ps 22:8, 14–15 at Lam 2:11, 15–16, 19

  • Holds: “they opened their mouths against me / you / us” — Ps 22:14; Lam 2:16; 3:46 only, plain, skeletal and by lemma. In Swete, ἤνοιξαν ἐπ- (“they opened against”) occurs only at Ps 21:14 [Heb 22:14] and Lam 2:16.
  • Error: Ps 22:15 does not pour out the heart (“I am poured out like water … my heart is like wax”). Lam 2:19’s “pour out your heart … before the Lord” is Ps 62:9 [Eng 62:8], “pour out your heart before Him” — which the index files under the longer form לֵבָב, so a search on לֵב alone misses it.
  • Rivals: Job 16:13 for Lam 2:11 (pour + “to the ground”, only those two verses); Ps 35:21 for the open mouth with “they said … our eyes have seen” (L47); Isa 37:22 for the head-shaking.
  • Rank: Ps 22 also ties for first when Jer 14 or Ezek 7 is the target — generic lament vocabulary, not particular to Lam 2.

Verdict: Needs reframing. Low–moderate. The one hard item is the mouth phrase, which reaches 3:46 as well. Do not present Ps 22 as a source pattern for Lam 2.

L47 — Ps 35:16–26 at Lam 2:16–17

  • The six-word window is mostly selection: dropping any one word leaves it unchanged; its core is the rare verb חרק (“gnash”, five verses) with “swallow”. An equally exclusive rival set comes from Ps 37:12 (gnash + teeth + “plot”, with Lam 2:16–17 within ±3).
  • What holds without the selection: the enemy’s gloat in the first person plural, “We have swallowed (him / her)”, occurs only at Ps 35:25 and Lam 2:16; κατεπίομεν likewise in Swete. In a fair test Ps 35 ranks 2nd and 4th of 726 chapters for 2:16 (Ps 37 first, Job 16 third or fourth). Ps 35:21 (mouth opened, “they said”, “our eyes have seen”) completes the frame.
  • Reassign: the 2:17 “rejoice” item to Ps 89:43.

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. Moderate–high. Lead with the gloat form and the Greek; name Ps 37:12–14 as a co-equal rival (it also has “bent their bow”, as Lam 2:4, and “to slaughter”, as 2:21).

L48 — Zeph 2:15 at Lam 2:15

  • City + hiss + shake: Zeph 2:15 and Lam 2:15 only — in fact hiss + shake alone gives the same two. זֹאת הָעִיר (“this is the city”): the same two only, plain and skeletal. Both have “pass by”. Partner score 24.8 (top tenth from 19.6); Zeph 2 ranks 9th of 858. The Greek verb pair is also unique to the two.
  • Nuance: Zephaniah’s passer-by shakes his hand, Lamentations’ passers-by their heads — closer to Jer 18:16. Job 27:23 adds the hand-clapping (clap + palm + hiss, Job 27:23 and Lam 2:15 only). Zeph 2:15 also has אֵיךְ (“How!”).

Verdict: Confirmed. Moderate–high to high. “Nineveh’s epitaph spoken over Jerusalem” is a reading; the texts share the taunt, direction open.

L49 — Isa 29:1–4 (Ariel) at Lam 2:5, 10

L50 — Isa 40:25; 46:5 at Lam 2:13

  • Greek loses “make equal” on both sides (Lam 2:13 τίς σώσει; Isa 40:25 ὑψωθήσομαι).

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance for the pair, moderate; “incomparable God / incomparable ruin” is an interpretive reading, low–moderate.

L51 — Isa 66:1 at Lam 2:1

  • The triple heaven + earth + footstool is real (two verses), but the footstool means different things: in Isa 66:1 the earth is God’s footstool; in Lam 2:1 it is Zion’s sanctuary (Ps 99:5; 132:7; 1 Chr 28:2).
  • Missed and stronger in kind: “How!” + “heaven” in one verse occurs only at Isa 14:12 and Lam 2:1 — “How you have fallen from heaven” — the same fall from heaven to earth, and the Greek matches (πῶς … ἐκ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ … εἰς τὴν γῆν).

Verdict: Needs reframing. Low–moderate for Isa 66:1. Present the footstool as Zion-cult language and add Isa 14:12 for the fall.

L54 — Isa 63:10 at Lam 2:4–5 (conceptual)

Also Jer 30:14 (“the blow of an enemy”); Job 30:21 (“You have turned cruel to me”, Isa 63:10’s construction); Job 16:9; 19:11. Job 13:24 and 33:10 run the other way and are not parallels.

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance, as a conceptual parallel. Low–moderate. Name Jer 21:5 beside it.

L35 — Ps 119 and Lam 3: phrases at the same letter (design)

Critical assessment — the gaps in this audit

  1. History of interpretation was not checked. Every verdict rests on the corpus. Ps 89 and 2 Chr 36 are the two most likely to have a commentary history; both are on the Logos list.
  2. The auditors were blind to the dig, but not to its phrasing. The claims were restated from the dig, and a restatement carries the proposer’s framing. Auditor A’s corrections to the brief itself (Lam 2:3 has no “exalt”; Jer 14:12 has no “lift”) show the restatement was tested, not trusted.
  3. The per-verse measure favours Lamentations’ own idiom. A control block of the Twelve scores higher than Lamentations at every setting, so “top of Lamentations” is not “top of the canon”. The 2 Chr 36:17 contact is an outlier within the book, below verbatim borrowing.
  4. The Psalter scan counts words, not syntax. Ps 89’s grammar match at 2:17 and Ps 74’s two-verb match at 2:3/2:8 were found by reading, not by the score.
  5. Window choices were made by the auditors. Each reported more than one setting; ranks that changed with the setting are reported as ranges.
  6. The BHS span at 2:19 is inferred, not read. Until it is checked, the contacts that rest on the fourth line of 2:19 carry [unchecked — apparatus spread].
