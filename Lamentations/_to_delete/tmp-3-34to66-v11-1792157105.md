## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims that emerged across several tools, as revised by claim audit 6 (16 October 2026):

1. **3:34–39 speaks in the language of the Torah's court and asks the question Job's speakers argue about — and the poem answers "the Lord does not see" with "You have seen".**
   - *The law court.* "To turn aside the justice (לְהַטּוֹת מִשְׁפַּט) of a man … to subvert a man in his lawsuit (בְּרִיבוֹ)" (3:35–36). "Turn aside" + "justice" + "lawsuit" within a verse either side occur only at **Exod 23:6** ("You shall not pervert the justice due to your needy brother in his lawsuit"), Prov 18:5 and Lam 3:35–36 (WLC). Exod 23:6 has the identical form **בְּרִיבוֹ** ("in his lawsuit"), found only at Exod 23:3, 6; Prov 18:17; Lam 3:36. The words are the Torah's own law against corrupt judges. `[T]` *Confidence: moderate–high* `[S: audit 6]`.
   - *Job's question.* עות ("pervert, subvert") + "justice" within a verse either side: Job 8:3; 19:6–7; 34:12 and Lam 3:35–36 (WLC); at two verses either side Ps 146:9 joins. The verb occurs in only ten verses, three in Job, and Eccl 1:15; 7:13 also make God its subject. In Lam 3:36 its object is a person, as in Job 19:6 ("God has wronged me"). **3:36 asks the question Job's speakers argue about; it does not demonstrably "enter their debate".** `[T]`; `[I]`, *moderate* `[S: audit 6]`.
   - *The crux.* אֲדֹנָי לֹא רָאָה (3:36) has three possible readings `[S: audit 6]`:
     - a statement, "the Lord does not approve" (ESV, NASB95). Num 23:21 ("He has not seen trouble in Israel") is a precedent for "see" as "regard";
     - an unmarked question, "Does the Lord not see?", matching 3:37 (Lord + "not" + perfect) and the unmarked question of 3:38;
     - the slogan of the guilty, "The LORD does not see" (Ezek 8:12; 9:9; Ps 10:11; 94:7). This is the weakest, because every one of the nine slogan verses has a verb of saying and 3:36 has none. *Uncertain, low.*
   - *The vengeance psalms.* "Crush" (every entry) with a negated "see" within two verses: **Ps 10:10–11**, Ps 94:5–7, Job 22:9–11 and Lam 3:34–36 (WLC). Ps 94 is the fullest member by vocabulary (joint 3rd of 6,107 windows, and in the top 20 at four settings). **Ps 10 is the closest in form**: "He will never see" (10:11) is answered by "You have seen" (רָאִתָה, 10:14), the move Lam 3:59–60 makes. `[T]`; *moderate* for the family, *low–moderate* for Ps 94 as a source `[S: audit 6]`.
   - *The answer.* "See" (ראה) occurs five times in ch. 3: the man "who has seen affliction" (3:1), "the Lord does not see" (3:36), "until the LORD looks down and sees from heaven" (3:50), and twice "You have seen" (3:59, 60). The wrong of 3:36 (לְעַוֵּת, "to subvert") is the wrong he has seen: עַוָּתָתִי ("my wrong", 3:59, found nowhere else) (WLC). **Whichever reading of 3:36 is taken, by 3:59 the Lord has seen.** `[T]` for the chain; `[I]`, *high*.
   - *Two more echoes.* 3:37 asks the question Ps 33:9 answers ("He spoke, and it came to be; He commanded, and it stood") `[I]`, *moderate*. "Why should a living man complain?" (3:39) uses a verb found elsewhere only at Num 11:1 (WLC; Greek γογγύζω in both). `[T]`
   - *What it does.* The man sets the injustice of the siege before the Torah's law and the old question "does God bend justice?" — **and lets the rest of the poem answer "the Lord does not see" by saying "You have seen."** `[I]`

   *Surfaced by:* Vocabulary, Repetition, Tool 11, Difficult Verses, Original Languages. *Confidence: high (the words and the "see" chain); moderate–high (Exod 23:6); moderate (Job's question; the vengeance-psalm family, Ps 10 and Ps 94; Ps 33:9); uncertain (the slogan reading).*

2. **3:40–45 prays as Solomon's prayer for exiles prescribed — and records that it was not answered.**
   - *Solomon's prayer.* 1 Kgs 8:46–50 provides for a people taken captive for sin: "if they … repent and make supplication … then hear in heaven … and forgive (וְסָלַחְתָּ) Your people … all their transgressions (פִּשְׁעֵיהֶם)". Lam 3:40–42: "Let us search our ways and return to the LORD; let us lift up our heart with our palms to God in heaven: we have transgressed (פָשַׁעְנוּ) and rebelled; **You have not forgiven** (לֹא סָלָחְתָּ)". `[T]`
   - *The evidence.* **Palms + heaven in one verse: only 1 Kgs 8:22, 8:54, 2 Chr 6:13 and Lam 3:41** (WLC) — Solomon spreading his palms toward heaven. 1 Kgs 8:47–58 is joint 2nd of 13,286 twelve-verse windows against 3:34–42 and stays in the top 15 at five settings; 2 Chr 6, the parallel prayer, passes as a control. "Forgive" + the verb "transgress" within a verse either side: 1 Kgs 8:50, Jer 33:8 and Lam 3:42 only (with the noun, Num 14:19 and Jer 5:7 join). `[T]` `[S: audit 6]`
   - *What is not Solomon's.* The confession "we transgressed and rebelled" is not 1 Kgs 8:47's formula ("we have sinned, done wrong, acted wickedly"), and "return to the LORD" is the idiom of Deut 4:30; 30:2 and Hos 14:2, not of Kings. The poem prays the exile prayer in its own and Deuteronomy's words. `[T]`; `[I]`.
   - *Refused pardon.* Kings itself has the verdict: "the LORD was not willing to forgive" (2 Kgs 24:4). Jer 5:7 asks, "How can I pardon you?" Jer 33:8 promises it ("I will forgive all their iniquities … by which they transgressed against Me"), as do Jer 31:34 and 50:20. **3:42 stands between the request and the promise.** `[I]`, *moderate*.
   - *The cloud.* "You have covered Yourself with a cloud so that no prayer can pass" (3:44). At the temple's dedication the cloud filled the house (1 Kgs 8:10–12), and the cherubim "covered" (8:7). Thematic, with a small lexical support. Isa 1:15 is a parallel on refused prayer, sharpest at Lam 3:8: "even though" + prayer only Isa 1:15 and Lam 3:8 (WLC). `[I]`, *moderate* `[S: audit 6]`.
   - *What it does.* The community does what the temple prayer prescribed for exile, and says honestly that pardon has not come. **The confession is not a technique that obliges God.** `[I]`

   *Surfaced by:* Tool 11, Structure, Vocabulary, Linking Words, Biblical Theology. *Confidence: high (the counts); moderate (1 Kgs 8:46–54; Jer 33:8; the cloud; Isa 1:15 as a parallel).*

3. **The man of 3:52–63 speaks as the psalmist wronged "without cause", who said "I am cut off" and was heard — and pleads his cause in the words of David's appeal to Saul.**
   - *Hated without cause.* "My enemies without cause (חִנָּם) hunted me down like a bird" (3:52). "Without cause" + "enemy" within a verse either side: Ps 35:19, Ps 69:5 [Eng 69:4] and Lam 3:52 only (WLC); the Greek of all three has δωρεάν. A pair of this kind is common by chance, so the weight lies elsewhere: **Ps 35 is the closer partner** — "without cause" with net and pit (35:7; cf. 3:52–53), **"You have seen, O LORD" (רָאִיתָה יְהוָה), only Ps 35:22 and Lam 3:59**, and "my justice … my cause … judge me" (35:23–24; cf. 3:58–59). **Jesus quotes the line: "They hated Me without a cause (ἐμίσησάν με δωρεάν)" (John 15:25).** `[T]`; *low–moderate* for the pair, *moderate* for Ps 35 `[S: audit 6]`.
   - *"I said, 'I am cut off' — You heard."* Three texts share the sequence "I said … cut off / thrust away … You heard my voice" `[S: audit 6]`:
     - **Jonah 2:3–6 [Eng 2:2–5]**: "I called … I cried for help; You heard my voice … waters closed over me … weeds wrapped around my head". The Greek of Lam 3:54, **εἶπα Ἀπῶσμαι** ("I said, I am thrust away"), is word for word Jonah 2:5 and occurs nowhere else.
     - **Ps 88:6–7 [Eng 88:5–6]**: "cut off (נִגְזָרוּ) from Your hand … in the lowest pit". Cut off + pit + lowest within a verse either side: only Ps 88:6 and Lam 3:54 (WLC).
     - **Ps 31:23 [Eng 31:22]**: "I said in my alarm, 'I am cut off from before Your eyes'; nevertheless You heard".
     With Ezek 37:11 ("we are cut off"), this is a family of the pit and its answer, not one source. `[T]`; *moderate* for the family, *low* for Ps 31 alone.
   - *David's appeal.* "May the LORD be judge … and **see**, and **plead my cause**" (1 Sam 24:16 [Eng 24:15]). Plead (verb) + cause + see + judge within a verse either side: **1 Sam 24:16 and Lam 3:58–59 only** (WLC), above chance on a fair test; the Greek κρῖνον τὴν κρίσιν μου is shared with **Ps 119:154**, "plead my cause and redeem me … give me life", which is co-equal. The wider claim that David's flight from Saul lies behind 3:52–61 and 4:18–20 **was not demonstrated**: the David–Saul block is above the median of random blocks, but every exclusive tie it holds contains "anointed" (4:20). `[T]`; *moderate* for 1 Sam 24:16; *low–moderate* for the field `[S: audit 6]`.
   - *The cause of my reproach.* Plead + cause + reproach within four verses: 1 Sam 25:39; Ps 74:22; Lam 3:58–61 (WLC) — the plea idiom. Ps 74:22–23 also has "all the day" and "those who rise up against You", as 3:62 has. `[T]`
   - *What it does.* The man's enemies, his pit and his rescue are told in the words of the psalms of the innocent sufferer and of Jonah's prayer. **The man of Lam 3 is not innocent (3:42) — but in 3:52–63 he is wronged.** `[I]`

   *Surfaced by:* Tool 11, Vocabulary, Original Languages, Christological Reading, Who Am I? *Confidence: high (the counts); moderate (Ps 35; the "cut off — heard" family; 1 Sam 24:16 with Ps 119:154); low–moderate (the David–Saul field).*

4. **"You heard … You drew near … 'Do not fear!' … You redeemed" (3:55–58) remembers a past hearing and argues from it, in the words of the lament psalms and the salvation oracle.**
   - *The lament psalms.* "Let my cry for help (שַׁוְעָתִי) come to You … incline Your ear to me; **in the day I call** answer me" (Ps 102:2–3). Cry + ear + day + call within two verses: Ps 102:2–3 and Lam 3:56–57 only; "in the day I call" in three verses (Ps 56:10; 102:3; Lam 3:57) (WLC). Without "day" the set also returns **Ps 18:7** (= 2 Sam 22:7), 3:56's best partner verse. Ps 102:20 has look down + heaven + LORD (with Ps 14:2 = 53:3; cf. Lam 3:50). **Ps 102 is one of a cluster of about eight lament psalms (22, 69, 74, 79, 88, 102, 137, 143) with dense Lamentations vocabulary; it stands out for rare-word density, not for exclusive ties.** Its superscription ("the afflicted", עָנִי) is a different word from Lam 3:1's "affliction" (עֳנִי). `[T]`; *moderate* `[S: audit 6]`.
   - *"Do not fear" and "You redeemed".* "Do not fear" (3:57) is the salvation-oracle formula (74 verses). Its pairing with "redeem" has an **Isaianic colouring** — Isa 41:13–14; 43:1; 54:4–6, with the Greek Μὴ φοβοῦ … λυτρόω at Isa 43:1 — but Isaiah 40–66 as a whole attracts the vocabulary of 3:54–58, and Isa 43 is not singled out. In Isa 43:1 God calls Israel by name; in Lam 3:55 the man calls on God's. "You have redeemed my life" (3:58) is the idiom of the **redeemer as legal advocate**: plead + cause + redeem within a verse only Jer 50:34; Ps 119:154; Prov 23:11; Lam 3:58 (WLC) `[S: audit 3]`. `[T]`; *low–moderate* for Isaiah's oracle `[S: audit 6]`.
   - *The perfects.* The forms are clear: 3:59 שָׁפְטָה and 3:63 הַבִּיטָה are imperatives; 3:56 "do not hide" is a negated jussive. 3:57 marks its quotation ("You said, 'Do not fear!'"); 3:56b is not marked, so it can be the content of the past prayer or a present plea breaking in. Rahlfs prints it as a quotation; Swete does not. `[T]` `[S: audit 6]`
     - **Read as quoted, 3:55–57 reports a past hearing**, and from 3:58 the perfects stand beside imperatives as grounds for the present plea: "You have seen — now judge". This is the reading the dig prefers; the forms allow it, but do not require it. `[I]`, *moderate*.
     - Both Greek editions read all of 3:55–61 as past (aorists), and render 3:59 as "You judged". `[T]`
   - *Poem 1's calls answered.* קָרָאתִי ("I called") occurs in the book only at 1:19 ("I called to my lovers, but they deceived me") and 3:55 ("I called on Your name"); by lemma 3:57 "in the day I called You" joins. "Hear" (שׁמע) has the peoples or the enemies at 1:18, 21, and God only at **3:56 and 3:61 — the only places in the book where God hears** (WLC). `[T]` for the chains `[S: audit 6]`; `[I]` for the answer, *high (lemmas); moderate (design)*.

   *Surfaced by:* Tool 11, Original Languages, Translations, Structure, Christological Reading. *Confidence: high (the counts; God hears only at 3:56, 61); moderate (Ps 102 within the lament cluster; the perfects as remembered grounds); low–moderate (Isaiah's oracle).*

5. **The imprecation (3:59–66) brings the enemies into God's court in the psalmists' and Jeremiah's words — and the pursuit turns from "us" to "them".**
   - *Ps 28:4.* "Requite them … according to the work of their hands (כְּמַעֲשֵׂה יְדֵיהֶם) … return to them their recompense (גְּמוּלָם)" (Ps 28:4). Lam 3:64: "You will return to them a recompense, O LORD, according to the work of their hands". Recompense + return + work + hand within a verse either side: Ps 28:4 and Lam 3:64 only (WLC); in Swete, κατὰ τὰ ἔργα τῶν χειρῶν αὐτῶν with ἀνταπόδομα only those two. "According to the work of their hands" also stands at **Jer 25:14**, paired, as in Ps 28:4, with "according to their deeds". `[T]` *Confidence: moderate–high* `[S: audit 6]`.
   - *The vengeance psalms.* Ps 94 ("O LORD, God of vengeance … render recompense to the proud … they crush Your people") shares the most vocabulary with 3:34–66 of any psalm, but no pairing that it alone shares; Ps 10 answers "He will never see" with "You have seen". `[T]`; *moderate* as a family `[S: audit 6]`.
   - *Jeremiah's confessions.* Vengeance + schemes within one to three verses: **Jer 11:20 and Lam 3:60 only** (WLC; the Greek of both has ἐκδίκησιν). But Jer 11:20 is "**Your** vengeance"; Lam 3:60 is "**their** vengeance" — the enemies' own vengeance, which stands elsewhere only at **Jer 20:10** ("let us take our vengeance on him"). Jer 18:18–23 has "let us devise schemes", the pit, "You know all their counsel against me" and "in the time of Your anger" (cf. 3:60–66). Jer 15:15 joins vengeance + pursue + reproach + "anger". Jer 20:7 already partners 3:14 `[S: audit]`. **The confessions that gave the man his laughingstock (3:14) give him his appeal (3:58–66).** `[T]`; `[I]`, *moderate* `[S: audit 6]`.
   - *The pursuit reversed.* "You have covered Yourself with anger and **pursued** us" (3:43) … "You will **pursue** them **in anger**" (3:66). **"Pursue in anger" (with the preposition) occurs only at Lam 3:43 and 3:66** (WLC) — an inclusio around the second half of the poem. (The wider "pursue + anger" list includes "nostrils" at Exod 15:8 and Lam 4:20 and "patience" at Jer 15:15, and is not evidence.) Amos 1:11 is Edom, who "pursued his brother with the sword … and his anger tore continually". `[T]` `[S: audit 6]`
   - *Answered later.* "Let all their wickedness come before You" (1:22) → "You will return to them a recompense" (3:64) → "the cup will pass to you also" (4:21) `[S: overview]`. `[T]`; `[I]`.

   *Surfaced by:* Tool 11, Repetition, Structure, Tone, Move 4. *Confidence: high (the 3:43 ↔ 3:66 inclusio); moderate–high (Ps 28:4); moderate (Jeremiah's confessions, Jer 20:10 leading; the vengeance-psalm family).*

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book level (from the overview, confirmed by the text).** Lamentations gives a ruined people ordered words — confession, protest, memory and petition — with which to keep addressing the LORD when he is silent `[S]` (overview); `[I]` as a reading of the whole. 3:34–66 holds all four of those words in one movement. `[I]`

**Passage level.** Four movements, each named by its first word: `[T]` for the contents

- **3:34–39, wisdom on justice.** The Lord does not approve (or see?) the crushing of prisoners and the twisting of justice; nothing happens unless he commands it; good and evil come from his mouth; why should a man complain? (Headline 1)
- **3:40–42, confession.** "Let us return … We have transgressed … You have not forgiven." (Headline 2)
- **3:43–54, complaint.** God's anger and pursuit; the cloud; scum among the peoples; the enemies' mouths; eyes that will not stop until the LORD looks down; hunted, pitted, drowned. (Headlines 2–3)
- **3:55–66, the appeal.** "I called … You heard … 'Do not fear' … You redeemed … judge my case … repay them." (Headlines 3–5)

**Passage-level purpose, in one sentence.** *To set the injustice of the siege before the Judge, confess without bargaining, keep weeping until he looks, and argue from a remembered hearing to a present verdict.* `[I]`

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement.** 3:31–33 is the book's centre: "the Lord will not reject forever … though He causes grief, He will have compassion … He does not afflict from His heart" (3:31–33). The centre leaves two questions:

1. **If God does not afflict "from his heart", what about the afflictions that men inflict?** The siege was carried out by people.
2. **If he will not reject forever, when does the rejection end?** `[I]`

**What 3:34–66 supplies, and does.**

1. **A judgement on human injustice.** The ל stanza continues the כ stanza's sentence: the Lord does not approve of crushing prisoners, denying justice and subverting a man's cause (3:34–36). `[T]`; `[I]`.
2. **A limit set by God's sovereignty.** Nothing happens unless the Lord commands it (3:37–38) — so the injustice is not outside his rule. `[T]`
3. **The community's first act.** 3:40–42 is the first "let us" in the book: the "we" appears for the first time outside 3:22 (overview). `[T]`
4. **A test of the centre.** "You have not forgiven" (3:42) and "You have covered Yourself with anger" (3:43) follow at once. **The centre's hope is tested in the same poem, not deferred to the next.** `[I]`
5. **The book's only hearing.** God hears only in 3:56 and 3:61 (Headline 4). `[T]`

**Why here and not elsewhere.** The book's centre falls between 3:33 and 3:34 (overview). Everything before it moves from assault to hope; everything after it must show what hope does while the assault goes on. The book's only quoted word of God (3:57) and its only "You have heard" (3:56, 61) have to come after the centre, because they are the experience the centre's theology promised. And they must come before poem 4, whose final declaration (4:22) presupposes that God has heard. `[I]`, *confidence moderate–high*.

#### Immediate context

- **Before.** 3:31–33, the three כִּי ("for") of the centre. `[T]`
- **After.** 4:1: "How dark the gold has become!" The narrator returns, and the man's "I" never comes back. `[T]`
- **Wider.** Poem 3 is a triple acrostic, each letter heading three verses; its 66 verses are as long as poems 1 and 2 in words (381 in the WLC index) `[S: overview]`. 3:34–66 is the half that follows the book's midpoint. `[T]`

### 3. Structure

**The form.** Eleven three-verse stanzas, ל to ת. As in poems 2 and 4, פ (3:46–48) precedes ע (3:49–51). BHS records a few manuscripts, some Greek manuscripts and the Syriac that reverse them `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`; Swete's letter-labels follow the standard order, so its Ἄιν stands in the middle of 3:45. **No point below rests on the order.** `[T]`

**The voices.** `[T]` (WLC, by person; counts of first- and second-person forms per stanza)

**Three movements of voice.** Impersonal wisdom (34–39) → "we" (40–47) → "I" (48–66). Address to God occurs at 3:42b–45 and 3:55–66. **The "I" of 3:48 is the "I" of 3:1, now speaking for the people's ruin ("the daughter of my people", 3:48).** `[T]`; `[I]`.

**Frames.** `[T]`

- **3:43 ↔ 3:66** — "You covered Yourself with anger and pursued us" … "You will pursue them in anger" `[S: sweep]`.
- **3:35–36 ↔ 3:58–59** — the court vocabulary returns. In the book, רִיב ("cause, lawsuit") occurs only at 3:36 and 3:58; מִשְׁפָּט ("justice") only at 3:35 and 3:59; and עות ("subvert") at 3:36 is answered by the hapax עַוָּתָה ("wrong") at 3:59 (WLC). **The poem's second half opens with justice perverted and closes with justice sought from the Judge.** `[T]` *Confidence: high.*
- **3:53 ↔ 3:58** — "they silenced **my life** (חַיַּי) in the pit" … "You redeemed **my life** (חַיָּי)"; the noun occurs in the book only in these two verses (WLC). `[T]`

**The divine names.** The book's two uses of עֶלְיוֹן ("Most High") are at 3:35 and 3:38, and its only אֵל ("God") is at 3:41. אֲדֹנָי ("Lord") stands at 3:36, 37 and 58, where BHS notes many manuscripts with יהוה `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`. יְהוָה ("the LORD") is at 3:40, 50, 55, 59, 61, 64 and 66 (WLC). **The rare titles cluster in the theology of 3:35–41; the covenant name carries the appeal.** `[T]` for the distribution; `[I]` for the pattern.

### 4. Linking Words

- **The infinitives of 3:34–36** (לְדַכֵּא … לְהַטּוֹת … לְעַוֵּת, "to crush … to turn aside … to subvert") have no main verb until "the Lord does not see/approve". They depend either on 3:33 ("He does not afflict … to crush") or on 3:36b. **The ESV and NASB95 take 3:36b as the main clause.** `[T]`; `[I]`.
- **מִי זֶה ("who is this …?", 3:37)** and **מַה ("why?", 3:39)** are rhetorical questions. `[T]`
- **The cohortatives of 3:40–41** (נַחְפְּשָׂה … וְנַחְקֹרָה … וְנָשׁוּבָה, "let us search … let us examine … let us return"). `[T]`
- **אַתָּה ("You", 3:42)** sets God against "we" (נַחְנוּ) in the same line: "**We** have transgressed … **You** have not forgiven." The emphatic pronouns face each other. `[T]`
- **עַד ("until", 3:50)** gives the weeping its term: "My eye will not stop … **until** the LORD looks down and sees". `[T]`
- **No connector before the imperfects of 3:64–66.** "You will repay … give … pursue" follow the imperatives of 3:59 and 3:63 without "therefore". `[T]`

### 5. Parallels

- **3:34–36** — three parallel infinitives, each a different miscarriage of justice: crushing prisoners, denying justice, subverting a case. `[T]`
- **3:42** — antithetic: "We have transgressed and rebelled" ‖ "You have not forgiven". `[T]`
- **3:43–44** — the same verb twice: סַכֹּתָה בָאַף … סַכּוֹתָה בֶעָנָן ("You covered Yourself with anger … You covered Yourself with a cloud"). `[T]`
- **3:47** — sound pairs: פַּחַד וָפַחַת … הַשֵּׁאת וְהַשָּׁבֶר (*paḥad wāpaḥat … haššēʾt wəhaššāber*, "terror and pit … devastation and destruction") `[S: audit]` for the first pair. `[T]`
- **3:48–51** — "my eye(s)" opens three lines (3:48, 49, 51). `[T]`
- **3:59–61** — "You have seen … You have seen … You have heard". `[T]`
- **3:64–66** — three imperfects, each of God against "them". `[T]`

### 6. Narrator's Comment

There is no narrator in poem 3. The man speaks throughout, as teacher (3:25–39), as one of the "we" (3:40–47) and as "I" (3:1–21, 48–66). `[T]` **The passage's only quoted speech is God's ("Do not fear", 3:57) and the man's own (3:54, 56).** `[T]`

### 9. Tone and Feel

1. **3:34–39: grave and argumentative.** Three infinitives, then three questions. It is the voice of a teacher reasoning, the most abstract verses in the book. `[I]`
2. **3:40–42: earnest, then stark.** Three cohortatives of resolve, then the line where "we" and "You" meet and nothing happens. `[I]`
3. **3:43–47: accusatory.** Six verbs with God as "You" in three verses, then the enemies' mouths and a rush of paired nouns. `[T]` for the counts.
4. **3:48–51: weeping without end.** "My eye … my eye … my eye". The only word of time is "until". `[I]`
5. **3:52–54: sinking.** Hunted, silenced, stoned, flooded: "I am cut off". `[T]`
6. **3:55–58: the turn.** The only verses in the book where God is heard, near, and speaking. `[T]`
7. **3:59–66: a legal plea becoming a sentence.** It begins "judge my case" and ends "destroy them". `[T]`

**Sound.** 3:47's double alliteration; 3:52's צוֹד צָדוּנִי (*ṣôd ṣādûnî*, "hunting they hunted me"); the -תָם ("their") ending at 3:60–63 — נִקְמָתָם, מַחְשְׁבֹתָם, חֶרְפָּתָם, הֶגְיוֹנָם, שִׁבְתָּם, קִימָתָם, מַנְגִּינָתָם — seven forms in four verses. `[T]`

**OT Citation Triad — Ps 28:4 at 3:64.**

- **Move 1, source context.** Ps 28 is David's lament: "Do not drag me away with the wicked … who speak peace with their neighbours, while evil is in their hearts. Requite them according to their work … according to the work of their hands; return to them their recompense. Because they do not regard the works of the LORD … He will tear them down" (28:3–5). It turns at once: "Blessed be the LORD, because He has heard the voice of my supplication" (28:6). `[T]`
- **Move 2, book usage.** Lamentations has asked for this before: "Let all their wickedness come before You; and deal with them as You have dealt with me" (1:22). `[T]`
- **Move 3, OT-to-OT.** The same request in Ps 94:2 ("render recompense to the proud"), Jer 51:6, 56 (against Babylon), Obad 15 (against Edom), Joel 4:4, 7 [Eng 3:4, 7]. `[T]` for the texts.
- **What the use does.** The man asks for the psalmist's justice in the psalmist's words, and like the psalmist he has already said "You have heard" (3:56, 61; Ps 28:6). **The imprecation is the prayer of a man who has been heard, not of one who despairs.** `[I]`, *high*.

**OT Citation Triad — 1 Kgs 8:46–50 at 3:40–42.**

- **Move 1.** Solomon's dedication prayer foresees exile: "If they sin against You — for there is no one who does not sin — and You are angry with them and deliver them to an enemy, so that they take them away captive … if they take thought … and repent … saying, 'We have sinned … we have acted wickedly' … and pray to You toward their land … then hear their prayer … and forgive (וְסָלַחְתָּ) Your people … all their transgressions" (1 Kgs 8:46–50). `[T]`
- **Move 2.** The book's earlier temple texts: the sanctuary entered by the nations (1:10); the altar rejected, the sanctuary disowned (2:7); and 1 Kgs 9:8's warning fulfilled at 2:15 `[S: overview]`. `[T]`
- **Move 3.** Deut 4:29–31; 30:1–3 (return from exile and God's compassion); Jer 33:8 (the promise to forgive); Dan 9:4–19 (the confession over a desolate Jerusalem: "O Lord, forgive!", 9:19) — two books later in the Writings. `[T]` for the texts.

### 12. Genre

- **3:34–39, wisdom** — sentences about God's justice and sovereignty, in the form of a teacher's argument (infinitives, questions). `[T]`
- **3:40–47, communal confession and complaint** — the form of Ps 44; 74; 79; 80 and of Solomon's prayer, but with God's non-forgiveness stated. `[T]`; `[I]`.
- **3:48–66, individual lament with a report of hearing** — the shape of Pss 22; 28; 31; 69: complaint, the report that God has heard, petition and imprecation. **The report of hearing comes in the middle, not at the end**, so the man goes on praying after he has been heard. `[I]`
- **Inside an acrostic.** The alphabet supplies stock words at each letter, so a same-letter match in another acrostic is partly convention `[S: 3:1–33 dig]`. `[I]`

### 13. Copycat

What the passage models, cautiously: `[I]`

- **Examining one's ways before complaining** (3:39–40).
- **Confession without bargaining** (3:42: the confession stands though pardon has not come).
- **Weeping as prayer, kept up until God looks** (3:49–50).
- **Arguing from a past hearing to a present plea** (3:55–59).
- **Leaving vengeance to God** (3:64–66) — the man asks; he does not act.

What it does not model: self-justification (3:42 forbids it), or the cursing of persons in private quarrels (Pitfall 4).

### 14. Bible Timeline

- **The event:** the siege and fall of 588–586 BC, presupposed, not narrated (overview). The pit, the stone and the water (3:53–54) belong to the poem's imagery; trap 5 forbids reading them as Jeremiah's cistern (Jer 38:6, which had no water) `[S: overview]`. `[I]`
- **The canon.** In the Writings, Lamentations' confession (3:40–42) is followed two books later by Daniel's (Dan 9:4–19), which asks for the forgiveness 3:42 says has not come: "O Lord, hear! O Lord, forgive!" (Dan 9:19). `[T]` for the texts; `[I]` for the sequence.
- **Behind the language:** the Torah's law court (Exod 23:6), the wilderness complaint (Num 11:1), the temple's dedication (1 Kgs 8), David's flight from Saul (1 Sam 24–26), the lament psalms (Pss 28; 31; 35; 69; 88; 94; 102), Job's debate, Jeremiah's confessions and Isaiah's salvation oracles. `[T]` for the texts.

**Where a hearer stands.** Most hearers will stand with the "we" of 3:40–42 more readily than with the man of 3:52–66. The passage asks them to do both: to confess with the community and to appeal with the man. **Some will hear 3:52–66 as their own story of injustice. The passage lets them bring it to court — God's court, not their own.** `[I]`

### 16. So What?

Raw material for /point-purpose, not a sermon:

- **God sees injustice** (3:36 → 3:59–60). The question "Does the Lord not see?" is answered by "You have seen". `[I]`
- **Confession is not a lever.** "We have transgressed … You have not forgiven" (3:42). The people pray Solomon's prayer and wait. `[I]`
- **Weep until he looks.** The man's tears are directed: "until the LORD looks down and sees" (3:50). `[I]`
- **Remembered hearing grounds present prayer.** "You heard … You drew near … You said, 'Do not fear!'" (3:55–57) is the basis for "Judge my case" (3:59). `[I]`
- **Bring the wrong to court.** The imprecation hands the enemy to God's justice in the words of the psalms (Ps 28:4). `[I]`
- **Candidate fallen-condition focus.** When we are wronged and God seems not to see, we either stop praying or take justice into our own hands; and when we have sinned, we treat confession as a way to make God answer. `[I]`

## Extensions

### Original Language Observations

- **אֲדֹנָי לֹא רָאָה (3:36).** Three readings `[T]` for the grammar:
  1. **"The Lord does not approve"** (ESV, NASB95): ראה in the sense "look on with favour" (cf. Hab 1:13). It takes 3:34–36 as the objects.
  2. **"The Lord does not see"**, as a statement: the slogan of Ezek 8:12; 9:9; Ps 94:7 — unthinkable on the man's lips after 3:31–33.
  3. **"Does the Lord not see?"**, an unmarked question, as Ps 94:9 asks it ("He who formed the eye, does He not see?").

  Readings 1 and 3 both fit; reading 3 has the support of Ps 94 (crush + "not see" in the same short span, Headline 1) and of the poem's own answer at 3:59–60. The Greek's "the Lord did not speak" is a different text (Tool 8). `[I]`, *moderate*: **say both 1 and 3 in the pulpit, and hold the chain.**
- **The perfects of 3:55–61** (Headline 4). The precative-perfect reading ("Hear! Draw near! Redeem!") is grammatically possible, but 3:56 quotes the past prayer that was heard, and 3:59 sets an imperative beside a perfect. `[T]`; `[I]`, *moderate–high*.
- **The jussive sense of 3:64–66.** תָּשִׁיב, תִּתֵּן, תִּרְדֹּף are imperfects, which can be future ("You will repay") or wish ("May You repay"); the form does not decide. וְתַשְׁמִידֵם ("and destroy them") continues them. `[T]`
- **The -תָם / -ם cluster** (3:60–63; Tool 9): the enemies are named only by what is "theirs". `[T]`
- **אַתָּה / נַחְנוּ (3:42).** The short form נַחְנוּ ("we") is rare: five verses in the Hebrew Bible — Gen 42:11; Exod 16:7, 8; Num 32:32; Lam 3:42 (WLC). Two are Moses and Aaron's "what are we?" in the wilderness grumbling (Exod 16:7–8), beside Num 11:1 at 3:39 `[T]`. Its weight is in the face-to-face pronouns; the wilderness tie is *low*.
- **Greek δωρεάν** at 3:52 = Ps 34:19; 68:5 (Swete) = John 15:25. `[T]` (Headline 3).

### Historical and Cultural Background

- **Prisoners and the courts.** "Prisoners of the earth" (3:34) and the perversion of justice (3:35–36) describe the conquered: captives under foot (cf. the victor's foot on the neck, Josh 10:24) and courts that no longer protect the weak. The Torah's courtroom laws (Exod 23:6; Deut 16:19; 24:17; 27:19) are the standard the verses invoke. `[T]` for the texts; `[I]` for the setting.
- **The pit.** A cistern (בּוֹר) used as a prison is attested in Gen 37:24 (Joseph) and Jer 38:6 (Jeremiah, "no water, only mud") `[T]`. 3:53–55 has water over the head, so the image is a drowning pit or the pit of death (Ps 88:5–7) rather than Jeremiah's cistern (overview, trap 5). `[I]`
- **Stoning.** "They threw a stone on me" (3:53) may be a stone sealing the pit (cf. Dan 6:18 [Eng 6:17]; Josh 10:18) or stoning. Either fits; the poem does not say. `[I]`
- **The salvation oracle.** "Do not fear" as a priestly or prophetic answer to a lament is a widely held form-critical reconstruction (Begrich) `[S]`, recalled, not checked. What the text shows is that the formula is Isaiah's (Isa 41:10–14; 43:1, 5; 44:2; 54:4), and Deut 31:6, 8; Josh 8:1 `[T]`.

### Original Audience Reception

- **The survivors** would hear in 3:34–36 their own experience of conquest — prisoners crushed, courts corrupt — and in 3:37–38 the hard claim that even this was not outside God's word. `[I]`
- **3:40–42** would sound like the temple liturgy they knew (1 Kgs 8), and its last line would be honest about the silence that followed. `[I]`
- **3:52–58** would offer a precedent: one of them had called from the pit and been heard. **The "I" makes the hope personal, but the "we" of 3:40–47 shows it is meant for all.** `[I]`
- **3:64–66** would give them words for their anger at the enemy (Babylon, Edom) that kept the vengeance in God's hands. `[I]`
- **The reader of the Writings** comes with Job's arguments about whether God perverts justice (Job 8:3; 19:6; 34:12) — and Job stands five books earlier in the BHS order `[S: overview]`. 3:34–36 enters that debate on the side of "the Almighty will not pervert justice" (Job 34:12), yet without denying the wrong done (3:59). `[I]`

### Biblical-Theological Themes

1. **God sees.** From Hagar's "You are a God who sees" (Gen 16:13) and the exodus ("I have surely seen the affliction of My people", Exod 3:7) to the denials "the LORD does not see" (Ezek 8:12; 9:9; Ps 94:7) and the answer "You have seen" (Lam 3:59–60). `[T]` for the texts.
2. **Forgiveness withheld and promised.** 1 Kgs 8:50 (asked) → Lam 3:42 (not yet) → Jer 31:34; 33:8; Dan 9:19 (promised, asked again) → the new covenant (Heb 8:12; 10:17). `[T]` for the texts.
3. **The innocent sufferer hated without cause.** Ps 35:19; 69:5 → Lam 3:52 → John 15:25. `[T]`
4. **The Redeemer who pleads the cause.** Prov 23:11; Jer 50:34; Ps 119:154; Lam 3:58; with Job 19:25 ("I know that my Redeemer lives") `[S: Job queue #58]`. `[T]` for the texts.
5. **Vengeance belongs to God.** Deut 32:35 ("Vengeance is Mine"); Ps 94:1; Lam 3:60–66; Rom 12:19. `[T]` for the texts.
6. **The call and the answer.** "Call upon Me in the day of trouble; I will deliver you" (Ps 50:15); "in the day I call" (Ps 102:3; Lam 3:57); "everyone who calls on the name of the LORD will be saved" (Joel 3:5 [Eng 2:32]; Rom 10:13). `[T]` for the texts.

### Christological Reading

Lamentations is not predictive prophecy (overview). The man is a type of the people for whom Christ suffers before he is a type of Christ (3:1–33 dig). `[I]`

1. **Hated without cause — the strongest route.** Lam 3:52 shares "without cause" + "enemies" exclusively with Ps 35:19 and Ps 69:5 (Headline 3), and the Greek of all three has δωρεάν. Jesus says of himself, "They hated Me without a cause" (John 15:25), quoting the psalm line. Ps 69 is also the psalm of zeal for God's house (John 2:17), reproach (Rom 15:3) and vinegar (Matt 27:34, 48). **The man of Lam 3 shares the persecuted psalmist's experience; Christ takes that psalmist's words as his own.** *Confidence: moderate–high.* `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the route.
2. **"I said, 'I am cut off' — You heard."** Ps 31 gives the man's turn (Headline 3), and Ps 31:6 [Eng 31:5] gives Jesus' last word, "Into Your hand I commit my spirit" (Luke 23:46) `[S: audit 5]` (the Ps 31 route, *moderate–high*). Isa 53:8 uses Lam 3:54's verb of the Servant: "he was cut off (נִגְזַר) out of the land of the living". *Confidence: moderate.*
3. **Crushed — by whom?** The man says the Lord does not approve "crushing" (דכא) prisoners (3:34). Isaiah says of the Servant: "He was crushed (מְדֻכָּא) for our iniquities … the LORD was pleased to crush (דַּכְּאוֹ) him" (Isa 53:5, 10). **The God who does not will the crushing of the oppressed willed the crushing of the Servant for the oppressors' sins.** This is a theological contrast, not a link the text draws. *Confidence: low–moderate* `[I]`.
4. **"Do not fear … I have redeemed you" — the Redeemer's word.** The salvation oracle the man receives (3:57–58) is Isaiah's (Isa 43:1); Isaiah's Redeemer "will come to Zion, to those in Jacob who turn from transgression" (Isa 59:20, quoted in Rom 11:26). *Confidence: moderate.*
5. **The contrast at the imprecation.** The man asks God to repay his enemies (3:64–66); Christ prays, "Father, forgive them" (Luke 23:34). **This is not a correction of Lam 3**, which leaves vengeance with God (Rom 12:19 quotes Deut 32:35), **but it marks where the type ends.** *Confidence: high (contrast).*

**Moralism check.** The tempting moral is "When you are wronged, pray like the man and God will vindicate you." The text resists it: the man has confessed shared guilt (3:42); his hearing is God's free act, not the reward of his prayer; and his plea ends in an imprecation the church prays only through Christ's cross. **The gospel ground is the God who sees, hears and redeems, and the Son who was hated without cause and cut off for the guilty.** `[I]`

### Difficult / Contested Verses

- **3:36** — see Original Language Observations. *Handling:* name the crux; preach "God sees" from the chain, not from 3:36 alone.
- **3:37–38 — evil from the mouth of the Most High.** "Is it not from the mouth of the Most High that both evils and good go out?" The claim is God's sovereignty over calamity (cf. Isa 45:7; Amos 3:6), not God's authorship of sin; 3:33 has just said "He does not afflict from His heart". Jer 44:17–18 shows the alternative the poem rejects: the exiles in Egypt blamed their calamity on neglecting the queen of heaven (Tool 11). *Handling:* hold 3:33 and 3:38 together. `[I]`
- **3:42 — "You have not forgiven."** A congregation may hear it as a denial of grace. It is a report of the present silence, framed by "He will not reject forever" (3:31) and followed by "Do not fear" (3:57). *Handling:* do not soften it; do not leave it alone. `[I]`
- **3:64–66 — the imprecation.** See overview trap 7, and Christological Reading 5. *Handling:* preach it as prayer that leaves vengeance with God (Rom 12:19), and name the difference the cross makes. `[I]`
- **3:65 — "dullness of heart … Your curse".** Two words found nowhere else; the sense is a hardened or covered heart (cf. Isa 6:10) and a curse. *Handling:* do not build a doctrine of judicial hardening on a hapax. `[I]`

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Preaching 3:36 as "God doesn't see"

**The mistake.** Taking "the Lord does not see" as the man's complaint, or hiding the crux behind "does not approve" and never mentioning "see".
**Why it is tempting.** The Hebrew can be read that way, and the English versions remove the problem.
**Corrective.** Name the readings and preach the chain: "does He not see?" (3:36) → "until He looks down and sees" (3:50) → "You have seen" (3:59–60). *Bites at:* 3:34–36.

### Pitfall 2: Confession as a guarantee

**The mistake.** Preaching 3:40–42 as "confess, and God will forgive you at once".
**Why it is tempting.** It is true in Christ (1 John 1:9), and it sounds pastoral.
**Corrective.** The verse says "You have not forgiven". The gospel promise must be reached through the canon's answer (Jer 33:8; Dan 9:19; the new covenant), not read into 3:42. *Bites at:* 3:40–45.

### Pitfall 3: "He has already rescued you"

**The mistake.** Building on the perfects of 3:55–61 as if the rescue were finished.
**Why it is tempting.** The English and the Greek read them as past.
**Corrective.** The man remembers a hearing and argues from it (Headline 4); he still asks "judge my case" (3:59) and "look" (3:63), and the book ends asking (5:22). *Bites at:* 3:55–63.

### Pitfall 4: Licensing private vengeance

**The mistake.** Using 3:64–66 to bless the congregation's wish to see a personal enemy destroyed.
**Why it is tempting.** The imprecation is vivid, and many hearers carry wrongs.
**Corrective.** The enemies are the destroyers of a people; the man hands them to God; the church prays after Christ's "Father, forgive them" (Luke 23:34) and Paul's "never avenge yourselves" (Rom 12:19). *Bites at:* 3:59–66.

### Pitfall 5: Jeremiah in the cistern

**The mistake.** Reading 3:52–55 as Jeremiah's experience in Jer 38.
**Why it is tempting.** A pit, enemies, a prophet's words elsewhere in the poem (3:14; 3:60).
**Corrective.** Jeremiah's cistern had no water (Jer 38:6); the man has water over his head (3:54). His words come from Jeremiah's confessions, but his experience is the psalmists' pit (Ps 69; 88) (overview, trap 5). *Bites at:* 3:52–55.

### Pitfall 6: Making the man Christ

**The mistake.** Preaching 3:52–66 as Christ's passion because of "without cause" and "cut off".
**Why it is tempting.** The NT quotes the psalm line (John 15:25), and Isa 53:8 has "cut off".
**Corrective.** The man confesses sin (3:42) and asks for vengeance (3:64). Christ is reached through the psalms he made his own, and in contrast with the imprecation (Christological Reading). *Bites at:* 3:52–66.
