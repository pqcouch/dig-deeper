## What this audit tested, and how

**Three blind auditors, run one after another.** None of them saw the three digs, the earlier audits or each other's reports. Each received a neutral brief, the claims restated, the staged corpus and the same searches, and was told to try to **break** the claims.

| Auditor | Claims | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | L116–L119, L123–L125, L128–L129 (with L130), L134–L135, and the 3:34–66 dig's internal counts | The claims under the five 3:34–66 headlines |
| **B** | L139–L142 (with L98), L146, L151, L156–L157, L99–L100 | The single contacts under the 4:1–16 and 4:17–22 headlines |
| **C** | L110 + L127 (the David–Saul field); L92 + L152–L155 (the prophetic-indictment field); L131 + L148 (Ps 102); L132; L104–L106; L109 | The field and design claims, and the prophetic "end" |

**Method for each claim:**

1. Check the wording on both sides in Hebrew and Greek.
2. Count how rare the shared word set is, **searching every entry wherever the lemma index splits a word-family**.
3. **Run the fair chance test** of audit 5 for any claim that rests on an exclusive pair or triple of ordinary words.
4. Search for rival sources, and rank the proposed partner among all partners of the Lamentations verse.
5. Run a chance baseline for any field or design claim, and report every rank under at least two settings.
6. Score the seven Hays criteria. "History of interpretation" is "not checked" throughout, because no commentary was consulted.
7. Give a verdict and a recommended confidence.

**Two field tests, as the queue asked.**

- **The David–Saul block.** 1 Sam 23:14–27:12 with 2 Sam 1 (146 verses) was set against 22,769 random blocks of the same length, and against eight named narrative and psalm blocks. The score was the number of word sets that the block alone shares with 3:52–66 and 4:17–20.
- **The prophetic-indictment field.** Isa 1, Isa 59 and Zeph 3 were ranked against 4:1–16 at four settings, beside nine other indictment chapters as controls (Isa 3, 5; Jer 5, 7; Ezek 22; Hos 4; Amos 5; Mic 3, 6), and with a block test of exclusive word sets over all 924 chapters.

**A third test, for C3:** the 150 psalms were ranked by the word sets each shares with the whole of Lamentations, adjusted for length, on three measures.

**The baselines reproduced.** All three auditors reproduced the per-verse baseline first: across the 154 verses of Lamentations, the best partner verse elsewhere scores a median of 13.6 and a top tenth of 19.6 (WLC). In poem 4 only 4:11 (23.9, with Zeph 3:8) and 4:2 (19.7, with Isa 30:14) reach the top tenth. Auditor B measured the audit-5 rate for poem 4: of 509 pairs of words (each in at most 300 verses) taken from single verses of Lam 4, **84 (16.5 %) are shared with exactly one other passage**. "Only these two" for ordinary words starts there.

**Positive controls** were run before every nil or "only" result, and every exclusivity search returned the Lamentations verse itself. The index traps that decided results are listed under *What the audit adds*.

## Claims Audited

### The 3:34–66 headline claims (Auditor A)

#### L116, L119 — "Ps 94 lies behind 3:34–36 and 3:59–66, and the slogan 'the LORD does not see' resolves the crux of 3:36"

**Claim.** The slogan of the guilty, "the LORD does not see", stands in Ezek 8:12; 9:9 and Ps 94:7. "Crush" with God as the one said not to see occurs within two verses only at Ps 94:5–7 and Lam 3:34–36. Ps 94 is joint 3rd of 6,107 twenty-three-verse windows against 3:34–66. Inside Lam 3, "see" at 3:36 is answered at 3:59–60.

**What held.** `[T]`

- The window rank reproduces: Ps 94:1–23 shares 13 rarer words with 3:34–66 and is joint 3rd, below two Isa 1 windows. It stays in the top 20 at four settings. Of the vengeance psalms it is clearly the strongest by volume (Ps 10 and Ps 109 rank 197th, Ps 73 639th).
- "See" in Lam 3 only at 3:1, 36, 50, 59, 60. The verb עות ("subvert", 3:36) and the noun עַוָּתָה ("my wrong", 3:59) share a root, though the index files them apart. The answer from 3:36 to 3:59 is real and internal.
- The Greek of 3:36 reads Κύριος οὐκ εἶπεν ("the Lord did not speak") in Swete and Rahlfs, and loses "see". Greek Ps 94:7 [Swete 93:7] has Οὐκ ὄψεται Κύριος, so the Greek link with Ps 94 is gone.

**What did not.**

- *"Only Ps 94" is wrong.* Searched across all four "crush" entries, the motif occurs at **Ps 10:10–11**, Ps 94:5–7, Job 22:9–11 and Lam 3:34–36. Ps 10:10's word is a ketiv filed under a different "crush" entry, and Ps 10:11's "not" is בַּל, not לֹא, so the dig's search missed it. Ps 10:11 has God as the subject ("He will never see"). Job 22:9–11 has a man, but at five verses either side Job 22:13–14 supplies the divine slogan.
- *Ps 10 is the closer in form.* It answers its own slogan at 10:14: רָאִתָה ("You have seen"). That is the move Lam 3:59–60 makes with רָאִיתָה twice.
- *No exclusive trace.* On a block test, Ps 94 shares **no** pair of words with 3:34–66 that no other passage has (rank 581, level with most of the canon). Jer 11, Ps 88 and Ps 69 each share three or four. A source would be expected to leave one.
- *The slogan family is wider, and it always names a speaker.* Nine verses quote the slogan — Ezek 8:12; 9:9; Ps 10:11; 64:6; 73:11; 94:7; Isa 29:15; 47:10; Job 22:13–14 — and every one introduces it with a verb of saying. **Lam 3:36 has none.**

**The crux, on the text alone.** Three readings are possible:

- a *statement*, "the Lord does not approve [of] crushing … turning aside … subverting", the simplest grammar. Num 23:21 ("He has not seen trouble in Israel") is a precedent for "see" as "regard"; it is one of only two verses where God is the subject of a negated perfect of "see" (the other is Ps 10:11);
- an *unmarked question*, "Does the Lord not see?", supported by the matching clause at 3:37 (Lord + "not" + perfect) and the unmarked question at 3:38, and by the book's chain 3:36 → 3:50 → 3:59;
- a *quoted slogan*, the weakest, because nothing marks a speaker.

Auditor A rates the first two roughly level, the question slightly ahead. **The slogan is an overlay, not a resolution.** `[I]`

**Verdict (L116): Needs reframing. Confidence: low–moderate** for "Ps 94 lies behind"; **moderate** for "3:34–36 and 3:59–66 share the idiom of the vengeance psalms, of which Ps 94 is the fullest member and Ps 10 the closest in form". **Verdict (L119): Uncertain — flag for research. Confidence: low** as the answer to the crux; present it as one of three readings. Ezek 8:12 and 9:9 stay as the slogan's prophetic setting.

#### L117, L118 — "3:35–36 speaks the Torah's court language and enters Job's debate"

- *Held, and stronger than claimed (L117).* Turn aside + justice in one verse: 11 verses (one of them, 1 Kgs 8:58, "incline our hearts", in another sense). With "lawsuit" within a verse either side: Exod 23:6, Prov 18:5 and Lam 3:35–36 only. **Exod 23:6 has the identical form בְּרִיבוֹ ("in his lawsuit")** and the same construct "the justice of [a person]". בְּרִיבוֹ occurs only at Exod 23:3, 6; Prov 18:17; Lam 3:36. Random triples in these frequency bands find one or two partners only 13–15 % of the time. `[T]`
- *Held as a count, reframed in sense (L118).* "Pervert" (עות) + justice within a verse either side: Job 8:3; 19:6–7; 34:12 and Lam 3:36, as claimed. At two verses either side, Ps 146:9 joins ("the way of the wicked He bends", beside 146:7 "who executes justice"). The verb occurs in only ten verses, three of them in Job, so the result largely reflects where the word lives. In Lam 3:36 the object of עות is **a person** (אָדָם), as in Job 19:6 ("God has wronged me") and Ps 119:78, not "justice" as in Job 8:3 and 34:12. And Eccl 1:15; 7:13 ("who can make straight what He has bent?") also make God the subject. The question "does God bend?" is wisdom-wide.
- *Greek.* 3:35 τοῦ ἐκκλῖναι κρίσιν keeps the court idiom; 3:36 καταδικάσαι ("to condemn") flattens עות.

**Verdict (L117): Confirmed. Confidence: moderate–high.** Lead with Exod 23:6 and בְּרִיבוֹ. **Verdict (L118): Needs reframing. Confidence: moderate** for "3:36 asks the question Job's speakers argue about"; **low** for "enters Job's debate", which implies a dependence the text does not show. Add Ps 146:9 and Eccl 7:13.

#### L123, L124, L125 — "3:40–45 prays Solomon's prayer for exiles (1 Kgs 8:46–50) and records that it was not answered; Jer 33:8 the promise; Isa 1:15 and 59:1–2 explain refused prayer"

- *A miscount (L124).* Forgive + transgress within a verse either side, verb **or** noun as the dig said, gives five places, not three: 1 Kgs 8:50, Jer 33:8, Lam 3:42, **Num 14:19** (the noun at 14:18) and **Jer 5:7** (the noun at 5:6). "Only three" holds for the **verb** of "transgress", at any window up to two verses. Jer 5:7 ("How can I pardon you?") is the closest in sense to "You have not forgiven". A negated "forgive" occurs in nine verses, among them **2 Kgs 24:4**: "the LORD was not willing to forgive" — the Kings narrative's own verdict on Jerusalem, a rival for 3:42b the dig does not name. `[T]`
- *The window rank holds (L123).* 1 Kgs 8:47–58 is joint 2nd against 3:34–42 (with Num 24, Isa 1, Ps 18 and Ps 44), and stays in the top 15 at five settings. The control passes: 2 Chr 6, the parallel prayer, ranks a little lower only because 2 Chr 6:39 omits "all their transgressions". The rivals the brief named rank far lower (Deut 4:29 195th; Hos 14:2 585th; Neh 9 585th; Dan 9 2,073rd).
- *The strongest datum, missed.* **Palms + heaven in one verse: 1 Kgs 8:22, 8:54, 2 Chr 6:13 and Lam 3:41 only** — Solomon spreading his palms toward heaven, and "let us lift up our heart with our palms to God in heaven". The Greek keeps part of it (χεῖρες … οὐρανόν). `[T]`
- *What weakens it.* The confession is not Solomon's: 1 Kgs 8:47 "we have sinned, done wrong, acted wickedly" is the formula of Ps 106:6 and Dan 9:5; Lam 3:42 "we transgressed and rebelled" is its own (the pair elsewhere only Ps 5:11). "Return to the LORD" (3:40) is Deuteronomy's and Hosea's idiom (Deut 4:30; 30:2; Hos 14:2), not Kings'. The Greek wording differs throughout.
- *The cloud.* Cover + cloud in one verse only Lam 3:44, as claimed; at three verses either side 1 Kgs 8:7 joins (the cherubim "covered", with the cloud at 8:10). Thematic, with a small lexical support.
- *Isa 1:15 and 59 (L125).* Hands + hide + prayer in one verse: Isa 1:15 only, as claimed, and the window ranks reproduce. But Isa 1:15's sharpest contact is with **Lam 3:8**, not 3:44: "even though" (גַּם כִּי) + prayer stands only in Isa 1:15 ("even though you multiply prayer I will not hear") and Lam 3:8 ("even though I cry out … He shuts out my prayer"). For "do not hide" at 3:56 the closer partner is **Ps 55:2 [Eng 55:1]** ("do not hide Yourself from my supplication"), the only other verse with the negative + עלם. Isa 59:2 uses a different verb for hiding.

**Verdict (L123): Confirmed with nuance. Confidence: moderate.** 3:40–44 reads well as the exiles' prayer of 1 Kgs 8:46–50 met with non-forgiveness; state it on palms + heaven and the window rank, and say that its confession and "return" idiom are not Solomon's. **Verdict (L124): Confirmed with nuance. Confidence: moderate**; correct the count to the verb, and add Jer 5:7 and 2 Kgs 24:4. "The promise" for Jer 33:8 is interpretation; Jer 31:34 and 50:20 are also forgiveness promises. **Verdict (L125): Needs reframing. Confidence: moderate** as a parallel on refused prayer — Isa 1:15 at 3:8 — not as the explanation of 3:44.

#### L128, L129 (with L130) — "The man of 3:52–56 is the psalmist hated without cause (Ps 35:19; 69:5) who said 'I am cut off' and was heard (Ps 31:23)"

- *Held, but chance-level (L128).* "Without cause" + enemy within a verse either side: Ps 35:19, Ps 69:5 and Lam 3:52, as claimed (at two verses, 1 Sam 25:31 joins). ## Critical assessment — the gaps in this audit

1. **History of interpretation was not checked.** Every verdict rests on the corpus. The ketiv at 4:3, the crux of 3:36, the perfects of 3:55–63, Jer 14 at Lam 4 and the Dan 9:24 qere are the likeliest to have a commentary history; all are on the Logos list.
2. **The auditors were blind to the digs, but not to their phrasing.** The claims were restated from the digs. Their corrections to the restatement — Isa 59:19 is not the formula; Ezek 5:1 is not "another sense"; the "nostrils" hits — show the wording was tested, not trusted.
3. **The block tests are post hoc in one respect.** The David–Saul block and the indictment chapters were named by the digs, so the controls were chosen afterwards. Random blocks guard against that, and both fields still failed to stand out; the result is a statement about rate, not a proof that no field exists.
4. **The chance tests measure exclusivity, not meaning.** A shared sequence (Cain) or an exact phrase (בְּרִיבוֹ) can matter more than a count shows, and a count can hide an idiom. The verdicts weigh both.
5. **One verdict rests on a reading the corpus cannot settle.** Whether 4:3 has serpents or jackals is a textual question; until it is read in Logos, the Deut 32:33 link carries `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`.
6. **L108 and the unflagged single contacts were not tested.** They stay queued and carry no weight in any headline.
