Headline Findings (sweep level)

The strongest claims from the whole sweep. Each emerged under several tools, and every word-level element was verified by lemma.

  1. Three words run as a thread from the book’s opening lament through its middle to its close: “crooked”, “straighten” and “set in order”. [T] (synthetic)
    • The first two open together. “What is מְעֻוָּת (‘crooked’) cannot be לִתְקֹן (‘straightened’)” (1:15). “Who is able לְתַקֵּן (‘to straighten’) what He עִוְּתוֹ (‘has bent’)?” (7:13).
    • Both roots return at the end. עות (“to bend”) comes back in the aged body, where “the mighty men הִתְעַוְּתוּ (‘stoop’)” (12:3). תקן (“to straighten, set in order”) comes back in the epilogue’s portrait of the sage, who תִּקֵּן (“arranged”) many proverbs (12:9).
    • The counts, by lemma in the WLC. תקן occurs 3 times in the Hebrew Bible, all in Ecclesiastes (1:15; 7:13; 12:9). עות occurs 3 times in this book (1:15; 7:13; 12:3), out of 11 in the Hebrew Bible.
    • What it shows. What man cannot straighten in the world, the sage can still set in order in words, and the frame commends him for it.
    • Surfaced by: Repetition, Vocabulary, Move 4 (units 2, 7 and 14), Structure. High confidence on the words; moderate on the design.
  2. In Solomon’s own vocabulary, Ecclesiastes lets the king who has everything be unable to enjoy it. [T] for the wording; [I] for the identification.
    • The triad. עֹשֶׁר וּנְכָסִים וְכָבוֹד (“riches and wealth and honour”, 6:2) is the triad God promises Solomon at 2 Chr 1:11–12.
    • The noun נְכָסִים (“wealth”) occurs 5 times in the Hebrew Bible: Eccl 5:19 (Heb 5:18); 6:2; 2 Chr 1:11, 12; Josh 22:8 (lemma, WLC).
    • Two more Solomon words.
      • מַדָּע (“knowledge”), Eccl 10:20, occurs otherwise only in 2 Chr 1:10–12 (Solomon’s request) and Dan 1:4, 17.
      • פֶּגַע (“chance, misfortune”), Eccl 9:11, occurs otherwise only at 1 Kgs 5:4 (Heb 5:18), where Solomon says he has “neither adversary nor misfortune”.
    • What the cluster does. It thickens the Solomonic dress that the overview found at 1:16 ‖ 1 Kgs 10:7. The book still never uses the name.
    • Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Move 2 (units 2, 6, 10 and 11), Tool 11. High confidence on the wording.
  3. The enjoyment passages are all grounded in God as giver, and none of them is “for tomorrow we die”. [T] (synthetic)
    • “Eat” and “drink” together — both lemmas in one verse — at 2:24; 3:13; 5:18 (Heb 5:17); 8:15; 9:7.
    • The joy vocabulary (שָׂמַח, “to rejoice”, or שִׂמְחָה, “joy”) also stands at 3:12, 22; 5:19–20; 11:8–9.
    • The ground is stated in almost every case. It is “from the hand of God” (2:24), “the gift of God” (3:13; 5:19), “which God has given” (5:18; 8:15; 9:9), or placed under judgement (11:9).
    • What the Greek shows. Where the Greek of 8:15 reads φαγεῖν … πιεῖν … εὐφρανθῆναι (“to eat … to drink … to be merry”), Luke 12:19 puts the same three verbs in the rich fool’s mouth, where the giver has dropped out.
    • Surfaced by: Repetition, Linking Words, Move 4, Tool 16, Christological Reading. High confidence.
  4. “Who knows?” and “cannot find out” carry the second half. [T] (synthetic)
    • The counts. מָצָא (“to find”) occurs 17 times in the book, 8 of them in 7:23–29. The verb occurs three more times in 8:17 (a subset of the 17). מִי יוֹדֵעַ (“who knows?”) stands at 2:19; 3:21; 6:12; 8:1.
    • Unit 12 makes the turn. It counts down “you do not know” four times (11:2, 5 ×2, 6), then gives the command וְדָע (“but know”, 11:9): that God will bring you to judgement.
    • What it shows. The only knowledge the book commands is the knowledge of judgement.
    • Surfaced by: Repetition, Structure, Move 4 (units 7–12). High confidence on the counts; moderate on the design.
  5. The book’s two statements about “the end of man” frame the second half, and the Greek keeps the link. [T] (synthetic)
    • The Hebrew. 7:2 reads הוּא סוֹף כָּל־הָאָדָם (“that is the end of every man”). 12:13 reads סוֹף דָּבָר … כִּי־זֶה כָּל־הָאָדָם (“the end of the matter … for this is the whole of man”).
    • The counts. סוֹף (“end”) occurs 3 times in the book (3:11; 7:2; 12:13). כָּל־הָאָדָם (“every man, the whole of man”) occurs 4 times (3:13; 5:19 [Heb 5:18]; 7:2; 12:13).
    • The Greek. Rahlfs 7:2 reads τέλος παντὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου (“end of every man”); 12:13 reads τέλος λόγου … πᾶς ὁ ἄνθρωπος (“end of the word … every man”).
    • What it shows. Death is “the end of every man” (7:2); fearing God and keeping his commandments is “the whole of man” (12:13).
    • A translation note. The NASB95’s “this applies to every person” hides the echo; the ESV’s and NIV84’s “the whole duty of man” keeps half of it.
    • Surfaced by: Repetition, Move 4, Translations, Original Languages. High confidence on the words; moderate on the design.

Book-Level Foundations

Worked once and assumed under every unit below.

Phase 0.5 — the overview’s four threads, extracted

  • Christological trajectory (overview): trajectory and contrast, not prophecy.
    • Qohelet as the son of David who reaches wisdom’s limits, over against Christ, the one “greater than Solomon” (Matt 12:42).
    • The death-question answered by resurrection.
    • Luke 12:16–21’s rich fool.
    • The “one Shepherd” (12:11), held at uncertain.
    • Judgement of the hidden (12:14).
    • The moralism check covers two errors: “enjoy while you can” and “life is meaningless, so get religion”.
  • Intertextual map (overview): citation-light and refrain-driven.
    • Live sources: Gen 2–3; Gen 1:26–27; Deut 23:21–23; Deut 4:2 and 12:32; Deuteronomy’s “fear … keep”; the Solomon narrative in Kings (1 Kgs 10:7; 8:46; 4:32); Prov 1:1, the Proverbs sluggard and the strange woman; Job 1:21; Job 3:16.
    • Flagged: Num 15:39 (uncertain to moderate); Ezek 34:23 and 37:24 (uncertain).
    • Absent: Isa 22:13.
    • Consequence: Move 4 carries the sweep.
  • Preaching traps (overview):
    1. Deciding Qohelet’s voice in advance.
    2. Carpe diem without the Giver.
    3. הֶבֶל (“breath, vanity”) as “meaningless”.
    4. 3:1–8 as a calendar poem.
    5. The woman of 7:26–28.
    6. Over-allegorising 12:1–7.
    7. Treating 12:13–14 as the whole book, or cutting it off.
  • Presenting situation (overview):
    • The voices. A frame narrator speaks about Qohelet (1:2; 7:27; 12:8–10); Qohelet speaks in the first person (1:12–12:7); the epilogue addresses “my son” (12:12).
    • The question. יִתְרוֹן (“advantage, gain”, 1:3), a word found only in this book.
    • The pressure. Death as the one מִקְרֶה (“fate”), and a moral order that does not visibly hold.
    • The warrants. Author, date and audience are [I]/[S].

Monograph-sourcing flag. The overview draws on no single monograph. Its named interpreters (Wright, Fox, Whybray, Longman, Bartholomew, Barthélemy) are recalled, not opened. They are [S] throughout, and the sweep adds nothing to their standing.

Author’s Purpose (Tool 1, book level)

Following the text: the book asks one question — what יִתְרוֹן (“advantage”) does a man have in all his עָמָל (“toil”) under the sun (1:3)? It asks it again at 3:9 and 5:16 (Heb 5:15), and asks the parallel question with יוֹתֵר (“advantage”) at 6:8 and 6:11 (phrase search, WLC). [T]

The answer the body gives is mostly negative: הַכֹּל הָבֶל (“all is vanity”) stands 6 times (1:2; 1:14; 2:11; 2:17; 3:19; 12:8). [T] Around that answer, three counter-movements recur:

  1. Receive the portion as God’s gift (Headline Finding 3).
  2. Fear God. The verb יָרֵא (“to fear”) has God as its object at 3:14; 5:7 (Heb 5:6); 8:12 ×2; 12:13. Its other two occurrences in the book (9:2; 12:5) are not about God. The adjective יָרֵא (“fearing”) is used of God at 7:18 and 8:13. [T]
  3. God will judge. 3:17; 11:9; 12:14. [T]

Purpose, as the sweep reads it. The book takes the wise reader to the edge of what wisdom can secure, and then turns him to receive, fear and await. [I] It confirms the overview’s purpose statement; see the tensions below for one qualification.

Genre (Tool 12)

Wisdom reflection in first-person royal voice, set inside a third-person frame.

  • Forms inside it:
    • a poem (1:4–11);
    • a list poem (3:2–8, set off by setumot in Leningrad — see below);
    • “better … than” sayings, dense in 7:1–12;
    • an example story (9:13–16);
    • proverb clusters (chapter 10);
    • a numerical saying (11:2);
    • an allegorical death-poem (12:1–7);
    • an epilogue (12:9–14).
  • Reading rule. Qohelet’s observations are reports of what he saw (רָאִיתִי, “I have seen”, is the book’s characteristic verb: רָאָה occurs 8 times in 1:12–2:26 alone). They are not necessarily the book’s verdict. The frame is where the book speaks about Qohelet. [T]
  • The Psalter caution does not apply, but its principle does: no form-critical label decides meaning.

Bible Timeline (Tool 14) and Canonical Position (Phase 0.55)

Extracted from the overview’s five-field block and checked there. In brief:

  • Section and neighbours. Ketuvim, among the Megilloth; Song of Songs before, Lamentations after (BHS order).
  • Presupposes. Gen 1–3, Deuteronomy (vows; add and take away; fear and keep), the Solomon narrative, Proverbs and Job, all verified by lemma.
  • Handoff. An open death-question, and a promised judgement of “every hidden thing” (12:14).
  • The sweep adds one field entry to Presupposes: 2 Chr 1:10–12 (see Headline Finding 2). Chronicles stands after Ecclesiastes in the BHS order and closes the canon — so the verbal link cannot be read as Ecclesiastes presupposing Chronicles as a book in canonical sequence. It is a shared Solomonic vocabulary, and the direction of dependence is open. [T] for the shared words; the direction is unresolved. (Per Phase 0.55: report both, resolve neither.)
  • The reader’s timeline. The reader stands this side of the resurrection. The death-question the book leaves open (3:21) is the one the New Testament answers. Qohelet’s observations about injustice are not cancelled by the cross; they are given a Judge (Acts 17:31) and a verdict.

Historical and Cultural Background

  • Setting:
    • Time period. Undated by the text. The Persian loanwords are פַּרְדֵּס (“park”, 2:5) and פִּתְגָם (“sentence”, 8:11). The relative particle שֶׁ־ (“which”) is frequent, and there is one Aramaic-looking noun, פֵּשֶׁר (“interpretation”, 8:1), a hapax in Hebrew against 31 occurrences of the Aramaic פְּשַׁר in Daniel (lemma, WLC). [T] for the forms; any date drawn from them is [S].
    • Location. “In Jerusalem” (1:1, 12, 16; 2:7, 9). [T]
    • Political context. A world of provinces and officials. הַמְּדִינוֹת (“the provinces”) occur at 2:8, and בַמְּדִינָה (“in the province”) at 5:8 (Heb 5:7), with “one official watching over another”. [T] An administered empire is [I].
  • What the original audience knew:
    • The wisdom school’s stock types: חָכָם (“wise”, 21×) and כְּסִיל (“fool”, 18×). [T]
    • Temple practice: vows, sacrifice and the house of God (5:1–7); clean and unclean (9:2). [T]
    • Royal court protocol (8:2–5; 10:4, 16–20). [T]
    • The Solomon tradition, in both Kings and Chronicles forms (Headline Finding 2). [I]
    • Parallel literature. Near Eastern wisdom literature has parallels, notably the tavern-keeper Siduri’s advice to Gilgamesh (“fill your belly … let your clothes be clean … let your wife delight in your bosom”), which closely matches 9:7–9. [S], recalled and not checked; flagged under unit 10.
  • What this changes about how we read. The court and province material (units 4, 6, 9 and 11) is not abstract. It addresses readers who live under officials they cannot correct, which is why “do not be shocked” (5:8) and “keep the king’s command” (8:2) are counsel rather than cynicism. [I]

Original Audience Reception (book level)

  • Canonical section and reader. The Writings; the reader stands exilic or post-exilic, has the Torah and the Prophets, and has Psalms, Job and Proverbs in the ear.
  • What they brought. Proverbs’ confidence that wisdom leads to life, and Deuteronomy’s promise that the God-fearer will תַּאֲרִיךְ יָמִים (“prolong days”) (Deut 4:40; 11:9; 17:20; 32:47).
  • Shocking:
    • The righteous man who perishes in his righteousness (7:15).
    • The wicked buried with honour (8:10, especially on the variant reading “praised”).
    • The congratulation of the dead (4:2).
  • Surprising. The divine name never appears (0×). God is only ever אֱלֹהִים (“God”), 40 times. [T]
  • Comforting. The portion is God’s gift (Headline Finding 3); God “has already approved your works” (9:7).
  • Disturbing. “Who knows?” about the spirit (3:21).
  • What we bring that they didn’t. A therapeutic reading of “meaningless” (Trap 3), and a post-Enlightenment assumption that death is simply extinction, which Qohelet neither asserts nor denies (3:21; 12:7).

Biblical-Theological Themes (book level)

  1. Wisdom and its limits. Solomon, the height of human wisdom (1 Kgs 3–10), ends in apostasy (1 Kgs 11). Ecclesiastes tests that wisdom against death. Christ is “the wisdom of God” (1 Cor 1:24, 30), “greater than Solomon” (Matt 12:42 — πλεῖον Σολομῶνος, “something greater than Solomon”, verified SBLGNT).
  2. Creation and fall. Dust and return (Gen 2:7; 3:19 → Eccl 3:20; 12:7). God “made man upright” (7:29 ← Gen 1:26–27). “Good or evil” at the very end (12:14; טוֹב … רָע, “good … evil”, pairs with Gen 2:9, 17; 3:5, 22; phrase search, WLC).
  3. Judgement. 3:17 → 11:9 → 12:14. The New Testament names the Judge: Rom 2:16; 2 Cor 5:10.

Christological Reading (book-level frame)

The overview’s categories stand: trajectory and contrast. The sweep’s refinements appear under the units. Two book-level adjustments:

  1. The 1 Cor 15:58 link is conceptual, not verbal in Greek. The Greek of Ecclesiastes renders עָמָל (“toil”) with μόχθος (“toil”) — the stem μοχθ- occurs 34 times in Swete’s Ecclesiastes — and κενός (“empty, in vain”) never occurs in it. Paul’s κόπος (“labour”) and κενός are not the Greek Ecclesiastes’ words. What is shared is ματαιότης (“futility”), the LXX’s word for הֶבֶל, at Rom 8:20. [T] for the counts. Book-Overview Tension 1.
  2. Rom 3:10 may be reading Eccl 7:20 in Greek. “Οὐκ ἔστιν δίκαιος” (“there is none righteous”, Rom 3:10) matches the Greek of Eccl 7:20. Rahlfs has ὅτι ἄνθρωπος οὐκ ἔστιν δίκαιος ἐν τῇ γῇ (“for there is not a righteous man on the earth”; Swete numbers it 7:21). Ps 13:1–3 LXX, the catena’s main source, reads instead οὐκ ἔστιν ποιῶν χρηστότητα (“there is none who does good”). This is a category 3 finding: the NT’s own text. The verbal data are verified; the claim that Paul blends Eccl 7:20 into the catena is [S] (recalled). Moderate confidence. See unit 8.

Moralism check (book level): see the overview. The sweep confirms it passage by passage (units 10, 12 and 13 are where the “enjoy while you can” trap bites hardest).

Textual and Apparatus Notes (book level)

  • Paragraph markers — in Leningrad, as the WLC prints them. There are only four: a petuchah after 1:11, and setumot after 3:1, 3:8 and 9:10. [T] for the witness. The setumot mark the times-list off from its heading and its sequel. This is a reading-tradition feature, not authorial. Its spread across the manuscripts was not checked ([unchecked — apparatus spread]), and no finding in this sweep rests on it.
  • Ketiv/qere, as BHS prints them. They occur at:
    • 4:8 (עיניו, “his eyes”, / עֵינוֹ, “his eye”);
    • 5:1 (Heb 4:17) (רגליך, “your feet”, / רַגְלְךָ, “your foot”);
    • 5:9 and 5:11 (Heb 5:8, 10);
    • 6:10 (שהתקיף / שֶׁתַּקִּיף, “who is stronger”);
    • 7:22 (את / אַתָּה, “you”);
    • 9:4 (יבחר, “is chosen”, / יְחֻבַּר, “is joined” — NASB95 follows the qere);
    • 10:3 and 10:20;
    • 12:6 (ירחק, “is removed”, / יֵרָתֵק, “is snapped”).
    All are reading-tradition features. None carries a finding except 9:4, where the qere is followed and noted.
  • Substantive variants weighed in this sweep (category 2): 2:25 (“apart from me” / “apart from him”); 3:21 (the vocalisation of הָעֹלָה, “which goes up” or “whether it goes up”); 5:6 (Heb 5:5) (הַמַּלְאָךְ, “the messenger”, / “God”); 8:10 (“forgotten” / “praised”); 9:2 (the added “and for the evil”). They are worked in their units.
  • The Greek. Mechanically literal (σύν with the accusative for the object marker אֶת, 10 times in Swete — overview, verified). Its readings are therefore good evidence for its Hebrew Vorlage where it differs, which is what gives weight to its readings at 2:25, 3:21 and 8:10.

Unit 1 — 1:1–11 ⭐ Vanity of Vanities

1:2 הֲבֵל הֲבָלִים אָמַר קֹהֶלֶת הֲבֵל הֲבָלִים הַכֹּל הָבֶל “Vanity of vanities,” says the Preacher, “Vanity of vanities! All is vanity.”

1:3 מַה־יִּתְרוֹן לָאָדָם בְּכָל־עֲמָלוֹ שֶׁיַּעֲמֹל תַּחַת הַשָּׁמֶשׁ What advantage does man have in all his work which he does under the sun? (NASB95)

Headline Findings

  • The book states its keyword, its question and its setting in its first three verses, and does it through the frame voice before Qohelet himself speaks. [T]
    • הֶבֶל (“vanity”) occurs 5 times in 1:2 alone.
    • יִתְרוֹן (“advantage”) appears at 1:3.
    • “Under the sun” appears at 1:3.
    • The first-person voice does not begin until 1:12.
    Surfaced by: Structure, Repetition, Vocabulary. High confidence.
  • The prologue poem (1:4–7) shows a world that moves without arriving. [T]
    • הָלַךְ (“to go”) occurs 5 times in 1:4–7 (1:4; 1:6 ×2; 1:7 ×2).
    • The verb סָבַב (“to go round”) occurs 3 times in 1:6.
    • שׁוּב (“to return”) occurs at 1:6 and 1:7.
    • “The sea is not מָלֵא (‘full’)” (1:7).
    All counts are by lemma in the WLC. The poem enacts the question of 1:3 before the book argues it. Surfaced by: Repetition, Parallels, Tone. High confidence.
  • The unit ends with forgetting: אֵין זִכְרוֹן (“no remembrance”, 1:11). [T] The book returns to it at 2:16 and 9:5, 9:15, and answers it at 12:1 (see Move 4). High confidence.

2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

  • Preceding movement. As the opening, the “preceding movement” is the canon. The reader of the Writings arrives from Proverbs, where wisdom brings life, and, in the BHS order, from the Song of Songs just before. [I]
  • Necessity answer. The book must pose its question before its king can test it. The frame voice (1:2) announces the verdict first, so the reader knows from the start that the experiment of 1:12–2:26 will end in הֶבֶל (“vanity”). The prologue exists here to make the question cosmic before it becomes personal. [I]
  • Implication. 1:2 is the frame narrator summarising Qohelet (“says Qohelet”), not Qohelet beginning. Preach it as the book’s thesis as the frame reports it, a verdict the epilogue will set in a larger frame (12:8–14). [T]/[I]

Context. A petuchah after 1:11 closes the unit in Leningrad, as the WLC prints it; it is a reading-tradition feature, and its manuscript spread is unchecked. 1:12 opens the first-person account. [T]

3. Structure

Verses Section Function
1:1 Superscription “The words of Qohelet, son of David, king in Jerusalem”
1:2 Thesis (frame voice) הֲבֵל הֲבָלִים (“vanity of vanities”) — a superlative, like “song of songs”
1:3 The question What יִתְרוֹן (“advantage”) in all his עָמָל (“toil”)?

The binding device is a thesis, then a question, then an illustration. The two halves of the poem mirror each other: the cosmos never arrives (1:4–7), and man is never satisfied (1:8). [T]

5. Parallels

  • 1:4, antithetic. “A generation goes and a generation comes, / but the earth remains לְעוֹלָם (‘forever’).” Man is transient; the stage is permanent. [T]
  • 1:8b, synonymous. “The eye is not תִשְׂבַּע (‘satisfied’) with seeing, / nor is the ear תִמָּלֵא (‘filled’) with hearing.” [T]
    • This matches “the sea is not מָלֵא (‘full’)” (1:7). The adjective מָלֵא (1:7) and the verb מָלֵא (1:8) are cognate, not one lemma (WLC). Man’s appetite is shaped like the sea.
    • שָׂבַע (“to be satisfied”) recurs at 4:8, 5:10 (Heb 5:9) and 6:3 (lemma, WLC).
  • 1:9, twin lines. “What has been … what has been done.” מַה־שֶּׁהָיָה (“what has been”) recurs at 3:15, 6:10 and 7:24 (phrase search, WLC). [T]

7. Vocabulary

  • הֶבֶל (“breath, vapour, vanity”). 38 occurrences in 30 verses in the book (lemma, WLC), 5 of them in 1:2. The sense is transience and insubstantiality: a breath. It is not “meaninglessness”. The same word is the name of Abel (הֶבֶל, Gen 4:2), the first man to die. [T] for the identical form; any intended allusion to Abel is [I] — uncertain, and not to be built on. Name-wordplay check: the form is identical, but nothing in Ecclesiastes invokes Abel. Inadmissible as an allusion; noted only.
  • יִתְרוֹן (“advantage, surplus, gain”). Found only in Ecclesiastes (10 occurrences in 9 verses, lemma, WLC). A commercial term for what is left over after the account is settled. [I] for the commercial register (moderate).
  • עָמָל (“toil, trouble”). The noun occurs 22 times in the book (lemma 5999, WLC). It carries the note of burden as well as work.
  • קֹהֶלֶת (“Qohelet”). See the overview. It is a feminine participle of קהל (“to assemble”).
  • Proper-noun inventory. David and Jerusalem (1:1). Both are load-bearing for the Solomonic identification (unit 2); no other names.

8. Translations

  • הֲבֵל הֲבָלִים (“vanity of vanities”). NASB95 and ESV: “Vanity of vanities”. NIV84: “Meaningless! Meaningless! … Utterly meaningless! Everything is meaningless.” The NIV84 turns a noun of transience into an adjective of senselessness (Trap 3).
  • Ancient versions. Swete reads Ματαιότης ματαιοτήτων (“futility of futilities”), the same superlative construction, and the source of Rom 8:20’s word.
  • יִתְרוֹן (“advantage”). NASB95 “advantage”; ESV “gain”; Swete περισσεία (“surplus”). No divergence bears on a finding.
  • Pulpit divergence note. N/A — no pulpit text declared. For an NIV84 engagement, the congregation will hear “meaningless” at every הֶבֶל (“vanity”) refrain, 28 times in the book.

9. Tone and Feel

Weary, circling, cosmic. The sentences go round (הוֹלֵךְ … וְסוֹבֵב … סוֹבֵב סֹבֵב הוֹלֵךְ, “going … and circling … circling, circling, going”, 1:6). Soundtrack: a ground bass that repeats and never resolves. Don’t preach it brightly. [T]

10. Repetition

  • Book-wide keywords introduced here:
    • הֶבֶל (“vanity”): 5 times in 1:2.
    • “Under the sun” (1:3, 9): 27 verses in all, with 29 occurrences.
    • עָמָל / עָמַל (“toil” / “to toil”), 1:3.
    • כְּבָר (“already”), 1:10 — the first of 9 occurrences.
    • זִכָּרוֹן (“remembrance”), twice in 1:11.
  • Idea-level repetition. “Nothing new” (1:9–10) and “no remembrance” (1:11) are the same claim seen from two sides: the future brings nothing new, and the past keeps nothing. [T]

11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

  • Live sources held in view (from the overview): Gen 1–3, Deuteronomy, the Solomon narrative, Proverbs and Job.
  • Gen 1 (conceptual). Sun, wind and waters form a creation catalogue. The phrase “the earth remains לְעוֹלָם (‘forever’)” has no verbal link to Gen 1. Uncertain. Move 1 only: Gen 1 orders these elements under God’s word, while Eccl 1:4–7 shows them turning without any stated purpose. Note the absence here: אֱלֹהִים (“God”) is missing from 1:1–12 and first appears at 1:13. [T] As a pattern-break, this is weak: the book has no established pattern of naming God in openings. Not reported as a finding.
  • Isa 43:19; 65:17 — “new” (canonical contrast, not allusion). Isaiah’s God does חֲדָשָׁה (“a new thing”, Isa 43:19), and creates שָׁמַיִם חֲדָשִׁים (“new heavens”, Isa 65:17). Qohelet says there is nothing חָדָשׁ (“new”) under the sun (1:9–10). חָדָשׁ occurs only at 1:9–10 in this book (lemma, WLC). Moderate as a canonical conversation — the Writings reader has Isaiah. It is not a claim that Qohelet alludes to Isaiah. Feeds the Christological reading: Rev 21:5, ἰδοὺ καινὰ ποιῶ πάντα (“behold, I am making all things new”).
  • Internal (Move 4) — planted for later:
    • 1:3 question. Repeated at 3:9 and 5:16 (Heb 5:15); asked in parallel with יוֹתֵר (“advantage”) at 6:8, 11. [T]
    • 1:2 thesis. Returns at 12:8 as an inclusio. [T]
    • 1:5–7 sun, wind and waters returning. The movement reverses at 12:2 (sun darkened; clouds “return”) and 12:7 (dust and spirit “return”). שׁוּב (“to return”) is verified at 1:6, 7 and 12:2, 7. [T]/[I]
    • 1:6 south and north. דָּרוֹם (“south”) and צָפוֹן (“north”) occur in the book only at 1:6 and 11:3 (lemma, WLC). In 1:6 the wind circles through them; in 11:3 a tree falls to one or the other and stays. [T] for the words; the echo is moderate.
    • 1:11 “no remembrance”. Answered at 12:1: “remember (זְכֹר) your Creator”. [T]
    • 1:4 לְעוֹלָם (“forever”). Replanted at 12:5: “man goes to בֵּית עוֹלָמוֹ (‘his eternal home’)”. [T]

16. So What? (brief)

  • Intended response. Stop expecting the world’s cycles to deliver a lasting gain.
  • Worldview. Toil and nature, taken on their own terms, return and repeat.
  • Candidate FCF. The fear that our work and our names will vanish (1:11).
  • Prayer. “Teach us to number our days” (Ps 90:12) — a Writings neighbour, not a link claimed from this text.

Passage-specific difficulties

  • 1:2 as nihilism. A pastoral landmine. See Trap 3. The frame reports Qohelet’s verdict; the book does not end there.
  • 1:9 against Christian newness. Qohelet’s “nothing new under the sun” is bounded by its horizon. New creation comes from above the sun (Isa 65:17; 2 Cor 5:17). Preach the boundary, not a contradiction.

Unit 2 — 1:12–2:26 ⭐ The King’s Experiment

1:16 דִּבַּרְתִּי אֲנִי עִם־לִבִּי לֵאמֹר אֲנִי הִנֵּה הִגְדַּלְתִּי וְהוֹסַפְתִּי חָכְמָה עַל כָּל־אֲשֶׁר־הָיָה לְפָנַי עַל־יְרוּשָׁלִָם I said to myself, “Behold, I have magnified and increased wisdom more than all who were over Jerusalem before me …”

2:24 אֵין־טוֹב בָּאָדָם שֶׁיֹּאכַל וְשָׁתָה וְהֶרְאָה אֶת־נַפְשׁוֹ טוֹב בַּעֲמָלוֹ גַּם־זֹה רָאִיתִי אָנִי כִּי מִיַּד הָאֱלֹהִים הִיא There is nothing better for a man than to eat and drink and tell himself that his labor is good. This also I have seen that it is from the hand of God. (NASB95)

Headline Findings

  • The experiment is conducted in the first person and for the self. [T] The counts, by lemma in the WLC:
    • אֲנִי (“I”): 15 occurrences in 11 verses of 1:12–2:26.
    • לֵב (“heart, mind”): 14 occurrences in 10 verses, the heart as both instrument and partner in dialogue (“I said to my heart”, 1:16; 2:1, 15).
    • לִי (“for myself”): 8 times in 2:4–8.
    The self is both the one who investigates and the one who benefits. Surfaced by: Repetition, Tone, Vocabulary. High confidence.
  • The king is dressed in Solomon’s words, and the words are the exact ones Kings and Chronicles use of Solomon. [T]
    • הִגְדַּלְתִּי וְהוֹסַפְתִּי חָכְמָה (“I have magnified and increased wisdom”, 1:16). The pairing “added wisdom” occurs only here and at 1 Kgs 10:7.
    • “more than all who were before me in Jerusalem” (1:16; 2:7, 9), matching 1 Kgs 3:12 and 2 Chr 1:12, where no king before him or after him will be like him (לְפָנֶיךָ, “before you”).
    • “all that my eyes שָׁאֲלוּ (‘asked’)” (2:10), against Solomon, who did not ask (שָׁאַל) for riches (1 Kgs 3:11, with the verb five times in that verse).
    • שָׁרִים וְשָׁרוֹת (“male and female singers”, 2:8), a pair found only here and at 2 Sam 19:35 (Heb 19:36).
    Surfaced by: Tool 11 Moves 1–3, Vocabulary. High confidence on 1:16; moderate on the others.
  • The unit ends with the first “nothing better” — and grounds it in God’s hand, not in the experiment. [T] “This also I have seen, that it is מִיַּד הָאֱלֹהִים (‘from the hand of God’)” (2:24). The joy the king could not produce by pursuing it (2:1–11) is received as a gift (2:24–26). Surfaced by: Structure, Linking Words, Move 4. High confidence.

2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

  • Preceding movement. The frame has declared the verdict (1:2) and posed the question (1:3). The prologue has shown that the cosmos yields no gain (1:4–11).
  • Necessity answer. The question now needs a test case at the highest stakes. If any human being could secure a יִתְרוֹן (“advantage”), it would be the wisest and richest king in Jerusalem. The experiment must come first, and be conducted by this man, so that every later observation (units 3–12) carries the weight of “even the king found none”. [I]
  • Implication. 2:11’s “there was no יִתְרוֹן (‘profit’) under the sun” answers 1:3 at once, and at the highest level. The rest of the book lives with that answer. [T]

4. Linking Words

  • 2:24 → 2:25 → 2:26. The chain runs כִּי … כִּי (“for … for”). “Nothing better … from the hand of God — for who can eat … apart from [him/me]? — for to the man good before Him He has given …” The two “for”s ground the gift in God’s sovereign giving. [T]
  • 1:18 כִּי (“because”). The proverb explains why wisdom is “striving after wind”: more wisdom brings more כַּעַס (“grief”). [T]

5. Parallels

  • 1:15, synonymous. “What is crooked (מְעֻוָּת) cannot be straightened (לִתְקֹן) / what is lacking cannot be counted.” The first half plants the עות/תקן thread (Headline Finding 1, sweep level). [T]
  • 1:18, synonymous-climactic. “In much wisdom much כַּעַס (‘grief’) / increasing knowledge increases pain.” יָסַף (“to increase”) occurs twice (lemma, WLC). [T]
  • 2:13–14, antithetic. Wisdom and folly, light and darkness, the wise man’s eyes and the fool walking in darkness. Then the pivot: “and yet I know that one fate befalls them both.” [T]

7. Vocabulary

  • תּוּר (“to explore, spy out”, 1:13; 2:3; also 7:25). This is the spies’ verb in Numbers (14 of its 23 occurrences in the Hebrew Bible are in Numbers; lemma, WLC), and the verb of Num 15:39: “do not תָתֻרוּ (‘go about’) after your heart and your eyes”. See Tool 11. Moderate.
  • עִנְיַן רָע (“an unhappy business, a grievous task”, 1:13). עִנְיָן occurs 8 times, all in Ecclesiastes (lemma, WLC). The 1:13 clause is repeated near-verbatim at 3:10 (phrase “לִבְנֵי הָאָדָם לַעֲנוֹת בּוֹ”, “for the sons of men to be busy with”: only 1:13 and 3:10). [T] It is the noun of the root ענה (“to be busy”) — see unit 11 on 10:19.
  • מִקְרֶה (“fate, what befalls”, 2:14–15). This is its first occurrence. 7 occurrences in 5 verses in the book (lemma, WLC). It means what happens to a person, not blind chance: its only other occurrences in the Hebrew Bible are 1 Sam 6:9; 20:26; Ruth 2:3 — and Ruth’s is the providential “she happened to come”. [T]; the Ruth connection is [I], moderate (a canonical neighbour).
  • חוֹטֶא (“sinner”, 2:26). This is its first occurrence. חָטָא (“to sin”) occurs 7 times in the book: 2:26; 5:6 (Heb 5:5); 7:20, 26; 8:12; 9:2, 18 (lemma, WLC).
  • סְגֻלַּת מְלָכִים (“the treasure of kings”, 2:8). סְגֻלָּה (“treasured possession”) occurs 8 times in the Hebrew Bible. It is Israel as God’s own treasure (Exod 19:5; Deut 7:6; 14:2; 26:18), and David’s private treasure given to the temple (1 Chr 29:3). [T] Here the king’s treasure is his own acquisition. Uncertain as a deliberate irony; noted, not built on.
  • Proper-noun inventory. Israel and Jerusalem (1:12, 16; 2:7, 9). Load-the-story rule applied: 1 Kgs 3; 10; 2 Chr 1 re-read in the WLC (see Tool 11).

8. Translations

  • 2:25 — a category 2 variant, and all three English versions follow the Greek. [T] for the readings.
    • The WLC reads חוּץ מִמֶּנִּי (“apart from me”): Qohelet speaks.
    • BHS app. 2:25 ᵇ records “pc Mss 𝔊𝔖 מִמֶּנּוּ” (“apart from him”). Swete: παρὲξ αὐτοῦ (“apart from him”).

      • “Him” fits the flow of 2:24–26, where God is the giver.

11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

1 Kgs 10:7 → Eccl 1:16 (high confidence)

  • Source context. The Queen of Sheba, having tested Solomon with riddles (10:1), says: “you have הוֹסַפְתָּ חָכְמָה וָטוֹב (‘added wisdom and prosperity’) beyond the report” (10:7). The chapter then catalogues his gold, silver, horses and trade (10:14–29). The next chapter opens with his many foreign wives and the turning of his heart (11:1–8). The verse is the climax of Solomon’s glory, and it stands immediately before the fall.
  • Book usage. First use in the book. Prepared by 1:1, whose “son of David, king” matches Prov 1:1 (overview).
  • OT-to-OT. The same glory is told in 2 Chr 1:11–12 and 9:1–28. Chronicles omits 1 Kgs 11. The two pictures of Solomon — with the fall and without it — are already in conversation within the canon. Ecclesiastes’ king has the glory of both and the frustration Kings reports.
  • What it adds. The experiment is run by the man at the top of Kings’ narrative, and the reader who knows 1 Kgs 11 knows where such wisdom went.

2 Chr 1:12 / 1 Kgs 3:12 → Eccl 1:16; 2:7, 9 (“before me”) (moderate)

  • Source context. At Gibeon (1 Kgs 3:5–14 // 2 Chr 1:7–12), God gives Solomon wisdom, “so that there has been none like you before you (לְפָנֶיךָ)”. He also gives what Solomon did not ask for (לֹא שָׁאַלְתָּ, “you have not asked”): riches and honour.
  • Book usage. This develops the 1:16 cluster.
  • OT-to-OT. The Kings and Chronicles doublet (see above).
  • What it adds. The king of 2:10 withholds from his eyes nothing they שָׁאֲלוּ (“asked”). That inverts the Gibeon scene, where the king was praised for what he did not ask. The inversion is [I]. Moderate: the shared words (שָׁאַל, “to ask”; לִפְנֵי, “before”) are common.

2 Sam 19:35 (Heb 19:36) → Eccl 2:8 (moderate)

  • Source context. Barzillai, 80 years old, declines David’s invitation to court: “Can I discern between good and bad? Can your servant taste what I eat or drink? Can I hear any longer the voice of שָׁרִים וְשָׁרוֹת (‘singing men and singing women’)?”
  • Book usage. First use.
  • OT-to-OT. No pairing in this unit.
  • What it adds. The king collects the singers that Barzillai, in old age, can no longer enjoy. Eccl 12:4 (“the daughters of song are brought low”) will bring old age to the singers too. The phrase occurs only in these two places (phrase search, WLC). Moderate: two-word match, strong thematic fit (old age, taste, good and bad). Cross-reported to unit 13.

Gen 2:5–10 → Eccl 2:4–6 (the king’s Eden) (moderate)

  • Source context. God plants (נָטַע) a garden (גַּן), makes every tree grow, and a river waters (לְהַשְׁקוֹת) it.
  • Match. Eccl 2:5–6: “I made myself gardens (גַּנּוֹת, from the cognate גַּנָּה) and parks, and planted (נָטַעְתִּי) in them every kind of fruit tree; I made myself pools of water to water (לְהַשְׁקוֹת) a forest.” The verb שָׁקָה (“to water”) is verified at Gen 2:6, 10 and Eccl 2:6; נָטַע (“to plant”) at Eccl 2:4, 5 (lemma, WLC).
  • Book usage. Gen 2–3 is a live source (3:20; 7:29; 12:7). This is its first appearance.
  • What it adds. The king builds himself an Eden “for myself”, eight times over — and finds הֶבֶל (“vanity”) in it. [I] Moderate: the vocabulary cluster is real, and the phrasing is ordinary horticulture.

Num 15:39 → Eccl 1:13; 2:3, 10; 11:9 (heart and eyes; תּוּר) (moderate — the overview’s flag, tested)

  • Source context. The tassels law closes Num 15. After the distinction between unintentional (שְׁגָגָה) and high-handed sin (15:22–31), and the Sabbath-breaker (15:32–36), comes the command: “remember all the commandments … and not תָתֻרוּ (‘go about’) after your own heart and your own eyes”.
  • Test.
    • תּוּר (“to explore”) is verified at Num 15:39 and at Eccl 1:13; 2:3; 7:25.
    • לֵב (“heart”) and עַיִן (“eye”) stand together at Eccl 2:10 and 11:9, and at Num 15:39.
    • שְׁגָגָה (“unintentional sin”) from the same chapter recurs at Eccl 5:6 (Heb 5:5) and 10:5.
  • What it adds. The king does exactly what the tassels forbid, and 11:9 allows heart and eyes only under judgement.
  • Verdict: the flag holds at moderate. Two independent Num 15 terms (תּוּר; שְׁגָגָה) appear in the book, which raises it above coincidence but not to high. Route to a claim audit.

    • 2:12, 18 “the man who comes after” → 4:15–16. The successor youth. [I]
    • 2:24 מִיַּד הָאֱלֹהִים (“from the hand of God”) → 9:1 בְּיַד הָאֱלֹהִים (“in the hand of God”). [T]
    • 2:26 “good before Him … sinner” → 7:26. The same pairing, turned from gift to rescue. [T]
    • 2:14 מִקְרֶה (“fate”) → 3:19; 9:2–3. [T]

13 / 15. Copycat and Who Am I?

The king is not a model to copy (2:1–11 is an experiment that fails). He is a foil: the best human case under the sun. The reader is the one invited to receive 2:24–26. Christologically, this is a contrast. The son of David who gathered everything for himself found nothing lasting. The Son of David who “though rich, became poor” (2 Cor 8:9) gathers a people for God. Moderate, as contrast. [I]

16. So What? (brief)

  • Stop. Treating acquisition, experience or intellect as the route to a lasting gain.
  • Start. Receiving food, drink and work as God’s gift (2:24).
  • Motive. God is the giver (2:26).
  • Candidate FCF. “If I had enough (wisdom, money, experience), I would be satisfied.” [T] (2:10–11).

Passage-specific difficulties

  • 2:26 — does God reward the “good” and punish the “sinner” with prosperity? Category: doctrinal.
    • Rhetorical function. It grounds 2:24–25 (the “for” chain).
    • Options. Moral retribution; or divine freedom in giving (“good before him” = one God favours).
    • Honest exegesis. The phrase is טוֹב לְפָנָיו (“good before him”) — relational, not merit language. Moderate.
    • Handling. Address briefly.
  • 2:25 variant. See Translations. Don’t build a sermon on “without Him” without saying the Hebrew reads “me”.

Unit 3 — 3:1–15 ⭐ A Time for Everything, and Eternity in the Heart

3:11 אֶת־הַכֹּל עָשָׂה יָפֶה בְעִתּוֹ גַּם אֶת־הָעֹלָם נָתַן בְּלִבָּם מִבְּלִי אֲשֶׁר לֹא־יִמְצָא הָאָדָם אֶת־הַמַּעֲשֶׂה אֲשֶׁר־עָשָׂה הָאֱלֹהִים מֵרֹאשׁ וְעַד־סוֹף He has made everything appropriate in its time. He has also set eternity in their heart, yet so that man will not find out the work which God has done from the beginning even to the end.

3:14 … עָלָיו אֵין לְהוֹסִיף וּמִמֶּנּוּ אֵין לִגְרֹעַ וְהָאֱלֹהִים עָשָׂה שֶׁיִּרְאוּ מִלְּפָנָיו … there is nothing to add to it and there is nothing to take from it, for God has so worked that men should fear Him. (NASB95)

Headline Findings

  • The times-list is fourteen pairs of opposites. Each עֵת (“time”) is God’s, and the list is set off in Leningrad. [T]
    • עֵת (“time”) occurs 28 times in 3:2–8, and 31 times across 3:1–17 (lemma, WLC).
    • Setumot stand after 3:1 and after 3:8, as the WLC prints them. They are a reading-tradition feature, and their manuscript spread is unchecked.
    • חֵפֶץ (“matter, delight”) at 3:1 and 3:17 brackets the list with its sequel (lemma, WLC).
    Surfaced by: Repetition, Structure, Parallels. High confidence.
  • The poem is the premise of an argument, not a devotional. [T] 3:9 repeats the question of 1:3 immediately. 3:10 repeats 1:13 almost word for word. 3:11 answers: God has made everything יָפֶה (“appropriate”) in its time, and man cannot find out the work. Surfaced by: Structure, Move 4, Linking Words. High confidence.
  • 3:14 applies to God’s work the Torah’s formula for God’s word: “nothing to add, nothing to take away” (Deut 4:2; 13:1 [Eng 12:32]). [T] Its stated purpose is “that men should fear Him”. This is the book’s first use of the fear-of-God phrase that closes it (12:13), and the verb-plus-preposition יָרֵא מִלְּפָנָיו (“fear before Him”) recurs at 8:12 (lemma, WLC). Surfaced by: Tool 11 triad, Move 4. High confidence.

2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

  • Preceding movement. The king has found no יִתְרוֹן (“advantage”) by his own effort (2:11). But he has found that the good of eating, drinking and toil comes “from the hand of God” (2:24), and that God gives to whom He pleases (2:26).
  • Necessity answer. 2:26 raises the question of God’s sovereign giving. 3:1–15 answers it at the level of time: everything has its God-appointed עֵת (“time”). The poem exists here to turn “God gives” (2:26) into “God times” (3:1–11). Man’s inability to find the work (3:11) is the reason the king’s experiment failed. [I]
  • Implication. Preach 3:1–8 as the ground of 3:11–15’s humility, not as a time-management text (Trap 4). [T]

3. Structure

Verses Section Function
3:1 Heading “For everything a זְמָן (‘season’), and a time for every matter under heaven”
3:2–8 Fourteen pairs Birth/death … war/peace — a merism for all of life

3:10 The burden again ≈ 1:13 (verified)

  • Device. A poem followed by commentary. The list opens with birth and death, and closes with war and peace. [T]
  • 3:15 מַה־שֶּׁהָיָה (“what has been”). It closes the unit by echoing 1:9, the end of the prologue. [T]

5. Parallels

  • 3:2–8. Each verse carries two pairs, and each pair sets a positive against a negative. The order alternates, sometimes positive first and sometimes negative first. [T]
  • 3:5 is contested. “Throw stones / gather stones” is read three ways: clearing a field, war, or sexual. The pairing with “embrace / refrain from embracing” invites the last. Uncertain. Don’t build on it.

7. Vocabulary

  • עֵת (“time”) and זְמָן (“appointed season”, 3:1).
    • זְמָן is a late word: 4 occurrences in the Hebrew Bible, the other three at Neh 2:6 and Esth 9:27, 31 (lemma, WLC). [T]
    • Swete renders the two as χρόνος (“time, duration”) and καιρός (“season”). The Greek keeps the two nouns distinct (verified in Swete).
  • יָפֶה (“appropriate, beautiful”, 3:11). It occurs only here and at 5:18 (Heb 5:17), “good and יָפֶה (‘fitting’)” (lemma, WLC).
    • NASB95 has “appropriate”; the ESV and NIV84 have “beautiful”.
    • The word’s common sense is “beautiful” (Gen 12:11; 1 Sam 16:12). Its pairing here with בְעִתּוֹ (“in its time”) favours “fitting”.
    • Moderate. The difference is a pulpit note (see Translations).
  • הָעֹלָם (3:11) — a lexical crux.
    • “Eternity” is the NASB95, ESV and NIV84 rendering, and Swete’s τὸν αἰῶνα (“the age, eternity”).
    • “The world” is another reading.
    • “Darkness, ignorance” is a third, derived from עלם (“to hide”).
    • In-unit evidence. 3:14 uses לְעוֹלָם (“forever”), the same noun, for the duration of God’s work. The book’s other uses are durative: 1:4, 10; 2:16; 9:6; 12:5 (lemma, WLC).
    • Position: “eternity / a sense of duration beyond the present” is best supported by the book’s own usage. Moderate–high. BHS proposes nothing about הָעֹלָם; its note is on בְּלִבָּם (“in their heart”), suspected of dittography (app. 3:11 ᵃ).
  • מַתַּת אֱלֹהִים (“the gift of God”, 3:13). It occurs twice in the book: 3:13; 5:19 (Heb 5:18) (lemma, WLC). [T]

8. Translations

  • 3:11 יָפֶה. NASB95 “appropriate”; ESV and NIV84 “beautiful”. Pulpit note (for an ESV or NIV84 engagement): the congregation will hear an aesthetic claim (“beautiful”) where the context stresses fittingness to God’s time.
  • 3:1 חֵפֶץ. NASB95 “event”; ESV “matter”. The word’s primary sense is “delight, desire” (see 5:4 [Heb 5:3], “He takes no delight”; 12:1, “no delight”; 12:10, “delightful words”). The translation loss hides that 3:1 speaks of a time for every pursuit. Moderate.
  • Ancient versions. No split on 3:11 (αἰών, “age, eternity”).
  • Pulpit divergence note. N/A — no pulpit text declared.

10. Repetition

  • עֵת (“time”): 28 times in 3:2–8, with 3:1, 11 and 17 besides.
  • עָשָׂה (“to make, do”), with God as subject: 3:11 ×2, 14 ×2.
  • “Forever”: לְעוֹלָם (3:14), set against הָעֹלָם (“eternity”, 3:11).
  • מָצָא (“to find”) appears here for the first time (3:11). Of its 17 occurrences in the book, 16 come later.

11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

Deut 4:2; 13:1 (Heb) [Eng 12:32] → Eccl 3:14 (high confidence)

  • Source context. Deut 4:1–2 opens Moses’ appeal: “Now, O Israel, listen to the statutes … You shall not add (לֹא תֹסִפוּ) to the word which I am commanding you, nor take away (וְלֹא תִגְרְעוּ) from it, that you may keep the commandments of the LORD your God.” Deut 13:1 (Heb) repeats it for the whole law, immediately before the test of the false prophet. The verse’s function is to guard the integrity of God’s word.
  • Book usage. This is the first Deuteronomy use in the book. Deuteronomy will return at 5:4–5 (vows), 8:13 (“prolong days”) and 12:13 (“fear God and keep his commandments”).
  • OT-to-OT. Prov 30:6, “Do not add (אַל־תּוֹסְףְּ) to his words”, already reworks the formula within the wisdom corpus (יָסַף, “to add”, verified at Prov 30:6).
    • Ecclesiastes moves it from God’s word to God’s work.
    • The epilogue’s וְיֹתֵר מֵהֵמָּה (“beyond these, my son, be warned”, 12:12) carries the same guard back to words. [I]
  • What it adds. God’s work, like His word, is complete and not open to human revision. The fitting human posture is the same in both cases: fear.

Internal (Move 4):

  • Answers §earlier. 3:9 is the question of 1:3 (phrase search, WLC). 3:10 is 1:13. 3:15 is 1:9. [T]
  • Planted for later:
    • 3:11 “man will not find out”. Returns at 7:24–29 and 8:17 (מָצָא, “to find”, 3 times in 8:17), and at 11:5 (“you do not know the work of God”). [T]
    • 3:11 סוֹף (“end”). Returns at 7:2 and 12:13. [T]
    • 3:14 “that men fear before Him”. Returns at 8:12 and 12:13. [T]
    • 3:11 הָעֹלָם (“eternity”) → 12:14 נֶעְלָם (“hidden”). The same consonants (ע-ל-ם), though the lexicons divide them into two roots (lemma 5769 against 5956). Uncertain as deliberate paronomasia. Noted for a claim audit.
    • 3:2 “a time to be born, a time to die” → 7:1 “the day of death better than the day of birth”. ילד (“to bear, be born”) and death terms occur in both (lemma, WLC). [T]

14. Bible Timeline — Christological note

God’s appointed times reach their “fullness” in the sending of the Son (Gal 4:4; Rom 5:6, κατὰ καιρόν, “at the right time”). The Greek of Eccl 3:1 uses καιρός (“season”) for עֵת. This is a trajectory through the category of divinely appointed time. It does not claim that Galatians alludes to Ecclesiastes. Moderate.

16. So What? (brief)

  • Worldview. Every season is under God’s appointment. Man sees the fittingness only in part.
  • Behaviour. Receive the present portion (3:12–13), and stop demanding to see the whole (3:11).
  • Motive. God’s work is permanent (3:14).
  • Candidate FCF. Anxiety over events we cannot control or understand. [T] (3:11).

Passage-specific difficulties

  • 3:1–8 at funerals. Pastorally valuable, but it must not be cut off from 3:11–14, which puts the times in God’s hand. Pastoral.
  • 3:3 “a time to kill”. Ethical. The list is descriptive of human life under God’s providence, not a licence. Name that briefly.
  • 3:15b “God seeks what has been driven away” (אֶת־נִרְדָּף). Interpretive.
    • Options. God recalls the past (so that it recurs); or God seeks the persecuted (Swete: ὁ θεὸς ζητήσει τὸν διωκόμενον, “God will seek the one pursued” — verified).
    • Handling. Flag it; don’t build on it.

Unit 4 — 3:16–4:16 Injustice, the One Fate, and the Lonely

3:20–21 הַכֹּל הוֹלֵךְ אֶל־מָקוֹם אֶחָד הַכֹּל הָיָה מִן־הֶעָפָר וְהַכֹּל שָׁב אֶל־הֶעָפָר׃ מִי יוֹדֵעַ רוּחַ בְּנֵי הָאָדָם הָעֹלָה הִיא לְמָעְלָה … All go to the same place. All came from the dust and all return to the dust. Who knows that the breath of man ascends upward …? (NASB95)

4:1 … וְהִנֵּה דִּמְעַת הָעֲשׁוּקִים וְאֵין לָהֶם מְנַחֵם … … behold I saw the tears of the oppressed and that they had no one to comfort them …

Headline Findings

  • Death in 3:19–21 is read through Gen 3:19 and Ps 104:29. [T] Both source texts are about the return to dust, and Ps 104:29 is about animals, which matches Qohelet’s man-and-beast frame.
    • עָפָר (“dust”), שׁוּב (“to return”) and רוּחַ (“spirit, breath”) are verified across Gen 3:19, Ps 104:29 and Eccl 3:20; 12:7.
    Surfaced by: Tool 11 triad, Vocabulary. High confidence.
  • “There is no one to comfort them” (4:1) is the refrain of Lamentations 1, the next book in the BHS order. [T]
    • The phrase אֵין (לָהֶם) מְנַחֵם (“[they have] no one to comfort [them]”) occurs, across plene and defective spellings, only at Eccl 4:1 (twice) and Lam 1:2, 9, 17, 21 (phrase search, WLC).
    Surfaced by: Tool 11, Canonical Position. High confidence on the wording; moderate on canonical intent.
  • The unit moves from injustice to solitude, and puts the two together. [T] אֶחָד (“one”) occurs 6 times in 4:8–12, then the “threefold cord” (4:12). The oppressed have no comforter (4:1); the lone man has no second (4:8). Surfaced by: Repetition, Structure. High confidence.

2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

  • Preceding movement. 3:17 grows out of 3:1–15: God has a time for everything. The unit tests that at once against injustice (3:16) and against death (3:18–21).
  • Necessity answer. Having claimed that God’s times are fitting (3:11), the book must face the times that look unfitting: justice perverted, man dying like a beast, the oppressed without a comforter. The unit exists here to stop 3:11 from becoming a platitude. [I]
  • Implication. 3:17, “God will judge”, is spoken by Qohelet himself, in the body. The judgement hope is not only the epilogue’s (12:14). [T] (Trap 1 check).

3. Structure

3:18–21 Man and beast One fate, one breath, one place, dust — “who knows?”
3:22 Nothing better Rejoice in one’s works — “his portion”

Device. A string of “I saw” observations — וְעוֹד רָאִיתִי (“furthermore I saw”, 3:16); וְשַׁבְתִּי אֲנִי וָאֶרְאֶה (“then I looked again”, 4:1, 7); רָאִיתִי (“I saw”, 4:4, 15). Each is answered by a “better” or a “nothing better”. [T]

5. Parallels

  • 3:16, synonymous. “The place of justice — wickedness / the place of righteousness — wickedness.” מָקוֹם (“place”) occurs 3 times in the unit (3:16 ×2, 3:20). The “place” of justice becomes the “one place” of the dead. [T]
  • 4:10–12, stepped. One falls, two lift; two keep warm, one cannot; one is overpowered, two resist, three are not quickly broken. [T]

7. Vocabulary

  • מוֹתַר (“advantage”, 3:19). A synonym of יִתְרוֹן (“advantage”): “the מוֹתַר of man over beast — אָיִן (‘there is none’)”. It occurs 3 times in the Hebrew Bible (Prov 14:23; 21:5; Eccl 3:19; lemma, WLC). Proverbs uses it positively (“in all labour there is profit”, Prov 14:23). Ecclesiastes denies it at death. [T] Moderate–high: a Proverbs word turned.
  • מְנַחֵם (“comforter”, 4:1, twice). See the headline finding. [T]
  • שַׁבֵּחַ (“I congratulated”, 4:2). שָׁבַח (“to praise, commend”) occurs twice in the book: 4:2 (the dead) and 8:15 (joy) (lemma, WLC). An internal reversal (see Move 4).
  • מִסְכֵּן (“poor”, 4:13). Found only in Ecclesiastes (4 occurrences: 4:13; 9:15 ×2; 9:16; lemma, WLC). It links the “poor wise youth” to the “poor wise man” of 9:15. [T]
  • בֵּית הָסוּרִים (“the prison”, 4:14). The OSHB index takes it from אָסַר (“to bind”), as BHS says (= הָאֲסוּרִים, “the prisoners”, app. 4:14 ᵃ). The same lemma stands in Joseph’s prison notices (Gen 39:20 ×2; 40:3, 5; verified). See Tool 11.
  • קִנְאָה (“rivalry, jealousy”, 4:4). Also at 9:6, among the passions that perish with the dead (lemma, WLC).

8. Translations

  • 3:21 — a category 2 matter of vocalisation, not consonants.
    • The consonants are the same in every witness.
    • The WLC points הָעֹלָה and הַיֹּרֶדֶת with the article: “the spirit … which goes up … which goes down”.
    • BHS app. 3:21 ᵇ ᶜ proposes the interrogative הַעֹלה and הֲיֹרדת (“whether it goes up … whether it goes down”) “cf Vrs”. Swete agrees: εἰ ἀναβαίνει … εἰ καταβαίνει (“whether it goes up … whether it goes down”).
    • Witness named. The pointing is Leningrad’s reading tradition as BHS prints it. Its manuscript spread is unchecked ([unchecked — apparatus spread]).
    • English versions. NASB95 “Who knows that the breath of man ascends upward” follows the article reading, roughly. ESV “whether”, and NIV84 “if” (with the MT in the margin), follow the versions.
    • What it changes. On the article reading, Qohelet’s doubt concerns who knows the upward-going spirit, which leaves the ascent assumed. On the interrogative, he doubts the ascent itself.
    • Position: the interrogative fits the flow of “who knows?” (3:21a; 6:12). The article reading may reflect a later theological preference [S], recalled. Moderate. Do not settle 3:21 against 12:7 without naming this.
  • 4:8 ketiv/qere. עיניו (“his eyes”, ketiv) / עֵינוֹ (“his eye”, qere). NASB95 “his eyes” follows the ketiv. No finding depends on it.
  • Pulpit divergence note. N/A. For an ESV or NIV84 engagement, 3:21 will be heard as “whether/if”: a doubt about the spirit’s ascent.

  • מִקְרֶה (“fate”): 3 times in 3:19.
  • הַכֹּל (“all”): 3:19, 20 ×3.
  • אֶחָד (“one”): 3:19 ×2, 20; 4:8–12 ×6.
  • עָשַׁק (“to oppress”) and its nouns: 4:1 ×3 (lemmas 6231 and 6217, WLC).
  • “Better”: טוֹב (“better”) at 4:3, 6, 9, 13.
  • אֵין קֵץ (“no end”): 4:8, 16 — and 12:12 (lemma, WLC).

11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

Gen 3:19 → Eccl 3:20 (high confidence)

  • Source context. God’s sentence on the man after the fall (Gen 3:17–19): cursed ground, toil and sweat, “till you return (שׁוּבְךָ) to the ground, for out of it you were taken; for you are dust (עָפָר), and to dust you shall return.” The verse is the verdict of death on the one who ate.
  • Book usage. The first explicit Gen 3 use, prepared by the toil of 1:3 (conceptually) and the returning of 1:6–7. It returns at 12:7.
  • OT-to-OT. Ps 104:29 (“You take away their breath (רוּחָם), they expire and return (יְשׁוּבוּן) to their dust (עֲפָרָם)”) speaks of the animals (104:25–28). Job 34:14–15 generalises it to “all flesh”. Both are Writings neighbours, and all three lemmas are verified in Ps 104:29. Ecclesiastes joins a conversation the Writings have already begun, and explains why its man-and-beast comparison is scriptural rather than cynical.
  • What it adds. The one fate is the curse of Gen 3, not a fact of nature. It frames 3:19–21 as the fall’s sentence, not biology — which is where the gospel’s answer lands.

Lam 1:2, 9, 17, 21 → Eccl 4:1 (moderate on direction; high on the wording)

  • Source context. Daughter Zion, widowed and exiled. “Among all her lovers she has אֵין מְנַחֵם (‘no one to comfort her’)” (1:2), the refrain of the whole first poem.
  • Book usage. First use.
  • OT-to-OT. Isa 40:1 (“Comfort, comfort my people”) answers the Lamentations refrain within the canon.
  • What it adds. In the BHS order Lamentations follows Ecclesiastes. Qohelet’s “no comforter” for the oppressed hands the reader to Zion’s “no comforter”. Direction open: the canonical sequence and any compositional relation are not resolved. [T] for the phrase.

Gen 39:20; 40:3, 5 → Eccl 4:13–14 (“from the prison he went out to reign”) (uncertain → moderate)

  • Move 1. Joseph, imprisoned where “the king’s prisoners (אֲסוּרֵי) were confined” (39:20), rises to rule Egypt (41:39–44).
  • Evidence. Lemma plus narrative pattern: a poor, wise young man goes from prison to rule. Moderate. The overview did not list it; flagged.

1 Kgs 1:1–4 → Eccl 4:11–16 (warmth; the old king; the youth) (uncertain)

  • Move 1. King David, old, “could not keep warm”. His servants: “let her lie (וְשָׁכְבָה) in your bosom, that my lord the king may keep warm (וְחַם)” (1:2). 1 Kgs 1 then tells how young Solomon replaces him.
  • Evidence. שָׁכַב (“to lie down”) and חָמַם (“to be warm”) are both verified at 1 Kgs 1:2 and Eccl 4:11. They are followed in Ecclesiastes by an old king and a young successor (4:13–16).
  • Verdict. An adjacency worth testing, not a finding. Uncertain. Route to Open Questions.

Prov 6:10; 24:33 → Eccl 4:5 (moderate, cognate)

  • Confirmed per the overview’s gate: the Proverbs texts have the noun חִבֻּק (“folding”); Ecclesiastes has the participle חֹבֵק (“folding”).
  • 4:6’s “better one handful” then qualifies the sluggard proverb: rest is not the fool’s idleness.

    • 4:2 שִׁבַּח (“commended”) the dead → 8:15 שִׁבַּח joy. The same verb reverses its object. [T] Moderate on design.
    • 4:3 the unborn are better → 6:3–5. The miscarriage is better. [T]
    • 4:13 מִסְכֵּן וְחָכָם (“poor and wise”) → 9:15. [T]
    • 4:13 לְהִזָּהֵר (“to be warned”) → 12:12 הִזָּהֵר (“be warned”). The only two occurrences in the book (lemma, WLC). The old king “no longer knows how to be warned”; the epilogue warns “my son”. [T] Moderate–high on design.
    • 4:8, 16 אֵין קֵץ (“no end”) → 12:12. “Of making many books there is אֵין קֵץ (‘no end’).” [T]

Christological note (brief)

“No comforter” (4:1) is answered in the canon by Isa 40:1, and in the New Testament by the Comforter (παράκλητος, “Advocate, Comforter”, John 14:16). The “one fate” (3:19) is the curse Christ bore (Gal 3:13) and broke (1 Cor 15:21–22). Trajectory; moderate. Resist allegorising the “threefold cord” (4:12) as the Trinity or as marriage-plus-God; the text counts companions. [I]

16. So What? (brief)

  • Worldview. Injustice is real and seen; God will judge (3:17).
  • Behaviour. Refuse rivalry as a motive for work (4:4). Value companionship over solitary accumulation (4:8–12).
  • Candidate FCF. Loneliness in success, and helplessness before injustice. [T]

Passage-specific difficulties

  • 3:18–21 — man no better than beast? Apologetic and pastoral.
    • Rhetorical function. It observes the shared fate (death), not a shared nature.
    • Honest exegesis. 3:21 is a question, and the vocalisation is disputed (see Translations).
    • Handling. Address it: this is the curse’s leveller, not a denial of the image of God (7:29).
  • 4:2–3 — better never to have been born? Pastoral. Handle with care before the suffering. The book corrects it with “a live dog is better than a dead lion” (9:4). [T]

Unit 5 — 5:1–7 (Heb 4:17–5:6) Guard Your Steps: Worship and Vows

5:4 (Heb 5:3) כַּאֲשֶׁר תִּדֹּר נֶדֶר לֵאלֹהִים אַל־תְּאַחֵר לְשַׁלְּמוֹ כִּי אֵין חֵפֶץ בַּכְּסִילִים אֵת אֲשֶׁר־תִּדֹּר שַׁלֵּם When you make a vow to God, do not be late in paying it; for He takes no delight in fools. Pay what you vow!

5:7 (Heb 5:6) כִּי בְרֹב חֲלֹמוֹת וַהֲבָלִים וּדְבָרִים הַרְבֵּה כִּי אֶת־הָאֱלֹהִים יְרָא For in many dreams and in many words there is emptiness. Rather, fear God. (NASB95)

Headline Findings

  • This unit brings the book’s first sustained imperatives addressed to the reader, and its first “fear God” command — in exactly the wording of 12:13. [T]
    • The command is אֶת־הָאֱלֹהִים יְרָא (“fear God”). The four-word phrase occurs only at 5:7 (Heb 5:6) and 12:13 (phrase search, plene and skeletal, WLC).
    Surfaced by: Structure, Move 4, Vocabulary. High confidence.
  • The vow law is Deut 23:21–23 (Heb 23:22–24), near-verbatim, with יהוה (“the LORD”) replaced by אֱלֹהִים (“God”). [T] (The overview’s finding, confirmed.) High confidence.
  • The unit’s warning about words is framed by Proverbs. [T]
    • בְּרֹב דְּבָרִים (“in many words”, 5:3 [Heb 5:2]) occurs only here and at Prov 10:19 (phrase search, WLC).
    • The unit is bracketed by חֲלוֹם (“dream”) and רֹב (“many”): 5:3 and 5:7 (Heb 5:2, 6), the only two occurrences of חֲלוֹם in the book (lemma, WLC).
    Surfaced by: Tool 11, Structure. High confidence.

2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

  • Preceding movement. Unit 4 ended with popularity fading and “striving after wind” (4:16). The book so far has been observation.
  • Necessity answer. After four chapters of what Qohelet saw, the book turns to what the reader must do before God, and the first place it sends him is the house of God. The unit exists here to show that the “fear” of 3:14 is practised in worship: listening, few words, vows paid. It is the first time the book addresses “you”. [I]
  • Implication. Qohelet is not irreligious (Trap 1). His first commands are about worship. [T]

  • כִּי (“for”) at 5:1, 2, 3, 4, 7 (Heb 4:17; 5:1, 2, 3, 6). Each command is grounded. The last כִּי at 5:7 (Heb 5:6) is adversative (“rather”; NASB95 “Rather, fear God”). [T]

7. Vocabulary

  • כְּסִיל (“fool”). 3 times in the unit: 5:1, 3, 4 (Heb 4:17; 5:2, 3) (lemma, WLC). The fool here is a worshipper.
  • שְׁגָגָה (“unintentional sin, mistake”, 5:6 [Heb 5:5]). A cultic term. 19 occurrences in the Hebrew Bible, 15 of them in Leviticus and Numbers (lemma, WLC). It is the category for which sin offerings atone (Lev 4; Num 15:22–29). Taxonomy check:
      1. The category is established in the Torah.
      1. The unit has other cultic markers: house of God, sacrifice, vow.
      1. The activation explains the excuse: the fool tries to reclassify a broken vow as a שְׁגָגָה, to get it into the sacrificial provision.
    • All three pass. Moderate–high. It recurs at 10:5, of a ruler’s “error”.
  • הַמַּלְאָךְ (“the messenger”, 5:6 [Heb 5:5]). Its only occurrence in the book. The Greek reads “before God” (BHS app. 5:5 ᵃ: 𝔊 τοῦ θεοῦ = הָאֱלֹהִים). See Translations.
  • חֵפֶץ (“delight”, 5:4 [Heb 5:3]). “He has no חֵפֶץ (‘delight’) in fools.” See Tool 11 on 1 Sam 15:22.

8. Translations

  • 5:1 (Heb 4:17) — the Greek. It reads ὑπὲρ δόμα τῶν ἀφρόνων θυσία σου (“better than the gift of fools is your sacrifice”; BHS app. 4:17 ᶜ ᵈ, where the Greek reads מִמַּתַּת, “than the gift”). This is category 2. The Greek turns a preference for listening over sacrifice into a preference for your sacrifice over fools’. The MT is the harder and more pointed reading, and it fits “draw near to listen”. Position: MT. Moderate.
  • 5:6 (Heb 5:5) הַמַּלְאָךְ. “Messenger” (MT), against “God” (Greek). NASB95 “the messenger of God” combines the two. ESV “the messenger” follows the MT. The messenger is probably a priest or temple official (cf. Mal 2:7, the priest as the LORD’s מַלְאָךְ). That identification is [I], uncertain.
  • 5:7 (Heb 5:6). NASB95 “emptiness … Rather, fear God”. ESV “vanity; but God is the one you must fear”. NIV84 “meaningless. Therefore stand in awe of God”. The echo with 12:13 survives in the NASB95 alone (“fear God”). The ESV rephrases it, and the NIV84’s “stand in awe” breaks the verbal tie to 12:13’s “Fear God”. Pulpit note (for an ESV or NIV84 engagement): the congregation will not hear the 5:7 → 12:13 echo unless the preacher points it out.
  • Pulpit divergence note. N/A — no pulpit text declared (see above for the ESV and NIV84).

Deut 23:21–23 (Heb 23:22–24) → Eccl 5:4–5 (Heb 5:3–4) (high confidence)

  • Source context. Deuteronomy’s code (chs. 12–26), in the laws on the purity of the assembly and the camp. “When you make a vow to the LORD your God, you shall not delay to pay it, for the LORD your God will surely require it of you, and it would be sin in you.     • The source’s register is covenant seriousness about speech before God.
  • Book usage. The second Deuteronomy use (after 3:14). It continues the Torah frame that 12:13 will close.
  • OT-to-OT. Num 30:3 (Heb) on vows; Prov 20:25 (“it is a trap … after the vows, to make inquiry”; נֶדֶר, “vow”, verified); Ps 76:12 (Heb). The wisdom tradition had already taken the vow law into its warnings about rash speech. Ecclesiastes sits between Torah and Proverbs.

Prov 10:19 → Eccl 5:3 (Heb 5:2) (high confidence)

  • Source context. In the first Solomonic collection: “When there are many words (בְּרֹב דְּבָרִים), transgression is unavoidable, but he who restrains his lips is wise.” Its function is antithetic wisdom about restraint in speech.
  • Book usage. Proverbs is a live source (1:1; 4:5).
  • OT-to-OT. With Deut 23 in this unit, Torah (vows) and Wisdom (words) are combined. Speech before God is governed by both.
  • What it adds. The worshipper’s many words are the fool’s many words. Proverbs’ general rule is applied to prayer.

1 Sam 15:22 → Eccl 5:1, 4 (Heb 4:17; 5:3) (moderate)

Samuel rebukes Saul for sparing Amalek’s best “to sacrifice”: “Has the LORD as much חֵפֶץ (‘delight’) in burnt offerings and זְבָחִים (‘sacrifices’) as in שְׁמֹעַ (‘obeying’) the voice of the LORD?   • Book usage. First Samuel use.
  • OT-to-OT. Prov 15:8; 21:27 (זֶבַח רְשָׁעִים, “the sacrifice of the wicked”, verified); Prov 21:3. Wisdom had already made “sacrifice of the X” a category, and Ecclesiastes’ “sacrifice of fools” is formed on that pattern.

16. So What? (brief)

  • Behaviour. Come to listen; pray with few words; keep vows or don’t make them.
  • Worldview. God is in heaven (transcendence), and fear is the fitting posture.
  • Christ. The better Worshipper who “learned obedience” (Heb 5:8) and whose “Yes” fulfils every promise (2 Cor 1:20). A trajectory from the vow theme. Moderate.
  • Candidate FCF. Religious talk without reverence. [T]

Passage-specific difficulties

  • Rash vows today. Pastoral. Church covenants, membership and marriage vows are the modern forms. Apply with care. Matt 5:37 is the New Testament’s word on the same concern.
  • “Fear God” read as terror. Vocabulary. In context it means reverent submission (3:14). It is not dread.

Unit 6 — 5:8–6:9 (Heb 5:7–6:9) Wealth, and the Gift of Enjoying It

5:19 (Heb 5:18) גַּם כָּל־הָאָדָם אֲשֶׁר נָתַן־לוֹ הָאֱלֹהִים עֹשֶׁר וּנְכָסִים וְהִשְׁלִיטוֹ לֶאֱכֹל מִמֶּנּוּ … Furthermore, as for every man to whom God has given riches and wealth, He has also empowered him to eat from them …

6:2 אִישׁ אֲשֶׁר יִתֶּן־לוֹ הָאֱלֹהִים עֹשֶׁר וּנְכָסִים וְכָבוֹד … וְלֹא־יַשְׁלִיטֶנּוּ הָאֱלֹהִים לֶאֱכֹל מִמֶּנּוּ כִּי אִישׁ נָכְרִי יֹאכֲלֶנּוּ … a man to whom God has given riches and wealth and honor … yet God has not empowered him to eat from them, for a foreigner enjoys them … (NASB95)

Headline Findings

  • The unit turns on a matched pair of verses. God gives the power to enjoy (5:19 [Heb 5:18]) or withholds it (6:2). [T]
    • Both verses share the object עֹשֶׁר וּנְכָסִים (“riches and wealth”).
    • Both share the verb שָׁלַט (“to empower”), hiphil, with לֶאֱכֹל מִמֶּנּוּ (“to eat from it”).
    • The first is affirmed (הִשְׁלִיטוֹ, “empowered him”); the second negated (לֹא־יַשְׁלִיטֶנּוּ, “has not empowered him”). The lemmas are verified in both.
    • BHS app. 5:18 ᵃ records a proposal to add וְכָבוֹד (“and honour”) to 5:18 “cf 6,2”. The editors saw the pairing too.
    Surfaced by: Structure, Parallels, Repetition. High confidence.
  • The triad “riches, wealth and honour” is God’s promise to Solomon (2 Chr 1:11–12). [T] The Solomonic king is the case in point. See Tool 11. High confidence on the wording.
  • The ills in this unit are named as sickness. [T]
    • רָעָה חוֹלָה (“a sick evil”) appears at 5:13 and 5:16 (Heb 5:12, 15); חֳלִי (“sickness”) at 5:17 (Heb 5:16) and 6:2.
    • The formula יֵשׁ רָעָה … רָאִיתִי תַּחַת הַשָּׁמֶשׁ (“there is an evil … I have seen under the sun”) stands at 5:13 (Heb 5:12), 6:1 and 10:5 (phrase search, WLC).
    Surfaced by: Repetition, Vocabulary. High confidence.

2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

  • Preceding movement. Unit 5 taught reverence and few words before God, ending with “fear God” (5:7).
  • Necessity answer. Fear of God must now meet the most common rival object of trust: money. The unit exists here because the worshipper who has just been told to keep his vows goes out into a world of officials, profits and hoarded riches (5:8–17). The book shows that enjoying what one has is as much God’s gift as having it (5:18–20; 6:2). [I]
  • Implication. This is the book’s central treatment of wealth. It closes the first half with the last “striving after wind” (6:9). [T]

11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

2 Chr 1:11–12 → Eccl 6:2 (and 5:19 [Heb 5:18]) (high confidence on the wording)

  • Source context. At Gibeon (Chronicles’ version), God answers Solomon’s request for חָכְמָה וּמַדָּע (“wisdom and knowledge”): “because you did not ask for עֹשֶׁר נְכָסִים וְכָבוֹד (‘riches, wealth, and honour’) … wisdom and knowledge are granted to you, and I will give you עֹשֶׁר וּנְכָסִים וְכָבוֹד (‘riches and wealth and honour’) such as none of the kings who were before you has possessed, nor will those who come after you.” The function is the divine grant that founds Solomon’s reign in Chronicles.
  • Book usage. The Solomon cluster (1:16; 2:7–10). This is its first Chronicles-specific wording. It returns with מַדָּע (“knowledge”) at 10:20.
  • OT-to-OT. The 1 Kgs 3:11–13 parallel has עֹשֶׁר וְכָבוֹד (“riches and honour”) without נְכָסִים (“wealth”). The triad is Chronicles’ own. [T]
  • What it adds. The man of 6:2 has what God gave Solomon — and lacks the one thing only God gives: the power to enjoy it. Canonical order: Chronicles closes the BHS Ketuvim, after Ecclesiastes. The direction of dependence is open; report both, resolve neither.

Job 1:21 → Eccl 5:15 (Heb 5:14) (high confidence — the overview’s link, triad added)

  • Source context. Job, having lost children and wealth, falls to the ground and worships: “Naked I came from my mother’s womb, and naked I shall return there. The LORD gave and the LORD has taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD.” The function is worship in loss: the verdict of a righteous man on the vanishing of wealth.
  • Book usage. First Job use (3:20 used Job 34:14–15 as an OT-to-OT partner only).
  • OT-to-OT. Ps 49:17–18 (Heb): the rich man “will carry nothing away when he dies” (a Writings neighbour). [I]; not verified by lemma in this run. Route to Open Questions.
  • What it adds. Qohelet uses Job’s words about a man who loses by foolish hoarding. Job’s sentence ends in “blessed be the name of the LORD”; Qohelet’s ends in “eats in darkness with great vexation” (5:17). The contrast marks two responses to the same nakedness. [I] Moderate–high.

Prov 16:26 → Eccl 6:7 (moderate–high)

  • Source context. “A worker’s (עָמֵל) appetite (נֶפֶשׁ) works for him, for his mouth (פִּיהוּ) urges him on.” Proverbs treats appetite as a motive for honest work.
  • Evidence. נֶפֶשׁ (“appetite”) and פִּיהוּ (“his mouth”) are verified in both verses. Proverbs has the adjective עָמֵל (“toiling”); Ecclesiastes the noun עָמָל (“toil”) — cognate.
  • Book usage. Proverbs is live (5:3 [Heb 5:2]; 4:5).
  • What it adds. Ecclesiastes adds “and yet the appetite is not filled”. Proverbs’ motive for work becomes Qohelet’s proof of the work’s endlessness.

Deut 28:30–33 → Eccl 6:2 (and 2:4, 18–21) (moderate)

  • Source context. Among the covenant curses: “you shall build a house, but you will not live in it; you shall plant a vineyard, but you will not use its fruit … a people whom you do not know shall eat (יֹאכַל) up the produce of your ground.”
  • Evidence. בָּנָה (“to build”), בַּיִת (“house”), נָטַע (“to plant”) and כֶּרֶם (“vineyard”) are verified at Deut 28:30 and Eccl 2:4. אָכַל (“to eat”) is verified at Deut 28:33 and Eccl 6:2.
  • What it adds. The stranger who eats (6:2) and the heir who takes (2:18–21) are the shape of the Deuteronomic futility curse, observed without the covenant frame. [I]

16. So What? (brief)

  • Stop. Loving money as if it could satisfy (5:10).
  • Start. Thanking God for the capacity to enjoy, not only the goods (5:19).
  • Four audiences.
    • The rich: see 6:2.
    • The anxious poor: see 5:12.
    • The church: “one official over another” — injustice is seen (5:8).
    • The unbeliever: “naked … so will he return” (5:15).
  • Christ. “What will it profit a man if he gains the whole world and forfeits his soul?” (Mark 8:36): the יִתְרוֹן (“advantage”) question on Jesus’ lips. A trajectory of the question, not a citation claim. Moderate.
  • Candidate FCF. Having much and enjoying little. [T]

Passage-specific difficulties

  • 6:2 — God withholds enjoyment? Doctrinal and pastoral.
    • Rhetorical function. It is the dark counterpart of 5:19.
    • Honest exegesis. The text attributes both giving and withholding to God (הָאֱלֹהִים, “God”, in both verses). Don’t soften it.
    • Handling. Address it: enjoyment is a grace, not a right.
  • 5:8–9 (Heb 5:7–8). Obscure. Don’t build a political theology on 5:9.

Unit 7 — 6:10–7:14 What Is Good? The “Better” Sayings

6:12 כִּי מִי־יוֹדֵעַ מַה־טּוֹב לָאָדָם בַּחַיִּים … כִּי מִי־יַגִּיד לָאָדָם מַה־יִּהְיֶה אַחֲרָיו תַּחַת הַשָּׁמֶשׁ For who knows what is good for a man during his lifetime …? For who can tell a man what will be after him under the sun?

7:1–2 טוֹב שֵׁם מִשֶּׁמֶן טוֹב … טוֹב לָלֶכֶת אֶל־בֵּית־אֵבֶל מִלֶּכֶת אֶל־בֵּית מִשְׁתֶּה בַּאֲשֶׁר הוּא סוֹף כָּל־הָאָדָם A good name is better than a good ointment … It is better to go to a house of mourning than to go to a house of feasting, because that is the end of every man … (NASB95)

Headline Findings

  • 6:10–12 is the book’s hinge. It asks two questions, and the second half answers them. [T]
    • The questions: “who knows what is good?” and “who can tell what will be after him?”
    • The “better” sayings of 7:1–14 take up the first: טוֹב (“good, better”) occurs 11 times in 7:1–14 (lemma, WLC).
    • The “cannot find out / who can tell” passages take up the second (7:14, 24; 8:7, 17; 10:14).
    Surfaced by: Structure, Repetition, Move 4. High confidence on the counts; moderate on the design.
  • 7:2 names death as “the end (סוֹף) of every man”. 12:13 names fearing God as “the whole of man” (כָּל־הָאָדָם) at “the end (סוֹף) of the matter”. [T] The Greek carries it too: τέλος (“end”) in both places (Rahlfs 7:2; Swete 12:13). High on the words.
  • The sayings are carried by sound as well as sense. [T] These are paronomasia, where the sound is the point:
    • טוֹב שֵׁם מִשֶּׁמֶן טוֹב (ṭôb šēm miššemen ṭôb, “better a name than oil”, 7:1).
    • כְקוֹל הַסִּירִים תַּחַת הַסִּיר (kĕqôl hassîrîm taḥat hassîr, “like the crackling of thorns under a pot”, 7:6), which follows שִׁיר כְּסִילִים (šîr kĕsîlîm, “the song of fools”, 7:5).
    Surfaced by: Original Languages, Tone. High.

2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

  • Preceding movement. The first half closed on “striving after wind” (6:9), having shown that neither wealth nor long life guarantees good.
  • Necessity answer. If having does not yield good, the question becomes: what is good? The book must now ask it outright (6:12). It answers with comparative wisdom (“better than”) rather than absolute gain. The unit exists here to turn the book from profit to relative good, since absolute יִתְרוֹן (“advantage”) has been ruled out. [I]
  • Implication. Every טוֹב … מִן (“better … than”) in 7:1–12 is Qohelet’s answer to 6:12. [T]/[I]

3. Structure

7:1–4 Death and mourning are better Name/oil; death/birth; mourning/feasting; sorrow/laughter

Device. A string of “better” sayings between two “consider God” frames: 6:10 (“already named”) and 7:13–14 (“God has made”). [T]

5. Parallels

  • 7:1, chiastic sound-pair. שֵׁם / שֶׁמֶן (“name” / “oil”), then death against birth. [T]
  • 7:4, antithetic. “The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning / the heart of fools in the house of joy.” בֵּית אֵבֶל (“house of mourning”) occurs at 7:2 and 7:4. [T]
  • 7:14, antithetic. “In the day of טוֹבָה (‘prosperity’) be happy / in the day of רָעָה (‘adversity’) consider.” Then: God has made one לְעֻמַּת (“as well as”) the other. עֻמַּת (“alongside”) also stands at 5:16 (Heb 5:15). [T]

7. Vocabulary

  • צֵל (“shadow, shade”). 6:12 (“like a shadow”), 7:12 ×2 (wisdom’s “protection”), 8:13 (“like a shadow”) (lemma, WLC). The same word is transience in 6:12 and 8:13, and protection in 7:12. [T]
  • כַּעַס (“vexation, sorrow”). The noun occurs at 1:18; 2:23; 7:3, 9; 11:10 (lemma, WLC). At 7:3 it is “better than laughter”; at 7:9 it “rests in the bosom of fools”. [T] The tension is deliberate: the grief of wisdom is good, the anger of the fool is not. [I]
  • שְׂחוֹק (“laughter”). 2:2; 7:3, 6; 10:19 (lemma, WLC).
  • Proper-noun inventory. None. אָדָם (“man”, 6:10): “it is known what אָדָם is”. A possible play on the name Adam is [I], uncertain. Not built on.

8. Translations

  • Swete versification. Swete prints 6:12 as its 7:1 (see the header). The Greek chapter therefore begins with the question “who knows what is good?” — which arguably marks the hinge better than the Hebrew chapter break does. [T] for Swete’s numbering.
  • 7:7 עֹשֶׁק (“oppression”). NASB95 “oppression makes a wise man mad”; Swete συκοφαντία (“extortion”). BHS notes a Qumran reading (app. 7:7 ᵃ, 𝔔). No finding depends on it.
  • 7:12. The Greek reads “in its shadow wisdom is as the shadow of silver” (BHS app. 7:12 ᵃ⁻ᵃ). A category 1 and category 2 mix; no finding depends on it.
  • 6:10, ketiv/qere. שהתקיף / שֶׁתַּקִּיף (“who is stronger”), as BHS prints them. The NASB95 follows the qere.
  • Pulpit divergence note. N/A — no pulpit text declared.

10. Repetition

  • טוֹב (“good, better”): 11 times in 7:1–14.
  • “Who knows / who can tell”: 6:12 ×2.
  • רָאָה (“to see”) as imperative: 7:13, 14 (רְאֵה, “consider”).
  • The עות/תקן pair (“bend” / “straighten”) returns at 7:13. “Who is able to straighten (לְתַקֵּן) what He has bent (עִוְּתוֹ)?” The only other verse with both is 1:15 (lemma, WLC). [T]

9. Tone and Feel

A grave, measured cadence. These are the sayings of the funeral house, not of the banquet. Soundtrack: a slow chorale. It is not morbid; it is sober. The humour of 7:6 (crackling thorns) punctures the fools’ noise. [T]

16. So What? (brief)

  • Worldview. Death clarifies what is good (7:2, “the living will take it to heart”).
  • Behaviour. Attend funerals; welcome rebuke; refuse nostalgia (7:10).
  • Candidate FCF. Avoidance of death and of correction. [T]

Passage-specific difficulties

  • 7:1b “the day of death better than the day of birth”. Pastoral. For the bereaved and the despairing, read it with 7:2b: mourning teaches the living. It does not commend death.
  • 7:10 against lament. Pastoral. It forbids idealising the past; it does not forbid grief (see 7:2–4).

Unit 8 — 7:15–29 ⭐ Righteousness, Wisdom, and What Cannot Be Found

7:20 כִּי אָדָם אֵין צַדִּיק בָּאָרֶץ אֲשֶׁר יַעֲשֶׂה־טּוֹב וְלֹא יֶחֱטָא Indeed, there is not a righteous man on earth who continually does good and who never sins.

7:23 כָּל־זֹה נִסִּיתִי בַחָכְמָה אָמַרְתִּי אֶחְכָּמָה וְהִיא רְחוֹקָה מִמֶּנִּי I tested all this with wisdom, and I said, “I will be wise,” but it was far from me.

7:29 לְבַד רְאֵה־זֶה מָצָאתִי אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה הָאֱלֹהִים אֶת־הָאָדָם יָשָׁר וְהֵמָּה בִקְשׁוּ חִשְּׁבֹנוֹת רַבִּים Behold, I have found only this, that God made men upright, but they have sought out many devices. (NASB95)

Headline Findings

  • The unit is a search that fails, told in its own vocabulary. [T]
    • מָצָא (“to find”) occurs 8 times in 7:24–29 (7:24, 26, 27 ×2, 28 ×3, 29).
    • בִּקֵּשׁ (“to seek”) occurs at 7:25, 28, 29.
    • חֶשְׁבּוֹן (“explanation, reckoning”) occurs at 7:25, 27, set against the cognate חִשְּׁבֹנוֹת (“devices”, 7:29). They are a different lemma (2810): cognate, not identical.
    The only thing found is that God made man upright and man went seeking devices. Surfaced by: Repetition, Structure, Vocabulary. High confidence.
  • “Far from me” (7:23) answers Deut 30:11, “it is not far from you”, in reverse. [T]
    • Deuteronomy says of the commandment: לֹא רְחֹקָה הִוא (“it is not far”). Qohelet says of wisdom: וְהִיא רְחוֹקָה מִמֶּנִּי (“and it was far from me”).
    • רָחוֹק (“far”), the pronoun הִיא (“it”), and מִן (“from”) with a suffix are verified in both verses.
    • Paul reads Deut 30:12–14 of Christ, the word that has come near (Rom 10:6–8).
    Surfaced by: Tool 11 triad, Christological Reading. Moderate–high.
  • 7:20 is Solomon’s own prayer (1 Kgs 8:46), and in Greek it is very probably part of Paul’s “none righteous” (Rom 3:10). [T] for the Hebrew and Greek wording; [S] that Paul uses it. The Hebrew link is moderate–high; the Greek link moderate (category 3).

2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

  • Preceding movement. Unit 7 ended: “consider the work of God … so that man will not find out anything” (7:13–14).
  • Necessity answer. The book now tests that inability at its most painful point. The righteous man perishes; the wicked man lives long (7:15). Qohelet then tests whether wisdom itself can be found (7:23–29). The unit exists here to push the “cannot find” of 7:14 into the moral order and into wisdom itself. It is also where Deuteronomy’s promise of “long days” is most openly contradicted by observation (7:15; see unit 9 for 8:12–13). [I]
  • Implication. 7:18’s “the one who fears God comes forth with both” is Qohelet’s own resolution inside the body (Trap 1). [T]

5. Parallels

  • 7:15, antithetic and chiastic. צַדִּיק אֹבֵד בְּצִדְקוֹ / רָשָׁע מַאֲרִיךְ בְּרָעָתוֹ (“a righteous man perishing in his righteousness / a wicked man prolonging in his evil”). [T]

  • תִּשּׁוֹמֵם (“ruin yourself”, 7:16). שָׁמֵם (“to be desolate”) hithpolel: “why be appalled / destroy yourself?” Moderate: the sense is disputed.

  • הָאִשָּׁה (“the woman”, 7:26), with the article. אִשָּׁה (“woman”) occurs 3 times in the book: 7:26, 28; 9:9 (lemma, WLC). The trap-woman of 7:26 stands against “the woman whom you love” of 9:9. [T]

  • Pulpit divergence note. N/A — no pulpit text declared.

11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

Deut 30:11 → Eccl 7:23 (moderate–high)

  • Source context. Moses’ closing appeal: “this commandment which I command you today is not too difficult for you, nor is it far off (וְלֹא רְחֹקָה הִוא). It is not in heaven … nor is it beyond the sea … But the word is very near you, in your mouth and in your heart, that you may observe it” (30:11–14). The function is assurance that God’s revealed word is accessible.
  • Book usage. Deuteronomy is live: 3:14 (add/take away), 5:4–5 (vows), 7:15 (prolong). This use of the covenant’s closing appeal is the fourth.
  • OT-to-OT. Job 28:12, 20 — “Where can wisdom be found (תִּמָּצֵא)?” (חָכְמָה, “wisdom”, and מָצָא, “to find”, verified) — a Writings neighbour on wisdom’s inaccessibility. Prov 20:5 (“deep waters”). And Rom 10:6–8 reads Deut 30:12–14 of Christ.
  • What it adds. Wisdom sought by the self is far (7:23). The commandment given by God is near (Deut 30:11) — and the epilogue’s last word is “keep his commandments” (12:13). The contrast anticipates the frame’s resolution. [I] Moderate–high.

1 Kgs 8:46 (// 2 Chr 6:36) → Eccl 7:20 (moderate–high — the overview’s link, triad added)

  • Source context. Solomon’s temple-dedication prayer: “When they sin against You (for there is no man who does not sin) and You are angry with them and deliver them to an enemy … if they take thought … and repent” (1 Kgs 8:46–50). The verse is a parenthetical universal premise, set inside a petition for forgiveness in exile.
  • Book usage. The Solomon cluster (1:16; 2:4–10; 6:2).
  • OT-to-OT. Ps 143:2, “in Your sight no man living is righteous (יִצְדַּק)” (verified). Rom 3:10 (above) carries the Writings’ verdict into the gospel.
  • What it adds. The universal sinfulness Qohelet observes is the premise of Solomon’s prayer for forgiveness. Read canonically, 7:20 points not to despair but to the need for atonement. [I]

Job 33:23–24; Job 9:3 → Eccl 7:28–29 (“one in a thousand”) (moderate)

  • Source context.
    • Job 9:3: Job cannot answer God “once in a thousand” (אַחַת מִנִּי־אָלֶף).
    • Job 33:23–24: Elihu speaks of “an angel as mediator, one out of a thousand (אֶחָד מִנִּי־אָלֶף), to remind a man what is right for him (יָשְׁרוֹ)”. God then says: “Deliver him from going down to the pit; I have found (מָצָאתִי) a ransom (כֹּפֶר).”
  • Evidence. In Job 33:23–24, “one” (אֶחָד), “thousand” (אֶלֶף), “uprightness” (יֹשֶׁר) and “I have found” (מָצָא) are verified. Eccl 7:28–29 has “one” (אֶחָד), “thousand” (אֶלֶף), “I have found” (מָצָאתִי) and the cognate יָשָׁר (“upright”).
  • Book usage. Job is live (5:15; 6:3; 7:14).
  • What it adds. The “one in a thousand” Qohelet can find (7:28) sits beside Elihu’s “one in a thousand” mediator who restores uprightness and for whom God finds a ransom. [I] Moderate: four shared lemmas is a strong cluster; the idiom “one in a thousand” is shared by three texts. Cross-reported to Christological Reading. Route to a claim audit.

Gen 1:26–27 (and Gen 3) → Eccl 7:29 (moderate — per the overview)

  • Confirmed. עָשָׂה (“to make”) and אָדָם (“man”) are verified in Gen 1:26 and Eccl 7:29.
  • What it adds. “They sought many devices” is a compressed fall. The plural “they” (הֵמָּה) after the singular הָאָדָם (“man”) reads אָדָם as humanity. [T]

12 / 15. Genre and Who Am I?

7:16–17 is advice set against over-scrupulous religion and against self-deceived moral striving, not a licence for moderate wickedness (see Difficulties). The reader is the one who “fears God” (7:18) and, with all humanity, the sinner of 7:20. Christ is the only exception to 7:20 (Heb 4:15; 2 Cor 5:21). Contrast. High. [I]

Christological Reading (unit)

  • Contrast. “No one righteous” (7:20) → the Righteous One (Acts 3:14) who became sin for us (2 Cor 5:21). Tests: category ✓ (righteousness); NT precedent ✓ (Rom 3:10–26, which moves from “none righteous” to “the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ”); escalation ✓; authorial pattern ✗ (the text is universal, not predictive). Contrast/trajectory, high.
  • Trajectory. Wisdom far off (7:23) → Christ, the wisdom of God come near (1 Cor 1:24, 30), and the word near you (Rom 10:6–8 on Deut 30). Moderate–high.
  • Uncertain. The “one in a thousand” and the mediator of Job 33:23. Do not preach it as a prophecy.

16. So What? (brief)

  • Worldview. Moral anomaly is real; universal sin is real; wisdom is not within grasp.
  • Behaviour. Fear God rather than chase a self-made righteousness (7:16–18). Hold your tongue about others’ words (7:21–22).
  • Candidate FCF. Frustrated moral striving, and the discovery that “I too have cursed others” (7:22). [T]

Passage-specific difficulties

  • 7:16 “do not be excessively righteous”. Doctrinal and weaponised.
    • Rhetorical function. Advice following 7:15’s anomaly.
    • Options.
      • Against self-righteousness or pretended righteousness.
      • Against a merit-seeking that expects righteousness to buy long life.
      • A cynical middle way.
    • Honest exegesis. The paired “do not be too wise” and the resolution “fear God” (7:18) favour the first two. Moderate.
    • Handling. Address it explicitly; it is commonly misused to excuse half-heartedness.
  • 7:26–28 the woman. Ethical and pastoral — the overview’s Trap 5.
    • Rhetorical function. Inside the find/not-find cluster, climaxing at 7:29’s universal “they”.
    • Options.
      • A specific kind of woman (the Proverbs adulteress) rather than women in general.
      • Qohelet’s personal (and perhaps criticised) finding, which the frame marks by “says Qohelet” at 7:27.
      • Folly personified as a woman, as in Prov 9:13–18.
    • Honest exegesis. 7:26’s woman is defined by a relative clause (“whose heart is snares”) — a type, not a sex. 7:28’s “not found a woman” stands beside “one man in a thousand” (1 in 1,000 is hardly praise of men). 7:29 then levels all. Moderate.
    • Handling. Don’t skip it. Name the difficulty, and read it through 7:29 and 9:9.

Unit 9 — 8:1–17 The King, Delayed Justice, and the Fear of God

8:12–13 … כִּי גַּם־יוֹדֵעַ אָנִי אֲשֶׁר יִהְיֶה־טּוֹב לְיִרְאֵי הָאֱלֹהִים אֲשֶׁר יִירְאוּ מִלְּפָנָיו׃ וְטוֹב לֹא־יִהְיֶה לָרָשָׁע וְלֹא־יַאֲרִיךְ יָמִים כַּצֵּל אֲשֶׁר אֵינֶנּוּ יָרֵא מִלִּפְנֵי אֱלֹהִים … still I know that it will be well for those who fear God, who fear Him openly. But it will not be well for the evil man and he will not lengthen his days like a shadow, because he does not fear God. (NASB95)

Headline Findings

  • 8:12–13 is Qohelet’s own confession, in the first person, that it will be well with those who fear God — in Deuteronomy’s terms. [T]
    • יוֹדֵעַ אָנִי (“I know”) is the confession.
    • The fear lemmas occur 3 times: the verb twice at 8:12, the adjective at 8:13.
    • וְלֹא־יַאֲרִיךְ יָמִים (“he will not prolong days”, 8:13) is the Deuteronomic phrase (Deut 4:40; 11:9; 17:20; 32:47).
    • The confession is set between two observations of the opposite (8:10–12a, 14).
    Surfaced by: Linking Words, Tool 11, Repetition. High confidence.
  • The king is spoken of in the language the Psalms and Job use of God. [T]
    • כָּל־אֲשֶׁר יַחְפֹּץ יַעֲשֶׂה (“whatever he pleases he does”, 8:3) matches כֹּל אֲשֶׁר־חָפֵץ עָשָׂה (“whatever he pleases he does”) of God at Ps 115:3 and 135:6. These are the only three occurrences of the pattern (phrase search, WLC).
    • מִי יֹאמַר־לוֹ מַה־תַּעֲשֶׂה (“who will say to him, ‘What are you doing?’”, 8:4) matches Job 9:12 of God. The lemmas אָמַר (“say”), מָה (“what”) and עָשָׂה (“do”) are verified in both. It also matches Isa 45:9.
    Surfaced by: Tool 11. Moderate–high.
  • The unit ends with the book’s strongest statement of human ignorance. [T] מָצָא (“to find”) occurs 3 times in 8:17: “man cannot find out the work … even the wise man who says ‘I know’ cannot find it.” This undercuts even the “I know” of 8:12. High.

2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

  • Preceding movement. Unit 8 found only that man sought “many devices” (7:29). The righteous perish and the wicked live long (7:15).
  • Necessity answer. A world of human devices is a world of rulers and delayed justice. The book now places the God-fearer under a king’s power (8:2–9) and before unpunished wickedness (8:10–11). It must then say whether fear of God still holds (8:12–13). The unit exists here to affirm the fear of God against the evidence of 7:15 — and then to deny that anyone can “find out” why (8:16–17). [I]
  • Implication. The confession of 8:12–13 is placed between two anomalies. That placement is the book’s argument: faith affirmed against sight. [I]

4. Linking Words

  • 8:11 אֲשֶׁר … עַל־כֵּן (“because … therefore”). Delayed sentence therefore hearts filled to do evil. [T]
  • 8:12 כִּי גַּם (“although / yet also”). The concessive pivot: though a sinner does evil a hundred times and lives long — yet I know. [T]
  • 8:13 אֲשֶׁר (“because”). He does not fear God. [T]

7. Vocabulary

  • פֵּשֶׁר (“interpretation”, 8:1). A hapax in Hebrew. The Aramaic פְּשַׁר occurs 31 times, all in Daniel (lemma, WLC). [T] It is a canonical neighbour: Daniel is the sage who gives the king the פְּשַׁר. Moderate as vocabulary; uncertain as allusion.
  • The root שלט (“to rule”). שִׁלְטוֹן (“authority”) at 8:4, 8; שַׁלִּיט (“ruler”) at 8:8 (also 7:19; 10:5); שָׁלַט (“to have power”) at 8:9 (lemmas, WLC). [T]
    • The movement. The king’s word is שִׁלְטוֹן (“authority”, 8:4) — but no one has שִׁלְטוֹן over the day of death (8:8).
    • What it shows. Royal power is real, and bounded.
  • שְׁבוּעָה (“oath”). 8:2 (“the oath of God”); 9:2 (lemma, WLC).
  • Pentateuchal and cultic words. עֵת וּמִשְׁפָּט (“time and judgement”, 8:5, 6); מְקוֹם קָדוֹשׁ (“holy place”, 8:10 — the only occurrence of קָדוֹשׁ in the book).

8. Translations

  • 8:10 — a category 2 variant, and the pulpit texts divide. [T] for the readings.
    • The WLC reads וְיִשְׁתַּכְּחוּ (“and they were forgotten”).
    • BHS app. 8:10 ᵈ records “nonn Mss וישתבּחו” (“and they were praised”). The Greek has καὶ ἐπῃνέθησαν (“and they were praised”); Aquila and Theodotion have “boasted”.
    • The difference is one letter (כ/ב), graphically similar.
    • English versions. NASB95 “they are soon forgotten” follows the WLC. ESV “were praised” and NIV84 “receive praise” (with the MT in the margin) follow the variant.

      • “Praised” gives the sharper anomaly: the wicked honoured in the city where they did evil. It also makes שָׁבַח (“to praise”) occur a third time, tying 8:10 to 4:2 and 8:15.
      • “Forgotten” fits 1:11; 2:16; 9:5.
      • Both are coherent.
    • Position: genuinely open. Moderate either way.
    • Pulpit note (for an ESV engagement, his own church): the congregation will hear “praised”; a reader with the NASB95 will see “forgotten”.

  • 8:1b. The Greek reads μισηθήσεται (“will be hated”) for יְשֻׁנֶּא (“is changed”, app. 8:1 ᶜ). No finding depends on it.
  • Pulpit divergence note. No pulpit text declared (see 8:10 above for the ESV and NIV84).

11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

Deuteronomy’s “prolong days” → Eccl 7:15; 8:12–13 (high confidence on the wording)

  • Source context. Deut 4:40: “keep His statutes … that it may go well (יִיטַב) with you and with your children after you, and that you may prolong your days (תַּאֲרִיךְ יָמִים)”. The phrase recurs through the covenant speeches (11:9; 17:20, of the king; 32:47). Its function is the covenant blessing on obedience.
  • Book usage. Deuteronomy is live (3:14; 5:4–5; 7:23). 7:15 observed the opposite.
  • OT-to-OT. Prov 3:2, 16 (wisdom brings “length of days”) (skeletal phrase search, WLC). Prov 28:16. Wisdom had already taken up the Deuteronomic promise.
  • What it adds. Qohelet sees the wicked “prolong” (7:15; 8:12). He confesses nevertheless that the wicked will “not prolong days” and that “it will be well (טוֹב) with those who fear God”. Deuteronomy’s promise is held against the evidence, not dropped. [I] High. This also bears on the overview’s 1 Cor 15:58 link: Paul’s “not in vain” belongs in the same believing against sight category.

Ps 115:3; 135:6 → Eccl 8:3 (moderate–high)

  • Source context. Ps 115:3: “our God is in the heavens; He does whatever He pleases” — against idols that cannot act. Ps 135:6 is the same, of creation.
  • Evidence. חָפֵץ (“to please”) and עָשָׂה (“to do”) are verified in both psalms and at Eccl 8:3.
  • Book usage. Psalms is not yet live (unit 4 used Ps 104:29 as an OT-to-OT partner).
  • OT-to-OT. Job 9:12 (below). Isa 46:10.
  • What it adds. Divine language is applied to a human king. Read with 8:8 (no power over death), it shows royal power that looks divine and is not. [I]

Job 9:12 (and Isa 45:9) → Eccl 8:4 (moderate–high)

  • Source context. Job 9:12: “Were He to snatch away, who could restrain Him? Who could say to Him, ‘What are You doing?’” This is God’s unanswerable sovereignty, in Job’s complaint. Isa 45:9 is the potter and the clay.
  • Evidence. The phrase מַה־תַּעֲשֶׂה (“what are you doing?”) is verified at Job 9:12; Isa 45:9; Eccl 8:4, along with its introductory “who will say”.
  • What it adds. The same point: the king is treated as unanswerable, in Job’s words about God.

Prov 19:16 → Eccl 8:5 (moderate–high)

  • Source context. “He who keeps the commandment (שֹׁמֵר מִצְוָה) keeps his soul.”
  • Evidence. The phrase occurs only at Prov 19:16 and Eccl 8:5 (plene phrase search, WLC).
  • What it adds. At 8:5 the מִצְוָה (“command”) is the king’s (NASB95 “royal command”). Proverbs’ phrase applied to the court prepares for 12:13, where the commandments are God’s. [I]

Num 6:25 → Eccl 8:1 (“makes his face shine”) (uncertain)

Move 1 only. The priestly blessing: “the LORD make His face shine (יָאֵר) on you.” Wisdom makes a man’s face shine. אוֹר (“to shine”, hiphil) and פָּנִים (“face”) are verified in both. The pair is common enough that the allusion is not established.

    • 8:13 “like a shadow” ← 6:12. [T]

16. So What? (brief)

  • Worldview. Fear of God is well-founded even when justice is delayed (8:12–13).
  • Behaviour. Respect lawful authority without joining evil (8:2–3). Don’t let delayed judgement embolden sin (8:11).
  • Motive. God’s judgement (3:17; 8:13).
  • Candidate FCF. The erosion of faith when evil goes unpunished. [T]

Passage-specific difficulties

  • 8:2–5 obedience to kings. Ethical and pastoral. It is counsel under an absolute monarchy, bounded by “do not join in an evil thing” (8:3) and “the oath of God” (8:2). Don’t apply it as unconditional civil obedience (Acts 5:29).
  • 8:10 variant. See Translations. Tell the congregation if the sermon turns on “praised”.

Unit 10 — 9:1–12 ⭐ One Fate — So Go, Eat Your Bread with Joy

9:1 … אֲשֶׁר הַצַּדִּיקִים וְהַחֲכָמִים וַעֲבָדֵיהֶם בְּיַד הָאֱלֹהִים … … righteous men, wise men, and their deeds are in the hand of God …

9:7 לֵךְ אֱכֹל בְּשִׂמְחָה לַחְמֶךָ וּשֲׁתֵה בְלֶב־טוֹב יֵינֶךָ כִּי כְבָר רָצָה הָאֱלֹהִים אֶת־מַעֲשֶׂיךָ Go then, eat your bread in happiness and drink your wine with a cheerful heart; for God has already approved your works. (NASB95)

Headline Findings

  • The carpe diem of 9:7–10 is grounded in God’s acceptance, and opened by God’s hand. [T]
    • The unit opens: the righteous and the wise are בְּיַד הָאֱלֹהִים (“in the hand of God”, 9:1) — the echo of 2:24’s מִיַּד הָאֱלֹהִים (“from the hand of God”).
    • The command is grounded in כְבָר רָצָה הָאֱלֹהִים (“God has already accepted”, 9:7). רָצָה (“to accept”) is used 11 times in Leviticus for the acceptance of offerings (e.g. Lev 1:4); its only occurrence in this book is here (lemma, WLC).
    Surfaced by: Linking Words, Vocabulary, Move 4. High confidence.
  • Death erases the very distinctions the cult maintains. [T] “One fate … for the clean and for the unclean; for the one who sacrifices and the one who does not” (9:2). טָהוֹר (“clean”) and טָמֵא (“unclean”) occur only here in the book, and are the priestly pair of Lev 10:10 (verified). זָבַח (“to sacrifice”) occurs twice in 9:2. Taxonomy activation passes all three tests. Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Tool 11. Moderate–high.
  • “Whatever your hand finds to do, do it” (9:10) is the phrase of Samuel’s commission to Saul (1 Sam 10:7), and the unit ends by snaring the reader in time (9:12). [T] The phrase אֲשֶׁר תִּמְצָא יָדְךָ (“whatever your hand finds”) is shared with Judg 9:33; 1 Sam 10:7; 25:8 (phrase search, WLC). Moderate.

2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

  • Preceding movement. Unit 9 ended with man unable to “find out” God’s work (8:17), after confessing that it will be well for those who fear God (8:12).
  • Necessity answer. If no one can find out God’s work, how should the God-fearer live? The book answers with its fullest command to enjoy (9:7–10). It sets that command between the one fate (9:2–6) and the snare of time (9:11–12). The unit exists here to turn the ignorance of 8:17 into action, not paralysis. [I]
  • Implication. 9:7–10 is the practical conclusion to 8:16–9:1. It is not an escape from them. [T]/[I]

8. Translations

  • 9:1–2 — a category 2 variant. [T] for the readings.
    • The WLC divides the verses …הַכֹּל לִפְנֵיהֶם׃ הַכֹּל כַּאֲשֶׁר לַכֹּל (“all is before them. All is as to all”).
    • BHS app. 9:2 ᵃ⁻ᵃ proposes הָבֶל (“vanity”) “cf 𝔊σ´𝔙”. Swete 9:1 ends τὰ πάντα πρὸ προσώπου αὐτῶν, ματαιότης ἐν τοῖς πᾶσιν (“all things are before them; vanity in all”).
    • Weighing. הַכֹּל (“all”) and הֶבֶל (“vanity”) are graphically close. The MT’s repetition of הַכֹּל could have arisen from הֶבֶל, or the Greek’s הֶבֶל from הַכֹּל.
    • Position: open. Moderate. If the variant stood, the book’s הֶבֶל count would rise by one. The count of 38 is a WLC count and stands as stated.
  • 9:2 “for the good and the evil”. BHS app. 9:2 ᵇ: the Greek inserts καὶ τῷ κακῷ (“and for the evil”; Swete verified). It completes the pair. A category 2 minor variant: it looks like a correction by the Greek to fill a gap. Moderate for the MT.
  • 9:4, ketiv/qere. יבחר (“is chosen”) / יְחֻבַּר (“is joined”). NASB95 “whoever is joined” follows the qere.
  • 9:9 הֶבֶל ×2. BHS marks the second clause “all the days of your vanity” as a possible addition, absent from several manuscripts and the Syriac (app. 9:9 ᵃ⁻ᵃ). The overview’s count of 38 includes both occurrences.
  • 9:9 הֶבְלֶךָ (“your vanity”). NASB95 “your fleeting life”; ESV “your vain life”; NIV84 “this meaningless life”. Pulpit note (NIV84): “meaningless” is at its most damaging here, beside “the wife whom you love”.
  • Pulpit divergence note. No pulpit text declared (see 9:9 above for the NIV84).

11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

Lev 10:10 → Eccl 9:2 (clean / unclean) (moderate–high)

  • Source context. After Nadab and Abihu, the priests are charged “to make a distinction between the holy and the profane, and between the unclean (הַטָּמֵא) and the clean (הַטָּהוֹר)”. Verified. The function is the priesthood’s core task of discrimination.
  • Book usage. The cultic register of 5:1–7 (sacrifice, vow, שְׁגָגָה, “error”).
  • OT-to-OT. Ezek 22:26; 44:23 — the prophets indict priests for failing to distinguish. Not verified in this run.
  • What it adds. At death no priest’s distinction holds. This is the one fate at its sharpest, because it levels the categories God himself instituted. [I]

1 Sam 10:7 → Eccl 9:10 (moderate)

  • Source context. Samuel to the newly anointed Saul: “when these signs come to you, do for yourself what the occasion requires (אֲשֶׁר תִּמְצָא יָדֶךָ), for God is with you.”
  • Evidence. The phrase is verified in four places (Judg 9:33; 1 Sam 10:7; 25:8; Eccl 9:10). It is an idiom.
  • What it adds. Where Samuel’s charge rests on “God is with you”, Qohelet’s rests on “there is no work in Sheol”. Moderate: idiomatic, so the allusion is not established. Contrast noted.

Prov 5:18 → Eccl 9:9 (moderate)

  • Source context. “Let your fountain be blessed, and rejoice (שְׂמַח) in the wife (מֵאֵשֶׁת) of your youth.”
  • Evidence. שָׂמַח (“to rejoice”) and אִשָּׁה (“wife”) are verified in Prov 5:18. Eccl 9:9 has אִשָּׁה with רְאֵה חַיִּים (“enjoy life”).
  • What it adds. The strange woman of Prov 5, recalled at Eccl 7:26, stands against the wife to be enjoyed: Prov 5:18 matches Eccl 9:9. The book repeats Proverbs’ own contrast between the two women. Moderate.

Ps 104:15 → Eccl 9:7–8 (and 10:19) (moderate)

  • Source context. God gives “wine which makes man’s heart glad, so that he may make his face glisten with oil, and food (לֶחֶם) which sustains man’s heart.”
  • Evidence. יַיִן (“wine”), שָׂמַח (“to gladden”), לֶחֶם (“bread”) and שֶׁמֶן (“oil”) are verified in Ps 104:15. Eccl 9:7–8 has bread, wine and oil; Eccl 10:19 has bread, wine and “makes glad”.
  • What it adds. Ecclesiastes’ ordinary goods are Ps 104’s creation gifts. Ps 104:29 (death) was already partnered at 3:20. Ps 104 is therefore a live source (units 4, 10, 11), which the overview did not list.

Near Eastern parallel — Gilgamesh (Siduri) [S] (recalled; not checked)

The tavern-keeper’s advice to Gilgamesh closely matches 9:7–9: fill your belly, clean clothes, wash, delight in wife and child. This is background, not canon. Its use is to show that 9:7–10 speaks a common human wisdom, which Qohelet grounds in God’s acceptance (9:7b) and God’s giving (9:9). Verify in a standard edition before citing.

Amos 3:5; Hab 1:14–15 → Eccl 9:12 (birds and fish snared) (moderate)

Christological Reading (unit)

  • Trajectory. “God has already accepted your works” (9:7). Read canonically, acceptance (רָצָה) is what the sacrifice secures (Lev 1:4). Joy is the fruit of acceptance, not the means to it. The New Testament’s “accepted in the Beloved” (Eph 1:6, ἐχαρίτωσεν ἡμᾶς ἐν τῷ ἠγαπημένῳ, “he has graced us in the Beloved” — wording verified, SBLGNT) sits on that line. Moderate. Do not claim Paul alludes to Ecclesiastes.
  • Contrast. “The dead know nothing … no work in Sheol” (9:5, 10) → the risen Christ who has the keys of death and Hades (Rev 1:18). High, as contrast.
  • Moralism check. “Enjoy yourself” is the temptation here. The ground is God’s acceptance (9:7) and gift (9:9). The limit is judgement (11:9).

16. So What? (brief)

  • Worldview. Life is a gift held in God’s hand; death levels; time surprises.
  • Behaviour.
    • Start: eating, drinking and loving with joy (9:7–9); working wholeheartedly (9:10).
    • Stop: postponing joy.
  • Motive. “God has already accepted your works.”
  • Four audiences.
    • The anxious: see 9:1.
    • The married: see 9:9.
    • The grieving: see 9:4–6; handle carefully.
    • The unbeliever: see 9:12.
  • Candidate FCF. Paralysed by uncertainty, or grasping by fear of death. [T]

Passage-specific difficulties

  • 9:5, 10 — the dead “know nothing”; no work in Sheol. Apologetic and doctrinal.
    • Rhetorical function. Motive for present action.
    • Options.
      • A phenomenological description (“under the sun”).
      • A denial of any afterlife, in tension with 12:7 and 12:14.
    • Honest exegesis. The text describes the dead’s relation to “all that is done under the sun” (9:6). It is not a full doctrine of the intermediate state. Moderate.
    • Handling. Address briefly, with 12:7, 14 and the New Testament’s fuller light.
  • 9:7–10 at weddings and funerals. Pastoral. Read the ground (9:7b) aloud with the command.

Unit 11 — 9:13–10:20 Wisdom’s Small Victories

9:15–16 וּמָצָא בָהּ אִישׁ מִסְכֵּן חָכָם וּמִלַּט־הוּא אֶת־הָעִיר בְּחָכְמָתוֹ וְאָדָם לֹא זָכַר אֶת־הָאִישׁ הַמִּסְכֵּן הַהוּא׃ וְאָמַרְתִּי אָנִי טוֹבָה חָכְמָה מִגְּבוּרָה … But there was found in it a poor wise man and he delivered the city by his wisdom. Yet no one remembered that poor man. So I said, “Wisdom is better than strength.” …

10:19 לִשְׂחוֹק עֹשִׂים לֶחֶם וְיַיִן יְשַׂמַּח חַיִּים וְהַכֶּסֶף יַעֲנֶה אֶת־הַכֹּל Men prepare a meal for enjoyment, and wine makes life merry, and money is the answer to everything. (NASB95)

Headline Findings

  • The example story (9:13–16) sets the pattern for the proverb cluster that follows: wisdom delivers, and folly outweighs it. [T]
    • The poor wise man saves the city and is forgotten (9:15, לֹא זָכַר, “no one remembered”).
    • Then: “one sinner destroys much good” (9:18) and “a little folly is weightier than wisdom” (10:1).
    Surfaced by: Structure, Linking Words, Move 4. High confidence.
  • Chapter 10 is Proverbs-shaped, and draws on Proverbs verbally. [T] Each pair below is lemma-verified in both texts. Only the last two are also exclusive to Proverbs and Ecclesiastes:
    • the slave and the prince (Prov 19:10 → Eccl 10:7);
    • the pit-digger (Prov 26:27 → Eccl 10:8);
    • מַרְפֵּא (“composure, calm”, Prov 14:30; 15:4 → Eccl 10:4);
    • עַצְלוּת (“laziness”). It occurs only twice in the Hebrew Bible: Prov 19:15 and, in the dual עֲצַלְתַּיִם, Eccl 10:18;
    • “the words of the wise”. The phrase occurs only at Prov 1:6; 22:17; Eccl 9:17; 12:11.
    Surfaced by: Tool 11, Vocabulary. High confidence on the wording.
  • יִתְרוֹן (“advantage”) makes its last two appearances here. Wisdom has an advantage (10:10), but the charmer bitten before charming has none (10:11). [T] The book’s question-word ends on the edge of relative gain. Surfaced by: Repetition, Move 4. High.

2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

  • Preceding movement. Unit 10 established that “bread is not to the wise” (9:11), and that time and chance fall on all.
  • Necessity answer. If wisdom does not guarantee success, does it matter at all? The unit answers with a case (9:13–16) and a cluster of sayings (10:1–20): wisdom does have an advantage, in small, fragile, easily-undone victories. The unit exists here to stop the one fate (unit 10) collapsing into “wisdom is worthless”. [I]
  • Implication. Qohelet is still a wisdom teacher (12:9). Chapter 10 is his wisdom in action. [T]/[I]

3. Structure

Verses Section Function
9:13–16 The poor wise man Delivers the city; forgotten; “wisdom better than strength” — yet despised
9:17–10:1 A little folly Quiet words; one sinner; dead flies
10:2–7 Folly in high places Right and left; the ruler’s temper; the “error” from the ruler; slaves on horses

Device. A proverb anthology held together by catchwords: כְּסִיל / סָכָל (“fool”), שַׁלִּיט / מֶלֶךְ (“ruler” / “king”), דְּבָרִים (“words”), יִתְרוֹן (“advantage”). [T]

5. Parallels

  • 10:2, antithetic. “The wise man’s heart to his right / the fool’s to his left.” [T]
  • 10:8–9, synonymous quartet. Dig / fall; break / bitten; quarry / hurt; split / endangered. [T]
  • 10:16–17, antithetic. אִי־לָךְ אֶרֶץ (“woe to you, O land”) against אַשְׁרֵיךְ אֶרֶץ (“blessed are you, O land”). [T]

7. Vocabulary

  • נָחָשׁ / לָחַשׁ (10:11). “If the nāḥāš (‘serpent’) bites before lāḥaš (‘charming’).” The sound play is the point. [T]
  • יַעֲנֶה (“answers”, 10:19). The OSHB lemma is ענה (“to answer”) — not the ענה (“to be busy”) of 1:13; 3:10; 5:20 (Heb 5:19). [T]
    • Sound, not lemma. yaʿăneh (“money answers everything”, 10:19) set against maʿăneh (“God keeps him busy with gladness”, 5:20).
    • What it suggests. The word for what God does with the heart is heard again in what money is said to do. Whether 10:19 is irony is [I]. Moderate.
  • כָּשֵׁר (“to succeed”). 10:10 (the hiphil infinitive, “to give success”); 11:6 (“which will succeed”). 3 occurrences in the Hebrew Bible (lemma, WLC). The cognate כִּשְׁרוֹן (“skill”) is at 2:21; 4:4; 5:11 (Heb 5:10). [T]
  • שְׁגָגָה (“error”, 10:5). See unit 5. A ruler’s “error” is folly placed on high (10:6). [T]
  • מַדָּע (“knowledge, thought”, 10:20). “Even in your מַדָּע (‘thought’) do not curse a king.” 6 occurrences in the Hebrew Bible: 2 Chr 1:10, 11, 12; Dan 1:4, 17; Eccl 10:20 (lemma, WLC). A Solomon word (sweep-level Headline Finding 2). BHS proposes בְּמַצָּעֲךָ (“on your bed”, app. 10:20 ᵃ), a conjecture. [T]
  • נַעַר (“youth, lad”, 10:16). “Woe to you, O land, whose king is a נַעַר.” See Tool 11 (1 Kgs 3:7).

  • Pulpit divergence note. N/A — no pulpit text declared.

16. So What? (brief)

  • Worldview. Wisdom is worth having, though it is fragile and often unrewarded.
  • Behaviour.
    • Keep calm before authority (10:4).
    • Sharpen the axe — prepare (10:10).
    • Guard speech even in private (10:20).
  • Christ. The poor wise man who delivered the city and was not remembered (9:15). A contrast and trajectory with the one “who, though rich, became poor” (2 Cor 8:9), who saves and is despised (Isa 53:3). Moderate. The story is not a prophecy; do not allegorise the “great king” or the “small city”.
  • Candidate FCF. Doing right and being overlooked. [T]

Passage-specific difficulties

  • 10:19 “money is the answer to everything”. Weaponised. It is often quoted as endorsement. Rhetorical function: the fools’ feast (10:16–19) — probably the self-justifying maxim of the morning-feasting princes. [I], moderate. Handling: read it in context.
  • 9:16 “wisdom is despised”. Pastoral. For the unrecognised faithful, the book does not promise recognition; the judgement of 12:14 is the answer.

Unit 12 — 11:1–10 Sow in the Morning; Rejoice, Young Man

11:5 כַּאֲשֶׁר אֵינְךָ יוֹדֵעַ מַה־דֶּרֶךְ הָרוּחַ כַּעֲצָמִים בְּבֶטֶן הַמְּלֵאָה כָּכָה לֹא תֵדַע אֶת־מַעֲשֵׂה הָאֱלֹהִים אֲשֶׁר יַעֲשֶׂה אֶת־הַכֹּל Just as you do not know the path of the wind and how bones are formed in the womb of the pregnant woman, so you do not know the activity of God who makes all things.

11:9 שְׂמַח בָּחוּר בְּיַלְדוּתֶיךָ … וְהַלֵּךְ בְּדַרְכֵי לִבְּךָ וּבְמַרְאֵי עֵינֶיךָ וְדָע כִּי עַל־כָּל־אֵלֶּה יְבִיאֲךָ הָאֱלֹהִים בַּמִּשְׁפָּט Rejoice, young man, during your childhood … And follow the impulses of your heart and the desires of your eyes. Yet know that God will bring you to judgment for all these things. (NASB95)

Headline Findings

  • “You do not know” four times, then “but know”. [T]
    • The four: 11:2, 5 ×2, 6.
    • The command: וְדָע (“but know”, 11:9) — that God will judge.
    • יָדַע (“to know”) occurs 5 times in 11:1–10 (lemma, WLC).
    Surfaced by: Repetition, Structure, Linking Words. High on the counts; moderate on the design.
  • Two phrases from earlier in the book return here. [T]
    • “The work of God who makes all things” (11:5) repeats 3:11. The pattern אֶת־הַכֹּל עָשָׂה / יַעֲשֶׂה אֶת־הַכֹּל (“He made / makes everything”) occurs only in these two verses (phrase search, WLC).
    • “Do not let your hand rest” (11:6) repeats 7:18 (אַל־תַּנַּח (אֶת־)יָדֶךָ; phrase search, WLC).
    Surfaced by: Move 4. High on the wording.
  • 11:9 permits heart and eyes only under judgement. The wording is Num 15:39’s pair, and the final clause is 12:14’s. [T] Both parts are lemma-verified (see unit 2). The judgement words יָבִא / יְבִיאֲךָ (“will bring”) and מִשְׁפָּט (“judgement”) are verified at 11:9 and 12:14. Surfaced by: Tool 11, Move 4. High on the words; moderate on the Num 15 allusion.

2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

  • Preceding movement. Unit 11 ended in “no one knows what will be … who can tell him?” (10:14), and with prudent speech.
  • Necessity answer. Ignorance of the future could paralyse. The book must now turn it into bold, generous action (11:1–6) and joyful youth (11:7–10). Both are placed under God’s work and God’s judgement. The unit exists here as the practical hinge into the death-poem: act and rejoice before the days of darkness (11:8 → 12:1). [I]
  • Implication. 11:9–12:1 is one exhortation across the chapter break: “rejoice … remove vexation … remember”. [T]

3. Structure

Verses Section Function
11:1–2 Cast and divide Bread on the waters; portion to seven, eight — “you do not know”
11:3–4 Clouds and trees What will be, will be; the watcher never sows
11:5 You do not know the work of God Wind; bones in the womb
11:6 Sow morning and evening “You do not know which will succeed”
11:7–8 Light is sweet Rejoice in many years; remember the dark days
11:9–10 Rejoice, young man Heart and eyes — “but know” judgement; remove vexation

Device. A countdown of ignorance (“you do not know” ×4) resolved by a single command to know (11:9). [T]

5. Parallels

  • 11:4, synonymous. “He who watches the wind will not sow / he who looks at the clouds will not reap.” [T]
  • 11:5, emblematic. “As you do not know the way of the wind / … so you do not know the work of God.” [T]
  • 11:2, numerical (x / x+1). “Seven, and even eight.” The same pattern as Mic 5:5 (Heb 5:4); both שֶׁבַע (“seven”) and שְׁמֹנֶה (“eight”) are verified. The form is common (Prov 30). [T]

7. Vocabulary

  • רוּחַ (11:4–5). “Wind” at 11:4. At 11:5 the NASB95 has “wind” and the ESV “spirit”. The word is deliberately open. [T]/[I]
  • הַמְּלֵאָה (“the pregnant woman”, literally “the full one”, 11:5). The adjective מָלֵא (“full”). Its only other occurrence in the book is 1:7, “the sea is not full” (lemma, WLC). The cosmos is never filled; the womb is. [T] Moderate on the design.
  • יַלְדוּת (“youth, childhood”, 11:9, 10). Cognate with יֶלֶד (“child, youth”, 4:13, 15). The youth of 4:13 was “poor and wise”; the youth here is told to rejoice and be warned. [T]
  • שַׁחֲרוּת (“prime of life”, 11:10). A hapax (lemma, WLC). “Dawn” or “black hair” — contested.

8. Translations

  • 11:5 כַּעֲצָמִים (“like the bones”). BHS: “l c mlt Mss 𝔗 בָּע׳” (“in the bones”). NASB95 “how bones are formed” smooths it. A category 2 minor variant. Moderate: no finding depends on it.
  • 11:5 הָרוּחַ. NASB95 “the path of the wind”; ESV “the way the spirit comes to the bones”. Pulpit note (for an ESV engagement): the congregation will hear “spirit”, which opens a John 3:8 connection the NASB95 closes.
  • 11:9 “and in the sight of your eyes”. BHS app. 11:9 ᵈ: many manuscripts and the versions read the singular וּבְמַרְאֵה (“sight”), as at 6:9. No finding depends on it.
  • Pulpit divergence note. No pulpit text declared (see 11:5 above for the ESV).

16. So What? (brief)

  • Behaviour.
    • Start: generous, diversified, diligent action despite uncertainty (11:1–6); rejoicing in youth and in years (11:8–9).
    • Stop: waiting for perfect conditions (11:4).
  • Motive. God makes everything (11:5), and God will judge (11:9).
  • Four audiences.
    • The young: 11:9.
    • The old: 11:8.
    • The cautious: 11:4.
    • The church: generosity to “seven or eight” (11:2).
  • Candidate FCF. Paralysis by uncertainty, or youthful licence without accountability. [T]

Passage-specific difficulties

  • 11:1 “cast your bread upon the waters”. Interpretive. Charity or trade (sea commerce)? The Hebrew allows both. Do not force either.
  • 11:9 “follow your heart”. Weaponised — often quoted without “but know”. Always read the whole verse.

Unit 13 — 12:1–8 ⭐ Remember Your Creator

12:1 וּזְכֹר אֶת־בּוֹרְאֶיךָ בִּימֵי בְּחוּרֹתֶיךָ עַד אֲשֶׁר לֹא־יָבֹאוּ יְמֵי הָרָעָה … Remember also your Creator in the days of your youth, before the evil days come …

12:7 וְיָשֹׁב הֶעָפָר עַל־הָאָרֶץ כְּשֶׁהָיָה וְהָרוּחַ תָּשׁוּב אֶל־הָאֱלֹהִים אֲשֶׁר נְתָנָהּ then the dust will return to the earth as it was, and the spirit will return to God who gave it. (NASB95)

Headline Findings

  • The poem is built on three “before”s, and ends with Gen 3:19 turned partly outward. [T]
    • עַד אֲשֶׁר לֹא (“before”) opens 12:1, 12:2 and 12:6 — its only occurrences in the book (phrase search, WLC).
    • At 12:7 the dust returns to the earth (Gen 3:19), but “the spirit returns to God who gave it”. Gen 2:7 used נְשָׁמָה (“breath”), not רוּחַ; Ecclesiastes’ word matches Ps 104:29.
    Surfaced by: Structure, Tool 11, Move 4. High confidence.
  • The poem brings back, one by one, what the book has already shown. [T]
    • The sun and light (1:5; 11:7).
    • The clouds and rain (11:3–4).
    • “Stoop” (עות — the crooked of 1:15; 7:13).
    • The singers (2:8).
    • The mourners (3:4).
    • “Return” (שׁוּב, 1:6–7).
    • “Eternal home” (עוֹלָם, 1:4).
    Every term is lemma-verified at both ends. Surfaced by: Move 4, Repetition. High on the words; moderate on the design (synthetic).
  • The cosmic darkening of 12:2 shares the image family of Isa 13:10. In Greek it is the family Jesus uses at Matt 24:29. [T] Sun, moon, stars and “darken” are all verified in Isa 13:10 (Hebrew) and Eccl 12:2. σκοτίζω (“to darken”) with ἥλιος (“sun”) appears in Swete Eccl 12:2, Isa 13:10 LXX and Matt 24:29. Moderate: a shared image, not a demonstrable citation.

2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

  • Preceding movement. 11:7–10 commanded joy in youth, “remembering the days of darkness”, under judgement.
  • Necessity answer. The “days of darkness” (11:8) must now be shown. The command “remember” must reach its true object: not the dark days, but the Creator (12:1). The poem exists here because the book’s argument ends at death. Qohelet’s last word before the frame is the return of dust and spirit (12:7). [I]
  • Implication. 12:1 is the first time the book names God as בּוֹרֵא (“Creator”), and the book’s only use of בָּרָא (“to create”) (lemma, WLC). [T]

3. Structure

Verses Section Function
12:1 Remember — before (1) Your Creator; before the evil days, “no delight”
12:2–5 Before (2) Cosmic darkening; the house in decline; the town in mourning; man to his eternal home
12:6–7 Before (3) Cord, bowl, pitcher, wheel; dust to earth, spirit to God
12:8 Frame “Vanity of vanities … all is vanity”

Device. Three עַד אֲשֶׁר לֹא (“before”) clauses, each with a cluster of images. The images move from cosmos (12:2) to household (12:3–4) to street and nature (12:5) to the well (12:6), and end in dust (12:7). The frame closes the inclusio with 1:2. [T]

5. Parallels

  • 12:6, synonymous quartet. Cord snapped / bowl crushed / pitcher shattered / wheel crushed. [T]
  • 12:7, antithetic direction. Dust to the earth / spirit to God. [T]

7. Vocabulary

  • בּוֹרְאֶיךָ (“your Creator”, 12:1). Written with a yod before the suffix, as a plural form would be. The explanation is disputed [S], recalled.
    • BHS conjecture. בּוֹרְךָ (“your cistern”, app. 12:1 ᵃ).
    • Sound play. It would echo בּוֹר (“cistern”, 12:6): bôrʾeykā / bôr.
    • Proverbs. בּוֹר is the cistern of Prov 5:15, “drink water from your own cistern”, the image for one’s wife (verified).
    • Position: the conjecture has no manuscript support as BHS prints it. Hold the MT, “Creator”. High. The sound play with 12:6 is real in the MT and needs no emendation. Moderate.
  • חֶבֶל הַכֶּסֶף (“the silver cord”, 12:6). ḥebel (“cord”) beside hebel (“vanity”, 12:8). Different first consonants (ח / ה), near-homophones. [T] for the forms. Uncertain as a deliberate pun; noted.
  • אֲרֻבּוֹת (“windows”, 12:3). 9 occurrences in the Hebrew Bible, including the “windows of heaven” of the flood (Gen 7:11; 8:2) (lemma, WLC). Uncertain as an allusion (de-creation).
  • שָׁקֵד (“almond”, 12:5). Jer 1:11–12 puns šāqēd (“almond”) / šōqēd (“watching”). The lemma is verified at Jer 1:11. Uncertain in Ecclesiastes. Not built on.
  • בֵּית עוֹלָמוֹ (“his eternal home”, 12:5). Unique in the Hebrew Bible (phrase search, WLC). [T]
  • הִתְעַוְּתוּ (“stoop”, 12:3). עות (“to bend”). See the sweep-level Headline Finding 1.

8. Translations

  • 12:6, ketiv/qere. ירחק (“is removed”) / יֵרָתֵק (“is snapped”), as BHS prints them. The versions translate a verb of breaking. NASB95 “is broken” follows the sense of the qere. Reading tradition.
  • 12:5 “the caperberry is ineffective”. The Greek has διασκεδασθῇ (“be scattered”) and Aquila “bear fruit” (app. 12:5 ᵈ). NASB95 “caperberry”; ESV “desire fails”. Pulpit note (for an ESV engagement): the congregation will hear an interpretation (“desire”), not the plant.
  • 12:1 “your Creator”. NASB95 “Remember also your Creator”; the ESV the same; NIV84 “Remember your Creator”. The NASB95 and ESV “also” renders the conjunction ו־ (“and”), keeping the link to 11:10. The NIV84 drops it. Minor pulpit note.
  • Pulpit divergence note. No pulpit text declared (see above for the ESV and NIV84).

10. Repetition

  • עַד אֲשֶׁר לֹא (“before”): 3 times.
  • שׁוּב (“to return”): 12:2, 7 ×2.
  • רָעָה (“evil”): 12:1.
  • The house: “the keepers of the house” (12:3); “the doors … the street” (12:4).
  • שׁוּק (“street”): 12:4, 5.
  • The frame’s הֶבֶל (“vanity”): 3 times in 12:8.

11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

Gen 3:19 (and 2:7) → Eccl 12:7 (high confidence — the overview’s link)

  • Source context. See unit 4. Gen 2:7 is the forming of man from עָפָר (“dust”) and the inbreathing of נִשְׁמַת חַיִּים (“the breath of life”). נְשָׁמָה (“breath”) is verified at Gen 2:7; רוּחַ is not in Gen 2:7.
  • Book usage. Second use (after 3:20). This time the return is double.
  • OT-to-OT. Ps 104:29–30. The breath (רוּחַ) taken away and the return to dust (104:29) is followed by “You send forth Your Spirit (רוּחֲךָ), they are created (יִבָּרֵאוּן)” (104:30). בָּרָא (“to create”) and רוּחַ (“spirit”) are both verified at Ps 104:30. Ps 104:29–30 therefore gives exactly the pair Eccl 12:1, 7 uses: the Creator, and the spirit returning to God. Ps 104 is a live source (units 4, 10, 11, 13). Job 34:14–15.
  • What it adds. Qohelet’s “who knows?” about the spirit’s destination (3:21) is answered here with a statement of direction — “to God who gave it”. It is not a full eschatology. [I] Moderate–high.

2 Sam 19:35 (Heb 19:36) → Eccl 12:1–5 (and 2:8) (moderate)

  • Source context. Barzillai, at 80: no discernment of good and bad, no taste (טָעַם), no hearing of שָׁרִים וְשָׁרוֹת (“singing men and women”). All verified.
  • What it adds. The old man of 2 Samuel is the case the poem generalises: “the years in which you will say, ‘I have no delight in them’” (12:1); “all the daughters of song are brought low” (12:4). Moderate.

Isa 13:10 → Eccl 12:2 (moderate)

  • Source context. The oracle against Babylon, on the day of the LORD: “the stars of heaven … will not flash forth their light; the sun will be dark (חָשַׁךְ) when it rises and the moon will not shed its light.” Verified.
  • Book usage. Isaiah was a canonical contrast at 1:9.
  • OT-to-OT and NT. Joel 2:10; 3:4 (Heb) (not verified in this run). Matt 24:29 quotes Isa 13:10 LXX for the end of the age.
  • What it adds. A person’s death is described in the language of the cosmic Day. Every death is a small day of the LORD. [I] Moderate.

Gen 7:11; 8:2 → Eccl 12:3 (אֲרֻבּוֹת, “windows”) (uncertain)

Move 1 only. Noted, not built on.

9. Tone and Feel

Elegiac and tender, grave and beautiful. The images are domestic and slow, the pace a funeral procession. Soundtrack: a solo cello fading. The “before … before … before” is a pulse. This is the text to preach most gently in care-home ministry.

Christological Reading (unit)

  • Trajectory. The Creator to be remembered (12:1) is the Word through whom all was made (John 1:3). The dust that returns (12:7) is the dust the last Adam entered and rose from (1 Cor 15:45–49). Tests: category ✓ (creation; Adam); NT precedent ✓ (1 Cor 15:45–49 Adam typology); escalation ✓; authorial pattern — partial. Trajectory, moderate–high.
  • Contrast. The darkening of sun and moon (12:2) against the city with no need of sun or moon (Rev 21:23). Moderate.
  • Moralism check. The temptation is “remember God while you’re young, before it’s too late”, preached as bare duty. The gospel grounding: the Creator who gave the spirit (12:7) receives it back, and in Christ the one who dies is received (Luke 23:46; Acts 7:59).

16. So What? (brief)

  • Worldview. Ageing and death are real, and under God.
  • Behaviour. Remember the Creator now.
  • Pastoral. For the aged, the poem names losses with dignity. Do not play it for laughs.
  • Candidate FCF. The fear of decline and death. [T]

Passage-specific difficulties

  • 12:2–6 allegory. Interpretive (Trap 6). The options:
    • an anatomical allegory (teeth, eyes, and so on);
    • a household or estate in decline;
    • a cosmic or storm scene;
    • a mixture.
    Honest exegesis: the text mixes registers. The “house” imagery (12:3–4) favours a real household in decline as the vehicle for ageing. Moderate. Handling: present the images; decode sparingly.
  • 12:7 and the intermediate state. Doctrinal. The verse states the direction of the spirit; it does not describe its condition. Don’t overload it.
  • Pastoral. Care-home congregations. Pair it with 2 Cor 4:16–5:1.

Unit 14 — 12:9–14 ⭐ The End of the Matter

  • The frame’s closing command is the body’s own command, word for word, and its final clauses bring in the Torah and Gen 2–3. [T]

  • Four threads that began early close here. [T] (synthetic)

    High on the words; moderate on the design.

2. Context — Positional Necessity Check

  • Preceding movement. The frame has just repeated the thesis, “all is vanity” (12:8), after the death-poem.
  • Necessity answer. A book that ends on “all is vanity” would leave the reader in the body’s verdict. The epilogue exists to tell the reader three things:
    1. How to receive Qohelet: as a sage whose goads come from one Shepherd (12:9–11).
    2. What not to add (12:12).
    3. What the whole comes to: fear, commandments, judgement (12:13–14).
    It is the frame’s reading of the body. [I]
  • Implication. The epilogue neither cancels the body nor merely repeats it. It locates it, and does so in words the body itself has already used (5:7; 3:17; 11:9). [T] Trap 7 is confirmed as a live danger in both directions.

4. Linking Words

  • וְיֹתֵר (“and besides”, 12:9, 12). Twice — יוֹתֵר (“more, besides”), the book’s advantage-word in its last two uses (lemma, WLC). The last “advantage” is that Qohelet taught; the last warning is against going “beyond” these. [T]
  • כִּי (“for”, 12:13, 14). “For this is the whole of man … for God will bring every act to judgement.” The command is grounded twice. [T]

8. Translations

  • 12:13b כִּי־זֶה כָּל־הָאָדָם.
    • NASB95: “because this applies to every person”.
    • ESV and NIV84: “for this is the whole duty of man”.
    • The Hebrew has no “duty” and no “applies”: “for this is the whole of man”.
    • The Greek: ὅτι τοῦτο πᾶς ὁ ἄνθρωπος (“for this is every man”).
    • All three English versions interpret. Only the terse form keeps the echo with 7:2 and 6:10.
    • Pulpit note: whichever version is declared, the preacher may need to give the literal wording.
  • 12:9 “prb add (gl)” (BHS). An editorial judgement that 12:9 is a gloss. Not a manuscript reading; do not cite it as one (overview Trap 7).
  • 12:10 וְכָתוּב (“and written”). BHS reads וְכָתַב (“and he wrote”) with a few manuscripts and the versions. NASB95 “to write” follows the sense. No finding depends on it.
  • 12:11 בַּעֲלֵי אֲסֻפּוֹת (“masters of collections”). NASB95 “masters of these collections”; ESV “the collected sayings”. Obscure. BHS proposes מִבַּעֲלֵי (“from the masters”). Uncertain.

11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

Prov 22:17–21 → Eccl 12:10–11 (high confidence)

  • Source context. The heading of the “words of the wise” collection (Prov 22:17–24:22): “Incline your ear and hear the words of the wise (דִּבְרֵי חֲכָמִים) … so that your trust may be in the LORD … to make you know the certainty of the words of truth (אִמְרֵי אֱמֶת), that you may correctly answer (אֲמָרִים אֱמֶת, ‘words of truth’) to him who sent you” (22:17–21). The function is the self-introduction of a wisdom collection, stating its purpose: trust in the LORD, and truthful speech.
  • Book usage. Proverbs is the book’s most frequently used source (units 1, 4, 5, 7, 9, 11). The phrase דִּבְרֵי חֲכָמִים (“words of the wise”) has already appeared at 9:17.
  • OT-to-OT. Prov 1:6 (also דִּבְרֵי חֲכָמִים) is in Proverbs’ own prologue. The Proverbs phrase stands at both the prologue and a collection heading. The epilogue uses the vocabulary Proverbs uses to introduce itself.
  • What it adds. The frame presents Qohelet’s work as one of the “words of the wise” — canonical wisdom alongside Proverbs, not a rival to it. This bears directly on Trap 1: the frame endorses Qohelet as a sage (12:9–10). It then states the conclusion in its own terms (12:13–14). [I] High.

Ezek 34:23; 37:24 → Eccl 12:11 (moderate on the phrase; uncertain on the allusion — per the overview)

  • Source context. God will set over His scattered flock “one shepherd, My servant David” (Ezek 34:23), and “they will all have one shepherd” (37:24), in the restoration promises.
  • Book usage. The Davidic frame: “son of David” (1:1).
  • OT-to-OT. Ps 80:2 (Heb) “Shepherd of Israel”; Gen 48:15; 49:24; Ps 23:1 — God as shepherd. Not verified by lemma in this run.
  • What it adds. Two options, neither settled:
    1. The Shepherd is God, and the words of the wise are God-given — a claim of inspiration.
    2. The Shepherd is a (Davidic) teacher.
    If Ezekiel is in the ear, the two converge on “one shepherd, David”, a son of David giving God’s words. [I] Uncertain. Do not build a sermon on it (overview; unchanged).

Deut 6:2; 8:6 → Eccl 12:13 (high — per the overview)

  • Source context. Deut 6:2: “so that you … might fear the LORD your God, to keep all His statutes and His commandments”; 8:6 the same. The function is the purpose clause of the covenant.
  • Book usage. The fifth Deuteronomy use (3:14; 5:4–5; 7:23; 8:13). The book ends on the Torah’s purpose clause.
  • OT-to-OT. Prov 19:16, “he who keeps the commandment” (see unit 9).
  • What it adds. The God Qohelet calls אֱלֹהִים (“God”) throughout is the covenant LORD whose commandments are to be kept. [I] High.

Gen 2:9, 17; 3:5, 22 → Eccl 12:14 (“good or evil”) (moderate)

  • Source context. The tree of the knowledge of טוֹב וָרָע (“good and evil”).
  • Evidence. The phrase is verified in Gen 2–3 (and Deut 1:39; Jer 42:6). Ecclesiastes has אִם־טוֹב וְאִם־רָע (“whether good or evil”).
  • What it adds. The book ends where Gen 2–3 began. The judgement of “good or evil” belongs to God, which answers the human grasping for it (7:29, “they sought many devices”). [I] Moderate.

New Testament (category 3 and trajectory).

  • Rom 2:16 and 1 Cor 4:5: God judges τὰ κρυπτά (“the hidden things”).
  • The Greek of Eccl 12:14 reads ἐν παντὶ παρεωραμένῳ (“in everything overlooked”), not κρυπτός (“hidden”). The New Testament link is therefore conceptual, not verbal-in-Greek. Moderate.
  • 2 Cor 5:10: “each one may be recompensed … whether good or bad”. The good-or-bad pairing matches 12:14 conceptually.

Christological Reading (unit)

  • Trajectory — the Shepherd. See the overview. Uncertain.
  • Trajectory — judgement. 12:14 → the Son to whom all judgement is given (John 5:22; Acts 17:31). High, as trajectory.
  • Trajectory — “greater than Solomon”. 16. So What? (brief)

  • Worldview. The whole of a human life is fear of God and obedience, under a coming judgement that sees the hidden.
  • Behaviour. Stop endless theorising (12:12); start fearing and obeying (12:13).
  • Motive. The judgement (12:14), and the goodness of God-given words (12:11).
  • Candidate FCF. Intellectual restlessness, and a secret life. [T]

Passage-specific difficulties

  • 12:9–14 as a later “orthodox” correction. Apologetic. That is a composition-critical hypothesis [S]. The text itself presents 12:9–14 as the frame’s commendation of Qohelet, not a correction (12:9–10). Handling: acknowledge it briefly; preach the final form.
  • 12:12 anti-intellectualism. Weaponised. The warning is against going “beyond these”, not against study as such. It is the Deut 4:2 guard applied to words (see unit 3).

Cross-Passage Convergent Findings

What becomes visible only when the fourteen units are read as one sequence. Every finding here is synthetic. Each constituent reference was verified by lemma or by phrase search (WLC). Design claims are held at moderate pending a claim audit.

1. The crooked and the straight: a thread from lament to commendation

  • The lemmas. עות (“to bend”) occurs at 1:15; 7:13; 12:3. תקן (“to straighten, set in order”) occurs at 1:15; 7:13; 12:9 — its only three occurrences in the Hebrew Bible.
  • The movement.
    1. What is crooked cannot be straightened (1:15, unit 2).
    2. Who can straighten what God has bent? (7:13, unit 7).
    3. The mighty men “bend” in old age (12:3, unit 13).
    4. Qohelet “set in order” many proverbs (12:9, unit 14).
  • What it shows. The world’s crookedness is beyond man’s correcting. The one thing the frame says Qohelet did straighten is words.
  • Confidence. High on the words; moderate on the design.

2. Solomon, in both of his canonical portraits

  • Kings. 1:16 (= 1 Kgs 10:7); 2:10 (cf. 1 Kgs 3:11–13); 7:20 (= 1 Kgs 8:46); 9:11 (פֶּגַע, “chance”, = 1 Kgs 5:4 [Heb 5:18]); 12:9 (cf. 1 Kgs 4:32 [Heb 5:12]).

  • Confidence. High on each verbal link; moderate on the cluster as design.

3. God’s gift and man’s occupation

13. Return

  • The chain. The wind and rivers return (1:6–7) → all return to dust (3:20) → the clouds return (12:2) → the dust and the spirit return (12:7). שׁוּב (“to return”) is verified throughout.
  • What it shows. The cosmos’ ceaseless round becomes the body’s final return, and the spirit’s return to its giver.
  • Confidence. High on the words; moderate on the design.

Preaching Pitfalls

Book-wide pitfalls, sharpened by the sweep. Unit-level difficulties are under each unit.

Pitfall: Settling Qohelet’s voice before reading him

  • What it looks like. “Chapters 1–11 are a man without God; wait for chapter 12.” Or: “Qohelet is a cheerful preacher of joy.”
  • Why it’s wrong. Both skip the sweep’s evidence:
    • The body commands the fear of God in the epilogue’s exact words (5:7 → 12:13).
    • Qohelet confesses God’s judgement (3:17; 8:12–13; 11:9).
    • The frame commends him as a sage (12:9–11).
    • Yet the body also reports anomalies the frame never explains away (7:15; 8:14), and a doubt the frame answers only partly (3:21 → 12:7).
  • The corrective. Read each passage in its voice, and let the tension stand. Name the reading tested in each sermon.

Pitfall: Carpe diem without the Giver

  • What it looks like. “Life’s short — enjoy it.”
  • Why it’s wrong. Every enjoyment passage is grounded in God (Cross-Passage 3). The book’s joy is received.
  • The corrective. Always preach the ground clause: 2:24b; 3:13b; 5:19b; 9:7b; 11:9b.

Pitfall: “Meaningless”

  • What it looks like. “The Bible says life is meaningless without God.”
  • Why it’s wrong. הֶבֶל means breath, transience. The book pairs it with goods to be enjoyed (9:9).
  • The corrective. “Fleeting” or “vapour”. Warn an NIV84 congregation explicitly.

Pitfall: Quoting only one half of the anomaly

  • What it looks like. Preaching 8:12–13 (“it will be well with those who fear God”) as a promise of prosperity, without 7:15 and 8:14. Or preaching 7:15 as proof that faith is futile, without 8:12–13.
  • Why it’s wrong. Unit 9’s structure sets the confession between two anomalies. The book holds both.
  • The corrective. Preach faith against sight, as the text does.

Pitfall: Reading Ecclesiastes as anti-Proverbs

  • What it looks like. “Proverbs is naive; Ecclesiastes is realistic.”
  • Why it’s wrong. Ecclesiastes uses Proverbs more than any other source, and the frame presents Qohelet among “the words of the wise” (Cross-Passage 5).
  • The corrective. Present them as partners in one school. Ecclesiastes tests Proverbs’ generalisations against death and against anomaly.

Pitfall: Stating “Solomon wrote this” as a text-observation

  • What it looks like. “Solomon tells us…”
  • Why it’s wrong. The book dresses its king in Solomon’s words and never names him. The identification is an inference.
  • The corrective. “The son of David, king in Jerusalem — the Solomonic king” — and let the reader hear the echoes.
