Claim Audit: Ecclesiastes — the Structural Arc Map

Passages audited: the whole book. The claims tested are the Structural Arc Map in book-overview-ecclesiastes.md (v0.1.0): its seven movements, its seams, and its binding device. Date: 17 September 2026 Purpose: To test the overview’s structural claims, which the sweep (dig-deeper-ecclesiastes-sweep.md) marked as synthetic and provisional, before the Finalise pass. The joins were worked from the Hebrew first; A. G. Wright’s proposal was then checked in Logos rather than recalled. Primary texts: WLC Hebrew with the lemma and morphology index; Swete LXX; BHS apparatus export. Study text: NASB95. Pulpit text: none — no sermon in view.

Warrant tags: [T] text · [I] inference · [S] secondary source.

Method note — Hays adapted for structure. Hays’ criteria test allusions, not seams, so each seam is scored here on six structural criteria instead:

  1. Terminal marker — a refrain or formula closing the unit before the seam.
  2. Opening marker — a formula opening the unit after it.
  3. Voice or address shift.
  4. Content shift.
  5. Scribal marker — a Leningrad petuchah/setumah, as the WLC prints it. This is a reading-tradition feature; its spread across the manuscripts has not been checked.
  6. Secondary support — Logos, checked this run.

Scores are Strong / Possible / Weak / Absent.

Evidence gathered first (text-first, before Logos)

First-person-singular forms (verbs, pronouns, suffixes) ≈124 ≈38

The first half is overwhelmingly report (“I saw … I said in my heart”). The second half is predominantly address (“you … do not … know”). [T] The counts are derived from the OSHB morphology tags and are approximate, because suffixes and verb forms are counted together. The shift is large enough not to depend on the exact figures.

By section, the densest first-person block is 1:12–2:26 (≈3.1 forms a verse). The densest address blocks are 5:1–7 (Heb 4:17–5:6), 11:1–6 and 11:7–10, at ≈3.1, ≈2.0 and ≈3.5 forms a verse.

B. Where the refrains fall

“Striving after wind.” רְעוּת רוּחַ occurs at 1:14; 2:11, 17, 26; 4:4, 6; 6:9. Its variant רַעְיוֹן רוּחַ occurs at 1:17; 4:16. All nine are in 1:1–6:9 (lemma, WLC). [T]

“Find.” מָצָא occurs at 3:11; 7:14, 24, 26, 27 ×2, 28 ×3, 29; 8:17 ×3; 9:10, 15; 11:1; 12:10. Seven of its twelve verses are in 6:10–8:17. [T]

Negated “know.” It occurs at 4:13; 5:1 (Heb 4:17); 6:5; 8:5; 9:1, 5, 12; 10:14, 15; 11:2, 5, 6 (phrase search, WLC). Seven of the twelve are in 9:1–11:6. [T]

“Who knows?” מִי יוֹדֵעַ occurs at 2:19; 3:21; 6:12; 8:1. Two of the four are in the first half. [T]

C. The Leningrad paragraph markers (as the WLC prints them)

There are only four: a petuchah after 1:11, and setumot after 3:1, 3:8 and 9:10. [T] for the witness. Their spread across the manuscripts has not been checked, and they are too sparse to test most seams.

D. Logos — checked this run (Study Assistant; four cited sources)

Wright’s scheme (CBQ 1968), as reported by Murphy (WBC 23A, xxxviii–xxxix) and Rooker (The World and the Word, 542):

  • First half (1:12–6:9).
    • Two introductions, 1:12–15 and 1:16–18, each ending with the vanity phrase plus a proverb.
    • Six units, each closed by the refrain: 2:1–11; 2:12–17; 2:18–26; 3:1–4:6; 4:7–16; 4:17–6:9.
  • Second half (6:10–11:6).
    • Four units marked by “not discover / who can discover”: 7:1–14; 7:15–24; 7:25–29; 8:1–17.
    • Four units marked by “does not know”: 9:1–12; 9:13–10:15; 10:16–11:2; 11:3–6.
  • The close. A poem, 11:7–12:8, then the epilogue, 12:9–14.
  • The midpoint. 222 verses, with the midpoint at 6:9/6:10.

Reception.

  • Positive.
    • Murphy reports that some have adopted the scheme with minor changes, and that others call it “illuminating”.
    • Rooker describes it approvingly.
  • Sceptical.
    • Eaton (TOTC 18:56) doubts that the phrases were meant as precise dividers.
    • Whybray (Ecclesiastes, Sheffield Academic Press 1997, 43) calls the analysis subjectivist, because the formal markers do not match the thematic divisions Wright proposes. Whybray’s example is that treating 2:18–6:9 as centred on “toil” ignores much of those chapters.

Claims Audited

Claim 1: The prologue, 1:1–11 (seam 1:11 / 1:12)

Source: Overview, Structural Arc Map, movement 1 (moderate). Claim: 1:1–11 is a unit: superscription, thesis, question, poem.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Terminal marker Possible “No remembrance” (1:11) closes the poem
Opening marker Strong “I, Qohelet, have been king” (1:12)
Voice / address shift Strong First-person forms: 0 in 1:1–11, then ≈2.3 a verse in 1:12–18
Content shift Strong Cosmos → the king’s experiment
Scribal marker Strong (unchecked spread) Petuchah after 1:11
Secondary support Strong Wright’s first half begins at 1:12

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning: The seam is the best-marked in the book. The nuance: 1:2 is the frame voice (“says Qohelet”), matched by 12:8. So 1:1 is the superscription, and 1:2 is the opening bracket of a frame that closes at 12:8 (see Claim 10). [T]

Book-overview action required: Note in the arc table that 1:2 pairs with 12:8 as a bracket.

Criterion Score Reasoning

Opening marker Strong The times-list heading (3:1)

Claim 3: God’s times and man’s toil, 3:1–6:9, as a single movement

Source: Overview, movement 3. Claim: 3:1–6:9 is one movement.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Terminal marker Strong The refrain at 6:9 (its last occurrence)
Internal seams Strong Refrain closures at 4:6 and 4:16. The opening formula וְשַׁבְתִּי אֲנִי וָאֶרְאֶה (“then I looked again”) at 4:1 and 4:7
Voice / address shift Strong inside the movement 5:1–7 (Heb 4:17–5:6) has zero first-person forms and ≈3.1 address forms a verse — the sharpest internal break in the first half
Content shift Possible Times → injustice → companionship → worship → wealth
Scribal marker Possible (unchecked spread) Setumot after 3:1 and 3:8
Secondary support Strong Wright: three units, 3:1–4:6; 4:7–16; 4:17–6:9

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning: The outer seams hold. Inside, the “movement” is three refrain-closed units (Wright), not one argument. Most of the overview’s internal subdivisions are not where the text’s markers are. The overview breaks after 3:15, 3:22, 4:16 and 5:7 (Eng). Only the break after 4:16 (= Heb 4:16/4:17) coincides with a marker; the other marker-seam, 4:6/4:7, is missed. [T]

Book-overview action required: - Describe movement 3 as “three refrain-closed units: 3:1–4:6; 4:7–16; 4:17–6:9 (Heb)”. - Correct the sweep’s Open Question: if unit 4 (3:16–4:16) is split for preaching, split it at 4:6/4:7, not at 4:1. The refrain closes 4:6, and 4:7 repeats the opening formula of 4:1.

Claim 4: The hinge at 6:9 / 6:10 — two halves

Source: Overview, arc map; option 1 of its structural options (moderate); the sweep, unit 7 (moderate). Claim: The book divides into halves at 6:9/6:10.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Terminal marker Strong The last “striving after wind” (6:9); all nine refrain occurrences precede it
Opening marker Strong מַה־שֶּׁהָיָה (“whatever has been”, 6:10), then the two questions of 6:12, which the second half takes up
Voice / address shift Strong Report dominates the first half; address dominates the second (Evidence A)
Content shift Strong “What profit?” → “what is good, and who can know?”
Numerical Strong 111 + 111 verses in the WLC [T]; Wright makes the same observation [S: Murphy]
Scribal marker Absent —
Secondary support Strong, contested Wright, Murphy and Rooker for; Eaton and Whybray doubt that such markers were meant as dividers

Verdict: Confirmed.

Reasoning: Four independent kinds of evidence converge: the refrain’s distribution, the voice shift, the verse count and the question-and-answer design. The voice-shift evidence is new to this audit, and it is independent of Wright’s verbal markers. [T]

Book-overview action required: - Raise the hinge from moderate to high. - Record the voice-shift evidence. - Replace “Wright … not verified here” with the Logos citations.

Claim 5: The binding device — “the second half is marked by ‘who knows / cannot find out’”

Source: Overview, Structural Arc Map, device paragraph; the sweep’s Headline Finding 4. Claim: The second half’s marker is “who knows / cannot find out”.

Criterion Score Reasoning
“Who knows?” confined to the second half Weak Two of four in the first half (2:19; 3:21)
“Find” in 6:10–8:17 Strong Seven of its twelve verses; unit endings at 7:14, 7:24, 7:29, 8:17
“Not know” in 9:1–11:6 Strong Seven of its twelve verses; unit endings at 9:12, 10:15, 11:2, 11:6
Secondary support Strong Wright names exactly these two markers

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning: “Who knows?” is a book-wide question, not a second-half marker. The second half has two successive markers, each closing its own units: “not find” (6:10–8:17), then “not know” (9:1–11:6). [T] The sweep’s “knowledge countdown” (Cross-Passage 8) stands, but its description should use this two-stage form.

Book-overview action required: Reword the device paragraph: “the first half’s units close with ‘vanity and striving after wind’; the second half’s with ‘not find’ (6:10–8:17) and then ‘not know’ (9:1–11:6)”. Remove “who knows” as a marker.

Claim 6: What is good — and who can find it? 6:10–8:17

Source: Overview, movement 4. Claim: 6:10–8:17 is one movement.

Criterion Score Reasoning

Internal markers Strong “Find” at 7:14, 7:24 and 7:29

Claim 7: The seam at 8:17 / 9:1

Source: Overview, arc map (movements 4 → 5); flagged in the sweep’s reply as soft. Claim: A new movement begins at 9:1.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Terminal marker Strong “Cannot find” ×3 (8:17)
Opening marker Possible 9:1 opens with כִּי (“for”) and כָּל־זֶה (“all this”), which point back
Linking features Strong נָתַתִּי אֶל־לִבִּי (“I laid to heart”, 9:1) echoes נָתַתִּי אֶת־לִבִּי (“I gave my heart”, 8:16); the lemmas are verified
Forward marker Strong אֵין יוֹדֵעַ (“does not know”, 9:1) — the first “not know” of the new series; “in the hand of God”
Secondary support Strong Wright begins the “not know” units at 9:1

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning: The seam is real, and 9:1 is a hinge verse: it looks back to 8:16–17 and opens the “not know” series. [T] Preaching note: 9:1–12 should be introduced from 8:16–17.

Book-overview action required: Mark 9:1 as a hinge verse.

Claim 8: The one fate, and the wisdom that still serves, 9:1–11:6

Source: Overview, movement 5. Claim: 9:1–11:6 is one movement.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Terminal marker Strong אֵינְךָ יוֹדֵעַ (“you do not know”, 11:6)
Internal markers Strong “Not know” at 9:12, 10:15, 11:2
Content Possible The one fate; the example story; proverbs on rulers; action without knowledge. Chapter 10 is thematically loose (Whybray’s objection applies here)
Nuance on 10:15 — 10:15’s “does not know” is the fool not knowing the way to town. It is formally the marker but not the book’s theme of human ignorance
Secondary support Strong, contested Wright: 9:1–12; 9:13–10:15; 10:16–11:2; 11:3–6

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning: The outer seams hold. Wright’s third unit (10:16–11:2) crosses the chapter break. It therefore cuts across both the overview’s preaching units (9:13–10:20 | 11:1–10) and the sweep’s units. The formal markers and the thematic units do not coincide here — Whybray’s point, and the text bears it out. [T]/[S]

Book-overview action required: Note the discrepancy. Keep the preaching units, since they follow the themes, and say so.

Claim 9: The seam at 11:6 / 11:7

Source: Overview, arc map (movements 5 → 6). Claim: A new movement (“Rejoice and remember”) begins at 11:7.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Terminal marker Strong “You do not know” (11:6), the last of the series
Opening marker Possible וּמָתוֹק הָאוֹר (“and sweet is the light”, 11:7) — opens with waw
Content shift Strong Action under uncertainty → light and darkness, youth and age
Lexical shift Strong הֶבֶל (“vanity”) returns at 11:8, 10 after a gap since 9:9. “Not know” stops
Cross-seam link Strong וְדָע (“but know”, 11:9) answers the “not know” series — a deliberate bridge
Address Continuous ≈2.0 and ≈3.5 address forms a verse on either side
Secondary support Strong Wright: the poem begins at 11:7

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning: The seam holds, and 11:9 deliberately reaches back across it. The sweep’s preaching unit 11:1–10 is defensible as a thematic unit even though the formal seam falls inside it. [T]

Book-overview action required: Note the bridge at 11:9.

Claim 10: The placement of 12:8 — with the epilogue (12:8–14)

Source: Overview, arc map (movements 6 and 7: 11:7–12:7 | 12:8–14). Claim: 12:8 begins the epilogue.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Voice Mixed 12:8 is the frame voice (“says the Qohelet”). So is 1:2 — which the overview places inside movement 1
Inclusio Strong 12:8 closes the bracket opened at 1:2; they are near-verbatim
Opening marker at 12:9 Strong וְיֹתֵר (“and besides”) opens the epilogue, and recurs at 12:12
Content Strong 12:8 is the verdict on the death-poem. 12:9–14 appraises Qohelet and addresses “my son”
Secondary support Strong Wright (per Murphy): poem 11:7–12:8; epilogue 12:9–14

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning: The overview treats 1:2 and 12:8 inconsistently. It keeps 1:2 in the prologue but moves 12:8 into the epilogue. The text supports reading 1:2 and 12:8 as the two brackets of Qohelet’s words (1:2–12:8), with 12:9’s וְיֹתֵר (“and besides”) marking the epilogue. [T] The overview’s own preaching units (12:1–8 | 12:9–14) already follow this division; only the arc table is out of step.

Book-overview action required: - Arc table: movement 6 becomes 11:7–12:8 (“Rejoice and remember — and the closing bracket”). - Movement 7 becomes 12:9–14 (Epilogue). - Device paragraph: the frame is “1:1 superscription; 1:2 ‖ 12:8 bracket; 12:9–14 epilogue”.

Claim 11: The refrain scheme as authorial design

Source: Overview, structural options (Wright [S], recalled). Claim: The verbal markers are the author’s intended dividing lines.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Markers present Strong All of Wright’s markers verified in the WLC (Evidence D)
Markers at unit ends Strong Each closes its unit
Correspondence to themes Possible Weak in chapter 10 and in 2:18–6:9 (Whybray)
Independent corroboration Strong The voice shift at 6:10 (Evidence A) does not depend on Wright’s markers
History of interpretation Divided Murphy and Rooker positive; Eaton and Whybray sceptical

Verdict: Uncertain — flag for research.

Reasoning: That the markers exist and fall at unit ends is [T]. That they were meant as the book’s architecture is [I], and it is contested. The macro-division (two halves; the closing poem; the epilogue) is better supported than Wright’s finer units, especially in chapter 10.

Book-overview action required: - Present Wright’s scheme as “verbal markers verified; structural intention contested”, citing Murphy, Rooker, Eaton and Whybray. - Commend the macro-division at high confidence and the finer units at moderate. - If intention becomes load-bearing, read the primary article (Wright, CBQ 30, 1968).
