## Headline Findings

1. **The unit is bracketed by Paul's joy and by Paul's own flesh, and everything inside the bracket is a minister's labour "for you".** `[T]` χαίρω ("to rejoice") occurs in the letter only at 1:24 and 2:5 — the first and last verses of the unit (SBLGNT, by lemma). Between them σάρξ ("flesh") is used three times of Paul's own body: suffering "in my flesh" (1:24), unseen "in the flesh" (2:1), absent "in the flesh" (2:5). ὑπέρ ("on behalf of") stands three times (twice at 1:24, once at 2:1). *Surfaced by:* Tool 3 (structure), Tool 10 (repetition), Tool 7 (vocabulary), Tool 9 (tone). The passage is not autobiography for its own sake. It is the stranger in chains telling a church that has never seen him that his suffering and striving are for them. *High confidence.*

2. **The mystery is a person, and the unit names him three times, each time more closely: "Christ in you, the hope of glory" (1:27); "God's mystery — Christ" (2:2); "in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (2:3).** `[T: as NA28 prints it]` NA28 reads τοῦ μυστηρίου τοῦ θεοῦ, Χριστοῦ ("the mystery of God, Christ") at 2:2 with 𝔓⁴⁶ and B. The apparatus records seven other readings, and every one of them reads as an attempt to explain the short text (Textual Variants). μυστήριον ("mystery") occurs in Colossians only at 1:26, 1:27, 2:2 and 4:3 (SBLGNT). *Surfaced by:* Tool 7, Tool 10, Tool 4 (the relatives ὅ, ὅν, ἐν ᾧ that chain the identifications), Textual Variants, Original Audience. The content of the secret is not a doctrine or a technique. It is Christ, now open to Gentiles, and already holding every treasure the rival teaching will claim to add (2:8, 23). *High confidence on the reading as printed; the variant is the one place the headline rests on a textual decision.*

3. **Colossians 1:24 is about a minister's suffering for the church, not about any shortfall in the atonement — and the letter's own vocabulary shows it.** `[T]`/`[I]` In Colossians ὑπέρ ("on behalf of") occurs in six verses — 1:7, 1:9, 1:24, 2:1, 4:12, 4:13 — and every one describes a minister (Epaphras or Paul) praying, suffering or labouring for churches. It is never used of Christ's death (SBLGNT). θλῖψις ("affliction") is predicated of Christ nowhere else in the New Testament: of its 43 verses, only Col 1:24 joins it to Christ as his own (Rom 8:35 and Phil 1:17 have both words, but the afflictions there are the believer's and Paul's) `[T]`. The reconciliation has already been stated in finished, aorist terms (1:20, 22) `[T]`. And the phrase "in my flesh … for the sake of His body" answers "in the body of His flesh" at 1:22 `[T]`: Christ reconciled in his body of flesh; Paul suffers in his flesh for Christ's body. The two other New Testament uses of ἀναπληρόω ("to fill up") with ὑστέρημα ("what is lacking") — 1 Cor 16:17 and Phil 2:30 — both mean supplying what someone's absence leaves lacking `[T]`. *Surfaced by:* Tool 7, Tool 10, Tool 2, Tool 11 (Pauline usage), Difficult Verses. Which of the live readings of "what is lacking in Christ's afflictions" is right remains open (Difficult / Contested Verses). The absence reading has a named proponent, Garland, and on the evidence of Phil 2:30 it survives Beale's grammatical objection to it (v1.1) `[S: Logos 1]`. The atonement reading is ruled out by the letter's own grammar of ὑπέρ and its finished verbs. **v1.3:** the three commentaries now supplied in full — Dunn, O'Brien and Wright — all read the verse by the messianic woes joined to Christ's sharing in his body's sufferings, and none takes the absence reading `[S: Logos 2]`. *High confidence that the verse is not about atonement; moderate on the positive reading, with a slight lean to the woes-and-body reading (v1.3).*

4. **The passage turns the opening prayer into Paul's labour, and the letter's closing hands the same labour to other men.** `[T]` The prayer of 1:9 — "filled with the knowledge (ἐπίγνωσις) of His will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding (σύνεσις)" — returns in Paul's struggle at 2:2: "the full assurance (πληροφορία) of understanding (σύνεσις), resulting in a true knowledge (ἐπίγνωσις) of God's mystery". σύνεσις ("understanding") occurs in the letter only at 1:9 and 2:2. Then 4:12 gives Epaphras "always labouring earnestly (ἀγωνιζόμενος) for you (ὑπὲρ ὑμῶν) in his prayers, that you may stand perfect (τέλειοι) and fully assured (πεπληροφορημένοι)". ἀγωνίζομαι ("to strive") occurs only at 1:29 and 4:12, and τέλειος ("complete, mature") only at 1:28 and 4:12. παρακαλέω ("to encourage") with καρδία ("heart") stands only at 2:2 and 4:8, where Tychicus is sent "that he may encourage your hearts" (all SBLGNT, by lemma). *Surfaced by:* Tool 11 Move 4, Tool 10, Tool 2. Paul's apostolic agony for a church he has not seen is carried, in the closing names, by the church's own founder and envoys. **Synthetic as a design:** the closing block 4:2–18 shares more of this unit's rarer vocabulary than any other seventeen-verse stretch of the letter (thirteen words that occur in at most four verses of Colossians; the next independent stretch, 1:7–23, shares eleven). The design is *moderate*; each individual link is *high*. **v1.2:** the 13-against-11 margin is not itself evidence: a margin of two or more is ordinary in the letter, occurring for 41–53 of its 85 eleven-verse passages. What is unusual is the long range — apart from the confession's redeployment in chapter 2, this is the letter's only long-range pairing at this level. And Romans has the same frame (15:14–33 with 1:8–17), though Philippians, 2 Corinthians and 2 Timothy do not. The πληροφορ- link (2:2 → 4:12) holds as a word family but is not one of the thirteen lemma matches `[S: audit 1]`.

5. **The unit is the hinge that turns the confession into the warning.** `[T]`/`[I]` "I say this so that no one (μηδείς) will delude you with persuasive argument" (2:4) is the first warning in the letter, and it is restated four verses later: "See to it that no one (μή τις) takes you captive through philosophy and empty deception" (2:8). Before either warning, the passage has placed "all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" in Christ (2:3). σοφία ("wisdom") runs 1:9 → 1:28 → 2:3 → 2:23, where the rival rules have only "the appearance of wisdom" `[T]`. And the warning is softened by joy: Paul rejoices to see their "good discipline and the stability of your faith" (2:5). *Surfaced by:* Phase 0.6, Tool 4 (three ἵνα clauses), Tool 11 Move 4, Tool 9. The polemic of 2:6–23 is prophylactic, aimed at a church in good order, and it is set up by the claim that nothing is left outside Christ for anyone to sell them. *High confidence on the links; the "prophylactic" reading is `[I]`, moderate–high.*

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book level** (overview): to keep a young Gentile church walking "in Him" as they began (2:6), by setting before them the Christ in whom all the fullness dwells, so that no rival "philosophy" can persuade them they lack anything `[I]`.

**Passage level.** Before Paul can warn a church he has never met (2:1), he must answer two unspoken questions: *who is this man to us?* and *what is the message he guards?* The passage answers both. To the first: a minister (διάκονος, 1:23, 25) by God's appointment "for you" (εἰς ὑμᾶς, 1:25), whose suffering, labour and struggle are spent on their behalf (1:24, 29; 2:1) `[T]`. To the second: a once-hidden purpose of God now openly disclosed — Christ among the Gentiles — which holds all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge (1:26–2:3) `[T]`. The stated purpose comes at the end: "I say this so that no one will delude you" (2:4) `[T]`. The author's aim in this passage is therefore **to bind the Colossians to the apostolic message by binding them to its suffering messenger, and to establish, before the polemic, that the message is already complete in Christ** `[I]`.

The passage contributes something to the book's purpose that no other unit does. The letter's warnings rely on Paul's authority over a church that did not hear him. This passage is where that authority is rooted: not in presence or rhetoric (2:4 rejects "persuasive argument"), but in a stewardship received from God and a suffering endured for them `[I]`.

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement.** 1:15–20 confesses Christ as creator and reconciler. 1:21–23 applies the reconciliation to the readers — "yet He has now (νυνί) reconciled you in the body of His flesh through death" (1:22) — on condition that they "continue in the faith firmly established (τεθεμελιωμένοι) and steadfast (ἑδραῖοι)" (1:23). It ends: "the gospel … which was proclaimed in all creation under heaven, and of which I, Paul, was made a minister (οὗ ἐγενόμην ἐγὼ Παῦλος διάκονος)" (1:23) `[T]`. That clause is the seed of the next passage. 1:25 repeats it exactly — ἧς ἐγενόμην ἐγὼ διάκονος ("of which I was made a minister"), now of the church rather than the gospel `[T]`. So the preceding movement has set up three things this passage provides: **(a)** an introduced but unexplained minister; **(b)** a reconciliation "in the body of His flesh" whose relation to the church, "His body" (1:18), has not yet been worked out; **(c)** a condition — "if indeed you continue … steadfast" — that waits for an answer.

**Necessity answer.** The passage exists here because the confession must become a *message* before it can become a *defence*. 1:15–23 has said who Christ is and what he has done; 2:6–23 will say "therefore do not let anyone take you captive". Between them the letter needs to show **how the Christ of 1:15–20 reaches Colossae** — through a stewardship given to Paul "for you" (1:25), through proclamation to "every man" (1:28), through Epaphras (1:7) and soon Tychicus (4:7–8) — and **that the rival's currency is already Christ's** (wisdom and knowledge, 2:3). The answers to (a)–(c) fall in order: the minister explained (1:24–29); his flesh suffering "for His body" in counterpart to Christ's "body of His flesh" (1:22, 24); and the condition of 1:23 met by Paul's report of their "stability" (στερέωμα, 2:5) `[T]`/`[I]`. Placed anywhere else, the warning of 2:8 would come from a stranger with no stated claim on them, and the claim of 2:9–10 ("in Him you have been made complete") would have no prior statement that the treasures are already in Christ.

**Implication.** Read without 1:21–23, 1:24 sounds like a free-standing theory about suffering. Read with it, 1:24 is the minister's counterpart to 1:22, and the passage as a whole is the bridge from *who Christ is* to *why you need nothing more*. **Revisit after Tool 11:** Move 4 sharpens the answer. The passage plants a cluster of vocabulary — mystery, revealing, encouraging hearts, striving "for you", "complete", "fully assured", Laodicea — that the closing chapter takes up name by name (4:3–4, 7–8, 12–13, 17). The positional reason for the passage is also forward-facing. It defines a ministry the letter's last chapter shows being shared.

**Immediately before:** 1:21–23 — reconciliation applied, condition stated, Paul named as minister. **Immediately after:** 2:6–7 — "Therefore (οὖν) as you have received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in Him", the hinge of the letter (overview). **Section:** the first half of the letter (1:3–2:5), which ends here. NA28 marks the unit as a paragraph `[T: as NA28 prints it]`; chapter 2 begins inside it, so the versification is not the seam (the overview says the same).

**Is the passage widely read out of context?** Yes, at two points. 1:27 "Christ in you, the hope of glory" is lifted as a slogan of private inner experience. 1:24 is lifted into debates about the atonement and the treasury of merit (Difficult Verses) `[S]`.

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|--------|---------|----------|
| 1:24 | **Paul's suffering** — "I rejoice … I fill up … in my flesh … for His body" | Opens the bracket (joy; flesh); the minister's suffering as service to the church |
| 1:25–27 | **Paul's stewardship and its content** — "minister … stewardship … to fulfil the word: the mystery … Christ in you" | The commission (1:25) and its content defined in three steps: hidden → manifested → made known (1:26–27) |
| 1:28–29 | **Paul's (and his colleagues') work** — "Him we proclaim … admonishing … teaching … that we may present … for this I labour, striving" | The method and aim of the ministry; the power behind it |
| 2:1–3 | **Paul's struggle for them and its aim** — "how great a struggle I have for you … that their hearts may be encouraged … the knowledge of God's mystery, Christ" | The general ministry (1:28) now aimed at Colossae and Laodicea; the mystery identified as Christ, the holder of the treasures |
| 2:4–5 | **The purpose stated, and the joy** — "I say this so that no one deludes you … I rejoice to see your order and the stability of your faith" | Turns the whole to warning; closes the bracket (flesh; joy) |

**Device.** The passage is **bracketed** (an inclusio) by χαίρω ("rejoice"), at 1:24 and 2:5 — its only two occurrences in the letter — and by Paul's σάρξ ("flesh"), present at 1:24 and absent at 2:5 `[T]`. Inside the bracket the movement is **linear and narrowing**: from the church universal ("His body", 1:24; "the Gentiles", 1:27; "every man", 1:28) to this church and its neighbour ("for you and for those at Laodicea", 2:1) `[T]`. The mystery is **defined three times in a climbing sequence** — the mystery is "Christ in you" (1:27); the mystery of God is "Christ" (2:2); in him are the treasures (2:3) `[T]`. The first person also moves: singular in 1:24–25 ("I"), plural at 1:28 ("we proclaim"), singular again from 1:29 ("I labour") `[T]`. Paul's apostleship is not solitary. The "we" includes the co-sender Timothy (1:1) and, by 1:7, Epaphras `[I]`.

```
              καὶ τὸ στερέωμα τῆς εἰς Χριστὸν πίστεως ὑμῶν.
```

**Central assertions, by section, with mood:**

- 1:24 — "I rejoice in sufferings for you, and I fill up … for His body" — *truth* (indicative; Paul's report of his own state).
- 1:25–27 — "I became a minister … to fulfil the word of God: the mystery … which is Christ in you" — *truth*. The section's weight is carried by the appositional chain, not a main verb.
- 1:28–29 — "Him we proclaim … that we may present every man complete in Christ; for this I labour" — *truth* with a purpose clause. The participles νουθετοῦντες ("admonishing") and διδάσκοντες ("teaching") hang under καταγγέλλομεν ("we proclaim") and describe it; they are not separate tasks `[T]`.
- 2:1–3 — "I want you to know how great a struggle I have for you … that their hearts may be encouraged … unto the knowledge of God's mystery, Christ" — *truth* in the form of a disclosure ("I want you to know").
- 2:4–5 — "I say this so that no one deludes you" — *truth*, with a purpose that functions as a warning. **There is no imperative in the whole passage** `[T]`. The first imperative of the letter's argument comes at 2:6 (περιπατεῖτε, "walk"). The passage informs and binds; it does not yet command.

### 4. Linking Words

- **Νῦν ("now"), 1:24** — temporal-logical: "now", after the reconciliation of 1:22 (νυνί) and in the present of Paul's imprisonment. With νῦν δέ ("but now") at 1:26 it gives the cluster νυνί / νῦν / νῦν across 1:22–26 — three of the letter's four "now" words (νυνί 1:22; 3:8; νῦν 1:24, 26; SBLGNT) `[T]`. The passage is framed in the "now" of revelation.
- **καί ("and"), 1:24** — joins "I rejoice" and "I fill up": the joy and the filling up are one act seen twice, not cause and effect `[T]`.
- **ὑπέρ ("for, on behalf of") ×2, 1:24; ×1, 2:1** — the passage's controlling preposition (Headline 3) `[T]`.
- **ὅ ἐστιν ("which is"), 1:24 and 1:27** — the same identifying formula twice: "His body, which is the church"; "this mystery … which is Christ in you" `[T]`. The NA28 reads the neuter ὅ at 1:27 with 𝔓⁴⁶ A B and others; many witnesses have the masculine ὅς ("who"), attracting the relative to Χριστός (Textual Variants).
- **νῦν δέ ("but now"), 1:26** — the hinge of the hidden/revealed contrast `[T]`. The NA28 marks the break with a dash; the sentence changes construction at the hinge (the participle ἀποκεκρυμμένον gives way to the finite ἐφανερώθη, "was manifested"), and the anacoluthon makes the "now" louder `[T]`.
- **ἵνα ("so that") ×3** — 1:28 (the aim of proclamation: present every man complete); 2:2 (the aim of struggle: hearts encouraged … knowledge of the mystery); 2:4 (the aim of saying all this: that no one deludes you) `[T]`. The three purpose clauses carry the passage's logic. The third reveals that the first two were aimed at it.
- **εἰς ὅ ("for this purpose"), 1:29** — points back to the ἵνα of 1:28: Paul's labour serves the presenting of every man complete `[T]`.
- **γάρ ("for"), 2:1** — grounds 1:29 ("I labour, striving") in the particular: here is how great the struggle is, and for whom `[T]`.
- **εἰς … εἰς ("unto … unto"), 2:2** — two goals of the encouraged and knit-together hearts: "all the wealth of the full assurance of understanding" and "the knowledge of God's mystery". The second restates the first `[I]`.
- **ἐν ᾧ ("in whom"), 2:3** — the relative pronoun that makes Christ the treasury. It is the second of the letter's ἐν ᾧ clauses (1:14; 2:3; 2:11; overview) `[T]`.
- **Τοῦτο λέγω ἵνα ("I say this so that"), 2:4** — "this" points back over 2:1–3 at least, and more naturally over the whole unit `[I]`. The NA28's comma after λέγω makes the ἵνα clause the purpose of the saying `[T: as NA28 prints it]`.
- **εἰ γὰρ καί … ἀλλά ("for even though … nevertheless"), 2:5** — concessive conditional. The εἰ is not doubtful: Paul *is* absent; the καί makes it "even if, as is the case" `[T]`. The γάρ grounds 2:4: Paul can warn them because, though absent, he is with them and can see their order `[I]`.

**Translation note:** the NASB95 keeps all three ἵνα clauses visible ("so that … that … so that"); the ESV keeps them ("that … that … in order that") `[T]`.

### 5. Parallels

Not Hebrew parallelism, but the passage uses **paired and stacked prose patterns** that bear on meaning:

- **The tripled object at 1:28:** νουθετοῦντες πάντα ἄνθρωπον καὶ διδάσκοντες πάντα ἄνθρωπον … ἵνα παραστήσωμεν πάντα ἄνθρωπον ("admonishing every man and teaching every man … that we may present every man") `[T]`. ἄνθρωπος ("man, person") occurs three times in this one verse (SBLGNT). The repetition is emphatic and inclusive: no inner circle `[I]`. Some Western witnesses (D* F G and others) omit the second "every man", which shows scribes found the repetition heavy (Textual Variants).
- **Hidden ‖ manifested, 1:26:** "hidden from the ages and from the generations — but now manifested to His saints" — an antithetical pair across the dash `[T]`.
- **Flesh ‖ spirit, 2:5:** "absent in the flesh, nevertheless with you in the spirit" — antithetical `[T]`; a Pauline formula (1 Cor 5:3, where it is σῶμα, "body", and πνεῦμα, "spirit") `[T]`.
- **The double object of 2:5:** τὴν τάξιν καὶ τὸ στερέωμα ("your order and the firmness") — a pair (Tool 7 on a possible military image).

### 6. Narrator's Comment

**N/A** — epistle, not narrative. The nearest equivalent is the disclosure formula θέλω γὰρ ὑμᾶς εἰδέναι ("for I want you to know", 2:1), with which Paul steps out of description to tell the readers something about himself they could not otherwise know `[T]`, and τοῦτο λέγω ("I say this", 2:4), where he comments on the purpose of his own words `[T]`.

### 7. Vocabulary

**Proper-noun inventory.** Χριστός ("Christ"), five verses of eleven (1:24, 27, 28; 2:2, 5); θεός ("God"), 1:25, 27; 2:2; Λαοδίκεια ("Laodicea"), 2:1; "Paul" is implied, named at 1:23 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. **No canonical freight detected** for Laodicea: it has no Old Testament history (it is not in the Greek OT) and its later canonical appearance (Rev 3:14–22) is downstream. Its letter-level function is in the Echo Table (2:1 → 4:13, 15–16; overview #23) `[T]`.

**θλῖψις ("affliction") and πάθημα ("suffering"), 1:24.** *High confidence.* Both words occur in Colossians only here (SBLGNT). πάθημα is the ordinary Pauline word for sufferings, including Christ's: τὰ παθήματα τοῦ Χριστοῦ ("the sufferings of Christ") at 2 Cor 1:5 and in 1 Pet 4:13 and 5:1 `[T]`. Colossians uses πάθημα of *Paul's* sufferings and θλῖψις of *Christ's*. θλῖψις in the New Testament is overwhelmingly the pressure endured by God's people (Rom 5:3; 2 Cor 1:4; 1 Thess 1:6; Rev 1:9 and others) and the eschatological "tribulation" (Matt 24:21, 29; Mark 13:19, 24) `[T]`. **It is never used of Christ's passion except, on one reading, here** — no other verse of its 43 joins it to Christ as his own (SBLGNT) `[T]`. The choice of word therefore points away from the cross and toward afflictions that Christ endures *in and with his people* `[I]`. **v1.3 — confirmed by two commentators, and given a canonical line.** Dunn ("Paul never calls Christ's sufferings 'afflictions'", NIGTC, 114–117, n. 7) and Wright (θλῖψις "is never, in fact, used of the cross", TNTC, 91–93) make this dig's point independently `[S: Logos 2]`. The eschatological sense has a traceable line: Dan 12:1 — Old Greek ἐκείνη ἡ ἡμέρα θλίψεως, οἵα οὐκ ἐγενήθη; Theodotion καιρὸς θλίψεως, θλῖψις οἵα οὐ γέγονεν — taken up in Mark 13:19 (θλῖψις οἵα οὐ γέγονεν) and Matt 24:21 (Swete; SBLGNT) `[T]`. That is the lexical footing of the commentators' "messianic woes". Col 1:24 shares the word alone, so Daniel is background here, not an allusion `[I]`.

**ἀνταναπληρόω ("to fill up in turn"), 1:24.** *High confidence on the facts; moderate on the force of the prefix.* It occurs once in the New Testament (SBLGNT) `[T]` and nowhere in Swete's LXX (no word in Swete begins with its stem) `[T]`. It is ἀντί ("in turn, in place of") + ἀνά ("up") + πληρόω ("to fill"). The ἀντί most naturally means "I in my turn" — Paul's part answering Christ's `[I]`. The NASB95's "I do my share" tries to carry the prefix; the ESV's "I am filling up" does not `[T]`. The πληρ- family is the overview's keynote: here it stands twice in two verses (ἀνταναπληρῶ, 1:24; πληρῶσαι, 1:25), and again in πληροφορία (2:2) — three of the family's nine occurrences in this one unit (overview microscript, verified) `[T]`.

**ὑστέρημα ("what is lacking"), 1:24.** *High confidence.* Eight New Testament verses (SBLGNT). In two of them it takes the verb ἀναπληρόω: "they supplied (ἀνεπλήρωσαν) what was lacking (ὑστέρημα) on your part" (1 Cor 16:17), and Epaphroditus risked his life "to complete (ἀναπληρώσῃ) what was deficient (ὑστέρημα) in your service to me" (Phil 2:30) `[T]`. In both, what is "lacking" is the *presence* of the senders, which their delegates supply in person. Two more take προσαναπληρόω ("to supply in addition"), of material need (2 Cor 9:12; 11:9) `[T]`. None of the six implies a defect in the thing whose lack is filled `[I]`. This is the strongest single lexical control on 1:24. **v1.1 — the idiom has a proponent and a critic.** Garland takes the 1 Cor 16:17 / Phil 2:30 idiom as the key: Paul, suffering, represents Christ's suffering bodily presence, now "lacking" because Christ has died (Beale, BECNT, 161–162, reporting Garland) `[S: Logos 1]`. Beale objects (a) that Garland's parallels use ἀναπληρόω, not ἀνταναπληρόω, and Garland does not discuss the prefix; and (b) that in 1:24 the focus is on "afflictions" that are lacking, not on Christ's presence `[S: Logos 1]`. **The corpus answers (b) better than Beale allows.** Phil 2:30 has the same shape: τὸ ὑμῶν ὑστέρημα τῆς πρός με λειτουργίας, "your lack *of the service* to me" — ὑστέρημα, then a genitive of the thing lacking, with the absent persons whose thing it is (SBLGNT) `[T]`. There too what is "lacking" is not presence as such but something the absent cannot deliver in person, which their delegate supplies. Col 1:24's τὰ ὑστερήματα τῶν θλίψεων τοῦ Χριστοῦ has the same pattern `[T]` for the syntax, `[I]` for the reading. 1 Cor 16:17 (τὸ ὑμέτερον ὑστέρημα) has no thing-genitive and is the looser parallel `[T]`. Objection (a) marks a gap in Garland's case, not a refutation. The force of ἀντι- is itself disputed (Abbott, ICC, 229) `[S: Logos 1]`, and "in place of" would, if anything, suit the absence reading `[I]`, low–moderate. **v1.3 — Dunn uses the same parallels and does not draw the inference.** He glosses ἀνταναπληρῶ as "literally 'fill up in place of'" and cites 1 Cor 16:17 and Phil 2:30 for the phrase (with Abbott, 229–230, and Lohse, 71 n. 25), but reads the verse eschatologically (NIGTC, 114–117) `[S: Logos 2]`. In every other Pauline use of the idiom — 1 Cor 16:17; 2 Cor 9:12; 11:9; Phil 2:30 — a lack that belongs to *persons* ("yours", "mine", "the saints'") is supplied by others acting for them (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Dunn's own gloss and parallels are the ingredients of the absence reading `[I]`, low–moderate.

**οἰκονομία ("stewardship"), 1:25.** *High confidence.* A household-management word: the office of a steward entrusted with someone else's goods (Luke 16:2–4) `[T]`. Nine NT verses; in Paul at 1 Cor 9:17 ("I have a stewardship entrusted to me"), Eph 1:10; 3:2, 9, and here (SBLGNT) `[T]`. The genitive "of God" and the participle "given to me for you" make Paul a steward of God's household with a charge toward the Gentiles `[T]`/`[I]`. It answers the overview's household theme from the other end: Paul is a servant in God's household before he addresses households (3:18–4:1) `[I]`, moderate.

**πληρῶσαι τὸν λόγον τοῦ θεοῦ ("to fulfil the word of God"), 1:25.** *High confidence on the wording; moderate on the sense.* πληρόω with "the word" or "the gospel" occurs in the New Testament only here, Rom 15:19 ("I have fully preached the gospel of Christ", πεπληρωκέναι) and, in a different sense, Mark 1:15 ("the time is fulfilled") (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Rom 15:19 is the close parallel: Paul "fills" the gospel by carrying it to its full geographical reach `[T]`. Here the infinitive is then defined as "the mystery … among the Gentiles" (1:26–27), so "fulfilling the word" means bringing it to the Gentiles, its full intended extent `[I]`.

**μυστήριον ("mystery"), 1:26, 27; 2:2.** *High confidence.* Not a secret kept from the uninitiated but a purpose of God hidden in past ages and now disclosed (1:26) `[T]`. In the Greek OT it is concentrated in Daniel 2 (Tool 11) and the later books (Wisdom, Sirach, Tobit, Judith, 2 Maccabees) `[S]` — recalled; the Daniel occurrences were verified in both Greek texts. Paul's usage: Rom 11:25; 16:25 (bracketed in NA28); 1 Cor 2:7; 4:1; 15:51; Eph 1:9; 3:3–9; 5:32; 6:19; Col 1:26–27; 2:2; 4:3 (SBLGNT, with the NA28 for Romans 16) `[T]`. In Colossians the mystery is defined three times as Christ himself (Headline 2). **The word would also have sounded, to Gentile ears, like the language of the mystery cults** `[S]` — Original Audience.

**φανερόω ("to manifest"), 1:26** — and γνωρίζω ("to make known"), 1:27. *High confidence.* φανερόω occurs in Colossians at 1:26, 3:4 (twice in the verse) and 4:4 (SBLGNT): the mystery manifested (1:26), Christ and the believers manifested in glory (3:4), Paul praying to "make it clear" (4:4) (overview echo #9) `[T]`.

**πλοῦτος ("riches"), 1:27; 2:2.** *High confidence.* Only these two verses in the letter; with the adverb πλουσίως ("richly") at 3:16, the word of Christ dwelling "richly" (SBLGNT) `[T]`. "Riches of glory" and "all the wealth of the full assurance" (2:2): the language of abundance against any suggestion of lack `[I]`.

**τέλειος ("complete, mature, perfect"), 1:28.** *High confidence on the occurrences; moderate on the shade.* Only 1:28 and 4:12 in Colossians (SBLGNT) `[T]`. The goal of ministry is a person brought to completion "in Christ", not "beyond" him `[I]`. In the mysteries the τέλειος could be the "initiate": BDAG (995) and Louw–Nida (123) record the technical sense, but Chester finds no example where τέλειος itself — as against τελέω, τέλος and τελετή — carries it (*Conversion at Corinth*, 285–286) `[S: Logos 1]`. **Possible, contested, not to be preached as fact** (v1.1). Dunn makes "complete, mature" the principal sense, with a sacrificial overtone: "entire and without blemish", as of the Passover lamb, πρόβατον τέλειον (Exod 12:5 LXX; checked in Swete), which he ties to 1:22 (NIGTC, 125–126) `[S: Logos 1]`. The letter supports the tie: παρίστημι ("present") stands at 1:22, with ἀμώμους ("blameless", a sacrificial word), and at 1:28, with τέλειον (overview echo #11) `[T]` — *moderate* for the overtone. In Paul, 1 Cor 2:6 speaks of wisdom "among the mature (τελείοις)" in a passage about a hidden mystery (1 Cor 2:7) `[T]`, so the pairing of τέλειος with μυστήριον has a Pauline precedent.

**ἀγών / ἀγωνίζομαι ("struggle" / "to strive"), 1:29; 2:1.** *High confidence.* Athletic and combat vocabulary (the contest) `[S]`, recalled. In Colossians only 1:29, 2:1 and 4:12 (overview echo #12, verified) `[T]`. With κοπιάω ("to labour to exhaustion", 1:29). The two verbs stand together in one verse of the SBLGNT only here `[T]`.

**ἐνέργεια ("working, energy"), 1:29.** *High confidence.* Only 1:29 and 2:12 in Colossians (SBLGNT) `[T]`: "according to His working, which works (ἐνεργουμένην) mightily within me" (1:29), and "faith in the working (ἐνεργείας) of God, who raised Him from the dead" (2:12). **The energy driving Paul's labour is the same working of God that raised Christ** `[T]`/`[I]`. The noun with its own cognate verb in one verse occurs in the New Testament only at 1:29 (SBLGNT) `[T]` — the emphasis is deliberate `[I]`.

**συμβιβάζω ("to knit together"), 2:2.** *High confidence.* Only 2:2 and 2:19 in Colossians (SBLGNT): hearts "knit together in love" (2:2), and the body "held together (συμβιβαζόμενον) by the joints and ligaments (συνδέσμων)" (2:19) `[T]`. Love itself is called "the bond (σύνδεσμος) of perfection (τελειότητος)" at 3:14 `[T]`. σύνδεσμος occurs in the letter only at 2:19 and 3:14; ἀγάπη ("love") at 1:4, 1:8, 1:13, 2:2, 3:14 (SBLGNT). **A verified three-way link: knit together in love (2:2) → the body knit together by ligaments (2:19) → love the ligament of completeness (3:14)** `[T]`. Synthetic as a design; each pair high.

**πληροφορία ("full assurance"), 2:2.** *High confidence.* Four NT verses (Col 2:2; 1 Thess 1:5; Heb 6:11; 10:22); the verb πληροφορέω six (SBLGNT) `[T]`. The noun is absent from Swete's LXX; the verb occurs there once, in a bad sense — the heart of men "fully set" on evil (Eccl 8:11) `[T]`. So the word is New Testament vocabulary. In Colossians it is answered at 4:12 (πεπληροφορημένοι, "fully assured") `[T]`.

**σύνεσις / ἐπίγνωσις / γνῶσις / σοφία ("understanding / knowledge / knowledge / wisdom"), 2:2–3.** *High confidence.* σύνεσις: only 1:9 and 2:2 in the letter. ἐπίγνωσις: 1:9, 1:10, 2:2, 3:10. γνῶσις: only 2:3. σοφία: 1:9, 1:28, 2:3, 2:23, 3:16, 4:5 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. The cluster is the vocabulary of the opening prayer (1:9) reassembled around the mystery. θησαυρός ("treasure") with σοφία ("wisdom") or with γνῶσις ("knowledge") occurs in one New Testament verse only, Col 2:3 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. The nearest New Testament verse is Rom 11:33, "the depth of the riches both of the wisdom and knowledge of God" — πλοῦτος, σοφία and γνῶσις together in the NT only there; Col 2:2–3 has all three across two verses `[T]`.

**ἀπόκρυφος ("hidden"), 2:3.** *High confidence on the facts; moderate on the sense.* Three NT verses (Mark 4:22; Luke 8:17; Col 2:3), with ἀποκρύπτω ("to hide") at 1:26 and κρύπτω ("to hide") at 3:3 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Placed last in its clause as a predicate, it says the treasures are in Christ *as hidden things* `[T]`. Since the mystery has been manifested (1:26), "hidden" here most naturally means *stored up, treasured* — not inaccessible, but located nowhere else `[I]`. This is a paradox the passage intends: the revealed mystery is a person in whom treasures remain stored `[I]`, moderate.

**παραλογίζομαι and πιθανολογία ("to delude" and "persuasive argument"), 2:4.** *High confidence on the facts.* παραλογίζομαι: Col 2:4 and Jas 1:22 ("deluding yourselves") only; πιθανολογία: once in the NT (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Both are argument-words: to "reckon falsely" and "plausible speech". The second is a term for argument from probability. Plato's Socrates sets πιθανολογία against ἀπόδειξις ("demonstration") — "you base your statements on probability" — at *Theaetetus* 162e (Dunn, NIGTC, 133; Copenhaver and Arthurs, Kerux, 118) `[S: Logos 1]` (v1.1: verified at second hand). The sense is attested. Whether Colossian hearers caught a technical register is `[I]`, moderate; the Logos answer's further step — that Paul is answering marketplace rhetoricians — goes beyond the evidence. Paul names the threat as **an argument**, not yet a practice `[I]`.

**τάξις and στερέωμα ("order" and "firmness"), 2:5.** *High confidence on the facts; moderate on the image.* στερέωμα occurs once in the New Testament (SBLGNT) `[T]`. In Swete's LXX it is the "firmament" of Genesis 1 (Gen 1:6–8) and Ezekiel 1, the Lord as "my rock" (Ps 17:3 LXX = Ps 18:2), and — once — the main body of an army: "Bacchides and the strength (στερέωμα) of the army were on the right" (1 Macc 9:14) `[T]`. τάξις occurs in eight NT verses — five of them the "order" of Melchizedek in Hebrews, with Luke 1:8, 1 Cor 14:40 and this verse `[T]`. Read together, "order and solid front" may be a military picture of a disciplined line `[S: Logos 2]` (v1.3: Lightfoot, 174, as reported by Dunn, with Lohmeyer, Moule, Martin, Caird and Wright; also Robertson and Vincent). The LXX supports the military sense of στερέωμα as available `[T]`. The picture fits the coming "take you captive" (2:8, συλαγωγέω, "to carry off as booty") `[I]`. *Moderate.* **v1.3 — the image has holders, and a limit.** Dunn grants that both words could be military (τάξις, troops in battle order; στερέωμα, the strength of an army, 1 Macc 9:14), but holds the allusion "at most somewhat casual": it calls for no particular discipline, and "the confidence expressed hardly implies a pressing crisis and numerous defections" (NIGTC, 134–135) `[S: Logos 2]`. He confirms that τάξις occurs elsewhere in Paul only at 1 Cor 14:40, which the corpus bears out `[T]`. Vincent's LXX evidence checks out, with two verse numbers adjusted to Swete: 1 Macc 10:50 ἐστερέωσεν πόλεμον ("he made the battle firm"); Ezek 13:5 οὐκ ἔστησαν ἐν στερεώματι; Ps 17:3 (his "17:2"), "the Lord is my στερέωμα"; and Esth 9:29 (his "9:22"), of confirming a letter `[T]`. Lam 2:4 adds the verb in a battle picture: ἐστερέωσεν δεξιὰν αὐτοῦ ὡς ὑπεναντίος, "he set his right hand like an adversary" `[T]`. **Robertson needs two corrections.** His "2 Thess 3:6, 8, 11 … ταξις" is the opposite word, ἀτάκτως / ἀτακτέω (3:6, 7, 11) `[T]`. And his "a few stragglers had gone over to the Gnostics" has no ground in the text `[I]`. Dunn's conclusion agrees with Headline 5: the polemic is aimed at a church in good order. *Moderate on the image; a passing picture, not a theme.*

**Name-wordplay check:** none found.

**Ancient versions check.** The modern versions agree on the rendering that carries most weight — "filling up what is lacking in Christ's afflictions" (1:24). The **Vulgate** reads *Qui nunc gaudeo in passionibus pro vobis, et adimpleo ea quae desunt passionum Christi, in carne mea pro corpore ejus, quod est Ecclesia* (v1.1, supplied from Logos) `[S: Logos 1]`. The edition is not named; the spelling *ejus* suggests the Clementine text `[I]`. The Latin renders θλίψεων ("afflictions") by *passionum* ("sufferings, passions"), the word the Latin tradition used for Christ's Passion. **Translation-tradition split:** the Greek uses the word never used of the cross (θλῖψις); the Latin uses the word most associated with it — **and uses it twice**: *in passionibus* renders Paul's own παθήμασιν ("sufferings") as well. The Latin therefore levels the distinction the Greek draws between Paul's παθήματα and Christ's θλίψεις (Tool 7; Headline 3) `[T: as supplied]`. The Latin reading lends itself to the theology of Christ's sufferings being supplemented, and that is the tradition in which the verse was later cited for the treasury of merits `[S]`. For preaching, the Greek word matters: it points to afflictions Christ shares *with* his people, not to his atoning death. *Vulgate wording supplied in v1.1.*

**Pulpit divergence note (ESV).** Where the ESV and the NASB95 part company in this unit, the ESV is mostly closer to the Greek, and it will serve the congregation well on the internal chains: "struggling … struggle … struggling" (1:29; 2:1; 4:12), "mature … mature" (1:28; 4:12) and "knit together … knit together" (2:2; 2:19) are all audible in the ESV and broken in the NASB95. Three things the congregation **will not hear** from the ESV: (1) that "flesh" is said of Paul three times — the ESV has "my flesh" (1:24), then "face to face" (2:1) and "absent in body" (2:5), so the bracket of Headline 1 is half-hidden. Say it. (2) That "revealed" (1:26) is a different word from "revealed" in other letters and is the same word as "appears" in 3:4 and "make it clear" in 4:4. (3) That the "you" of "Christ in you" is plural and Gentile. "In you" is the likelier reading (v1.1), but it is Christ indwelling a Gentile church, not only a private heart. Tell them.

### 9. Tone and Feel

**Joyful hardship, intimate and urgent.** The passage opens with "Now I rejoice in my sufferings" — joy and suffering in the same breath — and closes "rejoicing to see your good discipline" `[T]`. Between them the language is of labour to exhaustion (κοπιάω), contest (ἀγωνίζομαι, ἀγών), and of a God-given power "working mightily" (1:29) `[T]`. The register is **pastoral and personal**: "I want you to know how great a struggle I have for you" (2:1) is a man disclosing his inner labour to strangers `[T]`.

**Devices.** The disclosure formula (2:1); the exclamatory ἡλίκον ("how great!", 2:1); the threefold "every man" (1:28); the accumulation of riches-words ("riches of glory", "all the wealth", "all the treasures"); the sudden shift from cosmic ("hidden from the ages and generations") to local ("those at Laodicea") `[T]`.

**Tonal shift.** One shift only, at 2:4: the warmth turns watchful ("so that no one will delude you"), and immediately turns back to warmth (2:5) `[T]`. The warning is spoken by a friend who is glad, not by a judge who is alarmed `[I]`.

**Soundtrack.** A solo voice over a deep, steady bass: tenderness carried by effort. The sermon should not sound like a lecture on the theology of suffering, and it should not sound like a rebuke; it should sound like a letter from prison read to a church that has just heard its name in it.

And Isa 45:1 names Cyrus τῷ χριστῷ μου ("My anointed") `[T]`.

*Source context (Move 1).* Isaiah 45 is the Cyrus oracle: the LORD will give his anointed Cyrus the hidden treasures of the nations "so that you may know" (45:3), for Israel's sake (45:4), so that from east to west all may know "there is none besides Me" (45:6). The nations then come to Israel confessing "God is in you, and there is no other" (45:14), and "every knee shall bow" (45:23) `[T]`.

*Book usage (Move 2).* **The one cross-passage thread in this unit.** The 1:15–20 dig ranked Isaiah 45 7th–23rd of 1,259 chapters against 1:15–20, and named it as a candidate live source with 2:3 `[S: 1:15–20 dig]`. Here it is two point contacts (2:3; 1:27) without a field rank `[T]`. Paul elsewhere quotes Isa 45:23 (Rom 14:11) and echoes it (Phil 2:10–11) `[T]`, so the chapter was in his hand `[I]`.

*OT-to-OT (Move 3).* Isa 45:3 and Prov 2:4 share מַטְמוֹן (above).

*What it adds.* If live, Isaiah 45 supplies the reversal of 1:27: the nations once confessed that God was "in" Israel; now the mystery is that Christ is "in" (or "among") the Gentiles `[I]`. *Hays:* volume low–moderate (two-word contacts at two verses); recurrence moderate (with 1:15–20); coherence moderate; history of interpretation unchecked. Audited (C5, C8, C11): see the note above `[S: audit 1]`. Not used in a Headline.

### 12. Genre

Pauline epistle; the passage is **apostolic self-presentation** (an *apologia* for his ministry is too strong — there is no defence against an accuser) in the body of the letter, ending in the first warning `[I]`. It is not hymn, not narrative, not exhortation: it has no imperative (Tool 3). Reading rule: it is to be read as a minister's disclosure that binds the readers to the message, and preached declaratively — this is what God has done through his servant for you — not as a model of ministerial heroism.

### 13. Copycat

Not narrative, but the passage describes Paul's actions in the first person, so the copycat question is live.

| What Paul does | Category | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Receives a stewardship "for you" — the Gentiles (1:25) | **Unique** | The apostolic commission to the Gentiles (cf. Eph 3:2, 8) `[T]`; not a template for every minister |
| "Fills up what is lacking in Christ's afflictions" (1:24) | **Unique in its apostolic form; pattern in its principle** | The "in my turn" belongs to the apostle's place in the mission `[I]`. Yet suffering for the church is shared by Epaphras (4:12–13) and is the pattern of 2 Cor 1:6 ("if we are afflicted, it is for your comfort") `[T]` |
| Proclaims Christ, admonishing and teaching every man in all wisdom (1:28) | **Normative pattern — and the letter says so** | At 3:16 the whole church is told to do the same: "with all wisdom teaching and admonishing one another" `[T]` (Move 4). This is the clearest transfer from apostle to congregation in the letter |
| Labours by God's power (1:29) | **Pattern** | The power is "His working", the resurrection power (2:12) — available to the church, not Paul's alone `[I]` |
| Struggles in prayer for churches he has not seen (2:1) | **Normative pattern** | Epaphras does exactly this (4:12) `[T]` |
| Rejoices in suffering (1:24) | **Pattern** | Commended elsewhere (Rom 5:3; Jas 1:2) `[S]` recalled; but not a demand that sufferers feel joy on command — the joy is grounded in the purpose ("for you") `[I]` |

### 14. Bible Timeline

This side of the cross and the resurrection, in the "now" of revelation (1:26) `[T]`. The reconciliation is accomplished (1:20, 22); the mystery hidden "from the ages" has been manifested (1:26); the Gentiles are being gathered (1:27); and the "hope of glory" (1:27) is still ahead, at Christ's appearing (3:4) `[T]`. **The passage is about the in-between time precisely**: the time of proclamation to "every man", of the church's afflictions, and of the minister's labour toward a presentation still future ("that we may present", 1:28; cf. 1:22) `[T]`. Whether "the afflictions of Christ" belong to an eschatological measure of tribulation before the end is the background question (Historical Background) `[S]`.

### 15. Who Am I?

| Figure | Function |
|---|---|
| Paul | **Apostle and steward — not "me"** for the preacher or the congregation. The preacher is not Paul; the congregation is the "you" for whom Paul suffers `[I]` |
| "You" (the Colossians), "those at Laodicea", "those who have not seen my face" | **Recipients — us.** A Gentile church that has received the apostolic word through others, and never saw the apostle. That is the position of every later church `[I]` |
| "Every man" (1:28) | **Representative of humanity** — the scope of the message, without exclusion |
| "No one" who would delude (2:4) | **Unnamed potential deceiver** — not a character to identify, but a voice to recognise |
| The saints (1:26) | **Us**, as the people to whom the mystery has been manifested |

**The commonest misreading is "Paul is me"** — the minister in the pulpit taking 1:24 or 1:29 as a description of his own sufferings or labour. The text guards against it: the apostolic stewardship is given "to me for you" (1:25) `[T]`. The proper identification is with the Colossians — those who receive, in their own day, the fruit of an apostle's suffering they did not see.

### 16. So What?

**Stage 1 — the author's intended response.** That the Colossians should know (2:1) how much the apostle has spent for them, recognise the gospel they already hold as the whole of God's revealed mystery, and so be unpersuadable by any argument that offers more (2:4). The response is *settled confidence and loyalty* — to the message and to its messengers — rather than a new action `[I]`.

**Stage 2 — worldview, behaviour, motivation.**

- *Worldview:* there is no hidden "next level" beyond Christ. God's secret has been told, and it is Christ; all wisdom and knowledge are stored in him (2:3). A Christian who has Christ has not been given the introductory course. Suffering in Christian ministry is not evidence of failure; it can be the means by which Christ's body is served (1:24).
- *Stop:* stop looking for depth elsewhere — in the more impressive speaker, the more sophisticated system, the more "advanced" teacher who offers what plain teaching did not (2:4). Stop treating the ordinary minister who brought you the gospel (Epaphras, 1:7) as second-rate.
- *Start:* start doing for one another what Paul did for you — admonishing and teaching with all wisdom (1:28 → 3:16). Start praying and striving for churches you have not seen (2:1; 4:12).
- *Motivation:* the riches are already yours ("Christ in you, the hope of glory", 1:27); the power for labour is the power that raised Christ (1:29; 2:12); the apostle rejoices over your steadiness (2:5).

**Four audiences.**

- *For me:* where am I listening for a "persuasive argument" that the gospel is not enough — a technique, an experience, an intellectual system? What would it mean to treat Christ as the treasury rather than the entrance hall?
- *For a Christian friend:* the friend who thinks their faith is shallow because they came to it simply, through an ordinary church, needs to hear that the mystery was told to "every man", not to the elite, and that "complete in Christ" (1:28) is the goal, not the beginning.
- *For the church community:* the "you" is plural and Gentile. "Christ in you" is a word to a congregation. The church's mutual teaching and admonishing (3:16) is the apostolic ministry continued, not a lesser thing.
- *For an unbeliever:* the gospel is not a secret for initiates. It is a disclosed mystery, proclaimed to every person, and its content is a person. And its first messengers suffered for it — which is a claim worth weighing.

**Prayer.** Father, we thank You that the mystery hidden for ages has been made known to us, and that it is Christ in us, the hope of glory. Keep us from every persuasive argument that would draw us from Him, in whom all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are stored. Give Your servants who labour for us Your strength, the power that raised Him from the dead; and make us, in our turn, a people who teach and admonish one another in all wisdom, until You present us complete in Christ.

#### Grammatical and syntactical features

- **The anacoluthon at 1:26.** The participle ἀποκεκρυμμένον ("hidden") is followed not by another participle but by a finite verb, νῦν δὲ ἐφανερώθη ("but now it was manifested"); NA28 marks the break with a dash. The grammar breaks at the moment of revelation `[T]`; that the break is rhetorical emphasis is `[I]`, moderate.
- **ὅ ἐστιν twice (1:24, 27).** The neuter relative "which is" identifies the body as the church and the mystery as "Christ in you". At 1:27 NA28's neuter ὅ agrees with μυστήριον ("mystery"); the majority's masculine ὅς is attracted to Χριστός (Textual Variants) `[T]`.
- **The apposition at 2:2.** τοῦ μυστηρίου τοῦ θεοῦ, Χριστοῦ — "the mystery of God, Christ". Grammatically Χριστοῦ ("Christ") is most naturally in apposition to μυστηρίου ("mystery"): *God's mystery, namely Christ* `[I]`. A second reading takes it as appositional to θεοῦ: *the mystery of God-Christ* — which would make a striking Christological statement but is less natural `[S]` (recalled). **v1.1:** the sources returned by the Logos round all take the first reading — Beale (BECNT, 155), Beale and Gladd (*Hidden but Now Revealed*, 208), Osborne (56, 63: "the mystery of God, namely Christ") — and do not discuss the second `[S: Logos 1]`. A search that does not find a reading has not shown that no one holds it, so the second stays a recalled minority view. Both modern versions take the first, and this dig agrees `[I]`, high — now with commentary support.
- **Present tenses of ongoing ministry (1:24, 28, 29; 2:1).** χαίρω, ἀνταναπληρῶ, καταγγέλλομεν, κοπιῶ, ἔχω — continuous action: the ministry is in progress while he writes `[T]`.
- **Aorist infinitive πληρῶσαι (1:25)** — the whole task viewed as one act: to bring the word to its full extent `[T]`; aspect `[I]`.
- **The perfect ἀποκεκρυμμένον (1:26)** — hidden and remaining hidden through the ages, until the "now" `[T]`; aspect `[I]`.
- **The aorist passive participle συμβιβασθέντες (2:2)** — a nominative attached to a genitive noun (αἱ καρδίαι αὐτῶν … συμβιβασθέντες): the participle agrees with the people, not the hearts (*constructio ad sensum*) `[T]`. Many later witnesses "correct" it to the genitive συμβιβασθέντων (Textual Variants).
- **The shift of persons at 2:1–2:** "for **you** … and those at Laodicea … and as many as have not seen my face … that **their** hearts may be encouraged" `[T]`. The third person "their" gathers all three groups, so the Colossians are among those who have not seen Paul's face `[I]`, high — confirming the overview's Presenting Situation.
- **The concessive εἰ … καί … ἀλλά (2:5)** — "even though (as is true)" `[T]`.

#### Wordplay and sound-features

- **ἀνταναπληρῶ … πληρῶσαι (1:24–25)** — a verb and its root in consecutive verses: Paul "fills up" Christ's afflictions and "fulfils" the word `[T]`. The repetition binds suffering and preaching as one ministry `[I]`, moderate.
- **ἀποκεκρυμμένον (1:26) … ἀπόκρυφοι (2:3)** — the same root brackets the mystery section: the mystery hidden (and now manifested) — the treasures hidden (and still stored) `[T]`; the paradox is `[I]`.

#### 2:2 — "the mystery of God, Christ"

- **The issue:** what follows τοῦ μυστηρίου ("the mystery").
- **Manuscript evidence:** NA28 prints τοῦ θεοῦ, Χριστοῦ with **𝔓⁴⁶ B** (and a few Vulgate manuscripts and Hilary). The apparatus lists **seven** other readings `[T]`: "of God" (D¹ H P 1881 2464 and some Sahidic); "of Christ" (81 1241ˢ 1739 b; Fulgentius); "of God, which is Christ" (D*, some Latin; Augustine); "of God who is in Christ" (33; Clement, Ambrosiaster); "of God the Father of Christ" (א* A C 048 1175, some Latin and Coptic); "of God and Father of Christ" (א² Ψ 365 945 1505); "of the God and Father and of Christ" (D² K L 075 0208 0278 104 630 and the majority text 𝔪, with the Harklean Syriac).
- **Most likely original:** the NA28 text. It is the shortest and the hardest, and every alternative reads as an attempt to explain or smooth it — by dropping a word, by spelling out "which is", or by turning the apposition into "Father of Christ" `[I]`. *High confidence*, with the caveat that its Greek support is two manuscripts (though they are the two oldest).
- **Preaching implication:** both the ESV and the NASB95 follow it. Preach "God's mystery, which is Christ". The KJV reader will have "the mystery of God, and of the Father, and of Christ", the majority reading; if a member of the congregation asks, the answer is that the oldest manuscripts have the short text and the long one is a later expansion.

#### 1:27 — ὅ ("which") / ὅς ("who")

- **The issue:** neuter ὅ (agreeing with "mystery") or masculine ὅς (agreeing with "Christ").
- **Manuscript evidence:** NA28 ὅ with 𝔓⁴⁶ A B F G P 33 1739 1881 and the Latin; ὅς with א C D H I K L Ψ and others, including 𝔪 `[T]`.
- **Most likely original:** ὅ, the neuter, with the earliest witnesses; ὅς is the scribe making the identification explicit `[I]`, high.
- **Preaching implication:** none — both mean that the mystery *is* Christ in you. The variant shows that the earliest readers took the clause as an identification. **v1.3:** the same two oldest witnesses, 𝔓⁴⁶ and B, read the neuter again at 2:10, where the NA28 does *not* follow them (2:6–15 dig, Textual Variants) `[T]`. ὅ ἐστιν ("which is") is a formula of this letter — 1:24, 1:27, 3:14, with ἅ ἐστιν at 2:17 and 2:22 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Vincent notes the 1:27 variant without deciding it `[S: Logos 2]`.

#### 1:24 — "my sufferings"

- **The issue:** a few witnesses (א² 075 81 323 326 629 1241ˢ 1505 2464, some Latin and the Harklean Syriac; Chrysostom) add μου ("my") after παθήμασιν ("sufferings") `[T]`.
- **Most likely original:** without μου (all the other witnesses) `[I]`, high. The English versions all supply "my" anyway (Tool 8).
- **Preaching implication:** none, except that the possessive the congregation hears is the translators'.

#### 1:28 — "in Christ [Jesus]"; the second "every man"

- **The issue:** the majority adds Ἰησοῦ ("Jesus") after Χριστῷ; D* F G and some Latin witnesses omit the second πάντα ἄνθρωπον ("every man") `[T]`.
- **Most likely original:** NA28's text (𝔓⁴⁶ א* A B C D* F G for the first; nearly all witnesses for the second) `[I]`, high.
- **Preaching implication:** the omission of "every man" shows scribes found the threefold repetition heavy; the repetition is original and emphatic (Tool 5).

#### 2:1, 2:2 (syntax), 2:3, 2:4 — minor

- 2:1: περί ("concerning") for ὑπέρ ("for") in the majority; NA28 ὑπέρ with 𝔓⁴⁶ א A B C and others `[T]`. Two minuscules and some versions add "and those in Hierapolis" from 4:13 `[T]` — a scribe hearing the echo this dig reports (Move 4).
- 2:2: συμβιβασθέντων (genitive) in the majority, correcting the nominative συμβιβασθέντες (Original Language Observations) `[T]`; and three variant forms of πᾶν πλοῦτος ("all the wealth") `[T]`.
- 2:3: the majority adds τῆς ("the") before γνώσεως ("knowledge") `[T]`.
- 2:4: the majority adds δέ ("and, now") after τοῦτο, and reads μή τις for μηδείς ("no one") `[T]`. No change of meaning.

**No variant in this unit affects 1:24's hard phrase.** The difficulty there is interpretive, not textual.

### Historical and Cultural Background

#### Setting

- **Time and place of writing:** Paul is imprisoned (4:3, 10, 18) `[T]`; the place — Rome, Ephesus or Caesarea — and so the date are not stated `[S]`.
- **Location of the readers:** Colossae, with Laodicea and Hierapolis nearby (2:1; 4:13, 15–16) `[T]`. The three towns stood close together in the Lycus valley in Phrygia `[S]`; Laodicea was the larger and wealthier (cf. Rev 3:17) `[S]`. An earthquake struck Laodicea in the early 60s `[S]` (Tacitus, *Annals* 14.27, recalled).
- **The letter's delivery:** Tychicus and Onesimus carry it (4:7–9) `[T]`, and it is to be read aloud and exchanged with Laodicea (4:16) `[T]`.

#### What the original audience knew

1. **"Mystery" (μυστήριον) was a word of Greek religion.** The mystery cults — of Demeter at Eleusis, of Isis, of Dionysus, of Mithras — offered secret rites disclosed only to initiates, who were forbidden to reveal them `[S]`. Phrygia had its own cult of the Great Mother `[S]`. A Gentile convert would hear "mystery" first as *a secret for initiates*. Paul's mystery is the opposite: once hidden by God, now proclaimed openly to "every man" (1:28) `[I]`.
2. **"Mystery" was also a word of Jewish apocalyptic.** In Daniel (Aramaic רָז, Greek μυστήριον) and in the Qumran writings (רָז) it means God's hidden plan for history, disclosed by revelation `[S]` (Qumran recalled; Daniel verified). This, not the cults, is the semantic home of Paul's usage `[I]`, high.
3. **"Complete" (τέλειος) may have sounded like "initiated"** in the mystery language. BDAG records the sense; Chester finds no instance of τέλειος itself carrying it `[S: Logos 1]`. *Contested* (v1.1). If the overtone was heard, 1:28 claims the word: the "initiate" is everyone brought to completeness in Christ. Dunn's principal sense, maturity, does not need it.
4. **The messianic woes.** Jewish apocalyptic texts expected a period of intense suffering for God's people before the end, sometimes as a fixed measure that had to be completed (Dan 12:1; *1 Enoch* 47:1–4; *4 Ezra* 4:33–37; *2 Baruch*) `[S]` — recalled, except Dan 12:1. On this background, "what is lacking in Christ's afflictions" would be the remaining measure of the church's sufferings, which Paul takes on himself `[S]`. This is the reading taken by all three commentaries now supplied in full — Dunn, O'Brien and Wright `[S: Logos 2]` (v1.3). Dunn cites Rev 6:9–11 and *4 Ezra* 4:33–43 for an appointed sum of suffering, with Dan 7:21–27; 12:1–3, *Jubilees* 23, the Hodayot and the *Testament of Moses* for the woes; Wright calls the fixed quota "not provable" `[S: Logos 2]`.
5. **Rhetoric and its suspicion.** Greek culture prized persuasive speech, and philosophers had long contrasted it with truth — "plausible argument" was a term in that contrast `[S]`. Paul's ministry elsewhere renounces "persuasive words of wisdom" (1 Cor 2:4) `[T]`.
6. **Letters and absence.** Ancient friendship letters often claimed presence in spirit despite bodily absence `[S]` (recalled; the commonplace is well attested). 2:5 uses the convention — and fills it, since Paul says he *sees* their order.
7. **Prisoners and suffering.** A prisoner depended on friends for food and support; Paul's chains (4:18) would be a cause of shame by ordinary standards `[S]`. To rejoice in them (1:24) is a deliberate inversion `[I]`.

#### What this changes about how we read

- The word "mystery" has drifted in English to mean "something we can't understand". The Colossians would have heard "a secret only for the initiated". Paul means neither: **a secret God has now told, to everyone**. The preacher needs to strip both misunderstandings.
- The messianic-woes background (if adopted) makes 1:24 an eschatological statement rather than a mystical one: the church's sufferings are "Christ's" because they belong to the age of the Messiah, and Paul absorbs them for the church `[S]`.

### Original Audience Reception

#### Canonical audience

- **Canonical section:** Pauline corpus; the fourth of the prison letters in canonical order (overview).
- **What the canonical reader knows:** Ephesians, two letters back, has already said much of this — the stewardship, the mystery hidden from the ages, the Gentiles, "my afflictions for you" (Eph 3:1–13). The canonical reader will hear Colossians 1:24–2:5 as familiar (overview's Reading implication). **What is new here** is the identification of the mystery as Christ himself (2:2), his possession of all the treasures (2:3), and the immediate use of this against a rival argument (2:4).
- **Canonical register:** the Pauline "hidden … now revealed" sequence (Rom 16:25–26; 1 Cor 2:7; Eph 3:9) and the Ketuvim's wisdom and mystery texts (Proverbs 2; Daniel 2).

#### The first hearers

- **Who they were:** a largely Gentile church (1:21, 27; 2:13) `[I]`, taught by Epaphras, one of their own (1:7; 4:12) `[T]`, who had never seen Paul (2:1) `[I]`.
- **Their situation:** a church in good order (2:5) `[T]`, with a teaching in the air that offered "philosophy" (2:8) and the appearance of wisdom (2:23) `[T]`.
- **What they brought:** pagan religious vocabulary in which "mystery" and "initiation" meant private access to the divine `[S]`; possibly the suspicion that what Epaphras taught them was a beginner's version `[I]`.

#### Where the passage fits their world

1. A stranger, an apostle in chains, tells them he suffers *for them* (1:24) and struggles *for them* (2:1). For a church taught by a lesser figure, that is a bond of belonging to the whole mission `[I]`.
2. "Among the Gentiles … Christ in you", the "you" plural (1:27), tells former pagans that God's secret was meant for them — not for an inner circle, not for Israel only `[I]`.
3. "All the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (2:3) removes the rival's offer before it is named `[I]`.

#### Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances

- **Shocking:** "I rejoice in my sufferings" (1:24) — a prisoner who calls his chains joy; and "Christ's afflictions", which joins the exalted Lord of 1:15–20 to suffering that continues `[I]`.
- **Surprising:** the "mystery" is told to every man, and its content is a person `[I]`.
- **Comforting:** Paul knows about them, labours for them, sees their order and rejoices (2:1, 5) `[T]`. They are named in the apostle's agony.
- **Disturbing:** "so that no one will delude you" (2:4) — the first hint that they might be talked out of what they have `[T]`.

#### What we bring that they didn't

- *Individualism:* we hear "Christ in you" as a private inward experience; they heard "you" plural, and "among the Gentiles".
- *The modern meaning of "mystery":* we hear "an unsolved puzzle", and either despair of knowing or turn the gospel into vague spirituality.
- *A professionalised ministry:* we may hear 1:24–29 as a description of the clergy, and the congregation as spectators; the letter itself hands the ministry of 1:28 to the whole church at 3:16 `[T]`.

#### Candidate Fallen Condition Focus

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **What they felt** | The pull to think that what they had received plainly — from Epaphras, not from Paul — was not the whole, and that the "persuasive" teacher offered depths beyond it (2:4, 8, 23) `[T]`/`[I]` |
| **Candidate FCF (shared concern)** | We suspect that the Christ we were given is the beginning of the spiritual life rather than its whole content, and so we are open to anyone who promises something more |
| **Shared / differs** | Shared: the appetite for a hidden "next level" and the appeal of an impressive speaker. Differs: their "more" came in the clothing of angels, visions and regimens (2:16–23); ours comes in techniques, experiences and systems |
| **Confidence** | Anchored (2:2–4 state the threat and its answer); the "beginner's version" suspicion is inferred |

#### Implication for the sermon

Recover the joy and the bond: this is a letter from a suffering man to strangers he loves. Recover the openness of the mystery: God has told his secret, to everyone, and it is Christ. Then the warning (2:4) lands as a friend's care, not as a scolding.

### Biblical-Theological Themes

#### Mystery and revelation

- **Where this passage sits:** the disclosure stage. God's purpose, hidden "from the ages and generations", is "now manifested" (1:26).
- **Before:** the God who "reveals mysteries" to Daniel (Dan 2:28) — a purpose for the ages concerning his kingdom, beyond the wise of the nations; the prophets' longing (1 Pet 1:10–12) `[S]`.
- **After:** the mystery proclaimed (1:28; 4:3), the believers' life hidden and to be manifested in glory (3:3–4).
- **What it contributes:** the gospel is not one religious secret among many but the disclosure of God's hidden plan for history — and its content is Christ among the Gentiles.
- **Confidence:** high (on the Pauline pattern); moderate (on Daniel as source).

#### Wisdom sought and found

- **Where this passage sits:** fulfilment. The wisdom Proverbs tells the son to seek "as hidden treasures" (Prov 2:4) is located in Christ (2:3).
- **Before:** Prov 2:1–6; Job 28 ("where shall wisdom be found?"); Dan 2:20–23 (wisdom belongs to God, who gives it).
- **After:** "Christ … the wisdom of God" (1 Cor 1:24, 30) `[T]`; the church teaching "in all wisdom" (3:16).
- **What it contributes:** the rival offers wisdom (2:23); the canon has been asking where wisdom is; the answer is a person.
- **Confidence:** high (theme); moderate (Proverbs 2 as source, audited; Wisdom 6–7 a co-candidate `[S: audit 1]`).

#### The suffering of God's servants for God's people

- **Where this passage sits:** the apostolic stage. The servant's labour (1:29; cf. Isa 49:4 `[S]`), Paul's "afflictions for you" (Eph 3:13; 2 Cor 1:6), the risen Christ's identification with his persecuted church (Acts 9:4–5, "why are you persecuting Me?") `[T]`.
- **Before:** the Servant who labours for the nations (Isa 49:1–6) `[S]`; the prophets who suffered (Jer 20).
- **After:** the church filling up the measure (Rev 6:11, the number of martyrs "completed") `[S]` recalled.
- **What it contributes:** "Christ's afflictions" are the sufferings Christ undergoes *in his body* in the present age — and the apostle takes on a share for the church's sake.
- **v1.3 — Dunn's Servant frame.** Dunn holds that Paul understood his mission as completing that of the Servant of Isaiah, and reads 1:24 in that light (NIGTC, 114–117, n. 13) `[S: Logos 2]`. His evidence checks out: Rom 15:21 quotes Isa 52:15 and 2 Cor 6:2 quotes Isa 49:8 word for word, and Gal 1:15 shares ἐκ κοιλίας μητρός μου ("from my mother's womb") with Isa 49:1 (Swete; SBLGNT) `[T]`. Within Col 1:24–29, though, the only word shared with Isaiah 49 is κοπιάω ("labour", 1:29; Isa 49:4) `[T]`. The Servant frame is Pauline background, not a contact in this passage `[I]`. Queued for the claim audit as **C23**, low–moderate. (Dunn's "deutero-Isaiah" is his term.)
- **Confidence:** moderate.

**Confluence — climactic convergence.** The three themes meet in one claim: the hidden wisdom of God (themes 1 and 2) is a person, Christ, and it reaches the nations through a servant who suffers for them (theme 3). The passage's own order shows it: suffering (1:24) → stewardship of the mystery (1:25–27) → wisdom proclaimed (1:28) → all the treasures in Christ (2:3).

### Christological Reading

*NT passage:* Christ is named in five of its eleven verses (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Four Christological claims are made:

1. **Christ is the content of the mystery** — "Christ in you" (1:27); "God's mystery, Christ" (2:2). Not a doctrine about him, but he himself `[T]`.
2. **Christ is the treasury** — "in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (2:3). The confession's "in Him" (1:16, 17, 19) is now the location of all wisdom `[T]`.
3. **Christ suffers in his body** — "the afflictions of Christ … for the sake of His body, which is the church" (1:24) `[T]`. The risen Lord who reconciled all things (1:20) still shares his people's afflictions `[I]`.
4. **Christ's power works in his servant** — "according to His working, which works mightily within me" (1:29); that working is the power that raised him from the dead (2:12) `[T]`/`[I]`.

**Typology in view:** none claimed. Daniel 2 supplies a revelation-pattern, not a type.

**Moralism check.**

- *The "be like X" temptation:* "Be like Paul — rejoice in suffering, labour harder, strive in prayer." The passage does describe things that become patterns (Copycat). But the passage's subject is what Christ has done through Paul *for them*.
- *The gospel grounding:* the mystery has been manifested; Christ is in them; the power is his; the goal is that they be presented complete "in Christ" (1:28), which is God's work before it is Paul's (1:22).
- *Christ as hero:* the minister labours "according to His working" (1:29). Paul is the instrument, not the protagonist.

**Confidence:** high on the four claims as stated by the text; moderate on the "Christ suffers in his body" reading of 1:24.

### Difficult / Contested Verses

#### 1:24 — "filling up what is lacking in Christ's afflictions"

- **Category:** doctrinal (the sufficiency of Christ's work); weaponised (the treasury of merit; Christian suffering as meritorious); apologetic (does Paul think Christ's work incomplete?).
- **The difficulty:** read alone, the verse appears to say that something was lacking in Christ's sufferings, which Paul supplies.
- **Common misreadings:** (a) Christ's atonement was insufficient; (b) the saints' sufferings add to a store of merit that can be applied to others `[S]`; (c) a hurried denial — "it can't mean that" — without showing what it does mean.
- **Rhetorical function in context:** the verse opens Paul's self-presentation as the church's minister. It is the counterpart to 1:22 and gives the ground of his claim on them: *I suffer for you*. Its function is relational and ministerial, not soteriological `[I]`, high. Whatever "what is lacking" means, it must fit a verse whose job is to bind the readers to the minister, written after the letter has said in finished terms that Christ reconciled all things "through the blood of His cross" (1:20) and "in the body of His flesh through death" (1:22) `[T]`.
- **The main interpretive options** `[S]` (v1.0 recalled them; v1.1 adds the names the Logos round supplied `[S: Logos 1]` — Moo, Lightfoot, Harris and Dunn were asked for but not reported). The commentators' own taxonomy has three readings of "Christ's afflictions": **Christ's sufferings on the cross** (Hendriksen; Wright, as summarised — **v1.3: Wright's own text does not bear this out**; see below); **believers' afflictions for Christ's sake** (Bruce); **the sufferings of believers before Christ's return** (O'Brien). A cross reading is not an atonement-deficiency reading — these commentators do not, as far as reported, make the atonement incomplete — but it is weakened by θλῖψις, never used of the passion elsewhere (Tool 7). Check how Hendriksen and Wright construe "lacking" before citing them. Mapped onto this dig's list:
  1. **The messianic woes:** a measure of affliction appointed for the Messiah's people before the end; Paul takes on a share so that less falls on the church (the eschatological reading; O'Brien).
  2. **Christ suffering in his body:** the risen Christ so identifies with his church that its sufferings are "his" (Acts 9:4); Paul's sufferings fill up what remains of those afflictions in the present age.
  3. **What is lacking in Paul's own share:** the afflictions "of Christ" are sufferings *for* Christ, and Paul fills up what is still lacking in his own portion.
  4. **The absence reading** (the 1 Cor 16:17 / Phil 2:30 idiom): the Colossians have not had Paul in person; his sufferings "for you" supply in his flesh what his absence leaves lacking to them — the ministry of the body. **v1.1:** Garland holds a version of it — Paul represents Christ's suffering bodily presence, now absent — and Beale criticises it (Tool 7) `[S: Logos 1]`.
  5. **The treasury reading:** the saints' sufferings supplement Christ's satisfaction and become available to others (the medieval and later Roman Catholic use) `[S]`.
- **v1.3 — the commentaries in full** `[S: Logos 2]`. **Wright is not a cross reading.** He says that Paul "is not adding to the achievement of Calvary" and that θλῖψις "is never, in fact, used of the cross", and reads the verse through corporate Christology and the messianic woes — options 1 and 2 together. The fixed quota is "not provable", and Paul may be "drawing the enemy's fire on to himself" (TNTC, 91–93). **O'Brien** (*New Bible Commentary*, 1267 — his one-volume commentary; his WBC is not in Patrick's library): the afflictions are "not his death on the cross … but the afflictions of his people which he endures", with a quota limited by God; "the more he suffers the less the Colossians have to" — options 1 and 2 again. **Dunn** (NIGTC, 114–117): Christ's sufferings as the eschatological tribulation, believers' participation in his death, an appointed sum of suffering, and Paul's mission as the Servant's. He reports Lightfoot — "the afflictions … which Christ endured" is "the only natural interpretation" — and a near-consensus that Christ's vicarious sufferings are not in view. **Every commentary in hand takes options 1 and 2 together; none takes option 4.** Dunn also finds this dig's 1:22 → 1:24 crossing for himself: "a degree of continuity between Christ's body of flesh and Paul's flesh for Christ's body". Moo, Lightfoot, Harris and Hendriksen are not in Patrick's library; Hendriksen stays as summarised in round 1. Dunn's further step — that the verse implies Paul was alive, and that the letter was written "not by him, but with his approval" — is his authorship argument, not something the verse settles `[I]`.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the text rules out any reading in which the atonement is incomplete — by the word chosen (θλῖψις, never of the cross), by the letter's finished verbs (1:20, 22), and by the letter's grammar of ὑπέρ (ministers for churches, never Christ's death) `[T]`. Option 5 cannot stand on the Greek. Options 1 and 2 are both compatible with the words, and they are not exclusive: the afflictions of the Messiah's age are Christ's because he shares them with his body `[I]`. Option 4 is the reading this dig's lexical work makes most visible — the idiom elsewhere is about supplying for absence, and the passage ends with Paul's absence in the flesh (2:5) — and v1.1 finds it held by Garland, criticised by Beale, and closest of all the readings to the syntax of Phil 2:30 (Tool 7) `[I]`, moderate. Option 3 is weakest: it has to take "of Christ" as "for Christ" `[I]`. **v1.3:** options 1 and 2 together are the reading of all three commentaries in hand, and they fit θλῖψις's eschatological line (Dan 12:1 → Mark 13:19; Tool 7) `[T]`/`[S: Logos 2]`. Option 4 keeps the best support at the level of idiom, but it has one proponent. *Slight lean to 1 + 2 as the frame, moderate; option 4 still live.*
- **Suggested handling for the preacher:** **address directly**, in the sermon, briefly and positively. Say what it cannot mean (and why — 1:20, 22); then say what it does: Paul's sufferings are for the church, which is Christ's body, and Christ shares the afflictions of his body. Do not make the sermon a lecture on the options. *Confidence:* high that it does not concern the atonement; moderate on the positive reading.

#### 1:27 — "Christ in you"

- **Category:** commonly misused.
- **The difficulty:** read as individual mystical indwelling divorced from the verse's frame ("among the Gentiles").
- **Rhetorical function in context:** the climax of the mystery's definition — what is surprising is *where* Christ is: among the Gentiles `[I]`.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** ἐν ὑμῖν can be "in you" or "among you" (plural). v1.0 leaned to "among", on the verse's own ἐν τοῖς ἔθνεσιν ("among the Gentiles"). **v1.1 reverses the lean.** Wright prefers "in you" on the model of Rom 8:10, where indwelling guarantees resurrection; Dunn argues that "among you" would add hardly anything to "among the nations" and weaken the train of thought (Wright, TNTC, 96; Dunn, NIGTC, 122–123) `[S: Logos 1]`. The corpus supports Wright's parallel: Rom 8:10–11 joins Χριστὸς ἐν ὑμῖν (plural) to the future life of "your mortal bodies", as Col 1:27 joins it to "the hope of glory" (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Dunn's Stoic and Wisdom background, and his "(each of) you", are his own additions `[S]`; the ὑμῖν stays plural `[T]`. *"In you", moderate.* The point is still corporate: Christ indwelling the Gentile church. **v1.3:** Vincent leaves it open, citing Eph 3:17; 2 Cor 13:5; Gal 4:19 `[S: Logos 2]`. Of these, Gal 4:19 (μορφωθῇ Χριστὸς ἐν ὑμῖν, "until Christ is formed in you") is plainly "in"; 2 Cor 13:5 is the same open phrase as 1:27 `[T]`/`[I]`. *Lean unchanged.*
- **Suggested handling:** address briefly — preserve the plural.

#### 2:1 — who "have not seen my face"?

- **Category:** minor exegetical.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the "their hearts" of 2:2 gathers all three groups, so the Colossians are among them `[I]`, high.
- **Suggested handling:** sidestep with a note.

#### 1:28 — "every man … complete"

- **Category:** possible misuse (universalism; perfectionism).
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** "every man" is the scope of *proclamation*, not a promise of universal salvation; "complete" (τέλειος) is the goal of presentation at the end (cf. 1:22), not a present state of sinlessness `[I]`, high.
- **Suggested handling:** acknowledge in passing if the congregation is likely to hear it otherwise.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: 1:24 as a deficient atonement — or explained away in a sentence

- **What it looks like:** "Paul says Christ's sufferings were not enough", or the opposite, a hurried "of course it doesn't mean that" with nothing said about what it does mean.
- **Why it's wrong:** the letter has just said the reconciliation is finished (1:20, 22). The word for "afflictions" is never used of the cross. And Colossians uses "on behalf of" only for ministers labouring for churches. But the verse is not empty: it says something true and costly about the church's sufferings and the minister's.
- **The corrective:** say what it cannot mean and why, in one breath; then preach what it does mean — Christ shares his people's afflictions, and Paul bears a share *for* the church. Use 1:22 as the key: Christ reconciled in the body of his flesh; Paul suffers in his flesh for Christ's body.

### Pitfall: "Christ in you" as a private spiritual experience

- **What it looks like:** a sermon on the indwelling Christ in the individual heart, using 1:27 as a slogan.
- **Why it's wrong:** the verse is about the *Gentiles*: "this mystery among the Gentiles, which is Christ in you" — and the "you" is plural. What is astonishing is not that Christ lives in a heart but that God's long-hidden purpose has reached the nations.
- **The corrective:** preach the plural. The mystery is Christ in the midst of a former-pagan congregation, their hope of glory (3:4).

### Pitfall: "Mystery" preached as "something we cannot understand" — or as secret depth for the advanced

- **What it looks like:** "God's ways are a mystery", or "there are deeper truths for the mature".
- **Why it's wrong:** the mystery has been "manifested" (1:26), "made known" (1:27), "proclaimed" to "every man" (1:28). It is a secret God has told, to everyone.
- **The corrective:** define the word as Paul does — a purpose hidden and now disclosed — and give its content: Christ.

### Pitfall: Paul the hero, or the preacher as Paul

- **What it looks like:** a sermon on the heroic sufferings of the apostle, or a minister applying 1:24–29 to himself ("this is what ministry costs me").
- **Why it's wrong:** the stewardship was given "to me for you" (1:25); the congregation is the "you". The labour is "according to His working" (1:29). And the passage's method of ministry is handed to the whole church at 3:16.
- **The corrective:** let the congregation hear themselves as the people for whom Paul suffered, and then hear 3:16 — they are now to admonish and teach one another in all wisdom.

### Pitfall: 2:4–5 as a scolding of a failing church

- **What it looks like:** "The Colossians were being led astray, and Paul is warning them."
- **Why it's wrong:** Paul rejoices "to see your good discipline and the stability of your faith" (2:5). The warning is preventative.
- **The corrective:** preach the warning as a friend's care for a church in good order — and so as a word for a healthy congregation, not only for a troubled one.

## Appendix — Candidate Toolkit Amendment (drafted, not applied)

**Standing instruction (10 September 2026): a method lesson from a run is drafted into the skill as concrete edits; Patrick decides whether to apply.**

**The lesson.** The scan tool's New Testament mode accepts a passage within one chapter only. A preaching unit that crosses a chapter boundary — Col 1:24–2:5 here; 3:18–4:1 later in this sweep, and many others in the NT — cannot be scanned as one passage without a work-round. Two half-passage scans would rank partners against half the passage's words, which is a different test.

**The edit.** In the folder's scan tool, let the New Testament passage specification accept a range of the form *book, chapter:verse to chapter:verse* (for example, Colossians 1:24 to 2:5), by comparing chapter-and-verse pairs rather than verses within one chapter. One small change to the function that collects the passage's words; no change to the six runs, the stems or the sense check. **Positive control after the change:** the 1:15–20 result must be reproduced exactly (Psalm 88 first in all six runs), and a cross-chapter run on 1:24–2:5 must reproduce this dig's table.

**Where it goes.** The tool itself, and one line in the dig-deeper skill's external-data section beside the New Testament scan, noting that a cross-chapter range is accepted.
