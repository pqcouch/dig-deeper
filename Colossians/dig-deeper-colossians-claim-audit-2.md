# Claim Audit 2: Colossians

**Passages audited:** Col 1:19; 1:24–2:5; 2:16–23; 3:12–16
**Date:** 27 October 2026
**Version:** 1.0
**Purpose:** The second claim audit on Colossians. It covers the whole queue, thirteen candidates (C16–C28). C16–C22 are the new candidates found by claim audit 1. C23 was queued from the second Logos round (Dunn's Servant frame). C24–C28 were queued by the 2:16–23 dig. Trigger 1 had fired: thirteen items were queued against a threshold of about ten.
**Primary texts:** SBLGNT with its lemma index (counts and parsing); NA28 and its apparatus (Logos export) for Colossians; Swete's LXX, searched both by word-beginning and by stem anywhere in the word, with every hit read for sense; Rahlfs (Logos exports) for Exodus, Psalms, Proverbs, Isaiah, Haggai and both Greek texts of Daniel; the WLC with its lemma index for the Hebrew. **There is no Rahlfs text of Wisdom, Sirach or the Maccabees in the corpus.** Every finding in those books rests on Swete alone, and the report says so where it matters.
**Method:**
- Two blind auditors, run one after the other (Patrick's two-auditor rule of 16 October 2026).

- Each auditor read a brief, a claims file and the corpus only. Neither saw the digs, the overview, the first audit, the Logos answers or the other auditor's report.
- The main session then reproduced the results each verdict rests on (see *Spot checks*).

## Positional Frame (Phase 0.6)

**What the letter has set up.** The confession (1:15–20) said who the Son is. The apostle's labour (1:24–2:5) said how that confession reached the Colossians: it is a mystery once hidden, now disclosed, with every treasure of wisdom and knowledge hidden in Christ (2:3). The charge (2:6–15) turned it into a command and a ground. The polemic (2:16–23) then measures the rival regulations against that ground `[T]`. The claims in this queue all bear on three places in that movement:
- the treasury of 2:3 (C16, C17, C20, C21);
- the dwelling of 1:19 (C18, C22);
- the polemic of 2:16–23 and its answer in 3:12–16 (C19, C24–C28).

C23 bears on Paul's labour at 1:24–29.

**Why the frame matters for the verdicts.** Colossians quotes nothing with a formula. The one secure Old Testament wording in it is the Isaiah 29:13 phrase at 2:22 `[S: audit 1]`. The rest of the letter's argument runs on its own internal repetitions `[T]`. Audit 1 set the test that follows from this, and this audit keeps it: an external source must add something the letter's own chains do not already supply. Two verdicts below turn on it:
- C24: the 2:8 frame explains the turn to "wisdom" at 2:23 without Isaiah 29:14.
- C25: 3:16's closest echo is 1:28, not 2:23.

## Claims Audited

### Claim C16: Wisdom 6:22–7:17 as a field partner of Col 1:26–2:3 (and 1:29)

**Source:** claim audit 1, New Candidates (found by both of its auditors independently); the 1:24–2:5 dig, Tool 11 rivals (v1.2) `[S: audit 1]`.

**Claim:** Wis 6:22 is the only Swete verse joining μυστήρια ("mysteries"), a hiding word and σοφία ("wisdom"). Wis 7:13–14 says "I do not hide her wealth … she is an unfailing treasure". ἐνέργεια ("working") occurs at Wis 7:17 and 7:26. The scan ranks Wisdom 6–7 at a median of about 5 against 1:24–29. The claim is that Wisdom 6–7 is a co-candidate field with Proverbs 2, rated moderate.

**Findings (Auditor B):**
- **The verse counts hold exactly** (Swete).
  - Wis 6:22 is the only verse joining μυστήρια, a hiding word and σοφία. The positive control finds the hiding root with μυστήριον at Wis 14:23, Tob 12:7 and 12:11, and Dan 2:47.
  - Wis 7:13 is the only verse joining πλοῦτος ("wealth") with a hiding word.
  - ἐνέργεια occurs at Wis 7:17 and 7:26 `[T]`.
- **The hiding is negated in both Wisdom verses.** Wis 6:22 reads οὐκ ἀποκρύψω ὑμῖν μυστήρια ("I will not hide mysteries from you"), and Wis 7:13 reads τὸν πλοῦτον αὐτῆς οὐκ ἀποκρύπτομαι ("I do not hide her wealth"). In both, the author is promising to be open. Colossians says the opposite: the mystery "had been hidden from the ages … but now has been revealed" (1:26) `[T]`. The words are shared; the hidden-then-revealed shape is not.
- **The ranks belong to Wisdom 7, not Wisdom 6.**
  - Against 1:24–29, Wisdom 7 ranks 2, 5, 3, 6, 4 and 10 across the six runs (median 5). This was reproduced by the main session.
  - Against the claim's own span, 1:26–2:3, it ranks first in five runs and third in the sixth.
  - Wisdom 6 is weak as a chapter: median 31 against 1:24–29, and 24 against 1:26–2:3.
- **Leave-one-out.**
  - Wisdom 7 does not move without μυστήριον and σοφία, even with both removed together.
  - **Without the hiding word, it falls from 1 to 14** against 1:26–2:3, and from 5 to 29 against 1:24–29.
  - Wisdom 6 falls from 24 to 62 without μυστήριον.

  So the field result for Wisdom 7 rests on a hiding word that Wisdom negates.
- **Null.**
  - "A Wisdom chapter in the top ten" occurs for 43 per cent of random six-verse Pauline passages (52 of 120). It proves nothing by itself.
  - Wisdom 7 in particular is rare: median 5 or better in 5 of 120 random six-verse passages, and median 1 in 1 of 120 random nine-verse passages.
- **Rivals.** Two chapters beat the null as clearly as Wisdom 7 against 1:26–2:3:
  - **Theodotion's Daniel 2: median 2.** It carries the revelatory shape: "He reveals deep and hidden things" (2:22), and "revealing mysteries … he made known" (2:28).
  - **Proverbs 2: median 7.** No random passage of 120 reached it. It carries the "treasures of wisdom and knowledge" vocabulary.
  - Sirach 24 is a setting artefact (high on whole chapters, 90–125 in every window run).

**Hays table**

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Possible | Pre-Pauline `[S]`; Swete only |
| Volume | Possible | Wisdom 7 tops the scan and is rare in the null, but its decisive word is negated and no phrase is shared |
| Recurrence | Possible | Wis 7:26 has εἰκών ("image"; cf. 1:15) |
| Thematic coherence | Possible | Wealth and treasure fit 1:27 and 2:3; open teaching does not fit hidden-then-revealed |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Wisdom readings of Colossians are established `[S]` |
| History of interpretation | Possible `[S]` | Not yet put to Logos |
| Satisfaction | Possible | No better than Daniel 2 or Proverbs 2 |

**Verdict:** **Needs reframing.** **Moderate for Wisdom 7 as one of three field partners** (with Theodotion's Daniel 2 and Proverbs 2); **low–moderate for Wis 6:22** specifically.

**Reasoning:** The field result is real and rare in the null, but it belongs to Wisdom 7. It rests on a hiding word used in the opposite sense to Colossians. Daniel 2 supplies the shape that Wisdom lacks, and Proverbs 2 supplies the vocabulary. Wisdom 7's distinctive contribution is wealth and treasure (1:27; 2:3), and στοιχεῖα (see New Candidates, C29).

**Book-overview action required:** In the proposed new row for Prov 2:1–6 (audit 1, revision 1), name the field as **Proverbs 2 · Theodotion Daniel 2 · Wisdom 7**, and drop "Wis 6:22–7:17" as a unit. In the 1:24–2:5 dig, Tool 11 rivals, replace "Wis 6:22–7:17 co-candidate" with "Wisdom 7 (wealth, treasure; its hiding negated)".

### Claim C17: Isaiah 33:6 at Col 2:3

**Source:** claim audit 1, New Candidates (Auditor A).

**Claim:** אוֹצָר ("treasure") with חָכְמָה ("wisdom") and דַּעַת ("knowledge") stands in one Hebrew verse only, Isa 33:6. The Greek keeps treasure and wisdom. Rated low–moderate.

**Findings (Auditor A):**
- **The count holds** (WLC lemma index; reproduced by the main session).
- **The sense is looser than the triad suggests.** The verse reads "a wealth of salvation, wisdom and knowledge; the fear of YHWH is his treasure" `[T]`. The treasure is the fear of YHWH, not wisdom and knowledge.
- **The Greek** (Swete; Rahlfs agrees in substance) reads ἐν θησαυροῖς ἡ σωτηρία ἡμῶν … σοφία καὶ ἐπιστήμη … θησαυροὶ δικαιοσύνης ("in treasures is our salvation … wisdom and understanding … treasures of righteousness"). The treasures belong to salvation and righteousness, and the knowledge-word is ἐπιστήμη, not γνῶσις `[T]`. One small difference: Swete reads ἥκει ("comes") where Rahlfs reads ἐκεῖ ("there").
- **Rival.** Proverbs 2 is closer in both languages:
  - The Greek of Prov 2:1–6 holds κρύψῃς ("you treasure up"), σοφία, σύνεσις ("understanding"), θησαυρούς ("treasures"), ἐπίγνωσιν θεοῦ ("knowledge of God") and γνῶσις ("knowledge").
  - **The WLC window Prov 2:4–6 is the only place where מַטְמוֹן ("hidden treasure"), חָכְמָה and דַּעַת fall within two verses** (reproduced).
  - In the scan against 2:2–3, Proverbs 2 has a median of 7 and Isaiah 33 a median of 19.

**Hays table**

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Isaiah |
| Volume | Weak | The Greek shares treasure and wisdom only; the triad is Hebrew |
| Recurrence | Weak | No other Pauline use of Isa 33:6 |
| Thematic coherence | Possible | Treasure, wisdom and salvation in Zion |
| Historical plausibility | Weak | Paul would have to read the Hebrew against a Greek that loses it |
| History of interpretation | Possible `[S]` | Not yet put to Logos |
| Satisfaction | Weak | Prov 2:1–6 accounts for the Greek of 2:2–3 far better |

**Verdict:** **Needs reframing.** **Low** as a standalone contact; a minor member of the treasury-of-wisdom idiom.

**Reasoning:** The Hebrew uniqueness is real but rests on a looser sense than the claim implies, and it disappears in the Greek that Colossians uses.

**Book-overview action required:** Intertextual Map row 5 (the treasury idiom): keep Isa 33:6 in the list, but last, and add "(Hebrew triad; in Greek the treasures are salvation's)".

### Claim C18: The Zion-dwelling companions to Psalm 68 at Col 1:19

**Source:** claim audit 1, New Candidates (Auditor A); C1's reasoning in audit 1.

**Claim:** The temple function of 1:19 belongs to a Zion-dwelling tradition: Hag 1:8, 3 Macc 2:15–16 and Ps 131:13–14 (Hebrew 132:13–14). The claim is moderate as a tradition and is to be folded into the Ps 68 claim (C1).

**Findings (Auditor A):**
- **The accepted contact holds.** εὐδοκέω ("be pleased") with κατοικέω ("dwell") in one verse: Ps 67:17 only (Swete; Rahlfs agrees). The Hebrew of Ps 68:17 uses חָמַד ("desire"). It is the only WLC verse joining a desire verb to both ישב and שכן ("dwell") `[T]`.
- **The named companions are thematic, not verbal.**
  - Hag 1:8 has a future tense and no dwelling verb (εὐδοκήσω ἐν αὐτῷ, "I will be pleased with it").
  - Ps 131:13–14 uses ἐκλέγομαι ("choose") and αἱρετίζω ("prefer"), not εὐδοκέω.
- **A correction to 3 Macc 2:15–16** (Swete only). The κατοικητήριον ("dwelling place") of 2:15 is **heaven**, "unreachable by men". It is set against the earthly sanctuary, not used as a word for it. 2:16 has εὐδόκησας ("you were pleased") with an accusative and no infinitive `[T]`. The theme is close to Col 1:19–20: heaven's God choosing to place his glory on earth.
- **A member the claim missed: 2 Macc 14:35** (Swete only). It reads ηὐδόκησας ναὸν τῆς σῆς σκηνώσεως ἐν ἡμῖν γενέσθαι ("you were pleased that a temple of your dwelling should be among us"): aorist εὐδοκέω, an infinitive, a dwelling noun and ἐν ("among") `[T]`. After Ps 67:17 it is the nearest construction to Col 1:19 in Swete. This was reproduced by the main session.
- **Recurrence.** Eph 4:8 quotes Ps 67:19, two verses after 67:17. Eph 4:10 glosses it with "that he might fill all things", and Eph 1:23 has τὸ πλήρωμα ("the fullness") (SBLGNT) `[T]`. A closely related letter therefore reads Psalm 68 together with fullness language. Its authorship is debated `[S]`.
- **The Hebrew tradition.** It is the Deuteronomic chosen-place formula: בחר ("choose") with שכן ("make dwell"), "the place the LORD will choose to make His name dwell" (Deut 12:11; 14:23; 16:2; 16:6; 16:11; 26:2; Neh 1:9, WLC). The LXX renders it "for His name to be called there", so the Greek reader loses the indwelling `[T]`.

**Hays table**

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Psalms, Haggai, Maccabees |
| Volume | Possible | Ps 67:17 strong; 2 Macc 14:35 possible; the named three weak verbally |
| Recurrence | Possible | Ps 68 at Eph 4:8–10 with fullness language |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | God choosing where his presence will dwell; 1:20's reconciliation of heaven and earth |
| Historical plausibility | Strong | Temple Christology in Paul `[S]` |
| History of interpretation | Possible `[S]` | Not yet put to Logos; C1's own history is on the round-3 sheet (3.2) |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Explains εὐδόκησεν as an idiom for divine choice of dwelling |

**Verdict:** **Confirmed with nuance.** **Moderate as a tradition**, folded into C1.

**Reasoning:** The tradition supports the temple reading of 1:19, and so supports C1's *function* rating. It also confirms what audit 1 found: Ps 67:17 keeps its place by construction, not by theme. 2 Macc 14:35 is the nearest rival. Three changes are needed:
1. Add 2 Macc 14:35.
2. Correct 3 Macc 2:15.
3. Call Hag 1:8 and Ps 131:13–14 thematic members.

**Book-overview action required:** Intertextual Map row 4 (Ps 68 at 1:19): name the companions as "2 Macc 14:35 (nearest construction); 3 Macc 2:16; Ps 131:13–14; Hag 1:8 (thematic)", and add Eph 4:8–10 as recurrence. In the 1:15–20 dig, Tool 11 (Ps 68), make the same change.

### Claim C19: The adjudication frame 2:16, 18 → 3:15, 16

**Source:** claim audit 1, New Candidates (folds into C4); the 2:16–23 dig, **Headline 3**.

**Claim:** "Let no one judge you" (2:16), "let no one disqualify you" (2:18), "let the peace of Christ rule" (3:15) and "let the word of Christ dwell" (3:16) are the only four third-person imperatives in Colossians. Two are negative with human adjudicators, and two are positive with subjects "of Christ". καταβραβεύω and βραβεύω each occur once in the NT. The claim is that this is a designed frame, rated moderate–high.

**Findings (Auditor B):**
- **The count holds exactly.** Colossians has thirty imperatives. The four third-person forms are κρινέτω (2:16), καταβραβευέτω (2:18), βραβευέτω (3:15) and ἐνοικείτω (3:16) (SBLGNT parse; reproduced) `[T]`.
- καταβραβεύω and βραβεύω each occur once in the NT. The βραβ- root occurs twice in Swete, both times in augmented forms that only a stem-anywhere search finds: ἐβράβευσεν (Wis 10:12) and συνεβράβευσαν (1 Esd 9:14) `[T]`.
- **ἐνοικέω ("dwell in") is not an adjudication verb.** Its NT uses (Rom 8:11; 2 Cor 6:16; Col 3:16; 2 Tim 1:5, 14) are all about indwelling. Inside Colossians it goes with κατοικέω, used of the fullness at 1:19 and 2:9 `[T]`. **The adjudication frame proper is three verbs, not four.**
- **The negative side belongs to a longer series.** The "no one … you" warning occurs four times in quick succession, in three moods `[T]`:
  - 2:4, "so that no one deludes you" (subjunctive);
  - 2:8, "see that no one takes you captive" (future indicative);
  - 2:16, "let no one judge you" (imperative);
  - 2:18, "let no one disqualify you" (imperative).

  Elsewhere in the NT the formula occurs only at Matt 24:4, Mark 13:5, Eph 5:6 and 2 Thess 2:3 `[T]`. Colossians holds four of about eight. Only the last two happen to be imperatives.
- **Baseline.** Four third-person imperatives is unremarkable for a letter of 95 verses (Galatians has 5 in 149 verses, and Ephesians 8 in 155). Six other Pauline letters mix negative and positive third-person imperatives. The nearest analogue is **Rom 14:3–5**:
  - it concerns the same food-and-days dispute;
  - it opens with a negative pair, "let him not despise … let him not judge" (μὴ κρινέτω, the same verb);
  - a positive follows, "let each be fully convinced" (14:5) `[T]`.

  Rom 6:12, "let not sin reign", is the negative counterpart of an abstract power ruling in the person (3:15) `[T]`.
- **What is distinctive is lexical.** The compound καταβραβεύω ("rule against") at 2:18 is answered by the simple βραβεύω ("rule") at 3:15. This compound-against-simple play is in character for Paul: compare διακρίνω, κρίνω and κατακρίνω ("discern", "judge", "condemn") at 1 Cor 11:29–32 `[T]`.

**Design table**

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Counts verified | Yes: four third-person imperatives; each βραβ- verb once in the NT |
| Baseline | A negative pair followed by positives is in Rom 14 (same dispute, same verb) and Eph 4–5; a set of four defined by mood is a small-number artefact |
| Sense | κρίνω, καταβραβεύω and βραβεύω judge; ἐνοικέω indwells |
| Alternative explanation | Paul's warning formula (2:4, 8, 16, 18) and his habit of playing a compound against its simple verb |
| History of interpretation | `[S]` — C4's history is pending Logos round 3 (question 3.5) |

**Verdict:** **Needs reframing.** **Moderate–high for the καταβραβεύω → βραβεύω antithesis** (C4 unchanged); **moderate for the four-part frame.**

**Reasoning:** Audit 1 raised C4 partly on "the exact two-against-two imperative frame" `[S: audit 1]`. The rarity of the two verbs and the compound-to-simple answer still carry C4 at moderate–high. But the frame of four is a grammatical coincidence: the negative side is a four-clause warning series in three moods, and 3:16's verb indwells rather than judges. Preach it as "the one who disqualifies you is replaced by Christ's peace as umpire" (2:18 → 3:15), and drop the symmetry of four.

**Pulpit note (ESV).** The congregation will hear "Let no one disqualify you" (2:18) and "let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts" (3:15). The NASB95 has "keep defrauding you of your prize" and "rule". Neither English version lets the hearer hear the same verb twice. The preacher has to supply the link.

**Book-overview action required:** Echo Table #17 (the umpire pair, moderate–high): keep the rating, replace "the four third-person imperatives" with the compound/simple antithesis, and add Rom 14:3–5 as the Pauline analogue. **The 2:16–23 dig, Headline 3, needs a patch:** see *Confidence Change Propagation*.

### Claim C20: Isaiah 45:3, 15 in Hebrew — a סתר ("hide") frame around 45:14

**Source:** claim audit 1, New Candidates (Auditor A).

**Claim:** In the Hebrew, Isa 45:3 (מִסְתָּרִים, "secret places") and 45:15 (אֵל מִסְתַּתֵּר, "a God who hides Himself") frame 45:14, "God is in you". This would parallel Col 1:26–27, where "Christ in you" is framed by a hidden mystery. The LXX does not keep 45:15. Rated low, record only.

**Findings (Auditor A):**
- **The lemmas are correct** (WLC) `[T]`.
- **The frame is not a frame.** The root סתר occurs a third time in the chapter, *after* 45:14: לֹא בַסֵּתֶר דִּבַּרְתִּי ("I did not speak in secret", 45:19). This was reproduced by the main session. The pattern is a hiddenness motif running through the chapter and ending in God's denial that he spoke in secret. It is not an envelope.
- **The claim has the languages the wrong way round.** The Greek keeps hiddenness at 45:3 (ἀποκρύφους, "hidden") and at 45:19 (οὐκ ἐν κρυφῇ λελάληκα, "I have not spoken in secret"). It drops it only at 45:15 (Swete; Rahlfs) `[T]`.
- **Where the chapter does touch Colossians** is the Greek of 45:3 against 2:3. θησαυρός with ἀπόκρυφος in one verse occurs only at Isa 45:3 and 1 Macc 1:23 (Swete), against about 0.1 verses expected by chance. That is audit 1's C5, already rated.

**Hays table**

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Isa 45 at Rom 14:11; probably Rom 9:20 |
| Volume | Weak | Hebrew-only on the 45:15 side |
| Recurrence | Strong (chapter) | — |
| Thematic coherence | Possible | Gentiles confessing "God in you" |
| Historical plausibility | Weak | Requires a Hebrew reading against the Greek |
| History of interpretation | Weak `[S]` | Not yet put to Logos |
| Satisfaction | Weak | 45:19 breaks the frame |

**Verdict:** **Discard.** **Low.** C5 (Isa 45:3 at 2:3) is unaffected.

**Reasoning:** The envelope does not exist in the Hebrew. The Greek, which is what Colossians reads, keeps the motif at the very points the claim said it lost.

**Book-overview action required:** None. It was never entered as a row.

### Claim C21: Sirach 20:30; 41:14; 1:25 at Col 2:3

**Source:** claim audit 1, New Candidates (Auditors A and B); C5's verdict in audit 1.

**Claim:** Sir 20:30 and 41:14 ("hidden wisdom and an unseen treasure — what use is either?") are the only Swete verses joining wisdom, a hiding word and treasure. Sir 1:25 has ἐν θησαυροῖς σοφίας ("in the treasures of wisdom"), the exact noun pair of Col 2:3. Rated low–moderate; to be folded into the treasury idiom.

**Findings (Auditor A; Swete only, because there is no Rahlfs Sirach in the corpus):**
- **The counts hold.**
  - Sir 20:30 and 41:14 are the only verses with treasure, wisdom and a hiding word.
  - **Sir 1:25 is the only Swete instance of "treasure(s)" followed by a wisdom or knowledge noun.**
  - The main session reproduced both results.
- **No numbering problem appears.** Swete's numbering matches the references as stated. 20:30–31 and 41:14–15 are a doublet.
- **20:30 and 41:14 cut against Colossians.** They censure *withheld* wisdom: hidden wisdom profits no one. In Colossians the treasures are hidden *in Christ* and disclosed (1:26; 2:2). Their hiding words are different lexemes from Colossians' ἀπόκρυφοι: κεκρυμμένη ("hidden") and ἀφανής ("unseen") `[T]`.
- **Sir 1:24–25 fits better.** "Until the time he will hide his words" (ἕως καιροῦ κρύψει, 1:24) stands beside "the treasures of wisdom". That gives a hidden-until-the-time pattern next to the phrase. Its subject, though, is the patient man, not God `[T]`.

**Hays table**

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Possible | Swete only |
| Volume | Possible (1:25) / Weak (20:30; 41:14) | The exact noun pair at 1:25 |
| Recurrence | Weak | No secure Pauline citation of Sirach |
| Thematic coherence | Possible (1:25) / contrary (20:30; 41:14) | — |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | — |
| History of interpretation | Weak–Possible `[S]` | Not yet put to Logos |
| Satisfaction | Weak | Proverbs 2 and Isa 45:3 account for more |

**Verdict:** **Confirmed with nuance, as idiom evidence only.** **Low–moderate for Sir 1:25** inside the treasury-of-wisdom idiom; **low for Sir 20:30 and 41:14**, recorded as tension rather than support.

**Reasoning:** The counts hold, and Sir 1:25 is the phrase's only LXX instance. But the idiom is headed by Proverbs 2, which both auditors placed above it independently. The two Sirach maxims cut against the letter's point.

**Book-overview action required:** Intertextual Map row 5: order the idiom **Prov 2:1–6 · Isa 45:3 · Sir 1:25 · Isa 33:6**, and note Sir 20:30 = 41:14 as a contrary maxim.

### Claim C22: Psalm 23:1 LXX (and kin) at Col 1:19

**Source:** claim audit 1, New Candidates (Auditor B).

**Claim:** πλήρωμα ("fullness") with κατοικ- ("dwell") occurs in one verse at Ps 23:1, Ps 97:7, Jer 8:16, Jer 29:2 and Ezek 12:19 (Swete). Both words are used in other senses there. Rated low.

**Findings (Auditor A):**
- **The count holds exactly:** five verses, reproduced. Rahlfs agrees for Ps 23:1.
- **Every hit is the same formula:** "the earth (sea, land) and its fullness, the world and those who dwell in it". πλήρωμα means the contents, and the dwelling word is a participle for human inhabitants. πλήρωμα is never the subject of the dwelling, and no hit concerns God's dwelling `[T]`.
- **Ps 67:17 is far closer.** It is the only Swete verse with the construction of 1:19.
- **1 Cor 10:26 is a cross-reference for elsewhere.** Paul quotes Ps 23:1 there to settle a food question ("eat whatever is sold in the meat market … for the earth is the Lord's, and all it contains"). That bears on Col 2:16–23, not on 1:19.
- **A closer "fullness" verse.** Ps 88:12 (Hebrew 89:11) has "the world and its fullness you founded". This belongs to audit 1's C7 and is not new.

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Quoted at 1 Cor 10:26 |
| Volume | Weak | Two words in other senses |
| Recurrence | Possible | Once in Paul, for a different point |
| Thematic coherence | Weak | Ownership of creation, not indwelling |
| Historical plausibility | Weak | — |
| History of interpretation | Weak `[S]` | Not yet put to Logos |

### Claim C25: 3:12–16 as the reversal of 2:18–23

**Source:** the 2:16–23 dig, **Headline 3** (the second sentence) and Tool 11 Move 4.

**Claim:** Words from 2:18–23 return in 3:12–16 with their value reversed:
- ταπεινοφροσύνη ("self-abasement" → "humility");
- σύνδεσμος ("ligament" → love, "the bond of perfection");
- σῶμα ("body" → "called in one body");
- λόγος with σοφία ("word" with "wisdom"), together in one verse only at 2:23 and 3:16;
- "teachings" (2:22) → "teaching" (3:16);
- the umpire verbs.

The stated baseline: among 2:16–23's content words found in at most eight verses of the letter, 3:12–17 shares five, tied with 2:6–13 and 1:26–2:4, against a median of 2. The claim rests on sense, not count. Rated moderate as design.

**Findings (Auditor B):**
- **The baseline reproduces**, with one correction. 1:26–2:4 shares **six** words if μηδείς ("no one") is counted, and five if it is treated as a pronoun. **14 of the 77 six-verse stretches** outside 2:16–23 reach five or more, so 3:12–17 sits at about the top fifth, not the top. At the stricter threshold (words in at most four verses), 3:12–17 shares two words, and 2:6–13 three. Other stretch lengths give the same picture: above the median, never exceptional.
- **The items, judged one by one for sense:**
  1. **ταπεινοφροσύνη: a genuine reversal, and the strongest item.** It has seven NT uses, all positive except Col 2:18 and 2:23. The noun is absent from Swete. Its cognates are present there (positive controls: Ps 130:2; Prov 29:23) `[T]`. The pejorative use is unique to Colossians 2, and 3:12 reclaims the word.
  2. **λόγος with σοφία: a genuine reversal.** These are the only two Colossians verses with both. "Having a reputation of wisdom" (2:23) becomes "the word of Christ … in all wisdom" (3:16) `[T]`.
  3. **σύνδεσμος: not a reversal.** The ligaments of 2:19 belong to the body that grows from the head, which is positive. The fault lies with the one "not holding fast to the head". Love as the "bond" (3:14) continues the same positive value `[T]`.
  4. **σῶμα: a partial reversal.** Only 2:23's "severe treatment of the body" is negative. "In one body" (3:15) continues 2:19 `[T]`.
  5. **"Teachings" → "teaching": weak.** It is better explained by 1:28. **Col 1:28 and 3:16 are the only NT verses joining νουθετέω ("admonish") and διδάσκω ("teach"), and both add "in all wisdom"** `[T]` (reproduced). So 3:16 hands Paul's own ministry (1:28) to the congregation.
  6. **The umpire verbs:** see C19.
- **Control.** Auditor B compared 1:26–2:4 against 2:16–23 and found about three reversals:
  - θέλω: God "willed" (1:27) against the rival "delighting" (2:18);
  - σοφία;
  - ἄνθρωπος: "every man" made complete (1:28) against "the teachings of men" (2:22).

  That is as many as 3:12–17 shows. 2:6–13 shows about one.

### Claim C26: The triple σῶμα (2:17, 19, 23) as deliberate play

**Source:** the 2:16–23 dig, **Headline 2** (which rests on the occurrences and the σῶμα / σάρξ contrast, not on the play) and Original Language Observations.

**Claim:** σῶμα occurs in eight Colossians verses, three of them in 2:16–23, with σωματικῶς at 2:9. The three are used in three senses in sequence:
- the reality that casts the shadow (2:17);
- the church (2:19);
- the physical body treated harshly (2:23), set against σάρξ.

σκιά ("shadow") with σῶμα occurs together nowhere in Swete. The claim is that the play is deliberate, rated moderate–high.

**Findings (Auditor B):**
- **The counts hold** (SBLGNT; reproduced).
  - σῶμα: 1:18, 22, 24; 2:11, 17, 19, 23; 3:15.
  - σωματικῶς: 2:9.
  - 2:23 ends τῆς σαρκός ("of the flesh"). σάρξ also occurs at 2:18, so 2:18–23 is bracketed by "flesh" `[T]`.
  - σκιά with σῶμα occurs in no Swete verse (reproduced). The positive control is that σκια- returns 72 verses. In the NT the pair occurs only at Col 2:17 `[T]`.
- **Baseline: shifting senses of σῶμα is Paul's norm.**
  - σῶμα forms ten clusters of three or more verses within an eight-verse span in Paul's letters, and about seven of them shift sense.
  - 1 Cor 6:13–20 and 1 Cor 15:35–44 run through three senses at greater density.
  - Colossians itself does it at 1:18–24 (the church, then Christ's "body of flesh", then the church).
  - **The 2:17–23 cluster is looser than most**: it drops out at a six-verse span `[T]`. The sense judgements are the auditor's and can be checked.
- **The sense of 2:17.** Shadow against body is a stock Hellenistic idiom for appearance against reality (Philo; Josephus) `[S]`. "Substance" is ordinary Greek and needs no play. The overtone "the body of Christ" is invited by 1:18, 1:24 and 2:19, but cannot be proved `[I]`.

**Design table**

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Counts verified | Yes |
| Baseline | Sense-shifting σῶμα clusters are normal for Paul (about seven of ten); this one is looser than most |
| Sense | Three senses really are present; 2:17 uses a stock idiom |
| Alternative explanation | Paul's habitual polysemy plus the shadow/body idiom |
| History of interpretation | `[S]` — **pending Logos round 3 (question 3.4)** |

**Verdict:** **Confirmed with nuance.** **Moderate** (down from moderate–high).

**Reasoning:** The three senses are there in sequence, and the contrast in 2:23 is textual. But shifting the sense of σῶμα within a few verses is ordinary Pauline style, and 2:17 is idiom. The play is real as style; "deliberate design" claims more than the evidence shows.

**Pulpit note (ESV).** The ESV and the NASB95 both render 2:17 "the substance belongs to Christ". The congregation will not hear "body" there (the 2:16–23 dig, Tool 8: only the KJV keeps it). A sermon that leans on the triple must supply the Greek.

**Book-overview action required:** None. **The 2:16–23 dig, Headline 2,** stands; it rests on the occurrences and on the 2:23 σῶμα / σάρξ contrast, both `[T]`. Lower the Original Language Observations "wordplay" line from moderate–high to moderate.

  - Rahlfs agrees.

No Swete verse joins "taste" and "touch" in a prohibition. Col 2:22 says these things "perish with use", which is language for consumables. Sinai is about approaching a mountain on pain of death `[T]`.
