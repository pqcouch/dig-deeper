The Passage

NA28:

2 Τῇ προσευχῇ προσκαρτερεῖτε, γρηγοροῦντες ἐν αὐτῇ ἐν εὐχαριστίᾳ, 3 προσευχόμενοι ἅμα καὶ περὶ ἡμῶν, ἵνα ὁ θεὸς ἀνοίξῃ ἡμῖν θύραν τοῦ λόγου λαλῆσαι τὸ μυστήριον τοῦ Χριστοῦ, δι᾽ ὃ καὶ δέδεμαι, 4 ἵνα φανερώσω αὐτὸ ὡς δεῖ με λαλῆσαι. 5 Ἐν σοφίᾳ περιπατεῖτε πρὸς τοὺς ἔξω τὸν καιρὸν ἐξαγοραζόμενοι. 6 ὁ λόγος ὑμῶν πάντοτε ἐν χάριτι, ἅλατι ἠρτυμένος, εἰδέναι πῶς δεῖ ὑμᾶς ἑνὶ ἑκάστῳ ἀποκρίνεσθαι.

Where the SBLGNT differs: the words are identical. SBLGNT adds commas after λόγου (4:3) and after ἔξω (4:5), which make the purpose infinitive and the participle a little more separate; nothing turns on them [T: as each edition prints it].

NASB95 (study text):

2 Devote yourselves to prayer, keeping alert in it with an attitude of thanksgiving; 3 praying at the same time for us as well, that God will open up to us a door for the word, so that we may speak forth the mystery of Christ, for which I have also been imprisoned; 4 that I may make it clear in the way I ought to speak. 5 Conduct yourselves with wisdom toward outsiders, making the most of the opportunity. 6 Let your speech always be with grace, as though seasoned with salt, so that you will know how you should respond to each person.

ESV (pulpit text):

2 Continue steadfastly in prayer, being watchful in it with thanksgiving. 3 At the same time, pray also for us, that God may open to us a door for the word, to declare the mystery of Christ, on account of which I am in prison— 4 that I may make it clear, which is how I ought to speak. 5 Walk in wisdom toward outsiders, making the best use of the time. 6 Let your speech always be gracious, seasoned with salt, so that you may know how you ought to answer each person.

Headline Findings

  1. The letter’s last general command is to pray, and it returns the opening prayer to the church — the praying apostle now asks to be prayed for. [T] Surfaced by: Positional Necessity, Repetition, Move 4. προσεύχομαι (“pray”) stands in the letter at 1:3 and 1:9 (Paul for them) and 4:3 (them for Paul); προσευχή (“prayer”) at 4:2 and 4:12 (Epaphras, “always labouring … in his prayers”) (SBLGNT) [T]. εὐχαριστία (“thanksgiving”) stands only at 2:7 and 4:2 — the overview’s frame (Echo 20) — and closes the thanks-chain of 3:15–17 [T]. The vocabulary is the church’s from the beginning: προσκαρτερέω with προσευχή (“devote oneself to prayer”) shares a verse only at Acts 1:14, 2:42, 6:4, Rom 12:12 and Col 4:2 (SBLGNT) [T]. And γρηγορέω (“keep awake, watch”) shares a verse with prayer in only three NT verses: Gethsemane’s “watch and pray” (Matt 26:41; Mark 14:38) and Col 4:2 [T]. High on the words.

  2. A prisoner asks for a door — not out of prison, but for the word — and the mystery thread of the letter ends in his mouth. [T]/[I] Surfaced by: Structure, Vocabulary, Move 4. “That God may open to us a door for the word, to speak the mystery of Christ, for which I have been bound (δέδεμαι)” (4:3) [T]. The “door” is Paul’s figure for opportunity to preach (1 Cor 16:9; 2 Cor 2:12; cf. Acts 14:27) [T]. μυστήριον (“mystery”) stands in the letter at 1:26, 1:27, 2:2 and 4:3; φανερόω (“make manifest”) at 1:26, 3:4 and 4:4 [T] — the overview’s Echo 9, reframed by claim audit 1 as “the mystery thread” [S: audit 1]. What God “has now manifested to his saints” (1:26) Paul asks to “manifest” by speaking (4:4) [T]/[I]. The prayer is for the word’s freedom, not first for his own [I], high — though Phlm 22 shows he also hoped for release “through your prayers” [T]. High on the words.

  3. The word passes from the apostle to the church: “the word” for which a door must open (4:3) becomes “your word” (4:6), and Paul’s “must” becomes theirs. [T] Surfaced by: Repetition, Linking Words, Tool 8. λόγος (“word”) stands at 4:3 (θύραν τοῦ λόγου, “a door for the word”) and 4:6 (ὁ λόγος ὑμῶν, “your word, speech”), completing a chain through the letter: “the word of the truth, the gospel” (1:5), “the word of God” fully carried out (1:25), “the word of Christ” dwelling (3:16) [T] (overview Echo 22, moderate). δεῖ (“it is necessary, must”) stands in the letter only at 4:4 (“as I must speak”) and 4:6 (“how you must answer”); λαλέω (“speak”) only at 4:3 and 4:4 [T] (δεῖ is filed under δέω in the SBLGNT index; counted by form). The apostle’s obligation to speak and the church’s obligation to answer are put in the same words [I], high. Both English versions break the λόγος link (“the word” / “your speech”); only the ESV keeps “ought” in both verses (Tool 8).

  4. The walk ends facing outward, “in wisdom”, buying up the time — and Daniel 2 stands behind the scene again. [T]/[I] Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Tool 11 (the scan), Move 4. περιπατέω (“walk”) stands in the letter at 1:10, 2:6, 3:7 and 4:5 — its fourth and last use, now “toward those outside” (πρὸς τοὺς ἔξω; cf. 1 Thess 4:12, the only other NT verse with both words) [T]. σοφία (“wisdom”) makes its sixth and last appearance (1:9, 28; 2:3, 23; 3:16; 4:5) [T]: the wisdom prayed for “so that you will walk” (1:9–10) is the wisdom they walk in [I]. τὸν καιρὸν ἐξαγοραζόμενοι (“buying up the time”) stands in the NT only here and at Eph 5:16, and in Swete only at Daniel 2:8 (both Greek texts), where Nebuchadnezzar says the wise men “are buying time” [T]. The scan ranks Daniel 2 first for this passage (both texts in the top three): a captive prays with his friends “concerning this mystery” (2:18), blesses the God who “gives wisdom to the wise” and “changes times” (2:20–21), gives thanks (2:23), and speaks the mystery to a pagan king (2:27–30) [T]. Claim audit 1 rated Daniel 2 moderate as the shape behind 1:26–27 [S: audit 1]. Its return here, with the one rare verb, makes it a letter-level field [I], moderate; as the source of 4:5’s wording, low–moderate (the sense is reversed: the wise men stall, the church seizes). Queued as C57.

  5. Speech “with grace, seasoned with salt”, fitted “to each one” — and three contacts with the sayings of Jesus in five verses. [T]/[I] Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Tool 11, Move 4. ἐν χάριτι (“with grace”) repeats 3:16; λόγος and χάρις share a verse in Colossians only at 3:16 and 4:6 [T] (3:12–17 dig [S: dig]). ἅλας (“salt”) with ἀρτύω (“season”) stands in the NT only at Mark 9:50, Luke 14:34 and Col 4:6 (SBLGNT) [T]; Mark 9:50 goes on, “have salt in yourselves, and be at peace with one another” [T]. With “watch and pray” (Gethsemane, Headline 1) and “the mystery … those outside” (μυστήριον with ἔξω in one verse: Mark 4:11 only; here across 4:3–5) [T], the passage has three of its rarer pairings in Jesus’ sayings, two of them in Mark [T]. Whether that is design or shared church idiom is uncertain [I]. “To each one” (ἑνὶ ἑκάστῳ) makes the answer personal, not scripted [I], high; compare 1 Pet 3:15 [T]. High on the words; uncertain on the Jesus-tradition cluster (queued as C58).

The Sixteen Tools

1. Author’s Purpose

Book-level: to keep the church walking in Christ, persuaded it lacks nothing, and to show what that sufficiency looks like in speech, household and work (overview) [I].

Passage-level: to send the church out of the household into prayer and into the world. It is to keep praying, watchfully and thankfully (4:2); to pray for the apostle’s freedom to speak the mystery (4:3–4); and to live and speak towards outsiders with wisdom, opportunism, grace and fitness (4:5–6) [T]/[I]. The letter that began with Paul praying for them (1:3–14) ends its exhortation with them praying for him, and its message going out through their speech [I], high.

2. Context

Positional Necessity Check

Preceding movement: 3:18–4:1 set the household under “the Lord” [T]. Before that, 3:12–17 clothed the church’s inner life, ending with word, song and thanks (3:16–17) [T]. Further back: the opening prayer and thanks (1:3–12), the word bearing fruit “in all the world” (1:6), Paul’s labour to make the mystery known among the Gentiles (1:24–2:3), and the rival’s “word of wisdom” (2:23) [T].

Necessity answer — why the passage exists here.

  1. The new life needs its outward face. 3:12–17 is the church; 3:18–4:1 the household; 4:2–6 the world, through prayer and speech [T]/[I]. The 3:18–4:1 dig placed the household “between the church’s inner life (3:12–17) and its face to outsiders (4:2–6)” [S: dig].
  2. 1:3–14 requires it. Prayer, thanks, wisdom, walk, the word, and “all the world” — the opening’s words — return here, in reverse: the church now prays, and the word goes out (Move 4) [T].
  3. 1:24–2:5 requires it. Paul “fully carries out the word of God, the mystery” (1:25–26) and labours “that I may present every man complete” (1:28–29); now the church is enlisted in that labour by prayer and by its own speech [I], moderate–high.
  4. 3:16 requires it. The word of Christ that dwells within is to be spoken outward, by the apostle (4:3–4) and by everyone (4:6) [T]/[I].
  5. 2:23 is answered last. The rival had “a word (λόγος) … of wisdom”; the church has a word “with grace” and walks “in wisdom” towards those outside [I], moderate.

Implication for this passage: it is not an appendix of miscellaneous duties. It is where the whole letter turns from the church’s sufficiency in Christ to the world that does not yet know him [I], moderate–high.

Revisit after Tool 11: Move 2 (Daniel 2) sharpens it: the scene of a captive praying for, receiving and speaking a God-given mystery before a pagan court, with the wise men “buying time”, supplies a shape for 4:2–5 [I], moderate as field.

Immediately before: 4:1, “Masters … you too have a Master in heaven” — the household code ends; 4:2 begins without a connective (asyndeton), as 3:18 did [T]. Immediately after: 4:7–9, Tychicus and Onesimus, “that you may know about our circumstances” — the news of the prisoner the church has just been asked to pray for [T].

Section: 3:1–4:6, the life of the risen; 4:2–6 is its close [T] (overview). The ESV heads 4:2 “Further Instructions” [T].

3. Structure

Verses Section Function
4:2 Keep praying Command; watchful, thankful
4:3–4 Pray for us Participle; the content: a door for the word; Paul’s chains; that he may manifest the mystery as he must
4:5 Walk towards outsiders Command; in wisdom; buying up the time
4:6 Your word Verbless clause (imperatival); with grace, salted; knowing how to answer each

Device 1 — two commands, each with a participle. προσκαρτερεῖτε … γρηγοροῦντες … προσευχόμενοι (4:2–3); περιπατεῖτε … ἐξαγοραζόμενοι (4:5) [T]. Then the verbless 4:6 [T].

Device 2 — Paul’s speech mirrored by theirs. λαλῆσαι … ὡς δεῖ με λαλῆσαι (“to speak … as I must speak”, 4:3–4) / εἰδέναι πῶς δεῖ ὑμᾶς … ἀποκρίνεσθαι (“to know how you must answer”, 4:6) [T]. The apostle announces; the church answers [I], moderate–high.

Device 3 — inward to God, outward to the world. 4:2–4 faces God (prayer); 4:5–6 faces “those outside” [T]. Both halves are about the word [I].

Clause-Flow Layout (NA28 wording):

  • Τῇ προσευχῇ προσκαρτερεῖτε — COMMAND: “devote yourselves to prayer” (present imperative)
    • γρηγοροῦντες ἐν αὐτῇ — MANNER: “keeping awake in it” (present participle)
      • ἐν εὐχαριστίᾳ — “with thanksgiving”
    • προσευχόμενοι ἅμα καὶ περὶ ἡμῶν — “praying at the same time also for us” (present participle)
      • ἵνα ὁ θεὸς ἀνοίξῃ ἡμῖν θύραν τοῦ λόγου — CONTENT: “that God may open to us a door for the word”
        • λαλῆσαι τὸ μυστήριον τοῦ Χριστοῦ — PURPOSE: “to speak the mystery of Christ”
          • δι᾽ ὃ καὶ δέδεμαι — “on account of which I have also been bound”
        • ἵνα φανερώσω αὐτό — PURPOSE: “that I may make it manifest”
          • ὡς δεῖ με λαλῆσαι — “as I must speak”
  • Ἐν σοφίᾳ περιπατεῖτε πρὸς τοὺς ἔξω — COMMAND: “walk in wisdom towards those outside” (present imperative)
    • τὸν καιρὸν ἐξαγοραζόμενοι — MANNER: “buying up the time / opportunity” (present middle participle)
  • ὁ λόγος ὑμῶν πάντοτε ἐν χάριτι — COMMAND (verbless): “your word always with grace”
    • ἅλατι ἠρτυμένος — “seasoned with salt” (perfect passive participle)
    • εἰδέναι πῶς δεῖ ὑμᾶς ἑνὶ ἑκάστῳ ἀποκρίνεσθαι — RESULT/PURPOSE: “so as to know how you must answer each one”

Central assertions, one line each: - 4:2 — command: persevere in prayer, awake and thankful. - 4:3–4 — request: pray that God will open a door for the word, so that the bound apostle may speak the mystery of Christ as he must. - 4:5 — command: live wisely towards outsiders, seizing the time. - 4:6 — command: let your speech always be gracious and salted, so that you know how to answer each person.

Two imperatives and one verbless command; one request inside the first [T]. The passage’s centre of gravity is speech: λόγος twice, λαλέω twice, ἀποκρίνομαι once, in five verses [T].

4. Linking Words

  • No connective at 4:2 — asyndeton, after the household code; a new paragraph [T].
  • ἅμα καί, 4:3 — “at the same time also”: their prayer for Paul is to be part of their persevering prayer, not a separate duty [I]. ἅμα with prayer recurs at Phlm 22: “at the same time also (ἅμα δὲ καί) prepare me a lodging, for I hope that through your prayers I will be given to you” [T] — the companion letter, the same idiom [I].
  • ἵνα, 4:3 — “that”: the content of the prayer [T].
  • λαλῆσαι, 4:3 — infinitive of purpose: “so as to speak” [T].
  • δι᾽ ὅ, 4:3 — “on account of which”: the mystery is the cause of the chains [T]. B F G read δι᾽ ὅν (“on account of whom”, Christ) (Textual Variants).
  • ἵνα, 4:4 — “that”: a second purpose clause; probably dependent on the prayer (“pray … that I may manifest it”) rather than coordinate with 4:3’s ἵνα [S], moderate.
  • ὡς δεῖ, 4:4 — “as it is necessary”: manner — or the ground of obligation [I].
  • εἰδέναι, 4:6 — infinitive: result or purpose — “so that you know” [S], moderate.
  • πῶς δεῖ, 4:6 — “how one must”: the same idiom at 1 Thess 4:1, 2 Thess 3:7, 1 Tim 3:15 (SBLGNT) [T].

5. Parallels

Prose, with parallel constructions [T]: - λαλῆσαι … λαλῆσαι (4:3–4) — an inclusio round the mystery and the chains [T]. - δεῖ με λαλῆσαι / δεῖ ὑμᾶς … ἀποκρίνεσθαι (4:4, 6) — synthetic: the apostle’s speaking and the church’s answering under one necessity [T]/[I]. - ἐν σοφίᾳ … ἐν χάριτι (4:5, 6) — the manner of walk and of word [T].

N/A — epistle, not narrative.

7. Vocabulary

Proper-noun and group inventory: θεός (“God”, 4:3), Χριστός (“Christ”, 4:3); “us” (ἡμῶν, ἡμῖν — Paul and his companions; cf. 1:1, Timothy) and “I” (δέδεμαι, φανερώσω); “those outside” (οἱ ἔξω); “each one” [T]. Triage: “those outside” — a candidate: ἔξω only here in the letter [T]. In Paul οἱ ἔξω are non-believers: 1 Cor 5:12–13; 1 Thess 4:12; cf. 1 Tim 3:7 [T]. Mark 4:11 uses the same expression for those outside to whom the mystery comes in parables [T].

προσκαρτερέω (“devote oneself to, persist in”), 4:2 [T], high. Ten NT verses (SBLGNT). With προσευχή: Acts 1:14 (the hundred and twenty), 2:42 (the first church), 6:4 (the apostles: “we will devote ourselves to prayer and to the ministry of the word”), Rom 12:12, Col 4:2 [T]. Acts 6:4 is the only NT verse joining it to λόγος — prayer and the word together, as in Col 4:2–3 [T]/[I].

γρηγορέω (“keep awake, be watchful”), 4:2 [T], high on the word; moderate on the background. Twenty-two NT verses: Jesus’ parables of watching for the coming (Matt 24:42–43; 25:13; Mark 13:34–37), Gethsemane (Matt 26:38–41; Mark 14:34–38), and in Paul 1 Cor 16:13, 1 Thess 5:6, 10 (SBLGNT) [T]. In Colossians only here [T]. The watchfulness is both alertness in prayer and eschatological wakefulness (1 Thess 5:6) [I], moderate–high.

ἐν εὐχαριστίᾳ (“with thanksgiving”), 4:2 [T], high. εὐχαριστία: 2:7 (“overflowing with gratitude”) and 4:2 only (overview Echo 20) [T]. Prayer with thanksgiving shares a verse also at Phil 4:6 and 1 Tim 2:1 [T]. The fourth “thanks” in five verses of the letter’s paraenesis (3:15, 16, 17; 4:2) [T].

θύρα τοῦ λόγου (“a door for the word”), 4:3 [T], high. θύρα with ἀνοίγω (“open”) shares a verse in twelve NT verses; in Paul’s mission language, 1 Cor 16:9 (“a wide door … has opened to me”) and 2 Cor 2:12 (“a door was opened for me in the Lord”); compare Acts 14:27 (“he had opened a door of faith to the Gentiles”) (SBLGNT) [T]. The genitive “of the word” is the door’s purpose: an opening for preaching [I], high. Eph 6:19’s parallel speaks of “the opening (ἀνοίξει) of my mouth” [T].

τὸ μυστήριον τοῦ Χριστοῦ (“the mystery of Christ”), 4:3 [T], high. The same phrase stands at Eph 3:4 (ἐν τῷ μυστηρίῳ τοῦ Χριστοῦ) [T]. In Colossians the mystery is “Christ in you, the hope of glory” (1:27), “God’s mystery, Christ” (2:2) [T]. The genitive is probably epexegetic — the mystery that is Christ [I], moderate–high (the 1:24–2:5 dig [S: dig]).

δέδεμαι (“I have been bound”), 4:3 [T], high. Perfect: bound and still bound [I]. δέω (“bind”) only here in the letter; δεσμός (“chain”) at 4:18 (“remember my chains”) [T]. The ESV’s “I am in prison” and the NASB95’s “imprisoned” are fair but lose the chain image (Tool 8).

φανερώσω (“that I may manifest it”), 4:4 [T], high. φανερόω at 1:26 (the mystery “now manifested to his saints”), 3:4 (twice: “when Christ … is manifested, then you also will be manifested with him”) and 4:4 [T]. Claim audit 1 confirmed the counts and found the sequence “largely the mystery thread” [S: audit 1]. The mystery God has manifested (1:26) Paul asks to manifest (4:4) [I].

ὡς δεῖ με λαλῆσαι (“as I must speak”), 4:4 [T], high. The exact clause stands in the NT only at Col 4:4 and Eph 6:20, where it follows “that I may speak boldly (παρρησιάσωμαι)” (SBLGNT string search) [T]. In Colossians the stress is on clarity (“that I may make it manifest”); in Ephesians on boldness [T]/[I]. One witness (A) adds ἐν παρρησίᾳ (“with boldness”) at 4:3, from Ephesians [T].

ἐν σοφίᾳ περιπατεῖτε (“walk in wisdom”), 4:5 [T], high. Headline 4. σοφία with περιπατέω in one verse: only Col 4:5 in the NT (SBLGNT) [T]; across adjacent verses, 1:9–10 (“in all spiritual wisdom … so that you will walk”) [T]. Eph 5:15 is the parallel: “be careful how you walk, not as unwise but as wise” [T].

πρὸς τοὺς ἔξω (“towards those outside”), 4:5 [T], high. ἔξω with περιπατέω: 1 Thess 4:12 (“walk properly towards outsiders”) and Col 4:5 only (SBLGNT) [T].

τὸν καιρὸν ἐξαγοραζόμενοι (“buying up the time”), 4:5 [T], high on the counts; moderate on the sense. ἐξαγοράζω occurs four times in the NT: Gal 3:13 and 4:5 (“Christ redeemed us from the curse of the Law”; “to redeem those under the Law”), Eph 5:16 and Col 4:5 — the last two with καιρός (SBLGNT) [T]. In Swete the verb occurs only at Dan 2:8, in both Greek texts, with καιρόν: “I know for certain that you are buying time” (Headline 4; Tool 11) [T]. The middle voice suggests “buying up for yourselves” — seizing every opportunity as a merchant buys up stock [S], moderate; “redeeming” (KJV) carries the Galatians overtone, but in Galatians the object is people under the curse, here the time [I]. καιρός is the opportune or appointed time, not mere duration [S], high. It occurs only here in Colossians [T].

ὁ λόγος ὑμῶν (“your word, speech”), 4:6 [T], high. Headline 3. λόγος in Colossians: 1:5, 1:25, 2:23, 3:16, 3:17, 4:3, 4:6 [T].

πάντοτε ἐν χάριτι (“always with grace”), 4:6 [T], moderate on the sense. πάντοτε (“always”) stands at 1:3 (Paul’s prayer, “always”), 4:6, and 4:12 (Epaphras, “always labouring … in his prayers”) [T]. χάρις at 1:2, 1:6, 3:16, 4:6, 4:18 [T]. Here probably “graciously, with charm” — but in a letter whose χάρις is God’s grace (1:2, 6; 4:18), the word would carry it [I], moderate.

ἅλατι ἠρτυμένος (“seasoned with salt”), 4:6 [T], high on the counts; moderate on the sense. ἅλας occurs four times in the NT: Matt 5:13; Mark 9:50 (twice in the verse); Luke 14:34; Col 4:6. ἀρτύω occurs three times: Mark 9:50, Luke 14:34, Col 4:6 (SBLGNT) [T]. In Swete, ἀρτύω was not found on its stem (ἀρτυ-, ἠρτυ-) [T] (absence or stem fault — the augment would show as ἠρτυ-, also searched). Greek writers called witty speech “salted” [S], moderate; Jewish usage associated salt with preservation and covenant [S]. In context — “with grace”, “to each one” — the salt is what makes speech palatable and apt, not cutting [I], moderate–high. Job 6:6 LXX joins salt and words: “Shall bread be eaten without salt? Or is there taste in empty words?” [T] — a field contact, low.

9. Tone and Feel

Quiet, urgent and outward-looking. No polemic; the imperatives are short [T]. A personal note breaks in — “for which I have been bound” — without self-pity, as the reason for the request [T]/[I]. The passage ends not on a command to speak but on a command to answer well [T].

Soundtrack: a door left ajar in a dark street — light spilling out, a voice from inside answering someone who knocks. The preacher should keep both the urgency (“buying up the time”) and the gentleness (“with grace”) [I].

12. Genre

Pauline epistle; closing paraenesis with a prayer request — a common Pauline pattern at the end of the ethical section (1 Thess 5:25; 2 Thess 3:1; Rom 15:30–32; Eph 6:18–20) [T] as texts. Reading rule: the request is not a formality; Paul gives its content and reason [I]. Short commands; wisdom-style maxims at 4:5–6 [I].

Partly applicable. Paul is a model, but of a particular kind: the apostle who needs the church’s prayers, whose chains do not change his request, and who wants clarity more than freedom [T]/[I]. 14. Bible Timeline

This side of the cross, before the Lord’s appearing (3:4): the time is short and opportune, to be bought up [T]/[I]. Paul is in custody; the church is a minority in a Phrygian city [T]/[S]. 16. So What?

Stage 1 — the response the author seeks: a church that prays persistently, alertly and thankfully; that prays for the freedom of the word and its messengers; and that lives wisely among non-Christians, seizing opportunities and speaking graciously and aptly to each person [I].

Stage 2: - Worldview. The word, not my comfort, is the main thing to pray for (4:3). God opens doors (4:3). The time is opportunity (4:5). Outsiders ask; we answer (4:6). - Stop: prayer that drifts or sleeps; prayer that is all requests and no thanks; leaving the outsiders to the professionals; speech towards non-Christians that is either insipid or sharp. - Start: praying regularly for those who preach, and for their clarity; noticing opportunities; preparing to answer, person by person. - Motive: the mystery of Christ, worth chains (4:3) [T].

Four audiences. For me: do I pray for the word’s freedom as much as for my circumstances? For a Christian friend: the one who feels unable to evangelise — the passage asks first for a walk and an answer, not a speech. For the church: does our prayer meeting pray for doors for the word? For an unbeliever: a community whose speech is meant to be gracious towards you, and whose message is a person, the mystery of Christ.

Prayer. God our Father, keep us watchful and thankful in prayer. Open a door for the word to those who preach it, that they may make Christ known as they ought. Give us wisdom towards those outside, to buy up the time, and let our speech always be gracious, seasoned with salt, that we may know how to answer each one.

Historical and Cultural Background

Setting

As for the letter: Paul imprisoned (4:3, 10, 18), the place unstated [T]/[S]; Colossae a small city in Phrygia, the church a minority among pagan neighbours and a Jewish community [S].

What the original audience knew

  1. Prisoners depended on friends. A Roman prisoner relied on visitors for food and help, and could often receive and send letters [S], high. Asking a church to pray was asking it to stand with a man in custody [I].
  2. “Doors” for opportunity were a familiar image [S], moderate; Paul uses it in his own correspondence (1 Cor 16:9; 2 Cor 2:12) [T].
  3. Salted speech. Greek and Latin writers called wit “salt” (Attic salt) [S], moderate. Salt was required with every offering as “the salt of the covenant” (Lev 2:13, Swete ἅλα διαθήκης) [T]; its bearing on speech is not shown [I].
  4. Outsiders’ suspicion. Early Christians were accused of antisocial behaviour; conduct “towards outsiders” mattered for the gospel’s reputation (1 Thess 4:12; 1 Tim 3:7; 1 Pet 2:12) [S]/[T].
  5. Fixed hours of prayer in Jewish practice (Dan 6:10; Acts 3:1) [S]; “devote yourselves to prayer” may include regular times without being limited to them [I].

What this changes about how we read

The request “pray for us” comes from someone whose situation was precarious; “a door for the word” is chosen over “a door out of prison” by a man who knew the difference [I], moderate.

Original Audience Reception

A reader of Ephesians knows Eph 5:15–16 and 6:18–20; of Thessalonians, 1 Thess 4:12; 5:6, 17 [T]. Colossians’ marks: thanksgiving in prayer, “the mystery of Christ”, clarity (“manifest”), and the gracious, salted answer [T].

  • Who: a church that had received the word through Epaphras (1:7), living among outsiders [T].
  • Situation: pressed by a rival teaching that prized its own “wisdom” (2:23) [T].

  • Comforting: outsiders are to be answered with grace, not feared [I].

A view of evangelism as an event or a programme; prayer lists dominated by health and circumstance; speech towards non-Christians shaped by online combat; a sense that “redeeming the time” means productivity [I].

Implication for the sermon

Start where the passage starts — prayer — and do not reduce 4:5–6 to a method of evangelism; it is a walk and a word shaped by grace [I].

Biblical-Theological Themes

Prayer and the word

  • Where: prayer for a door for the word (4:2–4) [T].
  • Before: Daniel 2 (prayer for the mystery; Tool 11) [T]; Acts 6:4 (prayer and the ministry of the word) [T].
  • After: 2 Thess 3:1; Eph 6:18–20 [T].
  • Contributes: the word’s progress is asked of God, and the church’s prayer is part of the mission.
  • Confidence: high.

The mystery made known to the nations

  • Where: “the mystery of Christ” to be manifested (4:3–4); outsiders (4:5) [T].
  • Before: Dan 2:28 (“a God in heaven who reveals mysteries”); in the letter 1:26–27 (“among the Gentiles”) [T].
  • After: Rom 16:25–26 (“the mystery … now manifested … made known to all the nations”) [S].
  • Contributes: the mystery once hidden is now for those outside.
  • Confidence: high on the idea; moderate on Daniel.

Wise speech

  • Where: 4:5–6 [T].
  • Before: Prov 15:1; 25:11 (the apt word) [T].
  • After: Jas 3; 1 Pet 3:15 [S]/[T].
  • Contributes: wisdom shows in the tongue.
  • Confidence: moderate.

Confluence: convergence on the word — the word prayed for (theme 1) is the mystery of Christ (theme 2), and it reaches outsiders also through the church’s wise and gracious speech (theme 3) [I].

Christological Reading

Type of connection: NT; the mystery is Christ (4:3; 1:27; 2:2) [T]. Daniel 2’s captive who speaks God’s mystery to the king is a pattern that Paul, in chains, fulfils in a greater way (the mystery now is Christ himself) — typology passes the theological-category test (revelation of God’s kingdom purpose), partly the NT-precedent test (Paul’s mystery language with Daniel’s verbs, audit 1), and the escalation test (from a dream’s meaning to Christ); low–moderate as typology. Gethsemane’s “watch and pray” (Headline 1) — if heard — sets the church’s watchfulness beside the Lord’s own hour [I], uncertain.

Moralism check: - The “be like X” temptation: “pray more, witness better.” - The gospel grounding: God opens doors (4:3); the mystery is Christ (4:3); the grace in their speech is the grace they have heard (1:6) [T]/[I]. - Christ as hero: he is the content of the word; the church’s speech serves him [T].

Confidence: moderate–high on the Christ-centredness.

Difficult / Contested Verses

4:5 — “redeeming the time”

  • Category: misread.
  • The difficulty: whether ἐξαγοράζομαι τὸν καιρόν means “buy back lost time”, “use time efficiently”, “seize each opportunity”, or “rescue the time from evil”.
  • Options: (a) seize the opportunity (middle: buy up for oneself) [S]; (b) redeem time from evil (Eph 5:16, “because the days are evil”) [S]; (c) time management (popular) — not in view [I].
  • What an honest exegesis surfaces: καιρός is opportunity; the setting is “towards outsiders”; (a) is most natural, with (b) as Ephesians’ reason [I], moderate–high. Dan 2:8 shows the phrase could mean “gaining time” in a negative sense; Paul turns it [T]/[I].
  • Suggested handling: address briefly; correct the productivity reading.

4:6 — “seasoned with salt”

  • Category: contested (image).
  • Options: (a) witty, interesting speech [S]; (b) wholesome, preserving speech (cf. Eph 4:29) [S]; (c) distinctive, as disciples are salt (Mark 9:50; Matt 5:13) [S].
  • What an honest exegesis surfaces: the governing phrase is “with grace” and the purpose “to answer each one”; salt is what makes grace palatable and apt [I], moderate. Not license for sharpness [I], high.
  • Suggested handling: brief; let the context define the image.

4:3 — prayer for release or for the word?

  • Category: pastoral.
  • What an honest exegesis surfaces: the request here is for the word’s door; Phlm 22 shows Paul also hoped for release through their prayers [T]. Both are legitimate; Colossians puts the word first [I].
  • Suggested handling: use as a model for praying for persecuted Christians: for their faithfulness and witness, as well as their release.

Preaching Pitfalls

Pitfall: “Pray for open doors” as prayer for my plans

  • What it looks like: “Pray that God will open doors for my job, my move, my project.”
  • Why it’s wrong: the door is “for the word”, to speak “the mystery of Christ”, requested by a man in chains.
  • The corrective: keep the door’s purpose — the word’s free course — and the cost that came with it.

Pitfall: “Redeem the time” as time management

  • What it looks like: a sermon on productivity and wasted hours.
  • Why it’s wrong: καιρός is opportunity, the setting is “towards outsiders”, and the next verse is about answering people.
  • The corrective: preach alert opportunism for the gospel, in ordinary relationships.

Pitfall: Salt as licence for sharpness

  • What it looks like: “Salty” speech — biting, combative, clever at others’ expense.
  • Why it’s wrong: the speech is “always with grace” first; the aim is to answer “each one”, not to win.
  • The corrective: grace governs, salt flavours.

Pitfall: Evangelism as a technique

  • What it looks like: turning 4:5–6 into a scripted method.
  • Why it’s wrong: the verbs are “walk” and “answer” — a life that prompts questions and a word fitted to each person.
  • The corrective: preach a watchful, prayerful, gracious life and readiness to answer, with prayer for the preachers as its partner.

Pitfall: Skipping 4:2 to reach the “mission” verses

  • What it looks like: treating prayer as preface.
  • Why it’s wrong: the passage’s first and main command is prayer; the mission depends on God opening doors.
  • The corrective: give 4:2–4 at least equal weight.
