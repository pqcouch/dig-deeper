## The Passage

**NA28** (as NA28 prints it; a paragraph begins at 2:6, a sentence at 2:11, and a new paragraph at 2:16):

> **2:6** Ὡς οὖν παρελάβετε τὸν Χριστὸν Ἰησοῦν τὸν κύριον, ἐν αὐτῷ περιπατεῖτε, **7** ἐρριζωμένοι καὶ ἐποικοδομούμενοι ἐν αὐτῷ καὶ βεβαιούμενοι τῇ πίστει καθὼς ἐδιδάχθητε, περισσεύοντες ἐν εὐχαριστίᾳ. **8** Βλέπετε μή τις ὑμᾶς ἔσται ὁ συλαγωγῶν διὰ τῆς φιλοσοφίας καὶ κενῆς ἀπάτης κατὰ τὴν παράδοσιν τῶν ἀνθρώπων, κατὰ τὰ στοιχεῖα τοῦ κόσμου καὶ οὐ κατὰ Χριστόν· **9** ὅτι ἐν αὐτῷ κατοικεῖ πᾶν τὸ πλήρωμα τῆς θεότητος σωματικῶς, **10** καὶ ἐστὲ ἐν αὐτῷ πεπληρωμένοι, ὅς ἐστιν ἡ κεφαλὴ πάσης ἀρχῆς καὶ ἐξουσίας. **11** Ἐν ᾧ καὶ περιετμήθητε περιτομῇ ἀχειροποιήτῳ ἐν τῇ ἀπεκδύσει τοῦ σώματος τῆς σαρκός, ἐν τῇ περιτομῇ τοῦ Χριστοῦ, **12** συνταφέντες αὐτῷ ἐν τῷ βαπτισμῷ, ἐν ᾧ καὶ συνηγέρθητε διὰ τῆς πίστεως τῆς ἐνεργείας τοῦ θεοῦ τοῦ ἐγείραντος αὐτὸν ἐκ νεκρῶν· **13** καὶ ὑμᾶς νεκροὺς ὄντας [ἐν] τοῖς παραπτώμασιν καὶ τῇ ἀκροβυστίᾳ τῆς σαρκὸς ὑμῶν, συνεζωοποίησεν ὑμᾶς σὺν αὐτῷ, χαρισάμενος ἡμῖν πάντα τὰ παραπτώματα. **14** ἐξαλείψας τὸ καθʼ ἡμῶν χειρόγραφον τοῖς δόγμασιν ὃ ἦν ὑπεναντίον ἡμῖν, καὶ αὐτὸ ἦρκεν ἐκ τοῦ μέσου προσηλώσας αὐτὸ τῷ σταυρῷ· **15** ἀπεκδυσάμενος τὰς ἀρχὰς καὶ τὰς ἐξουσίας ἐδειγμάτισεν ἐν παρρησίᾳ, θριαμβεύσας αὐτοὺς ἐν αὐτῷ.

The NA28 and the SBLGNT agree word for word in these ten verses — 170 words in each, compared word by word `[T]`. ## Headline Findings

1. **2:6–7 is the letter's first command and its thesis, and it is built out of the opening chapter.** `[T]` περιπατεῖτε ("walk", 2:6) is the first imperative of the letter's argument: 1:24–2:5 has none. One command governs four participles — rooted, being built up, established, overflowing — which describe the walking and are not further commands (Tool 3). Each element answers 1:3–23: "walk … the Lord" (περιπατέω with κύριος, 1:10 → 2:6); "just as you learned from Epaphras" (καθὼς ἐμάθετε, 1:7) → "just as you were taught" (καθὼς ἐδιδάχθητε, 2:7); the stability participles with "the faith" (τῇ πίστει, 1:23 → 2:7); thanksgiving (1:3, 12 → 2:7). περιπατέω ("walk") occurs in the letter only at 1:10, 2:6, 3:7 and 4:5 (SBLGNT, by lemma). *Surfaced by:* Tool 3, Tool 4 (οὖν, "therefore"), Tool 11 Move 4, Tool 10. The hinge of the letter is a call to keep going the way they began. *High confidence.*

2. **The polemic answers the rival with the confession's own words.** `[T]` The ground-clause ὅτι ἐν αὐτῷ ("for in Him") stands in Paul's letters only at 1:16, 1:19 and 2:9 (SBLGNT; the two other NT uses are in 1 John). πλήρωμα ("fullness") with κατοικέω ("dwell") stands in the NT only at 1:19 and 2:9. κεφαλή ("head") with ἀρχή ("rule") and ἐξουσία ("authority") stands in one NT verse only, 2:10. σταυρός ("cross") occurs in the letter only at 1:20 and 2:14; νεκρός ("dead") only at 1:18, 2:12 and 2:13 (all SBLGNT, by lemma). **Chance baseline, run here for the first time** (Book-Overview Tensions): of 1:15–20's 24 rarer words, the seven-verse stretch 2:8–14 shares seven, more than any other stretch of the letter; outside 2:5–19 no stretch shares more than three. *Surfaced by:* Tool 10, Tool 11 Move 4, Tool 2. The overview's "arsenal" claim stands on its specific links. As a measured design it is real but not singular: across the letter's ninety six-verse passages, fourteen have a partner stretch sharing seven or eight. *High confidence on the links; moderate–high on the design.*

3. **"Made complete in Him" (2:10) is spelt out as union, not addition.** `[T]` In ten verses ἐν αὐτῷ ("in Him") stands five times (2:6, 7, 9, 10, 15) — five of the letter's eight — and ἐν ᾧ ("in whom/which") twice (2:11, 12). Then three verbs compounded with σύν ("with"): συνθάπτομαι ("buried with", 2:12), συνεγείρω ("raised with", 2:12), συζωοποιέω ("made alive with", 2:13), with σὺν αὐτῷ ("together with Him") (SBLGNT). συνθάπτομαι occurs in the NT only here and Rom 6:4; συζωοποιέω only here and Eph 2:5. *Surfaced by:* Tool 10, Tool 7, Tool 4, Christological Reading. The rival offers something more. Paul answers that what the Colossians have is Christ himself, with whom they have died and risen. *High confidence.*

4. **The cross deals with everything the rival claims to manage — guilt, the record against us, and the powers.** `[T]` 2:13–15 runs three participle clauses into the cross: "having forgiven us all our transgressions" (2:13); "having blotted out the certificate of debt … nailing it to the cross" (2:14); "having stripped off the rulers and authorities … triumphing over them" (2:15). ἀρχαί and ἐξουσίαι ("rulers and authorities") are **created** through Christ at 1:16, **headed** by him at 2:10 and **stripped and paraded** at 2:15 — their only three appearances as a pair in the letter. 1:13's "domain (ἐξουσία) of darkness" is the fourth use of ἐξουσία (SBLGNT). χαρίζομαι ("to forgive") occurs only at 2:13 and 3:13. *Surfaced by:* Tool 3, Tool 10, Tool 11 Move 4, Biblical Theology. Who is the subject of 2:15, and whether ἐν αὐτῷ means "in Him" or "in it" (the cross), are open questions (Difficult Verses). The scope of the victory is not. *High confidence on the structure; moderate on the subject of 2:15.*

5. **The warning is defined by its standard: three "according to"s, one "not".** `[T]` "According to the tradition of men, according to the elements of the world, and **not** according to Christ" (2:8). Both phrases recur at the end of the polemic and frame it: τὰ στοιχεῖα τοῦ κόσμου ("the elements of the world") at 2:8 and 2:20, the letter's only two uses; "of men" at 2:8 and 2:22 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. And the "tradition" (παράδοσις) of 2:8 is set against what they "received" (παρελάβετε) at 2:6 — the pair Paul uses elsewhere for the handing-on of the gospel itself (1 Cor 11:23; 15:3; 2 Thess 3:6, SBLGNT) `[T]`/`[I]`. The only other NT verse with "tradition" and "men" together is Mark 7:8 `[T]`, where Jesus sets "the tradition of men" against God's command in a dispute about food and washing, which is the subject of Col 2:16–22. *Surfaced by:* Tool 4, Tool 3, Tool 10, Tool 11 (Mark 7). The question to ask of any teaching is not "is it deeper?" but "whose is it — human tradition, or Christ?" *High confidence on the links; moderate on the Mark 7 echo (Tool 11).*

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book level** (overview): to keep a young Gentile church walking "in Him" as they began (2:6), by setting before them the Christ in whom all the fullness dwells, so that no rival "philosophy" can persuade them they lack anything `[I]`.

**Passage level.** This is where the letter states its purpose in so many words. 2:6–7 is the command: keep walking in the Christ you received. 2:8 is the warning: let no one carry you off. 2:9–15 is the ground: everything the Colossians could want is already in Christ, and they are already in him `[T]`/`[I]`. The aim of the passage is **to make the readers' sufficiency in Christ concrete enough that no offer of "more" can look like gain** — by naming what they already have (fullness, circumcision, burial and resurrection, life, forgiveness) and what has already been done to everything that stood against them (the bond cancelled, the powers stripped) `[I]`.

What the passage contributes to the book is the argument. 1:15–20 confessed who Christ is; 1:24–2:5 bound the readers to the messenger and planted the first warning (2:4); this passage turns the confession into a defence. Chapter 2's later commands — "let no one judge you" (2:16), "let no one disqualify you" (2:18), "why … do you submit?" (2:20) — all draw their force from 2:9–15 `[I]`.

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement.** 1:15–20 confessed Christ as the one in whom "all the fullness was pleased to dwell", head of the body, creator of the rulers and authorities, reconciler "through the blood of His cross". 1:21–23 applied the reconciliation to the readers on condition that they "continue in the faith firmly established and steadfast". 1:24–2:5 showed how the gospel reached them, located "all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" in Christ (2:3), and named the danger: "so that no one will delude you with persuasive argument" (2:4). It ended with Paul seeing "the stability (στερέωμα) of your faith in Christ" (2:5) `[T]`. So the preceding movement has **(a)** a confession whose implications for the readers have been stated only once (1:21–23); **(b)** an unspecified danger (2:4: "no one … persuasive argument"); and **(c)** a church observed to be stable, but not yet told what to do.

**Necessity answer.** The passage exists here because the letter cannot move from description to command until it has a ground for the command. οὖν ("therefore", 2:6) draws the inference from everything since 1:3, most immediately from 2:5 (their "faith in Christ") and 2:3 (all treasures in him) `[T]`/`[I]`. The answers fall in order. **(c)** gets its command: walk in Him as you received him (2:6–7). **(b)** gets its content: philosophy and empty deceit, human tradition, the elements of the world (2:8). **(a)** gets its application, and a specific one: the confession's own words are applied to the readers — the fullness that dwells in him (1:19 → 2:9) fills them (2:10); the head of the body (1:18) is head of every power (2:10); the cross that made peace (1:20) cancels the bond against them (2:14). Placed anywhere else, the command would have no ground (if earlier) or the later commands of 2:16–23 would lack one (if later). This passage is where the letter's two halves meet.

**Implication.** 2:6–15 is not one argument among others in the polemic; it is the polemic's foundation. 2:16 opens with οὖν ("therefore") again, so everything from 2:16 to 2:23 is drawn from these ten verses `[T]`. A sermon on any of 2:16–23 that has not preached 2:9–15 has no ground to stand on.

**Revisit after Tool 11.** Move 4 sharpens this. The passage gathers the opening (1:7, 10, 12–14, 23) and the confession (1:15–20), and it plants vocabulary that chapter 3 picks up almost word for word: συνεγείρω ("raised with", 2:12 → 3:1); ἀπεκδύομαι ("strip off", 2:15 → 3:9); χαρίζομαι ("forgive", 2:13 → 3:13); περιτομή and ἀκροβυστία ("circumcision" and "uncircumcision", 2:11, 13 → 3:11). The passage is the hinge in both directions: it turns the confession into a defence and the defence into the new life of chapter 3.

**Immediately before:** 2:4–5 — the first warning, and Paul's joy at their order. **Immediately after:** 2:16–23 — "therefore let no one judge you … if you died with Christ … why do you submit?" **Section:** the polemic of chapter 2 (2:6–23), of which this is the first and doctrinal half `[T]`/`[I]`. NA28 starts a paragraph at 2:6 and another at 2:16 `[T: as NA28 prints it]`.

**Is the passage widely read out of context?** Yes. 2:9 is quoted as a free-standing proof of Christ's deity without its function — the "for" that grounds the warning. 2:11–12 is read for the baptism debates, in both directions, without asking what the passage is doing with it (Difficult Verses). 2:14 is read as "the law nailed to the cross" without asking what "the certificate of debt" is.

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|--------|---------|----------|
| 2:6–7 | **The charge** — "As you received Christ Jesus the Lord, walk in Him" | The command, with four participles describing the walk |
| 2:8 | **The warning** — "See to it that no one takes you captive" | The negative of 2:6–7: the alternative "walk", and its three standards |
| 2:9–10 | **The ground** — "For in Him the fullness dwells … and in Him you have been filled" | Why Christ alone is the standard (ὅτι, "for") |
| 2:11–12 | **Filled, as circumcised, buried and raised** — "In Him you were circumcised … buried with Him … raised with Him" | The fullness unpacked as union in his death and resurrection |
| 2:13–15 | **Made alive, forgiven, freed** — "you, being dead … He made you alive with Him … having forgiven … having blotted out … having stripped off" | The same union as God's act: life, forgiveness, the bond cancelled, the powers defeated |

**Device.** Linear argument with a **command → warning → ground** shape `[T]`. The ground (2:9–15) is built on **ἐν αὐτῷ / ἐν ᾧ** ("in Him / in whom") at the start of each unit — 2:9, 2:10, 2:11, 2:12 — and is closed by ἐν αὐτῷ at 2:15, the last word of the passage `[T]`. Inside the ground, 2:11–12 and 2:13 tell the same story twice. First in the passive, with the readers as subject ("you were circumcised … buried … raised"); then in the active, with God as subject ("He made you alive … having forgiven") `[T]`. The repetition moves from what happened to them to who did it `[I]`.

**Central assertions, by section:**

- 2:6–7 — "walk in Him" — *command*. The four participles are its manner and its ground (three of them describe what has been and is being done to them), not four new commands `[T]` for the grammar; `[I]` for the force of the perfect ἐρριζωμένοι ("having been rooted").
- 2:8 — "See to it that no one takes you captive" — *command* (warning).
- 2:9–10 — "in Him the fullness dwells … in Him you are filled" — *truth*, the ground of both commands.
- 2:11–12 — "in Him you were circumcised … buried with … raised with" — *truth*.
- 2:13–15 — "He made you alive with Him … He has taken it out of the way … He made a public display of them" — *truth*.

**Two commands, then eight indicative verbs.** The passage's weight falls on what is already true, not on what the readers must do `[T]`. That proportion is itself a finding against moralism (Christological Reading).

### 4. Linking Words

- **Ὡς … ἐν αὐτῷ περιπατεῖτε ("As … walk in Him"), 2:6.** Comparison: the walking is to match the receiving. There is no οὕτως ("so") in the Greek; the NASB95 and ESV supply "so" `[T]`.
- **οὖν ("therefore"), 2:6.** Inferential. It draws on 2:5 (their faith "in Christ") and, more widely, on 1:3–2:5 `[I]`. The letter's next οὖν is at 2:16, which draws on this passage `[T]`.
- **καθὼς ("just as"), 2:7.** "Just as you were taught" — the standard is the teaching already received, through Epaphras (1:7, also καθώς) `[T]`.
- **μή τις … ἔσται ("lest anyone … will be"), 2:8.** A warning with the future indicative, not the usual subjunctive. It treats the danger as a real prospect `[I]`, moderate — the grammar is unusual (Original Language Observations).
- **κατά … κατά … καὶ οὐ κατά ("according to … according to … and not according to"), 2:8.** Three standards, the third negated. The contrast is not between two philosophies but between two norms: human tradition and cosmic elements on one side, Christ on the other `[T]`.
- **ὅτι ("for, because"), 2:9.** Grounds the warning (and through it the command) in Christ's fullness `[T]`.
- **καί ("and"), 2:10.** Joins the two halves of the ground: in him the fullness dwells, *and* in him you are filled `[T]`.
- **ὅς ("who"), 2:10.** "Who is the head" — Christ. Three witnesses read the neuter ὅ ("which"), making the fullness the head (Textual Variants).
- **Ἐν ᾧ καί ("in whom also"), 2:11, and ἐν ᾧ καί ("in which/whom also"), 2:12.** The καί adds a further reality "also" in him `[T]`. The antecedent at 2:12 is open — "in whom" (Christ) or "in which" (baptism); both modern versions choose "in which" (Tool 8).
- **ἐν … ἐν … ἐν ("in … in … in"), 2:11.** "With a circumcision made without hands, *in* the stripping off of the body of the flesh, *in* the circumcision of Christ" — two further ἐν-phrases explaining the first `[T]`. Whether they are parallel (two descriptions of the same event) or sequential is debated `[S]`.
- **διὰ τῆς πίστεως ("through faith"), 2:12.** "Through faith in the working of God" — faith is the means by which they were raised with him, and its object is God's working that raised Christ `[T]`.
- **καί ("and"), 2:13.** "And you, being dead …" — a fresh start, resuming the readers as object `[T]` (it is the same frame as 1:21, Tool 11 Move 4).
- **Participles χαρισάμενος … ἐξαλείψας … προσηλώσας … ἀπεκδυσάμενος … θριαμβεύσας, 2:13–15.** Five aorist participles hang on three main verbs (συνεζωοποίησεν, "He made alive"; ἦρκεν, "He has taken away"; ἐδειγμάτισεν, "He made a public display"). They describe how the life was given and how the powers were dealt with `[T]`.
- **καί αὐτὸ ἦρκεν ("and He has taken it"), 2:14.** The perfect ἦρκεν ("has taken") is the only perfect among the main verbs: the bond, once removed, stays removed `[T]` for the tense; `[I]` for the force.

### 5. Parallels

Not Hebrew parallelism, but the passage has **paired prose structures** that bear on meaning:

- **The two halves of the ground, 2:9–10:** "in Him dwells all the fullness … in Him you have been filled" — ἐν αὐτῷ … πλήρωμα / ἐν αὐτῷ … πεπληρωμένοι ("fullness" / "filled") `[T]`. Synthetic: the second applies the first to the readers.
- **The stacked "according to"s, 2:8** (Tool 4) `[T]`.
- **Passive and active tellings, 2:11–12 / 2:13** (Tool 3): circumcised — buried with — raised with / made alive with — forgiven `[T]`. The same events seen from below and from above `[I]`.
- **Death and life, 2:12–13:** "raised Him from the dead (ἐκ νεκρῶν)" → "you, being dead (νεκρούς)" → "made you alive together with Him" — νεκρός twice in two verses, with life between `[T]`.
- **The two "strippings off", 2:11 / 2:15:** ἀπέκδυσις ("the stripping off") of the body of the flesh / ἀπεκδυσάμενος ("having stripped off") the rulers and authorities `[T]`. A deliberate pair: the cognate noun and verb occur in the NT only at 2:11, 2:15 and 3:9 (SBLGNT) `[T]`.

### 7. Vocabulary

**Proper-noun inventory.** Χριστὸς Ἰησοῦς ὁ κύριος ("Christ Jesus the Lord", 2:6) — the letter's fullest title, and the only verse in Colossians with all three names in one phrase (Ἰησοῦς occurs at 1:1, 1:3, 1:4, 2:6, 3:17, 4:11; SBLGNT) `[T]`; Χριστός at 2:8 and 2:11 `[T]`. **Greek-carrier check:** Ἰησοῦς is also the Greek name of Joshua, and in the Greek of Josh 5:2–9 it is Ἰησοῦς who circumcises Israel with knives of stone on entering the land `[T]` (Swete). Colossians says "the circumcision of *Christ*", not "of Jesus", at 2:11, so the name-hyperlink is not activated there `[I]`. *Uncertain — noted and not pursued.*

**παραλαμβάνω ("to receive"), 2:6.** *High confidence.* In Colossians only at 2:6 and 4:17 (Archippus "received" a ministry) (SBLGNT) `[T]`. In Paul it is the word for receiving a handed-down tradition: "I received (παρέλαβον) from the Lord what I also delivered (παρέδωκα) to you" (1 Cor 11:23); "I delivered … what I also received" (1 Cor 15:3); "the tradition (παράδοσιν) which you received (παρελάβοσαν) from us" (2 Thess 3:6) — the three NT verses outside John 19:16 joining the two roots (SBLGNT) `[T]`. The object here is not a tradition about Christ but "Christ Jesus the Lord" himself `[T]`. The contrast with "the tradition (παράδοσις) of men" (2:8) is pointed: they received a person, handed on to them through Epaphras; the rival offers a human tradition `[I]`, high.

**ῥιζόομαι, ἐποικοδομέω, βεβαιόω ("rooted", "built up", "established"), 2:7.** *High confidence.* ῥιζόομαι occurs in the NT only here and Eph 3:17 ("rooted and grounded in love") `[T]`. The images are of a plant and a building; the tenses differ — ἐρριζωμένοι is perfect ("having been rooted", once for all), ἐποικοδομούμενοι and βεβαιούμενοι are present ("being built up", "being established") `[T]`. The rooting is done; the building goes on `[I]`. βεβαιόω may carry a commercial-legal sense, "to confirm, guarantee" `[S]` (recalled; BDAG, verify).

**συλαγωγέω ("to carry off as booty"), 2:8.** *High confidence on the facts; moderate on the image.* Once in the NT; no word in Swete's LXX begins with its stem (SBLGNT; Swete) `[T]`. Literally "to lead away as spoil" `[S]` (recalled; the word is rare in Greek). The danger is not merely error but **abduction**: they would be someone's plunder `[I]`. It is answered at 2:15, where it is Christ (or God) who leads the powers in triumph — the would-be captor's masters made captives (Tool 11 Move 4) `[I]`, moderate.

**φιλοσοφία ("philosophy"), 2:8.** *High confidence.* Once in the NT (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Qualified by "and empty deceit" under one article (τῆς φιλοσοφίας καὶ κενῆς ἀπάτης): the phrase means "philosophy, that is, empty deceit", not two separate things `[I]`, moderate–high — a hendiadys-like construction (Original Language Observations). The word was used of any system of thought or way of life, including Jewish ones (Josephus calls the Jewish sects "philosophies") `[S]` (recalled). It does not of itself point to Greek academic philosophy.

**παράδοσις τῶν ἀνθρώπων ("the tradition of men"), 2:8.** *High confidence.* παράδοσις occurs in Colossians only here. With ἄνθρωπος in the same verse, only Col 2:8 and Mark 7:8 in the NT (SBLGNT) `[T]` (Tool 11).

**τὰ στοιχεῖα τοῦ κόσμου ("the elements of the world"), 2:8, 20.** *High confidence on the occurrences; uncertain on the sense — the overview's largest interpretive gap.* στοιχεῖον: Gal 4:3, 9; Col 2:8, 20; Heb 5:12; 2 Pet 3:10, 12 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. "Of the world" with it: Gal 4:3; Col 2:8, 20 only `[T]`. In the Greek OT: Wis 7:17 ("the working of the elements") and 19:18; 4 Macc 12:13 `[S]` — the scan's sense check confirms στοιχείων at Wis 7 `[T]`. The three main senses `[S]`: (a) the physical elements of the cosmos (earth, water, air, fire); (b) the elementary principles, the ABCs (as at Heb 5:12); (c) the spiritual powers associated with the elements or the heavenly bodies. **Text-first constraint:** in this passage the phrase stands beside "the tradition of men" as a *standard* (κατά, "according to"), and 2:20 speaks of having died "from" (ἀπό) them and of "submitting to decrees" `[T]`. Both suit "principles" or "powers"; neither requires the physical elements. The powers are dealt with at 2:15 under their own names (ἀρχαί, ἐξουσίαι), which is a small argument that στοιχεῖα is not simply another word for them `[I]`, moderate. **Do not decide this from the pulpit text** (Tool 8).

**θεότης ("Deity"), 2:9.** *High confidence.* Once in the NT (SBLGNT) `[T]`. To be distinguished from θειότης ("divinity, divine nature") at Rom 1:20 `[T]`. θεότης is the abstract noun of θεός ("God"): the being of God, not merely divine qualities `[S]` (recalled; the distinction is drawn by Lightfoot and Trench, verify). "All the fullness *of Deity*" makes 1:19's unqualified "all the fullness" explicit `[T]`.

**σωματικῶς ("bodily"), 2:9.** *High confidence on the facts; moderate on the sense.* Once in the NT `[T]`. Options `[S]`: (a) in bodily form — the incarnation, and now the risen body; (b) "really, in substance" (against the "shadow" of 2:17); (c) "as a whole, in one body" (the church as body, 1:18). The present tense κατοικεῖ ("dwells") makes it present, not past `[T]`. The adverb is the first of a run of σῶμα words in the chapter — "the body of the flesh" (2:11), "the substance (σῶμα) belongs to Christ" (2:17), "the whole body" (2:19), "severe treatment of the body" (2:23) (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Read with 2:17, "bodily" may already be setting up the contrast between the shadow and the substance `[I]`, moderate.

**πληρόω ("to fill"), 2:10.** *High confidence.* πεπληρωμένοι ("having been filled"), perfect passive participle: filled, and remaining so `[T]`. With πλήρωμα ("fullness") in the previous verse, it gives the keynote its sharpest form (overview microscript) `[T]`.

**περιτέμνω, περιτομή, ἀχειροποίητος ("circumcise", "circumcision", "made without hands"), 2:11.** *High confidence on the facts.* περιτομή occurs in Colossians at 2:11 (twice), 3:11 and 4:11 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. ἀχειροποίητος occurs in the NT only at Mark 14:58 (a temple "not made with hands"), 2 Cor 5:1 (a house "not made with hands") and here `[T]`. Its opposite, χειροποίητος ("made with hands"), is in the Greek OT the word for idols — Lev 26:1, 30; Isa 2:18; 10:11; 19:1; 21:9; 31:7; 46:6; Dan 5:4, 23 and others; fourteen passages in Swete, all of idols or idol-shrines (Isa 16:12) `[T]`. In the NT it is used of the temple (Mark 14:58; Acts 7:48) and, once, of physical circumcision: "the so-called 'circumcision', which is performed in the flesh by human hands (χειροποιήτου)" (Eph 2:11) `[T]`. **A circumcision "not made with hands" is therefore the work of God, set against human handiwork** `[I]`, high.

**ἀπέκδυσις ("stripping off"), 2:11, with ἀπεκδύομαι ("to strip off"), 2:15; 3:9.** *High confidence.* The noun occurs once in the NT; the verb only at 2:15 and 3:9 (SBLGNT) `[T]` (overview echo #15). A double compound (ἀπό + ἐκ + δύω): more than "put off" — "strip right off" `[S]` (recalled).

**τὸ σῶμα τῆς σαρκός ("the body of the flesh"), 2:11.** *High confidence on the facts; moderate–high on the reference.* The phrase "body … of the flesh" stands in the NT only at Col 1:22 — "in the body of **His** flesh through death" — and here (SBLGNT, by phrase search; the positive control returned both) `[T]`. At 1:22 it is Christ's body, given in death. That makes it at least possible, and on the letter's own usage likely, that "the stripping off of the body of the flesh" at 2:11 is Christ's death, and "the circumcision of Christ" a name for it (Difficult Verses) `[I]`, moderate–high. The phrase sits within a run of σάρξ in chapter 2 — 2:11, 2:13 ("the uncircumcision of your flesh"), 2:18, 2:23 `[T]`.

**βαπτισμός ("baptism, washing"), 2:12.** *High confidence on the facts; moderate on the significance.* Paul's usual word for Christian baptism is βάπτισμα (Rom 6:4) `[S]` (recalled); βαπτισμός occurs in the NT only at Mark 7:4 (the washing of cups and pots), Col 2:12, Heb 6:2 ("instruction about washings") and Heb 9:10 ("various washings") (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Here alone it means Christian baptism. Many witnesses replace it with the usual βάπτισμα (Textual Variants) `[T]`. Whether Paul chose the word that elsewhere names ritual washings to set baptism against them is possible but unprovable `[I]`, uncertain.

**συνθάπτομαι, συνεγείρω, συζωοποιέω ("buried with", "raised with", "made alive with"), 2:12–13.** *High confidence* (Headline 3). συνεγείρω occurs in the NT only at Eph 2:6, Col 2:12 and 3:1 (SBLGNT) `[T]`.

**παράπτωμα ("transgression, trespass"), 2:13.** *High confidence.* Twice in 2:13, its only uses in the letter `[T]`. With νεκρός ("dead") in the same verse: Eph 2:1, 2:5 and Col 2:13 only (SBLGNT) `[T]`. 1:14 used the more common ἁμαρτία ("sin") for the "forgiveness of sins" `[T]`.

**χειρόγραφον ("certificate of debt"), 2:14.** *High confidence on the facts; moderate on the reference.* Once in the NT `[T]`. In the Greek OT only in Tobit — 5:3 and 9:5, in both Greek texts — of a signed bond that entitles its holder to collect money (Swete) `[T]`. In the papyri the regular term for a hand-written IOU signed by the debtor `[S]` (recalled; standard). "Against us (καθ᾽ ἡμῶν) … hostile (ὑπεναντίον) to us" `[T]`. **What the debt-note is** — the Mosaic law as such, the law's verdict against transgressors, a heavenly record of sins, or humanity's own signed acknowledgement of obligation — is debated `[S]` (Difficult Verses).

**δόγμα ("decree"), 2:14.** *High confidence on the facts.* NT: Luke 2:1 (Caesar's decree), Acts 16:4 (the apostles' decrees), 17:7 (Caesar's decrees), Eph 2:15 ("the law of commandments in decrees"), Col 2:14 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. The verb δογματίζομαι ("to submit to decrees") follows at 2:20, its only NT use `[T]`. The rival's rules (2:20–21) are therefore described with the word used for what was cancelled at the cross `[I]`, high.

**προσηλόω ("to nail to"), 2:14.** *High confidence.* Once in the NT `[T]`. The only explicit mention in Paul of the nails of the crucifixion `[S]` (recalled; verify).

**δειγματίζω ("to make a public example of"), θριαμβεύω ("to lead in triumph"), 2:15.** *High confidence on the facts.* δειγματίζω occurs in the NT only at Matt 1:19 (Joseph not wishing to "disgrace" Mary publicly) and here; θριαμβεύω only at 2 Cor 2:14 and here (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Both are public-shame words. θριαμβεύω names the Roman triumph, in which a victorious general paraded his captives `[S]` (Historical Background). "Openly" (ἐν παρρησίᾳ) adds that it was done before all `[T]`.

**Ancient versions check.** The moderns agree, against the KJV, on ἐν αὐτῷ at 2:15 as personal ("in Him / through Him"). The **Vulgate** reads, as far as I recall, *in semetipso* ("in himself") — *uncertain, consider verifying*. If so, the Latin makes Christ the subject of 2:15 and the place of the triumph. **Translation-tradition split:** the Latin (if recalled correctly) and the modern versions read a person; the KJV and some modern dynamic versions read the cross. Both are grammatically possible; the choice changes who the sermon says triumphs — God by means of Christ, or Christ by means of his cross. *Verify the Vulgate wording before relying on it.*

**Pulpit divergence note (ESV).** Four places where the congregation will hear a decision the Greek leaves open. (1) **"Elemental spirits" (2:8)**: the ESV has decided for spiritual beings. Say that the Greek is "elements" and that it may mean principles. (2) **"God made you alive" (2:13)**: the Greek has only "He". It is defensible, but it closes a question that bears on 2:15. (3) **"In him" (2:15)**: the ESV keeps the personal reading, and so the congregation will not hear that "in it" (the cross) is possible. (4) **"Disarmed" (2:15)**: the congregation will not hear that 2:11's "putting off" and 2:15's "disarmed" are the same root, or that 3:9's "you have put off the old self" is the same verb. One place where the ESV serves better than the study text: **"filled in him" (2:10)** keeps the fullness keynote audible, and the NASB95's "made complete" does not.

### 9. Tone and Feel

**Confident, protective, celebratory.** The passage opens tender and steady ("as you received … walk", 2:6), turns watchful ("see to it", 2:8), and then builds into triumph (2:15) `[T]`. The movement of 2:9–15 is a crescendo: fullness, filling, circumcision, burial, resurrection, life, forgiveness, cancellation, nailing, stripping, public shaming, triumph `[T]`.

**Devices.** Accumulation of union-words (ἐν αὐτῷ, σύν-compounds); sudden concrete images after abstract ones — "nailing it to the cross", "triumphing over them" `[T]`; a shift from passive to active at 2:13 (God takes over as the actor) `[T]`; the closing image of a victory parade `[T]`/`[S]` for the background.

**Tonal shift.** One, at 2:8, from encouragement to warning; and back, from 2:9, into an increasingly exultant indicative `[T]`. The warning is framed on both sides by what they have.

**Soundtrack.** A march that becomes a triumphal procession: steady at 2:6–7, a drum-roll of alarm at 2:8, then brass from 2:9 to 2:15. The sermon should not end on the warning. The passage ends on the triumph.

Theodotion's Daniel 4 (Nebuchadnezzar's decree, rulers, authority, the Most High who rules, "transgressions") — royal decree vocabulary. **Two false friends caught by the sense check:** at Isaiah 8 the stem for προσηλόω ("nail to") caught προσῆλθον ("they came to"), and is dropped; in Esther 8 the stem for πίστις caught πιστευθέντων ("entrusted"), a cognate in another sense. **The scan's verdict:** the passage's field is that of *royal decree and public reversal*. No single OT chapter is its partner, and the circumcision texts (Gen 17; Lev 26; Deut 10; Jer 4) rank modestly or low. That fits the overview's "citation-light" finding `[T]`.

**Genesis 17:11–14 → Colossians 2:11, 13** *(moderate — overview row 9, confirmed)* `[T]` for the wording; `[I]` for the use

*The wording.* "You shall be circumcised in the flesh of your foreskin (τὴν σάρκα τῆς ἀκροβυστίας ὑμῶν), and it shall be the sign of the covenant" (Gen 17:11); the male "who is not circumcised in the flesh of his foreskin … shall be cut off" (17:14) (Swete) `[T]`. Colossians has τῇ ἀκροβυστίᾳ τῆς σαρκὸς ὑμῶν ("the uncircumcision of your flesh", 2:13) — the same two nouns, with the genitive **inverted** (overview) `[T]`.

*Source context (Move 1).* God's covenant with Abraham: a multitude of nations (17:4–6), the land (17:8), and circumcision as the sign "in your flesh" for every male, the uncircumcised to be cut off from his people (17:10–14) `[T]`. The function of the verses: to define covenant membership by a mark in the flesh `[I]`.

*What it adds.* The Colossians, Gentiles, were "dead in … the uncircumcision of your flesh" (2:13) — by Genesis 17's standard, outside the covenant and cut off `[I]`. Paul does not deny the description. He says they have received a circumcision "made without hands" in Christ (2:11), and that God made them alive "with Him" (2:13). The covenant sign is not abolished but fulfilled by God's own act in Christ `[I]`, moderate. Hays: volume moderate (two nouns, inverted); coherence high; the field rank is modest (median about 57th). **Moderate.**

**The circumcision of the heart — Deut 10:16; 30:6; Jer 4:4; Lev 26:41; Ezek 44:7, 9 → Colossians 2:11** *(moderate as a tradition; uncertain as a source)*

*The wording.* Deut 10:16 and Jer 4:4 (Swete) say "circumcise your hardness of heart" (περιτεμεῖσθε τὴν σκληροκαρδίαν) `[T]`; Deut 30:6 says the Lord will circumcise your heart (Hebrew וּמָל), which the Greek renders περικαθαριεῖ ("will cleanse") `[T]` — a translation difference that does not concern Colossians, since Colossians uses neither word. Lev 26:41 speaks of "their uncircumcised heart" in the same chapter that forbids idols "made with hands" (χειροποίητα, 26:1, 30) `[T]`. Ezek 44:7, 9 pairs "uncircumcised in heart and uncircumcised in flesh" (ἀπεριτμήτους καρδίᾳ καὶ ἀπεριτμήτους σαρκί) `[T]`.

*Source context (Move 1, the tradition).* In each text circumcision of the flesh is assumed, and Israel is called to (or promised) an inner circumcision that makes the outward sign true `[T]`.

*What it adds.* Colossians shares the idea of a circumcision not performed by human hands, but **none of the wording**: it says nothing of the heart `[T]` (overview row 10). Paul has the heart-language elsewhere — "circumcision is that which is of the heart, by the Spirit, not by the letter" (Rom 2:29) — and does not use it here `[T]`. The circumcision of 2:11 is located not in the heart but "in the circumcision of Christ" `[T]`. **Moderate as background; uncertain as an allusion.** The tradition supplies the category; Colossians fills it with Christ.

### 12. Genre

Pauline epistle; the passage is **exhortation grounded in doctrine**: two imperatives (2:6, 8) followed by an extended indicative ground (2:9–15) `[T]`. 2:13–15 has been read as a fragment of a hymn or confession `[S]`; the text shows rhythm and accumulated participles, but nothing that requires a pre-existing source `[I]`. Reading rule: read the indicatives as the reason for the imperatives, and preach them declaratively.

### 13. Copycat

**Largely N/A** — direct address to the church, with no narrative characters. The one live question: are the participles of 2:7 commands? No — they describe the walking, and three of them describe what has been done to the readers ("having been rooted", "being built up", "being established"; Tool 3). The one active participle, "overflowing with thanksgiving", is the fitting response, not a further task `[I]`.

### 14. Bible Timeline

This side of the cross, the resurrection and the ascension; before Christ's appearing (3:4) `[T]`. The passage speaks of the Christian's past in Christ — circumcised, buried, raised, made alive, forgiven (aorists) — and of a present state — "you have been filled" (perfect), "the fullness dwells" (present) `[T]`. **The timeline matters for 2:11:** the "circumcision made without hands" belongs to the new covenant order inaugurated by Christ's death. It does not belong to Israel's covenant sign, though it fulfils it `[I]`. **And for 2:14:** whatever the bond was, it was dealt with at a datable event, the crucifixion `[T]`.

### 15. Who Am I?

| Figure | Function |
|---|---|
| "You" (2:6–13) | **Us** — Gentile believers who received Christ, once dead in the uncircumcision of their flesh `[I]` |
| "Us" (2:13–14) | **Paul with the readers** — Jew and Gentile together forgiven, the bond "against us" `[I]` |
| "Anyone" who would take you captive (2:8) | **A voice to recognise**, not a person to identify with |
| The rulers and authorities (2:10, 15) | **The defeated enemy**, created through Christ (1:16), now headed and stripped |
| Christ (2:9–15) | **The one in whom all this happens** — the sphere, not the example |

The passage is direct address. The misreading to avoid is making the hearer the *agent* of 2:11–15 — "circumcise your heart", "strip off your sins", "nail your record to the cross" — when every verb in 2:11–15 has God (or Christ) as its agent and the readers as recipients `[T]`.

### 16. So What?

**Stage 1 — the author's intended response.** That the Colossians keep walking in Christ as they began, refuse every offer to take them captive to a human or cosmic standard, and do so because they already have in Christ all that God is and all that they need: circumcision, death and resurrection with him, life, forgiveness, and victory over the powers `[I]`.

**Stage 2 — worldview, behaviour, motivation.**

- *Worldview:* the Christian life is not a ladder from basic Christ to advanced something-else. It is living *in* the one you received. Completeness is a status given (2:10), not a level reached. Guilt has no remaining claim (the bond is cancelled), and the powers have no remaining authority (they are stripped and paraded).
- *Stop:* stop measuring your spiritual life by any standard other than Christ — tradition (however venerable), cosmic or spiritual principles (however impressive), or rules (however strict). Stop living as if the record against you were still current.
- *Start:* start being rooted and built up *in him* — through the teaching you received (2:7); start overflowing with thanksgiving, which is the posture of people who know they have been given everything.
- *Motivation:* "in Him you have been filled" (2:10); "He made you alive together with Him, having forgiven us all our transgressions" (2:13).

**Four audiences.**

- *For me:* where am I looking for a "more" — a technique, an experience, a regimen, a spiritual principle — that would add to Christ? Do I live as one whose debt is nailed to the cross, or as one still negotiating it?
- *For a Christian friend:* the friend who feels second-class because they lack some experience, practice or qualification others boast of needs to hear "you have been filled in him" and "a circumcision made without hands".
- *For the church community:* the commands are plural. A church guards itself together against being carried off, and its "walk" is corporate. The "us" of 2:13–14 joins Jew and Gentile in one forgiveness.
- *For an unbeliever:* "you, being dead" (2:13) is the starting point. The good news is not self-improvement but being made alive by God with Christ, and having the record cancelled.

**Prayer.** Father, we thank You that in Christ all the fullness of Deity dwells bodily, and that in Him we have been filled. We were dead, and You made us alive with Him; we were in debt, and You cancelled the bond and nailed it to the cross; we were under the powers, and You stripped them and led them in triumph. Keep us from every voice that would carry us off as plunder. Root us and build us up in Him, as we were taught, and make us overflow with thanksgiving.

#### Grammatical and syntactical features

- **The future indicative after μή (2:8).** Βλέπετε μή τις ὑμᾶς ἔσται ὁ συλαγωγῶν — literally "see lest anyone will be the one who carries you off". The future indicative, where the subjunctive is usual, presents the danger as a real prospect `[S]` (recalled from the grammars; Moule, BDF — verify). The articular participle ὁ συλαγωγῶν ("the one who carries off") makes the abductor a type `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **The single article in 2:8.** τῆς φιλοσοφίας καὶ κενῆς ἀπάτης: one article governing two nouns, so "philosophy, namely empty deceit" `[T]` for the grammar; `[I]` for the sense, moderate–high.
- **The present κατοικεῖ (2:9)** against the aorist of 1:19 (κατοικῆσαι, "to dwell"): what God was pleased to do (1:19) is a present reality (2:9) `[T]`.
- **The perfect πεπληρωμένοι (2:10)** — a completed state `[T]`.
- **The genitive τοῦ Χριστοῦ (2:11).** "The circumcision of Christ" — subjective (performed by Christ), objective (undergone by Christ — his death), or possessive (belonging to Christ, Christian circumcision) `[S]`. Difficult Verses.
- **The relative ἐν ᾧ (2:12).** Masculine "in whom" (Christ) or neuter "in which" (baptism) — the form is the same `[T]`.
- **The unexpressed subject of 2:13–15.** συνεζωοποίησεν ("He made alive") must have God as subject, since the object is "you … with Him" (Christ) and 2:12 has just named "God who raised Him" `[I]`, high. Whether the subject changes to Christ by 2:15 is debated; nothing in the Greek marks a change `[T]` (Difficult Verses).
- **The middle ἀπεκδυσάμενος (2:15).** In the letter's other use, 3:9, the middle with an object means "having stripped off *from yourselves* the old self" `[T]`. On that usage 2:15 would be "having stripped off from Himself the rulers and authorities" — the patristic and Lightfoot reading, with Christ as subject `[S]`. The common modern reading takes the middle as active, "having stripped (disarmed) the rulers" `[S]`. The letter's own usage favours the first `[I]`, moderate; it requires Christ, not God, as subject `[I]`.
- **The perfect ἦρκεν (2:14)** among aorists — "has taken away", with lasting effect `[T]`.

#### Wordplay and sound-features

- **πλήρωμα … πεπληρωμένοι (2:9–10):** fullness / filled `[T]`.
- **περιτομῇ ἀχειροποιήτῳ … περιτομῇ τοῦ Χριστοῦ (2:11):** the circumcision without hands *is* the circumcision of Christ `[T]`.
- **σοφία / φιλοσοφία (2:3, 8):** possible, not pressed (Tool 11 Move 4).

- **The issue:** whether "the head of all rule" is Christ (ὅς) or the fullness (ὅ).
- **Manuscript evidence:** NA28 ὅς with א A C K L P Ψ and most others, including 𝔪 and the Latin; ὅ with **𝔓⁴⁶ B D F G** (and part of Hilary) `[T]`.
- **Most likely original:** NA28's ὅς is followed with *moderate confidence*. The neuter has the two oldest witnesses and the Western text together — strong support. But it is easily explained as attraction to the neuter πλήρωμα ("fullness") two clauses back `[I]`.
- **Preaching implication:** small. Either way, Christ (or the fullness that dwells in him) is head of every power. Not for the pulpit.

#### 2:11 — "the body of the flesh" / "the body of the sins of the flesh"

- **The issue:** the majority text adds τῶν ἁμαρτιῶν ("of the sins").
- **Manuscript evidence:** the addition in א² D¹ K L Ψ 075 104 630 1505 𝔪 and the Syriac; the shorter text in 𝔓⁴⁶ א* A B C D* F G P and others, with the Latin and Coptic `[T]`.
- **Most likely original:** the shorter text — *high confidence*. The addition explains "the body of the flesh" as sinfulness, which closes off the reading that it is Christ's own body (Difficult Verses) `[I]`.
- **Preaching implication:** the KJV reader has "the body of the sins of the flesh". If asked, the oldest manuscripts lack "of the sins", and the shorter text leaves room for the reading that Paul is speaking of Christ's death.

#### 2:12 — βαπτισμῷ / βαπτίσματι ("baptism")

- **The issue:** the rarer word or Paul's usual one.
- **Manuscript evidence:** NA28 βαπτισμῷ with 𝔓⁴⁶ א² B D* F G 075 0278 6 365 1739 1881 and the Latin; βαπτίσματι with א* A C D² K L P Ψ and others, including 𝔪 `[T]`.
- **Most likely original:** βαπτισμῷ, the rarer word — *high confidence*. The change is a scribe normalising (Tool 7).
- **Preaching implication:** none.

#### 2:13 — [ἐν]; ὑμᾶς / ἡμᾶς; ἡμῖν / ὑμῖν

- **The issue:** whether "in" stands before "trespasses"; the second "you"; and whether "having forgiven *us*" or "*you*".
- **Manuscript evidence:** ἐν is omitted by א* B L Ψ and others, read by 𝔓⁴⁶ א¹ A C D F G K P and others, so NA28 brackets it. At the second "you" the NA28 reads ὑμᾶς with א* A C K L and others; 𝔓⁴⁶ B 33 read ἡμᾶς ("us"); א² D F G P Ψ and 𝔪 omit it. At "forgiven" the NA28 reads ἡμῖν ("us"); א² K* L P and others read ὑμῖν ("you") `[T]`.
- **Most likely original:** the NA28 text, with the switch from "you" to "us" at χαρισάμενος ἡμῖν — *moderate–high confidence*. Scribes moved in both directions, levelling the persons `[I]`.
- **Preaching implication:** the switch is part of the text. Paul includes himself, a circumcised Jew, in "all our transgressions" and in the bond "against us" `[I]`.

#### 2:7 and 2:15 — minor

- 2:7: τῇ πίστει ("in the faith") has variants ἐν and ἐν τῇ; "overflowing in thanksgiving" has a majority expansion, "overflowing *in it* with thanksgiving" (B D² and 𝔪) `[T]`. No change of sense.
- 2:15: 𝔓⁴⁶ B add καί ("and") before ἐδειγμάτισεν, making ἀπεκδυσάμενος a co-ordinate verb ("He stripped … and made a display") `[T]`. NA28 omits it. No change of sense.

### Historical and Cultural Background

#### Setting

- As in the overview: Paul imprisoned (4:3, 10, 18) `[T]`; the place not stated `[S]`; a Gentile church in the Lycus valley `[T]`/`[S]`.

#### What the original audience knew

1. **"Philosophy" was a way of life as well as a system.** Schools offered disciplines of diet, purity and ascetic practice alongside teaching; and Jewish writers (Philo, Josephus) called Judaism and its sects "philosophies" `[S]`. The word itself does not identify the rival teaching.
2. **The στοιχεῖα.** Greek thought knew the four elements (earth, water, air, fire), and later writings associated them, and the heavenly bodies, with spirits or powers to be placated `[S]`. Whether that association is as early as Colossians is debated `[S]` (verify). Gal 4:3–10 links "the elements" with calendar observance `[T]`.
3. **Circumcision.** A Gentile convert to Judaism was circumcised; Gentile believers in Christ, by the apostles' decision, were not (Acts 15) `[T]`/`[S]`. In a mixed town a teaching that offered "completion" might have included circumcision `[I]` (the overview: "suggests").
4. **The certificate of debt.** A χειρόγραφον was a hand-written acknowledgement of debt signed by the debtor; to cancel one it could be washed out (the ink was soluble) or struck through `[S]` (recalled; the "blotting out" verb fits washing or wiping). The idea that a bond was cancelled by nailing it up is attested late, if at all `[S]` — verify; the nailing is the cross's, not the bond's custom.
5. **The Roman triumph.** A victorious general entered Rome in procession, his captives and spoils displayed, the defeated kings in chains, sometimes executed at the end `[S]`. A Roman province would know the image from coinage and public monuments `[S]`.
6. **Crucifixion.** The condemned's charge (τίτλος) could be fixed to the cross (John 19:19) `[T]`. Paul's "nailing it to the cross" may draw on that: the debt is the charge nailed above the crucified `[S]`, moderate.

#### What this changes about how we read

- The triumph image turns the cross from defeat into victory parade. To a first-century ear, being "made a public display" was the fate of the conquered. The cross, the place of Christ's public shame, is where the *powers* were shamed `[I]`.
- The debt-note image makes forgiveness concrete and legal. A debt cancelled cannot be collected, and no one can sell you a further payment `[I]`.

### Original Audience Reception

#### Canonical audience

- **Canonical section:** Pauline corpus. The canonical reader comes from Romans (6:3–11, buried and raised with Christ), Galatians (circumcision, the elements of the world) and Ephesians (2:1–16) with the vocabulary already in the ear.
- **What is new here:** "raised with Him" already (2:12); circumcision "made without hands" (2:11); the bond and the triumph (2:14–15).

#### The first hearers

- **Who they were:** Gentile believers (2:13 "the uncircumcision of your flesh") `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **Their situation:** a church in good order (2:5), but within reach of a teaching that offered completion by "philosophy", tradition and elemental standards (2:8), and probably by circumcision (2:11) `[I]`.
- **What they brought:** a pagan past of placating powers (1:13, 21) `[I]`; possibly a sense that as uncircumcised Gentiles they were second-class in God's people `[I]`.

#### Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances

- **Shocking:** that they — uncircumcised — have been circumcised (2:11); that the cross was a triumph (2:15).
- **Surprising:** "you have been filled" (2:10) — already, not on reaching some level.
- **Comforting:** "having forgiven us *all* our transgressions" (2:13); the bond cancelled and nailed (2:14); the powers stripped (2:15).
- **Disturbing:** that they could be "carried off as booty" (2:8) — that a teaching could steal them.

#### What we bring that they didn't

- *A narrow idea of "philosophy"* as academic argument; theirs included a way of life and its disciplines.
- *Disenchantment:* we hear "rulers and authorities" as politics only, or not at all; they heard spiritual powers.
- *Baptism debates:* we come to 2:11–12 armed for a denominational argument; they heard it as a description of their union with Christ.

#### Implication for the sermon

Recover the triumph. The passage ends not with "beware" but with the powers paraded. The warning lands best when the congregation has first heard how much they have.

### Biblical-Theological Themes

#### Circumcision and covenant

- **Where this passage sits:** fulfilment. The covenant sign in the flesh (Gen 17) and the promised circumcision of the heart (Deut 30:6) are met in "the circumcision of Christ" (2:11) `[I]`.
- **Before:** Gen 17:10–14; Deut 10:16; 30:6; Jer 4:4; Ezek 44:7–9.
- **After:** "there is no distinction between … circumcised and uncircumcised … but Christ is all" (3:11) `[T]`; Phil 3:3, "we are the circumcision" `[T]`.
- **What it contributes:** Gentile believers lack nothing that the covenant sign signified. They have it in Christ.
- **Confidence:** high (theme); moderate (specific sources).

#### Death and resurrection with Christ

- **Where this passage sits:** the believer's participation in Christ's death and resurrection, by faith, signified in baptism (2:12).
- **Before:** Rom 6:3–11; the Passover and exodus pattern of death and new life `[S]`.
- **After:** 3:1–4, "if you have been raised with Christ … your life is hidden with Christ in God" `[T]`.
- **What it contributes:** union makes the "more" impossible. One cannot add to a death and resurrection that have already happened to you.
- **Confidence:** high.

#### The defeat of the powers

- **Where this passage sits:** the cross as the decisive victory.
- **Before:** Gen 3:15 (the serpent's head crushed); the LORD's triumph over Pharaoh's gods (Exod 12:12; 15) `[S]`; Ps 68:18 (the ascended LORD leading captives) `[T]`.
- **After:** the powers subjected (1 Cor 15:24; Eph 1:21); final defeat (Rev 20) `[T]`.
- **What it contributes:** the "elements" and "powers" the rival would placate are already beaten and shamed.
- **Confidence:** high (theme).

**Confluence — climactic convergence.** The three themes meet at the cross: the circumcision of Christ (his death, if the reading holds), the burial and resurrection with him, and the triumph over the powers all happen "in Him" at one event.

### Christological Reading

*NT passage.* Christ is named three times (2:6, 8, 11) and referred to as "Him" throughout `[T]`. The passage's Christological claims:

1. **Christ is the full dwelling of God** — "in Him all the fullness of Deity dwells bodily" (2:9), the strongest statement of Christ's deity in the letter `[T]`.
2. **Christ is the sphere of the believer's life** — "in Him" five times (Headline 3) `[T]`.
3. **Christ's death and resurrection are the believer's** — circumcised in his circumcision, buried and raised with him, made alive with him (2:11–13) `[T]`.
4. **Christ's cross is the place of forgiveness and of victory** (2:14–15) `[T]`.
5. **Christ is head of every power** (2:10) — the creator of 1:16, now their head and conqueror `[T]`.

**Typology in view:** circumcision (Gen 17) → "the circumcision of Christ". Tests: theological category (covenant sign) — pass; NT precedent (Rom 2:29; Phil 3:3) — pass; escalation (made without hands; the whole body of the flesh, not a part) — pass; authorial pattern (Deut 30:6 already points inward) — pass. **High confidence as typology**, whichever reading of the genitive is taken.

**Moralism check.**

- *The "be like X" temptation:* turning 2:11–15 into tasks — "circumcise your heart", "put off the flesh", "triumph over the powers".
- *The gospel grounding:* every verb in 2:11–15 has God or Christ as agent; the readers receive (Tool 15).
- *Christ as hero:* the triumph is his. The passage's two commands (2:6, 8) stand on eight indicatives (Tool 3).

### Difficult / Contested Verses

#### 2:11 — "the circumcision of Christ"

- **Category:** doctrinal; denominational (the baptism–circumcision link).
- **The difficulty:** what "the circumcision of Christ" is, and what "the stripping off of the body of the flesh" means.
- **Rhetorical function in context:** the first unpacking of "filled in Him" (2:10). It must be something the readers have *in Him*, done "without hands", and closely tied to burial in baptism (2:12) `[I]`.
- **The main interpretive options** `[S]` (recalled; verify): (1) **Christ's death** — "the circumcision of Christ" is the stripping off of his body of flesh at the cross, in which believers share (objective genitive); (2) **conversion-circumcision by Christ** — Christ circumcises believers spiritually, stripping off "the flesh" as the sinful nature (subjective genitive; the NIV84's "done by Christ" and "sinful nature"); (3) **Christian circumcision, that is, baptism** — the circumcision belonging to Christ's people (genitive of possession), identified with baptism in 2:12.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** "the body of the flesh" is used in the NT only here and at 1:22, of Christ's body in death `[T]`. The next clause is "buried with Him" (2:12), which presupposes a death `[T]`. Both favour option 1 `[I]`, moderate–high. Option 2 needs σῶμα τῆς σαρκός to mean "the sinful nature", which the letter nowhere else does — and the majority text's addition "of the sins" (Textual Variants) is the scribes making that reading possible `[I]`. Option 3 is a downstream inference from 2:12, not what 2:11 says, though baptism is clearly in view in the next verse `[I]`.
- **Suggested handling:** address briefly. Preach that believers were circumcised *in Christ's death* — "without hands", God's act — and that baptism signifies their burial with him. Do not make the sermon a defence of either side of the infant-baptism debate. The text's point is union, not sacramental theology. *Confidence:* moderate–high for option 1.

#### 2:12 — baptism and faith

- **Category:** doctrinal (baptismal regeneration; the mode and subjects of baptism) `[S]`.
- **The difficulty:** whether baptism effects the union, or signifies it.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the raising is "through faith (διὰ τῆς πίστεως) in the working of God" `[T]`; baptism is the occasion "in which" (or Christ is the one "in whom") it happens `[T]`. The text joins baptism and faith without explaining their relation `[I]`.
- **Suggested handling:** acknowledge. Keep "through faith in the working of God" in view, and avoid importing a denominational answer the verse does not give.

#### 2:14 — what is the "certificate of debt"?

- **Category:** doctrinal (law and gospel); weaponised ("the law is nailed to the cross", used against any moral law) `[S]`.
- **The main options** `[S]`: (1) the Mosaic law itself, with its decrees; (2) the law's verdict against transgressors — the debt incurred by breaking it; (3) a heavenly record of sins (Jewish apocalyptic); (4) humanity's own acknowledgement of obligation to God, Jew and Gentile.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the bond is "against *us*" and "hostile to *us*" `[T]` — Paul includes himself, which suits a law-related debt `[I]`. It "consists of decrees" (τοῖς δόγμασιν), and Eph 2:15 uses δόγμα for the law's commandments `[T]`. But what is nailed is the *debt-note*, not the commandments as such: a bond's power is its claim to be paid `[I]`. Option 2 best fits the wording, with option 1 nearby `[I]`, moderate.
- **Suggested handling:** address briefly. Say that what was cancelled is the record of our debt; do not preach "God's moral will is abolished".

#### 2:15 — who strips, and "in Him" or "in it"?

- **Category:** exegetical, with Christological weight.
- **The main options** `[S]`: (1) **God** is subject throughout 2:13–15: he disarmed the powers and triumphed over them *in Christ* ("in Him"); (2) **Christ** becomes subject by 2:14b or 2:15: he stripped off from himself the powers (the middle as in 3:9), and triumphed over them *in it* — the cross.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the grammar marks no change of subject `[T]`, which favours God `[I]`. The letter's other use of the middle ἀπεκδύομαι (3:9) favours "stripped off from himself", which suits Christ `[I]`. "In Him" fits the passage's refrain (Headline 3); "in it" fits the nearest antecedent, the cross `[I]`. No option is excluded; the dig leans towards option 1 with "in Him", following the refrain, at *moderate* confidence.
- **Suggested handling:** do not stake the sermon on the subject. Both readings put the victory at the cross and in Christ.

#### 2:8 — the στοιχεῖα

- **Category:** exegetical, with pastoral weight (spiritual powers).
- **Suggested handling:** tell the congregation the word is "elements" and has more than one meaning; preach the contrast the text makes — any standard "not according to Christ". *Confidence on the sense:* uncertain (Tool 7).

#### 2:9 — "the fullness of Deity bodily"

- **Category:** apologetic and doctrinal (the deity of Christ; against adoptionist or Gnostic readings) `[S]`.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** θεότης, not θειότης — the being of God `[T]`/`[S]`; present tense — now, in the risen Christ `[T]`. A strong text for the deity of Christ, used in context as the ground of the readers' completeness `[I]`.
- **Suggested handling:** preach it, in its function.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: Preaching against a reconstructed "Colossian heresy"

- **What it looks like:** a lecture on first-century Phrygian syncretism, Jewish mysticism or Gnosticism, then its modern equivalent.
- **Why it's wrong:** the passage names standards, not a system — "the tradition of men", "the elements of the world", "not according to Christ" (2:8). The reconstruction is `[S]` (overview Trap 2).
- **The corrective:** preach the test the text gives — *is this according to Christ?* — and let the congregation recognise their own "more".

### Pitfall: Turning the indicatives into tasks

- **What it looks like:** "We must circumcise our hearts, put off the flesh, nail our sins to the cross, triumph over the powers."
- **Why it's wrong:** every verb in 2:11–15 has God or Christ as its agent; the readers are circumcised, buried, raised, made alive and forgiven. The passage has two commands and eight indicatives.
- **The corrective:** preach what has been done, then the two commands as the response — walk in him; don't be carried off.

### Pitfall: Making 2:11–12 the sermon's battleground for baptism

- **What it looks like:** a sermon that becomes a defence of infant baptism (circumcision → baptism) or of believer's baptism (through faith).
- **Why it's wrong:** the passage's point is union with Christ in his death and resurrection, received through faith and signified in baptism. It does not explain the relation of circumcision and baptism.
- **The corrective:** say what the text says — circumcised *in Christ*, buried *with him* in baptism, raised *through faith* — and keep the debate for another setting.

### Pitfall: "The law was nailed to the cross" — so God's moral will is gone

- **What it looks like:** using 2:14 to dismiss any moral obligation.
- **Why it's wrong:** what is nailed is the *certificate of debt* "against us", not righteousness; the letter goes straight on to "put to death" and "put on" (3:5–17).
- **The corrective:** preach the debt cancelled and its claim on us ended.

### Pitfall: Leaving the powers in the first century — or preaching fear of them

- **What it looks like:** either silence about "rulers and authorities", or a sermon that makes the congregation more afraid of spiritual powers.
- **Why it's wrong:** the passage names the powers in order to show them defeated, stripped and paraded.
- **The corrective:** name them — and name them as beaten. The fear is what the rival sells; the triumph is what Christ gives.
