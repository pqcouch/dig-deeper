## Positional Frame (Phase 0.6)

**What the letter has set up.** The eleven claims fall into three groups `[T]`:
- **the thanksgiving's climax (1:12–14):** the Father "qualified you" for "the portion of the lot of the holy ones in light", "rescued us from the authority of darkness", and gave "redemption, the forgiveness of sins". Five claims meet here: C61, C62, C63, C69, C70, and the first half of C67;
- **the letter's architecture:** the frame (C65), the summary unit (C66), the joints (C71), the speech thread (C68), the dwelling verbs (C72), and the pronoun pattern across 1:13–14 and 2:13 (C67);
- **the letter's setting:** C69 bears on where and with whom Colossians was written, since Luke is named at 4:14.

**The test.** Audits 1–4 set it, and this audit keeps it: **an external source must add something that the letter's own chains, Paul's ordinary idiom, or the shared vocabulary of the early church do not already supply.** For the architecture claims there is a second test: **a design must beat chance.** Audits 3 and 4 found that most design claims fail the second test. This audit ran a baseline for every design claim, including the ones the overview rated highest.

## The Instrument

Three new faults were found in this run, and one known fault matters more than it seemed to.

- **The scan is nearly blind to 1:12–14.** It cannot see ἐρρύσατο ("he rescued") or μεθίστημι ("transfer") (known, audit 4). It does not link ἀπολύτρωσις ("redemption") with the verb λυτρόω ("redeem"). It also miscounts ἀφέστηκεν ("has stood far off", Isa 59:11, 14) as ἄφεσις ("forgiveness"). So every proposed partner of 1:12–14 ranks low and unstably, and the stable top three (Wis 18, Dan 4, Isa 59) owe part of their rank to these faults. **Every verdict on 1:12–14 below rests on direct searches, not on scan ranks.** Auditor A found this independently.
- **The SBLGNT lemma index files δεῖ ("must") under δέω ("bind").** A lemma count of δεῖ in Colossians therefore returns three verses, including δέδεμαι ("I am bound", 4:3). Count δεῖ by form. Auditor B found this.
- **The corpus's SBLGNT layer lacks Rom 16:25–27.** Romans ends at a bracketed 16:24. Any frame test of Romans therefore misses the doxology's μυστήριον ("mystery") and φανερόω ("make manifest"). Auditor B found this.
- **Swete prints Esther's Addition C inside 4:17 as bracketed sub-verses [1]–[30]**, not as Rahlfs's lettered verses. So audit 4's "Esth 4:17g–h" cannot be checked from the corpus. Swete's sub-verses 9–10 hold the relevant lines.

## Claims Audited

### Claim C61: Psalm 106 LXX (107 Hebrew) behind Colossians 1:13–14

**Source:** audit 4's queue, found by both auditors independently; low–moderate.\
**Claim:** Ps 106:2–20 LXX, the redeemed (106:2) rescued (106:6, 20) and brought out of darkness (106:10, 14), stands behind 1:13–14.

**Findings** (Auditor A; reproduced in part):
- **The shape is unique in Swete.** Rescue, redemption and the redeemed sitting in darkness come together only in Ps 106 LXX. Adding σκότος ("darkness") to ῥύομαι ("rescue") + λυτρ- ("redeem") within twelve verses leaves Ps 68 and Ps 106 LXX, and in Ps 68 the darkness falls on the enemies (68:24) `[T]`.
- **But the psalm never says "rescued from darkness."** Its ῥύομαι takes "distresses" (106:6) and "corruptions" (106:20); leaving the dark is ἐξάγω ("bring out", 106:14) `[T]`. The Hebrew agrees: יֹשְׁבֵי חֹשֶׁךְ ("sitters in darkness", 107:10) and מֵחֹשֶׁךְ ("from darkness", 107:14) `[T]`.
- **Its signature phrases go to Luke, not to Colossians.** Luke 1:79 repeats Ps 106:10 exactly: ἐν σκότει καὶ σκιᾷ θανάτου καθημένοις ("sitting in darkness and the shadow of death"). Luke 1:74 has ἐκ χειρὸς ἐχθρῶν ("from the hand of enemies"), as at Ps 106:2. Colossians has none of these phrases `[T]`.
- **The rival is an equal partner.** Isa 42:7 has καθημένους ἐν σκότει ("sitting in darkness"), the same Hebrew phrase as Ps 107:10. Isa 42:16 adds what the psalm lacks, "I will make the darkness into light for them". But it has no ῥύομαι ("rescue") or λυτρόω ("redeem") `[T]`. Each text supplies half of 1:12–14.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | The common Greek psalter |
| Volume | Weak | Three shared lemmas in different syntax; no shared phrase |
| Recurrence | Weak | No other Ps 106 contact in Colossians or in Paul |
| Thematic Coherence | Possible | The redeemed in darkness fits 1:13; Isa 42:7, 16 fits equally |
| Historical Plausibility | Possible | As idiom passed on (compare the Benedictus), not as a text Paul draws on |
| History of Interpretation | Weak | No owned commentator cites Ps 107 at 1:13; Pao cites Isa 42:7, 16 and Exod 6:6 `[S: Logos 5]` |
| Satisfaction | Weak | Explains neither "authority", "transfer", nor the light |

**Verdict:** Needs reframing.\
**Reasoning:** Ps 106 LXX is a source of the "redeemed from darkness" idiom, and Luke 1:74–79 uses it plainly. Colossians shares the idiom, not the psalm.\
**Rating:** **low** (from low–moderate). Restate it as idiom, with Isa 42:7, 16 as an equal partner.\
**Book-overview action:** row 16 and the queue note (see *Recommended revisions*).

### Claim C62: Deuteronomy 9:26–29 behind Colossians 1:12–14

**Source:** audit 4's queue (Auditor A of audit 4); moderate.\
**Claim:** Deut 9:26–29 LXX calls the people God's μερίς ("portion", 9:26) and κλῆρος ("lot", 9:29), redeemed (λυτρόω, "redeem", 9:26) from Egypt. That is all three Colossian ideas within four verses.

**Findings:**
- **The combination is rare.** μερίς ("portion") + κλῆρος ("lot") + a λυτρ- word ("redeem") within four verses occurs in Swete only at Deut 9:26–29 and in Esther's Addition C, which copies it `[T]`.
- Esther C (Swete 4:17, sub-verses [8]–[10]) has μὴ ὑπερίδῃς τὴν μερίδα σου ἣν σεαυτῷ ἐλυτρώσω ἐκ γῆς Αἰγύπτου ("do not overlook your portion, which you redeemed for yourself out of Egypt") and "be gracious to your lot". The same prayer has ῥῦσαι ("rescue") and ἐξουσία ("authority"). That makes it **the densest Greek OT match to 1:12–14**, though it has no darkness or light `[T]`.
- **The Hebrew** has נַחֲלָה ("inheritance") at both 9:26 and 9:29, so one word is rendered twice by different Greek nouns, as claimed. The verb at 9:26 is פָּדִיתָ ("you ransomed") `[T]`.
- **The direction is reversed.** In Deut 9 (and 32:9) Israel *is* God's portion. In Col 1:12 believers *receive* a portion: εἰς τὴν μερίδα τοῦ κλήρου τῶν ἁγίων ("for the portion of the lot of the holy ones") `[T]`.
- **The nearer formula** is the receiving sense of μερὶς καὶ κλῆρος ("portion and lot", Deut 10:9; 12:12; 14:27–29; 18:1; Josh 19:9). In the NT, **Acts 8:21 and Col 1:12 are the only two verses with both words** (SBLGNT; reproduced) `[T]`.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Deuteronomy LXX; Esther C shows the passage reused in prayer |
| Volume | Possible | Three ideas in four verses; Colossians has the noun ἀπολύτρωσις, not the verb |
| Recurrence | Weak | No other Deut 9 contact in Colossians |
| Thematic Coherence | Possible | Exodus redemption with inheritance; but the portion's direction is reversed |
| Historical Plausibility | Possible | Esther C shows the cluster alive in Greek-Jewish prayer |
| History of Interpretation | Possible | Deut 32:9 is cited at 1:12 by Pao, Osborne and Brown (and Wall). Deut 9:26 is cited only by Merkle, at Eph 1:7 `[S: Logos 5]` |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Explains the three ideas together; the formula and Eph 1:7 explain the wording better |

**Verdict:** Confirmed with nuance.\
**Reasoning:** the rarity is real, but Deut 9 is a prayer-cluster, not a source. The receiving formula (Deut 10:9; Acts 8:21) explains Col 1:12's wording better, and Paul's own Eph 1:7 supplies the redemption clause.\
**Rating:** **low–moderate** (from moderate).\
**Book-overview action:** row 16, and row 13 for the formula. Give the Esther reference in Swete's numbering, or check Rahlfs's letters before citing "4:17g–h".

### Claim C63: Luke 1:68–79 with Colossians 1:13–14

**Source:** audit 4's queue; moderate, as shared idiom.\
**Claim:** these are the only NT places where ῥύομαι ("rescue") and a λυτρ- word ("redeem") stand within twelve verses, with forgiveness and darkness nearby.

**Findings:**
- **The "only" holds** (SBLGNT; reproduced): Luke 1:68/74 and Col 1:13/14. It still holds at twenty verses. The nearest miss is Rom 7:24 → 8:23, twenty-four verses apart `[T]`.
- **A chance baseline** (Auditor A, scattering each word family at random 2,000 times):
  - rescue + redeem alone is ordinary: chance gives about one such pair, and 26 % of runs do as well;
  - adding forgiveness (0.1 % of runs) or darkness (0.75 %) makes the cluster rare;
  - all four together: under 0.05 %.
- Natural clustering of salvation words means these figures overstate the rarity. But only two examples exist.
- **The contact is lexical and thematic, not phrasal.** Luke's phrases come from Ps 105–106 LXX; Colossians' do not.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Possible | Shared idiom needs no written text |
| Volume | Possible | Four shared lemmas, no shared phrase |
| Recurrence | Possible | 1:12–14 also meets Acts 26:18, Luke 22:53 and Acts 8:21 (C69) |
| Thematic Coherence | Strong | Thanks for a past rescue and redemption into a new realm |
| Historical Plausibility | Strong | As liturgical and teaching idiom |
| History of Interpretation | Weak | No owned commentator compares the Benedictus `[S: Logos 5]` |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Places 1:13–14 in an idiom field |

**Verdict:** Confirmed, as shared idiom.\
**Rating:** **moderate** (unchanged). Add that the pair alone is within chance, and that the cluster is rare only with forgiveness and darkness.

### Claim C69: The Lukan affinities of Colossians

**Source:** overview, Presenting Situation and target 5; moderate.\
**Claim:** Colossians shares conversion and mission idiom with Luke–Acts in three places:
- Acts 26:18 at 1:12–14 (six rarer lemmas against three);
- Luke 1:68–79 at 1:13–14;
- Luke 8:15 at 1:6–11.

**Findings:**
- **Acts 26:18 re-counted.** It shares six lemmas with 1:12–14 (SBLGNT): σκότος ("darkness"), φῶς ("light"), ἐξουσία ("authority"), ἄφεσις ("forgiveness"), ἁμαρτία ("sin") and κλῆρος ("lot"). "Six against three" holds at a wide definition of "rarer", and any narrower one widens the gap `[T]`.
- **Two more exclusive contacts** (reproduced) `[T]`:
  - ἡ ἐξουσία τοῦ σκότους ("the authority of darkness") stands **only at Luke 22:53 and Col 1:13**. Eph 6:12 has the two words apart;
  - μερίς + κλῆρος ("portion" and "lot") stand together only at **Acts 8:21 and Col 1:12**.
- **The local contact is an outlier.** Auditor A took all 1,855 three-verse Pauline windows. Only two distinct passages match a single Luke–Acts verse on six or more rarer lemmas. One is 1 Cor 11:22–25 against Luke 22:19–20, the Last Supper words, a known shared tradition. **The other is Col 1:12–14 against Acts 26:18.**
- **The letter as a whole is the opposite.** Across the whole letter, Colossians has **the least Lukan vocabulary of the thirteen Pauline letters** (0.166, or 0.186 leaving Ephesians out, against 0.197–0.254 for the others). Only three of its lemmas occur elsewhere only in Luke–Acts. "Lukan affinities" is true of 1:12–14 and false of the letter.
- **The Luke 8:15 leg is weak.** καρποφορέω ("bear fruit") and ὑπομονή ("endurance") are in different Colossian verses (1:6, 10; 1:11). ὑπομονή is common in Paul (16 of its 31 NT verses). The sower's "bear fruit … hear … word" stands in all three Synoptics `[T]`. It is sower tradition, not Luke.
- **Direction.** Acts 26:18 is put on Christ's lips as Paul's commission. The contact may be Luke reporting Paul's conversion vocabulary as much as Paul echoing Luke's `[I]`.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Possible | Shared mission idiom; literary access untestable |
| Volume | Strong (1:12–14) / Weak (1:6–11) | Six lemmas and two exclusive phrases, against shared sower tradition |
| Recurrence | Possible | Four Lukan contacts converge on 1:12–14, and none elsewhere |
| Thematic Coherence | Strong | Conversion as transfer from darkness and Satan's authority into light, forgiveness and a lot |
| Historical Plausibility | Possible | Luke is named at 4:14 `[T]`; Acts 26:18 is presented as Paul's commission |
| History of Interpretation | Possible | Dunn reports Aletti, who "wonders whether it points to Lukan authorship"; Pao reads Acts 26:18 as confessional `[S: Logos 5]` |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Explains 1:12–14 well and the rest of the letter not at all |

**Verdict:** Confirmed with nuance: strong locally, wrong as a description of the letter.\
**Rating:**
- **moderate–high** for "the Lukan conversion idiom of 1:12–14" (Acts 26:18; Luke 22:53; Acts 8:21; Luke 1:68–79);
- **low** for "the Lukan affinities of Colossians";
- Luke 8:15 → shared sower tradition.

**Book-overview action:** retitle and restate in the Presenting Situation; any authorship inference stays `[I]`.

### Claim C70: The exodus redemption pattern behind 1:12–14

**Source:** overview row 16; moderate, as pattern with no single source.\
**Claim:** Col 1:12–14 uses exodus redemption language: Exod 6:6–8; 14:30; Deut 9:26–29; Ps 106 LXX.

**Findings:**
- **ῥύομαι ("rescue") is not an exodus word in Swete.** Of its 184 verses, 9.2 % lie in an exodus context (within three verses of Egypt, Pharaoh or the sea), against a baseline of 9.7 %. λυτρόω ("redeem") is concentrated at about twice the baseline (19.8 %) `[T]`.
- **Rescue + redeem within twelve verses:** 18 OT clusters. Only 5 are clearly exodus-related (Exod 6:6; Esther C; Isa 51:10–11; 52:3–9; 63:4–16). The other 13 are psalms of distress, Proverbs, Hosea and Daniel `[T]`.
- **Adding the allotted lot narrows it to Exod 6:6–8 and Esther C** `[T]`. Exod 6:6–8 is the one passage with the whole movement in order:
  - ῥύσομαι ὑμᾶς ἐκ τῆς δουλίας ("I will rescue you from the slavery");
  - λυτρώσομαι ("I will redeem");
  - λήμψομαι … λαόν ("I will take you as a people");
  - εἰς τὴν γῆν … ἐν κλήρῳ ("into the land … as a lot").

  That is rescue from a power, entry into a realm, and an allotted share, the sequence of Col 1:12–13 `[T]`.
- **Exod 14:30** contributes only ἐρρύσατο ("he rescued"). **Ps 106 LXX** has no exodus context at all: no Egypt, Pharaoh or sea, and the redeemed are gathered "from the lands" (106:3) `[T]`. It should leave the row.
- **The light/darkness element is missing from all four named texts.** The exodus tradition that supplies it is the darkness plague: Exod 10:23, "all the sons of Israel had light", retold at Wis 18:1–9, "for your holy ones there was very great light … they give thanks" `[T]`. Audit 4 rated Wisdom 17–18 *low* as a field, because its scan rank rested on false friends `[S: audit 4]`. Auditor A, working without the scan, re-proposes it on its wording. It is recorded below as a candidate, not restored.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Central texts |
| Volume | Possible | Exod 6:6–8 gives rescue + redeem + lot; no shared phrase |
| Recurrence | Possible | Several exodus texts each supply a part |
| Thematic Coherence | Strong | Rescue, transfer, allotted share |
| Historical Plausibility | Strong | The exodus as the pattern of redemption is common Jewish and Christian thought |
| History of Interpretation | Strong | Pao (Exod 6:6 LXX; Isa 41–44; Hos 13:14) and Osborne (Exod 6:6; 14:30; Deut 7:8) `[S: Logos 5]` |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Explains the sequence; the light needs the plague tradition or Isa 42 |

**Verdict:** Confirmed with nuance.\
**Rating:** **moderate**, as a pattern anchored in Exod 6:6–8. Ps 106 leaves the row. Exod 14:30 is supporting only. Say that ῥύομαι by itself is not an exodus marker. This **raises Exod 6:6–8 from audit 4's low–moderate to moderate** as the pattern's anchor, because the triad with κλῆρος ("lot") is concentrated there and in Esther C.

### Claim C67: The "you" → "us" shift at forgiveness (1:12–14; 2:13)

**Source:** overview Echo row 31; high on the pronouns, moderate–high as design; the 1:1–14 dig's Headline 4.\
**Claim:** at both places where Colossians speaks of forgiveness, the pronoun moves from second to first person plural, and the scribes level it at both.

**Findings.**

**The pronouns and the apparatus are confirmed** (NA28) `[T]`:
- **1:12.** ὑμᾶς ("you") in the text has א B 104 365 629 1175 1739 1881. ἡμᾶς ("us") has A C D F G K L P Ψ 𝔪 lat. **The NASB95 translates the levelled "us"**, so a reader of the study text alone does not see the shift.
- **2:13.** The second ὑμᾶς becomes ἡμᾶς in 𝔓⁴⁶ B and is omitted in 𝔪. ἡμῖν becomes ὑμῖν in א² K* L P f vg.
- **3:4.** ὑμῶν ("your") becomes ἡμῶν ("our") in B 𝔪. The scribes move toward the first person at a third salvation statement too.

**"Both places" is inaccurate.** Colossians has a third forgiveness statement, 3:13, καθὼς καὶ ὁ κύριος ἐχαρίσατο ὑμῖν ("as the Lord forgave you"). It keeps "you", because the comparison needs it (A) `[T]`. The shift comes at both *indicative* statements of forgiveness.

**The two auditors converged independently.** Both found that, apart from "God our Father" (1:2), every first-person plural in Colossians is apostolic (Paul and his co-workers: 1:3, 7–9, 28; 4:3, 8). **The only places where the letter's "we" includes the readers are 1:13–14 and 2:13–14**, its two indicative statements of forgiveness (A; B) `[T]`.

**But the move is Paul's habit.** Across the thirteen letters, Paul shifts from "you" to "us" at salvation statements no more often than elsewhere: 25.5 % against 22.6 %, and within one verse 4.4 % against 5.8 % (A's baseline; it cannot separate an inclusive from an apostolic "we", so it is approximate). The close parallels are:
- 1 Thess 1:9–10 (ἐπεστρέψατε → τὸν ῥυόμενον ἡμᾶς, "you turned → the one who rescues us", the same verb as Col 1:13);
- Gal 1:3–4;
- 1 Cor 1:30;
- Eph 1:13–14;
- above all **Eph 2:1–5**, the twin of Col 2:13 `[T]`.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | — | An internal feature |
| Volume | Strong | In the critical text at both places; the variants show the scribes noticed |
| Recurrence | Possible | Twice; not at 3:13 |
| Thematic Coherence | Strong | A confessional "us" at the saving act |
| Historical Plausibility | Strong | As Pauline habit |
| History of Interpretation | Possible | Osborne (p. 28) and Wilson (*ESV EC*) read the shift as deliberate and inclusive (Gentile readers drawn into the whole church); none mentions the 1:12 variant `[S: Logos 5]` |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Explains the variants; "design specific to Colossians" goes beyond the evidence |

**Verdict:** Needs reframing.\
**Ratings:**
- **high** on the pronouns and the apparatus;
- **moderate–high** for "in Colossians the inclusive 'we' occurs only at the two indicative statements of forgiveness" (two auditors, independently);
- **low–moderate** for "deliberate design specific to Colossians" (from moderate–high), because the move is Paul's habit.

**Book-overview action:** Echo row 31. **The 1:1–14 dig's Headline 4** keeps its fact but loses its design claim (see *Confidence Change Propagation*).

### Claim C65: The personal frame — 1:24–2:5 answered by 4:2–18

**Source:** overview Arc Map and target 1; macro-synthesis argument 9; moderate, synthetic.\
**Claim:** 4:2–18 shares more of 1:24–2:5's rarer vocabulary than any other seventeen-verse stretch. 4:12 gathers four of Paul's labour-words. Romans has the same frame; Philippians, 2 Corinthians and 2 Timothy do not.

**Findings** (Auditor B; the baseline reproduced exactly):
- **"More than any other stretch" holds at every threshold.** "Rarer" is tested at lemmas in at most 20, 50, 100 and 200 NT verses. At 50, eight lemmas are shared: Λαοδίκεια (Laodicea), γνωρίζω ("make known"), διάκονος ("minister"), μυστήριον ("mystery"), σοφία ("wisdom"), τέλειος ("mature"), φανερόω ("make manifest") and ἀγωνίζομαι ("strive") `[T]`.
- **The chance baseline:**
  - **80 of 4,692 window pairs (1.7 %)** share as many at the 50-verse threshold;
  - 5.5 % at 100;
  - 22.8 % at 20, so not unusual there;
  - without the place-name Laodicea: 5.1 % and 11.1 %.
- **The frame belongs to 1:25–2:7, and the shared words lie in 4:2–13.** The greetings of 4:14–18 add nothing.
- **Of the "four words" in 4:12:**
  - two are exact lemma repeats from 1:24–2:5: ἀγωνίζομαι ("strive") and τέλειος ("mature");
  - one is a cognate: πληροφορέω ("fully assure") answers πληροφορία ("full assurance", 2:2);
  - **θέλημα ("will") is not in 1:24–2:5 at all.** It comes from the opening prayer (1:9).
  - 4:12 echoes 1:9 as strongly as 1:28–2:2: ἵνα + a πληρ- verb + θέλημα + πᾶς ("that … fill / fully assure … will … all") `[T]`.
- **Among twelve Pauline letter-closings, Colossians' has the strongest link to an earlier stretch** (0.3 % at the 50-verse threshold).
- **The Romans comparison fails lexically.** Romans 1:8–17 against 15:14–33 stands out only at the 100-verse threshold (4 %), no better than 2 Timothy, which the proposal said lacks a frame. (The corpus lacks Rom 16:25–27; see *The Instrument*.) Philippians and 2 Corinthians lack it, as proposed `[T]`.
- **New strand (B):** μυστήριον ("mystery") + φανερόω ("make manifest") within two verses: only Col 1:26–27, Col 4:3–4 and 1 Tim 3:16 (SBLGNT) `[T]`.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Internal |
| Volume | Possible | 8–9 rarer lemmas; concentrated in 4:2–13; one a place-name; one of the "four words" misassigned |
| Recurrence | Possible | The mystery–manifest–wisdom cluster at 1:26–28; 2:2–3; 4:3–5 |
| Thematic Coherence | Strong | Struggle and prayer for maturity; the mystery made manifest; Laodicea |
| Historical Plausibility | Possible | Closings often pick up openings; Romans shows it only thematically |
| History of Interpretation | Strong | **Pao** (pp. 315–16) names the links: Epaphras's "striving" is Paul's verb (1:29); his prayer for maturity answers 1:28; "the will of God" answers 1:9 `[S: Logos 5]` |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Illuminates 4:3–4 and 4:12 |

**Verdict:** Confirmed with nuance.\
**Rating:** **moderate** (unchanged). Restate it:

> "4:2–13 picks up the vocabulary of Paul's labour in 1:25–2:7 — above all 'mystery' and 'make manifest' (1:26–27; 4:3–4), and 'strive' and 'mature' (1:28–29; 4:12) — and Epaphras's prayer also echoes the opening prayer (1:9). It is the strongest closing link of any Pauline letter on this measure (1.7 % of window pairs at the 50-verse threshold), but not at the strictest threshold."

Drop θέλημα from the list of 1:24–2:5 words. State the Romans parallel as thematic only.

### Claim C68: The speech chain (λόγος, "word")

**Source:** overview Echo row 59; macro-synthesis argument 9; high on the words, moderate as design.\
**Claim:** a chain on speech runs 3:8–9 → 3:16 → 4:3 → 4:6, carried by λόγος, with δεῖ ("must") at 4:4 and 4:6 only.

**Findings** (Auditor B; the counts reproduced):
- **λόγος ("word")** stands in seven Colossian verses: 1:5, 1:25, 2:23, 3:16, 3:17, 4:3, 4:6. **It is absent from 3:8–9** `[T]`. The first link is carried by other words: βλασφημία ("slander"), αἰσχρολογία ("obscene talk"), "from your mouth", and ψεύδομαι ("lie").
- **λόγος changes sense along the chain.** It is the gospel at 3:16 and 4:3, the believers' speech at 3:17 and 4:6, and "a reputation" at 2:23 `[T]`.
- **δεῖ ("must")**, counted by form, stands at 4:4 and 4:6 only. The lemma index files it under δέω ("bind"), with δέδεμαι ("I am bound") at 4:3 (see *The Instrument*) `[T]`.
- **The baseline.** 3:16–4:6 is denser in λόγος than about 96 % of 24-verse Pauline stretches. But Colossians as a whole runs at about twice the Pauline rate (7.4 % of verses against 3.9 %), so part of the excess belongs to the letter `[T]`.
- **A sharper link.** ἐν χάριτι ("in grace / with thankfulness") stands at **3:16 and 4:6**, and in only seven NT verses `[T]`.
  - **Caution from Logos round 5:** the follow-up leaned to "with thankfulness" at 3:16 (Pao), and 4:6 is plainly "gracious" speech.
  - If so, the shared phrase is a verbal link with **different senses**, a play on χάρις rather than a single meaning carried through `[I]`.
- **Ephesians has the same sequence** `[T]`: Eph 4:29 (λόγος σαπρός … ἵνα δῷ χάριν, "no rotten word … that it may give grace"); 5:4; 5:6; 5:19; 6:19–20 (ὡς δεῖ με λαλῆσαι, "as I must speak", verbatim with Col 4:4). This is Paul's paraenetic pattern, not a design peculiar to Colossians.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Internal |
| Volume | Possible | 4 λόγος verses in 24, ἐν χάριτι twice, δεῖ twice |
| Recurrence | Strong | Speech words at 3:8–9; 3:16–17; 4:3–4; 4:6 |
| Thematic Coherence | Strong | Foul speech put off → the word of Christ → the apostle's word → the church's word |
| Historical Plausibility | Strong | The same sequence in Ephesians |
| History of Interpretation | Weak | Not put to Logos as design |
| Satisfaction | Possible | A good reading, but λόγος shifts sense and misses the first link |

**Verdict:** Confirmed with nuance.\
**Rating:** **high** on the counts (δεῖ by form), **moderate** as design (unchanged). Restate it:

> "a speech thread (3:8–9 → 3:16–17 → 4:3–4 → 4:6) carried by a cluster of speech words, with ἐν χάριτι (3:16; 4:6) its most specific link; λόγος is absent from 3:8–9; Ephesians 4:29–6:20 has the same sequence."

### Claim C72: The dwelling chain (1:19; 2:9; 3:16)

**Source:** macro-synthesis (near the gate, not macro). Restated in Logos round 5 §14.5 as a temple thread, with 2 Cor 6:16 as the test case for ἐνοικέω ("dwell in").\
**Claim:** κατοικέω ("dwell", 1:19; 2:9) and ἐνοικέω ("dwell in", 3:16) carry a temple thread.

**Findings** (Auditor B; the key results reproduced):
- **1:19 and Ps 67:17 LXX.** Ps 67:17 (Ps 68:17 Hebrew) is **the only verse in Swete where εὐδοκέω ("be pleased") and κατοικέω ("dwell") have God as subject together**: τὸ ὄρος ὃ εὐδόκησεν ὁ θεὸς κατοικεῖν ἐν αὐτῷ ("the mountain in which God was pleased to dwell"). The Hebrew has חָמַד אֱלֹהִים לְשִׁבְתּוֹ ("God desired it for his dwelling") `[T]`. This confirms, by a direct search, the Ps 68:16 contact that audit 1 rated "confirmed with nuance" `[S: audit 1]`. Ps 67:19 LXX is quoted at Eph 4:8, so the psalm is available in Paul's circle `[T]`.
- **1:19 and 2:9 are one formula:** ἐν αὐτῷ … πᾶν τὸ πλήρωμα … κατοικ- ("in him … all the fullness … dwell"). 2:9 adds θεότης ("deity") and σωματικῶς ("bodily"), both NT hapaxes `[T]`.
- **κατοικέω in Swete has God as subject in about 16 of 569 verses (about 3 %)**, in temple, Zion and heaven settings. That is a minority use `[T]`. κατοικητήριον ("dwelling-place") is God's in 11 of 19 verses, most of them heaven (1 Kgs 8; 2 Chr 6) `[T]`. This agrees with Logos round 5 §2.
- **ἐνοικέω ("dwell in") never has God as subject in Swete** (33 verses; the subjects are human inhabitants). In the NT it is used of the Spirit (Rom 8:11; 2 Tim 1:14), God (2 Cor 6:16), faith (2 Tim 1:5) and the word of Christ (Col 3:16) `[T]`.
- **At 2 Cor 6:16 the verb is Paul's own**, not the LXX's. Lev 26:11–12 has ἐμπεριπατήσω ("I will walk among you"), and Ezek 37:27 has κατασκήνωσις ("dwelling") `[T]`. So 2 Cor 6:16 shows Paul choosing ἐνοικέω for divine indwelling inside a temple text. **That supports "indwelling" more than "temple"** `[I]`.
- **3:16 carries no temple vocabulary.** It has worship words (psalms, hymns, songs, thanksgiving) but no ναός ("temple"), οἰκοδομή ("building") or θυσία ("sacrifice") `[T]`.
- **The nearest true parallel to 3:16 is Eph 3:17**, "that Christ may dwell (κατοικέω) in your hearts" `[T]`.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Ps 67 LXX in Paul's circle (Eph 4:8) |
| Volume | Possible (1:19) / Weak (3:16) | εὐδοκέω + κατοικέω with God as subject: Ps 67:17 alone; ἐνοικέω never divine in Swete |
| Recurrence | Possible | 1:19 → 2:9 exact; 3:16 changes verb |
| Thematic Coherence | Possible | The fullness dwells in Christ; Christ's word dwells in the church. Colossians never calls the church a dwelling (contrast Eph 2:22) |
| Historical Plausibility | Possible | Pauline temple-indwelling texts use ναός ("temple"), which Colossians lacks |
| History of Interpretation | Possible | Pao reads 1:19's κατοικέω against God's heavenly and earthly dwelling, "Christ fulfills the role of the temple", with Ps 68 as a transfer from Sinai to Zion to Christ. At 3:16 he compares Rom 8:11, 2 Cor 6:16 and 1 Cor 3:16, but does not join 3:16 to 1:19. Beale's *Temple* is silent on 3:16 `[S: Logos 5]` |
| Satisfaction | Possible (1:19) / Weak (3:16) | The Zion echo illuminates 1:19; a temple reading adds little at 3:16 |

**Verdict:** Needs reframing.\
**Ratings:**
- **1:19 with Ps 67:17 LXX:** **moderate–high**, as an echo of Zion as God's chosen dwelling.
- **1:19 and 2:9 as a pair:** **high** as repetition; the temple overtone is **moderate**.
- **3:16 in a temple thread:** **low**.

Call it an **indwelling thread** in Paul's idiom (1:19; 2:9 → 3:16; cf. Eph 3:17; Rom 8:11), not a temple thread. **This corrects the main session's inference in Logos round 5 §14.5**, which read 2 Cor 6:16 as making ἐνοικέω a temple verb.\
**New candidate (B):** Wis 1:4, "[wisdom] will not dwell (κατοικήσει) in a body (σώματι) in debt to sin", near the vocabulary of 2:9 and 2:14 — *low–moderate*; needs research.
