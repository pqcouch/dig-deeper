Claim Audit 3: Colossians

Passages audited: Col 1:12; 1:17; 1:26–27; 2:2–3; 2:8, 20; 2:16–23; 3:17–4:1
Date: 28 October 2026
Version: 1.0
Purpose: The third claim audit on Colossians. It covers the whole queue of fourteen candidates: C29–C33 (new candidates from claim audit 2), C35–C42 (from the 3:18–4:1 dig) and C43 (from Logos round 3). C34 was record-only and is not audited. Trigger 1 had fired (fourteen queued against a threshold of about ten), and trigger 3 requires this audit before the Finalise pass on the overview.
Primary texts: - SBLGNT with its lemma index (counts and parsing); - NA28 and its apparatus (Logos export) for Colossians; - Swete’s LXX, searched by word-beginning and by stem anywhere in the word, with every hit read for sense; - Rahlfs (Logos exports) for Exodus, Leviticus, Deuteronomy, Joshua, Psalms, Proverbs, Isaiah, Chronicles, Hosea and both Greek texts of Daniel; - the WLC with its lemma index for the Hebrew.

There is no Rahlfs text of Wisdom, Sirach, Judith or the Maccabees in the corpus. C29, C31, part of C35 and the 4 Maccabees comparison rest on Swete alone.

Method: - Two blind auditors were run one after the other (Patrick’s two-auditor rule of 16 October 2026). - Auditor A took the eight single-contact claims, testing wording, rarity, rivals, Greek and Hebrew: C29, C30, C33, C35, C36, C37, C38, C43. - Auditor B took the six field and design claims, testing chance baselines, rank stability and leave-one-out: C31, C32, C39, C40, C41, C42. - Each auditor read a brief, a claims file and the corpus only. Neither saw the digs, the overview, the earlier audits, the Logos answers or the other auditor’s report. - The main session then reproduced the results each verdict rests on (see Spot Checks).

History of interpretation: the auditors’ row carries their recalled scholarship only, tagged [S]. The main session adds what Patrick’s Logos round 3 found, where it bears on a claim, tagged [S: Logos 3]. Where the round-4 question sheet already asks the question, the row is marked pending Logos round 4 with the question number. No verdict below rests on this row.

Warrant tags: [T] the text states or shows it · [I] a reasonable inference from the text · [S] a secondary source or recalled scholarship · [S: audit 2] claim audit 2 (27 October 2026) · [S: Logos 3] Patrick’s third Logos round (28 October 2026).

Positional Frame (Phase 0.6)

What the letter has set up. Each part of the letter does one thing [T]: - the confession (1:15–20) names the Son as creator, sustainer and reconciler; - the apostle’s labour (1:24–2:5) discloses the mystery, with every treasure of wisdom hidden in Christ (2:3); - the charge and its ground (2:6–15) set the rival “elements” against Christ; - the polemic (2:16–23) judges the rival regime by that ground; - the new life (3:1–17) closes with “whatever you do … in the name of the Lord Jesus” (3:17); - the household code (3:18–4:1) follows without a connective.

The fourteen claims bear on four points in that movement: - the elements and the creation language (C29); - the hidden treasury (C30, C33); - the polemic (C31, C32); - the household code (C35–C42), with C43 joining 1:12 to 2:18.

Why the frame matters for the verdicts. Colossians quotes nothing with a formula. Its one secure Old Testament wording is Isa 29:13 at 2:22 [S: audit 1]. Audits 1 and 2 set the test that follows, and this audit keeps it: an external source must add something that the letter’s own chains, or Paul’s ordinary idiom, do not already supply. That test decides most of the verdicts below. In the household code especially, the vocabulary of fear, service, reward and impartiality is already Paul’s own (2 Cor 5:9–11; Rom 2:6–11; Gal 4:1–7; Titus 2:9–10). A single Old Testament source has to beat that.

A method result that bears on every scan-based claim. Two null tests set the baseline for the scan, which ranks the chapters of Swete against a passage: - Auditor A: across 40 random four-verse NT passages, 19 had some Swete chapter at median rank 1 and 34 at median 2 or better. - Auditor B: across 60 random eight-verse Pauline passages, 38 (63 per cent) had a chapter at median 2 or better; across 20 comparable polemical and food passages, 14 (70 per cent).

A top scan rank is therefore not evidence by itself. What counts is whether that particular chapter is rare at the top of the null. Wisdom 14 is: it reaches median 2 or better in only 2 of B’s 80 null passages. But its rank against Col 2:16–23 rests on a single stem.

A positive control shows the reverse failure too. Isaiah 29, the certain source of 2:22, ranks only 22nd against the whole of 2:16–23, and rises to 2nd only when the window is cut to 2:20–23. A scan can bury a real allusion in a long window.

Claims Audited

Claim C29: Wisdom 7:17 behind Colossians 2:8, 20 (and 1:17)

Source: claim audit 2, new candidates (Auditor B); suggested low–moderate to moderate (Swete only).
Claim: Wis 7:17, εἰδέναι σύστασιν κόσμου καὶ ἐνέργειαν στοιχείων (“to know the structure of the world and the working of the elements”), is the only Swete verse joining στοιχεῖον (“element”) with κόσμος (“world”), the pair of 2:8 and 2:20; σύστασις (“structure”) is cognate with συνέστηκεν (“hold together”, 1:17).

Results (Auditor A; reproduced): - The count holds. στοιχει-, stem anywhere, occurs in four Swete verses: Wis 7:17, 19:18, 2 Macc 7:22 and 4 Macc 12:13. With κοσμ- in the same verse, the only one is Wis 7:17. - Within one verse either side, 2 Macc 7:22–23 also joins the two words. - Every Swete use of “elements” is physical: the stuff of the world or of a body. - The syntax differs. κόσμου governs σύστασιν, not στοιχείων. The phrase τὰ στοιχεῖα τοῦ κόσμου (“the elements of the world”) is not in Swete. - Against Col 2:8–10 and 2:20–23, Wisdom 7’s median rank is 8 and 9; 2 Maccabees 7 ranks higher in both. Its first place against Gal 4:3–8 is no better than random passages achieve.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability Possible Wisdom is Greek-only; Swete only in the corpus
Volume Weak Two words, in a different construction
Recurrence Weak Paul never cites Wisdom 7
Thematic coherence Weak Physical elements as objects of God-given knowledge, against “elements” as a regime one dies to (2:20)
Historical plausibility Possible The vocabulary is current in Hellenistic Jewish cosmology [S]
History of interpretation Possible Dunn cites Wis 7:17 for the physical sense of the phrase, as data for its meaning, not as a source [S: Logos 3]
Satisfaction Weak Adds nothing that Gal 4:3, 9 does not

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning: the rarity holds, but it shows only that the words were current in cosmological Greek. As an allusion C29 is low. As lexical background for the physical sense of “the elements of the world”, it is low–moderate, provided Wis 19:18, 2 Macc 7:22–23, 4 Macc 12:13 and 2 Pet 3:10, 12 stand beside it. The σύστασις–συνέστηκεν link (1:17) is a cognate, not a contact; Sir 43:26 and Wis 1:7 (A, recorded) are closer to 1:17’s thought.

Book-overview action required: at most a line in the στοιχεῖα note: the physical-sense evidence (Wis 7:17; 19:18; 2 Macc 7:22; 4 Macc 12:13). No Intertextual Map row.

Claim C30: Isaiah 29:14 behind Colossians 2:2–3, as an inversion

Source: claim audit 2, new candidates (Auditor A); suggested moderate (provisional).
Claim: God “will hide the understanding of the prudent” (Isa 29:14); Col 2:2–3 inverts it — all the treasures of wisdom are hidden in Christ and disclosed — by way of 1 Cor 1:19–2:7.

Results (Auditor A; reproduced): - The counts hold. σοφία (“wisdom”), σύνεσις (“understanding”) and a hiding word in one Swete verse occur only at Isa 29:14. - Job 28:20–21 must be added to the stated list of verses that have the triple within one verse either side. - The wording of Col 2:3, θησαυροὶ … ἀπόκρυφοι (“treasures … hidden”), is matched in one Swete verse only at Isa 45:3 and 1 Macc 1:23. Rahlfs Isa 45:3 agrees. - Isa 29:14 has neither “treasure” nor “mystery”. - Paul’s own quotation of the verse at 1 Cor 1:19 has ἀθετήσω (“I will set aside”) for κρύψω (“I will hide”) [S: audit 2]. - Availability is better than the claim says, because Col 2:22 uses Isa 29:13.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability Strong 1 Cor 1:19; Col 2:22 uses the previous verse
Volume Possible Three nouns, but not the treasure or mystery nouns; one shared form
Recurrence Possible 1 Cor 1:19 (with the hiding verb changed)
Thematic coherence Possible The inversion is a reading, not a datum
Historical plausibility Possible The Jesus tradition reads the verse as a hiding text (Matt 11:25; Luke 10:21); Paul’s dependence on it is unproven [S]
History of interpretation Weak–Possible [S] Round 3 found no commentator linking Isa 29:14 to 2:23 [S: Logos 3]; for 2:2–3 the question is pending Logos round 4 (Q4.5)
Satisfaction Possible A sharp sermon point, but Prov 2 and Isa 45:3 explain the wording better

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning: the best statement is that Col 2:2–3 draws on the wisdom-as-hidden-treasure tradition — Prov 2:1–6, Isa 45:3 and Theodotion’s Daniel 2 (audited as C10, C5 and C12) — with an Isaiah 29 overtone made more plausible by 2:22. As a named inversion of 29:14, C30 is low–moderate. Auditor A, blind to audits 1 and 2, independently reached the same three partners, and that agreement is itself a result.

Book-overview action required: none beyond audit 2’s treasury-idiom row. Optionally note Isa 29:14 as an overtone (low–moderate).

Claim C31: Wisdom 14 as the polemical partner of Colossians 2:16–23

Source: claim audit 2, new candidates (Auditor B); suggested moderate (Swete only). Audit 2 also named “Wisdom 14 and Isaiah 1” as the polemical partners in its C28 verdict.
Claim: Wisdom 14 shares pejorative θρησκεία (“worship”), κρύφια μυστήρια (“secret mysteries”), custom made law “by the commands of tyrants”, and the human origin of false worship; scan median 2, “rare in a null”.

Results (Auditor B; reproduced in part): - The six-run ranks reproduce exactly: 1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 2 (median 2), with 2 Maccabees 7 also at median 2. - “Rare in a null” is false for the rank as such. Some chapter reaches median 2 or better in 63–70 per cent of null passages (see Positional Frame). - Wisdom 14 as a chapter is rarely at the top (2 of 80 null passages: Rom 1:18–25 and 1 Tim 1:6–13), so its appearance here is specific. - The appearance rests on one stem. Leave θρησκ- out and Wisdom 14 drops to median 9. Remove one false friend — ποσίν (“feet”, 14:11) credited to πόσις (“drink”, Col 2:16) — and it drops to median 5. - The rank is not stable across windows: 2 for 2:8–23 and 2:16–23, 3 for 2:18–23, 7 for 2:20–23, 16 for 2:16–19. - μυστήριον (“mystery”) does not occur in 2:16–23, so that part of the claim reaches outside the passage. - θρησκ-, stem anywhere, occurs in six Swete verses: Wis 11:15; 14:16, 18, 27; 4 Macc 5:6, 13. - In 4 Maccabees 5 the word names Jewish observance in a food-law setting, as θρησκεία does in Acts 26:5. That is closer to Colossians’ subject than Wisdom’s anti-idol polemic.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability Possible Swete only
Volume Weak One distinctive stem; the rest generic or false friends
Recurrence Possible Rom 1:18–32 is often read against Wisdom 13–14 [S]; nothing else in Colossians draws on Wisdom 14
Thematic coherence Weak–Possible Idolatry, against a Jewish-ascetic, angelic regime; only “human origin” overlaps, and Isa 29:13 already supplies it
Historical plausibility Possible —
History of interpretation Weak [S] Not recalled; not put to Logos
Satisfaction Weak —

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning: C31 is low. What survives is a word-level observation, not a partner chapter: θρησκεία is rare in the Greek Old Testament, and in Wisdom 14 and 4 Maccabees 5 it is applied to worship judged false (Wisdom) or to Jewish observance under pressure (4 Maccabees). This corrects audit 2’s C28 wording, which named Wisdom 14 as one of “the polemical partners”.

Book-overview action required: do not add Wisdom 14 to the Intertextual Map or the Presenting Situation. Audit 2’s recommendation 3 (“with Wisdom 14 and Isaiah 1 as the polemical partners”) should be dropped.

Claim C32: Isaiah 1:13–14 at Colossians 2:16 and 2:23

Source: claim audit 2, new candidates (Auditor B); suggested moderate — “the πλησμονή contact is the test”.
Claim: Isa 1:13–14 joins new moons, sabbaths and feasts with πλησμονή (“surfeit”), the word of Col 2:23.

Results (Auditor B; reproduced): - ἑορτή (“feast”), νουμηνία (“new moon”) and σάββατα (“sabbaths”) in one Swete verse: eight verses, not seven, adding 1 Chr 23:31. Jdt 8:6 is a near-miss: it has the “eve of the sabbath” and “eve of the new moon” compounds and is excluded. - Isa 1:13–14 has the triad only across two verses. - Hos 2:11 and Ezek 45:17 match Col 2:16’s order exactly (Swete and WLC). - πλησμονή occurs in 27 Swete verses, plus the Psalms of Solomon. Isa 1:14 is the only one where it meets a calendar word; Rahlfs agrees. - But the senses differ. In Isaiah the “surfeit” is God’s disgust. In Col 2:23 it is the flesh’s gratification, the ordinary food sense of Exod 16:3, 8 and Ps 77:25. - The Hebrew of 1:14 has לָטֹרַח (“a burden”). Isa 1:13 in Swete and Rahlfs has νηστείαν (“fasting”) where the WLC has אָוֶן (“iniquity”). Both are Greek-only pluses.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability Strong Isaiah is used at 2:22
Volume Weak–Possible A formulaic triad (eight verses) and one shared word in another sense
Recurrence Possible Isaiah elsewhere in Colossians
Thematic coherence Possible Prophetic critique of the cult, against a “shadow” argument — not the same logic
Historical plausibility Possible —
History of interpretation Weak–Possible [S] Commentators usually cite Hos 2:11, Ezek 45:17 and 1 Chr 23:31 for the triad [S]; pending Logos round 4 (Q4.4)
Satisfaction Weak–Possible —

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning: C32 is low–moderate. The test the claim set itself — the πλησμονή contact — is unique in wording but fails on sense. Isa 1:13–14 can stand as one of several calendar texts, with its Greek pluses (πλησμονή; νηστεία, “fasting” — compare the fasting reading of ταπεινοφροσύνη) as a possible faint layer. It is not “the” source, and it is not a polemical partner (see C31).

Book-overview action required: Row 11 (the calendar) keeps Hos 2:11 and Ezek 45:17 as the exact-order texts. Add 1 Chr 23:31 to the triad’s verse list. Isa 1:13–14 goes in the note at low–moderate.

Claim C35: 2 Chronicles 19:6–7 (with Deuteronomy 10:17) behind Colossians 3:22–25; 4:1

Source: the 3:18–4:1 dig, Tool 11; low–moderate.
Claim: Jehoshaphat’s charge to judges — “not for man but for the LORD”; “the fear of the LORD”; “no unrighteousness nor partiality” — with Deut 10:17’s impartial “Lord of lords”, lies behind the code’s slave and master lines.

Results (Auditor A; reproduced): - A fear word, κύριος, an ἀδικ- (“wrong”) word and a partiality idiom in one Swete verse: 2 Chr 19:7 only, once a false hit at Theodotion Dan 1:10 is excluded. - “Not for man … but for the Lord”: 1 Chr 29:1 and 2 Chr 19:6 only (Swete and Rahlfs). - ἁπλότης (“sincerity”) with καρδία (“heart”) in one Swete verse: 1 Chr 29:17 and Wis 1:1. The dig named only the first. - The stated window list does not fully reproduce. The dig listed six windows (2 Chr 19:5–8, Isa 29:21–23, Job 11:13–14, Prov 29:25–27, Jer 40:7, Jdt 11:3). Auditor A could not reproduce all six as stated. The 2 Chr 19 window itself holds. - The NT’s partiality word family (προσωπολημψία and cognates) occurs in seven NT verses and nowhere in Swete: Acts 10:34; Rom 2:11; Eph 6:9; Col 3:25; Jas 2:1, 9; 1 Pet 1:17 (SBLGNT). That points to a shared Christian formulation. - Rivals of equal or better fit: - Lev 19:13–15: do not wrong, the hired man’s wages, fear the Lord, no partiality; - Sir 32:13–24 (Swete numbering): the Lord who repays, “no respect of persons with him”, repayment “according to his deeds”.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability Strong Chronicles; Deuteronomy
Volume Possible Unique collocations, but Colossians’ antithesis is reversed in form (“as to the Lord and not to men”) and its partiality noun is a Christian word
Recurrence Weak Paul never quotes Chronicles; Rom 2:11 states impartiality without its wording
Thematic coherence Possible Judges answerable to the Lord, against slaves and masters answerable to the Lord
Historical plausibility Possible Impartiality is a Deuteronomic and wisdom commonplace
History of interpretation Weak–Possible [S] Commentators cite Deut 10:17 and the topos generally [S]; pending Logos round 4 (Q4.3)
Satisfaction Possible —

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning: 2 Chr 19:6–7 is the densest single convergence, but it sits in a broad tradition of the impartial God as judge. That tradition includes Deut 10:17; Lev 19:13–15; Sir 32:13–24; and in the NT, Rom 2:11 and 1 Pet 1:17. The dig’s own caveat (Eph 6:5–9 has the same elements) is right.

Rating: low–moderate as a member of the impartial-judge topos; low as a specific source.

Book-overview action required: none for the Intertextual Map. The 3:18–4:1 dig’s Tool 11 should name the topos with its members. Lev 19:13–15 and Sir 32 are folded in here, not queued.

Claim C36: Leviticus 25:39–55 behind Colossians 3:22; 4:1

Claim: the fear of God as the master’s restraint (“you shall not rule over him with harshness but shall fear your God”, Lev 25:43), and the slaves as the Lord’s own (“they are My servants”, 25:42, 55), lie behind 3:22 and 4:1.

Thematic coherence Possible Slaves belonging to the Lord; but the fear is moved, and the scope is ethnic

Reasoning: C36 is low: one of several Torah and wisdom texts on masters answerable to God. Auditor B, without being asked, independently listed Lev 25:43 as “low–moderate background for the masters’ clause”; the two auditors differ by one step. Job 31:13–15 is queued as C44.

Claim C37: Isaiah 54:17 (Hebrew) behind Colossians 3:24

Source: the 3:18–4:1 dig; low–moderate.
Claim: זֹאת נַחֲלַת עַבְדֵי יְהוָה (“this is the heritage of the servants of YHWH”) is the only Hebrew verse where an inheritance belongs to YHWH’s servants, plural. The Greek has θεραπεύουσιν (“those who serve”), so Col 3:24’s δουλεύετε (“you serve as slaves”) is closer to the Hebrew than to the Greek.

Results (Auditor A; reproduced): - נַחֲלָה (“inheritance”) with עֶבֶד (“servant”) in one verse: twelve WLC verses. The count holds. - Isa 54:17 is the unique construct “the inheritance of the servants of YHWH”, and the first plural “servants” in Isaiah 40–66. - Swete and Rahlfs read θεραπεύουσιν at 54:17. - But the Greek Isaiah supplies the servants-as-heirs idea itself at 65:9: κληρονομήσουσιν οἱ ἐκλεκτοί μου καὶ οἱ δοῦλοί μου (“my chosen and my servants shall inherit”, Swete). The appeal to the Hebrew is therefore unnecessary. - ἀνταπόδοσις (“reward”) with κληρονομία (“inheritance”) in one Swete verse: none. Colossians’ “reward of the inheritance” has no Greek Old Testament precedent.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability Strong Isaiah 54 is used at Gal 4:27
Volume Weak No shared Greek wording at 54:17
Recurrence Possible Isaiah 54 at Gal 4:27, a different verse
Thematic coherence Possible Servants who inherit, in a slave paraenesis
Historical plausibility Possible —
History of interpretation Weak [S] Not recalled [S]; Q4.2 asks about the inheritance at 3:24 in general
Satisfaction Weak–Possible —

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance (the counts); Needs reframing (the source).

Reasoning: the claim is better restated as the Isaianic theme of the servants as heirs (Hebrew 54:17; Greek 65:9) as possible background to the paradox of slaves who inherit, which is low (from low–moderate). The Galatians use (Isa 54:1 at Gal 4:27, beside “the son of the slave woman shall not inherit”, Gal 4:30) shows that Paul knew the chapter. It does not carry 54:17 itself.

Book-overview action required: none.

Source: the 3:18–4:1 dig; low.
Claim: the ἐρεθ- family (“provoke”) with ὑπακούω (“obey”) occurs in Swete only at Deut 21:18 and 21:20. The rebellious son provokes and disobeys; in Colossians the child obeys and the father must not provoke.

Without that, the “reversal” disappears. - The NA28 apparatus at Col 3:21 records παροργίζετε (“provoke to anger”) as an assimilation to Eph 6:4, in א A C D* F G L and others. A deliberate echo would not be so readily replaced. - Deuteronomy 21’s scan rank is chance level.

Recurrence Weak —
Thematic coherence Weak A capital case, against paternal gentleness
Historical plausibility Weak–Possible —
History of interpretation Weak [S] Not recalled

Claim C39: The ἀρεσκ- family 1:10 → 3:20 → 3:22 as designed

Source: the 3:18–4:1 dig, Move 4; moderate.
Claim: the letter’s three ἀρεσκ- words — ἀρεσκεία (“pleasing”, 1:10, an NT hapax), εὐάρεστος (“well-pleasing”, 3:20) and ἀνθρωπάρεσκος (“men-pleaser”, 3:22) — make the code answer the opening prayer by design.

Results (Auditor B; reproduced in part): - The counts are exact (SBLGNT). - The baseline is against design. A prayer word recurring in the ethical section is normal in Paul: 9 of 10 opening prayers tested. - Every link has a stock parallel elsewhere in Paul: - “walk” and “please God” (1 Thess 4:1); - pleasing people against being Christ’s slave (Gal 1:10); - “well-pleasing” with “in everything” in a slave code (Titus 2:9); - “we make it our aim to be well-pleasing to him” before the judgement seat (2 Cor 5:9). - “In everything” is weakened by the density of πᾶς (“all”) and by its omission at 3:22 in 𝔓⁴⁶.

Test Result
Counts verified Yes, exactly
Baseline Prayer-to-ethics recurrence is the norm (9 of 10)
Sense Real: pleasing the Lord (1:10), pleasing in the Lord (3:20), its negative (3:22)
Alternative explanation Stock Pauline idiom (1 Thess 4:1; Gal 1:10; Titus 2:9; 2 Cor 5:9)

Verdict: Needs reframing.

Reasoning: C39 is low–moderate as design, and moderate–high as a thematic resonance within Paul’s idiom of pleasing. A hearer can hear the code answering the prayer, and the preacher may say so. The text does not show it to be a structural device.

Book-overview action required: the proposed Echo Table row 1:10 → 3:20, 22 stays, as a resonance (moderate on words, low–moderate as design).

Claim C40: Colossians 1:12 κλῆρος → 3:24 κληρονομία — the slaves among the heirs

Source: the 3:18–4:1 dig, Move 4; moderate–high.
Claim: the opening thanksgiving made all the readers heirs (“the share of the lot of the saints”, 1:12); the code tells the slaves that they are among them (“the reward of the inheritance”, 3:24).

Results (Auditor B; reproduced): - The counts are exact. κλῆρος (“lot”, 1:12) and κληρονομία (“inheritance”, 3:24) are Colossians’ only two κληρ- words. ἀνταπόδοσις (“reward”) is an NT hapax. - Ephesians has five κληρ- words (1:11, 14, 18; 3:6; 5:5), none in its code; Eph 6:8 has only “receive back from the Lord”. - Specificity: with only two such words in the letter, any recurrence would link 1:12 to 3:24. The link is exactly as specific as “1:12 → any inheritance language”. - What makes it pointed is the addressee: δοῦλοι (“slaves”). Paul’s slave–heir paradox is explicit at Gal 4:1, 7 (“no longer a slave but a son; and if a son, also an heir”).

Test Result
Counts verified Yes, exactly
Baseline Opening-to-ethics inheritance language also in Ephesians (1:14, 18 → 5:5); normal Pauline vocabulary
Sense Strong: the share given to all (1:12) is promised to slaves (3:24)
