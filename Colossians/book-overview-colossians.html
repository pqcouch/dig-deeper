Canonical Position

The NT equivalent of the Tanak block: position in the collection, what it presupposes, what it hands on.

Field
Section Pauline corpus, fourth of the four “prison” letters in the canonical order (Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, then Philemon at the end of the corpus). NA28 and the English Bibles agree on the order: Philippians before, 1 Thessalonians after. The order runs broadly by length, not by date [S].
Reading implication A canonical reader arrives at Colossians having just read Ephesians, and will hear much of it as familiar. That is the danger: the familiar phrases (“head”, “body”, “fullness”, the household code) are here put to a sharper, polemical use against a named rival “philosophy” (2:8). Read Colossians on its own terms first, and only then alongside Ephesians. [I]
Presupposes (1) A gospel already received through Epaphras (1:5–7; 2:6–7) [T]. (2) Psalm 110:1, the enthroned Lord at God’s right hand (3:1) [T]/[I] — see the Intertextual Map. (3) Genesis 1:26–27, humanity made κατ᾽ εἰκόνα (“according to the image”), replanted at 3:10 [T]. (4) Circumcision as the covenant sign of Genesis 17 (2:11, 13) [I]. (5) The Torah’s and the Prophets’ calendar of “festival, new moon, Sabbath” (2:16) [T]. (6) Earlier instructions about Mark, “Barnabas’s cousin” (4:10) [T]. (7) A letter “from Laodicea” which the church is to read (4:16) [T] — not identifiable from the text. Four identifications are held: a lost letter (Hiebert; Heiser), Ephesians as a circular, Philemon (John Knox’s proposal, as recalled), and the later Latin Epistle to the Laodiceans, which is a separate, apocryphal work [S: Logos 3]. Each reference checked against the NA28.
Handoff Thematic, not lexical. The letter hands on (a) the “Christ is all, and in all” confession (3:11) and (b) a household in which a runaway’s master and a fellow-worker named Archippus will reappear in Philemon. Eight names are shared with Philemon — Timothy, Epaphras, Aristarchus, Mark, Luke, Demas, Archippus, Onesimus — verified against the SBLGNT [T]. Of the six greeters of 4:10–14, five recur at Phlm 23–24 (all but Jesus Justus), and Aristarchus (Col 4:10) and Epaphras (Phlm 23) exchange the title “fellow prisoner” between the letters [T] (4:7–18 dig [S: dig], reproduced). Three lexical links strengthen it: ἀνήκω (“be fitting”, Col 3:18; Phlm 8), ἀδικέω (“do wrong”, Col 3:25; Phlm 18), and slave + flesh + Lord in one verse only at Eph 6:5, Col 3:22 and Phlm 16 (SBLGNT) [T] (3:18–4:1 dig [S: dig]). That the two letters went to the same town is [I] (Onesimus is “one of you”, 4:9).
Neighbours Philippians before and 1 Thessalonians after: Timothy is co-sender in all three (Phil 1:1; Col 1:1; 1 Thess 1:1) [T], but the adjacency does no interpretive work. The real neighbour is Ephesians, two letters back, and the digs have made the parallel letter-wide (see below).

The Ephesians parallel, measured. The two letters share a near-verbatim paragraph: Col 4:8 = Eph 6:22, seventeen words in the same order (SBLGNT; NA28 agrees) [T]. They also share three words found nowhere else in the New Testament: - ἀποκαταλλάσσω (“to reconcile”, Eph 2:16; Col 1:20, 22); - ὀφθαλμοδουλία (“external service”, Eph 6:6; Col 3:22); - ἀνθρωπάρεσκος (“men-pleaser”, Eph 6:6; Col 3:22) [T].

The digs found close clusters in every movement: - Eph 1:4; 2:12–16 at 1:21–23; - Eph 3:1–19 at 1:24–2:5 — 24 shared content words against 9–18 for eleven other Pauline passages of the same length; claim audit 1 rates it high [S: audit 1]; - Eph 1:20–2:6 at 2:6–15 (moderate–high) [S: audit 1]; - Eph 2:2–3; 4:22–25; 5:3–6 at 3:5–11; - Eph 4:1–3; 4:32; 5:18–20 at 3:12–17, including two pairings exclusive to the two letters; - Eph 5:15–16; 6:18–20 at 4:2–6, including the clause “as I ought to speak” (ὡς δεῖ με λαλῆσαι, NT only Eph 6:20; Col 4:4); - Eph 6:21–22 at 4:7–9 [T] (the digs [S: dig]).

Several Colossian variants are scribal harmonisations to Ephesians: 1:14; 3:21; 4:3 (Textual section).

Which letter used which remains an open question. No finding in this overview depends on it. The literature is divided [S: Logos 1]: - Ephesians depends on Colossians (Lincoln; Witherington); - the evidence is insufficient for dependence (Arnold, counting verbatim runs; Best); - the older view, one author writing twice (as Furnish reports it).

Presenting Situation

Author: Paul names himself three times (1:1; 1:23; 4:18), with Timothy as co-sender (1:1) [T]. The closing greeting is “in my own hand” (4:18), the same clause as 1 Cor 16:21 and 2 Thess 3:17 (SBLGNT) [T], which implies a scribe wrote the rest [I]. Whether Paul wrote the letter, or a later disciple in his name, is disputed in modern scholarship [S]. Positions recorded: - Pauline, written from Rome: a “slight majority” of those holding Pauline authorship (deSilva; Bartel; Gurtner); - Pauline, from Ephesus: Comfort, and the Marcionite prologue; - Pauline, from Caesarea: reported by deSilva; - Deutero-Pauline: MacDonald — at the end of Paul’s life or soon after, possibly Timothy; - Dunn: Timothy writing during Paul’s lifetime, with his approval [S: Logos 2].

The first four come through introductions and are not from the owned commentaries [S: Logos 3]. The letter’s own claim is plain, and it is the canonical reader’s starting point [T].

Circumstance of the writer: a prisoner — “for which I have also been imprisoned” (δέδεμαι, “I have been bound”, 4:3), “my fellow prisoner” (4:10), “remember my imprisonment” (δεσμῶν, “chains”, 4:18) [T]. The place (Rome, Ephesus or Caesarea) is not stated [S].

Audience: “the saints and faithful brethren in Christ who are at Colossae” (1:2) [T], a church in a town near Laodicea and Hierapolis (2:1; 4:13, 15–16) [T]. Paul has not visited them: he labours for those “who have not personally seen my face” (2:1), which most naturally includes the Colossians [I]. They learned the gospel from Epaphras, “one of your number” (1:7; 4:12) [T]. They are largely Gentile [I]: they were once “alienated” (1:21), the mystery is revealed “among the Gentiles … Christ in you” (1:27), and they were “dead in … the uncircumcision of your flesh” (2:13) [T]. Israel’s titles are given to them: “the saints” — every plural ἅγιοι in the letter means believers (1:2, 4, 12, 22, 26; 3:12), so 1:12’s “lot of the holy ones” is theirs (moderate–high [S: audit 4]) — and “the chosen of God, holy and beloved” (3:12) [T].

Occasion: Epaphras has reported their faith and love (1:4, 8) [T], and Paul sends Tychicus and Onesimus with news and encouragement (4:7–9) [T]. Paul’s fuller report is good: “rejoicing to see your good discipline and the stability of your faith” (2:5) [T] — χαίρω (“rejoice”) brackets 1:24–2:5, its only two uses in the letter (1:24; 2:5) [T]. The letter does not describe a church already captured. It warns: “so that no one will delude you” (2:4), “see to it that no one takes you captive” (2:8), “let no one act as your judge” (2:16), “let no one keep defrauding you” (2:18) [T]. Whether the threat is a present faction or a danger in the air is not settled by the text: Paul names no opponent, and the warnings use an indefinite singular (τις, “anyone”, 2:8, 16; μηδείς, “no one”, 2:4, 18) [T]/[I].

The rival teaching, as the text describes it (2:8–23) [T]: - a φιλοσοφία (“philosophy”, 2:8; the only use in the NT, SBLGNT) “according to the tradition of men” and “the στοιχεῖα (“elementary principles”) of the world" (2:8, 20); - rulings on food and drink and on “a festival or a new moon or a Sabbath day” (2:16); - ταπεινοφροσύνη (“self-abasement”) and “the worship of the angels” (2:18); - “visions he has seen” (2:18); - “Do not handle, do not taste, do not touch!” (2:21); - severe treatment of the body (2:23).

The circumcision paragraph (2:11–13) suggests that circumcision was also in view [I]. What this adds up to is a scholarly reconstruction [S], and so is the label “the Colossian heresy” [S]. The overview works from the features, not the label. The holders of each reconstruction are now recorded:

Two text-based notes bear on the choice without settling it: - A Jewish component is supported, without deciding the reconstruction. In the Greek Old Testament, 4 Maccabees calls Jewish food observance, approvingly, “our philosophy” and “religion” (θρησκεία) (4 Macc 5:6, 22–26, Swete) — the two words Colossians uses pejoratively. Moderate [S: audit 2] [S: audit 3]. The wider “Maccabean field” is the default field of almost any New Testament food passage, so it is low–moderate and not part of the situation [S: audit 2]. Wisdom 14 and Isaiah 1 are not “the polemical partners” of 2:16–23 [S: audit 3]. - ταπεινοφροσύνη as “fasting” at 2:18, 23 is moderate. The verbal idiom “humble the soul” for fasting stands at Lev 16:29, 31; 23:27–32; Ps 34:13 LXX; Isa 58:3, 5 (Swete) [S: Logos 3].

Pressure: Is Christ enough, or does the life of faith need something added — a higher knowledge, a stricter regimen, the favour of angelic powers? The letter’s answer is set out before the warning comes: all the πλήρωμα (“fullness”) dwells in Christ (1:19; 2:9), and “in Him you have been made complete” (2:10, πεπληρωμένοι, “filled”) [T]/[I].

Purpose: to keep a young Gentile church walking “in Him” as they began (2:6), by setting before them the Christ in whom creation, reconciliation and the fullness of God are found — so that no rival “philosophy” can persuade them they lack anything — and to show what that sufficiency looks like in speech, household and work [I].

The letter’s idiom, as the audits found it. Of the external links proposed across four audits, what survives is mostly Paul’s own vocabulary, or idiom he shares with the early church. That includes, notably, conversion and mission idiom he shares with Luke: - Acts 26:18 at 1:12–14 — six of the rarer lemmas in one verse, against three for any other NT verse; - Luke 1:68–79 at 1:13–14; - Luke 8:15 at 1:6–11 (SBLGNT) [S: audit 4].

Luke is with Paul at the close (4:14) [T]. This bears on the letter’s setting and proves nothing about authorship [I], moderate.

Device and shape — [T] unless tagged.

The hinge. The letter is linear, hinged at 2:6–7, where the opening confession becomes a command. The verb περιπατέω (“to walk”) marks the hinge: it stands in the opening prayer (1:10), at the hinge itself (2:6) and twice in the new life (3:7; 4:5), and nowhere else in the letter (verified by lemma, SBLGNT).

“In Him”. The phrase ἐν αὐτῷ (“in Him”) gathers the first half. All eight occurrences stand within 1:16–2:15 (1:16, 17, 19; 2:6, 7, 9, 10, 15 — SBLGNT), five of them in 2:6–15. ἐν ᾧ (“in whom”) adds 1:14, 2:3 and 2:11; 2:12 may mean “in which”, of baptism.

The two conditionals. Two matching conditionals turn the warning into the new life: - Εἰ ἀπεθάνετε σὺν Χριστῷ (“if you have died with Christ”, 2:20); - Εἰ οὖν συνηγέρθητε τῷ Χριστῷ (“therefore if you have been raised up with Christ”, 3:1).

The verb συνεγείρω (“to raise up with”) appears only at 2:12 and 3:1 in the letter, and ἀποθνῄσκω (“to die”) only at 2:20 and 3:3 (SBLGNT).

The confession of 1:15–20 is the letter’s arsenal. Its vocabulary is redeployed in the polemic of chapter 2. Each pairing below has been checked by lemma against the SBLGNT: - πλήρωμα (“fullness”) with κατοικέω (“to dwell”) — 1:19 → 2:9, the only two occurrences of each word in the letter; - κεφαλή (“head”) — 1:18 → 2:10, 2:19; - ἀρχαί (“rulers”) and ἐξουσίαι (“authorities”) — created in 1:16, subjected in 2:10, disarmed in 2:15; - σταυρός (“cross”) — 1:20 → 2:14, its only two occurrences.

The ground-clause ὅτι ἐν αὐτῷ (“for in Him”) stands at 1:16, 1:19 and 2:9 and nowhere else in Paul (SBLGNT); 2:9 repeats the confession’s own reasoning [T].

v1.0 — baselined. σῶμα (“body”) is dropped from the list, because it occurs in eight verses of the letter and is too common to carry the argument. Of the confession’s 23 rarer content words, the stretch 2:8–14 shares the most. Across the letter’s ninety six-verse passages, seven have some partner stretch that matches the concentration. The claim is therefore high on the specific links and moderate–high as a design. Eph 1:21–23 shows a similar cluster [S: audit 1].

The personal frame (proposal). 1:24–2:5 and 4:2–18 frame the letter’s personal material: Paul’s labour for the readers, then his colleagues’. 4:2–18 shares more of 1:24–2:5’s rarer vocabulary than any other seventeen-verse stretch, and 4:12 gathers all four of Paul’s labour-words — ἀγωνίζομαι (“strive”), τέλειος (“complete”), πληροφορέω (“fully assure”) and θέλημα (“will”) — into Epaphras’s prayer (1:28–2:2; 1:9) [T] (verified by lemma). Moderate; synthetic [S: audit 1].

3:12–17 as the summary of church life (proposal). In the 3:12–17 dig, Move 4 draws on more of the letter than in any other passage: the opening prayer, the confession, Paul’s ministry, the polemic and the putting-off. It is the last word to the whole church before the household [S: dig]. Moderate; synthetic.

The “hymn”. NA28 sets 1:15–20 as indented poetry, as it prints it [T]. The paired openings show two parallel strophes, creation and new creation [T]: - ὅς ἐστιν … πρωτότοκος (“He is … firstborn”, 1:15 and 1:18b); - ὅτι ἐν αὐτῷ (“for in Him”, 1:16 and 1:19).

Calling it a pre-Pauline hymn that Paul quotes and edits is a form-critical reconstruction [S]. The owned commentators divide [S: Logos 4]: - Paul’s own composition: Wright, with a four-member frame — ὅς ἐστιν · καὶ αὐτός ἐστιν · καὶ αὐτός ἐστιν · ὅς ἐστιν (1:15, 17, 18a, 18b), which reproduces (SBLGNT) [T]; Pao, in effect; - open: Witherington; - preformed material possible but unprovable: Dunn.

The “additions” (“the church”, 1:18; “through the blood of his cross”, 1:20) are reported as the consensus of reconstructions, not held by these four. On Wright’s frame, “the church” stands inside the third member [T]/[I]. Whether the passage was sung, quoted or composed for the letter cannot be known from the text.

Microscript

All the fullness of God dwells in Christ — creator, head and reconciler of all things — and you have been filled in Him; so walk in Him as you began: let no one take you captive with something “more”, but, having died and been raised with Christ, seek the things above and put on the new humanity in every word, relationship and task, with thanksgiving.

The keynote is fullness. The πληρ- family runs through every movement, nine occurrences in nine verses (SBLGNT, verified by lemma) [T]: - πληρόω (“to fill”) at 1:9; 1:25; 2:10; 4:17; - πλήρωμα (“fullness”) at 1:19; 2:9; - ἀνταναπληρόω (“to fill up”) at 1:24; - πληροφορία (“full assurance”) at 2:2; - πληροφορέω (“to be fully assured”) at 4:12.

The family’s spread across the letter, from the opening prayer (1:9) to the closing word to Archippus (4:17), is what makes it the spine rather than a stylistic tic. The keynote phrase is ἐν αὐτῷ (“in Him”). The closing confession is that Christ is τὰ πάντα (“all”, 3:11), the phrase the confession used for “all things” created and reconciled (1:16, 17, 20); NA28 brackets the article at 3:11 [T].

Test against the movements. - 1:3–23: the prayer that they be “filled” (1:9) and the confession of the fullness (1:19). - 1:24–2:5: Paul “fills up” afflictions and “fully carries out” the word (1:24–25), so that they reach “full assurance” (2:2). - 2:6–23: “in Him the fullness … in Him you have been filled” (2:9–10). - 3:1–4:6: the new self “renewed … according to the image” (3:10); “Christ is all” (3:11); the word of Christ dwelling “richly” (3:16). - 4:7–18: Epaphras prays that they stand “fully assured” (4:12), and Archippus is to “fulfil” his ministry (4:17).

Every movement serves the microscript [I]. v1.0: confirmed by the sweep. The 1:1–14 dig’s Move 4 found fifteen forward echoes and no external source, the clearest instance of the letter’s arguing by internal repetition [S: dig]. No dig proposed a change to the microscript.

1. Psalm 110:1 (LXX 109:1, “Sit at My right hand”) → 3:1 “where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God”. Grounds the “seek the things above” ethic in the enthroned Christ, and places the readers’ hidden life where he is.

Rarity and rival. δεξιός (“right”) with a verb of sitting stands in six verses in Swete’s LXX; only Ps 109:1 has the enthroned figure. Colossians uses the NT formula ἐν δεξιᾷ (“at the right hand”), not the psalm’s ἐκ δεξιῶν: δεξιός with κάθημαι (“to sit”) or καθίζω (“to seat”) occurs in 22 NT verses (SBLGNT), including Eph 1:20 and Heb 1:3.

v1.0: the NT scan misses the psalm, because the forms differ (κάθου). Its top-ranked “partner” for 3:1–4 is the Christian Gloria (Odes 14) — a control, not a source [S: dig] (3:1–4 dig). Colossians, unlike Eph 2:6, does not seat the believers with Christ; their share is a hidden life (3:3) [T].

Confidence: high (through the NT formula; not a quotation).

2. Genesis 1:26–27 (with 5:1) → 3:10 κατ᾽ εἰκόνα τοῦ κτίσαντος (“according to the image of the One who created”); background to 1:15 εἰκών (“image”). The new self is creation restored. Read with 1:15, the “image” humanity was made after is Christ.

Rarity and rival. κατ᾽ εἰκόνα with a creator verb and ἄνθρωπος (“man”) stands in the LXX only at Gen 1:26–27, 5:1 and Sir 17:3 [S: audit 1]. Sir 17:3 has “image” and “clothed” (ἐνέδυσεν) in one verse, as Col 3:10 does — a minor rival, moderate–low (3:5–11 dig [S: dig]). Rival in wording at 1:15: Wis 2:23, which shares κτίζω (“to create”) with εἰκών, where Genesis has ποιέω (“to make”).

v1.0: at 1:15 the row stands on new grounds. ἀόρατος (“invisible”) is withdrawn as evidence, because at Gen 1:2 it describes the earth and no LXX verse calls God invisible [S: audit 1]. Genesis 1 is one of three converging backgrounds at 1:15, with the wisdom field (row 6) and Ps 88 LXX (row 8). The Western addition “male and female” at 3:11 (from Gal 3:28) shows early readers hearing Genesis behind 3:10–11 [I]. The scan cannot see this row (median rank 204 at 3:10), because the ethnic list dominates the window — a known blind spot, not an absence [S: audit 1].

Confidence: high (3:10, audited); moderate (1:15).

3. Isaiah 29:13 (cited by Jesus, Mark 7:7; Matt 15:9) → 2:22 “the commandments and teachings of men”. Places the ascetic rules under the prophet’s (and Jesus’) verdict on humanly devised religion.

Rarity and rival. ἔνταλμα (“commandment”) with διδασκαλία (“teaching”) stands in one verse in the LXX — Isa 29:13. ἔνταλμα occurs in only three NT verses: Matt 15:9; Mark 7:7; Col 2:22 (SBLGNT).

Christological Trajectory

Colossians makes Christ both ground and sphere, and only then pattern.

  • Ground.
    • Christ is “the image of the invisible God”, the one “by” whom, “through” whom and “for” whom all things were created. He is “before all things”, and “in Him all things hold together” (1:15–17) [T].
    • He is “head of the body, the church”, “the beginning, the firstborn from the dead”, in whom “all the fullness” was pleased to dwell. Through him God reconciled all things, “having made peace through the blood of His cross” (1:18–20) [T].
    • The readers are reconciled “in His fleshly body through death” (1:22). θάνατος (“death”) occurs only there in the letter (SBLGNT) [T] [S: dig].
    • In him “all the fullness of Deity dwells in bodily form” (2:9) [T]. θεότης (“Deity”) occurs nowhere else in the NT (SBLGNT) [T].
    • At the cross he cancelled the certificate of debt and disarmed the rulers and authorities (2:14–15) [T]. Christ as the subject who “stripped off” the powers, in the true middle, is a slight lean, moderate [S: Logos 2].
    • He is now enthroned at God’s right hand (3:1) and will be revealed (3:4) [T].
    • The mystery is Christ: “Christ in you” (1:27), “God’s mystery, Christ” (2:2, on the reading of 𝔓⁴⁶ B), and “the mystery of Christ” (4:3) — the thread’s last station [T].
  • Sphere.
    • Believers are “in Him”: filled (2:10), circumcised (2:11), buried and raised with him (2:12; 3:1), their life “hidden with Christ in God” (3:3) [T]. Five of the letter’s eight ἐν αὐτῷ fall in 2:6–15, with two ἐν ᾧ and three σύν- compound verbs [T] (verified; 2:6–15 dig [S: dig]).
    • The σύν (“with”) language — “with Christ” (2:20; 3:3), “with Him” (2:13; 3:4) — makes union the engine of the ethics [T]/[I].
    • Unlike Eph 2:6, Colossians does not seat the believers with Christ: their share is a hidden life, to be revealed (3:3–4) [T] (3:1–4 dig [S: dig]).
  • Pattern.
    • It is explicit once: “just as the Lord forgave you, so also should you” (3:13) [T].
    • It is implicit twice:
        1. the new self is renewed “according to the image of the One who created him” (3:10), read with 1:15 [I], moderate;
        1. the five virtues of 3:12 are, elsewhere in Paul, the character of God and of Christ — kindness, forbearance and patience at Rom 2:4; compassions at Rom 12:1 and 2 Cor 1:3; “the gentleness of Christ” at 2 Cor 10:1 [T]. That is moderate–high as the family likeness. The Greek Old Testament’s divine-attribute formula (Exod 34:6, via Ps 144:7–9) is a low–moderate field behind it [S: audit 4]. The imitation pattern “as … so” is closest in Luke 6:35–36, Matt 18:33 and Eph 4:32 [S: audit 4].
    • Lord. “The fear of the Lord”, Israel’s formula, is the slave’s fear of Christ (3:22, with “the Lord Christ”, 3:24). This is moderate–high and a shared Pauline move (2 Cor 5:10–11; Eph 5:21) [S: audit 3]. κύριος (“Lord, master”) stands nine times in six verses of the household code (SBLGNT) [T].

Moralism check. Colossians contains its own warning against moralism. The rival teaching is a regimen — “Do not handle, do not taste, do not touch!” — and Paul’s verdict is that such rules “are of no value against fleshly indulgence” (2:21–23) [T]. A sermon on 3:5–17 that offers a list of vices to avoid and virtues to cultivate, without the death and resurrection of 2:20–3:4, preaches the very “philosophy” the letter rejects [I].

The commands of chapter 3 are addressed to people who “have died” and “have been raised” (3:1, 3). They are put in clothing language — ἀπεκδύομαι (“to lay aside”, 3:9) and ἐνδύω (“to put on”, 3:10, 12) — because the new self has already been put on (3:10) [T]; the aorist participles of 3:9–10 are causal, moderate–high [S: dig]. The virtues are put on by those already “chosen, holy and beloved” (3:12) [T]. Christ is Lord and life first (3:4, “Christ, who is our life”), and pattern second.

The confession is not an argument for “high Christology” in the abstract. In the letter it does a pastoral job: it leaves no remainder of “fullness” for angels, powers, visions or regimens to supply [I].

Preaching Traps

  1. “Firstborn” read as “first-created”. Bites at: 1:15–18. It sounds like “Jesus was the first and greatest of God’s creatures”. The next clause forbids it: “for by Him all things were created” (1:16) [T]. In the firstborn law (Gen 49:3; Deut 21:17) and the royal psalm (Ps 89:27, LXX 88:28, now moderate–high [S: audit 1]), πρωτότοκος (“firstborn”) speaks of rank and inheritance [I]. The opposite trap is to explain the word away and never preach its “from the dead” partner (1:18).

  2. Preaching against a reconstructed “Colossian heresy”. Bites at: 2:8–23. It sounds like a detailed lecture on first-century mysticism or syncretism, followed by its modern equivalent. The text gives features, not a system, and the reconstructions are many (Presenting Situation, now with holders) [S: Logos 3]. Preach the features and Paul’s answer to them — the sufficiency of Christ — and let the congregation recognise its own “something more”.

  3. 2:16–17 as a verdict on the Lord’s Day, or on any modern calendar. Bites at: 2:16–17. It sounds like “the Sabbath is abolished”, or, in the other direction, an explanation of why 2:16 cannot touch the Sabbath. This is a tribal landmine [I]. What the text says is that the observances are “a shadow of what is to come”, that “the substance belongs to Christ”, and that no one is to judge the believer by them (2:16–17) [T].

    v1.0:

    • The owned commentators: Wright explicitly includes the weekly Sabbath; so does Witherington in effect, and Dunn by implication. Pao’s “practices, not days” rests on an Adventist article [S: Logos 4].
    • A Greek-only point: σάββατα σαββάτων (“sabbath of sabbaths”) is used in Swete only of the Day of Atonement (Lev 16:31; 23:32), though the Hebrew שַׁבַּת שַׁבָּתוֹן (“a sabbath of complete rest”) is also used of the weekly Sabbath (Exod 31:15; 35:2; Lev 23:3, WLC) [S: Logos 3].
    • For the preacher: the dig’s “includes the weekly Sabbath” stands at moderate–high, and the settled debate is still not the passage’s question.

  4. The household code (3:18–4:1) either skipped or preached as a free-standing hierarchy. Bites at: 3:18–4:1. v1.0 — the count corrected:

    • The word κύριος (“Lord, master”) occurs nine times in six of these nine verses (3:18, 20, 22, 23, 24; 4:1) — nine of the letter’s sixteen (SBLGNT) [T].
    • The nine verses also hold nine of the letter’s thirty imperatives.
    • Colossians and Ephesians alone among the NT codes use κύριος for the owner (3:18–4:1 dig [S: dig]).

    Every instruction is set “in the Lord” or “as for the Lord”, and the masters “too have a Master in heaven” (4:1) [T].

    The slave instructions are the longest: 56 of the code’s 116 words. They are about the length of Ephesians’ slave section (59), while Colossians compresses the other lines — marriage takes 19 words against Ephesians’ 189 — so the emphasis is relative. A sermon that follows Ephesians’ proportions misreads Colossians [S: dig].

    The slave Onesimus is introduced with the same titles as Tychicus and Epaphras, “faithful and beloved brother”, and “one of you” (4:9; 1:7; 4:7, 12) [T]. This is a pastoral landmine. The pulpit text says “bondservants” (ESV) where the study text says “slaves” (NASB95); tell the congregation it is the same word, δοῦλος (“slave”). Nor will the ESV let them hear that Tychicus and Epaphras are “fellow slave” and “slave of Christ” (4:7, 12, ESV “servant”).

  5. 1:24 heard as “Christ’s sufferings were insufficient”. Bites at: 1:24. It sounds like either a scandal or a hasty denial. The verse speaks of “Christ’s afflictions” (θλίψεις, “afflictions”), not his cross, filled up in Paul’s flesh “on behalf of His body”, and the reconciliation was finished at 1:20–22 [T]/[I]. It is a hard verse; give it a full dig rather than a sentence.

    v1.0 — the controls [S: dig]:

    • θλῖψις is never used of the cross;
    • ὑπέρ in Colossians is always of ministers acting for churches;
    • 1:22 → 1:24 crosses “body” and “flesh”;
    • the ἀναπληρόω + ὑστέρημα idiom (1 Cor 16:17; Phil 2:30) is the “absence” idiom.

    The commentators in hand read the messianic woes, shared by Christ with his body (Dunn; Wright; O’Brien, NBC) — a slight lean, moderate — with Garland’s absence reading still live on the idiom [S: Logos 1] [S: Logos 2]. Round 1’s filing of Wright as a “cross” reading was wrong [S: Logos 2].

  6. 1:23 (“if indeed you continue”) preached either as a threat or as nothing. Bites at: 1:21–23. The condition is real, and its object is staying “not moved away from the hope of the gospel” — the same concern as 2:6–7 and 2:18 [T]/[I]. It is the letter’s warning in a single verse, not a separate doctrine of assurance.

    v1.0 [S: dig]:

    • εἴ γε is confident in Paul’s usage (Eph 3:2; 4:21);
    • the foundation is already laid (a perfect participle);
    • the participle “not being moved” is present.

  7. “Heavenly-minded” escapism from 3:1–4. Bites at: 3:1–4. It sounds like detachment from earthly duties. The “things above” lead directly to speech, sex, money, marriage, parenting and work (3:5–4:1). Heaven’s orientation is shown in earthly relationships [I]. v1.0: the textual answer — “on earth” is what Christ created and reconciled (1:16, 20), and the phrase returns at 3:2, 5 [T] [S: dig].

  8. v1.0 — new. 2:11–12 made a battleground for baptism. Bites at: 2:11–12. It sounds like a sermon on the mode or subjects of baptism. The passage’s subject is union with Christ — circumcised, buried and raised “in Him” — and baptism is its sign (2:12) [T]/[I]. The readings of the circumcision “of Christ” divide among the cross (moderate–high), conversion and baptism [S: Logos 2]. Preach the union, mention baptism, and do not settle a church dispute from this verse (2:6–15 dig [S: dig]).

  9. v1.0 — new. “Let the peace of Christ rule” (3:15) as a rule for private guidance. Bites at: 3:15. It sounds like “go with the decision you feel peace about”. The peace is Christ’s (1:20). The hearts are plural, the setting is complaints and forgiveness “in one body” (3:13–15), and the verb is the umpire’s, against the false umpire of 2:18 (Echo 17) [T]/[I] (3:12–17 dig [S: dig]).

  10. v1.0 — new. “Redeem the time” (4:5) as time management. Bites at: 4:5. It sounds like a sermon on productivity. καιρός (“time”) is opportunity. The setting is “towards outsiders”, and the next verse is about answering each person [T]/[I] (4:2–6 dig [S: dig]).

Variants that bear on findings (NA28 apparatus)

  • 2:2 — “the mystery of God, Christ”. NA28 prints τοῦ θεοῦ, Χριστοῦ (“of God, Christ”), with 𝔓⁴⁶ B alone among the Greek witnesses. The apparatus records seven other readings — “of God, which is Christ”; “of God the Father of Christ”; “of the God and Father and of Christ” (the majority text); “of Christ”; “of God” — every one of which reads as an attempt to explain the short text. This is the reading that most directly identifies the mystery with Christ himself. The ESV (“God’s mystery, which is Christ”) and the NASB95 (“God’s mystery, that is, Christ Himself”) both follow it [T: as NA28 prints it].
  • 2:18 — ἃ ἑόρακεν ἐμβατεύων (“taking his stand on visions he has seen”). The majority text and the corrected Sinaiticus add μή (“what he has not seen”, as in the KJV). NA28 follows 𝔓⁴⁶ א* A B D*. The shorter reading points to visionary experience; the longer to speculation. v1.0: the initiation sense of ἐμβατεύω is low. Dunn and Pao both reject it, and the Claros inscriptions are mid-second century [S: Logos 3].
  • 2:10 — ὅς (“who”) / ὅ (“which”). 𝔓⁴⁶ B D F G read the neuter, which makes the fullness head of every power. NA28’s ὅς is moderate. 𝔓⁴⁶ and B read ὅ at both 1:27 and 2:10, and NA28 follows them only at 1:27. ὅ ἐστιν is this letter’s formula (1:24, 27; 3:14), which may explain the neuter as assimilation [S: Logos 2] [S: Logos 3].
  • 1:14 — “through His blood”. Added by a few late witnesses (614 630 1505 2464), which the apparatus marks as from Eph 1:7. It is absent from NA28, the ESV and the NASB95 (present in the KJV). It is not part of Colossians’ text; the blood is at 1:20.
  • 1:12 — “qualified you” (ὑμᾶς, NA28: א B) / “qualified us” (ἡμᾶς: A C D and the majority). ESV “you”; NASB95 “us”. Then 1:13 has ἡμᾶς (“us”) in every witness. The shift between “you” and “us” is part of the passage, and the variant is a scribe smoothing it out [I] (Echo row 31).
  • 1:20 — the second [δι᾽ αὐτοῦ] (“through him”). Bracketed in NA28. It is omitted by B D* F G I L and others, Latin and Sahidic, and read by 𝔓⁴⁶ א A C and the majority. It is probably original on internal evidence [S: Logos 1]. Arguments that “through the blood of his cross” is a gloss lean partly on this uncertain phrase [S: Logos 4].
  • 3:4 — “your life” (ὑμῶν, NA28: 𝔓⁴⁶ א C D*) / “our life” (ἡμῶν: B and the majority). ESV “your”; NASB95 “our”.
  • 3:6 — “upon the sons of disobedience”. Bracketed in NA28; omitted by 𝔓⁴⁶ B. The ESV omits it in its text; the NASB95 includes it. The phrase is identical at Eph 5:6.
  • 3:11 — “male and female”. A Western addition (D* F G 629, Latin witnesses), marked by the apparatus as from Gal 3:28. The scribes heard Genesis 1:27 / Galatians 3:28 behind 3:10–11, which is indirect early support for the Genesis row of the Intertextual Map [I].

  • 3:21 — ἐρεθίζετε / παροργίζετε (“provoke” / “provoke to anger”). A harmonisation to Eph 6:4.
  • 3:22 — κύριον / θεόν (“the Lord” / “God”). The majority reads θεόν, and the KJV’s “fearing God” makes the transfer of the fear of the Lord to Christ disappear. The Clementine Vulgate reads timentes Deum [S: Logos 4].

Pulpit Notes — where the ESV and the NASB95 diverge in a way that bears on a finding

These are notes for the preacher about what the congregation will hear, not translation verdicts. v1.0: the digs found that the ESV pulpit text serves this letter better than the study text more often than not. Where it is better, it is mostly because it keeps a repeated Greek word as one English word.

Where the ESV keeps what the NASB95 loses: - The fullness thread. It is audible in the ESV at 1:9 (“filled”), 1:19, 2:9 (“fullness”), 2:10 (“you have been filled in him”) and 4:17 (“fulfil”). The NASB95 breaks it at 2:10 (“made complete”), and uses “complete” for two different Greek words (τέλειος at 1:28; πεπληρωμένοι at 2:10). Neither translation lets the thread be heard at 1:24, 1:25, 2:2 or 4:12. - Struggle and maturity. The ESV keeps “struggling … struggle … struggling” (1:29; 2:1; 4:12) and “mature … mature” (1:28; 4:12); the NASB95 breaks both. - “Knit together” (2:2; 2:19). - 3:5 — νεκρώσατε. ESV “Put to death” (an imperative, as the Greek). The NASB95’s “consider the members … as dead to” softens the command, and changes the list into what one is dead to. - 3:10 — “the One who created him”. Here the NASB95 is closer: the ESV’s “its creator” hides the Genesis “him” (αὐτόν). - 4:2 “watchful”, which lets Gethsemane be heard. - 4:5 “Walk”, the letter’s chain (1:10; 2:6; 3:7; 4:5); the NASB95 has “conduct yourselves”. - 4:4, 6 “ought … ought”, Paul’s δεῖ (“must”) and the church’s. - 4:16 “the letter from Laodicea”; the NASB95’s “my letter” decides who wrote it. - 4:18 “chains” (δεσμῶν). - 1:17–18a.

Where the ESV hides something the preacher should supply: - 1:16 levels the perfect ἔκτισται (“have been created”). - 1:20 moves “whether on earth or in heaven” and loses the closing reversal; it also omits the bracketed “through him”. - 1:11–12 — the punctuation. NA28 puts a full stop before μετὰ χαρᾶς (“with joy”) and attaches it to “giving thanks”. The ESV and NASB95 divide on it. - 1:27 — “Christ in you”. The “you” is plural. - 2:1, 2:5. “Flesh” is lost (“face to face”; “absent in body”). The ESV’s “body” at 2:5 collides with “His body, the church” (1:24). - 2:13 “God made you alive”. The ESV supplies a subject the Greek leaves unexpressed. - 2:15. “Disarmed” is the same verb as 3:9’s “put off”. “In him” could be “in it”, the cross. - 2:17 “substance”. Both versions lose σῶμα, so the triple “body” (2:17, 19, 23) is audible only in the KJV. - 2:18 “sensuous mind”. It turns the ascetic’s fleshly mind into a sensual one and loses the irony. The English-only echo of “qualified” (1:12) and “disqualify” (2:18) is not in the Greek. - 2:20 “regulations”, after 2:14’s “legal demands”, breaks the δόγμα link the NASB95 keeps (“decrees … decrees”). - 3:14 “binds everything together in perfect harmony” is a paraphrase; the NASB95’s “perfect bond of unity” interprets too. The Greek is “the bond of perfection”. - 3:16 “with thankfulness” hides “with grace” (cf. 4:6). - 3:18–4:1 — κύριος is one word for “masters”, “Master” and “Lord”; tell the congregation. - 3:20 “pleases the Lord” loses “in the Lord”. - 3:22 “earthly masters” loses “according to the flesh”, the flesh/heaven frame and the Philemon link. - 4:1 “justly and fairly” loses ἰσότης (“equality”) and the verb “grant”. - 4:3 “declare … speak” uses two English verbs for one Greek verb; “in prison” loses “bound”. - 4:6 “Let your speech … be gracious” hides both λόγος (“word”) and χάρις (“grace”). - 4:7, 12 “servant” for σύνδουλος / δοῦλος hides the slaves’ title (Trap 4).

The Greek names neither God nor the Father, and each translation supplies one, in a different place. v1.0: the owned technical commentators returned (Dunn, Pao, Wright) agree on “the fullness” as subject — a periphrasis for God — so the ESV’s reading is moderate–high [S: Logos 4]. The Greek allows both [S], so tell the congregation. - The ESV’s reading is the majority of recent commentary (Arnold). “Physical elements” (O’Brien) and “elementary principles” (Carr, Wink, Forbes) are still held [S: Logos 1]. - Dunn reads the elements as the cosmic elements, with Wis 7:17 cited [S: Logos 3]. - In the Greek Old Testament στοιχεῖον has the physical sense (Wis 7:17; 19:18; 4 Macc 12:13), without making Wisdom a source [S: audit 3]. - The evidence for a “spirits” sense before the second century is for the association of spirits with the elements (Jub 2:2; 1 En 60:11–12), not for the word [S: Logos 1]. - 3:16 — the songs. This book launches no Old Testament programme, and no argument is NT-authorised in the strict sense.

For the series as a whole: Colossians makes one claim — all the fullness of God is in Christ, and in him you have been filled — and makes it before the warning, so that nothing is left for a philosophy, a regimen or a power to add. The life that follows is union, not achievement. The letter welds each passage to the last, refuses both escape from the body and war on it, gives Gentiles Israel’s names, and gives Christ the LORD’s place. It ends by handing its own ministry to the church and showing its theology in named people. The preacher’s discipline follows from the letter itself: - preach the ground as long as the command; - read the last verse of the previous unit first; - supply the repeated Greek words the English hides; - leave out the Old Testament allusions the letter does not make.
