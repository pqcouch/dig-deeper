## The Passage

**NA28:**

> **21** Καὶ ὑμᾶς ποτε ὄντας ἀπηλλοτριωμένους καὶ ἐχθροὺς τῇ διανοίᾳ ἐν τοῖς ἔργοις τοῖς πονηροῖς, **22** νυνὶ δὲ ἀποκατήλλαξεν ἐν τῷ σώματι τῆς σαρκὸς αὐτοῦ διὰ τοῦ θανάτου παραστῆσαι ὑμᾶς ἁγίους καὶ ἀμώμους καὶ ἀνεγκλήτους κατενώπιον αὐτοῦ, **23** εἴ γε ἐπιμένετε τῇ πίστει τεθεμελιωμένοι καὶ ἑδραῖοι καὶ μὴ μετακινούμενοι ἀπὸ τῆς ἐλπίδος τοῦ εὐαγγελίου οὗ ἠκούσατε, τοῦ κηρυχθέντος ἐν πάσῃ κτίσει τῇ ὑπὸ τὸν οὐρανόν, οὗ ἐγενόμην ἐγὼ Παῦλος διάκονος.

## Headline Findings

1. **The passage turns the cosmic reconciliation of 1:20 onto the readers in a strict "then — now — to what end — on what condition" sequence.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Linking Words, Repetition, Positional Necessity. "And you" (Καὶ ὑμᾶς, 1:21) picks up "all things" (1:20); the verb ἀποκαταλλάσσω ("to reconcile") stands in the NT only at Eph 2:16 and Col 1:20, 22 (SBLGNT), so the confession's verb is used again within two verses of its first use, now with "you" as its object. The one sentence then moves **then** (ποτε, "once", 1:21) → **now** (νυνὶ δέ, "but now", 1:22) → **purpose** (παραστῆσαι, "to present", 1:22) → **condition** (εἴ γε, "if indeed", 1:23) → **the gospel and its minister** (1:23b). *High confidence.*

2. **The death of Christ is named here as nowhere else in the letter, and named physically.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Repetition, Move 4. θάνατος ("death") occurs **once in Colossians**, at 1:22 (SBLGNT; NA28 agrees); 1:20 has "the blood of his cross" and 2:14 "the cross", but only here is the death itself named. It is "in the body of his flesh" (ἐν τῷ σώματι τῆς σαρκὸς αὐτοῦ) — a phrase that stands in the NT only here and at 2:11 (by phrase search, SBLGNT) — which distinguishes his physical body from "the body, the church" of 1:18 two sentences earlier `[T]`/`[I]`. The reconciliation of "all things" happened in one human body on one day. *High confidence.*

3. **The goal of reconciliation is a presentation before God, and the NT's language for that presentation looks to the last day — which is why the condition of 1:23 belongs here.** `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the timing. Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Tool 11 (Pauline parallels), Original Languages, Difficult Verses. "Holy and blameless … before him" (ἁγίους καὶ ἀμώμους … κατενώπιον αὐτοῦ) is near-verbatim with Eph 1:4; ἄμωμος with κατενώπιον stands in the NT only at Eph 1:4, Col 1:22 and Jude 24, where Christ "is able … to make you stand in the presence of His glory blameless" (SBLGNT) `[T]`. ἀνέγκλητος ("beyond reproach") is what God will keep believers "on the day of our Lord Jesus Christ" (1 Cor 1:8) `[T]`. In Paul's presenting language, the presentation is mostly future (Rom 14:10; 2 Cor 4:14; 11:2; Eph 5:27; Col 1:28) `[T]`. So: reconciled **now**, presented **then**, continuing **in between** `[I]`. *Moderate–high on the eschatological reading.*

4. **The condition is real, and its object is staying put on a foundation already laid — not achieving something new.** `[T]`/`[I]` Surfaced by: Linking Words, Original Languages, Vocabulary, Move 4. εἴ γε ("if indeed") is Paul's alone in the NT (2 Cor 5:3; Gal 3:4; Eph 3:2; 4:21; Col 1:23), and at Eph 3:2 and 4:21 it introduces something Paul assumes is true `[T]`. What is required is to "remain" (ἐπιμένετε) in the faith; the readers are already "founded" (τεθεμελιωμένοι, a perfect participle) and "steadfast", and the danger is being "moved away" (μὴ μετακινούμενοι, a present **passive**) — shifted by someone else `[T]`. The letter's warnings follow: "that no one may delude you" (2:4); "that no one takes you captive" (2:8) `[T]`. The overview's Preaching Trap 6 is confirmed and given its grammar. *High on the words; moderate–high on the reading.*

5. **1:23 is the hinge between the confession and Paul's ministry: its last clause is repeated two verses later.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Repetition, Move 4. οὗ ἐγενόμην ἐγὼ Παῦλος διάκονος ("of which I, Paul, became a minister", 1:23) → ἧς ἐγενόμην ἐγὼ διάκονος ("of which I became a minister", 1:25) — of the gospel, then of the church. 1:23 also closes the bracket opened at 1:5–6 (hope, gospel, heard, universal scope). So the verse ends one movement (1:3–23) and opens the next (1:24–2:5) `[T]`. The 1:24–2:5 dig recorded the 1:23 → 1:25 link `[S: dig]` (reached independently). *High confidence.*

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book-level:** to keep a young Gentile church walking "in him" as they began, by setting before them a Christ in whom all fullness dwells, so that no rival can persuade them they lack anything (overview) `[I]`.

**Passage-level:** to tell the readers that the reconciliation of "all things" includes them; to name what they were, what God has done, what he means to do with them, and what they must do meanwhile; and to attach Paul to that gospel as its minister before he speaks of his own labour for them `[T]`/`[I]`. The passage does in three verses what the letter will do at length: the indicative of reconciliation (cf. 2:9–15), the call to continue (cf. 2:6–7), and the warning against being moved (cf. 2:8–23) `[I]`.

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement:** the confession has just said that God was pleased, through the Son, "to reconcile all things to himself, having made peace through the blood of his cross … whether things on earth or things in heaven" (1:20) `[T]`. That is universal in scope and impersonal in grammar ("all things", τὰ πάντα, neuter) `[T]`. It leaves a question open: *where do the readers stand in this "all things"?* Before that, the thanksgiving had told them they were "rescued … transferred … in whom we have redemption" (1:13–14) `[T]`, and the prayer had asked that they "walk worthily … in every good work" (1:10) `[T]`.

**Necessity answer — why the passage exists here.**

1. **Application of 1:20.** "And you" (1:21) answers the open question: the neuter "all things" becomes the plural "you", and the same rare verb is used (Headline 1) `[T]`. The 1:15–20 dig named this as the immediate application `[S: dig]` (reached independently). Without 1:21–23 the confession could be heard as cosmology; with it, it is the readers' own history `[I]`.
2. **A personal "then and now" to set beside the cosmic one.** The confession had its own before-and-after (creation, then reconciliation); the readers get theirs: estranged, hostile, doing evil works → reconciled → to be presented holy `[T]`/`[I]`.
3. **The condition must come before the warnings.** Chapter 2 is a series of "let no one" warnings (2:4, 8, 16, 18) `[T]`. 1:23 states the principle behind them all — remain, do not be moved — at the point where the gospel's content has just been fully set out `[I]`.
4. **The minister must be named before his ministry is described.** 1:23's last clause, repeated at 1:25, turns the letter from the gospel to Paul's service of it (Headline 5) `[T]`.

**Implication for this passage:** it is a hinge passage. It closes 1:3–23 (applying the confession and the thanksgiving to the readers) and it opens what follows (the minister in 1:24–2:5; the condition in 2:6–23). A sermon on 1:21–23 that ignores 1:20 loses its "and"; one that ignores 1:24–2:5 and 2:6–8 loses the reason for its "if" `[I]`.

**Revisit after Tool 11:** Move 2 is empty — no Old Testament source here. Move 4 confirms the positional answer from both directions: backwards (1:10, 1:13–14, 1:18, 1:20) and forwards (1:24, 1:25, 1:28, 2:5, 2:7, 2:11, 2:14, 3:7–8) (Tool 11, Internal).

**Immediately before:** 1:15–20, the confession, ending in reconciliation and peace through the blood of the cross. **Immediately after:** 1:24, "Now I rejoice in my sufferings for your sake … in my flesh … for the sake of his body" — which crosses the two nouns of 1:22, "body" and "flesh" (the 1:24–2:5 dig's control for that verse `[S: dig]`, taken from it) `[T]`.

**Section:** the close of 1:3–23 (overview Arc Map); the bracket 1:5–6 ↔ 1:23 confirms the boundary (1:1–14 dig) `[S: dig]`. The verse also overlaps the next section through its last clause `[T]`.

### 3. Structure

One Greek sentence, in five movements `[T]`:

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 1:21 | **Then** — ποτε | What they were: estranged, hostile in mind, in evil works |
| 1:22a | **Now** — νυνὶ δέ | What God has done: reconciled, in Christ's body of flesh, through death |
| 1:22b | **To what end** — παραστῆσαι | What God means to do: present them holy, blameless, unaccusable before him |
| 1:23a | **On what condition** — εἴ γε | What they must do meanwhile: remain in the faith, founded, steadfast, not moved from the hope |
| 1:23b | **The gospel and its minister** | What the hope rests on: the gospel they heard, proclaimed in all creation, of which Paul became minister |

**Device 1 — the "once / but now" turn.** ποτε … νυνὶ δέ ("once … but now") stands at 1:21–22 and again at 3:7–8, the only two occurrences of each word in the letter (SBLGNT) `[T]` — the overview's Echo 8. In the NT the two words stand within a verse of each other also at Eph 2:13 and Phlm 11 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. The first turn in Colossians is of reconciliation, the second of conduct `[S]` (overview).

**Device 2 — a hanging accusative.** "And you" (Καὶ ὑμᾶς) is fronted and in the accusative; it waits for a verb. In NA28 the verb arrives as "he reconciled" (ἀποκατήλλαξεν), and "you" is its object, restated at 1:22b `[T]`. In the SBLGNT's reading the verb is "you were reconciled", and the opening "you" is left without a construction (anacoluthon) — a harder sentence, which the SBLGNT marks with dashes `[T: as the SBLGNT prints it]`.

**Device 3 — triplets.** Three descriptions of the "then" (alienated, hostile in mind, in evil works); three of the presentation (holy, blameless, unaccusable); three of the continuance (founded, steadfast, not moved) `[T]`. The symmetry is clear in the Greek and in both English versions `[T]`.

**Clause-Flow Layout** (NA28 wording):

- **Καὶ ὑμᾶς** — "and you" (fronted object, resumed below)
    - ποτε ὄντας ἀπηλλοτριωμένους — "once being estranged"
    - καὶ ἐχθροὺς τῇ διανοίᾳ — "and hostile in mind"
    - ἐν τοῖς ἔργοις τοῖς πονηροῖς — "in the evil works"
- **νυνὶ δὲ ἀποκατήλλαξεν** — MAIN: "but now he has reconciled" (indicative, aorist)
    - ἐν τῷ σώματι τῆς σαρκὸς αὐτοῦ — "in the body of his flesh" (where)
    - διὰ τοῦ θανάτου — "through the death" (how)
    - παραστῆσαι ὑμᾶς — "to present you" (purpose)
        - ἁγίους καὶ ἀμώμους καὶ ἀνεγκλήτους — "holy and blameless and unaccusable"
        - κατενώπιον αὐτοῦ — "before him"
    - εἴ γε ἐπιμένετε τῇ πίστει — "if indeed you remain in the faith" (condition)
        - τεθεμελιωμένοι — "founded" (perfect participle)
        - καὶ ἑδραῖοι — "and steadfast" (adjective)
        - καὶ μὴ μετακινούμενοι ἀπὸ τῆς ἐλπίδος τοῦ εὐαγγελίου — "and not being moved from the hope of the gospel" (present passive)
            - οὗ ἠκούσατε — "which you heard"
            - τοῦ κηρυχθέντος ἐν πάσῃ κτίσει τῇ ὑπὸ τὸν οὐρανόν — "proclaimed in all creation under heaven"
            - οὗ ἐγενόμην ἐγὼ Παῦλος διάκονος — "of which I, Paul, became minister" → 1:25

**Central assertions, one line each:**

- **1:21–22a — truth:** "You, once estranged, he has now reconciled" — an indicative, aorist, with God (or Christ) as subject `[T]`.
- **1:22b — truth of purpose:** "to present you holy … before him" — God's intention, not a command `[T]`.
- **1:23a — condition (not a command in form):** "if indeed you remain in the faith" — indicative in a conditional clause; the passage has no imperative `[T]`.
- **1:23b — truth:** the gospel is the one they heard, universally proclaimed, served by Paul `[T]`.

The left-to-right caveat applies: the main clause is the reconciliation; the condition hangs from it. The passage is first a statement of what God has done, and only then a statement of what that requires `[T]`/`[I]`.

### 4. Linking Words

- **Καί, 1:21 — "and"**: continuation from 1:20. Not a mere connective: it adds "you" to "all things" — "and you too" `[T]`/`[I]`, high.
- **ποτε … νυνὶ δέ, 1:21–22 — "once … but now"**: temporal contrast with a strong adversative (δέ) `[T]`.
- **ὄντας, 1:21 — "being"**: a participle that the NASB95 makes concessive ("although you were") and the ESV relative ("who once were") `[T]`. The Greek is open; the NASB95's "although … yet" is an interpretation, though a natural one `[I]`.
- **ἐν … διά, 1:22 — "in … through"**: place and means — in his physical body, through his death `[T]`.
- **παραστῆσαι, 1:22 — "to present"**: purpose infinitive; the end in view of the reconciliation `[T]`.
- **εἴ γε, 1:23 — "if indeed"**: conditional with the particle γε. In Paul it can introduce something assumed (Eph 3:2, "if indeed you have heard of the stewardship"; Eph 4:21, "if indeed you have heard him") or something doubted (Gal 3:4, "if indeed it was in vain") `[T]`. So the particle does not by itself settle whether Paul is confident or anxious; the context does `[I]`. Here the context is a church whose "faith" Paul has just praised (1:4) and whose "stability of faith" he will rejoice to see (2:5) `[T]` — so confidence, with a real condition `[I]`, moderate–high.
- **ἀπό, 1:23 — "away from"**: separation — the hope is the place they could be moved from `[T]`.
- **οὗ … τοῦ … οὗ, 1:23 — relative chain**: three clauses hang off "the gospel": heard by you, proclaimed everywhere, served by me `[T]`.

### 5. Parallels

Prose with three triads (Tool 3) `[T]`. Two are worth noting for meaning:

- **The "then" triad and the presentation triad answer each other.** Estranged ↔ before him (presence); hostile in mind ↔ holy (devoted); in evil works ↔ blameless and unaccusable (no charge) `[I]`, moderate. The pairing is not marked by words; it is a symmetry of ideas.
- **The continuance triad moves from state to danger.** Founded (perfect: a completed foundation), steadfast (adjective: a quality), not moved (present passive: a resisted pressure) `[T]`.

### 6. Narrator's Comment

**N/A** — epistle, not narrative. The nearest equivalent is Paul's self-naming, "I, Paul" (ἐγὼ Παῦλος, 1:23), one of the letter's three uses of his name (1:1; 1:23; 4:18; SBLGNT) `[T]`. The emphatic ἐγώ with the name puts his personal authority behind the gospel just described `[I]`.

### 7. Vocabulary

**Proper-noun inventory:** Παῦλος (Paul) only `[T]`. No canonical freight; the name is the author's own self-reference.

**Words used once in Colossians.** Thirteen of the content words in these three verses occur nowhere else in the letter: ἀπαλλοτριόω ("to estrange"), ἐχθρός ("hostile"), διάνοια ("mind"), πονηρός ("evil"), θάνατος ("death"), ἄμωμος ("blameless"), ἀνέγκλητος ("unaccusable"), κατενώπιον ("before"), ἐπιμένω ("to remain"), θεμελιόω ("to found"), ἑδραῖος ("steadfast"), μετακινέω ("to move away"), κηρύσσω ("to proclaim") (SBLGNT) `[T]`. **The vocabulary of estrangement, of the cross's death, of the judgment-seat and of perseverance is concentrated here.** *High.*

**ἀπαλλοτριόω ("to estrange, alienate"), 1:21** `[T]`, *high*. Three NT verses: Eph 2:12 ("excluded from the commonwealth of Israel"), Eph 4:18 ("excluded from the life of God"), Col 1:21 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Eph 4:18 also has διάνοια — "darkened in their understanding (τῇ διανοίᾳ) … excluded from the life of God" — the only other NT verse joining the two words `[T]`. In Swete the perfect participle stands at Ps 68:9 LXX ("I have become estranged from my brothers") and Ezek 14:5 ("their hearts … estranged from me in their thoughts"), and the verb in about a dozen verses in all `[T]` (see the positive control under Tool 11). Colossians does not say *from what* they were estranged; the context (reconciled "to him", presented "before him") implies from God `[I]`, high.

**ἐχθρὸς τῇ διανοίᾳ ("hostile in mind"), 1:21** `[T]`, *high*. The hostility is located in the mind (διάνοια, "understanding, thinking") and expressed in works — not only in deeds `[T]`. The closest Pauline parallel is Rom 5:10, "if while we were enemies (ἐχθροὶ ὄντες) we were reconciled to God through the death (διὰ τοῦ θανάτου) of His Son" — ἐχθρός with καταλλάσσω ("to reconcile") stands within a verse only there (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Romans 5:10 and Colossians 1:21–22 share being enemies, being reconciled, and "through the death" `[T]`. The mind-language prepares the letter's contrast between two minds: "set your minds (φρονεῖτε) on things above" (3:2) and the rival's "fleshly mind" (ὑπὸ τοῦ νοὸς τῆς σαρκός, 2:18) `[T]` for the words, `[I]` for the thread — moderate.

**ἐν τοῖς ἔργοις τοῖς πονηροῖς ("in the evil works"), 1:21** `[T]`, *high on the words*. ἔργον ("work") stands in Colossians at 1:10, 1:21 and 3:17 (SBLGNT) `[T]`: "in every **good** work" (1:10), "in the **evil** works" (1:21), "whatever you do in word or **work**" (3:17). The prayer's "good work" and the past's "evil works" use the same noun with the same preposition, ἐν `[T]`. Both English versions say "good work" at 1:10 and "evil deeds" at 1:21, so the congregation will not hear the antithesis (Tool 8).

**τὸ σῶμα τῆς σαρκὸς αὐτοῦ ("the body of his flesh"), 1:22** `[T]`, *high on the facts*. The phrase "body … of the flesh" stands in the NT only here and at 2:11 (SBLGNT, phrase search; the 2:6–15 dig made the same search and found the same two verses `[S: dig]`, reached independently) `[T]`. In Swete the nearest is Sir 23:16 (ἐν σώματι σαρκὸς αὐτοῦ, of a fornicator) `[T]`; the Hebrew equivalent is attested at Qumran `[S]`. Here it does two jobs: it names Christ's **physical** body, against the "body, the church" of 1:18 `[I]`, high; and it makes the death a bodily death, which a teaching that shuns the body (2:23, "severe treatment of the body") would need to hear `[I]`, moderate.

**θάνατος ("death"), 1:22** `[T]`, *high*. Once in the letter (Headline 2). In NA28 the noun stands with the article and no "his" (διὰ τοῦ θανάτου, "through the death"); some manuscripts add αὐτοῦ ("his"), which the ESV's "by his death" also supplies (Textual Variants) `[T]`.

**παρίστημι ("to present"), 1:22** `[T]`, *high on the uses; moderate–high on the setting*. In Colossians only 1:22 (God's purpose) and 1:28 (Paul's aim) (SBLGNT) — the overview's Echo 11 `[T]`. In Paul the verb has three settings `[T]`:
- **sacrificial** — "present your bodies a living and holy sacrifice" (Rom 12:1; cf. Rom 6:13, 19);
- **judicial** — "we will all stand before (παραστησόμεθα) the judgment seat of God" (Rom 14:10); "present yourself approved to God" (2 Tim 2:15);
- **bridal** — "to present you as a pure virgin to Christ" (2 Cor 11:2); "that he might present to himself the church … holy and blameless" (Eph 5:27).
The three adjectives that follow span the settings: ἄμωμος is the sacrificial word, ἀνέγκλητος the judicial one, and "holy and blameless" is the bridal pair of Eph 5:27 `[T]`/`[I]`.

**ἄμωμος ("blameless, without blemish"), 1:22** `[T]`, *high on the count; moderate on the weight*. Eight NT verses (SBLGNT), including Heb 9:14 and 1 Pet 1:19 of Christ as the unblemished sacrifice `[T]`. In Swete, words beginning ἀμωμ- stand in **77 verses**, two-thirds of them cultic — Leviticus 21 verses, Numbers 19, Ezekiel 9, Exodus 2 — of animals "without blemish" for sacrifice; most of the rest are in the Psalms and Proverbs, of a "blameless" life (e.g. Ps 14:2 LXX) `[T]`. So a reader of the Greek Bible would hear the sacrifice behind the word, without it being forced on them `[I]`, moderate. The overview has no row for this; it is idiom, not allusion.

**ἀνέγκλητος ("unaccusable, beyond reproach"), 1:22** `[T]`, *high*. Five NT verses: 1 Cor 1:8 ("blameless in the day of our Lord Jesus Christ") and the qualifications for overseers and deacons (1 Tim 3:10; Titus 1:6, 7) (SBLGNT) `[T]`. In Swete only 3 Macc 5:31 has the stem `[T]`. It is a legal word: no charge can be laid `[S]`, high. In this letter it anticipates 2:14, where the "certificate of debt … which was hostile to us" is cancelled and nailed to the cross, and 2:16, "let no one act as your judge" `[T]` for the verses; `[I]` for the thread — moderate.

**κατενώπιον αὐτοῦ ("before him"), 1:22** `[T]`, *high*. Three NT verses: Eph 1:4, Col 1:22, Jude 24 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. All three join it with ἄμωμος (Headline 3). Whether "him" is God or Christ depends on the subject of the verb (Difficult Verses) `[I]`.

**εἴ γε ἐπιμένετε τῇ πίστει ("if indeed you remain in the faith"), 1:23** `[T]`, *high on the words; moderate on "the faith"*. ἐπιμένω means "to stay, remain, persist" `[S]`, high. The article with πίστις (τῇ πίστει) may mean "the faith" (its content) or "your faith" (your trust); the ESV and NASB95 both say "the faith" `[T]`. The same dative with the article stands at 2:7, "established in the faith (τῇ πίστει), just as you were taught" — and "as you were taught" favours content there `[T]`/`[I]`. *Moderate:* the two are hard to separate; trust in a taught gospel.

**τεθεμελιωμένοι καὶ ἑδραῖοι ("founded and steadfast"), 1:23** `[T]`, *high*. θεμελιόω ("to lay a foundation") stands in five NT verses, among them Matt 7:25 (the house "founded on the rock") and Eph 3:17 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. **Eph 3:17 has "rooted and grounded" (ἐρριζωμένοι καὶ τεθεμελιωμένοι)**; Colossians has "founded" here (1:23) and "rooted" at 2:7 (ἐρριζωμένοι) — the same two participles, the pair divided between the two passages `[T]`. ἑδραῖος ("seated, steadfast") stands in three NT verses, 1 Cor 7:37, 1 Cor 15:58 and here (SBLGNT) `[T]`; **1 Cor 15:58 has "steadfast, immovable" (ἑδραῖοι … ἀμετακίνητοι)**, the adjective and the negated cognate of Colossians' "not moved" `[T]`. That pairing — steadfast and unmoved — is therefore Pauline idiom, used at the close of 1 Corinthians 15 to apply the resurrection `[I]`, high.

**μὴ μετακινούμενοι ("not being moved"), 1:23** `[T]`, *high on the count; moderate on the voice*. The verb's only NT occurrence (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Present participle, parsed as passive or middle (the forms are the same) `[T]`. Read as passive, the danger is being moved by someone; read as middle, shifting oneself `[I]`. The ESV's "not shifting" leans middle; the NASB95's "not moved away" passive (Tool 8). In Swete the verb is used of moving a boundary-stone in the inheritance (Deut 19:14) and of the hills that "shall not be moved" while God's mercy stands (Isa 54:10) `[T]` — background, low.

**κηρυχθέντος ἐν πάσῃ κτίσει τῇ ὑπὸ τὸν οὐρανόν ("proclaimed in all creation under heaven"), 1:23** `[T]`, *high on the words*. κτίσις ("creation") stands in Colossians only at 1:15 ("firstborn of all creation") and 1:23 (SBLGNT) — the 1:15–20 dig's link `[S: dig]`, reached independently `[T]`. The gospel goes as far as the Son's rule over creation `[I]`. For the hyperbole, see Difficult Verses.

**διάκονος ("minister, servant"), 1:23** `[T]`, *high*. In Colossians 1:7 (Epaphras), 1:23 and 1:25 (Paul), 4:7 (Tychicus) (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Paul takes the title he has just given Epaphras `[T]`/`[I]`.

### 8. Translations

1. **1:21–22 — the shape of the sentence.** ESV: "And you, who once were alienated … he has now reconciled" — keeps the Greek's fronted "you" and the delayed verb `[T]`. NASB95: "And **although** you were formerly alienated … **yet** He has now reconciled you" — turns the participle concessive and adds "yet", making a smooth two-part sentence `[T]`. The ESV is closer to the Greek's shape; the NASB95's reading is defensible `[I]`.

2. **1:21 — "evil deeds" / "good work" (1:10).** Both versions, so the ἔργον antithesis is lost (Tool 7) `[T]`.

3. **1:22 — "his body of flesh" (ESV) / "His fleshly body" (NASB95).** The ESV keeps the phrase "body of flesh", and at 2:11 it has "the body of the flesh"; the NASB95 makes it an adjective here and keeps "the body of the flesh" at 2:11 `[T]`. **The ESV lets the 1:22 ↔ 2:11 link be heard; the NASB95 hides it.**

4. **1:22 — "by his death" (ESV) / "through death" (NASB95).** NA28 has no "his"; the ESV supplies it (a reading in some manuscripts, or simply English idiom) `[T]`. No difference in sense.

5. **1:22 — word order "holy … before him".** Both versions keep the three adjectives in the Greek order `[T]`. "Above reproach" (ESV) / "beyond reproach" (NASB95) for ἀνέγκλητος; "unaccusable" would make the legal sense audible `[I]`.

6. **1:23 — τεθεμελιωμένοι.** ESV "stable"; NASB95 "firmly established"; KJV "grounded" `[T]`. **Both modern versions lose the foundation image**, and so the building language that runs into 2:7 ("rooted and built up … established") is weakened `[I]`. The NASB95 also uses "established" for a different word (βεβαιούμενοι) at 2:7 `[T]`.

7. **1:23 — μὴ μετακινούμενοι.** ESV "not shifting"; NASB95 "not moved away"; KJV "be not moved away" `[T]`. The ESV's active-sounding "shifting" makes the danger internal; the NASB95 and KJV leave room for an outside force `[I]`. Given 2:4 and 2:8, the passive reading fits the letter well `[I]`, moderate.

8. **1:23 — ἐγενόμην.** ESV "**became** a minister"; NASB95 "**was made** a minister" `[T]`. The Greek is middle ("became"); the NASB95's passive is an interpretation (God made him so — true, cf. 1:25, but not stated here) `[I]`.

9. **1:23 — "the faith".** Both versions, consistent with 2:7 in the ESV ("established in the faith"); the NASB95 has "your faith" at 2:7 `[T]`. **The ESV keeps the 1:23 ↔ 2:7 link audible; the NASB95 does not.**

**Ancient versions check.** The renderings that carry weight — "holy and blameless and above reproach before him" and "if indeed" — are shared by the modern versions. The **Vulgate** reads *si tamen permanetis in fide fundati et stabiles et immobiles a spe evangelii*, keeping "founded" (*fundati*) and "immovable" *(moderate confidence — from recall; the Clementine text for this passage is not in the corpus)*. No translation-tradition split is claimed. *Uncertain — verify if the Latin is to be cited.*

**Pulpit divergence note.** The pulpit text is the **ESV (Anglicised)**. Where it and the NASB95 part company, **the ESV serves the findings better at three points**: it keeps the fronted "and you" of 1:21; it keeps "body of flesh", so the link to 2:11 can be heard; and it keeps "the faith" at both 1:23 and 2:7. It serves them less well at two: "stable" loses the foundation image, and "not shifting" makes the danger sound self-generated. **What the congregation will hear:** a clear "once … now", a bodily death, and a call to stay in the faith — but not that they have already been *founded*, nor that the threat may come from someone else, unless the preacher says so. **Edition check:** none of these words has a known Anglicised variant, but read the verses from the pew Bible before preaching `[I]`.

### 9. Tone and Feel

**Solemn assurance with a sober edge.** The "once" is stark — estranged, hostile, evil — and stated without softening `[T]`. The "now" is emphatic (νυνί, the strengthened form of "now") `[T]`. The purpose is lofty — presented holy before God `[T]`. Then the tone tightens with "if indeed", and steadies again with "founded and steadfast" `[T]`/`[I]`. The close is personal and dignified: "of which I, Paul, became a minister" `[T]`.

**Soundtrack:** a cello line — low and dark at 1:21, rising at 1:22 to a sustained high note ("holy and blameless … before him"), then a held pedal note under 1:23 (steady, not anxious). The preacher should not let the "if" sound like a threat; the register is steadiness, not alarm `[I]`.

### 12. Genre

Pauline epistle; the application of a confessional unit (1:15–20) to its readers, and the transition to the writer's self-presentation (1:24–2:5) `[T]`/`[I]`. Reading rule: an epistle's "once … now" is not autobiography but argument — it tells the readers where they now stand, to ground what follows `[I]`.

### 13. Copycat

**N/A** — no narrative characters. Paul's self-description as minister is unique to his role; the readers' "continuing" is a direct call, not a pattern to copy from someone else `[T]`/`[I]`.

### 14. Bible Timeline

This side of the cross: the reconciliation is done (aorist) `[T]`. The presentation is ahead (Headline 3) `[I]`. The readers live between, called to remain `[T]`. The modern church stands in the same place; no adjustment is needed `[I]`. The one timeline question is the hyperbole of 1:23, "proclaimed in all creation" — written within a generation of the resurrection (Difficult Verses).

### 15. Who Am I?

- **"You" (the Colossians)** — *recipients*; the modern Gentile church stands with them: once estranged, now reconciled, called to remain `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **God / Christ** — the reconciler; the one before whom they will stand `[T]`.
- **Paul** — the gospel's minister; not a model to imitate here, but a servant to receive (1:24–2:5) `[I]`.

### 16. So What?

**Stage 1 — the response the author seeks:** that the readers know themselves as reconciled enemies, look ahead to being presented holy before God, and therefore stay where they are — in the faith, on the foundation already laid, in the hope of the gospel they heard — however persuasive the voices that would move them `[I]`.

**Stage 2:**
- **Worldview.** I was not neutral before Christ; I was estranged and hostile in mind (1:21). My reconciliation happened in a body, through a death (1:22). My future is to stand before God with no charge against me (1:22). My security is on a foundation I did not lay (1:23).
- **Stop:** treating the Christian life as a search for a better foundation; letting a persuasive teacher move me from the hope of the gospel; reading "if indeed" as a reason for constant anxiety.
- **Start:** remembering what I was; thanking God for the cross as a bodily death; looking towards the day of presentation; staying put — in the faith as I was taught it (2:7).
- **Motive:** the condition hangs on the reconciliation, not the other way round. They are told to remain *because* they have been reconciled and *in order that* God's purpose — their presentation — may be reached `[T]`/`[I]`.

**Four audiences.** *For me:* is there a voice I am letting move me? *For a Christian friend:* the believer afraid of falling away — the call is to remain on a foundation God laid, not to lay it again. *For the church:* "you" is plural; stability is corporate (2:5, "your good discipline", τάξις) `[T]`. *For an unbeliever:* the description of 1:21 — estranged, hostile in mind, doing evil — and the offer of 1:22 — reconciled through a death.

**Prayer.** *Father, we were estranged and hostile in mind; you have reconciled us in the body of your Son's flesh through his death, to present us holy and blameless before you. Keep us in the faith, founded and steadfast, and let no one move us from the hope of the gospel.*

**Grammatical and syntactical features:**

- **The perfect participles bracket the passage.** ἀπηλλοτριωμένους ("having been estranged", 1:21) and τεθεμελιωμένοι ("having been founded", 1:23) are both perfect passives: a settled state then, a settled state now `[T]`. *Moderate–high* that the symmetry is worth preaching; *high* on the forms.
- **εἴ γε with a present indicative (ἐπιμένετε).** A first-class condition: the condition is assumed for the argument's sake, which fits confidence but does not prove it `[S]`, moderate. With γε, "if, as indeed" is possible (Eph 3:2; 4:21) `[T]`.
- **The aorist "reconciled" with the present "remain".** The act is done; the remaining is continuous `[T]`.
- **The anacoluthon (SBLGNT reading).** If ἀποκατηλλάγητε is original, "And you … you were reconciled" leaves the first "you" without a verb; the sentence breaks under the weight of what it says `[I]`. The NA28 reading is grammatically smooth, which is why it is suspected of being a correction `[S]`.

**Sound features:** ἁγίους καὶ ἀμώμους καὶ ἀνεγκλήτους (*hagious kai amōmous kai anenklētous*) — three adjectives in -ους joined by καί, a deliberate cadence `[T]`.

**Recommended verification:** the text of 1:22 (active or passive); the subject of the verb (God or Christ); the voice of μετακινούμενοι; the force of εἴ γε.

### Textual Variants

**1:22 — ἀποκατήλλαξεν ("he reconciled") / ἀποκατηλλάγητε ("you were reconciled")**
- **The issue:** active with God or Christ as subject and "you" as object, or passive with "you" as subject, leaving 1:21's "you" hanging.
- **Manuscript evidence** (NA28 apparatus): ἀποκατηλλάγητε 𝔓⁴⁶ B; ἀποκαταλλαγέντες ("having been reconciled") D* F G and some Latin witnesses; ἀποκατήλλακται ("has been reconciled") 33; ἀπήλλαξεν ("he released") 104; NA28's text ἀποκατήλλαξεν with א A C D² K L P Ψ 048 075 0278 81 1739 1881 and the majority, the Latin and Syriac `[T: as the apparatus prints it]`.
- **Most likely original:** genuinely divided. The passive is the harder reading and has the two oldest witnesses; the variety of other forms looks like attempts to mend it `[I]`. The active has the broadest support and makes good sense `[I]`. The SBLGNT prints the passive, NA28 the active `[T]`. The overview notes the same split `[S]`.
- **Preaching implication:** none for the meaning — either way, God has reconciled them through Christ's death. The ESV and NASB95 both follow NA28 ("he has now reconciled"). Do not build a point on who the grammatical subject is.
- **Confidence:** *uncertain — consider verifying* (Logos: Dunn, Pao on the passive).

**1:22 — αὐτοῦ ("his") after "death"** — added by א A P 81 and others; NA28 omits with 𝔓⁴⁶ B C D F G and the majority `[T]`. The ESV's "his death" agrees in sense. *Minor.*

**1:23 — "minister"** — κῆρυξ καὶ ἀπόστολος ("herald and apostle") in א* P; "herald and apostle and minister" in A; "minister and apostle" in 81 `[T]`. The scribes raised Paul's title; NA28's plain διάκονος is surely original `[I]`, high. *Minor, but telling:* the plain title is the one Paul also gives Epaphras and Tychicus.

**1:23 — τῇ ("the") before "creation"** (majority) and omission of καί before "not moved" (𝔓⁴⁶ᵛⁱᵈ 33) `[T]` — no bearing.

### Historical and Cultural Background

#### Setting
- **Time period, location, political context:** as for 1:1–14 (an imprisonment of Paul; Colossae in the Lycus valley; Roman Asia) `[T]`/`[S]`.

#### What the original audience knew
1. **Reconciliation (καταλλαγή and its verbs)** was the language of ending hostility — between estranged spouses, cities, or parties to a quarrel `[S]`. In Paul it is God who reconciles, and the hostility was on the human side ("hostile in mind") `[T]`/`[I]`.
2. **Sacrifice.** A Gentile hearer knew sacrificial victims had to be without defect; ἄμωμος is the Greek Bible's word for that `[T]` (Tool 7) / `[S]` for pagan practice.
3. **Courts.** ἀνέγκλητος was a legal term; a person against whom no ἔγκλημα (charge) could be brought `[S]`.
4. **Foundations.** The Lycus valley was earthquake country (Laodicea fell in AD 60, Tacitus, *Annals* 14.27) `[S]`; "founded … steadfast … not moved" would have had a concrete sound `[I]`, *uncertain — do not press it*.
5. **Gentile estrangement.** Jewish writers spoke of Gentiles as far from God and his covenants (cf. Eph 2:12 within the NT) `[T]`; a Gentile church told it was "once estranged" is being told the truth about its past, without being told it is second-class now `[I]`.

#### What this changes about how we read
- The three adjectives are not a list of moral attainments; they are a victim's, a bride's and a defendant's standing — granted, not earned `[I]`.

### Original Audience Reception

#### Canonical audience
- **Canonical section:** Pauline corpus; a reader coming from Romans and Ephesians knows Rom 5:10 and Eph 2:12–16 already `[T]`, and will hear 1:21–22 as Paul's familiar doctrine of reconciliation applied to these readers `[I]`.

#### The first hearers
- **Who they were:** Gentile believers, taught by Epaphras, "once estranged" `[T]`.
- **Their situation:** a gospel received and doing well (1:4–8; 2:5); a "philosophy" in the air (2:8) `[T]`.
- **What they brought:** the possibility that someone would tell them their reconciliation needed completing — by knowledge, regimen or angelic mediation (2:8–23) `[I]`.

#### Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances
- **Shocking:** that they had been God's enemies "in mind" — not merely ignorant `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **Surprising:** that the reconciliation was "in the body of his flesh through death" — a bodily, dying mediator, not an angel or a spirit `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **Comforting:** "holy and blameless and unaccusable before him" — no charge left `[T]`.
- **Disturbing:** "if indeed you remain" — the possibility of being moved `[T]`.

#### What we bring that they didn't
A therapeutic reading of "alienated" (as feeling disconnected) rather than a relational and moral one; a tendency to hear "if" as either a threat or a formality; a habit of separating doctrine (1:22) from perseverance (1:23) into two sermons `[I]`.

#### Candidate Fallen Condition Focus

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **What they felt** | The pull to be moved from the gospel they heard to something that seemed fuller (2:4, 8) `[I]`; possibly the fear that their standing before God was incomplete without it `[I]` |
| **Candidate FCF (shared concern)** | Believers who forget what they were and what Christ's death has done, and so are open to being moved from the hope of the gospel — either by fear (am I really accepted?) or by attraction (is there something more?) |
| **Shared / differs** | Shared: the persuasive alternative and the anxious conscience. Differs: the content of the alternatives |
| **Confidence** | Inferred, moderate–high: anchored in "not being moved" and the warnings it introduces |

#### Implication for the sermon
Preach the condition from inside the reconciliation: the "if" is the way the reconciled reach the presentation, and the danger it names is being moved — so the remedy is to stay where they have been put `[I]`.

### Biblical-Theological Themes

#### Reconciliation / peace
- **Where this passage sits:** the personal application of the cosmic reconciliation (1:20) `[T]`.
- **What comes before:** in the canon, God's covenant of peace (Isa 54:10, low) and Israel's estrangement (Ezek 14:5) `[T]` as texts; in Paul, Rom 5:10 and 2 Cor 5:18–20 `[S]`.
- **What comes after:** the peace of Christ ruling in the church (3:15) `[T]`; Eph 2:14–16 `[T]`.
- **Contributes:** the "peace" of 1:20 has persons in it.
- **Confidence:** *high.*

#### Sacrifice / presentation before God
- **Where this passage sits:** the reconciled presented "blameless" — the language of the unblemished offering applied to people `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **What comes before:** the cultic ἄμωμος of Leviticus and Numbers (Tool 7) `[T]`.
- **What comes after:** Christ the unblemished sacrifice (Heb 9:14; 1 Pet 1:19); believers presented as a living sacrifice (Rom 12:1); the bride presented without blemish (Eph 5:27) `[T]`.
- **Confidence:** *moderate* (idiom, not citation).

#### Perseverance / the foundation
- **Where this passage sits:** the call to remain on a foundation already laid `[T]`.
- **What comes before:** the house founded on the rock (Matt 7:25); the Zion foundation stone (Isa 28:16, low) `[T]`.
- **What comes after:** 2:6–7 in the letter; 1 Cor 15:58; Jude 24 (God able to keep and present) `[T]`.
- **Confidence:** *moderate–high.*

**Confluence:** **mutual illumination** — the presentation (theme 2) explains the perseverance (theme 3): one remains because one is being brought to stand before God; and reconciliation (theme 1) is what makes the presentation possible `[I]`.

### Christological Reading

**Type of connection:** NT, Christ the agent and means: "in the body of his flesh through death" `[T]`. Typology in view: the unblemished sacrifice (moderate — category passes; NT precedent passes, Heb 9:14; escalation passes; authorial signal weak).

**How the passage points to Christ:** reconciliation happened in his physical body by his death (1:22); the "hope of the gospel" (1:23) is "Christ in you, the hope of glory" (1:27) `[T]`.

**Moralism check:**
- **The "be like X" temptation:** "be holy, blameless and above reproach" as a moral programme.
- **The gospel grounding:** the three adjectives describe how God presents the reconciled, not what they must achieve to be reconciled `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **Christ as hero:** the one in whose body and through whose death the estranged were reconciled.

**Confidence:** *high* on the Christ-centredness; *moderate* on the sacrificial typology.

### Difficult / Contested Verses

#### 1:23 — "if indeed you continue in the faith"
- **Category:** doctrinal (assurance and perseverance).
- **The difficulty:** does the verse make salvation uncertain?
- **Rhetorical function in context:** it states the path between reconciliation and presentation, and prepares the warnings of chapter 2 — to a church whose faith Paul praises (1:4; 2:5) `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **The main interpretive options:** (a) a genuine condition, fulfilled by all whom God truly reconciles — continuance as the evidence of reality (the Reformed reading) `[S]`; (b) a genuine condition that some reconciled believers may fail to meet (the Arminian reading) `[S]`; (c) a rhetorical "since" with little conditional force — weakest on the grammar, since εἴ γε can doubt (Gal 3:4) `[T]`/`[S]`.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the condition is real; its expression is confident (εἴ γε, as at Eph 3:2; 4:21); its object is remaining, not achieving; the danger is being moved by others; and the God who reconciles also means to present (purpose infinitive) `[T]`/`[I]`. The passage does not itself settle the debate between (a) and (b) `[I]`.
- **Suggested handling:** address directly but pastorally — this is Trap 6. Preach both the reality of the "if" and the confidence of the "indeed".

#### 1:22 — whose verb, and before whom?
- **Category:** doctrinal / grammatical.
- **The difficulty:** is the reconciler God (subject carried from 1:19–20) or Christ? Is "before him" God or Christ?
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** 1:19–20 makes God (or "the fullness" — God in all his fullness) the one who reconciles "through him" (the Son); the natural continuation makes God the subject at 1:22, with "his" flesh the Son's `[I]`, moderate–high. The 1:15–20 dig leaned the same way at 1:19 `[S: dig]`. "Before him" is then before God, as at Eph 1:4 `[I]`, moderate. On the passive reading the question disappears `[I]`.
- **Suggested handling:** sidestep with a note; the act is the Father's through the Son either way.

#### 1:23 — "proclaimed in all creation under heaven"
- **Category:** apologetic.
- **The difficulty:** the gospel had not reached every person by AD 60.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the phrase echoes 1:6 ("in all the world") and 1:15 ("all creation") `[T]`; it claims universality of scope and range, not of completed coverage `[I]`, high. The aorist participle presents the proclamation as an accomplished fact in principle `[I]`, moderate.
- **Suggested handling:** a sentence if needed; the point is that their gospel is the universal one, not a local variant.

#### 1:21 — "alienated and hostile"
- **Category:** pastoral.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the description is of the readers' past, spoken to believers, and immediately followed by "but now" `[T]`. It is not an accusation of the congregation's present state `[I]`.
- **Suggested handling:** say it plainly; let the "but now" land.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: Preaching 1:23 as a threat — or as nothing

- **What it looks like:** "If you don't keep going you'll lose your salvation" — or "Of course, every true believer will continue", followed by a quick move on.
- **Why it's wrong:** the "if" is real, its expression confident (εἴ γε), its object remaining where they have been put, and its danger being moved by others. Both the threat and the dismissal miss the shape (overview Trap 6).
- **The corrective:** preach the condition from inside the reconciliation and towards the presentation: God has reconciled you to present you; stay where he has put you; do not let anyone move you.

### Pitfall: Detaching 1:21–23 from 1:20

- **What it looks like:** a sermon that begins "You were once alienated…" with no mention of the cosmic reconciliation just confessed.
- **Why it's wrong:** "And you" adds the readers to "all things"; the same rare verb is used. Without 1:20 the passage loses its scope; without 1:21–23, 1:20 loses its persons.
- **The corrective:** read 1:19–20 aloud first.

### Pitfall: "Holy, blameless, above reproach" as a moral checklist

- **What it looks like:** three points on how to become holy, blameless and above reproach.
- **Why it's wrong:** they describe the condition in which God presents those he has reconciled; the verb is God's, and the means is Christ's death.
- **The corrective:** preach them as standing (sacrificial, bridal, judicial), then let the call to remain follow.

### Pitfall: Spiritualising the death

- **What it looks like:** "Christ reconciled us to God" with no body and no death.
- **Why it's wrong:** this is the letter's only "death" and it is "in the body of his flesh". In a church being offered a body-denying spirituality (2:23), the bodily death matters.
- **The corrective:** name the body and the death.

### Pitfall: "Alienated" as a feeling

- **What it looks like:** "We all feel disconnected from God sometimes."
- **Why it's wrong:** the words are relational and moral — estranged, hostile in mind, in evil works — and past.
- **The corrective:** describe the past honestly, then the "but now".
