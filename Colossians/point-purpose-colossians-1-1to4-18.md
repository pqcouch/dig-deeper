## Scope Decision

**One sermon on the whole letter, as the introduction to Colossians.** This is a different preaching unit from any of the twelve the overview lists, and it does a different job: not to expound a pericope, but to let the congregation hear the letter's one claim and see its shape, so that each later sermon has somewhere to stand. Colossians suits this unusually well. The overview's microscript and keynote survived the whole sweep, five claim audits and the macro-synthesis unchanged, and the letter argues almost entirely by repeating its own words across its units (macro-argument 12). An introduction can therefore trace the letter's own threads instead of imposing a summary.

**Governing sections read first.** Every dig's latest revision reports "Stale headlines: none", so the Headline Findings below are cited at their current wording. The overview's *What Changed in v1.2* and *v1.2.2* govern three points this plan touches: Onesimus is not called a slave in Colossians (Trap 4); the 2:23 clause stands at two level readings, and the point every reading shares is *high* (moralism check); "welded seams" is discarded as a design, while the named distance echoes hold (macro-argument 5).

**Claim-audit check.** The queue (`claude/claim-audit-queue-colossians-after-audit-5.md`) is not armed: seven candidates queued against the rule of about ten, and no digs since audit 5. Nothing in this plan rests on a queued or unaudited allusion. Its load-bearing links are internal, verified by lemma against the SBLGNT, and already audited. No Old Testament allusion is load-bearing, as the letter quotes no Old Testament text.

**Suggested reading.** The whole letter is too long to read aloud in a service (95 verses). Read **1:15–20 and 2:6–10** — the confession, then the hinge that applies it. The sermon will quote the rest as it goes. If only one passage can be read, read 2:6–10, which carries both halves of the Teaching Point.

## Fallen Condition Focus

**We suspect that the Christ we received is the beginning of the Christian life rather than the whole of it, and so we are open to anyone — a teacher, a technique, a regimen, an experience — who offers us the missing piece.**

*Anchored in the text.* The letter is written to a church in good order: Paul rejoices to see "your good discipline and the stability of your faith" (2:5). Yet it warns four times that someone may "delude" them, "take them captive", "judge" them or "disqualify" them (2:4, 8, 16, 18) — always with an indefinite "no one … you", never with a named opponent. What is on offer is described only by its features: a φιλοσοφία ("philosophy", 2:8), rules about food and days (2:16), "the worship of the angels" and visions (2:18), "Do not handle, do not taste, do not touch!" (2:21). The pressure behind them all is one question: *is Christ enough, or does faith need something added?* (overview, Presenting Situation).

*Convergent across the digs.* Seven of the twelve candidate FCFs are a form of this one concern: second-class Christians looking beyond Christ (1:1–14); the suspicion that some other power must be feared or added (1:15–20); being moved away by fear or attraction (1:21–23); Christ as beginning, not content (1:24–2:5); doubting completeness and buying the missing piece (2:6–15); submitting to or imposing regimens (2:16–23); looking for life in what can be seen or achieved (3:1–4). The later units add its practical face: fighting sin with willpower or rules (3:5–11), and living for human eyes in relationships of power (3:18–4:1).

*Shared and different.* Theirs were angels, calendars and elemental powers; ours are more often techniques, spiritual experiences, programmes of self-improvement and moral performance. The suspicion underneath is the same. **It is a danger to a healthy church, not a diagnosis of a captured one** (2:5; the 1:24–2:5 dig, Headline 5, "prophylactic", *moderate–high*).

## Teaching Point

**All the fullness of God dwells in Christ — the creator, head and reconciler of all things — and in him you have been filled; so walk in him as you received him, adding nothing to him and living everything from him.**

- **Subject:** What does a church that has received Christ still lack?
- **Complement:** Nothing. All the fullness of God dwells in him, and in him they have been filled; so the Christian life is not adding to Christ but walking in him — dying and rising with him, and wearing his life in every word, relationship and task.

*Built from the convergent findings:*

- **The keynote.** The πληρ- family ("fill, fullness") runs through every movement, nine occurrences in nine verses, from the prayer that they be "filled" (1:9) to Archippus's charge to "fulfil" (4:17). πλήρωμα ("fullness") with κατοικέω ("to dwell") stands only at 1:19 and 2:9, and 2:10 then says πεπληρωμένοι ("you have been filled") (overview, Microscript; 1:15–20 dig, Headline 5; 2:6–15 dig, Headline 2). *High.*
- **"In him".** All eight ἐν αὐτῷ ("in him") stand within 1:16–2:15, five of them in 2:6–15 (overview, Arc Map; macro-argument 2). *High.*
- **The arsenal.** The confession of 1:15–20 is redeployed in the polemic: ὅτι ἐν αὐτῷ ("for in him") stands only at 1:16, 1:19 and 2:9 in Paul; the rulers and authorities are created (1:16), headed (2:10) and stripped (2:15) (2:6–15 dig, Headline 2; 1:15–20 dig, Headline 4). *High on the links; moderate–high as a design.*
- **The hinge.** 2:6–7 is the letter's first command and its thesis: "as you received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in him" (2:6–15 dig, Headline 1). *High.*
- **Gift before command.** In every movement the imperatives stand on what God has done (macro-argument 3; 1:1–14 dig, Headline 1; 3:12–17 dig, Headline 1). *Strong.*

The subject of every indicative is God or Christ: the Father who qualified, rescued and transferred (1:12–13); the Son in whom all things were created and reconciled (1:16, 20); the God who made you alive, forgave and cancelled (2:13–14). The hearer's "walk" is the response, never the ground.

## Purpose Statement

**To move hearers from looking for something more than Christ to walking in Christ with thankful confidence — persuaded that in him they lack nothing, and so freed both from the pull of the "more" and from the regimen that promises to supply it, to live his life in their speech, homes, work and church.**

- **For the believer who feels second-class:** you were not given a beginner's version of the gospel. The word you heard is "the word of the truth", bearing fruit "in all the world" (1:5–6), and the Father has already qualified you for the inheritance (1:12). Your life "is hidden with Christ in God" (3:3).
- **For the believer attracted to "more":** hear the warning as a friend's, to a church in good order (2:5, 8). Test every offer by the letter's own measure: "according to Christ", or "according to the tradition of men" (2:8)?
- **For the earnest and disciplined:** rules — however severe — are no remedy for the flesh, on every reading of 2:23 (overview, moralism check, *high*). The commands of chapter 3 are given to people who "have died" and "have been raised" (3:1, 3), and are put on because the new self has already been put on (3:10, 12).
- **For the unbeliever and the visitor:** the Christ of this letter is not one spiritual option among many. He is the one through whom all things were made and for whom they exist, and he made peace "through the blood of his cross" (1:16, 20). "You, who once were alienated … he has now reconciled" (1:21–22).
- **For the church as a body:** "Christ is all, and in all" (3:11), and the letter ends by showing it in named people — a Jewish circle of co-workers, a founder at prayer, and Onesimus, "our faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you" (4:9–12). The ministry Paul exercised is handed to the church (macro-argument 9).

## Candidate Big Question

**Is Christ enough — or are you still looking for something more?**

*Fuller alternative:* "What do you think you still need, that you do not already have in Christ?"

This is a candidate. The one-sentence-sermon step, or the preacher, settles the final wording. Either form opens the series as well as this sermon: each later unit can be heard as one more answer to it.

## Proposed Structure

The letter is linear, hinged at **2:6–7** (overview, Arc Map). Its spine is the verb περιπατέω ("to walk"), which stands only at 1:10, 2:6, 3:7 and 4:5, and the verb παραλαμβάνω ("to receive"), which stands only at 2:6 and 4:17. The four movements follow the overview's five (Greeting and Thanksgiving merged), and trace the letter's own order: gift, warning, new life, persons.

**Introduction — a letter from a stranger in chains.** Briefly: Paul has never seen them (2:1). They heard the gospel from Epaphras, "one of you" (1:7; 4:12). Paul writes as a prisoner (4:3, 18). The church is doing well (2:5), and someone is offering it "more" (2:4, 8). Pose the Big Question. *Keep this short:* the authorship and place debates belong in a footnote to the series, not here (Keep Honest 4).

1. **1:1–2:5 — Everything named first: the Christ you already have.**
   - The gospel they received is the complete, true and world-wide word, not a beginner's version (1:5–7; 1:1–14 dig, Headline 2).
   - Paul prays that they be "filled" with knowledge, wisdom and a worthy walk (1:9–10) — the very goods the rival will claim to add (1:1–14 dig, Headline 3).
   - The Father has already qualified, rescued, transferred; "we have" redemption (1:12–14).
   - The confession: Christ, creator of all powers, head of the church, firstborn from the dead, in whom "all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell", reconciler "through the blood of his cross" (1:15–20).
   - "And you … he has now reconciled in his body of flesh by his death" (1:21–22) — θάνατος ("death"), only here in the letter.
   - The mystery is a person: "Christ in you, the hope of glory" (1:27); "Christ, in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (2:2–3). **Only then** the first warning: "I say this in order that no one may delude you" (2:4).

   *Function:* surface the FCF and answer the Teaching Point's subject. The letter names the goods before the rival can offer them (macro-argument 1). **This movement must be the longest**, because the letter's own order is the argument.

2. **2:6–23 — Walk in him: nothing to add.**
   - The thesis: "as you received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in him, rooted and built up in him" (2:6–7).
   - The test: "according to human tradition … and not according to Christ" (2:8).
   - The fullness applied: "in him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily, and you have been filled in him" (2:9–10).
   - Union, not addition: circumcised, buried, raised, made alive, forgiven, the debt nailed to the cross, the powers stripped and paraded (2:11–15; 2:6–15 dig, Headlines 3–4).
   - Therefore let no one judge or disqualify you: the rules are "a shadow"; "the substance belongs to Christ" (2:16–17); they are human, perishing, and no remedy for the flesh (2:20–23).

   *Function:* the complement of the Teaching Point. The warning is not a new subject but the consequence of Movement 1: what has nothing missing needs nothing added. End the movement on the powers beaten (2:15), not on the rules (2:6–15 dig, *Implication for the sermon*: "recover the triumph").

3. **3:1–4:6 — Raised with him: life lived from union.**
   - The twin conditionals turn warning into new life: "If with Christ you died …" (2:20) → "If then you have been raised with Christ, seek the things that are above" (3:1) (3:1–4 dig, Headline 1).
   - "Your life is hidden with Christ in God … Christ who is your life" (3:3–4) — ζωή ("life") only here in the letter.
   - Put to death, put off, put on — because the old self has already been put off and the new put on (3:5, 9–10, 12); "Christ is all, and in all" (3:11).
   - The life is earthly and ordinary: forgiveness "as the Lord has forgiven you" (3:13); the word of Christ dwelling richly; thanks (3:15–17); the household under one Lord (3:18–4:1); prayer, and gracious speech to outsiders (4:2–6).

   *Function:* the Purpose Statement's main content — the ethics as the fruit of union, not a better regimen. Sweep the household code in a sentence or two, with "the Lord" as its governing word (κύριος, "Lord, master", nine times in six verses); do not try to expound it here.

4. **4:7–18 — "Christ is all" in persons: see that you fulfil what you have received.**
   - The letter-carriers are given the founder's titles (4:7; 1:7).
   - Onesimus, "our faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you" (4:9).
   - Three Jewish co-workers, "my only fellow workers for the kingdom of God" (4:11).
   - Epaphras prays the letter: "struggling on your behalf … that you may stand mature and fully assured in all the will of God" (4:12).
   - The letter goes on to Laodicea (4:16), and Archippus is told, "See that you fulfil the ministry that you have received in the Lord" (4:17).

   *Function:* land the Purpose Statement. The letter's two "receive" verses frame its whole ethic: "as you **received** Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in him" (2:6) and "the ministry that you have **received** in the Lord … fulfil" (4:17); and the fullness keynote ends where it began, in prayer (1:9) and in a charge (4:17) (macro-argument 2; 4:7–18 dig, Headline 5). Close by making Archippus's word the congregation's: what you received in the Lord, fill full — and open the series from there.

**Hinge to watch.** Movement 1 must be at least as long as Movements 2 and 3 together. If the sermon spends its time on the warnings and the commands, it has preached the rival's method: rules and effort to supply what is lacking (macro-argument 3; overview, moralism check).

## Keep Honest

1. **Preach the features and Paul's answer, not a reconstructed "Colossian heresy".** The text gives features, not a system, and the reconstructions are many, from Lightfoot's Essenes to Arnold's folk religion (overview, Presenting Situation; Trap 2). An introduction is the place where this temptation is strongest, because it seems to call for "background". Give one sentence: someone was offering "more", and Paul never names him. Then let the congregation recognise its own "more". And do not portray the church as already captured: Paul rejoices in its order and stability (2:5).

2. **Do not turn the letter's ethics into a new regimen.** A sermon that offers chapter 3 as a list of vices to avoid and virtues to cultivate, without the death and resurrection of 2:20–3:4, preaches the very "philosophy" the letter rejects (overview, moralism check). In an overview the danger is compression: the commands survive the summary and the indicatives do not. Keep "you died … you have been raised … you have put on" in every sentence about the commands.

3. **Get "firstborn" and the powers right in passing.** "Firstborn of all creation" (1:15) is explained by its own next clause, "*for* by him all things were created" (1:16): it is a title of rank, not a place in the list of creatures (1:15–20 dig, Headline 2; Trap 1). And name the powers as the letter does: created by him (1:16), under his headship (2:10), stripped at the cross (2:15) — neither ignored nor feared (2:6–15 dig, Pitfalls).

4. **Leave the letter's landmines for their own Sundays, and do not overstate what an introduction can settle.**
   - The Sabbath question (2:16), baptism (2:11–12) and the household code (3:18–4:1) each have a full dig and a weighting; an introduction that pauses on any of them will be heard as having settled it (Traps 3, 4, 8).
   - Onesimus is not called a slave in Colossians; that comes from Philemon 16. If the two letters are joined, say so (overview v1.2; Trap 4).
   - On authorship: preach the letter's own claim (1:1; 1:23; 4:18). All six of Patrick's owned commentators place it in Paul's lifetime, but they divide on how much of the composition is Paul's own, and on whether it was written from Rome or Ephesus (Presenting Situation). That is footnote material, not pulpit material.
   - Do not import Old Testament allusions the letter does not make (macro-synthesis, *What This Means for the Series*).

## Christ-Centred Check

- **"Be like X" or "trust the Christ whom X points to"?** The payoff is not "be a faithful Epaphras" or "be a disciplined Christian". It is walking in the Christ in whom they have already been filled. The named people of 4:7–18 are offered as evidence of what Christ has made, not as models to copy. **Pass.**
- **Is Christ the subject?** Creator, head, reconciler and the dwelling-place of the fullness (1:15–20; 2:9); the one in whom the believers were circumcised, buried and raised (2:11–12); "your life" (3:4); "all, and in all" (3:11). The walk responds to him. **Pass.**
- **Is the good news grounded in Christ's saving work?** Peace "through the blood of his cross" (1:20); reconciled "in his body of flesh by his death" (1:22); the debt cancelled and nailed to the cross, the powers disarmed (2:14–15). **Pass.**

## Pulpit-Text Note (ESV against NASB95)

Checked against the ESV and NASB95 exports in `_texts/logos-exports/02-New-Testament/`.

**Where the ESV carries the plan better than the study text:**

- **2:10 — πεπληρωμένοι ("having been filled").** ESV "you have been filled in him"; NASB95 "you have been made complete". **This is the Teaching Point's hinge**, and only the pulpit text lets the congregation hear 2:10 answer 1:19 and 2:9. Read 1:19, 2:9 and 2:10 aloud together.
- **The fullness thread.** In the ESV the congregation will hear the English "fill/full" family at all nine places: "filled" (1:9), "fullness" (1:19), "filling up" (1:24), "fully known" (1:25), "full assurance" (2:2), "fullness" (2:9), "filled" (2:10), "fully assured" (4:12), "fulfil" (4:17). The NASB95 loses it only at 2:10. *A note for the overview:* its Pulpit Notes say that neither version lets the thread be heard at 1:24, 1:25, 2:2 or 4:12. In English, at least, both versions do. Offered as a correction; not applied.
- **περιπατέω ("to walk").** ESV "walk" at 1:10, 2:6, 3:7 and 4:5; the NASB95 has "conduct yourselves" at 4:5. The plan's spine is fully audible from the pulpit text.
- **3:5 — νεκρώσατε ("put to death").** ESV "Put to death", an imperative as in the Greek; the NASB95's "consider the members … as dead to" softens it. This bears on Movement 3.

**Where the two agree, and the plan can lean on the ESV:**

- **2:6 and 4:17 — παραλαμβάνω ("to receive").** Both have "received" at both places, so Movement 4's frame is audible. (The ESV also has "received instructions" at 4:10, a different verb, λαμβάνω ("to receive"). It will not confuse the frame if 2:6 and 4:17 are read together.)
- **"Christ is all, and in all"** (3:11); **"your life is hidden with Christ in God"** (3:3); **"Christ in you, the hope of glory"** (1:27); **"all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge"** (2:3).

**Where the ESV hides or decides something the preacher should supply:**

- **1:19.** ESV "in him all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell"; NASB95 "it was the Father's good pleasure for all the fullness to dwell in Him". The Greek names neither. The ESV's reading, with "the fullness" as subject, is the likelier (*moderate–high*), so preach the pulpit text (1:15–20 dig, Headline 5).
- **2:8, 20 — τὰ στοιχεῖα τοῦ κόσμου.** ESV "the elemental spirits of the world"; NASB95 "the elementary principles of the world". This is the largest interpretive gap in the letter (overview, Pulpit Notes). If Movement 2 quotes 2:8, say in a phrase that the Greek can mean either spiritual powers or basic principles, and that Paul's point stands either way: "not according to Christ".
- **2:20 / 3:1 — the twin conditionals.** ESV "If with Christ you died …" and "If then you have been raised with Christ …": the word order differs, so the pair is less audible than in the NASB95 ("If you have died with Christ … if you have been raised up with Christ"). Movement 3 needs the pair: read the two verses one after the other, and stress "with Christ".
- **4:7, 4:9, 4:12 — δοῦλος ("slave") and σύνδουλος ("fellow slave").** ESV "fellow servant", "servant", and "bondservants" at 4:1. Movement 4 does not rest on this, but if Onesimus is mentioned with Philemon beside him, say "slave" once, plainly.

**Caveat.** The ESV in the corpus is the 2025 text, not explicitly the Anglicised edition (it reads "fulfill" at 4:17). The wording above has not been checked against an Anglicised copy, which is not in Patrick's Logos library (on pre-order). Nothing in the plan turns on spelling.
