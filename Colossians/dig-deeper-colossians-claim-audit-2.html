Claim Audit 2: Colossians

Passages audited: Col 1:19; 1:24–2:5; 2:16–23; 3:12–16 Date: 27 October 2026 Version: 1.0 Purpose: The second claim audit on Colossians. It covers the whole queue, thirteen candidates (C16–C28). C16–C22 are the new candidates found by claim audit 1. C23 was queued from the second Logos round (Dunn’s Servant frame). C24–C28 were queued by the 2:16–23 dig. Trigger 1 had fired: thirteen items were queued against a threshold of about ten. Primary texts: SBLGNT with its lemma index (counts and parsing); NA28 and its apparatus (Logos export) for Colossians; Swete’s LXX, searched both by word-beginning and by stem anywhere in the word, with every hit read for sense; Rahlfs (Logos exports) for Exodus, Psalms, Proverbs, Isaiah, Haggai and both Greek texts of Daniel; the WLC with its lemma index for the Hebrew. There is no Rahlfs text of Wisdom, Sirach or the Maccabees in the corpus. Every finding in those books rests on Swete alone, and the report says so where it matters. Method: - Two blind auditors, run one after the other (Patrick’s two-auditor rule of 16 October 2026). - Auditor A took the seven single-contact claims: wording, rarity, rivals, Greek and Hebrew. - Auditor B took the six field and design claims: block tests, chance baselines, rank stability and leave-one-out. - Each auditor read a brief, a claims file and the corpus only. Neither saw the digs, the overview, the first audit, the Logos answers or the other auditor’s report. - The main session then reproduced the results each verdict rests on (see Spot checks).

History of interpretation: the Hays row for history of interpretation carries the auditors’ recalled scholarship only, tagged [S]. It is marked pending Logos round 3 where the round-3 sheet already asks the question: - C24: question 3.1 - C25: question 3.3 - C26: question 3.4 - C27: question 3.8 - C28: question 5.1

Elsewhere it is marked not yet put to Logos. No verdict below rests on that row.

Positional Frame (Phase 0.6)

What the letter has set up. The confession (1:15–20) said who the Son is. The apostle’s labour (1:24–2:5) said how that confession reached the Colossians: it is a mystery once hidden, now disclosed, with every treasure of wisdom and knowledge hidden in Christ (2:3). The charge (2:6–15) turned it into a command and a ground. The polemic (2:16–23) then measures the rival regulations against that ground [T]. The claims in this queue all bear on three places in that movement: - the treasury of 2:3 (C16, C17, C20, C21); - the dwelling of 1:19 (C18, C22); - the polemic of 2:16–23 and its answer in 3:12–16 (C19, C24–C28).

C23 bears on Paul’s labour at 1:24–29.

Why the frame matters for the verdicts. Colossians quotes nothing with a formula. The one secure Old Testament wording in it is the Isaiah 29:13 phrase at 2:22 [S: audit 1]. The rest of the letter’s argument runs on its own internal repetitions [T]. Audit 1 set the test that follows from this, and this audit keeps it: an external source must add something the letter’s own chains do not already supply. Two verdicts below turn on it: - C24: the 2:8 frame explains the turn to “wisdom” at 2:23 without Isaiah 29:14. - C25: 3:16’s closest echo is 1:28, not 2:23.

Claim C16: Wisdom 6:22–7:17 as a field partner of Col 1:26–2:3 (and 1:29)

Source: claim audit 1, New Candidates (found by both of its auditors independently); the 1:24–2:5 dig, Tool 11 rivals (v1.2) [S: audit 1].

Claim: Wis 6:22 is the only Swete verse joining μυστήρια (“mysteries”), a hiding word and σοφία (“wisdom”). Wis 7:13–14 says “I do not hide her wealth … she is an unfailing treasure”. ἐνέργεια (“working”) occurs at Wis 7:17 and 7:26. The scan ranks Wisdom 6–7 at a median of about 5 against 1:24–29. The claim is that Wisdom 6–7 is a co-candidate field with Proverbs 2, rated moderate.

Findings (Auditor B): - The verse counts hold exactly (Swete). - Wis 6:22 is the only verse joining μυστήρια, a hiding word and σοφία. The positive control finds the hiding root with μυστήριον at Wis 14:23, Tob 12:7 and 12:11, and Dan 2:47. - Wis 7:13 is the only verse joining πλοῦτος (“wealth”) with a hiding word. - ἐνέργεια occurs at Wis 7:17 and 7:26 [T]. - The hiding is negated in both Wisdom verses. Wis 6:22 reads οὐκ ἀποκρύψω ὑμῖν μυστήρια (“I will not hide mysteries from you”), and Wis 7:13 reads τὸν πλοῦτον αὐτῆς οὐκ ἀποκρύπτομαι (“I do not hide her wealth”). In both, the author is promising to be open. Colossians says the opposite: the mystery “had been hidden from the ages … but now has been revealed” (1:26) [T]. The words are shared; the hidden-then-revealed shape is not. - The ranks belong to Wisdom 7, not Wisdom 6. - Against 1:24–29, Wisdom 7 ranks 2, 5, 3, 6, 4 and 10 across the six runs (median 5). This was reproduced by the main session. - Against the claim’s own span, 1:26–2:3, it ranks first in five runs and third in the sixth. - Wisdom 6 is weak as a chapter: median 31 against 1:24–29, and 24 against 1:26–2:3. - Leave-one-out. - Wisdom 7 does not move without μυστήριον and σοφία, even with both removed together. - Without the hiding word, it falls from 1 to 14 against 1:26–2:3, and from 5 to 29 against 1:24–29. - Wisdom 6 falls from 24 to 62 without μυστήριον.

So the field result for Wisdom 7 rests on a hiding word that Wisdom negates. - Null. - “A Wisdom chapter in the top ten” occurs for 43 per cent of random six-verse Pauline passages (52 of 120). It proves nothing by itself. - Wisdom 7 in particular is rare: median 5 or better in 5 of 120 random six-verse passages, and median 1 in 1 of 120 random nine-verse passages. - Rivals. Two chapters beat the null as clearly as Wisdom 7 against 1:26–2:3: - Theodotion’s Daniel 2: median 2. It carries the revelatory shape: “He reveals deep and hidden things” (2:22), and “revealing mysteries … he made known” (2:28). - Proverbs 2: median 7. No random passage of 120 reached it. It carries the “treasures of wisdom and knowledge” vocabulary. - Sirach 24 is a setting artefact (high on whole chapters, 90–125 in every window run).

Claim C17: Isaiah 33:6 at Col 2:3

Source: claim audit 1, New Candidates (Auditor A).

Claim: אוֹצָר (“treasure”) with חָכְמָה (“wisdom”) and דַּעַת (“knowledge”) stands in one Hebrew verse only, Isa 33:6. The Greek keeps treasure and wisdom. Rated low–moderate.

Findings (Auditor A): - The count holds (WLC lemma index; reproduced by the main session). - The sense is looser than the triad suggests. The verse reads “a wealth of salvation, wisdom and knowledge; the fear of YHWH is his treasure” [T]. The treasure is the fear of YHWH, not wisdom and knowledge. - The Greek (Swete; Rahlfs agrees in substance) reads ἐν θησαυροῖς ἡ σωτηρία ἡμῶν … σοφία καὶ ἐπιστήμη … θησαυροὶ δικαιοσύνης (“in treasures is our salvation … wisdom and understanding … treasures of righteousness”). The treasures belong to salvation and righteousness, and the knowledge-word is ἐπιστήμη, not γνῶσις [T]. One small difference: Swete reads ἥκει (“comes”) where Rahlfs reads ἐκεῖ (“there”). - Rival. Proverbs 2 is closer in both languages: - The Greek of Prov 2:1–6 holds κρύψῃς (“you treasure up”), σοφία, σύνεσις (“understanding”), θησαυρούς (“treasures”), ἐπίγνωσιν θεοῦ (“knowledge of God”) and γνῶσις (“knowledge”). - The WLC window Prov 2:4–6 is the only place where מַטְמוֹן (“hidden treasure”), חָכְמָה and דַּעַת fall within two verses (reproduced). - In the scan against 2:2–3, Proverbs 2 has a median of 7 and Isaiah 33 a median of 19.

Claim C18: The Zion-dwelling companions to Psalm 68 at Col 1:19

Source: claim audit 1, New Candidates (Auditor A); C1’s reasoning in audit 1.

Claim: The temple function of 1:19 belongs to a Zion-dwelling tradition: Hag 1:8, 3 Macc 2:15–16 and Ps 131:13–14 (Hebrew 132:13–14). The claim is moderate as a tradition and is to be folded into the Ps 68 claim (C1).

Findings (Auditor A): - The accepted contact holds. εὐδοκέω (“be pleased”) with κατοικέω (“dwell”) in one verse: Ps 67:17 only (Swete; Rahlfs agrees). The Hebrew of Ps 68:17 uses חָמַד (“desire”). It is the only WLC verse joining a desire verb to both ישב and שכן (“dwell”) [T]. - The named companions are thematic, not verbal. - Hag 1:8 has a future tense and no dwelling verb (εὐδοκήσω ἐν αὐτῷ, “I will be pleased with it”). - Ps 131:13–14 uses ἐκλέγομαι (“choose”) and αἱρετίζω (“prefer”), not εὐδοκέω. - A correction to 3 Macc 2:15–16 (Swete only). The κατοικητήριον (“dwelling place”) of 2:15 is heaven, “unreachable by men”. It is set against the earthly sanctuary, not used as a word for it. 2:16 has εὐδόκησας (“you were pleased”) with an accusative and no infinitive [T]. The theme is close to Col 1:19–20: heaven’s God choosing to place his glory on earth. - A member the claim missed: 2 Macc 14:35 (Swete only). It reads ηὐδόκησας ναὸν τῆς σῆς σκηνώσεως ἐν ἡμῖν γενέσθαι (“you were pleased that a temple of your dwelling should be among us”): aorist εὐδοκέω, an infinitive, a dwelling noun and ἐν (“among”) [T]. After Ps 67:17 it is the nearest construction to Col 1:19 in Swete. This was reproduced by the main session. - Recurrence. Eph 4:8 quotes Ps 67:19, two verses after 67:17. Eph 4:10 glosses it with “that he might fill all things”, and Eph 1:23 has τὸ πλήρωμα (“the fullness”) (SBLGNT) [T]. A closely related letter therefore reads Psalm 68 together with fullness language. Its authorship is debated [S]. - The Hebrew tradition. It is the Deuteronomic chosen-place formula: בחר (“choose”) with שכן (“make dwell”), “the place the LORD will choose to make His name dwell” (Deut 12:11; 14:23; 16:2; 16:6; 16:11; 26:2; Neh 1:9, WLC). The LXX renders it “for His name to be called there”, so the Greek reader loses the indwelling [T].

Claim C19: The adjudication frame 2:16, 18 → 3:15, 16

Source: claim audit 1, New Candidates (folds into C4); the 2:16–23 dig, Headline 3.

Claim: “Let no one judge you” (2:16), “let no one disqualify you” (2:18), “let the peace of Christ rule” (3:15) and “let the word of Christ dwell” (3:16) are the only four third-person imperatives in Colossians. Two are negative with human adjudicators, and two are positive with subjects “of Christ”. καταβραβεύω and βραβεύω each occur once in the NT. The claim is that this is a designed frame, rated moderate–high.

Findings (Auditor B): - The count holds exactly. Colossians has thirty imperatives. The four third-person forms are κρινέτω (2:16), καταβραβευέτω (2:18), βραβευέτω (3:15) and ἐνοικείτω (3:16) (SBLGNT parse; reproduced) [T]. - καταβραβεύω and βραβεύω each occur once in the NT. The βραβ- root occurs twice in Swete, both times in augmented forms that only a stem-anywhere search finds: ἐβράβευσεν (Wis 10:12) and συνεβράβευσαν (1 Esd 9:14) [T]. - ἐνοικέω (“dwell in”) is not an adjudication verb. Its NT uses (Rom 8:11; 2 Cor 6:16; Col 3:16; 2 Tim 1:5, 14) are all about indwelling. Inside Colossians it goes with κατοικέω, used of the fullness at 1:19 and 2:9 [T]. The adjudication frame proper is three verbs, not four. - The negative side belongs to a longer series. The “no one … you” warning occurs four times in quick succession, in three moods [T]: - 2:4, “so that no one deludes you” (subjunctive); - 2:8, “see that no one takes you captive” (future indicative); - 2:16, “let no one judge you” (imperative); - 2:18, “let no one disqualify you” (imperative).

Elsewhere in the NT the formula occurs only at Matt 24:4, Mark 13:5, Eph 5:6 and 2 Thess 2:3 [T]. Colossians holds four of about eight. Only the last two happen to be imperatives. - Baseline. Four third-person imperatives is unremarkable for a letter of 95 verses (Galatians has 5 in 149 verses, and Ephesians 8 in 155). Six other Pauline letters mix negative and positive third-person imperatives. The nearest analogue is Rom 14:3–5: - it concerns the same food-and-days dispute; - it opens with a negative pair, “let him not despise … let him not judge” (μὴ κρινέτω, the same verb); - a positive follows, “let each be fully convinced” (14:5) [T].

Rom 6:12, “let not sin reign”, is the negative counterpart of an abstract power ruling in the person (3:15) [T]. - What is distinctive is lexical. The compound καταβραβεύω (“rule against”) at 2:18 is answered by the simple βραβεύω (“rule”) at 3:15. This compound-against-simple play is in character for Paul: compare διακρίνω, κρίνω and κατακρίνω (“discern”, “judge”, “condemn”) at 1 Cor 11:29–32 [T].

Claim C23: Isaiah 49 (the Servant) as the frame of Paul’s suffering in 1:24–29

Source: Patrick’s second Logos round: Dunn, NIGTC, 114–117 and n. 13 [S: Logos 2]. The 1:24–2:5 dig, Biblical Theology (v1.3).

Claim: Paul understood his mission in the light of the Servant of Isaiah 49: - Rom 15:21 quotes Isa 52:15, and 2 Cor 6:2 quotes Isa 49:8, both word for word. - Gal 1:15 shares ἐκ κοιλίας μητρός μου (“from my mother’s womb”) with Isa 49:1. - Inside Col 1:24–29, only κοπιάω (“labour”) is shared (1:29; Isa 49:4).

Rated low–moderate, as background only.

Findings (Auditor B): - The three Pauline uses are verbatim (SBLGNT against Swete).
