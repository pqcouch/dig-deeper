## Scope Decision

**One sermon on 1:15–20.** The confession is six verses of two matched strophes around a bridge (dig, Tool 3), and it cannot be divided without breaking the parallel on which its argument rests: the cross stands in the second strophe where the act of creation stood in the first (Headline 1). It is weighted ⭐ HIGH, so it earns a sermon of its own rather than sharing one with 1:21–23.

**Two neighbours, named.**

- **Before:** the passage is grammatically the end of the thanksgiving. "Who is the image" (ὅς, 1:15) describes "his beloved Son" (1:13) (dig, Tool 4). **Read from 1:13**, so that the congregation hears whose kingdom this is.
- **After:** 1:21–23 turns the cosmic reconciliation onto the readers — ἀποκαταλλάσσω ("to reconcile") stands at 1:20 and 1:22 within two verses. That is next week's sermon. Close this one by pointing to "And you" (1:21), not by preaching it.

**Governing sections read first.** The dig has no tidy pass, but its revisions record that the Headlines were reworded in place: Headline 3 after claim audit 1 (Psalm 89 as a verse contact, not a field design), Headline 5 after Logos round 4 (the subject of "was pleased" leans to "the fullness", *moderate–high*). Claim audit 5 (v1.8) changed no Headline; it rates Ps 67:17 LXX at 1:19 *moderate–high* and the temple overtone of the dwelling *moderate*.

**Claim-audit check.** The queue is not armed (seven candidates queued; no digs since audit 5). None of the queued candidates touches 1:15–20. v1.3 recorded that Trigger 2 for point-purpose on this passage was satisfied: every claim a Headline rests on has been audited.

## Fallen Condition Focus

**We suspect that Christ is not enough — that some other power must be feared, courted or added to make our standing with God secure.**

*Anchored in the text.* The dig's candidate FCF is *anchored*: the passage answers in advance a temptation the letter names later — something "more" to complete what they had in Christ (2:4, 8, 16–23). The confession names every kind of power — "thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities" (1:16) — and puts each one inside the Son's creation. The readers had just been delivered "from the domain [ἐξουσία, "authority"] of darkness" (1:13), and they would soon hear of "worship of angels" (2:18). Gentile converts were used to an unseen world crowded with powers that had to be honoured (dig, Original Audience).

*Shared and different.* Their powers were named angels and elemental spirits. Ours are more often wellness regimes, techniques, ideologies, markets, rulers, the occult, and a general fear of chaos (dig, Candidate FCF). The two temptations are the same in both centuries: to fear the powers, or to court them for a fuller life.

## Teaching Point

**The Son into whose kingdom we have been brought is first over all things — the Creator in, through and for whom every power was made and holds together, and the firstborn from the dead in whom all the fullness of God dwells and through whose cross God has made peace; so there is nothing outside him to fear, nothing over him to court, and nothing to add to him.**

- **Subject:** Who is the Son into whose kingdom the Father has transferred us (1:13)?
- **Complement:** He is first over everything twice over — as the one through whom all things were made, and as the one through whose cross all things are reconciled — and all God's fullness dwells in him.

*Built from the convergent Headline Findings:*

- **Headline 1 — two matched strophes.** Both open ὅς ἐστιν … πρωτότοκος ("he is … firstborn", 1:15; 1:18b), both are grounded with ὅτι ἐν αὐτῷ ("for in him", 1:16; 1:19), both run "in him … through him … for/to him", and "heaven … earth" (1:16) returns reversed as "earth … heaven" (1:20). The one who made all things is the one who reconciles all things. *High.*
- **Headline 2 — "firstborn" explained by its own "for".** The ὅτι ("for", 1:16) gives the reason for the title and puts the Son on the Creator's side of the line. He is "before all things", and all things hold together in him (1:17). *High on the grammar; moderate–high on the royal background.*
- **Headline 4 — every power named and placed inside his creation.** ἐξουσία ("authority") runs from the domain of darkness (1:13) to the powers created (1:16), headed (2:10) and disarmed (2:15). *High.*
- **Headline 5 — the fullness.** πλήρωμα ("fullness") with κατοικέω ("to dwell") stands only at 1:19 and 2:9, and 2:10 says "you have been filled in him". *High on the chain.*
- **Headline 3 — Psalm 89.** The firstborn made "the highest of the kings of the earth" (Ps 89:27; LXX 88:28) is the closest Old Testament partner for "firstborn", and Rev 1:5 reads that verse of the risen Christ. *Moderate–high for the verse contact; moderate for the psalm as a whole* `[S: audit 1]`.

Every finite verb in the passage is indicative; there is not one command (dig, Tool 3). Christ is the subject throughout, and the imperative this confession grounds comes later, at 2:6–8.

## Purpose Statement

**To move hearers to worship Christ as first over everything — and therefore to stop fearing the powers, stop courting them, and stop looking for anything to add to him — resting in the peace God has made through the blood of his cross.**

- **For the believer who fears unseen evil** — the occult, a curse, "something in the house", fate: thrones and authorities are his creatures, made through him, held together by him, and stripped at the cross (1:16; 2:15) (dig, So What).
- **For the believer looking for "more"** — a technique, an experience, a security: "all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell" in him (1:19). There is no other fullness to find.
- **For the member who has met the doorstep reading of "firstborn":** the next word, "for", answers it (1:16), and the passage gives the reason in its own grammar (Keep Honest 1).
- **For the church:** the body has one head (1:18). A church that looks for a second source of spiritual authority has already loosened its hold on the head (2:19).
- **For the unbeliever:** the claim is total — it concerns all things, not a private religious preference — and the one who makes it was crucified. That is where the peace was made (1:20).

The response sought is worship that settles into a refusal of every "something more" (dig, So What). The motive is not "try harder", but "you are already in the one who made and holds all things" (2:10).

## Candidate Big Question

**Is there anything in your world bigger than Jesus?**

*Fuller alternative:* "What do you fear, or reach for, that he did not make — and has not already overcome at the cross?"

This is a candidate. The one-sentence-sermon step, or the preacher, settles the final wording.

## Proposed Structure

The passage is two strophes and a bridge (dig, Tool 3). The three movements follow them exactly, and the sermon closes by pointing to 1:21.

1. **1:15–16 — Over everything he made.**
   - "He is the image of the invisible God" (1:15): the God no one sees is seen in the Son (εἰκών, "image"; compare the new self renewed "after the image of its creator", 3:10).
   - "The firstborn of all creation" — and the reason follows: "**for** by him all things were created" (1:16). The firstborn of the firstborn laws (Gen 49:3; Deut 21:17) and of Ps 89:27 holds rank and inheritance; he is not first in the list of creatures but the one for whom the list exists (Headline 2).
   - The creation list names the powers one by one: "in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities — all things were created through him and for him" (1:16).

   *Function:* surface the FCF and answer it at once. Name the powers your hearers fear and place each one inside 1:16 (dig, Pitfall 5).

2. **1:17–18a — He holds it all, and he heads the church.**
   - "And he is before all things, and in him all things hold together" (1:17): the Son did not make the world and leave it. It holds together in him now.
   - "And he is the head of the body, the church" (1:18a). The bridge faces both ways: back to creation, forward to the new creation. The ESV lets the twin "And he is … And he is" (καὶ αὐτός ἐστιν) be heard.

   *Function:* the present tense of the Teaching Point. The one who holds the universe together is the head of this congregation. Keep it short; it is a bridge, and the passage hurries on.

3. **1:18b–20 — First from the dead; peace by the blood of his cross.**
   - "He is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead" (1:18b): the second "firstborn". πρωτότοκος ("firstborn") with νεκρός ("dead") stands in two NT verses only, here and Rev 1:5. "That in everything he might be preeminent" — γένηται ("might come to be") marks a first place entered by resurrection (dig, Tool 4, *moderate–high*).
   - "For in him all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell" (1:19). The word for "dwell" (κατοικέω) is used of God's dwelling — Ps 67:17 LXX is the only verse in Swete where "be pleased" and "dwell" have God as subject together (*moderate–high* `[S: audit 5]`). God has not lent the Son some of his fullness; he dwells in him wholly.
   - "And through him to reconcile to himself all things, whether on earth or in heaven, making peace by the blood of his cross" (1:20). The register breaks here: after four verses of cosmic grandeur, a Roman gallows (dig, Tone and Feel).

   *Function:* the complement of the Teaching Point and its climax. **Land the sermon on 1:20 and let it land hard** (dig, Pitfall 2). Then one sentence: "And you, who once were alienated …" (1:21) — the reconciliation of all things comes to us next week.

**Hinge to watch.** The temptation in this passage is not moralism — there are no imperatives — but abstraction: an exalted lecture on the cosmos with no "so what" (dig, moralism check). Movement 1 must name real fears and real "more"s, and Movement 3 must reach the blood. If either is missing, the sermon is high Christology without its pastoral purpose.

## Keep Honest

1. **Preach both firstborns — and do not let the debate swallow the sermon.**
   - "Firstborn of all creation" is not "first created": the ὅτι ("for") clause gives the reason, and he is "before all things" (1:16–17). Expect someone to have met the Arian or Watchtower reading on a doorstep, and address it directly (dig, Difficult Verses, *high*).
   - But do not spend the sermon refuting it. The passage's own partner is "firstborn from the dead" (1:18b): heir of the old creation and first of the new (dig, Pitfall 1).
   - The pulpit text says "firstborn **of** all creation", like the Greek. (The NIV84's "over" decides the question for the reader; if anyone quotes it, it is a fair decision but a decision.)

2. **Do not preach the cosmic Christ without the cross, or the confession without its purpose.**
   - The passage ends on "the blood of his cross" (1:20); a sermon on Christ's majesty that never reaches it has lost the second strophe (dig, Pitfall 2).
   - The confession is the description of the Son of 1:13, and the letter cashes it at 2:8–23. Name the "something more" your congregation is tempted by and let 1:16–19 answer it (dig, Pitfall 3).
   - Leave the "pre-Pauline hymn" theory in the study. It is a reconstruction (`[S]`), Patrick's own commentators divide on it (Wright and Pao: Paul's own; Witherington open; Dunn possible but unprovable), and it does nothing for the congregation (overview, Arc Map).

3. **"All things reconciled" is neither universalism nor an embarrassment.** Do not soften "all things", and do not read 1:20 apart from 1:23 ("if indeed you continue in the faith") and 2:15 (the powers disarmed and paraded, not converted). The verse is about the reach of the cross over the whole created order, not a promise that every individual will be saved (dig, Difficult Verses, *moderate–high*). "Making peace" can include peace imposed on enemies.

4. **Hold the Old Testament background at its audited weight.**
   - Psalm 89 is a verse contact at 89:27 (LXX 88:28), *moderate–high*, with Rev 1:5 as the NT's own reading of it. The psalm as a field partner is only *moderate*: its high scan rank rests on shared words, not on the firstborn verse `[S: audit 1]`. Use the verse; do not build a movement on the psalm.
   - The temple overtone of "dwell" (1:19) is *moderate* `[S: audit 5]`: say that "dwell" is the word for God's dwelling, and leave it there.
   - The Wisdom inversion (Wisdom "was created"; all things were created in the Son) is *moderate*, and Philo supplies concept, not wording (v1.7). Neither belongs in the pulpit.

## Christ-Centred Check

- **"Be like X" or "trust the Christ whom X points to"?** There is no human figure to imitate; the readers appear only as the body of which he is head (1:18). The response is worship and trust in him. **Pass.**
- **Is Christ the subject?** Absolutely: image, firstborn, Creator-agent, sustainer, head, beginning, firstborn from the dead, dwelling-place of the fullness, reconciler. **Pass.**
- **Is the good news grounded in Christ's saving work?** The passage ends with peace made "by the blood of his cross" (1:20), and 1:21–22 at once applies the reconciliation to the readers "by his death". The cosmic Christ is the crucified Christ. **Pass.**

## Pulpit-Text Note (ESV against NASB95)

Checked against the dig's Tool 8 and the ESV and NASB95 exports in `_texts/logos-exports/02-New-Testament/`.

**Where the ESV carries the plan better than the study text:**

- **1:19 — who "was pleased"?** ESV "in him all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell" ("the fullness" as subject); NASB95 "it was the Father's good pleasure for all the fullness to dwell in Him". The Greek names neither. The lean is to the ESV's reading, *moderate–high*: Wright, Pao and Dunn take "the fullness" as a way of naming God himself (Headline 5). **This is the most consequential divergence in the passage, and the congregation will hear the likelier reading.** The ESV's "of God" is supplied, so say "all the fullness — God in all his fullness".
- **1:17–18a — "And he is … And he is".** The ESV keeps the bridge's twin opening (καὶ αὐτός ἐστιν); the NASB95 drops the first "and" and turns the second into "He is also". Movement 2 can lean on the ESV.

**Where the ESV hides or decides something the preacher should supply:**

- **1:16 — "by him".** Both versions render ἐν αὐτῷ as "by him" here and "in him" at 1:17 and 1:19. The letter's "in him" chain — eight ἐν αὐτῷ, all within 1:16–2:15 — is broken at its first link. Say once that 1:16 is "in him" in the Greek, so that the three prepositions — in, through, for — can be heard.
- **1:16 — "were created … were created".** The ESV levels the perfect ἔκτισται ("have been created", "stand created") to the same tense as the aorist. The NASB95 keeps it ("have been created"). Minor; mention only if expounding the line.
- **1:18 — "that in everything he might be preeminent".** The ESV loses γένηται ("might come to be") and the play between πρωτεύων ("to be first") and πρωτότοκος ("firstborn"). Movement 3 needs the point that the resurrection is how he *came to be* first in the new creation: say it, since the ESV does not.
- **1:20 — the order of the last line.** The ESV moves "whether on earth or in heaven" before "making peace by the blood of his cross", so the congregation will not hear the passage end on "heaven", the reversal that closes the frame of 1:16. That actually helps Movement 3, because the ESV ends on the cross. Keep the ESV's order, and mention the frame only if the structure is being shown.
- **1:20 — "to himself".** Every English version reads εἰς αὐτόν as God ("to himself"). The dig leans slightly to the Son, *moderate* — the same construction as "for him" at 1:16 — with the commentators divided. Nothing in the plan rests on it; do not correct the pulpit text from the pulpit.
- **1:20 — "through him, I say".** The ESV omits NA28's bracketed second δι᾽ αὐτοῦ ("through him"), which is probably original (*moderate*). No point turns on it.

**Caveat.** The ESV in the corpus is the US edition, not explicitly the Anglicised. The wording of 1:13–20 has not been checked against an Anglicised copy, which is not in Patrick's Logos library (on pre-order). Read the passage from the pew Bible before preaching.
