## Summary

- **The three cruxes of 2:16–23 now have their commentators.** At **2:23**, Dunn, Pao and Wright all take τιμή as "value". None of them illustrates πρός = "against": Pao asserts it, Dunn has "in regard to" and Wright "in relation to". Dunn's structure (ἀφειδίᾳ σώματος in apposition, with no "and", set against πλησμονὴν τῆς σαρκός) reaches the dig's Headline 2 (body against flesh) independently. At **2:18, the angels**, Dunn inclines to the *subjective* genitive (Francis), and the Assistant's survey misfiles him as objective. On **ἐμβατεύω**, Dunn and Pao both reject the initiation sense; Claros is mid-second century. The initiation sense drops to *low*.
- **Verified outside the Assistant:** the Clementine Vulgate at 2:18 and 2:23 matches what the dig recalled. The KJV of 2:16–23 matches every KJV cell in the dig's Tool 8. Both texts are now filed in `_texts/logos-exports/`.
- **Corrections to the Assistant's answers:** Brown's survey files Wright as a "cross" reading at 1:24 again, and Dunn as objective at 2:18. The "Brown" of answer 1.7 is David Brown of Jamieson–Fausset–Brown (1871), not Derek Brown's *Logos Research Commentary*. The Assistant's grammar for the neuter ὅ at 2:10 is back to front. The Sabbath answer rests almost entirely on Seventh-day Adventist authors, and it labels the Adventist position too simply.
- **New from the corpus:** in Swete, σάββατα σαββάτων ("sabbath of sabbaths") is used only of the Day of Atonement (Lev 16:31; 23:32), and both verses also have "humble your souls". The Hebrew uses the same compound, שַׁבַּת שַׁבָּתוֹן, of the weekly Sabbath (Exod 31:15; 35:2; Lev 23:3, WLC), where Swete has σάββατα ἀνάπαυσις. Bacchiocchi's argument, that the simple σάββατα cannot mean ceremonial sabbaths, therefore holds for the Greek and not for the Hebrew.

## 1. Named commentators

### 1.1 Colossians 2:23, the last clause

- **Dunn** (NIGTC, 195–97) — *τιμή:* "value, worth" (LSJ; perhaps a Latinism); *πρός:* "in regard to"; cites Lightfoot for "to check, prevent"; *πλησμονή:* "satiety", hence "gratification"; LXX mostly neutral, negative at Ezek 16:49; Hos 13:6; *Structure / reading:* Rejects reading in an adversative before οὐκ. Reports the parenthesis reading ("which things are for gratification of the flesh": Reicke; Hollenbach; O'Brien; Pokorný; Fowl). Prefers **ἀφειδίᾳ σώματος in apposition** (no "and"), with σῶμα against σάρξ carrying the sense: "severe treatment of the body, not of value to anyone in regard to the gratification of the flesh"
- **Pao** (ZECNT, 196–98) — *τιμή:* "value" (Matt 27:9; Acts 4:34; 1 Cor 6:20; 7:23); *πρός:* "against", **asserted, not illustrated** (his n. 7); *πλησμονή:* "gratification"; *Structure / reading:* Concessive clause; οὐκ answers μέν (Moule; BDF §447(4)); NAB. Rejects the periphrastic reading (Harris) and Hollenbach's "result in indulgence" (NET)
- **Wright** (TNTC, 131–33) — *τιμή:* "value"; "honour (to the body)" rejected; *πρός:* "in relation to" (n. 13: there is "no word for 'restrain'"); *πλησμονή:* "gratifying"; *Structure / reading:* NIV / RSV: "lack any value in restraining sensual indulgence"; the parenthesis reading strains the sentence "almost unbearably"
- **Brown (LRC)** — *Structure / reading:* **Not asked or not returned**

**Corpus checks:**

- **Dunn's LXX list** (Swete): the phrase εἰς πλησμονήν is verified at Exod 16:3, 8; Lev 25:19; Hos 13:6. **Ezek 16:49 has ἐν πλησμονῇ**, not εἰς, and it is the one clearly negative use ("pride, fullness of bread"). Further neutral uses: Lev 26:5; Ezek 39:19. Prov 3:10 and Lam 5:6 were not checked in this run.
- **The "and" before ἀφειδίᾳ** (NA28 apparatus): [καί] is bracketed. It is omitted by 𝔓⁴⁶ B 1739 b m vgᵐˢˢ Hil Ambst Spec, and read by א A C D F G H K L P Ψ 075 0278 33 81 104 365 630 1175 1241ˢ 1505 1881 2464 𝔪 lat sy Cl. Dunn's reading builds on the shorter text, which has the oldest witnesses.
- **The Clementine Vulgate** (filed this run): *non in honore aliquo ad saturitatem carnis* — **as recalled**. It reads *et non ad parcendum corpori*, so it has the "and", and it renders the α-privative of ἀφειδία with *non*.

**Verdict.** On how the three owned commentators read the sentence, the modern reading (1) stands as the consensus. Its key word "against" is **asserted, not shown**. Dunn's and Wright's weaker "in regard to / in relation to" is the better-attested sense of πρός, and it still yields "no value". **Reading 1, moderate**, preferably in the form "of no value in dealing with the flesh". Reading 2 (the regimen *serves* the flesh) now has named holders at second hand (Reicke; Hollenbach; O'Brien; Pokorný; Fowl; NET; RSV margin) and is *live, low–moderate*. Reading 3 ("not in any honour to the body") is rejected by Wright; Lohse and MacDonald take τιμή as "honour" on other grounds. **The dig's shared point stands, high:** on every reading, severity to the body is powerless against the flesh. Dunn's σῶμα / σάρξ structure is independent support for Headline 2.

### 1.2 Colossians 2:18, "the worship of the angels"

**Dunn (179–83):** he begins by calling the objective genitive "a wholly natural rendering", then argues that it does not fit the "strongly Jewish character" of the teaching. He develops Francis's **subjective** reading at length: joining the angels' worship, attested in T. Job 48–50; Apoc. Abr. 17; Asc. Isa. 7–9; Apoc. Zeph. 8; 1QSa 2:8–9; 1QSb 4:25–26; and the Songs of the Sabbath Sacrifice (4Q400–405). He judges a Colossian synagogue sharing that worship "quite possible". The object of Paul's rebuke, he says, is "the attitude of dismissive superiority" (καταβραβευέτω), not angelic worship as such. In his n. 36 he concedes the weak point: the hostile powers of 2:8, 10, 15, 20.

**Dunn's evidence for the subjective genitive** is "4 Macc 5:7; Josephus, *Ant.* 12.253". **Swete has θρησκεία in 4 Maccabees only at 5:6 and 5:13.** The verse is the one the dig cites (τῇ Ἰουδαίων … θρησκείᾳ), under a different number, presumably Rahlfs'. The Rahlfs numbering was not checked.

**The Assistant's survey** (from Brown, LRC) names the holders:

- **Participatory / subjective:** Francis; O'Brien; Bird; Davids. From Dunn's n. 21: Lincoln; Rowland; Evans; Sappington; Bruce (second edition, "an early form of merkabah mysticism", having changed his mind).
- **Veneration / objective:** Wolter; DeMaris (Dunn n. 14); Arnold; Hendriksen (via Brown).
- **No need to choose:** Stuckenbruck (via Brown).

**Correction:** Brown's survey files **Dunn** under the objective, "syncretistic" view. Dunn states that view, but his own case is for the subjective reading. This is the same kind of misfiling as round 2's Wright and Still.

**Laodicea:** Dunn's n. 15 gives, through Lightfoot, the canons' "name angels" (ἀγγέλους ὀνομάζειν), together with "judaizing" and sabbaths (canons 29, 35, 37, 38). This confirms the dig's canon 35 at second hand. Dunn's n. 15 also dates the Ankara inscription "to the great God Most High … and his holy angels" to the **late second or third century**.

**Verdict:** the grammar still does not decide (unchanged; Wis 14:27 against 4 Macc 5:6, Swete). The field is now sourced and evenly held. The dig's handling stands: name both readings in a sentence and preach the answer, which covers both. Dunn's observation that the fault is the visionary's superiority is a usable sermon point, `[S]`.

### 1.3 Colossians 2:18, ἃ ἑόρακεν ἐμβατεύων

- **Dunn** (183–84) — *Sense:* "enter"; legal papyri of entering into an inheritance; Josh 19:49, 51; apocalyptic **entry into heaven / the heavenly temple** (1 En 14; T. Levi 2; 4Q405 14–15, 23); an echo of Josh 19:49, 51 at Col 1:12; *On Claros:* Not a technical term; at Claros "entering into the sanctuary to consult the oracle" (Francis); the Fathers unaware of any special sense (Lampe, *PGL*)
- **Pao** (190–91) — *Sense:* "entering an inheritance to take possession" (Francis; Beetham), with Col 1:12 and the χειρόγραφον of 2:14; the visionary tries to take possession of the heavenly realm; *On Claros:* Technical only alongside related vocabulary (Nock); the cult reading cannot explain "humility" and "worship of angels" as what an initiate sees
- **Assistant** — *On Claros:* **Stuckenbruck dates the Claros inscriptions to the mid-second century**

**Assistant error:** it says Dunn "does not discuss" Claros, but the Dunn passage Patrick pasted discusses the Klaros inscriptions.

**Corpus:** Swete has ἐμβατεύω in seven verses: Josh 19:49, 51; 1 Macc 12:25; 13:20; 14:31; 15:40; 2 Macc 2:30. Dunn's "two OT occurrences" are the two in Joshua, and he cites 2 Macc 2:30 in his n. 33. **Dunn's Col 1:12 echo is thin lexically.** Josh 19:49 shares κλῆρος with Col 1:12 (εἰς τὴν μερίδα τοῦ κλήρου), and μερίς and κλῆρος stand together in the allotment chapters (Josh 18:5–7; 19:9), but nowhere with ἐμβατεύω and both nouns in the same verse. It is **queued as C43**, low–moderate, with Pao and Beetham as its holders.

**Verdict:** the initiation sense (c) moves from *moderate* to **low**: two owned commentators reject it, and the evidence is second-century. Between (a) "enter, take possession" (Dunn; Pao; Francis; Beetham) and (b) "go into detail" (Moo, via Pao n. 38; ESV, NIV84; the "investigate closely" of Preisker, Lyonnet and DeMaris, via Dunn n. 33), there is a **slight lean to (a), moderate**. The dig's handling ("his authority is what he has seen") is unchanged.

### 1.4 ταπεινοφροσύνη as fasting (2:18, 23)

**Dunn (178–79):** "most" follow the LXX phrase "to humble one's soul" = mortify oneself or fast. His holders: Percy; Grundmann; Schweizer; Caird; Lincoln. Against: Giesen (EDNT 3.334); Lohse ("readiness to serve"); DeMaris (a Christian virtue). His evidence for the noun meaning fasting is **Hermas** (*Vis.* 3.10.6; *Sim.* 5.3.7). The Assistant adds Lincoln (*NIB* 11:562–63): Tertullian and Hermas, and fasting before visions (Dan 10:2–9; 4 Ezra; 2 Baruch).

**Corpus (Swete):** the *verbal* idiom is verified at Lev 16:29, 31; 23:27, 29, 32; Ps 34:13 (35:13, ἐταπείνουν ἐν νηστείᾳ τὴν ψυχήν μου); Isa 58:3, 5. The *noun* is still absent from Swete. **New observation:** Lev 16:31 and 23:32 join "humble your souls" with σάββατα σαββάτων. In the Greek Pentateuch, fasting-as-humbling belongs to a calendar day, the Day of Atonement. In Col 2:16–18, the calendar and ταπεινοφροσύνη stand two verses apart. `[I]`, **low–moderate**; record only.

**Watch-for met:** Hermas (first half of the second century `[S]`) is the only evidence for the *noun* in the fasting sense. The first-century evidence is the verbal phrase.

**Verdict:** "fasting" moves from *uncertain* to **moderate**. The ESV's "asceticism" has a scholarly basis, though it is not settled. The 3:12 reclaiming stands on either sense: a technical word turned back to the virtue, or the virtue counterfeited.

**Pao's answer** was to another question: καταβραβεύω, without the athletic image. See 3.2.

### 1.5 The στοιχεῖα (2:8, 20) — Dunn at last

**Dunn (148–51):**

- The basic sense is the physical elements, "by far the most common usage" before Paul (Wis 7:17; 2 Pet 3:10, 12). Philo knew them divinised (*Contempl.* 3; *Decal.* 53).
- "Stars as powers" for στοιχεῖα is **not attested until after the NT** (T. Sol. 8:2–4; Diog. Laert. 6.102).
- The issue "does not turn on … personal versus impersonal".
- He accepts "elemental forces / elemental spirits of the universe" (RSV / NRSV, NEB / REB) and calls "principles" (NIV) "too vague".
- He ties the phrase to Gal 4:3, 9 and the law given "through angels" (Gal 3:19).
- The angels-over-elements texts he cites (Jub. 2:2; 1 En 75:1; 2 En 4:1) are evidence for spirits *associated with* the elements, **not for the word used of spirits**. Dunn draws that distinction himself, so the watch-for is met by the source.
- The minority for "elementary teaching": Moule; Carr; Sappington; Yates; Bandstra; DeMaris.

**Corpus (SBLGNT, Swete):** Gal 4:3, 9; Heb 5:12 (τὰ στοιχεῖα τῆς ἀρχῆς, "elementary principles"); 2 Pet 3:10, 12; Wis 7:17 (ἐνέργειαν στοιχείων); Wis 19:18; 4 Macc 12:13 — all verified.

**Verdict:** Dunn favours **the elements as cosmic forces under which human life was thought to be lived**. That is closer to sense 1 than to sense 3, and in his view it makes the 1 / 3 distinction less important. The 2:6–15 dig's pulpit note should be softened: the ESV's "elemental spirits" over-specifies, but it is not without a defender. The 2:20 construction (ἀπό with ἀποθνῄσκω, as of a regime) still fits. Dunn citing Wis 7:17 is **history of interpretation for C29**.

### 1.6 2:10, ὅς or ὅ

- **Dunn and Brown:** nothing.
- **Comfort** (*NT Text and Translation Commentary*, 627) defends the neuter as the harder reading. He calls the NA28's acceptance of ὅ at 1:27 and rejection at 2:10 "atomized eclecticism".

**Corpus (NA28 apparatus):**

- At **2:10**, ὅ is read by **𝔓⁴⁶ B D F G** (Hilᵖᵗ).
- At **1:27**, the neuter is the text, with **𝔓⁴⁶ A B F G P 33 1739 1881 latt**. ὅς is read by א C D H I K L Ψ 075 0278 81 104 365 630 1175 1241ˢ 1505 2464 𝔪.

The neuter at 1:27 has A, P, 33, 1739, 1881 and the whole Latin tradition; at 2:10 it has none of them. **Comfort's charge of inconsistency overlooks a real difference in external support.**

**The Assistant's grammar is back to front.** Attraction to the predicate (Robertson, *Grammar* 712) would give the *feminine* ἥ at 2:10 (ἡ κεφαλή), not ὅ. At 1:27 it is the *masculine* ὅς that would be attraction to the predicate (Χριστός); the neuter ὅ there agrees with μυστήριον. The neuter at 2:10 is better explained by the letter's ὅ ἐστιν formula (1:24, 27; 3:14), as the dig proposed, or by reference back to πλήρωμα (2:9).

**Verdict:** NA28's ὅς stays at **moderate**. The open question is **closed**: one named defender of the neuter (Comfort), and his argument has been assessed.

### 1.7 2:15, ἐν αὐτῷ

- **Dunn (169):** "Paul's attempt to retain the focus on what has been done 'in Christ' on the cross"; the metaphors "trip over each other".
- **"Brown":** **Jamieson–Fausset–Brown**, not Derek Brown's LRC. "In it — His cross": the Greek fathers; the Latins "in Him".
- **Campbell:** in Christ, by the cross.
- **Beale (BECNT, 203):** both possible — "difficult to determine which is best". For "in it": the cross is the nearest antecedent (2:14), with the irony of victory through weakness.
- **Pao:** in him; the display at the exaltation.

**Corpus:** Dunn's count, "the fifth since 2:9", reproduces in SBLGNT: 2:9, 2:10, 2:11 (ἐν ᾧ), 2:12 (ἐν ᾧ), 2:15. "The fifteenth in the letter" reproduces only if ἐν Χριστῷ (1:2, 4, 28) and ἐν ᾧ are counted with ἐν αὐτῷ. ἐν αὐτῷ itself occurs eight times (1:16, 17, 19; 2:6, 7, 9, 10, 15).

**Watch-for:** no commentator ties ἐν αὐτῷ to his view of the subject. Dunn takes Christ as subject (round 2) and still reads "in Christ", accepting the awkwardness.

**Verdict:** **evenly balanced; moderate.** Every holder joins the two: the triumph is in Christ and by the cross. The question is closed for preaching: say "in him, at the cross".

## 2. The state of debates

### 2.1 The Sabbath in 2:16

The answer rests on **Bacchiocchi; Gane (*Perplexing Doctrinal Questions Answered*); du Preez** (Andrews University Press) — **all Seventh-day Adventist scholars** — plus a 1729 tract. No commentary in the Colossians collection is cited.

**Watch-for met in another form:** the confessional question governs the sources, not only the conclusions. "The SDA view = ceremonial sabbaths" is also too simple: Bacchiocchi and Gane, both Adventists, take the weekly Sabbath to be meant. The answer's own "mediating position" (the rituals of the day, not the day, are the shadow) is Gane's.

**Corpus test of Bacchiocchi's linguistic argument:**

- In Swete's Exodus–Numbers, σάββατα σαββάτων occurs only at **Lev 16:31; 23:32**, the Day of Atonement.
- The Hebrew שַׁבַּת שַׁבָּתוֹן is used of the **weekly Sabbath** at Exod 31:15; 35:2; Lev 23:3, and of the Day of Atonement and the sabbatical year (Lev 16:31; 23:32; 25:4) (WLC). There, Swete renders the weekly Sabbath σάββατα ἀνάπαυσις.

So the argument works in the Greek, which is the language Colossians uses, and "linguistically impossible" overstates it.

**The triad is verified:** Hos 2:11 (Swete) and Ezek 45:17 both run ἑορτή … νουμηνία … σάββατα.

**Verdict:** the dig's "includes the weekly Sabbath", **moderate–high**, stands, now with a Greek-only argument added. **A better follow-up** is to ask the same question of Dunn, Pao, Beale and Moo (if owned), so that the preacher has the commentators' view, not only the Adventist one.

## 4. Lexicon, grammar, background

**4.1 ἐθελοθρησκία.** "Never appears in Greek literature prior to Colossians": Arnold (WBC 2025, 590–91). Coined by Paul: Moulton–Milligan, via Gilbrant. The opponents' word, used sarcastically: O'Brien, via Gilbrant. The opponents' catchword from their initiatory background: Arnold, who frames it within his mystery-cult reconstruction. The ἐθελο- formation is paralleled by ἐθελοδουλεία. **Verdict:** "not known before Colossians" is now sourced, **moderate–high → high** as a report. Who coined the word is open, with both sides named.

**4.2 θέλων ἐν (2:18).**

- "Delight in", from the Hebrew חָפֵץ בְּ: Barth–Blanke (Anchor 34B, 342–43). Dunn's own paraphrase of 2:18 is "delighting in the humility and worship of the angels" (1.2 above).
- Adverbial "wilfully": Robertson; Kent, who prefers it and cites 2 Pet 3:5.
- **Corpus:** 2 Pet 3:5 has θέλοντας **without ἐν** ("λανθάνει γὰρ αὐτοὺς τοῦτο θέλοντας"), so it is not the same construction (SBLGNT).
- **Clementine Vulgate:** *volens in humilitate* — the Latin "willing" is the root of the KJV's "voluntary humility".
- No holder was found for the ESV's "insisting on".

**Verdict: moderate–high, unchanged** — the NASB95's "delighting in".

**5.1 4 Maccabees:** see C28 above.

**5.2 Angels in Phrygia.**

- **Theodoret** on Col 2:18, in translation (NPNF² 14:150, with the Laodicean canons; and via Gill, 1773): the worshippers "were leading to worship angels such as were defending the Law; for, said they, the Law was given through angels"; the vice "lasted for a long time in Phrygia and Pisidia"; the synod of Laodicea forbade prayer to angels by a canon; "even to-day an oratory of St. Michael can be seen among them".
- **Foster** (BNTC 289–90): inscriptions to "the Good Angel", "the Divine Angel", "the Angelic Divinity", **undated in the answer**.

**Watch-for met in part:**

- The Assistant's claim that the inscriptions "may be earlier" is its own inference.
- The answer gives no exact wording of canon 35; Dunn's "name angels" (ἀγγέλους ὀνομάζειν) is the nearest, through Lightfoot.
- Theodoret is fifth century (died c. 457 `[S]`). The Council of Laodicea is fourth century; I recall c. 363, and NPNF dates it only between 343 and 381 `[S]`.
- Chonae is not addressed.

**New and useful:** Theodoret links the angel-worship to the *law given through angels* (cf. Gal 3:19; Acts 7:53; Heb 2:2), the same association Dunn makes for the στοιχεῖα (1.5).

**Verdict:** the dig's caution ("all of it centuries later") stands. Theodoret's wording is now sourced in translation.
