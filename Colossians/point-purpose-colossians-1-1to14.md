## Scope Decision

**One sermon on 1:1–14, the overview's first preaching unit (weighted Standard).** The passage is a greeting and two Greek sentences — the thanksgiving (1:3–8) and the prayer (1:9–14) — and it hangs together on two thanksgiving brackets (1:3 → 1:12) and a hearing bracket (1:6 → 1:9). It is short enough for one sermon and has no internal seam that would justify two.

**Two overlaps, named.**

- **The unit ends mid-sentence.** A chain of relatives runs from the Father "who has qualified you" (1:12) through the Son "in whom we have redemption" (1:14) to "who is the image of the invisible God" (1:15). Neither edition has a full stop at 1:14 (dig, Headline 4; Preaching Units note). **Read 1:1–15**, and let the last verse point to next week.
- **The introduction has already summarised this ground.** This sermon should not repeat it. The introduction said *that* the gospel is complete; this sermon shows *how* the opening of the letter says it — by thanksgiving, by prayer and by the Father's finished acts.

**Governing sections read first.** *Revision v1.4* reports "Stale headlines after this pass: none". Headlines 4 and 5 were rewritten in place after claim audit 5 and are used here at their current ratings: the "you → us" shift is *high* on the pronouns but *low–moderate* as a design peculiar to Colossians; the exodus colouring of 1:12–14 is *moderate*, anchored in Exod 6:6–8. *Revision v1.2* moves the 1:11–12 punctuation to a slight lean to "with joy giving thanks", *low–moderate*.

**Claim-audit check.** The queue is not armed (seven candidates queued; no digs since audit 5), and the queue file records that Trigger 2 for point-purpose on 1:1–14 was cleared by the audit-5 patch. Of the queued candidates, C73–C76 touch 1:12–14 (Luke 22:53; the "portion and lot" formula; the darkness plague; Esther's Addition C). **None of them is load-bearing in this plan**, and Keep Honest 4 keeps them out of the sermon.

## Fallen Condition Focus

**We suspect we are under-equipped or second-class Christians — that the gospel we were given is a beginner's version — and so we look beyond Christ for what will make us complete.**

*Anchored in the text.* The passage does not name the fear; it answers it before anyone can exploit it (dig, Candidate FCF: *inferred, moderate–high*). The gospel they heard is "the word of the truth" (1:5) and "the grace of God in truth" (1:6) — ἀλήθεια ("truth") stands only in these two verses of the letter. It is the same gospel that is bearing fruit "in the whole world", "as it also does among you" (1:6). It came to them from a local man, Epaphras, "a faithful minister of Christ" (1:7). And the Father "has qualified you" — ἱκανόω ("to make sufficient") — for the inheritance (1:12). Each of these is a status-conferring word for Gentiles, a provincial church, and pupils of a fellow townsman whose standing might be questioned (dig, *Where the passage fits their world*).

*Shared and different.* The rival would later offer them wisdom, visions and regimens (2:4, 8, 18, 23). We are more often offered a deeper experience, a more serious discipline, a better technique, or a teacher who seems to know more than the one who first taught us. The suspicion is the same: *what I received was only the start.*

*Not a crisis.* The prayer answers good news, not an emergency: "And so, from the day we heard" (1:9) follows the report of their faith, love and hope (1:4–8). Preach to a healthy church that is being made secure, not to a church being rescued.

## Teaching Point

**The Father has already qualified us, rescued us and brought us into the kingdom of his beloved Son, in whom we have forgiveness; so the gospel we received is the whole truth, and what we need is to be filled with it — not added to it.**

- **Subject:** What does a church that has received the gospel still need in order to grow?
- **Complement:** Not something beyond the gospel, but to be filled with the knowledge of God's will in it — because the Father has already made them fit, rescued them, transferred them and forgiven them in his Son.

*Built from the convergent Headline Findings:*

- **Headline 1 — thanks grounded in finished acts.** Paul thanks the Father (1:3) and prays that they will thank the Father (1:12); the third and last use of εὐχαριστέω ("to give thanks") in the letter is also to the Father (3:17). Between the two thanksgivings the verbs are present participles; at 1:12–14 they become aorists — "qualified" (ἱκανώσαντι), "delivered" (ἐρρύσατο), "transferred" (μετέστησεν) — and a present of possession, "we have" (ἔχομεν). *High.*
- **Headline 2 — the gospel's credentials before the warnings.** The true, world-wide word, taught by a faithful minister, bracketed by 1:23 (hope, gospel, heard, universal scope). The warnings arrive only at 2:4 and 2:8. *High on the words; moderate–high on the design.*
- **Headline 3 — the prayer asks for what they will be told they have.** "That you may be filled" (πληρωθῆτε, 1:9) → "you have been filled" (πεπληρωμένοι, 2:10). The prayer also plants ἐπίγνωσις ("knowledge"), σοφία ("wisdom") and περιπατέω ("to walk") before the rival can claim them. *High on the words.*
- **Headline 5 — rescue concentrated here.** ἁμαρτία ("sin"), ἄφεσις ("forgiveness"), ἀπολύτρωσις ("redemption"), ῥύομαι ("to rescue"), μεθίστημι ("to transfer"), σκότος ("darkness"), φῶς ("light"), μερίς ("share") and κλῆρος ("lot") each occur once in the letter, all in 1:12–14. *High on the concentration.*

The subject of every saving verb is the Father; the sphere of the redemption is the Son — "in whom" (ἐν ᾧ), not "through whom" (dig, Christological Reading). The church's growth is the response, and two of its four participles are passives: "being increased" and "being strengthened" — things God does (dig, Original Language Observations).

## Purpose Statement

**To move hearers from suspecting they are second-class Christians who need something more, to thankful confidence in what the Father has already done for them in his Son — and from there to pray Paul's prayer for themselves and one another: to be filled with the knowledge of God's will, walk in a manner worthy of the Lord, bear fruit, and endure with joy, out of gratitude rather than fear of falling short.**

- **For the believer who feels second-class:** the word is "qualified" (1:12). God made you sufficient for the inheritance; you did not have to make yourself so — the word means sufficiency, not merit (dig, Tool 8, on the Vulgate's *dignos*). The gospel that reached you through an ordinary local teacher is the same one bearing fruit in the whole world (1:6–7).
- **For the believer attracted to "more":** the prayer asks God for more — more knowledge, wisdom, fruit, strength, endurance (1:9–11) — but more *of the same*, never something beyond Christ. Growth in Colossians is being filled, not being supplemented.
- **For the anxious decision-maker:** "the knowledge of his will" (1:9) is God's revealed purpose in Christ, shown in a worthy walk and good works (1:10), not a hidden code for private choices (dig, Difficult Verses, *high*). That is a release, and the sermon can say so.
- **For the church:** the "you" is plural throughout. Pray for others as Paul prays here — especially for other churches, and for growth, not only for circumstances (dig, So What).
- **For the unbeliever:** there are two realms, "the domain of darkness" and "the kingdom of his beloved Son". The move between them is God's act, and the redemption it brings is "the forgiveness of sins" (1:13–14).

## Candidate Big Question

**If you sometimes feel like a second-class Christian, who qualified you — and what more do you think you need?**

*Shorter alternative, for the pulpit:* "Is the gospel you were given enough to grow on?"

This is a candidate. The one-sentence-sermon step, or the preacher, settles the final wording. The shorter form also links back to the introduction's question ("Is Christ enough?").

## Proposed Structure

The passage falls into a greeting and two long Greek sentences (dig, Tool 3). The three movements follow them, with 1:15 read as the landing.

1. **1:1–8 — The gospel you have is the real thing.**
   - The greeting names what the passage will use at once: the will of God (1:1 → 1:9), the saints (1:2 → 1:4, 12), God "our Father" (1:2 → 1:3, 12).
   - Paul thanks God for a report: he has not seen them (2:1), but he has heard of their faith and love, "because of the hope laid up for you in heaven" (1:4–5). Hope is the cause of faith and love, not just the third member of a list (dig, Tool 4, *moderate*).
   - They heard that hope "in the word of the truth, the gospel", which is "bearing fruit and increasing" in the whole world "as it also does among you" (1:5–6).
   - They learned it from Epaphras, "our beloved fellow servant … a faithful minister of Christ" (1:7).

   *Function:* surface the FCF and answer it at once. The second-class suspicion is met by the credentials of the gospel and its local messenger. **Do not rush this movement** (dig, Pitfall 1): the warnings of chapter 2 stand on it.

2. **1:9–11 — Filled, not supplemented: what Paul prays for a healthy church.**
   - "And so, from the day we heard" (1:9) — the prayer is the response to good news. The phrase ἀφ᾽ ἧς ἡμέρας ("from the day") stands in the NT only at 1:6 ("since the day you heard") and 1:9 ("from the day we heard"): the church heard the gospel; the apostle heard of the church (dig, Tool 3).
   - The request is passive: "that you may be **filled** with the knowledge of his will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding" (1:9). God fills; they do not fill themselves.
   - Its purpose is a walk: "so as to walk in a manner worthy of the Lord" (1:10). ἀξίως ("worthily") means "fittingly", not "deservingly" (dig, Difficult Verses).
   - The walk is unfolded in four participles, which describe it rather than add four commands: bearing fruit, being increased in the knowledge of God, being strengthened "according to his glorious might, for all endurance and patience", giving thanks (1:10–12).
   - The gospel's growth (1:6) and the believers' growth (1:10) are described in the same two words, καρποφορέω ("to bear fruit") and αὐξάνω ("to increase"): the fruit is the gospel producing faith, love, good works and knowledge of God, not numbers (dig, Pitfall 5).

   *Function:* the complement of the Teaching Point — growth in what they already have. **Hinge to watch:** this movement must not end as a programme. The fourth participle, "giving thanks", is the bridge to Movement 3; end Movement 2 on it.

3. **1:12–14 (→ 1:15) — Already qualified, delivered, transferred, forgiven.**
   - The thanks has content: the Father "has qualified you to share in the inheritance of the saints in light" (1:12) — Israel's language of "share" and "lot" (μερίς, κλῆρος) spoken over Gentiles. The "saints" are most likely believers, as every other plural ἅγιοι ("saints") in the letter is (*moderate–high*).
   - "He has delivered us from the domain of darkness and transferred us to the kingdom of his beloved Son" (1:13). The verbs are completed aorists: the darkness is behind them (dig, Difficult Verses).
   - "In whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins" (1:14) — present possession, held "in" the Son. This is the only place in the letter where "sin" is named.
   - Then 1:15: the sentence runs on, "who is the image of the invisible God". The kingdom introduces its king.

   *Function:* the ground of the whole passage, and the landing of the Purpose Statement. Here the sermon shows that **1:9–11 rests on 1:12–14, not the reverse** (dig, Pitfall 3; Christological Reading). Close by turning the thanks into the congregation's own — and by pointing to next week's confession of who the Son is.

**Hinge to watch, for the whole sermon.** Movement 3 is grammatically subordinate — it hangs off a participle — but theologically it carries everything (dig, Tool 3). Give it at least the time of Movement 2. If the sermon spends its energy on the prayer's requests and treats 1:12–14 as a closing flourish, it has turned the prayer into a ladder.

## Keep Honest

1. **Do not treat 1:1–8 as polite preliminaries.** "After the usual greetings, Paul gets to the point" forfeits the ground the warnings of chapter 2 stand on (dig, Pitfall 1). The credentials of the gospel and of Epaphras are the passage's first argument.

2. **Do not make the prayer a ladder, or "worthy" a merit word.** "Bear fruit, grow, endure and give thanks, and you will be found worthy of the inheritance" reverses the order: the Father "has qualified" (aorist) and the walk is "worthy" in the sense of fitting (dig, Pitfall 3). ἱκανόω ("to make sufficient") occurs in the NT only here and at 2 Cor 3:6, and in both God makes fit for what the recipient could not supply. Hearers shaped by older, Latin-influenced theology may hear "made us worthy" (the Vulgate's *dignos nos fecit*); the Greek word is of sufficiency, not merit.

3. **"The knowledge of his will" is not private guidance, and the darkness is not a spiritual-warfare manual.**
   - The will in view is the will that made Paul an apostle (1:1) and in which Epaphras prays they stand "fully assured" (4:12); its knowledge shows in a worthy walk (1:10) (dig, Pitfall 2).
   - "The domain of darkness" is a real authority (compare 2:15), but the verb says they have left it. Preach the transfer, not the darkness (dig, Difficult Verses).

4. **Hold the background at the weight the audits gave it.**
   - The exodus colouring of 1:12–14 — rescue, redemption, an allotted inheritance — is *moderate*, anchored in Exod 6:6–8, the one passage with the whole movement in order — ῥύσομαι … λυτρώσομαι … ἐν κλήρῳ ("I will rescue … I will redeem … as a lot", Swete). **No Old Testament text is quoted or closely reproduced**, and ῥύομαι ("to rescue") by itself is not an exodus word. Mention the pattern once, as the shape of the story, and do not build a movement on it.
   - The shift from "you" (1:12) to "us" (1:13–14) is real in the Greek and audible in the ESV, but it is Paul's habit when he states the saving act (1 Thess 1:9–10; Gal 1:3–4; Eph 2:1–5), not a design peculiar to Colossians (*low–moderate*). It can be said in a sentence — "Paul cannot speak of the rescue without including himself" — but it is not a sermon point.
   - The royal-deportation background to μεθίστημι ("to transfer") is *low–moderate* and rests on Josephus, which the corpus cannot check. Leave it out.
   - Do not stop at 1:14 (dig, Pitfall 4): read 1:15.

## Christ-Centred Check

- **"Be like X" or "trust the Christ whom X points to"?** The temptation is "be like the Colossians — fruitful, loving, faithful" or "be like Paul — pray more" (dig, moralism check). The payoff here is thanksgiving for the Father's finished acts in the Son, and a prayer that God will fill and strengthen. Paul and Epaphras are witnesses to the gospel, not models. **Pass.**
- **Is Christ the subject?** The Father acts and the Son is the sphere: "the kingdom of his beloved Son, in whom we have redemption" (1:13–14), and the passage hands over to the confession of his person (1:15). He is the Lord of the worthy walk (1:10), not its example. **Pass.**
- **Is the good news grounded in Christ's saving work?** Redemption and forgiveness are held "in" the Son (1:14), and the transfer is the Father's completed act (1:13). The cross itself is not named until 1:20, so point forward to it rather than import it here. **Pass.**

## Pulpit-Text Note (ESV against NASB95)

Checked against the ESV and NASB95 exports in `_texts/logos-exports/02-New-Testament/`, and the dig's Tool 8.

**Where the ESV carries the plan better than the study text:**

- **1:12 — "who has qualified you"** (ESV, with NA28's ὑμᾶς). NASB95 "qualified us" levels the pronoun shift. The congregation will hear "you … us … we" — the move Keep Honest 4 allows a sentence on.
- **1:13 — the heading.** The ESV starts a new sentence ("He has delivered us"), which the Greek does not, but it keeps 1:13–14 with 1:12 and puts its heading ("The Preeminence of Christ") at 1:15. The NASB95 puts "For He rescued us" and the heading "The Incomparable Christ" at 1:13, cutting the rescue off from the thanksgiving. On the structure Movement 3 depends on, the pulpit text serves the passage better.
- **1:9 and 2:10 — "filled" … "filled".** Both audible in the ESV, so Headline 3's forward link can be heard: read 2:10 aloud when making it. (The NASB95 breaks it at 2:10 with "made complete".)
- **1:6 / 1:9 — "since the day you heard" / "from the day we heard".** Both versions let the hearing bracket be heard.

**Where the ESV hides or decides something the preacher should supply:**

- **1:11–12 — "with joy".** The ESV attaches it to endurance ("for all endurance and patience with joy; giving thanks"); NA28 and the NASB95 attach it to the thanksgiving ("joyously giving thanks"). The lean is slight, to the thanksgiving (*low–moderate*: Pao; Wright says "it is impossible to tell"). **What the congregation will hear is joyful endurance.** Both are Pauline. Do not rest a point on either reading; if Movement 2 ends on thanksgiving, simply say that the joy may belong with the thanks.
- **1:9 — "And so".** The ESV softens Διὰ τοῦτο καὶ ἡμεῖς ("for this reason we also") and drops the emphatic "we". Movement 2's point — the prayer answers good news — needs the causal link: say "for this reason".
- **1:7 — "on your behalf".** The ESV follows NA28's ὑμῶν ("your"), making Epaphras the church's own minister; the NASB95 follows the older witnesses' ἡμῶν ("our"), making him Paul's representative. The reading is *uncertain* (Robertson and the *Lexham Textual Notes* favour "our"). Movement 1 does not depend on it: say "faithful minister" and leave "on whose behalf" alone.
- **1:13 — "his beloved Son".** All the versions say so; the Greek is "the Son of his love", and ἀγάπη ("love") also stands at 1:4 and 1:8. If it helps Movement 3, say it once: the love that Epaphras reported among them (1:8) is the Father's love for the Son into whose kingdom they have been brought.
- **1:12 — "in light".** The ESV leaves "light" lower-case; the NASB95's capital ("Light") makes it a title. Follow the ESV.

**Caveat.** The ESV in the corpus is the US edition, not explicitly the Anglicised. The dig found no word in 1:1–15 that the two editions are known to spell differently, but the wording has not been checked against an Anglicised copy, which is not in Patrick's Logos library (on pre-order). **Read the passage from the pew Bible before preaching.**
