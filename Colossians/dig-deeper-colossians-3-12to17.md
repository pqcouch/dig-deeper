## The Passage

**NA28:**

> **12** Ἐνδύσασθε οὖν, ὡς ἐκλεκτοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ ἅγιοι καὶ ἠγαπημένοι, σπλάγχνα οἰκτιρμοῦ χρηστότητα ταπεινοφροσύνην πραΰτητα μακροθυμίαν, **13** ἀνεχόμενοι ἀλλήλων καὶ χαριζόμενοι ἑαυτοῖς ἐάν τις πρός τινα ἔχῃ μομφήν· καθὼς καὶ ὁ κύριος ἐχαρίσατο ὑμῖν, οὕτως καὶ ὑμεῖς· **14** ἐπὶ πᾶσιν δὲ τούτοις τὴν ἀγάπην, ὅ ἐστιν σύνδεσμος τῆς τελειότητος. **15** καὶ ἡ εἰρήνη τοῦ Χριστοῦ βραβευέτω ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις ὑμῶν, εἰς ἣν καὶ ἐκλήθητε ἐν ἑνὶ σώματι· καὶ εὐχάριστοι γίνεσθε. **16** Ὁ λόγος τοῦ Χριστοῦ ἐνοικείτω ἐν ὑμῖν πλουσίως, ἐν πάσῃ σοφίᾳ διδάσκοντες καὶ νουθετοῦντες ἑαυτούς, ψαλμοῖς ὕμνοις ᾠδαῖς πνευματικαῖς ἐν [τῇ] χάριτι ᾄδοντες ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις ὑμῶν τῷ θεῷ· **17** καὶ πᾶν ὅ τι ἐὰν ποιῆτε ἐν λόγῳ ἢ ἐν ἔργῳ, πάντα ἐν ὀνόματι κυρίου Ἰησοῦ, εὐχαριστοῦντες τῷ θεῷ πατρὶ δι᾽ αὐτοῦ.

**Where the SBLGNT differs:** the words are the same except that SBLGNT prints ἐν χάριτι without the article that NA28 brackets (3:16). **The punctuation of 3:16 differs in two places, and both bear on the sense** `[T: as each edition prints it]`:
- NA28 puts a comma after πλουσίως ("richly"), so "in all wisdom" goes with the teaching; SBLGNT puts a stop after σοφίᾳ ("wisdom"), so it goes with the dwelling.
- NA28 puts a comma after ἑαυτούς ("one another"), so the psalms, hymns and songs go with the singing; SBLGNT has no comma there and one after χάριτι, so they go with the teaching and admonishing.

SBLGNT also punctuates 3:12 with a comma after τοῦ θεοῦ, setting "holy and beloved" beside "chosen of God"; NA28 has none. See Tool 3, Tool 8 and Open Questions.

## Headline Findings

1. **"Put on therefore": the garments follow the new self already put on, and the ground of the command is a name — Israel's election titles given to a Gentile church.** `[T]`/`[I]` Surfaced by: Positional Necessity, Linking Words, Vocabulary, Tool 11 (Deut 7). Ἐνδύσασθε ("put on", aorist imperative, 3:12) takes up ἐνδυσάμενοι ("having put on", aorist participle, 3:10): ἐνδύω stands in the letter only in these two verses (SBLGNT) `[T]`. What was done (3:10) is now to be done (3:12) — the 3:5–11 dig's indicative-then-imperative reading `[S: dig]`. The readers are addressed "as the chosen of God, holy and beloved" `[T]`. ἐκλεκτός ("chosen") is its only use in the letter, ἀγαπάω ("love") occurs only here and at 3:19, and ἅγιος ("holy") resumes 1:2, 1:12, 1:22 and 1:26 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. In the Greek Deuteronomy, Israel is "a holy people" whom the LORD "chose" because he "loved" them (Deut 7:6–8; cf. 4:37; 10:15, Swete) `[T]`. **The designations of Israel are spoken over people who "once walked" in the vices of 3:5–7** `[I]`. *High on the words; moderate on Deuteronomy 7 as the specific background.*

2. **The five virtues are the character of God and of Christ, and the forgiveness is patterned on the Lord's.** `[T]`/`[I]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Repetition, Tool 11 (Exod 34:6; Rom 2:4), Move 4. Elsewhere in Paul, χρηστότης ("kindness") and μακροθυμία ("patience") are God's (Rom 2:4, with ἀνοχή, the noun of the verb ἀνέχομαι, "bear with", 3:13). οἰκτιρμοί ("compassions") are God's at Rom 12:1 and 2 Cor 1:3. πραΰτης ("gentleness") is "the gentleness of Christ" at 2 Cor 10:1 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. In the Greek Old Testament, "compassionate … patient" is the LORD's self-description (Exod 34:6), repeated in the Psalms and the prophets (Tool 11) `[T]`. Then 3:13 says it outright: "just as the Lord forgave you, so also you" `[T]`. χαρίζομαι ("forgive") stands in Colossians only at 2:13 (God "having forgiven us all our transgressions") and 3:13 (overview Echo 14, high) `[T]`. And ταπεινοφροσύνη, the rival's "self-abasement" at 2:18 and 2:23, returns here as "humility" `[T]`. Claim audit 2 rated this reclaiming moderate as design `[S: audit 2]`. *High on the words; moderate–high that the list is meant as the family likeness.*

3. **Love is put on over everything as "the bond of perfection", and the image continues the body knit together in love (2:2, 19), not a reversal of it.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Repetition, Move 4, Textual Variants. ἐπὶ πᾶσιν δὲ τούτοις ("over all these", 3:14) keeps the clothing image `[I]`. σύνδεσμος ("bond, ligament") stands in four NT verses: Acts 8:23; Eph 4:3; Col 2:19; 3:14 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. At 2:19 the body is "supplied and held together by the joints and ligaments (συνδέσμων)" from the head; at 2:2 the hearts are "knit together (συμβιβασθέντες) in love". συμβιβάζω occurs in the letter only at 2:2 and 2:19 `[T]`. Claim audit 2 found σύνδεσμος to be a continuation, not a reversal `[S: audit 2]`. This run agrees, and adds the 2:2 link. τελειότης ("perfection, completeness") occurs only here and at Heb 6:1 in the NT. Its adjective τέλειος stands in the letter at 1:28 (Paul's aim, "to present every man complete in Christ") and 4:12 (Epaphras's prayer that they "stand complete") `[T]`. **Love is what holds the body together towards the maturity that the apostle and Epaphras labour for** `[I]`, moderate–high. *High on the words.*

4. **Christ's peace takes the umpire's chair and Christ's word takes up residence — and the congregation does for itself what Paul does for it.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Vocabulary, Original Languages, Move 4. "Let no one act as umpire against you" (καταβραβευέτω, 2:18) is answered by "let the peace of Christ act as umpire" (βραβευέτω, 3:15). Both verbs occur only here in the NT `[T]`. The antithesis is rated moderate–high `[S: audit 1]` `[S: audit 2]`. "The word of Christ" (ὁ λόγος τοῦ Χριστοῦ, 3:16) is unique in the NT (SBLGNT), and scribes changed it to "of the Lord" or "of God" `[T]`. Its verb, ἐνοικείτω ("let it dwell in"), continues the letter's dwelling chain after 1:19 and 2:9 (κατοικέω, "dwell") `[S: audit 1]`. "Teaching and admonishing … in all wisdom" joins νουθετέω ("admonish") and διδάσκω ("teach") in only two NT verses, Col 1:28 and 3:16, both with ἐν πάσῃ σοφίᾳ ("in all wisdom") (SBLGNT; reproduced from claim audit 2) `[T]`. **What Paul says "we" do (1:28), the church is to do "for one another" (3:16)** `[I]`, high. *High on the words.*

5. **Thanksgiving three times in three verses, and a summary — "whatever you do … in the name of the Lord Jesus" — that closes the new life and opens the household.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Repetition, Structure, Move 4. εὐχάριστοι γίνεσθε ("be thankful", 3:15; εὐχάριστος occurs only here in the NT), ἐν [τῇ] χάριτι ("with thankfulness / with grace", 3:16), εὐχαριστοῦντες τῷ θεῷ πατρί ("giving thanks to God the Father", 3:17) (SBLGNT) `[T]`. The prayer report began the same way: εὐχαριστοῦμεν τῷ θεῷ πατρί ("we give thanks to God the Father", 1:3), and εὐχαριστοῦντες τῷ πατρί ("giving thanks to the Father", 1:12). πατήρ ("Father") is used of God in the letter only at 1:2, 1:3, 1:12 and 3:17 `[T]`. **The church is to pray as its apostle prays for it** `[I]`, moderate–high. "And everything, whatever you do (πᾶν ὅ τι ἐὰν ποιῆτε)" (3:17) is picked up by ὃ ἐὰν ποιῆτε ("whatever you do", 3:23) — the only two NT occurrences. Claim audit 3 rated the pick-up moderate–high, and the whole household code as 3:17 applied moderate `[S: audit 3]`. *High on the words.*

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book-level:** to keep the church walking in Christ, persuaded it lacks nothing, and to show what that sufficiency looks like in speech, household and work (overview) `[I]`.

**Passage-level:** to clothe the new humanity of 3:10–11 in its common life: the virtues that make it possible to live together (3:12–13), love as the bond over them (3:14), Christ's peace as arbiter and Christ's word as resident teacher (3:15–16), thanksgiving throughout, and a rule that covers every word and act (3:17) `[T]`/`[I]`. Against a teaching that offered humility as a regimen and rulings as a referee (2:18–23), the passage offers humility as a garment of the chosen, peace as the referee and the word of Christ as the teacher `[I]`, moderate–high.

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement:** 3:9–10 said the readers had "put off the old self" and "put on the new" `[T]`. 3:5–9 named what must die and be put away: two lists of five, the second about speech `[T]`. 3:11 dissolved the divisions — "Christ is all, and in all" `[T]`. Further back: the rival's "self-abasement", umpiring, "teachings of men" and "word of wisdom" (2:18–23) `[T]`; and Paul's prayer for "all spiritual wisdom", "all patience and longsuffering" and thanks "to the Father" (1:9–12) `[T]`.

**Necessity answer — why the passage exists here.**

1. **3:10 requires 3:12.** "Having put on the new" (participle) must be lived out: "put on therefore" (imperative) `[T]`. The garments of 3:12 are the new self's clothes `[I]`, high.
2. **3:5–9 requires it.** Five vices and five sins of speech have been put off. The empty place is filled by five virtues and by a speech that teaches, admonishes, sings and gives thanks (3:16–17) `[T]`/`[I]`. The 3:5–11 dig planted 3:8–9 → 3:16–17 `[S: dig]`.
3. **3:11 requires it.** A people in which Greek and Jew, slave and free now stand together needs the virtues of bearing with one another and of forgiveness, and one body under one peace (3:13–15) `[I]`, moderate–high.
4. **2:18–23 is answered.** The rival's humility, his umpiring and his teachings meet their true counterparts (Headlines 2 and 4) `[T]`.
5. **1:9–12 is answered.** What Paul prayed for — wisdom (1:9), patience (1:11), thanks to the Father (1:12) — is now commanded (3:12, 16, 17) `[T]`.

**Implication for this passage:** it is not a list of nice qualities. Every clause answers something earlier in the letter, and it is the last thing said to the whole church before the household (3:18) `[I]`, high.

**Revisit after Tool 11:** Move 2 (Exod 34:6; Deut 7:6–8) sharpens the answer. The church is named as Israel was named (chosen, holy, loved) and is to bear the character the LORD proclaimed of himself (compassionate, kind, patient) `[I]`, moderate.

**Immediately before:** 3:5–11 (above). **Immediately after:** 3:18, "Wives, be subject …", without a connective. 3:23's "whatever you do" picks up 3:17 `[S: audit 3]`. 4:2 resumes "with thanksgiving" (ἐν εὐχαριστίᾳ) `[T]`.

**Section:** 3:1–4:6, the life of the risen; 3:5–17 is the overview's "put off, put on", and 3:12–17 its second half `[T]`. 3:17 is the hinge between the church's common life and the household `[I]`, high.

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 3:12–13 | Put on: the five virtues | Command; the ground ("as the chosen of God, holy and beloved"); five virtues; two participles (bearing, forgiving); the pattern ("as the Lord … so also you") |
| 3:14 | Over all: love | The outer garment; "the bond of perfection" |
| 3:15 | Let peace rule | Third-person imperative; "to which you were called in one body"; "and be thankful" |
| 3:16 | Let the word dwell | Third-person imperative; participles of teaching, admonishing and singing |
| 3:17 | Everything in the Lord's name | Summary; giving thanks to God the Father through him |

**Device 1 — the five virtues answering the five and five.** 3:5 and 3:8 each list five; 3:12 lists five `[T]`. The virtues face other people: compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, patience `[I]`. Two participles follow, both about others: "bearing with one another" (ἀλλήλων) and "forgiving each other" (ἑαυτοῖς) `[T]`.

**Device 2 — two "let" commands.** βραβευέτω ("let it umpire", 3:15) and ἐνοικείτω ("let it dwell", 3:16): third-person imperatives with Christ's peace and Christ's word as subjects `[T]`. In Colossians the other third-person imperatives are the two prohibitions of 2:16 and 2:18. Claim audit 2 counted four in thirty imperatives and judged the adjudication frame proper to be three verbs, not four `[S: audit 2]`.

**Device 3 — the thanksgiving chain.** εὐχάριστοι (3:15), χάριτι (3:16), εὐχαριστοῦντες (3:17): each verse ends on thanks `[T]`. Three verses, three words from the χαρ- family `[T]`.

**Device 4 — an inclusio of the Lord.** "As the Lord forgave you" (3:13) … "in the name of the Lord Jesus" (3:17) `[T]`. Between them stand Christ's peace and Christ's word (3:15–16) `[T]`. Christ is named four times in five verses (κύριος twice, Χριστός twice, with Ἰησοῦς once) `[T]`.

**Clause-Flow Layout** (NA28 wording and punctuation):

- **Ἐνδύσασθε οὖν** — COMMAND: "put on therefore" (aorist imperative)
    - ὡς ἐκλεκτοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ ἅγιοι καὶ ἠγαπημένοι — GROUND: "as the chosen of God, holy and beloved"
    - σπλάγχνα οἰκτιρμοῦ, χρηστότητα, ταπεινοφροσύνην, πραΰτητα, μακροθυμίαν — the five garments (objects)
    - ἀνεχόμενοι ἀλλήλων — MANNER: "bearing with one another" (present participle)
    - καὶ χαριζόμενοι ἑαυτοῖς — MANNER: "and forgiving each other" (present participle)
        - ἐάν τις πρός τινα ἔχῃ μομφήν — CONDITION: "if anyone has a complaint against anyone"
        - καθὼς καὶ ὁ κύριος ἐχαρίσατο ὑμῖν, οὕτως καὶ ὑμεῖς — PATTERN: "just as the Lord forgave you, so also you"
    - ἐπὶ πᾶσιν δὲ τούτοις τὴν ἀγάπην — "and over all these, love" (object of the same verb)
        - ὅ ἐστιν σύνδεσμος τῆς τελειότητος — "which is the bond of perfection"
- καὶ **ἡ εἰρήνη τοῦ Χριστοῦ βραβευέτω ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις ὑμῶν** — COMMAND: "and let the peace of Christ umpire in your hearts"
    - εἰς ἣν καὶ ἐκλήθητε ἐν ἑνὶ σώματι — "to which you were indeed called in one body"
- καὶ **εὐχάριστοι γίνεσθε** — COMMAND: "and be thankful" (present imperative)
- **Ὁ λόγος τοῦ Χριστοῦ ἐνοικείτω ἐν ὑμῖν πλουσίως** — COMMAND: "let the word of Christ dwell in you richly"
    - ἐν πάσῃ σοφίᾳ διδάσκοντες καὶ νουθετοῦντες ἑαυτούς — "in all wisdom teaching and admonishing one another" (participles; NA28 punctuation)
    - ψαλμοῖς ὕμνοις ᾠδαῖς πνευματικαῖς ἐν [τῇ] χάριτι ᾄδοντες ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις ὑμῶν τῷ θεῷ — "with psalms, hymns, spiritual songs, singing with thankfulness in your hearts to God"
- καὶ **πᾶν ὅ τι ἐὰν ποιῆτε ἐν λόγῳ ἢ ἐν ἔργῳ, πάντα ἐν ὀνόματι κυρίου Ἰησοῦ** — SUMMARY: "and everything, whatever you do in word or in deed, all in the name of the Lord Jesus" (no verb; ποιεῖτε, "do", understood)
    - εὐχαριστοῦντες τῷ θεῷ πατρὶ δι᾽ αὐτοῦ — "giving thanks to God the Father through him"

**Central assertions, one line each:**
- **3:12–13 — command and ground:** as God's chosen, holy and loved, put on the virtues that bear with and forgive, as the Lord forgave you.
- **3:14 — command:** over all, put on love, the bond of completeness.
- **3:15 — command:** let Christ's peace decide among you, the one body; be thankful.
- **3:16 — command:** let Christ's word live among you richly, teaching, admonishing and singing to God.
- **3:17 — summary:** do everything in the Lord Jesus' name, giving thanks to the Father through him.

One second-person imperative governs 3:12–14; two third-person imperatives and one second-person command fill 3:15–16; 3:17 has no main verb `[T]`. **Every participle in the passage concerns "one another" or God; none is turned in on the self** `[I]`, moderate–high.

### 4. Linking Words

- **οὖν, 3:12 — "therefore"** →*therefore*→ from 3:9–11, especially "having put on the new" (3:10) `[T]`. The letter's fifth οὖν (2:6, 2:16, 3:1, 3:5, 3:12); the last three launch the new life (the 2:16–23 dig's count `[S: dig]`).
- **ὡς, 3:12 — "as"**: identity, not comparison — "being what you are" `[I]`, high. Compare 2:20, "as if (ὡς) living in the world" `[T]`.
- **καθὼς … οὕτως, 3:13 — "just as … so"**: pattern and ground together `[I]`. καθώς stands in the letter at 1:6 (twice), 1:7, 2:7 and 3:13 (SBLGNT) `[T]`.
- **ἐπὶ πᾶσιν δὲ τούτοις, 3:14 — "over all these"**: spatial (the outer garment) or of rank ("above all"); the clothing context favours the spatial sense without excluding rank `[I]`, moderate.
- **ὅ ἐστιν, 3:14 — "which is"**: a neuter relative after the feminine ἀγάπη. The neuter is the letter's formula of identification: 1:24 (ὅ ἐστιν ἡ ἐκκλησία, "which is the church"), 1:27, 3:14 `[T]`. Scribes "corrected" it to ὅς and ἥτις (Textual Variants) `[T]`.
- **εἰς ἥν, 3:15 — "to which"**: peace as the goal of the calling `[T]`.
- **καί, 3:15, 3:17 — "and"**: the passage strings its commands with "and"; no contrast, no causal particle after 3:12's οὖν `[T]`.
- **δι᾽ αὐτοῦ, 3:17 — "through him"**: the Son as mediator of thanks, as of creation and reconciliation (δι᾽ αὐτοῦ, 1:16, 1:20) `[T]`/`[I]`.

### 5. Parallels

Prose, with parallel constructions `[T]`:
- **The two third-person imperatives (3:15–16)**: ἡ εἰρήνη τοῦ Χριστοῦ βραβευέτω ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις ὑμῶν / ὁ λόγος τοῦ Χριστοῦ ἐνοικείτω ἐν ὑμῖν — subject with genitive "of Christ", verb, "in you" `[T]`. Synonymous parallelism: the peace and the word are two ways Christ rules his people `[I]`, moderate–high.
- **"In your hearts" twice (3:15; 3:16)** — ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις ὑμῶν `[T]`. The heart is where both peace and song are located `[I]`.
- **"Bearing with one another" / "forgiving each other"**, and **"teaching and admonishing one another"** — the reciprocal pronouns ἀλλήλων and ἑαυτοῖς / ἑαυτούς `[T]`. Here ἑαυτούς means "one another" (as at 3:13), not "yourselves" individually `[S]`, high.
- **"In word or in deed" (3:17)** — a merism for the whole of action `[I]`, high.

### 7. Vocabulary

**Proper-noun and group inventory:** θεός ("God", 3:12, 16, 17), κύριος ("Lord", 3:13, 17), Χριστός ("Christ", 3:15, 16), Ἰησοῦς ("Jesus", 3:17), πατήρ ("Father", 3:17) `[T]`. *Triage:* the five-verse density of divine names is high — routine words, but the alternation is a candidate: God elects (3:12) and receives the songs and thanks (3:16–17); the Lord forgives (3:13) and gives his name (3:17); Christ's peace and word rule (3:15–16) `[T]`/`[I]`. The Spirit is not named; πνευματικαῖς ("spiritual", 3:16) is the only trace (see Tool 11, Ephesians). πνεῦμα ("Spirit") occurs in the letter only at 1:8 and 2:5, and πνευματικός at 1:9 and 3:16 (SBLGNT) `[T]`.

The letter's τέλειος at 1:28 and 4:12 favours completeness as the goal `[I]`, moderate. The Western reading ἑνότητος ("of unity", D* F G, Old Latin) is a scribe's interpretation of the phrase. Eph 4:3 has "the bond of peace" for the same idea `[T]`/`[I]`.

Mutual admonition stands also at Rom 15:14 and 1 Thess 5:14 `[T]`. Teaching was not left only to officers `[I]`, high. διδάσκω occurs in Colossians at 1:28, 2:7 ("as you were taught") and 3:16 `[T]`.

- as the sphere — "in the grace (of God)", which the article favours if it is read;

λόγος and χάρις share a verse in Colossians only at 3:16 and 4:6 `[T]`. The article (𝔓⁴⁶ א² B D* F G Ψ 1739, against א* A C and the majority) would point to "the grace" `[S]`/`[I]`, moderate.

### 8. Translations

1. **3:12 — σπλάγχνα οἰκτιρμοῦ.** ESV "compassionate hearts"; NASB95 "a heart of compassion"; KJV "bowels of mercies" *(KJV from recall)* `[T]` for the moderns. Both moderns keep the sense. Neither can show the link to Philemon's σπλάγχνα (Phlm 7, 12, 20) `[I]`.

2. **3:12 — πραΰτης.** ESV "meekness"; NASB95 "gentleness" `[T]`. "Meekness" connects with Matt 5:5 and 11:29 in English. "Gentleness" is closer in modern usage `[I]`. No difference in substance.

3. **3:12 — ταπεινοφροσύνη.** Both have "humility" here. At 2:18 and 2:23 the ESV has "asceticism" and the NASB95 "self-abasement" `[T]`. **Neither version lets the hearer hear the same word three times** — the overview's Pulpit Note `[S]`; claim audit 2 `[S: audit 2]`.

4. **3:13 — "whoever has a complaint against anyone" (NASB95) / "if one has a complaint against another" (ESV).** The ESV keeps the conditional ἐάν ("if"); the NASB95 makes it a relative `[T]`. The ESV adds "must forgive" at the end, where the Greek has no verb ("so also you") `[T]`. The NASB95's "so also should you" is closer `[I]`.

5. **3:14 — "which is the perfect bond of unity" (NASB95) / "which binds everything together in perfect harmony" (ESV).** The Greek has ὅ ἐστιν σύνδεσμος τῆς τελειότητος, "which is the bond of perfection (completeness)" `[T]`. **Both versions interpret.** The NASB95 makes the genitive an adjective ("perfect bond") and adds "of unity", which comes close to the Western reading ἑνότητος. The ESV turns the noun into a verb ("binds … together") and τελειότης into "harmony" `[T]`/`[I]`. Neither keeps "bond" as the word of 2:19's "ligaments", and neither keeps the τέλειος of 1:28 (ESV "mature", NASB95 "complete") `[T]`.

6. **3:15 — βραβευέτω.** Both have "rule" `[T]`. The umpire image, and the play against 2:18 (ESV "disqualify", NASB95 "keep defrauding you of your prize"), are lost in both — claim audit 2's pulpit note `[S: audit 2]`.

7. **3:16 — the punctuation, in two places** (see The Passage). The ESV has "teaching and admonishing one another in all wisdom, singing psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, with thankfulness in your hearts". It attaches wisdom to the teaching and the songs to the singing — NA28's punctuation. The NASB95 has "with all wisdom teaching and admonishing one another with psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing with thankfulness". It attaches wisdom to the teaching and the songs to the teaching — SBLGNT's punctuation for the songs `[T]`. **The difference is real.** On the NASB95 reading, the songs are themselves the means of teaching and admonition. On the ESV's, they are what is sung `[I]`. The Greek can carry either `[I]`, moderate. The 1:28 parallel (wisdom with teaching) fits both on the first point.

8. **3:16 — ἐν [τῇ] χάριτι.** Both "with thankfulness" `[T]`. The link to 4:6 ("let your speech always be with grace", NASB95; ESV "gracious") is lost `[T]`/`[I]`.

9. **3:17 — word order.** ESV "giving thanks to God the Father through him"; NASB95 "giving thanks through Him to God the Father" `[T]`. The ESV follows the Greek order `[T]`.

10. **3:13, 3:16, 3:17 in the KJV.** The KJV follows the majority readings: "even as Christ forgave you" (3:13), "singing with grace in your hearts to the Lord" (3:16), "giving thanks to God and the Father" (3:17) *(from recall — moderate confidence)* `[I]`. A congregation with older Bibles in hand will hear these differences.

**Ancient versions check.** The moderns agree on the substance throughout. The Vulgate reads *verbum Christi habitet in vobis abundanter* at 3:16 *(from recall — moderate confidence)* `[unverified — check wording]`. No translation-tradition split is claimed.

**Pulpit divergence note.** The pulpit text is the **ESV (Anglicised)**. On the findings:
- **(a)** 3:12's "humility" will not sound like 2:18's "asceticism" — say that it is the same word.
- **(b)** 3:14's "binds everything together in perfect harmony" is a paraphrase: say "the bond of completeness", and point to 2:19.
- **(c)** 3:15's "rule" hides the umpire.
- **(d)** 3:16's "with thankfulness" hides "with grace" (4:6).
- **(e)** On 3:16 the ESV's punctuation (songs sung) differs from the NASB95's (songs as teaching); the preacher can say both are possible.

**Edition check:** no word here has a known Anglicised variant; read from the pew Bible before preaching `[I]`.

### 9. Tone and Feel

**Warm, communal and expansive.** After the drumbeat of 3:5–9, the passage changes key: garments rather than corpses, "beloved", compassion, peace, singing `[T]`/`[I]`. The commands remain imperatives, but their objects are gifts — Christ's peace, Christ's word `[T]`/`[I]`. The rhythm rises through "and … and … and" to a summary that takes in "everything" `[T]`.

**Soundtrack:** a congregation singing in parts — many voices, one hymn. The preacher should let the warmth show without losing the realism of "if anyone has a complaint against anyone" (3:13) `[I]`.

**The Exodus 34:6 formula → 3:12–13** *(moderate as field; low–moderate as allusion)* — Moves 1 and 3.

*Source context:* at Sinai, after the golden calf, the LORD passes before Moses and proclaims his name: "The LORD, the LORD God, compassionate (οἰκτείρμων) and gracious, slow to anger (μακρόθυμος) and abounding in mercy and truth" (Exod 34:6, Swete) `[T]`. The function: God's self-revelation as the one who forgives "iniquity, transgression and sin" (34:7), at the moment Israel has broken the covenant `[T]`/`[I]`.

*Counts:* an οἰκτ- word with a μακροθυμ- word stands in one verse in eight Swete verses: Exod 34:6; Neh 9:17; Ps 85:15; 102:8; 144:8; Joel 2:13; Jonah 4:2; and one other use in the Daniel text the corpus labels "Dat" (4:24) `[T]`. Seven are the formula. χρηστ- joins it at Ps 144:9 (next verse), Wis 15:1 ("you, our God, are kind and true, patient") and Ps 68:17 `[T]`.

*OT-to-OT (Move 3):* the formula is the Old Testament's most repeated confession about God's character `[S]`, high. **One pair of psalms turns it into an ethic.** Psalm 110 LXX says "the Lord is merciful and compassionate (ἐλεήμων καὶ οἰκτίρμων)" (110:4). Its twin, Psalm 111, says the same of the righteous man: "merciful and compassionate and righteous" (111:4), and then "good (χρηστός) is the man who is compassionate (οἰκτίρμων) and lends" (111:5) `[T]`. The human being who fears the LORD comes to bear the LORD's own adjectives `[I]`, high on the wording.

*What it adds:* Colossians uses nouns, not the formula's adjectives, and it does not have ἐλεήμων ("merciful") `[T]`. So the evidence does not reach a deliberate allusion `[I]`. But the field is clear: **compassion, kindness and patience are, in the Greek Bible, the LORD's name**. At 3:13 Paul states the principle Psalms 110–111 enact, "as the Lord … so also you" `[I]`, moderate. **Queued as C54.**

### 12. Genre

Pauline epistle; paraenesis with a virtue list, then directives for worship. Virtue lists, like vice lists, were common in Hellenistic moral writing `[S]`. Reading rule: the list is representative; its five virtues are chosen for living together, and the "worship" directives are about mutual edification as much as praise `[I]`. Third-person imperatives ("let …") address the church through its rulers: Christ's peace and word `[I]`.

### 13. Copycat

**Partly applicable.** No narrative characters; but the passage itself names the pattern: "as the Lord forgave you, so also you" (3:13) `[T]`. The pattern is Christ's own action, not a hero's example `[I]` — see Christological Reading.

### 14. Bible Timeline

This side of the cross, in a church gathered in households. They are chosen and loved already (perfect participle), forgiven already (aorist ἐχαρίσατο), called already (aorist ἐκλήθητε), and now living out the result (present imperatives and participles) `[T]`. The modern church stands in the same place `[I]`. The forms of song have changed; the command to teach and admonish by them has not `[I]`.

### 15. Who Am I?

- **"You" (the Colossians)** — chosen, holy, beloved, forgiven; given complaints to forgive and songs to sing `[T]`. The modern church stands with them `[I]`.
- **"Anyone … against anyone" (3:13)** — the aggrieved and the offender, both inside the church `[T]`.
- **God** — who chose and loves; the Father to whom songs and thanks go `[T]`.
- **The Lord / Christ** — who forgave; whose peace rules and whose word dwells; in whose name everything is done; through whom thanks is given `[T]`.

### 16. So What?

**Stage 1 — the response the author seeks:** a church that wears the character of its Lord towards one another — compassionate, kind, humble, gentle, patient, bearing and forgiving — bound by love, governed by Christ's peace, taught by Christ's word through its own members, singing, and thankful in everything `[I]`.

**Stage 2:**
- **Worldview.** I am chosen, holy and loved before I have done any of it (3:12). The virtues I am to wear are God's own (Rom 2:4; Exod 34:6). I have been forgiven by the Lord (3:13). The church's disputes have an umpire: Christ's peace, not the loudest voice (3:15; 2:18).
- **Stop:** nursing complaints; treating humility as self-punishment (2:18, 23); leaving teaching and admonition to the minister alone; singing without listening to what is sung; separating "spiritual" life from ordinary words and acts.
- **Start:** forgiving particular people for particular complaints; letting Christ's peace settle disputes in the body; letting the word of Christ fill sermons, songs and conversation; giving thanks as a habit, three times over.
- **Motive:** "as the Lord forgave you" (3:13) and "chosen … beloved" (3:12) `[T]`.

**Four audiences.** *For me:* who has a complaint I am keeping? *For a Christian friend:* someone wounded in church — the command presumes real grievances and offers the Lord's forgiveness as both pattern and power; it does not ask them to pretend. *For the church:* are songs and admonition shared among the members, or held by a few? *For an unbeliever:* a community whose life together is grounded in being forgiven; and a God who names himself compassionate and patient.

**Prayer.** *Father, you chose us, made us holy and love us. Clothe us with the compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness and patience of your Son; teach us to forgive as the Lord forgave us; let his peace rule among us and his word dwell in us richly; and make us thankful in everything we say and do, in the name of the Lord Jesus.*

**Grammatical and syntactical features:**
- **The objects of one verb (3:12–14).** The five virtues and love are all accusatives of ἐνδύσασθε `[T]`. Love is the last garment, not a new command `[I]`, high.
- **Present participles of manner (3:13).** Bearing with and forgiving are the way the virtues are worn `[I]`.
- **Neuter relative ὅ for feminine ἀγάπη (3:14).** The letter's formula "which is" (1:24, 27) `[T]`. It identifies; it does not agree `[S]`, high.
- **Third-person imperatives (3:15–16).** Peace and word are the subjects; the readers are to give them room `[I]`.
- **Nominative participles after a dative (3:16).** A loose construction (anacoluthon), common in Paul's paraenesis `[S]`, moderate–high.
- **A verbless summary (3:17).** "Everything … all in the name" — the verb is understood from ποιῆτε `[T]`/`[I]`.

**Sound features:** the five accusatives in asyndeton (3:12) `[T]`; χαρ- in χαριζόμενοι, ἐχαρίσατο (3:13), εὐχάριστοι (3:15), χάριτι (3:16), εὐχαριστοῦντες (3:17) — five words of one root in five verses `[T]`. **Forgiveness, grace and thanks sound alike in Greek** `[I]`, moderate; English cannot carry it.

**Recommended verification:** the genitive τῆς τελειότητος; the sense of ἐν χάριτι; the attachment of "in all wisdom" and of the songs; the referent of κύριος at 3:13.

### Historical and Cultural Background

#### Setting
As for the letter (an imprisonment of Paul; Colossae in Phrygia; Roman Asia) `[T]`/`[S]`. The church met in houses (4:15, Nympha's) `[T]`. Philemon's house is the likely meeting place of the Colossian church (Phlm 2) `[S]`, moderate–high.

#### What the original audience knew
1. **Humility was not a Greek virtue.** In Greek moral writing ταπεινός and its cognates usually meant "base, servile" `[S]`, high. To call humility a garment of the elect was counter-cultural. To find it reclaimed from a rival's ascetic use (2:18, 23) was doubly pointed `[I]`.
2. **Singing in gatherings.** Pliny, writing to Trajan about fifty years later, reports that Christians sang "a hymn to Christ as to a god" `[S]`, high on the report. Jewish synagogues sang psalms; Greek associations sang hymns to their gods `[S]`.
3. **Umpires.** The βραβευτής decided contests at the games; the verb was also used of arbitration in ordinary disputes `[S]`, moderate–high.
4. **Mutual admonition** was a recognised practice in philosophical schools (the Epicureans' "frank speech") `[S]`, moderate. The church practises it as a whole body, not under one master `[I]`.
5. **Household churches mixed slave and free** (3:11; 3:22–4:1; Philemon) `[T]`. Forbearance and forgiveness across those lines were not abstractions (Phlm 15–18) `[I]`.

#### What this changes about how we read
"Bearing with one another" and "if anyone has a complaint against anyone" were said to a church that included Philemon and, soon, Onesimus `[T]`/`[I]`. The virtues are not decorations of private piety. They are what make a mixed household church possible `[I]`, moderate–high.

### Original Audience Reception

#### Canonical audience
A reader coming from Ephesians and Galatians knows Eph 4:1–3, 4:32, 5:18–20 and Gal 5:22–23 `[T]`. Colossians' distinctive marks are "the peace of Christ" as umpire, "the word of Christ" in place of the Spirit-filling of Eph 5:18, and the threefold thanks `[T]`/`[I]`.

#### The first hearers
- **Who:** largely Gentile believers, newly told they are "chosen of God, holy and beloved" — Israel's words `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **Situation:** offered a humility of fasting and visions, a self-appointed umpire, and "teachings of men" (2:18–23) `[T]`.
- **What they brought:** a culture that despised humility; an honour code that made forgiveness a loss of face; worship as performance for the gods `[S]`/`[I]`.

#### Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances
- **Surprising:** that humility, the rival's badge, belongs to the elect — as a garment, not a regimen `[I]`.
- **Comforting:** "beloved" (perfect participle); "the Lord forgave you" `[T]`.
- **Disturbing:** "so also you" — the forgiven must forgive `[T]`.
- **Shocking:** that every member teaches and admonishes, and that songs do it `[I]`.

#### What we bring that they didn't
A habit of reading "let peace rule in your hearts" as an inner feeling that guides personal decisions; worship-style disputes that want 3:16 to settle them; a professionalised ministry of the word; a therapeutic model of forgiveness that is about the self rather than the other `[I]`.

### Biblical-Theological Themes

#### The character of God shared with his people
- **Where:** the virtues of 3:12–13; "as the Lord … so also you" `[T]`.
- **Before:** Exod 34:6–7; Ps 102:8; 144:8–9; Pss 110–111 LXX (the LORD's adjectives given to the righteous) `[T]`.
- **After:** Eph 4:32–5:2 ("be imitators of God"); 1 Pet 1:15–16 `[S]`.
- **Contributes:** the ethic is the family likeness of the forgiven.
- **Confidence:** *moderate–high.*

#### Election and the people of God
- **Where:** "chosen of God, holy and beloved" (3:12) `[T]`.
- **Before:** Deut 7:6–8; 4:37; 10:15; Isa 43:20; 65:9 `[T]` as texts.
- **After:** 1 Pet 2:9 ("a chosen race … a holy nation") `[T]`.
- **Contributes:** a Gentile church addressed as Israel `[I]`.
- **Confidence:** *high on the idea; moderate on Deuteronomy 7 specifically.*

#### Word and song in the assembly
- **Where:** 3:16 `[T]`.
- **Before:** the Psalter as Israel's song-book (its Greek titles: ψαλμός, ᾠδή, ὕμνος) `[T]`; Deut 31:19–22 (Moses' song as a witness to teach Israel) `[S]`.
- **After:** 1 Cor 14:26; Rev 5:9; 14:3; 15:3 (the new song) `[T]`.
- **Contributes:** singing is a means of the word's indwelling and of mutual instruction.
- **Confidence:** *high.*

### Difficult / Contested Verses

#### 3:15 — "let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts"
- **Category:** weaponised (privatised).
- **The difficulty:** often used as a rule for personal guidance — "go with the decision you feel peace about".
- **Rhetorical function:** the arbiter of the body's life: "to which you were called in one body" `[T]`; the answer to the rival "umpire" (2:18) `[T]`.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the peace is Christ's (1:20), the hearts are plural, and the setting is complaints and forgiveness (3:13) `[T]`/`[I]`. The sense is corporate first. Inner assurance may follow, but it is not the topic `[I]`, high.
- **Suggested handling:** address directly; it is a common misreading.

#### 3:16 — "psalms and hymns and spiritual songs"
- **Category:** contested (worship practice).
- **The difficulty:** whether the three terms prescribe Psalms only (exclusive psalmody), distinguish three genres, or overlap.
- **Options:**
  - (a) the Psalter's titles, so the Psalms alone `[S]`;
  - (b) three overlapping words for Christian song, including the Psalms `[S]`;
  - (c) three distinct genres `[S]`.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the titles fact is real (Tool 11). But "spiritual" and "the word of Christ" point to Christ-centred song, and the NT knows new songs (1 Cor 14:26; Rev 5:9). The terms overlap `[I]`, moderate. The verse commands content and purpose — teaching and admonishing with Christ's word — more than genre `[I]`, moderate–high.
- **Suggested handling:** mention briefly; do not let the sermon become a worship-style debate. Audit C56 before leaning on (a).

#### 3:13 — forgiveness and wrongdoing
- **Category:** pastoral.
- **The difficulty:** whether "forgiving each other" requires a wronged person to drop all consequences or to resume trust at once.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the complaint is real (ἔχῃ μομφήν, "has a complaint"); forgiveness is patterned on the Lord's, which deals with sin rather than ignoring it (2:13–14) `[T]`/`[I]`. The passage does not address abuse or civil wrong, and it should not be pressed to forbid protection or justice `[I]`, high.
- **Suggested handling:** preach the command fully. Be explicit pastorally that forgiving is not pretending, and that it does not mean leaving the vulnerable unprotected.

#### 3:17 — "in the name of the Lord Jesus"
- **Category:** misread (formulaic).
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** not a phrase to attach to prayers or deeds, but the authority and allegiance under which everything is done; it covers "word or deed", and it leads into the household (3:23) `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **Suggested handling:** address briefly; make the bridge to 3:18–4:1.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: A virtue list as self-improvement

- **What it looks like:** "Five qualities every Christian should develop."
- **Why it's wrong:** the virtues are put on by those already "chosen, holy and beloved" (3:12), and patterned on the Lord's forgiveness (3:13); without that, the sermon preaches the rival's regimen in a kinder voice (2:20–23; the overview's moralism check).
- **The corrective:** preach the names before the garments, and the Lord's forgiving before ours.

### Pitfall: "Let peace rule" as a guide to private decisions

- **What it looks like:** "If you have peace about it, do it."
- **Why it's wrong:** the peace is Christ's, the hearts are the church's, and the setting is complaints and forgiveness in "one body" (3:13–15).
- **The corrective:** preach Christ's peace as the arbiter of the church's disputes — the true umpire against the false (2:18).

### Pitfall: 3:16 as a worship-style ruling

- **What it looks like:** a sermon that turns on whether hymns or choruses are allowed.
- **Why it's wrong:** the verse's main point is the word of Christ dwelling richly and the members teaching and admonishing one another; the three nouns overlap.
- **The corrective:** ask whether what is sung carries the word of Christ and is sung to one another as well as to God.

### Pitfall: Ministry of the word left to the minister

- **What it looks like:** treating "teaching and admonishing" as the pulpit's task, with the congregation as audience.
- **Why it's wrong:** "one another" (3:16); what Paul does (1:28) the church does.
- **The corrective:** name concrete ways the congregation teaches and admonishes — in song, in conversation, in gentle correction (Gal 6:1).

### Pitfall: Forgiveness as pretending nothing happened

- **What it looks like:** "Just let it go," said to someone seriously wronged.
- **Why it's wrong:** the text assumes a real complaint; the Lord's forgiveness deals with sin (2:13–14), it does not deny it.
- **The corrective:** preach forgiveness as releasing a debt one really holds, patterned on the cross; be explicit that it is not the same as staying in harm's way.
