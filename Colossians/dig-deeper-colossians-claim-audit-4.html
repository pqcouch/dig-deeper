Claim Audit 4: Colossians

Passages audited: Col 1:5–14; 3:3–4; 3:12–16; 3:22–4:1; 4:2–6
Date: 31 October 2026
Version: 1.1 (4 November 2026: history-of-interpretation rows from Patrick’s fifth Logos round)
Purpose: The fourth claim audit on Colossians. It covers the whole queue of twelve candidates:

  • C44–C46, from claim audit 3;
  • C48–C50, from the 1:1–14 dig;
  • C52, from the 3:1–4 dig;
  • C54–C56, from the 3:12–17 dig;
  • C57–C58, from the 4:2–6 dig.

C47, C51, C53, C59 and C60 are record-only and are not audited. Trigger 1 had been armed since the 3:5–11 dig. Patrick deferred the audit until the last three passage digs were done. They are now done (3:12–17, 4:2–6 and 4:7–18, all 31 October 2026), and this audit is required before the Finalise pass on the overview.
Primary texts:

  • SBLGNT with its lemma index, for counts and parsing;
  • NA28 and its apparatus (Logos export) for Colossians;
  • Swete’s LXX, searched by word-beginning and by stem anywhere in the word, with every hit read for sense;
  • Rahlfs (Logos exports) for the Hebrew canon, including both Greek texts of Daniel and the Psalter;
  • the WLC with its lemma index, for the Hebrew.

There is no Rahlfs text of Wisdom, Sirach, the Psalms of Solomon or the Maccabees in the corpus. C46 (Wis 5:5), C49 (Wisdom 17–18) and the 2 Maccabees results under C52 rest on Swete alone.

Method:

  • Two blind auditors, run under Patrick’s two-auditor rule of 16 October 2026.
  • Auditor A took the six single-contact claims, testing wording, rarity, rivals, Greek and Hebrew: C44, C45, C46, C48, C52, C55.
  • Auditor B took the six field, design and cluster claims, testing chance baselines, rank stability and leave-one-out: C49, C50, C54, C56, C57, C58.
  • Each auditor read a brief, a claims file and the corpus only. Neither saw the digs, the overview, the earlier audits, the Logos answers or the other auditor’s report.
  • The main session then reproduced the results each verdict rests on (see Spot Checks).

Positional Frame (Phase 0.6)

What the letter has set up. The twelve claims fall at five points in the letter’s movement [T]:

  • the opening prayer and thanksgiving (1:3–14): the word “bearing fruit and increasing” (1:6, 10), and the Father’s rescue of the readers from darkness into the Son’s kingdom, with a share in “the lot of the holy ones in light” (1:12–14) — C46, C48, C49, C50;
  • the hidden life (3:3–4): “your life is hidden with Christ in God … you also will be revealed with him in glory” — C52;
  • the garments of the new self (3:12–16): the titles “chosen of God, holy and beloved”, the five virtues, “as the Lord forgave you”, and the “psalms, hymns and spiritual songs” — C54, C55, C56;
  • the household code (3:22–4:1): fear, service, reward, and the masters’ Master — C44, C45;
  • the outward face (4:2–6): prayer, the mystery spoken through an open door, buying up the time, speech seasoned with salt — C57, C58.

Why the frame matters for the verdicts. Colossians quotes nothing with a formula. Audits 1–3 set the test that follows, and this audit keeps it: an external source must add something that the letter’s own chains, Paul’s ordinary idiom, or the shared vocabulary of the early church do not already supply. Three of the twelve claims fail on exactly this test, because Paul’s own letters supply the pattern (C52 by 1 Cor 4:5 and Col 1:26; C55 by 2 Thess 2:13 and Eph 1:4; C58 by Eph 6:18–20 and 1 Thess 5:6, 17). A fourth is resolved by the conversion vocabulary that Paul shares with Luke (C46, by Acts 26:18). This audit also adds a second test, which Auditor B made general: a “stable top partner” in the contact scan is what most Pauline passages produce. The partner counts as evidence only if it is specific to the passage and survives the removal of its strongest word.

The Instrument (new finding)

Auditor B tested the scan of the New Testament against Swete before using it.

General null. B drew 150 random Pauline passages at each of three lengths (2, 3 and 5 verses) and recorded the best median rank that any Swete chapter achieved for each passage:

Length Some chapter at median 1 at median ≤ 2 at median ≤ 3
2 verses 54 % 83 % 93 %
3 verses 46 % 77 % 89 %
5 verses 42 % 67 % 90 %

This sharpens audit 3’s finding (63–70 % at median ≤ 2) [S: audit 3]. “Chapter X is the most stable partner, median 1 or 2” is what three Pauline passages in four produce, whatever they say. A rank is evidence only if:

    1. the same chapter does not top unrelated passages with the same key word; and
    1. the rank survives leave-one-out and sense correction.

Silent stem floors. The scan misses several words without warning:

  • μεθίστημι (“transfer”, Col 1:13), which does occur in Swete (Dan 2:21 in both Greek texts; 1 Rgns 6:12; 1 Macc 8:13);
  • every aorist and future of ῥύομαι (“rescue”) — ἐρρύσατο, ῥῦσαι, ῥύσεται — about 180 verses, including Ps 106 LXX:6, 20;
  • φῶς (“light”) in its oblique cases;
  • δέω (“bind”).

ἀρτύω (“season”) is a true absence: no word beginning ἀρτυ- or ἠρτυ- occurs in Swete [T]. The main session independently confirmed the μεθίστημι nil in the 1:1–14 dig, and the ῥύομαι gap at Exod 6:6 (where the scan does not see ῥύσομαι) [T]. Ranks in earlier Colossians digs that depend on these words should be read with this floor in mind (see Recommended revisions).

Claims Audited

Claim C44: Job 31:13–15 behind Colossians 4:1

Source: claim audit 3, Auditor A (new candidate); the 3:18–4:1 dig v1.1, Tool 11 (named as closer than Lev 25). Suggested low–moderate.
Claim: Job’s oath that he did not despise the cause of his male or female slave, “for what shall I do when God rises up … did not he who made me in the womb make him?” (31:13–15). That is the master answerable to God for his slave, under one Maker. The claim says this is closer to Col 4:1 (“you too have a Master in heaven”) than Lev 25:43, 53, whose Greek has ἐν τῷ μόχθῳ (“with toil”).

Findings (Auditor A; reproduced by the main session where marked):

  • The “one Maker” is in the Hebrew only. The WLC reads עֹשֵׂנִי עָשָׂהוּ … אֶחָד (“he who made me made him … One”). The Greek of Job 31:15 has no verb of making and no divine subject. Swete and Rahlfs both read γεγόναμεν δὲ ἐν τῇ αὐτῇ κοιλίᾳ (“we came to be in the same womb”) (reproduced) [T].
  • The Greek shares one word with Col 4:1: κύριος (“lord”). It has no δίκαιον (“what is right”) and no ἰσότης (“fairness”) [T].
  • ἰσότης occurs in Swete only at Job 36:29 and Zech 4:7, neither of them ethical. Its only ethical use is Pss Sol 17:46 (reproduced) [T] — already on record under C47.
  • Rivals at least as close in Greek:
    • Lev 25:42–43, with κύριος and the master’s duty to “fear the Lord your God”;
    • Prov 22:2 and Mal 2:10, which state the “one Maker” idea in Greek (“the Lord made them both”; “did not one God create us?”) [T].

Claim C45: 1 Kingdoms 12:24 and Psalm 18:8–14 LXX behind Colossians 3:22–24

Source: claim audit 3, Auditor B (new candidate); the 3:18–4:1 dig v1.1, Tool 7 / Tool 11.
Claim:

    1. “Fear the Lord and serve him … with all your heart” (1 Rgns 12:24; cf. 12:14) is a formula behind “fearing the Lord … whatever you do, work from the soul (ἐκ ψυχῆς)” (3:22–23). Rated moderate.
    1. Ps 18 LXX:10–14 has the fear of the Lord, “your servant”, and ἀνταπόδοσις πολλή (“great reward”, 18:12). It is said to be the only Swete passage with reward, servant and fear within four verses, and so to stand behind the NT hapax ἀνταπόδοσις (“recompense”, 3:24). Rated low–moderate.

Findings (Auditor A):

  • (i) The formula is real, but it is Deuteronomic, not Samuel’s alone. Fear, serve and heart stand in one Swete verse at 1 Rgns 12:24 (δουλεύω, “serve”) and at Deut 10:12 (λατρεύω, “serve”, with ψυχή, “soul”, as well) [T]. Deut 10:12 is closer to Col 3:22–23, which has both “heart” and “soul”.
  • (ii) The “only” holds, but it is a chance result.
    • ἀνταπόδοσις with a δοῦλος-family word and a fear word within four verses: Ps 18:10–14 only (Swete) [T].
    • But 648 of Swete’s 30,201 verses (2.1 %) already have δοῦλος and a fear word within four verses. On that baseline, about one random set in four of fifteen verses would produce such a hit (Auditor A’s computation; not re-run by the main session).
  • The noun’s sense cuts against the claim. Of the fourteen Swete verses with ἀνταπόδοσις, only 2 Rgns 19:36 and Ps 18:12 clearly mean reward; eleven mean retribution [T]. Colossians uses both sides at 3:24–25: reward for the slaves; “he who does wrong will receive (κομίσεται) the consequences of the wrong” [T].

Claim C46: “The lot of the holy ones in light” (Colossians 1:12)

Source: claim audit 3, Auditor A; the 1:1–14 dig, Tool 11 and Difficult Verses; overview row 13 (moderate).
Claim:

  • Wis 5:5 is the only Swete verse that joins κλῆρος (“lot”) with the holy ones in this sense.
  • OG Dan 7:18–27 (the holy ones receive the kingdom) and Qumran’s “lot of the holy ones” [S] are further background.
  • Acts 26:18 shares six rarer lemmas with Col 1:12–14 in one verse; the next NT rival has four.
  • The holy ones are believers, not angels, on the letter’s own usage.

Rated moderate.

Findings (Auditor A; reproduced where marked):

  • (a) Wis 5:5. κλῆρος with an ἁγι- word in one Swete verse: 1 Chr 24:5, Neh 11:1 and Wis 5:5 only, and only Wis 5:5 places a lot “among the holy ones” (reproduced; the wider κληρ- search also returns κληρονομία verses, which are a different word) [T]. But its holy ones stand beside “the sons of God” and are probably angels [I], moderate.
  • A new and better construction: Ps 15 LXX:3–5. “The holy ones (τοῖς ἁγίοις) in his land” (15:3); “the Lord is the portion of my inheritance (ἡ μερὶς τῆς κληρονομίας μου)” (15:5). It is Swete’s only μερίς (“portion”) governing a genitive κληρ- noun, the construction of εἰς τὴν μερίδα τοῦ κλήρου (reproduced) [T]. In the Hebrew it is the only place where גּוֹרָל (“lot”) and קָדוֹשׁ (“holy”) fall within three verses (WLC; Auditor A). Its holy ones are human [I].
  • The stock doublet. μερίς with κλῆρος in one Swete verse: 11 verses, not 20. With κληρονομία (“inheritance”) counted as well it is 29 verses (main session reproduced 29). The “20” in the queue mixed the two [T]. The 1:1–14 dig already had the correct 11 [S: dig]. The pair is chiefly the Levitical formula “no portion or lot” (Deut 10:9; 14:27–29; 18:1) [T].
  • (b) Dan 7. “Kingdom” and “authority” connect with 1:13, not with the “lot” of 1:12 [I]. Uncertain.
  • (c) Acts 26:18 is stronger than stated.
    • It shares six content lemmas with Col 1:12–14: σκότος (“darkness”), φῶς (“light”), ἐξουσία (“authority”), ἄφεσις (“forgiveness”), ἁμαρτία (“sin”), κλῆρος (“lot”). The next best NT verse shares three, not four (Acts 2:38; 1 Pet 2:9) (reproduced) [T].
    • Random Pauline three-verse passages reach a six-lemma match with some NT verse in 1.3 % of 298 trials (Auditor A; not re-run).
  • (d) Saints, not angels. Plural ἅγιος occurs six times in Colossians (1:2, 4, 12, 22, 26; 3:12). All five besides 1:12 refer to believers (reproduced) [T].

Claim C48: Exodus 6:6–8 behind Colossians 1:12–14

Source: the 1:1–14 dig, Tool 11 and Headline 5 (“moderate on the exodus colouring”). Suggested moderate.
Claim: Exod 6:6–8 has ῥύσομαι ὑμᾶς (“I will rescue you”), λυτρώσομαι (“I will redeem”) and the land given ἐν κλήρῳ (“as a lot”). ῥύομαι with λυτρόω in one verse is said to occur in three Swete verses — Exod 6:6, Ps 68:19 and Hos 13:14 — with only Exod 6 adding κλῆρος.

Findings (Auditor A; reproduced):

  • The count is four, not three. OG Dan 3:88 has it too, “he redeemed us … and rescued us”. Rahlfs confirms the reading (reproduced) [T]. Swete’s Addition C to Esther, printed as one long verse at 4:17, also carries both verbs; in Rahlfs it is 4:17a–z (main session) [T].
  • Exod 6 is not privileged. Three rivals are as close or closer:
    • Deut 9:26–29 has μερίς (“portion”), κλῆρος (“lot”) and λυτρόω (“redeem”) within four verses, of the people brought out of Egypt. It is the only Swete passage with all three in an exodus sense (Swete and Rahlfs; Auditor A). The doubling is the translator’s: he renders נַחֲלָה (“inheritance”) twice. It is echoed in Addition C to Esther (Rahlfs 4:17g–h).
    • Exod 14:30 has the exact aorist ἐρρύσατο (“rescued”), as in Col 1:13 [T].
    • Ps 106 LXX:2–20: λυτρόω (106:2), ἐρρύσατο (106:6, 20), and “he brought them out of darkness” (106:14). With Ps 68 it is the only Swete passage with rescue, redemption and darkness within eight verses [T].
  • In the NT, ῥύομαι and a λυτρ- word stand within twelve verses only at Luke 1:68–79 and Col 1:13–14, both with darkness (SBLGNT; Auditor A) — a second Lukan companion beside Acts 26:18.
  • The 1:1–14 dig’s point stands: the scan cannot see the exodus texts, because the ῥύομαι forms fall outside its stem (see The Instrument) [S: dig].

Claim C49: Wisdom 17:2–18:9 (the plague of darkness retold) as a field for Colossians 1:12–13

Source: the 1:1–14 dig, Tool 11 (“the most stable partner, median rank 2”). Suggested moderate (field).
Claim: Wisdom 17–18 retells the plague of darkness:

  • the lawless are “prisoners of darkness” (17:2);
  • “for your holy ones there was very great light” (18:1);
  • the righteous give thanks (18:2);
  • “your sons” (18:4).

Wisdom 18 is said to be the most stable scan partner of 1:12–14.

Findings (Auditor B; reproduced where marked):

  • The rank reproduces — 1, 2, 3, 3, 1, 2 across the six runs (median 2) [T].
  • It rests on εὐχαριστέω (“give thanks”), and in Wis 18:2 it is the Egyptians who give thanks. They do so for not being harmed by those they had wronged, and “beg pardon” in the same clause (reproduced; Swete 18:1–2) [T]. Without the verb Wisdom 18 falls to median 16 (11 for 1:12–13).
  • Sense corrections: with the false friends ἱκανός (“sufficient”, credited to ἱκανόω) and βασιλεύς / βασίλειος (“king”, “royal”, credited to βασιλεία) removed as well, and the stem floors repaired, Wisdom 18 falls to median 118 (1:12–14) and 109 (1:12–13) (Auditor B; not re-run).
  • The roles are reversed. In Wisdom the holy ones were never in darkness; in Colossians the rescued were (1:13) [I]. Ps 106 LXX:10–14 (“sitting in darkness … he brought them out of darkness”) and Isa 42:7, 16 have the Colossian shape [T].
  • Holy ones: Wis 18:1 has ὅσιοι (“devout”); ἅγιοι comes only at 18:9 [T].
  • The null (The Instrument): a median of 2 is what 77 % of three-verse Pauline passages produce.

Claim C50: The sower (Mark 4:8, 20; Luke 8:13–15) behind Colossians 1:5–11

Source: the 1:1–14 dig, Tool 11; the overview’s row 15 (Gen 1:22, 28 proposed, uncertain), which the dig proposed to widen. Suggested moderate–uncertain (synthetic).
Claim: the word heard, bearing fruit and increasing (1:5–6, 10), with endurance and joy (1:11). Luke 8:15 is the only NT verse with καρποφορέω (“bear fruit”) and ὑπομονή (“endurance”); Luke 8:13 has “with joy”.

Findings (Auditor B; reproduced where marked):

  • The counts hold. καρποφορέω occurs in 8 NT verses, and Luke 8:15 is the only one with ὑπομονή. καρποφορέω with αὐξάνω (“grow”) in one verse occurs only at Col 1:6 and 1:10 (SBLGNT) [T] (main session: the καρποφορέω list reproduces).
  • “With joy” is the wrong soil. In Mark 4:16 and Luke 8:13 it describes the rocky-ground hearers who fall away [T].
  • By overall shared vocabulary, Col 1:5–11 does not stand out against the sower. Luke 8 is closer than Mark 4, and Mark is not a close neighbour (Auditor B’s baseline).
  • Rivals: Acts’ formula “the word of God grew” (Acts 6:7; 12:24; 19:20) [T], and Matt 13:38 (“the field is the world”, beside Col 1:6 “in all the world”) [T].
  • Four of the six NT uses of καρποφορέω outside Colossians are in seed parables [T]. Genesis lacks καρποφορέω entirely, so the sower is a better rival to row 15 than Genesis is to itself.

Claim C52: Deuteronomy 29:29 behind Colossians 3:3–4

Source: the 3:1–4 dig, Tool 11. Suggested low–moderate.
Claim: “the secret things (τὰ κρυπτά) belong to the Lord our God, but the things revealed (τὰ φανερά) to us” is said to be the only Swete verse with both a κρυπτ- and a φανερ- word. Colossians would invert it: the believers’ life hidden in God, to be revealed.

Findings (Auditor A; reproduced):

  • Numbering: Swete prints it as 29:29 (with the English versions); Rahlfs and the WLC have 29:28 [T].
  • The “only” fails. A κρυ- word with a φανερ- word in one Swete verse occurs at Deut 29:29, 2 Macc 12:41 (τὰ κεκρυμμένα φανερὰ ποιοῦντος, “who makes the hidden things manifest”) and 2 Macc 1:33 (not a pair) (reproduced; Swete only for the Maccabees) [T]. 2 Macc 12:41 is closer to Paul’s eschatological sense than Deuteronomy is.
  • φανερόω itself occurs in only two Swete verses (Jer 40:6; 2 Macc 3:28) [T].
  • Paul’s own pattern explains 3:3–4. “The mystery which has been hidden (ἀποκεκρυμμένον) … but has now been manifested (ἐφανερώθη)” (Col 1:26), and the Lord “who will bring to light the things hidden … and manifest (φανερώσει)” (1 Cor 4:5) [T].
