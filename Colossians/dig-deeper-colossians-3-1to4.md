## Headline Findings

1. **3:1 is the positive twin of 2:20, and the two conditionals turn the letter from warning to new life.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Positional Necessity, Structure, Linking Words, Move 4. Εἰ ἀπεθάνετε σὺν Χριστῷ ("if you died with Christ", 2:20) → Εἰ οὖν συνηγέρθητε τῷ Χριστῷ ("if then you were raised with Christ", 3:1); and 3:3 restates the first: ἀπεθάνετε γάρ ("for you died"). ἀποθνῄσκω ("to die") stands in Colossians only at 2:20 and 3:3, and συνεγείρω ("to raise with") only at 2:12 and 3:1 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. The overview identified the pair `[S]`; the 2:16–23 dig built its positional answer on it `[S: dig]` (reached independently). **What the readers died *from* (2:20: the elements, the regulations) is answered by what they were raised *to* (3:1: the things above, where Christ is).** *High confidence.*

2. **The command is grounded in a location — "where Christ is" — and Colossians, unlike Ephesians, does not seat the believers there.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Tool 11 (Ps 110:1; Eph 2:6), Christological Reading. "Seek the things above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God" (3:1). In Eph 2:6 God "raised us up with him and **seated us with him** in the heavenly places"; συγκαθίζω ("to seat with") stands in the NT only at Luke 22:55 and Eph 2:6 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Colossians has the raising (3:1) but places only **Christ** on the throne, and puts the believers' **life** there, *hidden* (3:3) `[T]`. The orientation, not the enthronement, is the readers' share now `[I]`, high.

3. **"Life" is named only here in the letter, and it is defined as Christ.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Repetition, Move 4. ζωή ("life") stands in Colossians only at 3:3 and 3:4 (SBLGNT): "your life is hidden with Christ in God … Christ, your life" `[T]`. The verb ζάω ("to live") stands only at 2:20 ("as if you were living in the world") and 3:7 ("when you were living in them") `[T]`. Between an old way of "living" on either side, the passage names the believer's real life — and it is a person `[I]`. *High.*

4. **Hidden now, revealed then: the passage moves the letter's "hidden and revealed" language from God's plan to the believer's own life.** `[T]` for the words; the design `[S: audit 1]`. Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Tool 11 (Move 4; 1 Cor 4:5), Original Languages. κέκρυπται ("is hidden", perfect) and φανερωθήσεσθε ("you will be revealed", future) stand beside 1:26, "the mystery hidden (ἀποκεκρυμμένον) … now revealed (ἐφανερώθη)", and 1:27, "Christ in you, the hope of glory" → "revealed with him **in glory**" (3:4) `[T]`. Claim audit 1 rated the hidden-and-revealed sequence as a whole low–moderate *as design*, but found that **3:3–4 is the letter's distinctive station: no Pauline parallel has κρύπτω ("to hide") governing the believer's life** `[S: audit 1]`. This run confirms that finding from the index `[T]`. *High on the words; low–moderate on the design of the whole sequence.*

5. **"Not the things on earth" is not contempt for the earth: the same phrase has just been used of what Christ created and reconciled.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Repetition, Move 4, Preaching Pitfalls. τὰ ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς ("the things on earth") stands in Colossians at 1:16 (created in him), 1:20 (reconciled through him), 3:2 (not your mind's object) and 3:5 (the "members" to be put to death) — γῆ ("earth") occurs in no other verse of the letter (SBLGNT) `[T]`. So "earth" here names an orientation and a set of practices (3:5), not creation itself `[I]`, high. This answers the overview's Preaching Trap 7 ("heavenly-minded escapism") from the text.

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book-level:** to keep a young Gentile church walking "in him" as they began, persuaded that in Christ they have everything, and to show what that looks like in speech, household and work (overview) `[I]`.

**Passage-level:** to turn the readers from the rival's regime (2:16–23) to the new life (3:5–4:6), by stating — before any detailed command — where their life now is, where their minds should be, and what will happen to them `[T]`/`[I]`. It answers the rival's offer of heavenly things by another route: the things above are reached by union with the risen Christ, not by visions, angel-worship or self-denial (2:18, 23) `[I]`, moderate–high.

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement:** 2:20–23 asked, "If you have died with Christ to the elementary principles of the world, why, as if you were living in the world, do you submit yourself to decrees?" — and dismissed those decrees as "of no value against fleshly indulgence" `[T]`. That leaves two things unfinished: **(a)** death with Christ has been invoked only negatively — as release from rules; **(b)** "fleshly indulgence" has been named, but no remedy given `[T]`/`[I]`. Earlier still, 2:12 said they were "buried with him in baptism, in which you were also raised up with him" `[T]`.

**Necessity answer — why the passage exists here.**

1. **It completes the pair.** 2:20 used death-with-Christ to free them *from*; 3:1 uses resurrection-with-Christ to direct them *to* `[T]`. The 2:16–23 dig recorded that the two conditionals require each other `[S: dig]` (reached independently).
2. **It supplies the remedy 2:23 denied the rules.** The rules cannot restrain the flesh (2:23); the next thing said is "seek the things above … set your minds" (3:1–2) and then "put to death … the members on earth" (3:5) `[T]`. The remedy is a new orientation from a new life, not a stricter regime `[I]`, high.
3. **It is the hinge into the ethics.** Every command of 3:5–4:6 rests on it: 3:5 begins "therefore" (οὖν) and repeats "on earth" `[T]`.

**Implication for this passage:** it cannot be preached as free-standing heavenly-mindedness. It is the positive half of a contrast with a regime of self-denial, and the ground of the ethics that follow. Preached alone, it floats; preached between 2:20–23 and 3:5, it is the letter's answer to "how then shall we live?" `[I]`.

**Revisit after Tool 11:** Move 2 (Ps 110) adds the throne to the positional answer: the rival offered access to the heavenly realm through angels (2:18); Paul points to the one already seated there, over all rule and authority (2:10) `[I]`.

**Immediately before:** 2:20–23 (above). **Immediately after:** 3:5, "Put to death therefore what is earthly in you" (ESV) — the first concrete command of the new life `[T]`.

**Section boundaries.** The ESV and NASB95 both place the heading "Put On the New Self" at 3:1, grouping 3:1–4 with 3:5–17 `[T]`. The Greek joins 3:1 equally to 2:20 (the matching conditional) and to 3:5 (the "therefore") `[T]`. The passage faces both ways; the overview's "⭐ if preached as the hinge" is right `[I]`.

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 3:1 | Premise and first command | "If you were raised with Christ" → "seek the things above" → "where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God" |
| 3:2 | Second command, with its negative | "Set your minds on the things above, not on the things on earth" |
| 3:3 | Ground (γάρ, "for") | "You died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God" |
| 3:4 | Promise | "When Christ, your life, is revealed, then you also will be revealed with him in glory" |

**Device 1 — a doubled command with a doubled ground.** "The things above" twice (3:1, 2) and "with Christ / with him" twice in the grounds (3:3, 4) `[T]`. The command is stated, located (where Christ is), sharpened (minds, not earth), grounded in the past (you died), the present (hidden) and the future (revealed) `[T]`/`[I]`.

**Device 2 — a time-line in three tenses.** Aorist "you were raised" (3:1) and "you died" (3:3); perfect "is hidden" (3:3); future "will be revealed" (3:4) `[T]`. The commands (present imperatives) sit in the present between them `[T]`.

**Device 3 — "Christ" four times in four verses.** Χριστός stands at 3:1 (twice), 3:3 and 3:4, with "with him" at 3:4 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Every clause is anchored to him `[I]`.

**Clause-Flow Layout** (NA28 wording):

- Εἰ οὖν συνηγέρθητε τῷ Χριστῷ — "if then you were raised with Christ" (premise; aorist)
    - **τὰ ἄνω ζητεῖτε** — "seek the things above" (COMMAND; present imperative)
        - οὗ ὁ Χριστός ἐστιν — "where Christ is"
            - ἐν δεξιᾷ τοῦ θεοῦ καθήμενος — "seated at the right hand of God"
    - **τὰ ἄνω φρονεῖτε** — "set your minds on the things above" (COMMAND; present imperative)
        - μὴ τὰ ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς — "not on the things on earth"
        - ἀπεθάνετε γάρ — "for you died" (ground; aorist)
        - καὶ ἡ ζωὴ ὑμῶν κέκρυπται σὺν τῷ Χριστῷ ἐν τῷ θεῷ — "and your life has been hidden with Christ in God" (ground; perfect)
- ὅταν ὁ Χριστὸς φανερωθῇ, ἡ ζωὴ ὑμῶν — "when Christ, your life, is revealed" (temporal)
    - **τότε καὶ ὑμεῖς σὺν αὐτῷ φανερωθήσεσθε ἐν δόξῃ** — "then you too will be revealed with him in glory" (PROMISE; future)

**Central assertions, one line each:**
- **3:1–2 — command:** "seek / set your minds on the things above", because "you were raised with Christ" and "Christ is there".
- **3:3 — truth:** "you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God".
- **3:4 — promise:** "you will be revealed with him in glory".

The passage has two imperatives and two indicative grounds and ends on a promise, not a command `[T]`. The left-to-right caveat applies: the commands are grammatically main; the grounds carry the weight `[I]`.

### 4. Linking Words

- **Εἰ οὖν, 3:1 — "if then"** →*therefore*→ with an evidential "if" (first-class condition, aorist indicative): "since you were raised" `[T]`/`[I]`, high. οὖν ("therefore") draws on 2:12 ("raised with him") and 2:20 (the matching conditional) `[T]`. The 2:16–23 dig classed 2:20's εἰ as evidential, and 3:1's is the same construction `[S: dig]`.
- **οὗ, 3:1 — "where"**: location of the things above — defined by Christ's presence, not by cosmology `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **μή, 3:2 — "not"**: the negative counterpart `[T]`.
- **γάρ, 3:3 — "for"** ←*for*←: grounds both commands in the death and the hidden life `[T]`.
- **καί, 3:3 — "and"**: links the death (past) to the hidden life (present result) `[T]`.
- **ὅταν … τότε, 3:4 — "whenever … then"**: indefinite temporal clause, then the consequence; τότε ("then") stands only here in Colossians (SBLGNT) `[T]`. The time is certain, the date unstated `[I]`.
- **καὶ ὑμεῖς, 3:4 — "you also"**: the readers share his revealing `[T]`.

### 5. Parallels

Prose with clear parallel construction `[T]`:
- **3:1b / 3:2a**: τὰ ἄνω ζητεῖτε / τὰ ἄνω φρονεῖτε — identical object, two verbs: seeking (the will and pursuit) and minding (the thought and disposition) `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **3:2**: τὰ ἄνω / τὰ ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς — antithetic `[T]`.
- **3:3 / 3:4**: κέκρυπται σὺν τῷ Χριστῷ / σὺν αὐτῷ φανερωθήσεσθε — hidden with him / revealed with him: antithetic in verb, identical in companion `[T]`. *What it shows:* the believer's life follows Christ's own visibility — hidden while he is hidden, revealed when he is `[I]`, high.

**N/A** — epistle, not narrative.

### 7. Vocabulary

**Proper-noun inventory:** Χριστός (four times) and θεός (twice); no places or persons `[T]`. No canonical freight beyond the title "Christ", whose OT freight is carried here by "at the right hand of God" (Tool 11).

**Words used only here in Colossians:** ζητέω ("to seek"), φρονέω ("to set the mind on"), δεξιός ("right"), κάθημαι ("to sit"), κρύπτω ("to hide"), τότε ("then"), and ζωή ("life", 3:3, 4 only); ἄνω ("above") at 3:1, 2 only (SBLGNT) `[T]`. *High.*

**συνεγείρω ("to raise with"), 3:1** `[T]`, *high*. Three NT verses: Eph 2:6; Col 2:12; Col 3:1 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Aorist passive συνηγέρθητε: a completed act, done to them `[T]`. Paul's Romans keeps the believers' resurrection future: "if we died with Christ, we believe that we shall also live with him" (Rom 6:8); "we shall be [united] in the likeness of his resurrection" (Rom 6:5) `[T]`. Colossians states it as past (2:12; 3:1) `[T]`, while keeping the *revelation* of it future (3:4) `[T]`/`[I]`. This difference is widely discussed as a mark of Colossians `[S]`; what the text itself shows is that the "already" is the raising, the "not yet" the revealing `[I]`, high.

**τὰ ἄνω ("the things above"), 3:1, 2** `[T]`, *high*. ἄνω stands in nine NT verses; the substantive "the things above" otherwise only at John 8:23 ("I am from above", ἐκ τῶν ἄνω), with "the Jerusalem above" (Gal 4:26) and "the upward call" (Phil 3:14) in Paul (SBLGNT) `[T]`. The phrase is defined at once by "where Christ is" (3:1) — the "above" is a person's location, not a philosophical upper world `[T]`/`[I]`.

**φρονέω ("to set one's mind on, to think"), 3:2** `[T]`, *high*. Twenty NT verses. **The closest parallel is Phil 3:19–21**: the enemies of the cross "set their minds on earthly things (τὰ ἐπίγεια φρονοῦντες). For our citizenship is in heaven, from which also we eagerly wait for a Savior … who will transform the body of our humble state into conformity with the body of His glory" (NASB95) — φρονέω with "earthly things" stands only in Phil 3:19, and φρονέω with "earth" only at Col 3:2 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. The sequence is the same: mind on earth / heaven where Christ is / his coming / glory `[T]`/`[I]`.

**ἐν δεξιᾷ τοῦ θεοῦ καθήμενος ("seated at the right hand of God"), 3:1** `[T]`, *high*. The overview counts δεξιός with κάθημαι or καθίζω in 22 NT verses `[S]`. Colossians uses the NT formula, ἐν δεξιᾷ ("at the right hand"), not the psalm's ἐκ δεξιῶν (Ps 109:1 LXX) `[T]`. Tool 11.

**ζωή ("life"), 3:3, 4** `[T]`, *high*. Headline 3. "Your life" (3:3) and "Christ, your life" (3:4) — the second is an apposition: Christ *is* the life `[T]`.

**κρύπτω ("to hide"), 3:3** `[T]`, *high on the count*. Perfect κέκρυπται: hidden and remaining hidden `[T]`. "With Christ in God" — the life is with him, and both are "in God" `[I]`. The hiding is security as well as concealment (cf. "kept", 1 Pet 1:4–5) `[S]`, moderate.

**φανερόω ("to reveal, make visible"), 3:4** `[T]`, *high*. In Colossians at 1:26, 3:4 (twice), 4:4 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. In Paul's letters φανερόω is used of Christ's coming again **only here**; elsewhere Paul uses it of the gospel or the mystery made known (Rom 3:21; Col 1:26), of believers "made manifest" before the judgment seat (2 Cor 5:10) and of Christ's first coming (1 Tim 3:16; 2 Tim 1:10) (SBLGNT list read through) `[T]`/`[I]`. The closest uses for the return are 1 Pet 5:4 ("when the Chief Shepherd appears, you will receive the unfading crown of glory") and 1 John 2:28; 3:2 ("when he appears, we will be like him") `[T]`. φανερόω with δόξα ("glory") stands in one verse at John 2:11, Col 3:4, 1 Tim 3:16 and 1 Pet 5:4 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. *What it adds:* the return is described not as arrival from elsewhere but as the *making visible* of one who is already reigning, and of a life already his `[I]`, high.

**ἐν δόξῃ ("in glory"), 3:4** `[T]`, *high*. δόξα in Colossians: 1:11 ("the might of his glory"), 1:27 ("Christ in you, the hope of glory"), 3:4 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. The "hope of glory" (1:27) becomes "revealed … in glory" (3:4) `[T]`/`[I]`.

### 8. Translations

1. **3:1 — ζητεῖτε, present imperative.** NASB95 "**keep** seeking"; ESV "seek" `[T]`. The NASB95 brings out the continuous aspect; the ESV is shorter and not wrong `[I]`.

2. **3:2 — φρονεῖτε, plural.** ESV "Set your **minds**"; NASB95 "Set your **mind**" `[T]`. The ESV keeps the plural address — a church's shared mind (cf. Phil 2:2, 5) `[I]`, moderate.

3. **3:3 — ἀπεθάνετε, aorist.** Both "you **have died**" `[T]`. The Greek is a simple past, "you died" — the death in baptism (2:12, 20) `[I]`. A small loss; "you died" sounds more decisive.

4. **3:4 — φανερωθῇ … φανερωθήσεσθε.** ESV "**appears** … will **appear**"; NASB95 "**is revealed** … will be **revealed**" `[T]`. Both keep the repetition within the verse. **Neither keeps the link to 1:26**: ESV 1:26 "now **revealed**" / 3:4 "appears"; NASB95 1:26 "now **manifested**" / 3:4 "revealed" `[T]`. The 1:24–2:5 dig made the same observation `[S: dig]`. *For the congregation:* the ESV's "appear" is natural English for Christ's coming, but it hides that the *hidden* life will be *revealed* — the hidden/revealed pair of 3:3–4 itself. The NASB95 keeps "hidden … revealed" audible within the passage.

5. **3:4 — "your life" (ESV, NA28 ὑμῶν) / "our life" (NASB95, ἡμῶν).** Textual (Textual Variants). The ESV keeps the second person throughout 3:1–4; the NASB95 introduces a "we" `[T]`.

6. **The heading at 3:1.** Both versions place "Put On the New Self" at 3:1 `[T]`, which joins 3:1–4 to 3:5–17 and separates it from 2:20–23. The Greek ties 3:1 to both (Tool 2). The preacher should read 2:20 with 3:1.

7. **KJV (historic witness):** "If ye then be risen with Christ, seek those things which are above … Set your affection on things above" `[T]`. "Affection" for φρονέω narrows a word of thought and disposition to feeling `[I]`.

**Ancient versions check.** The modern versions agree on "seated at the right hand of God" and "hidden with Christ in God". The **Vulgate** reads *quae sursum sunt quaerite … quae sursum sunt sapite … vita vestra abscondita est cum Christo in Deo* *(moderate confidence — from recall; not in the corpus)* — *sapite* ("savour, think") for φρονέω, with "your" life at 3:3 and, at 3:4, *vita vestra* ("your life") `[unverified — check wording]`. No translation-tradition split claimed.

**Pulpit divergence note.** The pulpit text is the **ESV (Anglicised)**. Bearing on the findings: **(a)** "your life" (3:4) keeps the second person, as NA28 prints it — the congregation hears the promise addressed to them; **(b)** "appears … appear" (3:4) is clear but hides the hidden/revealed pair of Headline 4 — **the preacher may want to say "revealed"**; **(c)** the heading at 3:1 cuts 3:1 off from 2:20 on the page — read across it. On "set your minds" (plural) the ESV is closer than the study text. **Edition check:** no word here has a known Anglicised variant; read from the pew Bible before preaching `[I]`.

### 9. Tone and Feel

**Settled, elevated assurance with quiet urgency** `[I]`. Short clauses, after the long sentences of chapter 2 `[T]`. The two imperatives are present-tense and gentle (no "must"); the grounds are emphatic and final ("you died"); the close is a crescendo — "then you also … with him … in glory" `[T]`/`[I]`.

**Soundtrack:** a clear trumpet line after a long, argumentative passage — not a fanfare, but a call to lift the eyes, ending on a sustained major chord at "in glory". The preacher should let 3:4 land as promise, not exhortation.

**Idea-level:**
- **With Christ.** Raised with (3:1), hidden with (3:3), revealed with (3:4) — the σύν-sequence completed: buried with, raised with (2:12), made alive with (2:13), died with (2:20) `[T]`. Pauline epistle; the opening of the paraenesis (exhortation), framed by indicative grounds `[T]`. ### 14. Bible Timeline

This side of the resurrection and ascension, before the return: Christ is seated (present), the believers have died and been raised with him (past), their life is hidden (present state), and their revealing awaits his (future) `[T]`. ### 15. Who Am I?

- **"You" (the Colossians)** — *direct recipients*: raised, died, hidden, to be revealed `[T]`. The modern church stands with them `[I]`.
- **Christ** — *the subject of every location*: raised, seated, hiding their life, to be revealed `[T]`.
- **God** — the one at whose right hand Christ sits and in whom the life is hidden `[T]`.

### 16. So What?

**Stage 1 — the response the author seeks:** a church that seeks and thinks according to where Christ is, because its life is there; that resists the rival's "higher" spirituality, not by a stricter regime but by a truer orientation; and that waits for its revealing with Christ `[I]`.

**Stage 2:**
- **Worldview.** My life is not first what others can see; it is hidden with Christ in God (3:3). The decisive events of my life are past (I died, I was raised) and future (I will be revealed). Heaven is not a mood; it is where Christ reigns now (3:1).
- **Stop:** seeking spiritual "height" by technique, visions or self-denial (2:18–23); setting the mind on standing, possessions, appetites or reputation as though they were life (3:5); measuring my life by what is visible now.
- **Start:** deliberately orienting desires and thoughts to Christ's reign; treating death-and-resurrection with Christ as the motive for practical change (3:5ff.); living with expectation of his revealing.
- **Motive:** union with Christ — what is true of him is true of me (with him raised, hidden, revealed) `[T]`/`[I]`.

**Four audiences.** *For me:* where is my mind set this week? *For a Christian friend:* the believer whose life looks small and unsuccessful — "your life is hidden"; its glory is not yet visible. *For the church:* "set your minds" is plural — a community's shared orientation (cf. 3:15–17) `[T]`/`[I]`. *For an unbeliever:* a life that is "with Christ" or not; and a day when Christ will be revealed.

**Prayer.** *Father, you have raised us with Christ. Set our minds on the things above, where he is seated at your right hand. Keep our life hidden with him in you, until he is revealed, and we with him in glory.*

- **Evidential conditional (3:1).** εἰ + aorist indicative: "since" `[T]`/`[I]`, high.
- **Present imperatives (3:1, 2).** Continuous: keep seeking, keep minding `[T]`.

### Historical and Cultural Background

#### Setting
As for the letter (an imprisonment of Paul; Colossae in the Lycus valley; Roman Asia) `[T]`/`[S]`.

#### What the original audience knew
1. **The right hand** was the place of honour and delegated authority beside a ruler `[S]`, high. A Gentile audience would hear royal enthronement; a hearer of the Greek Psalms, Ps 110 `[I]`.
2. **"Above" and "below"** were common religious and philosophical categories; a Platonic tradition spoke of the soul's ascent to the higher realm `[S]`. Whether the Colossian rival used such language is not stated; the text shows only visions, angel-worship and self-denial (2:18, 23) `[T]`. Paul's "above" is defined by Christ's presence, not by an ascent `[I]`.
3. **Baptism** as dying and rising (2:12) would have been known from their own initiation `[T]`/`[I]`.
4. **Visibility and honour.** In an honour culture, a life that is "hidden" is a life without public standing `[S]`; the promise of being "revealed … in glory" is a promise of public vindication `[I]`, moderate.

#### What this changes about how we read
"Hidden" is not mystical inwardness; it is a present lack of visible honour, held securely with Christ, to be reversed publicly at his appearing `[I]`, moderate.

### Original Audience Reception

#### Canonical audience
A reader coming from Romans, Ephesians and Philippians knows Rom 6, Eph 1:20–2:6 and Phil 3:19–21 `[T]`; Colossians will sound familiar, with two sharp differences: the raising is past, and the believers are not said to be seated (Headline 2) `[I]`.

#### The first hearers
- **Who:** Gentile believers, baptised (2:12), taught by Epaphras `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **Situation:** offered a route to the heavenly realm through visions, angel-worship and severity to the body (2:18, 23) `[T]`.
- **What they brought:** the wish for spiritual height and assurance `[I]`.

#### Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances
- **Surprising:** that the heavenly realm the rival offered by effort is already theirs by union — they were raised with Christ `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **Comforting:** "your life is hidden with Christ in God" — safe, not lost `[T]`; "you will be revealed with him in glory" `[T]`.
- **Disturbing:** "you died" — the old life is finished, with all its claims `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **Shocking (to a status culture):** that their true life is invisible now `[I]`.

#### What we bring that they didn't
A habit of reading "heaven" as a place we go when we die, rather than the realm where Christ now reigns; a split between "spiritual" and "practical" that the passage does not allow (3:5 follows at once); individualism (the commands are plural) `[I]`.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: Heavenly-minded escapism

- **What it looks like:** "Don't be so attached to this world; think about heaven."
- **Why it's wrong:** "the things on earth" in this letter are what Christ created and reconciled (1:16, 20); the things to be renounced are named at 3:5 (immorality, greed …). The "above" is where Christ reigns, and it produces 3:5–4:1.
- **The corrective:** preach "above" as loyalty to the reigning Christ, worked out in speech, sex, money, home and work (overview Trap 7).

### Pitfall: Preaching 3:1–4 without 2:20–23

- **What it looks like:** a sermon that starts at the ESV heading "Put On the New Self".
- **Why it's wrong:** the "if then" answers 2:20's "if you died"; the passage is the positive alternative to a regime of self-denial.
- **The corrective:** read 2:20–23 first; show the two routes to the "higher" life — the rival's and Christ's.

### Pitfall: Moralism — "think heavenly thoughts"

- **What it looks like:** a technique for focusing the mind on spiritual things.
- **Why it's wrong:** the commands rest on what has happened (raised, died, hidden) and what will happen (revealed). Without the grounds, "set your minds" becomes the very discipline the letter rejects (2:23).
- **The corrective:** spend most of the sermon on 3:1a, 3 and 4; let the commands arise from them.

### Pitfall: "Hidden" as mystical inwardness

- **What it looks like:** "Your real self is a hidden spiritual self deep within."
- **Why it's wrong:** the life is hidden "with Christ in God" — located with a person, not within the self — and it will be revealed publicly.
- **The corrective:** preach security and future vindication: the life is kept, and will be shown.

### Pitfall: Speaking of believers already "seated in heavenly places" from this text

- **What it looks like:** importing Eph 2:6 into Col 3:1.
- **Why it's wrong:** Colossians seats only Christ; the believers' life is hidden with him (3:3).
- **The corrective:** keep the two letters distinct; in Colossians the believer's share now is orientation and hidden life, the glory is at the revealing.
