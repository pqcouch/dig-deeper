## Canonical Position

The NT equivalent of the Tanak block — position in the collection, what it presupposes, what it hands on.

| Field | |
|---|---|
| **Section** | Pauline corpus, fourth of the four "prison" letters in the canonical order (Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, then Philemon at the end of the corpus). NA28 and the English Bibles agree on the order: Philippians before, 1 Thessalonians after. The order runs broadly by length, not by date `[S]`. |
| **Reading implication** | A canonical reader arrives at Colossians having just read Ephesians, and will hear much of it as familiar. That is the danger: the familiar phrases ("head", "body", "fullness", the household code) are here put to a sharper, polemical use against a named rival "philosophy" (2:8). **Read Colossians on its own terms first, and only then alongside Ephesians.** `[I]` |
| **Presupposes** | (1) A gospel already received through Epaphras (1:5–7; 2:6–7) `[T]`. (2) Psalm 110:1, the enthroned Lord at God's right hand (3:1) `[T]`/`[I]` — see the Intertextual Map. (3) Genesis 1:26–27, humanity made κατ᾽ εἰκόνα ("according to the image"), replanted at 3:10 `[T]`. (4) Circumcision as the covenant sign of Genesis 17 (2:11, 13) `[I]`. (5) The Torah's and the Prophets' calendar of "festival, new moon, Sabbath" (2:16) `[T]`. (6) Earlier instructions about Mark, "Barnabas's cousin" (4:10) `[T]`. (7) A letter "from Laodicea" which the church is to read (4:16) `[T]` — not identifiable from the text; its identification (lost letter, or Ephesians) is `[S]`. Each reference checked against the NA28. |
| **Handoff** | Thematic, not lexical. The letter hands on (a) the "Christ is all, and in all" confession (3:11) and (b) a household in which a runaway's master and a fellow-worker named Archippus will reappear in Philemon. Eight names are shared with Philemon — Timothy, Epaphras, Aristarchus, Mark, Luke, Demas, Archippus, Onesimus — verified against the SBLGNT `[T]`. That the two letters went to the same town is `[I]` (Onesimus is "one of you", 4:9). |
| **Neighbours** | Philippians before and 1 Thessalonians after: Timothy is co-sender in all three (Phil 1:1; Col 1:1; 1 Thess 1:1) `[T]`, but the adjacency does no interpretive work. The real neighbour is **Ephesians**, two letters back. The two share a near-verbatim paragraph — Col 4:8 = Eph 6:22, seventeen words in the same order (SBLGNT; NA28 agrees) `[T]` — and three words found nowhere else in the New Testament: ἀποκαταλλάσσω ("to reconcile", Eph 2:16; Col 1:20, 22), ὀφθαλμοδουλία ("external service", Eph 6:6; Col 3:22) and ἀνθρωπάρεσκος ("men-pleaser", Eph 6:6; Col 3:22) `[T]`. Which letter used which is **an open question**: not resolved here, and no finding in this overview depends on it `[S]`. v0.1.1: the literature is divided — dependence of Ephesians on Colossians (Lincoln; Witherington), insufficient evidence for dependence (Arnold, counting verbatim runs; Best), and the older view of one author writing twice (as Furnish reports it) `[S: Logos 1]`. |

## Presenting Situation

**Author:** Paul names himself three times (1:1; 1:23; 4:18), with Timothy as co-sender (1:1) `[T]`. The closing greeting is "in my own hand" (4:18), which implies a scribe wrote the rest `[I]`. Whether Paul wrote it or a later disciple wrote in his name is disputed in modern scholarship `[S]`. The letter's own claim is plain and is the canonical reader's starting point `[T]`.

**Circumstance of the writer:** a prisoner — "for which I have also been imprisoned" (4:3), "my fellow prisoner" (4:10), "remember my imprisonment" (4:18) `[T]`. The place (Rome, Ephesus or Caesarea) is not stated `[S]`.

**Audience:** "the saints and faithful brethren in Christ who are at Colossae" (1:2) `[T]`, a church in a town near Laodicea and Hierapolis (2:1; 4:13, 15–16) `[T]`. Paul has not visited them: he labours for those "who have not personally seen my face" (2:1), which most naturally includes the Colossians `[I]`. They learned the gospel from Epaphras, "one of your number" (1:7; 4:12) `[T]`. They are largely Gentile `[I]`: they were once "alienated" (1:21), the mystery is revealed "among the Gentiles … Christ in you" (1:27), and they were "dead in … the uncircumcision of your flesh" (2:13) `[T]`.

**Occasion:** Epaphras has reported their faith and love (1:4, 8) `[T]`, and Paul sends Tychicus and Onesimus with news and encouragement (4:7–9) `[T]`. Paul's fuller report is *good*: "rejoicing to see your good discipline and the stability of your faith" (2:5) `[T]`. **The letter does not describe a church already captured.** It warns: "so that no one will delude you" (2:4), "see to it that no one takes you captive" (2:8), "let no one act as your judge" (2:16), "let no one keep defrauding you" (2:18) `[T]`. Whether the threat is a present faction or a danger in the air is not settled by the text: Paul names no opponent, and the warnings use an indefinite singular (τις, "anyone", 2:8, 16; μηδείς, "no one", 2:4, 18) `[T]`/`[I]`.

**The rival teaching, as the text describes it (2:8–23)** `[T]`: a φιλοσοφία ("philosophy", 2:8; the only use in the NT, SBLGNT) "according to the tradition of men" and "the στοιχεῖα ('elementary principles') of the world" (2:8, 20); rulings on food and drink and on "a festival or a new moon or a Sabbath day" (2:16); ταπεινοφροσύνη ("self-abasement") and "the worship of the angels" (2:18); "visions he has seen" (2:18); "Do not handle, do not taste, do not touch!" (2:21); severe treatment of the body (2:23). The circumcision paragraph (2:11–13) suggests that circumcision was also in view `[I]`. **What this adds up to — a Jewish mystical piety, a Hellenistic syncretism, local folk religion — is a scholarly reconstruction** `[S]`, and so is the label "the Colossian heresy" `[S]`. The overview works from the features, not the label.

**Pressure:** *Is Christ enough, or does the life of faith need something added — a higher knowledge, a stricter regimen, the favour of angelic powers?* The letter's answer is set out before the warning comes: all the πλήρωμα ("fullness") dwells in Christ (1:19; 2:9), and "in Him you have been made complete" (2:10, πεπληρωμένοι, "filled") `[T]`/`[I]`.

**Purpose:** to keep a young Gentile church walking "in Him" as they began (2:6), by setting before them the Christ in whom creation, reconciliation and the fullness of God are found — so that no rival "philosophy" can persuade them they lack anything — and to show what that sufficiency looks like in speech, household and work `[I]`.

## Structural Arc Map

| Verses | Movement | Function |
|--------|----------|----------|
| 1:1–2 | Greeting | Paul and Timothy to the saints at Colossae |
| 1:3–23 | Thanksgiving, prayer and the Christ-confession | Thanks for the gospel bearing fruit (1:3–8); prayer for "the knowledge of His will" (1:9–14); the poetic confession of Christ as creator and reconciler (1:15–20); its application to the readers, "you … He has now reconciled" (1:21–23) |
| 1:24–2:5 | Paul's labour for the mystery | Paul's suffering and stewardship of "the mystery … Christ in you" (1:24–29); his struggle for Colossae and Laodicea, so that no one deludes them (2:1–5) |
| 2:6–23 | The central charge and the warning | Thesis: "as you have received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in Him" (2:6–7); the fullness and completeness found "in Him" (2:8–15); therefore let no one judge or disqualify you (2:16–19); "if you have died with Christ … why … submit?" (2:20–23) |
| 3:1–4:6 | The life of the risen | "If you have been raised up with Christ" (3:1–4); put off, put on (3:5–17); the household (3:18–4:1); prayer and conduct towards outsiders (4:2–6) |
| 4:7–18 | Envoys, greetings and instructions | Tychicus and Onesimus; greetings from six named companions; the letter exchange with Laodicea; a word to Archippus; Paul's own hand |

**Device and shape** `[T]` unless tagged. The letter is linear, hinged at **2:6–7**, where the opening confession becomes a command. The verb περιπατέω ("to walk") marks the hinge: it stands in the opening prayer (1:10), at the hinge itself (2:6) and twice in the new life (3:7; 4:5), and nowhere else in the letter (verified by lemma, SBLGNT). The phrase ἐν αὐτῷ ("in Him") gathers the first half: **all eight occurrences stand within 1:16–2:15** (1:16, 17, 19; 2:6, 7, 9, 10, 15 — SBLGNT), and ἐν ᾧ ("in whom") adds 1:14, 2:3 and 2:11 (2:12 may mean "in which", of baptism). Two matching conditionals turn the warning into the new life: Εἰ ἀπεθάνετε σὺν Χριστῷ ("if you have died with Christ", 2:20) and Εἰ οὖν συνηγέρθητε τῷ Χριστῷ ("therefore if you have been raised up with Christ", 3:1). The verb συνεγείρω ("to raise up with") appears only at 2:12 and 3:1 in the letter, and ἀποθνῄσκω ("to die") only at 2:20 and 3:3 (SBLGNT).

**The confession of 1:15–20 is the letter's arsenal.** Its vocabulary is redeployed in the polemic of chapter 2, and each pairing below has been checked by lemma against the SBLGNT: πλήρωμα ("fullness") with κατοικέω ("to dwell") — 1:19 → 2:9, the only two occurrences of each word in the letter; κεφαλή ("head") — 1:18 → 2:10, 2:19; ἀρχαί ("rulers") and ἐξουσίαι ("authorities") — created in 1:16, subjected in 2:10, disarmed in 2:15; σταυρός ("cross") — 1:20 → 2:14, its only two occurrences; σῶμα ("body") — 1:18 → 2:17, 2:19. The ground-clause ὅτι ἐν αὐτῷ ("for in Him") stands at 1:16, 1:19 and 2:9 and nowhere else (SBLGNT): 2:9 repeats the confession's own reasoning. `[T]`

**The "hymn".** NA28 sets 1:15–20 as indented poetry, as it prints it `[T]`. The paired openings — ὅς ἐστιν … πρωτότοκος ("He is … firstborn", 1:15 and 1:18b) and ὅτι ἐν αὐτῷ ("for in Him", 1:16 and 1:19) — show two parallel strophes, creation and new creation `[T]`. Calling it a pre-Pauline **hymn** that Paul quotes and edits is a form-critical reconstruction `[S]`. Whether it was sung, quoted or composed for the letter cannot be known from the text.

## Microscript

*All the fullness of God dwells in Christ — creator, head and reconciler of all things — and you have been filled in Him; so walk in Him as you began: let no one take you captive with something "more", but, having died and been raised with Christ, seek the things above and put on the new humanity in every word, relationship and task, with thanksgiving.*

The keynote is **fullness**. The πληρ- family runs through every movement: πληρόω ("to fill") at 1:9; 1:25; 2:10; 4:17; πλήρωμα ("fullness") at 1:19; 2:9; ἀνταναπληρόω ("to fill up") at 1:24; πληροφορία ("full assurance") at 2:2; πληροφορέω ("to be fully assured") at 4:12 — **nine occurrences in nine verses** (SBLGNT, verified by lemma) `[T]`. The family's spread across the letter, from the opening prayer (1:9) to the closing word to Archippus (4:17), is what makes it the spine rather than a stylistic tic. The keynote phrase is ἐν αὐτῷ ("in Him"), and the closing confession is that Christ is τὰ πάντα ("all", 3:11), the phrase the confession used for "all things" created and reconciled (1:16, 17, 20); NA28 brackets the article at 3:11 `[T]`.

**Test against the movements.** 1:3–23: the prayer that they be "filled" (1:9) and the confession of the fullness (1:19). 1:24–2:5: Paul "fills up" afflictions and "fully carries out" the word (1:24–25), so that they reach "full assurance" (2:2). 2:6–23: "in Him the fullness … in Him you have been filled" (2:9–10). 3:1–4:6: the new self "renewed … according to the image" (3:10), "Christ is all" (3:11), the word of Christ dwelling "richly" (3:16). 4:7–18: Epaphras prays that they stand "fully assured" (4:12), and Archippus is to "fulfil" his ministry (4:17). Every movement serves the microscript `[I]`.

Swete's LXX (including the books outside the Hebrew canon) unless stated. The notes give, for each row, what the use does, the rarity count and the closest rival.

**1. Psalm 110:1 (LXX 109:1, "Sit at My right hand") → 3:1 "where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God".** Grounds the "seek the things above" ethic in the enthroned Christ; places the readers' hidden life where he is. *Rarity and rival:* δεξιός ("right") with a verb of sitting: six verses in Swete's LXX; only Ps 109:1 has the enthroned figure. Colossians uses the NT formula ἐν δεξιᾷ ("at the right hand"), not the psalm's ἐκ δεξιῶν: δεξιός with κάθημαι ("to sit") or καθίζω ("to seat") occurs in 22 NT verses (SBLGNT), including Eph 1:20 and Heb 1:3. *Confidence:* high (through the NT formula; not a quotation).

**2. Genesis 1:26–27 (with 5:1) → 3:10 κατ᾽ εἰκόνα τοῦ κτίσαντος ("according to the image of the One who created"); background to 1:15 εἰκών ("image").** The new self is creation restored. Read with 1:15, the "image" humanity was made after is Christ. *Rarity and rival:* The phrase κατ᾽ εἰκόνα ("according to the image") occurs in five LXX verses: Gen 1:26, 1:27, 5:1; Sir 17:3; Hos 13:2 (idols). Rival in wording: **Wis 2:23** shares κτίζω ("to create") with εἰκών ("image"), where Genesis has ποιέω ("to make"). *Confidence:* high (3:10); moderate (1:15 alone).

**3. Isaiah 29:13 (cited by Jesus, Mark 7:7; Matt 15:9) → 2:22 "the commandments and teachings of men".** Places the ascetic rules under the prophet's (and Jesus') verdict on humanly devised religion. *Rarity and rival:* ἔνταλμα ("commandment") with διδασκαλία ("teaching"): **one verse in the LXX — Isa 29:13**. ἔνταλμα occurs in only three NT verses: Matt 15:9; Mark 7:7; Col 2:22 (SBLGNT). The wording is **reordered**: Isaiah has ἐντάλματα ἀνθρώπων καὶ διδασκαλίας, Colossians τὰ ἐντάλματα καὶ διδασκαλίας τῶν ἀνθρώπων. *Confidence:* high.

**4. Psalm 68:16 (LXX 67:17, "the mountain where God was pleased to dwell") → 1:19 ἐν αὐτῷ εὐδόκησεν … κατοικῆσαι ("in Him … was pleased … to dwell"); 2:9.** What the psalm said of God's chosen mountain, the confession says of Christ: the place where God was pleased to dwell. *Rarity and rival:* εὐδοκέω ("to be pleased") with κατοικέω ("to dwell"): **one verse in the whole of Swete's LXX — Ps 67:17**, which also has ἐν αὐτῷ ("in it"). No rival found. Ephesians quotes the next verse but one (Ps 67:19 at Eph 4:8), which shows the psalm was in use in this circle `[I]`. *Confidence:* moderate–high on wording; function moderate — **first target for the claim audit**.

**5. Isaiah 45:3 ("treasures … hidden, unseen") with the wisdom tradition → 2:3 "in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge".** Locates in Christ the hidden treasures that a rival "wisdom" (2:23) claims to offer. *Rarity and rival:* θησαυρός ("treasure") with ἀπόκρυφος ("hidden"): two verses in the LXX — Isa 45:3 and 1 Macc 1:23 (the temple treasury plundered). Isa 45:3 also has ἀόρατος ("unseen"; cf. 1:15–16). **Rivals:** Sir 1:25 ἐν θησαυροῖς σοφίας ("in the treasures of wisdom" — the exact noun pair); Sir 20:30 and 41:14 "hidden wisdom and an unseen treasure"; Prov 2:4–6. *Confidence:* moderate (a wisdom idiom; no single source).

**6. Proverbs 8:22–25 / Sirach 1:4; 24:9 (Wisdom "created … the beginning … before") → 1:15–18 "firstborn of all creation … all things have been created … before all things … the beginning".** Ascribes to Christ the place Israel's wisdom tradition gave to Wisdom — but as Creator, not first creature. *Rarity and rival:* κτίζω ("to create") with ἀρχή ("beginning"): eight verses in the LXX, two of them Wisdom speaking (Prov 8:22; Sir 24:9). **Closest rival in wording is Sir 1:4** — προτέρα πάντων ἔκτισται σοφία ("Wisdom has been created before all things") — which shares the perfect ἔκτισται ("has been created", Col 1:16) and the idea of πρὸ πάντων ("before all things", Col 1:17). *Confidence:* moderate (field, not a single source).

**7. Genesis 49:3; Deuteronomy 21:17 (the firstborn as "the beginning of his strength") → 1:18 ἀρχή, πρωτότοκος ("the beginning, the firstborn").** Firstborn status as priority and inheritance, not as the first item made. *Rarity and rival:* πρωτότοκος ("firstborn") with ἀρχή ("beginning"): four verses in the LXX, of which Gen 49:3 and Deut 21:17 are the firstborn-law idiom (Exod 6:14 and 1 Chr 26:10 are incidental). *Confidence:* moderate (idiom background).

**8. Psalm 89:27 (LXX 88:28, "I also shall make him My firstborn, the highest of the kings of the earth") → 1:15, 18 πρωτότοκος ("firstborn"), with 1:13 "the kingdom of His beloved Son".** The Davidic king as God's "firstborn": rank, not origin. *Rarity and rival:* πρωτότοκος occurs in five verses of the Psalms in Swete; four concern Egypt's firstborn, and Ps 88:28 alone is royal (the Psalms of Solomon add two more, 13:8 and 18:4, of Israel as God's firstborn son). Single word; conceptual. *Confidence:* moderate–uncertain.

**9. Genesis 17:11–14 (σὰρξ τῆς ἀκροβυστίας, "the flesh of your foreskin") → 2:11, 13 "circumcision made without hands … the uncircumcision of your flesh".** The covenant sign answered by a circumcision "made without hands" in Christ. *Rarity and rival:* σάρξ ("flesh") with ἀκροβυστία ("uncircumcision"): seven LXX verses — Gen 17:11, 14, 24, 25; 34:24; Lev 12:3; Jdt 14:10. Colossians **inverts** the genitive (τῇ ἀκροβυστίᾳ τῆς σαρκός). *Confidence:* moderate.

**10. Deuteronomy 10:16; Jeremiah 4:4 (circumcision of the heart) → 2:11.** Conceptual background to the "circumcision made without hands". *Rarity and rival:* Deut 10:16 and Jer 4:4 are the only LXX verses joining περιτέμνω ("to circumcise") with σκληροκαρδία ("hardness of heart"). Deut 30:6 has περικαθαρίζω ("to cleanse") for the Hebrew "circumcise". **No shared wording with Colossians**, which says nothing of the heart. *Confidence:* uncertain (conceptual only).

**11. The cultic calendar — Hos 2:11 (Eng 2:13); Ezek 45:17; 1 Chr 23:31; 2 Chr 2:4; 31:3 → 2:16 "a festival or a new moon or a Sabbath day".** Sets the rival rulings against the calendar of Israel's worship, then calls it "a shadow of what is to come" (2:17). *Rarity and rival:* νουμηνία / νεομηνία ("new moon") with σάββατον ("Sabbath"): **twelve LXX verses** — a stock triad, not a single source. Colossians' order (festival, new moon, Sabbath) matches **Hos 2:11** and **Ezek 45:17** exactly; Chronicles reverses it. NA28 reads νεομηνίας, the SBLGNT νουμηνίας. *Confidence:* moderate (idiom; above ten verses, so not rated above moderate on wording).

**12. Daniel 2:28–30 (Theodotion: God "reveals mysteries … made known") → 1:26–27 the mystery "hidden … now manifested … God willed to make known"; 1:28 "with all wisdom"; 2:2–3.** The long-hidden purpose now disclosed — and disclosed not to a king but to "His saints" and "among the Gentiles". *Rarity and rival:* μυστήριον ("mystery") with γνωρίζω ("to make known"): **three verses in Swete — Theodotion's Daniel 2:28, 29, 30**; 2:30 also has σοφία ("wisdom"). Colossians uses φανερόω ("to manifest"), not Daniel's ἀποκαλύπτω ("to reveal"). Theodotion's text is a later recension `[S]`. *Confidence:* moderate.

**13. Inheritance language — Deut 10:9; 32:9; Num 18:20; with Wis 5:5 → 1:12 "to share in the inheritance (τὴν μερίδα τοῦ κλήρου) of the saints in Light".** Gives Gentile believers the language of Israel's allotted portion. *Rarity and rival:* μερίς ("share") with κλῆρος ("lot, inheritance"): 35 LXX verses — idiom. The exact phrase "the share of the lot" does not occur. **Wis 5:5** ἐν ἁγίοις ὁ κλῆρος αὐτοῦ ("his lot is among the saints") is the closest rival for "the saints". *Confidence:* moderate (idiom).

**14. Isaiah 43:25; Psalm 51:9 (LXX 50:11, "blot out my iniquities") → 2:14 ἐξαλείψας ("having cancelled") the certificate of debt.** Forgiveness pictured as an erased record. *Rarity and rival:* ἐξαλείφω ("to blot out") with sin: nine LXX verses. Colossians blots out a *document*, not sins; Exod 32:32 (blotted out of the *book*) is as close. *Confidence:* uncertain.

**15. "Bearing fruit and increasing" — Gen 1:22, 28? → 1:6, 10 καρποφορούμενον καὶ αὐξανόμενον ("bearing fruit and increasing").** A creation-blessing echo has been proposed `[S]`. *Rarity and rival:* καρποφορέω ("to bear fruit") is absent from LXX Genesis; Genesis pairs αὐξάνω ("to increase") with πληθύνω ("to multiply"). The closer rival is the sower parable, Mark 4:8 (καρπὸν … αὐξανόμενα). *Confidence:* uncertain.

**Citation-light — the key finding for the sweep.** Colossians has **no formula quotation** of the Old Testament: no "as it is written", no "Scripture says" `[T]`. Its use of the Scriptures is allusive and woven into its own sentences, most densely in the Christ-confession (1:15–20) and the polemic (2:11–23). **No source is used twice in a way that survives the rival search**, so there is no live source in the strict sense. Genesis 1 comes nearest (3:10 high; 1:15 moderate), but 1:15 depends on 3:10 to be heard `[I]`. The argument is carried far more by **internal repetition** — the fullness family, ἐν αὐτῷ ("in Him"), the hidden-and-revealed pair, put off and put on — than by external citation. For the sweep, dig-deeper's **Move 2 (book-usage tracking) will find little to track**, and Tool 11's weight should fall on **Move 4 (internal echo)**. Do not manufacture Old Testament allusions to fill this map; the sparseness is the finding `[T]`/`[I]`.

**The density scan** that ranks Old Testament chapters against a book is not yet built for New Testament books; the rarity counts above were made row by row against Swete instead (see Colophon).

## Christological Trajectory

Colossians makes Christ both **ground** and **sphere**, and only then **pattern**.

- **Ground.** Christ is "the image of the invisible God", the one "by" whom, "through" whom and "for" whom all things were created; he is "before all things", and "in Him all things hold together" (1:15–17) `[T]`. He is "head of the body, the church", "the beginning, the firstborn from the dead", in whom "all the fullness" was pleased to dwell, and through whom God reconciled all things, "having made peace through the blood of His cross" (1:18–20) `[T]`. In him "all the fullness of Deity dwells in bodily form" (2:9) `[T]`; θεότης ("Deity") occurs nowhere else in the NT (SBLGNT) `[T]`. At the cross he cancelled the certificate of debt and disarmed the rulers and authorities (2:14–15) `[T]`. He is now enthroned at God's right hand (3:1) and will be revealed (3:4) `[T]`.
- **Sphere.** Believers are "in Him": filled (2:10), circumcised (2:11), buried and raised with him (2:12; 3:1), their life "hidden with Christ in God" (3:3) `[T]`. The σύν ("with") language — "with Christ" (2:20; 3:3), "with Him" (2:13; 3:4) — makes union the engine of the ethics `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **Pattern.** It comes late and is explicit once: "just as the Lord forgave you, so also should you" (3:13) `[T]`. The new self is renewed "according to the image of the One who created him" (3:10); read with 1:15, the pattern is the image himself `[I]`, moderate confidence.

**Moralism check.** Colossians contains its own warning against moralism. The rival teaching *is* a regimen — "Do not handle, do not taste, do not touch!" — and Paul's verdict is that such rules "are of no value against fleshly indulgence" (2:21–23) `[T]`. **A sermon on 3:5–17 that offers a list of vices to avoid and virtues to cultivate, without the death and resurrection of 2:20–3:4, preaches the very "philosophy" the letter rejects** `[I]`. The commands of chapter 3 are addressed to people who "have died" and "have been raised" (3:1, 3), and they are put in clothing language — ἀπεκδύομαι ("to lay aside", 3:9) and ἐνδύω ("to put on", 3:10, 12) — because the new self has already been put on (3:10) `[T]`. Christ is Lord and life first (3:4, "Christ, who is our life"), and pattern second.

**The confession is not an argument for "high Christology" in the abstract.** In the letter it does a pastoral job: it leaves no remainder of "fullness" for angels, powers, visions or regimens to supply `[I]`.

## Preaching Traps

1. **"Firstborn" read as "first-created".** *Bites at:* 1:15–18. It sounds like "Jesus was the first and greatest of God's creatures". The next clause forbids it: "*for* by Him all things were created" (1:16) `[T]`. In the firstborn law (Gen 49:3; Deut 21:17) and the royal psalm (Ps 89:27), πρωτότοκος ("firstborn") speaks of rank and inheritance `[I]`. The opposite trap is to explain the word away and never preach its "from the dead" partner (1:18).

2. **Preaching against a reconstructed "Colossian heresy".** *Bites at:* 2:8–23. It sounds like a detailed lecture on first-century mysticism or syncretism, followed by its modern equivalent. The text gives features, not a system (see Presenting Situation), and the reconstruction is `[S]`. **Preach the features and Paul's answer to them** — the sufficiency of Christ — and let the congregation recognise its own "something more".

3. **2:16–17 as a verdict on the Lord's Day, or on any modern calendar.** *Bites at:* 2:16–17. It sounds like "the Sabbath is abolished" or, in the other direction, an explanation of why 2:16 cannot touch the Sabbath. This is a tribal landmine `[I]`. What the text says is that the observances are "a shadow of what is to come", that "the substance belongs to Christ", and that no one is to *judge* the believer by them (2:16–17) `[T]`. The settled debate is not the passage's question.

4. **The household code (3:18–4:1) either skipped or preached as a free-standing hierarchy.** *Bites at:* 3:18–4:1. The word κύριος ("Lord, master") occurs **nine times in these nine verses** — nine of the letter's sixteen (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Every instruction is set "in the Lord" or "as for the Lord", and the masters "too have a Master in heaven" (4:1) `[T]`. The slave instructions are the longest (3:22–25), and the slave Onesimus is "our faithful and beloved brother" (4:9) `[T]`. This is a pastoral landmine. The pulpit text says "bondservants" (ESV) where the study text says "slaves" (NASB95); the congregation should be told it is the same word, δοῦλος ("slave").

5. **1:24 heard as "Christ's sufferings were insufficient".** *Bites at:* 1:24. It sounds like either a scandal or a hasty denial. The verse speaks of "Christ's afflictions" (θλίψεις, "afflictions"), not his cross, filled up in Paul's flesh "on behalf of His body", and the reconciliation was finished at 1:20–22 `[T]`/`[I]`. It is a hard verse; give it a full dig rather than a sentence. *v0.1.1:* the 1:24–2:5 dig (v1.1) now has the controls and the commentators' readings — the cross (Hendriksen), believers' afflictions for Christ (Bruce), the eschatological measure (O'Brien), and Garland's absence idiom (1 Cor 16:17; Phil 2:30), which Beale criticises `[S: Logos 1]`.

6. **1:23 ("if indeed you continue") preached either as a threat or as nothing.** *Bites at:* 1:21–23. The condition is real, and its object is staying "not moved away from the hope of the gospel" — the same concern as 2:6–7 and 2:18 `[T]`/`[I]`. It is the letter's warning in a single verse, not a separate doctrine of assurance.

7. **"Heavenly-minded" escapism from 3:1–4.** *Bites at:* 3:1–4. It sounds like detachment from earthly duties. The "things above" lead directly to speech, sex, money, marriage, parenting and work (3:5–4:1). Heaven's orientation is shown in earthly relationships `[I]`.

The image in whom creation was made becomes the pattern of the renewed self. *Confidence:* high.

**4.** 1:16 ἀρχαί, ἐξουσίαι ("rulers, authorities") created "through Him" → 2:10 "head over all rule and authority"; 2:15 "disarmed the rulers and authorities". **Develops, then resolves** — ἀρχή ("rule") with ἐξουσία ("authority"): 1:16; 2:10; 2:15 (with 1:13's "domain [ἐξουσία] of darkness"). *Confidence:* high.

**5.** 1:18 κεφαλὴ τοῦ σώματος ("head of the body") → 2:19 "not holding fast to the head, from whom the entire body … grows"; 3:15 "called in one body". **Develops** — the rival teacher's failure is to let go of the head. κεφαλή ("head") only at 1:18, 2:10, 2:19. *Confidence:* high.

**6.** 1:19 πᾶν τὸ πλήρωμα κατοικῆσαι ("all the fullness to dwell") → 2:9–10 κατοικεῖ πᾶν τὸ πλήρωμα … πεπληρωμένοι ("all the fullness … dwells … made complete"). **Replants and applies** — the confession's clause is repeated and turned to the readers. *Confidence:* high.

**7.** 1:20 εἰρηνοποιήσας ("having made peace") → 3:15 ἡ εἰρήνη τοῦ Χριστοῦ βραβευέτω ("let the peace of Christ rule"). **Resolves** — the peace made at the cross becomes the church's arbiter. *Confidence:* moderate.

**8.** 1:21–22 ποτε … νυνὶ δέ ("formerly … yet now") → 3:7–8 ποτε … νυνὶ δέ ("once … but now"). **Replants** — the two "formerly … but now" turns, and the only two occurrences of each word in the letter: the first of reconciliation, the second of conduct. *Confidence:* high.

**9.** 1:26 τὸ μυστήριον τὸ ἀποκεκρυμμένον ("the mystery … hidden") … νῦν δὲ ἐφανερώθη ("now manifested") → 2:3 θησαυροὶ … ἀπόκρυφοι ("hidden treasures"); 3:3–4 ἡ ζωὴ ὑμῶν κέκρυπται … φανερωθήσεσθε ("your life is hidden … you will be revealed"); 4:4 ἵνα φανερώσω αὐτό ("that I may make it clear"). **Develops** — the hidden-and-revealed pair moves from God's plan (1:26), to Christ's treasures (2:3), to the believer's life (3:3–4), to Paul's preaching (4:4). The hiding words (ἀποκρύπτω, ἀπόκρυφος, κρύπτω) and φανερόω ("to reveal") stand only at these places. *Confidence:* high (lexically); the design of the sequence is `[I]`, moderate.

**10.** 1:6, 10 καρποφορέω καὶ αὐξάνω ("bearing fruit and increasing") → 2:19 αὔξει τὴν αὔξησιν τοῦ θεοῦ ("grows with a growth which is from God"). **Develops** — the gospel's growth (1:6) and the believer's (1:10) are the body's growth from the head. *Confidence:* moderate (cognate, not the same lemma).

**11.** 1:22 παραστῆσαι ὑμᾶς ἁγίους καὶ ἀμώμους ("to present you … holy and blameless") → 1:28 ἵνα παραστήσωμεν πάντα ἄνθρωπον τέλειον ("that we may present every man complete"); 4:12 ἵνα σταθῆτε τέλειοι ("that you may stand perfect"). **Develops** — God's presenting (1:22) becomes Paul's aim (1:28) and Epaphras's prayer (4:12). τέλειος ("complete") occurs only at 1:28 and 4:12. *Confidence:* high.

**12.** 1:29 ἀγωνιζόμενος ("striving"); 2:1 ἀγῶνα ("struggle") → 4:12 Epaphras ἀγωνιζόμενος ὑπὲρ ὑμῶν ("labouring earnestly for you"). **Resolves** — Paul's struggle is shared by the church's own envoy. The ἀγών family occurs only at these three places. *Confidence:* high.

**13.** 2:6 παρελάβετε τὸν Χριστόν ("you have received Christ") → 4:17 τὴν διακονίαν ἣν παρέλαβες ("the ministry which you have received") — to Archippus. **Replants** — παραλαμβάνω ("to receive") occurs only at 2:6 and 4:17: the church received Christ; Archippus received a ministry. *Confidence:* moderate.

**14.** 2:13 χαρισάμενος ἡμῖν πάντα τὰ παραπτώματα ("having forgiven us all our transgressions") → 3:13 χαριζόμενοι ἑαυτοῖς … καθὼς καὶ ὁ κύριος ἐχαρίσατο ὑμῖν ("forgiving each other … just as the Lord forgave you"). **Resolves** — God's forgiving becomes the church's own. χαρίζομαι ("to forgive") occurs only in these two verses. *Confidence:* high.

**15.** 2:11 ἀπεκδύσει ("removal"); 2:15 ἀπεκδυσάμενος ("disarmed") → 3:9 ἀπεκδυσάμενοι τὸν παλαιὸν ἄνθρωπον ("laid aside the old self"). **Replants** — Christ "stripped off" the powers; believers have "stripped off" the old self. ἀπεκδύομαι occurs only at 2:15 and 3:9 in the NT, and ἀπέκδυσις only at 2:11 (SBLGNT). *Confidence:* high.

**16.** 2:12 συνηγέρθητε ("you were raised up with") → 3:1 Εἰ οὖν συνηγέρθητε ("therefore if you have been raised up"). **Applies** — the baptismal statement becomes the premise of the new life. *Confidence:* high.

**17.** 2:18 μηδεὶς ὑμᾶς καταβραβευέτω ("let no one keep defrauding you of your prize") → 3:15 ἡ εἰρήνη τοῦ Χριστοῦ βραβευέτω ("let the peace of Christ rule"). **Reverses** — the false umpire is replaced by the true one. Each verb occurs only here in the NT (SBLGNT). **Neither the ESV nor the NASB95 lets the congregation hear it** ("disqualify" / "rule"; "defrauding … of your prize" / "rule"). *Confidence:* high (lexically); moderate as intended wordplay `[I]`.

**18.** 2:18, 23 ταπεινοφροσύνη ("self-abasement") → 3:12 ταπεινοφροσύνη ("humility"). **Reverses** — the same word, false and true: three occurrences, these three verses. Both translations change the English between chapters, and the congregation will not hear the echo. *Confidence:* high.

**19.** 2:11 περιτομή ("circumcision"); 2:13 ἀκροβυστία ("uncircumcision") → 3:11 "no distinction between … circumcised and uncircumcised"; 4:11 οἱ ὄντες ἐκ περιτομῆς ("from the circumcision") — the Jewish fellow-workers. **Develops** — a circumcision made without hands (2:11), a people in which the distinction no longer divides (3:11), and Jewish co-workers named with gratitude (4:11). *Confidence:* high (lexically); the sequence `[I]`.

**20.** 2:7 περισσεύοντες ἐν εὐχαριστίᾳ ("overflowing with gratitude") → 3:15 εὐχάριστοι γίνεσθε ("be thankful"); 3:17 εὐχαριστοῦντες ("giving thanks"); 4:2 ἐν εὐχαριστίᾳ ("with thanksgiving"). **Frames** — the thanksgiving family (εὐχαριστέω, εὐχαριστία, εὐχάριστος) occurs at 1:3, 1:12, 2:7, 3:15, 3:17 and 4:2 — from the opening thanksgiving to the closing exhortations, though not in 1:24–2:5. *Confidence:* high.

**21.** 2:8 κατὰ τὴν παράδοσιν τῶν ἀνθρώπων ("according to the tradition of men"); 2:22 "teachings of men" → 3:22 ἀνθρωπάρεσκοι ("men-pleasers"); 3:23 ὡς τῷ κυρίῳ καὶ οὐκ ἀνθρώποις ("as for the Lord rather than for men"). **Develops** — human tradition in religion (chapter 2), human approval in work (chapter 3). *Confidence:* moderate `[I]`.

**22.** 1:25 πληρῶσαι τὸν λόγον τοῦ θεοῦ ("fully carry out the preaching of the word of God") → 3:16 ὁ λόγος τοῦ Χριστοῦ ἐνοικείτω ("let the word of Christ richly dwell"); 4:3 "a door for the word"; 4:17 ἵνα αὐτὴν πληροῖς ("that you may fulfil it"). **Develops** — the word Paul "fulfils" dwells in the church, is to go out through an open door, and is the ministry Archippus is to "fulfil". *Confidence:* moderate.

**23.** 2:1 "those who are at Laodicea" → 4:13, 15–16 Laodicea and Hierapolis; the letters exchanged. **Resolves** — the struggle "for Laodicea" becomes a practical instruction. Λαοδίκεια ("Laodicea") occurs only at 2:1, 4:13, 4:15 and 4:16. *Confidence:* high.

**Hold 1:3–14 open against chapter 4.** The letter ends by doing what it opened by praying for: Epaphras prays the 1:9 prayer, the church is to "walk in wisdom" (4:5; cf. 1:9–10), and the word that bore fruit "in all the world" (1:6) is to go out through "a door for the word" (4:3). **Addressee differentiation:** the closing names one man, Archippus (4:17), and gives him the letter's two key verbs, "received" (2:6) and "fulfil" (1:25), in a single sentence — a pointed, personal application. Why is not known `[I]`.

## The Text: NA28 Apparatus and Pulpit Notes

### Where the NA28 and the SBLGNT differ in Colossians

A word-by-word comparison of the two editions, verse by verse, finds **six differences** (brackets aside). None changes a count above.

| Verse | NA28 | SBLGNT |
|---|---|---|
| 1:7 | ὑπὲρ ὑμῶν ("on your behalf") | ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν ("on our behalf") |
| 1:22 | ἀποκατήλλαξεν ("he reconciled") | ἀποκατηλλάγητε ("you were reconciled") |
| 2:16 | καὶ ἐν πόσει; νεομηνίας | ἢ ἐν πόσει; νουμηνίας |
| 3:16 | [τῇ] χάριτι | χάριτι |
| 3:22 | ὀφθαλμοδουλίᾳ | ὀφθαλμοδουλίαις |
| 4:12 | Χριστοῦ [Ἰησοῦ] | Χριστοῦ |

- **1:7.** NA28 prints ὑμῶν **against 𝔓⁴⁶ א\* A B D\* F G**, which read ἡμῶν. The ESV follows NA28; the NASB95 follows the older witnesses. Epaphras as Paul's deputy ("our behalf") or as the church's minister ("your behalf").
- **1:22.** SBLGNT follows 𝔓⁴⁶ B. The passive leaves the sentence grammatically broken, which explains why the active looks like a correction `[S]`.
- **2:16.** "and drink" / "or drink"; the spelling of "new moon".
- **3:16.** bracketed article.
- **3:22.** singular / plural "eye-service".
- **4:12.** bracketed name.

### Variants that bear on findings (NA28 apparatus)

- **2:2 — "the mystery of God, Christ".** NA28 prints τοῦ θεοῦ, Χριστοῦ ("of God, Christ"), with **𝔓⁴⁶ B** alone among the Greek witnesses. The apparatus records seven other readings — "of God, which is Christ"; "of God the Father of Christ"; "of the God and Father and of Christ" (the majority text); "of Christ"; "of God" — every one of them reads as an attempt to explain the short text. **This is the reading that most directly identifies the mystery with Christ himself**; both the ESV ("God's mystery, which is Christ") and the NASB95 ("God's mystery, that is, Christ Himself") follow it. A dig on 1:24–2:5 should treat the reading as load-bearing and name its two witnesses. `[T: as NA28 prints it]`
- **2:18 — ἃ ἑόρακεν ἐμβατεύων ("taking his stand on visions he has seen").** The majority text and the corrected Sinaiticus add μή ("what he has *not* seen", as in the KJV). NA28 follows 𝔓⁴⁶ א\* A B D\*. The shorter reading points to visionary experience; the longer to speculation. The crux for the 2:16–19 dig.
- **1:14 — "through His blood".** Added by a few late witnesses (614. 630. 1505. 2464), which the apparatus marks as from Eph 1:7. Absent from NA28, the ESV and the NASB95 (present in the KJV). Not part of Colossians' text; the blood is at 1:20.
- **1:12 — "qualified you" (ὑμᾶς, NA28: א B) / "qualified us" (ἡμᾶς: A C D and the majority).** ESV "you"; NASB95 "us". Then 1:13 has ἡμᾶς ("us") in every witness. The shift between "you" and "us" is part of the passage, and the variant is a scribe smoothing it out `[I]`.
- **3:4 — "your life" (ὑμῶν, NA28: 𝔓⁴⁶ א C D\*) / "our life" (ἡμῶν: B and the majority).** ESV "your"; NASB95 "our".
- **3:6 — "upon the sons of disobedience".** Bracketed in NA28; omitted by 𝔓⁴⁶ B. The ESV omits it in its text; the NASB95 includes it.
- **3:13 — "the Lord forgave you" (κύριος, NA28: 𝔓⁴⁶ A B D\*) / "Christ" (the majority) / "God" (א\*).** Both translations read "the Lord".
- **3:15–16 — "the peace of Christ … the word of Christ".** NA28 reads Χριστοῦ ("of Christ") in both verses; the majority text has "of God" at 3:15, and some witnesses have "of the Lord" or "of God" at 3:16. "The word of Christ" is unusual in Paul `[I]`, and the scribes' substitutions show they noticed it.
- **3:11 — "male and female".** A Western addition (D\* F G 629, Latin witnesses), marked by the apparatus as from Gal 3:28. The scribes heard Genesis 1:27 / Galatians 3:28 behind 3:10–11, which is indirect early support for the Genesis row of the Intertextual Map `[I]`.
- **4:15 — Nympha and "her" house.** NA28 reads αὐτῆς ("her"), with B 0278 6 1739 1881. The majority text has αὐτοῦ ("his", the man Nymphas) and א A C have αὐτῶν ("their"). The name's accent decides the person's sex, and the earliest uncials print no accent. ESV and NASB95 both read "Nympha … her house".

### Pulpit Notes — where the ESV and the NASB95 diverge in a way that bears on a finding

These are notes for the preacher about what the congregation will hear, not translation verdicts.

- **The fullness thread is audible in the ESV at 1:9 ("filled"), 1:19, 2:9 ("fullness"), 2:10 ("you have been filled in him") and 4:17 ("fulfil").** The NASB95 breaks it at 2:10 ("made complete"). Neither translation lets it be heard at 1:24, 1:25, 2:2 or 4:12. **The ESV pulpit text serves the keynote better than the study text does.**
- **1:19.** ESV "in him all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell"; NASB95 "it was the Father's good pleasure for all the fullness to dwell in Him". The Greek names neither God nor the Father. Each translation supplies one, in a different place. The congregation hears the fullness as the subject; the study text makes the Father the subject. *v0.1.1:* the ESV's reading is slightly the likelier on the commentators checked — "the fullness" as God in all his fullness (Wright; Moo) `[S: Logos 1]`; the NASB95's remains defensible. See the 1:15–20 dig (v1.2), which also finds the question of εἰς αὐτόν ("to himself", 1:20) evenly balanced.
- **1:20.** NASB95 "through Him, I say" (NA28's bracketed δι᾽ αὐτοῦ); the ESV omits it. *v0.1.1:* the phrase is probably original on internal evidence (homoioteleuton after αὐτοῦ; Wright; the NET note) `[S: Logos 1]`, so here the ESV follows the shorter, probably secondary text.
- **2:8, 20 — τὰ στοιχεῖα τοῦ κόσμου.** ESV "the elemental spirits of the world"; NASB95 "the elementary principles of the world". **This is the largest interpretive gap in the letter.** The pulpit text has already decided for personal spiritual powers, an interpretation; the study text has decided for principles, an interpretation in the other direction. The Greek allows both `[S]`. Tell the congregation. *v0.1.1:* the ESV's reading is the majority of recent commentary (Arnold), with "physical elements" (O'Brien) and "elementary principles" (Carr, Wink, Forbes) still held; the evidence for a "spirits" sense before the second century is for the association of spirits with the elements (Jub 2:2; 1 En 60:11–12), not for the word `[S: Logos 1]`.
- **2:18, 23 / 3:12 — ταπεινοφροσύνη.** ESV "asceticism … asceticism … humility"; NASB95 "self-abasement … self-abasement … humility". The congregation will not hear that the false "humility" and the true one are the same word (see Echo Table).
- **2:18 / 3:15 — καταβραβεύω / βραβεύω.** ESV "disqualify" / "rule"; the umpire image is lost.
- **3:5 — νεκρώσατε.** ESV "Put to death" (an imperative, as the Greek); NASB95 "consider the members … as dead" (an interpretation that softens the command).
- **3:22–4:1 — δοῦλος.** ESV "bondservants"; NASB95 "slaves". It is one word; see Preaching Trap 4.
- **1:7, 1:12, 3:4, 3:6.** Textual, not translational: see the variants above. The ESV follows NA28's printed text at 1:7, 1:12 and 3:4, and drops NA28's bracketed words at 3:6.

**Chains verified:** about ninety lemmas counted in the letter, and every chain in the Arc Map, Microscript and Echo Table checked by lemma against the SBLGNT word index (the fullness family; ἐν αὐτῷ, "in Him"; περιπατέω, "to walk"; κεφαλή, "head"; σῶμα, "body"; εἰκών, "image"; ἀρχή and ἐξουσία, "rule" and "authority"; the hiding and revealing words; ταπεινοφροσύνη, "humility"; καταβραβεύω and βραβεύω; ἀπεκδύομαι, "to strip off"; συνεγείρω, "to raise with"; χαρίζομαι, "to forgive"; παραλαμβάνω, "to receive"; τέλειος, "complete"; the ἀγών family; the thanksgiving family; κύριος, "Lord"; Λαοδίκεια, "Laodicea", and the others in the Echo Table). Each claimed reference was confirmed to contain its lemma. About fifty NT-wide counts were made from the SBLGNT index (for example θεότης, "Deity", 1 verse; ἀπεκδύομαι 2; ἔνταλμα, "commandment", 3; ἀποκαταλλάσσω 3; the eight names shared with Philemon). **Positive controls:** the first exact-form lemma searches for ποτέ ("formerly") and δογματίζω ("to submit to decrees") returned nothing. Accent-insensitive re-searches returned ποτέ at 1:21 and 3:7, and the index files the verb at 2:20 under δογματίζομαι, so the first results were a search fault, not an absence. The first count of πρωτότοκος ("firstborn") in the Psalms returned none because the hit list had been cut short; the full list gave five verses in the Psalms (with two more in the Psalms of Solomon), including Ps 88:28. The LXX rarity counts were made on accent-stripped word beginnings in Swete, which has no lemma index. **Such counts can under-report** (a word whose stem changes will be missed): the search for περιτέμνω ("to circumcise") with "heart" first returned nothing, because the LXX writes σκληροκαρδία ("hardness of heart") as one word. Treat the LXX counts as floors.
