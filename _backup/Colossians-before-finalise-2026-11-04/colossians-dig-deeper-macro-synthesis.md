## How to Read This Document

Each argument below recurs across **three or more pericopes**. It was found by reading the runs' Headline Findings, Convergent Findings, Book-Overview Tensions and Christological Readings, not by re-working the sixteen tools. The arguments are **emergent, not imposed**. Where a pattern seemed attractive but rested on one or two runs, or was cut down by an audit, it is listed at the end under *Candidate Threads — Not Yet Macro*.

**Confidence key.** The toolkit grades confidence as follows:
- **high** — NT-authorised (a New Testament text cites or fulfils the chain);
- **strong** — converging text-first findings, reached independently in several runs;
- **moderate** — real but looser.

Colossians is an NT letter with no formula quotation, so **no argument here reaches "high" on that criterion**. The strong ones are strong because separate runs, each working from the Greek, kept finding the same thing.

**The audits' rule carries over.** The runs' design claims are kept at their audited ratings. A run's `[S]` stays `[S]`. Each argument says whether it is a claim about **words** (verified by lemma in the runs) or about **design**. Across four audits the words held where they were counted correctly, and the designs mostly needed reframing.

## 1. Nothing Left to Add: The Letter Names the Goods Before the Rival Can Offer Them

**Claim.** Before any warning is given, Colossians puts in Christ, or in the church's hands, everything the rival "philosophy" will claim to supply: fullness, knowledge, wisdom, a worthy walk, and mastery over the powers. The polemic of chapter 2 then cashes what chapters 1–2:5 have already deposited.

**Converging evidence.**
- **The prayer plants the vocabulary** (§1). They are to "be filled" (πληρωθῆτε, 1:9), and 2:10 says they "have been filled" (πεπληρωμένοι).
  - ἐπίγνωσις ("knowledge") stands at 1:9, 10; 2:2; 3:10.
  - σοφία ("wisdom") at 1:9, 28; 2:3; 2:23 (the rival's "appearance of wisdom"); 3:16; 4:5.
  - περιπατέω ("walk") at 1:10; 2:6; 3:7; 4:5.

  `[T]`, high (SBLGNT, by lemma).
- **The gospel's credentials come first** (§1, §3). "The word of the truth" (1:5–6) bears fruit "in all the world". The bracket of hope, gospel, hearing and universal scope (1:5–6 ↔ 1:23) closes before Paul's ministry begins `[T]`, high on the words, moderate–high on the design.
- **The confession is the arsenal** (§2, §5).
  - ὅτι ἐν αὐτῷ ("for in Him") stands in Paul only at 1:16, 1:19 and 2:9.
  - πλήρωμα ("fullness") with κατοικέω ("dwell") stands in the NT only at 1:19 and 2:9.
  - κεφαλή ("head") with ἀρχή ("rule") and ἐξουσία ("authority") stands in one NT verse, 2:10.
  - σταυρός ("cross") occurs in the letter only at 1:20 and 2:14.

  `[T]`, high on the links. *Moderate–high as a measured design* `[S: audit 1]`: 2:8–14 shares 7 of the confession's 23 rarer content words, a level reached by 7 of the letter's 90 six-verse passages.
- **The treasury is in Christ before the first warning** (§4). "In whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (2:3) is followed at once by "I say this so that no one will delude you" (2:4) `[T]`, high.
- **The powers are placed before they are feared** (§1, §2, §5). The ἐξουσία ("domain, authority") of darkness, from which they were rescued (1:13), becomes the created thrones, dominions, rulers and authorities (1:16), the head over every rule and authority (2:10), and the rulers and authorities stripped and paraded (2:15). These are the pair's only three appearances in the letter, plus the fourth ἐξουσία at 1:13 `[T]`, high on the words.

**Confidence: strong.** The links are text-first and were found in five runs independently. The *strategy* — that the order is meant to pre-empt the rival — is `[I]`, moderate–high, and the arsenal design is audited at moderate–high.

**Payoff.** Preach chapter 1 as the church's armoury, not as a theological preface. The series has a natural question for every sermon on chapters 1–2: *what is the "something more" this passage has already put in Christ?*

## 2. Fullness and "In Him": The Keynote Runs Through Every Movement

**Claim.** The πληρ- family (words for filling and fullness) and the phrase ἐν αὐτῷ ("in Him") are the letter's spine. Together they say that Christ is the whole of what God gives, and that union with him is the whole of what believers have.

**Converging evidence.**
- **The πληρ- family stands in nine verses** (SBLGNT, verified by lemma in v1.0):
  - πληρόω ("to fill") at 1:9; 1:25; 2:10; 4:17;
  - πλήρωμα ("fullness") at 1:19; 2:9;
  - ἀνταναπληρόω ("to fill up") at 1:24;
  - πληροφορία ("full assurance") at 2:2;
  - πληροφορέω ("to be fully assured") at 4:12.

  `[T]`. These verses fall in §1, §2, §4, §5 and §12.
- **"In Him" belongs to the first half.** All eight ἐν αὐτῷ fall within 1:16–2:15, five of them in 2:6–15 (§2, §5) `[T]`. ἐν ᾧ ("in whom") adds 1:14 (§1) and 2:3 (§4).
- **Christ is "all" (τὰ πάντα) at the close of the putting-off** (3:11, §8). The phrase takes up the τὰ πάντα ("all things") created, held and reconciled in him (1:16, 17, 20, §2) `[T]`, high on the words.
- **The theme returns in the last instructions.** Archippus is to "fulfil" (πληροῖς) the ministry he "received" (4:17, §12), and Epaphras prays that they stand "fully assured" (4:12) `[T]`.

**Confidence: strong.** The words are high, and the overview's microscript is confirmed by every dig. No run proposed changing it.

**Payoff.** The series has one sentence it can return to every week: *in him the fullness; in him you are filled.* The ESV keeps the thread audible at 1:9, 1:19, 2:9, 2:10 and 4:17. The NASB95 breaks it at 2:10 ("made complete").

## 3. Gift Before Command: The Letter Refuses Its Own Rival's Method

**Claim.** In every movement the imperatives stand on what God has already done, and the runs found the same grammar each time: aorists and perfects for the gift, passives for growth, imperatives last. The letter's own polemic against a regimen (2:20–23) is thereby turned into a rule for preaching its ethics.

**Converging evidence.**
- **§1 (1:1–14).** The prayer's present participles give way at 1:12–13 to aorists: "qualified", "rescued", "transferred". Two of the four participles are passive `[T]`.
- **§3 (1:21–23).** "Holy, blameless, beyond reproach" describe how God presents the reconciled, and the readers are already "founded" (a perfect participle) `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **§5 (2:6–15).** There are two commands on eight indicatives, and every verb in 2:11–15 has God or Christ as its agent `[T]`.
- **§6 (2:16–23).** The regimen is rejected as "of no value against fleshly indulgence" (2:23) `[T]`.
- **§7 (3:1–4).** "Seek" and "set your mind" rest on "you were raised", "you died" and "your life is hidden" `[T]`.
- **§8 (3:5–11).** "Put to death *therefore*" (οὖν, "therefore", 3:5) draws on 3:3. The old self "has been stripped off" (ἀπεκδυσάμενοι, "having stripped off", aorist participle, 3:9) `[T]`. The participles are causal, moderate–high.
- **§9 (3:12–17).** "Put on" (Ἐνδύσασθε, 3:12) takes up "having put on" (ἐνδυσάμενοι, 3:10) — ἐνδύω's only two verses in the letter. The names come before the garments: "chosen of God, holy and beloved" `[T]`.
- **§10 (3:18–4:1).** Every household line stands "in the Lord" or "as for the Lord". Every run's moralism check found the same trap here: a sermon of roles without the Lord is the rival's regimen in a kinder voice `[T]`/`[I]`.

**Confidence: strong** (eight runs, reached independently; the grammar is `[T]`).

**Payoff.** This is the series' homiletic rule: **preach the ground at least as long as the command.** The letter is its own warning against preaching chapter 3 as self-improvement. Seven runs named the same pitfall in different words: "a list without a ground", "think heavenly thoughts", "a virtue list as self-improvement", "the prayer as a ladder", "a sermon of roles".

## 4. Died, Buried, Raised, Hidden, Revealed — "With Christ"

**Claim.** The ethic of chapters 3–4 is driven by union with Christ's death and resurrection, told as one sequence across four passages.

**Converging evidence.**
- **Buried, raised and made alive with him** (2:12–13, §5): συνθάπτομαι ("be buried with"), συνεγείρω ("raise with"), συζωοποιέω ("make alive with"), with σὺν αὐτῷ ("together with Him") (SBLGNT) `[T]`. συνθάπτομαι occurs in the NT only here and at Rom 6:4.
- **The two conditionals** (2:20, §6; 3:1, §7). "If you died with Christ" (Εἰ ἀπεθάνετε σὺν Χριστῷ) is answered by "if then you were raised with Christ" (Εἰ οὖν συνηγέρθητε τῷ Χριστῷ). ἀποθνῄσκω ("to die") occurs in the letter only at 2:20 and 3:3, and συνεγείρω only at 2:12 and 3:1 `[T]`, high.
- **Hidden and revealed** (3:3–4, §7). The life is "hidden with (σύν) Christ in God", and "you will be revealed with (σύν) him in glory" `[T]`. No Pauline parallel has κρύπτω ("to hide") governing the believer's life `[S: audit 1]`.
- **Put off and put on** (3:9–10, §8). This is told as done. The same verb, ἀπεκδύομαι ("to strip off"), was used of Christ stripping the powers (2:15, §5) — its only two NT uses `[T]`.
- **The sequence's limit** (§7). Colossians, unlike Eph 2:6, does not seat the believers with Christ. Their share now is a hidden life `[T]`.

A check made for this synthesis confirms the "with" sequence by phrase: σύν with Christ or "Him", or a σύν- compound of dying or rising, stands at 2:12, 2:13, 2:20, 3:1, 3:3 and 3:4 (SBLGNT). The other σύν of the letter (2:5; 3:9; 4:9) are not about union.

**Confidence: strong** (§5, §6, §7, §8; the words are high).

**Payoff.** Preach 2:11–3:4 as one arc, even across several Sundays. Every ethical sermon in chapters 3–4 can open from one of its six stations. And keep Colossians' restraint: hidden now, not yet seated.

## 5. The Letter's Seams Are Welded With Its Own Words

**Claim.** At each joint between movements, the next passage picks up a rare word or phrase from the one before. The seams are placed, not merely stacked.

**Converging evidence.**
- **1:14 → 1:15 (§1 → §2).** The relative chain runs on: in neither edition is there a full stop at 1:14 `[T]`.
- **1:20 → 1:21–22 (§2 → §3).** "And you" (Καὶ ὑμᾶς) takes up "all things". ἀποκαταλλάσσω ("to reconcile") stands in the NT only at Eph 2:16 and Col 1:20, 22 `[T]`.
- **1:23 → 1:25 (§3 → §4).** "Of which I, Paul, became a minister" is repeated two verses later `[T]`.
- **2:5 → 2:6–7 (§4 → §5).** The first imperative is built from 1:3–23: "walk" with "Lord" (1:10 → 2:6); "as you learned" (1:7) → "as you were taught" (2:7); "the faith" (1:23 → 2:7) `[T]`.
- **2:8 ↔ 2:20, 22 (§5 ↔ §6).** τὰ στοιχεῖα τοῦ κόσμου ("the elements of the world") and "of men" frame the polemic, and δόγμα ("decree", 2:14) → δογματίζομαι ("submit to decrees", 2:20) `[T]`.
- **2:20 → 3:1 (§6 → §7).** The twin conditionals (argument 4) `[T]`.
- **3:2 → 3:5 (§7 → §8).** "On earth" (ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς), with "therefore" `[T]`.
- **3:10 → 3:12 (§8 → §9).** ἐνδύω ("put on"), its only two verses `[T]`.
- **3:17 → 3:23 (§9 → §10).** ὃ ἐὰν ποιῆτε ("whatever you do"), its only two NT occurrences `[T]`. The pick-up is moderate–high, and the whole code as 3:17 applied is moderate `[S: audit 3]`.
- **3:22–4:1 → 4:9 (§10 → §12).** The slave addressed, then the slave named `[T]`.

**Confidence: strong on each weld** — every one is a verified exclusive or near-exclusive contact. **Moderate as a claim that the whole letter was built this way**, since no chance baseline has been run on "seam density" `[I]`. That claim is named as a target for audit 5.

**Payoff.** No passage in the series can be preached as free-standing. Each sermon should open by reading the last verse of the previous unit. The runs' Preaching Units notes (1:14/1:15; 1:21–23 as hinge; 2:20–23 before 3:1) all say this.

## 6. Salvation in a Body, on the Earth: Body, Flesh and Earth

**Claim.** Colossians refuses two opposite errors with one vocabulary: an escapist "heavenly-mindedness" and a regimen that punishes the body. The reconciliation happened in Christ's body of flesh. The church is his body. "On earth" is what he created and reconciled. And the problem was never the body but the flesh.

**Converging evidence.**
- **"In the body of his flesh through death"** (1:22, §3). θάνατος ("death") occurs only here in the letter, and σῶμα τῆς σαρκός ("the body of the flesh") stands in the NT only at 1:22 and 2:11 `[T]`, high.
- **Paul's flesh for Christ's body** (1:24, §4): "in my flesh … for the sake of His body". σῶμα ("body") and σάρξ ("flesh") are crossed from 1:22 `[T]`.
- **σῶμα in three senses** (2:17, 19, 23, §6): substance, church, the physical body. Then σῶμα is set against σάρξ at 2:23: severity to the body does nothing against the flesh. The contrast holds on every reading of the crux `[T]`/`[I]`, high on the words. The play is *moderate* as deliberate `[S: audit 2]`.
- **"On earth"** (§2, §7, §8). ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς ("on earth") stands at 1:16, 1:20, 3:2 and 3:5, and γῆ ("earth") occurs in no other verse of the letter `[T]`, high.
- **The members to be put to death are practices, not limbs** (3:5, §8) `[T]`/`[I]`.

**Confidence: strong** (§3, §4, §6, §7, §8; the words are high; the reading is `[I]`, moderate–high).

**Payoff.** The series can answer both temptations with one word: *body*. Christ died in a body; the church is his body; the body is not the enemy. The two pulpit texts hide this at 2:17 ("substance") and 2:18 ("sensuous mind"). Only the KJV lets the triple "body" be heard, so the preacher has to supply it.

## 7. Israel's Names Spoken Over a Gentile Church

**Claim.** Without quoting the Old Testament, the letter gives a largely Gentile congregation Israel's titles, Israel's inheritance and Israel's covenant sign — each redefined in Christ.

**Converging evidence.**
- **"The saints"** (1:2, 1:12, 1:22, 1:26; 3:12 — §1, §3, §4, §9). Every plural ἅγιοι in the letter means believers. So "the lot (κλῆρος) of the saints in light" (1:12) is theirs: *moderate–high* `[S: audit 4]`.
- **The share and the lot** (1:12, §1). μερίς ("share") with κλῆρος ("lot") is Israel's Levitical idiom, standing in eleven Swete verses `[S: audit 4]`. The idiom is moderate.
- **Inheritance given to slaves** (3:24, §10). κληρονομία ("inheritance") occurs only here in Colossians, and the people Roman law treated as property are addressed as heirs `[T]`/`[S]`. The link to 1:12 is moderate `[S: audit 3]`.
- **A circumcision made without hands** (2:11, §5). The covenant sign (Gen 17) is answered in Christ. The typology passes all four tests in the 2:6–15 run, but as a link the row is rated *moderate* `[S: dig]`.
- **"The chosen of God, holy and beloved"** (3:12, §9). These are Israel's election titles (Deut 7:6–8; the LXX's "beloved" for Jeshurun, with Isa 44:1–2). Moderate as an idea; low–moderate for Deuteronomy 7 as the specific text `[S: audit 4]`.
- **The distinction kept in persons** (4:11, §12). "Of the circumcision" are named with gratitude among the fellow workers for the kingdom `[T]`.

**Confidence: strong** as converging text-first findings (§1, §5, §9, §10, §12). **Moderate on every specific Old Testament source**, since none of them is quoted.

**Payoff.** For a Gentile congregation, the series can say what the letter does: you are given names that were Israel's, because you are in Israel's Messiah. The preacher should claim this from the letter's own words, not from a single Old Testament proof-text; the audits gave none of the proposed sources more than moderate.

## 8. Israel's Lord Named as Christ

**Claim.** In places scattered across the letter, Christ is given what the Greek Old Testament gave the LORD: the place where God was pleased to dwell, the fear owed to the LORD, the LORD's forgiving, and the LORD's character.

**Converging evidence.**
- **The dwelling** (1:19, §2; 2:9, §5). "All the fullness was pleased to dwell" in him. εὐδοκέω ("be pleased") governing an infinitive of κατοικέω ("dwell") with ἐν αὐτῷ ("in it/Him") is unique to Ps 67:17 LXX (Ps 68:16), of God's mountain. The row is moderate–high on wording and moderate on function `[S: audit 1]`. 2:9 adds the strongest deity statement in the letter, θεότης ("Deity", nowhere else in the NT) `[T]`.
- **The fear of the Lord** (3:22, §10). "Fearing the Lord" is directed to "the Lord Christ" two verses later (3:24). This is *moderate–high*, and a shared Pauline move (2 Cor 5:11; Eph 5:21) `[S: audit 3]`. The majority text's θεόν ("God") shows that scribes felt the weight.
- **"Just as the Lord forgave you"** (3:13, §9). χαρίζομαι ("forgive") stands only at 2:13 (God's forgiving) and 3:13 (the Lord's) `[T]`, high.
- **The family likeness** (3:12, §9). Kindness, compassion, gentleness and patience are, elsewhere in Paul, God's and Christ's (Rom 2:4; 12:1; 2 Cor 1:3; 10:1) `[T]`. This is *moderate–high* as the family likeness, with the Exodus 34:6 formula a *low–moderate* field behind it `[S: audit 4]`.
- **The King's firstborn** (1:15, 18, §2). Ps 88:28 LXX (89:27) is the closest partner, *moderate–high* as a verse contact `[S: audit 1]`.
- **Κύριος over every station** (§10). κύριος ("Lord, master") occurs nine times in six verses of the household code — nine of the letter's sixteen — and one word covers the owner and Christ `[T]`.

**Confidence: strong** as a converging pattern (§2, §5, §9, §10). Each instance carries its own audited rating, from moderate to moderate–high; no instance is high on Old Testament wording.

**Payoff.** The series' Christology is not only the confession of 1:15–20. Every ethical passage puts Christ where Israel put the LORD. This is what makes the household code theological rather than merely social.

## 9. The Apostle's Ministry Handed to the Church — and the Close Answering the Opening

**Claim.** What Paul does for the church in chapters 1–2 — praying, giving thanks, teaching and admonishing, speaking the word, labouring for maturity and full assurance — is handed to the church and to its own ministers by chapters 3–4.

**Converging evidence.**
- **Teaching and admonishing** (1:28, §4 → 3:16, §9). νουθετέω ("admonish") with διδάσκω ("teach") stands in only two NT verses, Col 1:28 and 3:16, both "in all wisdom" `[T]` `[S: audit 2]`.
- **Thanksgiving to the Father** (1:3, 1:12, §1 → 3:17, §9). εὐχαριστέω ("give thanks") stands with "(God) the Father" at all three of its occurrences in the letter `[T]`.
- **Prayer returned** (1:3, 9 → 4:2–3, §11). Paul prays for them; they are to pray for him `[T]`.
- **The word handed on** (§11). "A door for the word" (4:3) becomes "your word" (4:6). δεῖ ("must") is Paul's (4:4) and the church's (4:6), its only two uses in the letter (counted by form) `[T]`. The fuller speech chain (3:8–9 → 3:16 → 4:3 → 4:6; §8, §9, §11) is *moderate* as design and a target for audit 5.
- **Epaphras prays the letter** (4:12, §12). He gathers Paul's struggle (ἀγωνίζομαι, "strive"), maturity (τέλειος, "complete"), full assurance (πληροφορέω, "be fully assured") and the will of God (θέλημα) from 1:9, 1:28–2:2 `[T]`, high.
- **Titles and verbs passed on** (§1, §4, §12).
  - Tychicus receives Epaphras's titles (σύνδουλος, "fellow slave", with διάκονος, "minister"; NT only 1:7 and 4:7).
  - The envoys "make known" (γνωρίζω), as God made the mystery known (1:27 → 4:7, 9).
  - Tychicus is to "encourage your hearts" (παρακαλέω with καρδία, "encourage" with "heart"; only 2:2 and 4:8) `[T]`.

**The frame.** The personal material of 1:24–2:5 and 4:2–18 frames the letter: 4:2–18 shares more of 1:24–2:5's rarer vocabulary than any other seventeen-verse stretch. This is *moderate*, and synthetic `[S: audit 1]`. It is also a target for audit 5. Eight of the overview's 23 Draft echoes land in 4:7–18 (§12).

**Confidence: strong** for the handing-over (§1, §4, §9, §11, §12; the words are high). **Moderate** for the frame as a design.

**Payoff.** The last sermons of the series are not an appendix. The letter ends by showing its theology carried by named people: the congregation praying, speaking and admonishing, and its own man praying the letter. Two pitfalls named by the runs belong here: ministry of the word "left to the minister" (§9), and "Paul the hero" (§4).

## 10. "Christ Is All, and in All" Lived Out in Persons

**Claim.** The letter names the divisions of its world, denies them any power to rank in Christ, keeps the categories, addresses them, and then enacts the new order in the people named at the close.

**Converging evidence.**
- **Circumcision** (§5, §8, §12). περιτομή ("circumcision") stands at 2:11 (made without hands), 3:11 ("no … circumcised and uncircumcised") and 4:11 ("of the circumcision", the Jewish fellow workers) `[T]`, high.
- **Slaves** (§8, §10, §12). δοῦλος ("slave") at 3:11 ("slave and free"), then the slaves addressed at the greatest length in the code (3:22–25: 56 of 116 words), then Onesimus. Onesimus is "the faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you" (4:9) — the titles of Epaphras and Tychicus — and "one of you" is said only of him and of Epaphras `[T]`, high.
- **The order of the list** (3:11, §8). It begins "Greek and Jew", the reverse of Gal 3:28 and 1 Cor 12:13, and adds Σκύθης ("Scythian"), its only NT occurrence `[T]`. The ordering is deliberate: *moderate–high*.
- **Superiors restrained** (§10). Every superior is restrained, and slave and master are held under one impartial Lord (3:25; 4:1) `[T]`.

**Confidence: strong** (§5, §8, §10, §12; the words are high).

**Payoff.** 3:11 is not a slogan for erasing distinctions, nor a merely inward truth — both pitfalls are named in §8. The test of the sermon on 3:11 is the sermon on 4:7–18: who is called brother, who is welcomed (Mark), and who is "one of you".

## 11. The Rival's Words Answered, and Two Reclaimed

**Claim.** The ethics of chapter 3 answer the polemic of chapter 2 with some of its own vocabulary: the false umpire replaced by the true, and the rival's "humility" and "word of wisdom" reclaimed.

**Converging evidence.**
- **The umpire.** "Let no one disqualify you" (καταβραβευέτω, 2:18, §6) is answered by "let the peace of Christ rule" (βραβευέτω, 3:15, §9). Each verb occurs once in the NT, and the compound-against-simple play is *moderate–high* `[S: audit 1]` `[S: audit 2]`.
- **Humility.** ταπεινοφροσύνη is "self-abasement" at 2:18 and 2:23, and "humility" at 3:12 — its three occurrences. *Moderate* as reclaiming `[S: audit 2]`.
- **Word and wisdom.** λόγος ("word") with σοφία ("wisdom") stands at 2:23 and 3:16 only. High on the words; *moderate* as reversal `[S: audit 2]`.
- **The decrees.** δόγμα ("decree") cancelled at 2:14 (§5) → "why do you submit to decrees?" (δογματίζομαι, 2:20, §6) `[T]`.
- **Wisdom.** σοφία runs 1:28 → 2:3 → 2:23 → 3:16 (§4, §6, §9) `[T]`.

**Confidence: moderate.** The specific pairs are firm, but claim audit 2 rated 3:12–16 as a *designed reversal of the whole* of 2:18–23 **low–moderate**: a control passage showed as many reversals. The argument is kept to its pairs.

## 12. The Evidence That Held: How Colossians Argues, and What the Sweep Taught the Method

**Claim.** Colossians argues by **internal repetition**, not external citation. The sweep's own record shows which kinds of evidence carried weight. This is the epistemic finding, and it governs every sermon in the series.

**Converging evidence.**
- **Citation-light.** There is no formula quotation. Every run's Tool 11 found its yield in Move 4 (internal echo) rather than in the Old Testament: §1 found fifteen forward echoes against no external source. The Echo Table grew from 23 to 70 rows, and the Intertextual Map ended *thinner* — seventeen proposals set aside. **No live Old Testament source survived** in the strict sense `[S: audit 1–4]`.
- **The Ephesian parallel is letter-wide.** There are clusters in seven of the twelve passages (§3, §4, §5, §8, §9, §11, §12), including NT-exclusive pairings and clauses (§9, §11); two were measured against other Pauline passages (Eph 3:1–13 at §4; Eph 2 at §5). The direction of dependence is open `[S: Logos 1]`. Several variants harmonise *to* Ephesians (1:14; 3:21; 4:3).
- **What held and what did not.** Across four audits and fifty-eight claims, every exclusive *wording* contact held where it was correctly counted. Most rarity "only"s and "most stable partner" claims needed correcting, and design and field claims mostly needed reframing `[S: audit 1–4]`.
- **The scan's limits.** Some Swete chapter reaches median rank 2 or better for 67–83 % of random Pauline passages, and the scan is silent on several stems `[S: audit 4]`. A scan rank by itself is not evidence; that was learnt from Colossians.
- **The pulpit text.** The declared pulpit text (ESV, Anglicised) kept the letter's repeated words better than the study text more often than not (§2, §4, §11, §12). It hid them at 2:17, 2:18, 3:12, 3:15, 4:6 and 4:7 `[S: dig]`.

**Confidence: strong as a measured finding** (all twelve runs; four audits).

**Payoff.**
- **For the preacher:** follow the letter's own repetitions and let the congregation hear them, and do not import Old Testament allusions the letter does not make.
- **For the method:** the sweep confirms the rule now written into the gate — exclusive wording contacts are where this kind of letter can be trusted, and patterns need a baseline.

## What This Means for the Series as a Whole

Colossians makes one claim and lets every passage serve it: **all the fullness of God is in Christ, and in him you have been filled.** So nothing remains for a philosophy, a regimen or a power to add (arguments 1–2).

The letter makes that claim *before* the warning, and then shows that the life which follows is union, not achievement: died, buried, raised and hidden with him, putting on what has already been put on (arguments 3–4). It welds each passage to the last (argument 5). It refuses both escape from the body and war on it (argument 6). It gives Gentiles Israel's names and gives Christ the LORD's place (arguments 7–8). And it ends by handing its own ministry to the church and showing its theology in named people — a slave called brother, Jewish co-workers, a founder praying the letter (arguments 9–10).

It argues almost entirely from its own words, which the congregation needs help to hear (arguments 11–12). The preacher's discipline for the series follows from the letter itself:
- preach the ground as long as the command;
- read the last verse of the previous unit first;
- supply the repeated Greek words where the English hides them;
- leave out the Old Testament allusions the letter does not make.
