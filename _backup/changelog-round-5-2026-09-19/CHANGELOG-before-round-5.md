*Revised September 2026, round 4 (the toolkit-wide round: original script with English gloss). One amendment reaches this skill from a round that spans all four skills of the preaching toolkit — `book-overview`, `dig-deeper`, `point-purpose`, `sermon-review` — the first round to treat them as one system rather than fixing this skill alone. **(S)** A new section, **Original languages — script and gloss**, requires every Hebrew or Greek word to be given in its own script with an English gloss in brackets immediately after, at every occurrence rather than the first; the gloss is the NASB95 rendering where the word is discussed as it stands in a verse and a lexical gloss where it is discussed as a word; the form that stands in the text is given where the form is the point and the lemma where the lexeme is the point. **Transliteration is retained only where the sound carries the argument** — paronomasia, assonance, alliteration, a name-pun — and then script, gloss and transliteration are given together. `references/extensions/original-languages.md` gains the same rule at the head, and a Key Principle records it; the manuscript stage of `sermon-review` deliberately inverts the order, because those pages are read aloud, and the rule says so here to stop the inversion being carried back. Rationale: the user is learning Hebrew and Greek, and a transliteration teaches nothing about the script he is trying to read. **The amendment could not be installed in `SKILL.md` alone.** The Consistency Contract instructs every run to match the worked example, so the examples are operative instruction rather than illustration; measured before the round, `psalm-33-worked.md` contained **zero** Hebrew characters against 49 transliteration marks and `romans-8-31to39-worked.md` zero Hebrew and 35 Greek against 22 marks. Both were rewritten with every word checked against the corpus, and the checking produced three findings of its own. **Psalm 33's second Headline Finding was wrong**: it claimed "all" appears five times in vv.1–9, counting "the earth is **full**" at v.5, where the Hebrew מָלְאָה הָאָרֶץ contains no כֹּל at all — the count had been made in English and is four, and the example now carries the correction as its own worked demonstration of gate item (e3). **An open question was closed**: נֵד ("heap") stands in six verses of the whole Hebrew Bible (Exod 15:8; Josh 3:13, 16; Isa 17:11; Ps 33:7; Ps 78:13), so the rarity claim is now checked rather than impressionistic, and the Psalter's second occurrence at Ps 78:13 falls in exactly the exodus-rehearsal psalm the Move 2 note had only guessed at. **Two Romans claims were verified canon-wide**: ὑπερνικάω stands once in the New Testament, and χωρίζω twice in Romans, both inside this passage. Both examples gain the header block (primary texts, study text, pulpit text N/A) and Psalm 33 gains the Text-First Declaration it predated.*

*Revised September 2026, round 3 (extent, two-pass diff, per-book checks, filing). Four amendments, K–N, arising from the synoptic run on Isa 36:1–39:8 ‖ 2 Kgs 18:13–20:21 — the mode's first **open** direction-of-dependence case, and the one that could have broken it. **Amendment G comes off trial:** the Phase 0.5d opening now reads "adopted September 2026; trial completed 11 September 2026", records honestly that the evidence base is two runs on one library, and keeps the withdrawal test. **(K)** Point 7's filing rule assumed a shared parent folder, which only the Torah pairs have; most known pairs (Isa ‖ Kgs, 2 Sam ‖ Ps, Obad ‖ Jer, Sam–Kgs ‖ Chr) share none. The report now goes at the level above both where a shared parent exists, and otherwise into **one** book's folder with a one-line pointer page in the other's, the host chosen by where the *unique* material sits — never two copies, which drift. **(L)** The mechanical diff runs in **two passes**: consonantal, then skeletal with waw, yod and word-final *he* deleted, so plene/defective spelling collapses to identity. A one-pass consonantal diff of the Hezekiah pair returned 71 of 76 aligned units as differing and a mean similarity of 0.595; the second pass showed 13 of those differ only in spelling. The one-pass figure is not wrong but it inflates difference, which is exactly what a synoptic run must not do while the direction is open. The same normalisation is added to gate item (e3) for whole-canon searching, with the worked instance: a plene-only search for קְדוֹשׁ יִשְׂרָאֵל returns 24 verses of Isaiah; the figure is 25, because 49:7 spells it קְדֹשׁ. **(M)** A new point 2 sets the **extent before working, at each book's own seams rather than at the overlap** — the supplied references are frequently misaligned (2 Kgs 18:17 answers Isa 36:2, not Isa 37), and a boundary drawn where the texts run together excludes material unique to one side, which on an open direction is precisely the evidence the mode exists to weigh; 2 Kgs 18:14–16 has no parallel in Isaiah and carried a headline finding. The extent decision and its reason go in the scope note. **(N)** The **per-book checks are run twice**: Phase 0.6 as before, and now Move 4 and the house-vocabulary test, because an internal echo is a fact about one book's composition and a single pass over "the passage" produces a list belonging to neither. The asymmetry between the two Move 4 lists is itself a finding. The house-vocabulary test produced the run's central result in both directions at once — יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת three times out of three in Isaiah's version where Kings has plain יְהוָה; נָגִיד, Kings' own royal-designation term, in Kings' oracle where Isaiah has nothing; and קְדוֹשׁ יִשְׂרָאֵל in **both**, at Isa 37:23 = 2 Kgs 19:22, its only occurrence in the whole of Joshua–Kings. Two data one way, one the other: that symmetry is what turned "open" from a refusal into a result. Consequential edits in five further places the spec had not named — gate item (e3), the scope-note template line, the Tool 11 Move 4 block, the Move 4 quality marker, and the filename-convention note — found by grepping the whole file rather than the paragraphs the spec pointed at.*

*Revised September 2026, round 2 (Synoptic Mode, apparatus spread, research-tool discipline, phrase-search normalisation). Four amendments arising from the synoptic Decalogue run of 10 September, the first run to work two canonically twinned passages as a pair. **(G) Adopted on trial** — a new Phase 0.5d adds **Synoptic Mode** for passages the canon tells twice, requiring the direction of dependence to be settled first as stated / open / contested, with the "open" case (Isa 36-39 || 2 Kgs 18-20, already the paradigm at Phase 0.55) barred from narrating either text as editing the other; then three movements rather than a composite, Phase 0.6 run per passage, a mechanical diff with accents, maqqef and paseq normalised, the Triad's Move 2 and Move 4 noted as coinciding where the source is intra-corpus, reconciliation against both book-overviews with any maturity gap reported as a tension, filing above both books, and depth floors met per report rather than per passage; consequential edits follow in Required Input, the template H1 and Passage block, and the Quality Markers. Unlike the rest of its round this mode rests on a single run, and is marked in the file as a hypothesis to be tested by the next synoptic run rather than as settled method. Rationale: four of the Decalogue run's five headline findings were unreachable from either passage alone, and at least seven further pairs stand untouched. (H) Phase 10.5 gains gate item (e4), the **apparatus-spread check**: a finding resting on parashoth, accentuation, ketiv/qere or Masorah must name its witness rather than the tradition, state that the feature is reception and not authorial, and have its manuscript spread checked in the library before entering an overview - because the corpus holds one manuscript tradition and prints these features without variants, so an unrepresentative witness and a verified count are indistinguishable from inside it. Rationale: the Decalogue run's paragraph-division finding passed every existing gate as a claim about "the Masoretic paragraphing" while resting on a setumah absent from 234 of Kennicott's 694 manuscripts. Phase 0.2 gains the corresponding warning; `textual-variants.md` gains a fourth-category section distinguishing variation inside the Hebrew from the Hebrew/Greek triage; the Text-First Declaration gains an apparatus-findings line. (I) Handling Tools That Need External Data gains a fifth point: a research tool's answers are claims to test, with its two characteristic errors named - reasoning from the English version by default (a Study Assistant reported "Ten Commandments" as the biblical designation of a collection the Hebrew calls "the ten words", with correct citations) and answering from general knowledge under a disclaimer easily missed beside cited material in the same reply. (J) Gate item (e3) and Tool 7's verify-before-you-report bullet gain the phrase-search rule: maqqef and paseq are not combining marks, survive NFD-stripping, and silently defeat whitespace patterns - two searches in the Decalogue run returned nil on texts containing the phrase.*

*Revised September 2026 (Primary-text corpus, Tanak order, chain verification, study-vs-pulpit text, synthetic-claim burden). The old formulation licensed working from the ESV as though it were the Hebrew; the Proverbs 2A note is the refutation — "the ESV alone would have reproduced the sweep." **(B)** A new **Phase 0.55** requires the Tanak position of every OT book, with a five-field template (Section · Reading implication · Presupposes · Handoff · Neighbours) in which Presupposes carries the testable claims; Tool 14 gains the full canonical-position subsection with Ruth, Daniel and Chronicles tabulated, the verified Ruth/Proverbs *ʾēšet-ḥayil* seam, the BHS-vs-Leningrad Ketuvim caveat, and the rule that where canonical and compositional order diverge (Isa 36–39 ‖ 2 Kgs 18–20) both are reported and neither resolves the other; Tool 2 gains a canonical-context level. Rationale: four instances across Exodus and Jonah of "a verbal chain extended by inference into a verse that does not contain the word", the failure mode the Jonah audit named, plus four more found by the OT chain audit of 2 September. **(D)** Default Translation becomes **Translations — Study Text and Pulpit Text**: the NASB95 (1995 edition, not the 2020 revision) is the fixed study text; the pulpit text is a variable declared per engagement — ESV (Anglicised) at the preacher's own church, NIV84 or another version elsewhere — to be asked for when a sermon is in view and never assumed. *Revised August 2026 (Hebrew/Greek divergence — a three-way triage, replacing any blanket text preference). `references/extensions/textual-variants.md` gains a governing section, "Governing rule for the OT: the three-way triage", and `SKILL.md` a matching top-level section, "Hebrew and Greek — the Three-Way Triage", placed before the Output Format. Where the Hebrew and the Greek diverge, the divergence must be classified before it is reported: (1) **translation loss** — a Hebrew literary feature (repeated root, wordplay, Leitwort, count of formulae, name-pun) that no translation carries, including the ESV; reported under Tool 7/8 or `original-languages.md` as a fact about the Hebrew, never as a reliability judgement or a `Translation-tradition split`, with the standing formula that any count is stated or implied "in the Hebrew"; (2) **substantive variant** — a different *Vorlage*, recension or edition; the only category this extension carries, weighed case by case on external evidence, which reading explains the other, and translator-habit vs *Vorlage*, with **no presumption in favour of the MT** (LXX Jeremiah ~1/8 shorter with 4QJer-b support; 4QSam-a siding with the LXX against an MT haplography; Deut 32:8 "sons of God" now in the ESV text; LXX Exodus 35–40 genuinely disputed as to priority); (3) **the NT's own text** — a Greek rendering the NT quotes or argues from (*kappōret*→*hilastērion*→Rom 3:25; *tabnît*→*typos*→Heb 8:5; *qāran*→δεδόξασται driving 2 Cor 3:7–18), load-bearing whichever text is prior and never demoted because the Hebrew is the original. The working default is noted as already Hebrew (English OTs translate the MT), and WCF 1.8 is distinguished as a claim about the Hebrew *original* and its providential preservation rather than a ruling on a variant — the distinction the Owen/Walton dispute turned on. Companion edits: the OT catalogue preamble reframed (uniform transmission ≠ priority of reading; all entries are category 2); an OT-specific pitfall added; the Tool 8 cross-reference in `04-words-and-translations.md` § Ancient versions check expanded to route divergences through the triage; the Tool 8 and Textual Variants report templates in `SKILL.md` updated so each category is filed in the right section; and Phase 10.5 gains hard gate item (e1) requiring every Hebrew/Greek divergence in the report to carry a triage category, category 2 to carry evidence and a confidence flag, and no divergence to be resolved by a blanket preference. Rationale: the Exodus sweep surfaced five broken Hebrew links and the natural inference — "prefer the Hebrew, the LXX is less reliable" — would have conflated three unlike things, inflating translation loss into text-critical findings, deciding real variants by default against the Qumran evidence, and undercutting NT arguments that rest on Greek readings the overview itself relies on.*

*Revised August 2026 (Genre — no form-critical classification of the Psalms). Tool 12 (Genre, `references/06-canonical-reading.md`) gains a governing subsection, "The Psalter — genre vs form-critical classification," that separates two senses of "genre": the literary kind of a book or large sub-unit (poetry, law, gospel, letter — what the tool classifies) from the Gunkel–Mowinckel *Gattung* of an individual psalm (lament, hymn, thanksgiving, royal…). The tool now classifies the Psalms only at the book/collection level ("Hebrew poetry", plus the sub-collection's canonical shape) and does **not** sort individual psalms into form-critical categories. Rationale (Hely-Hutchinson, *Answering the Psalmist's Perplexity*): the Psalter's theological coherence and messianic trajectory take priority over form-critical frameworks; genre analysis is secondary to the editors' shaping. Form criticism reads a psalm outward to a reconstructed cultic setting — history-behind-the-text the Text-First Discipline already flags — so the shaping question is routed to Tool 2 (Positional Necessity), Tool 11, and the biblical-theology / Christological extensions instead. Reading-rules for poetry (permission to grieve; doxology) remain, as reading the poetry rather than cataloguing the psalm. Companion edits: a caveat under the genre table, an operational-guidance bullet, a pitfall line, and the OT-poetry calibration example (`examples/psalm-33-worked.md`) rewritten so it models book-level genre only (its Tool 12 and two stray "hymn" labels corrected) — necessary because that worked example is mandatory calibration reading and previously taught the form-critical move.*

*Revised July 2026 (Move 4 — Internal echo check). Tool 11 gains a fourth, per-passage move: does this passage answer, invert, fulfil, or plant something within its own book? Required for every passage, citation or not, with an explicit nil-return when empty. Two disciplines: (1) the book's opening unit(s) are held open during the check — openings are the densest planting ground and the most commonly forgotten by the book's second half; the checking stage's "neighbouring reports" is correspondingly redefined so the opening report is a permanent neighbour of every passage; (2) an addressee-differentiation check for books that open by naming distinct parties (seven churches, twelve sons, three friends) — each named party's situation is run forward to its individual resolution. Findings carry the `Internal:` prefix with direction marked, feeding book-overview Finalise Stage 2's loop-back enrichment. Rationale: the Revelation sweep missed quiet internal echoes (Laodicea 3:17 ↔ Babylon 18:7) because the method only hunted external sources and "neighbouring" was read as thematic adjacency; internal echoes need no external source and no verbal flag, so nothing prompted the question. Quality declaration extended accordingly.*

*Revised July 2026 (Non-verbal allusion carriers). Tool 11 gains step 7, a weighted catalogue of allusion carriers beyond words and names, all on the standard high/moderate/uncertain scale with Hays criteria: (1) **character dossiers** for recurring figures — 2–4-line canonical arc (first appearance, defining episodes, trajectory), load-the-story rule applies to load-bearing figures; (2) **route as allusion** — sequence-of-places matches against canonical precedent, direction-sensitive, weighted by number of matching waypoints plus corroborating detail; single-place overlap is routed back to 7c; (3) **pattern-break absence** — the narrow admissible form of the absence argument, gated hard: an absence may be reported only where the established pattern that makes it visible can be cited with instances (broken refrains, omitted type-scene elements, book-level absences like the divine name in Esther); never weighted above moderate; inadmissible absences excluded entirely, not hedged. Phase 10.5 gate item (e2) extended to enforce weights and the absence gate. Rationale: these carriers fail verbal-echo searches like name-hyperlinks do; the absence type is included only because the pattern-citation gate makes it noticeable rather than guessed, per the design decision that guessed silences are not findings.*

*Revised July 2026 (Name-hyperlink discipline). Tool 7c upgraded from a recall check into a four-part discipline, synced into Tool 11 step 6, the SKILL.md Tool 7 template line, and the Phase 10.5 gate: (1) **proper-noun inventory** — list every place/personal name, gentilic, and significant number before triaging, so the check runs on the passage's actual names rather than the ones that happened to be noticed; (2) **load-the-story rule** — any moderate+ load-bearing name-hyperlink requires the source narrative to be re-read (mirroring Citation Triad Move 1), not recalled; memory-only hyperlinks tagged `[unverified]`; (3) **outstanding-word check** — is there a curse, vow, oath, or prophecy attached to this name that this passage activates or fulfils (paradigm: 1 Kgs 16:34 ← Josh 6:26; also Shiloh/Jer 7, Gibeon/Josh 9→2 Sam 21, Bethel/1 Kgs 13→2 Kgs 23), including unflagged activations; (4) **name-wordplay check** — narrative puns (Nabal), polemical distortions (Beth-aven), sound-interaction with passage keywords, and the Greek carrier for NT (Iēsous = Joshua). Rationale: name-based hyperlinks fail verbal-echo searches entirely, and the prior three-question check could be satisfied from free association — exactly where model runs diverge and the load-bearing link (Jericho's curse, not "Jericho = conquest") gets missed.*
