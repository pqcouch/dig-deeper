## The Passage

**NA28** (sigla removed; verse numbers added):

> **3:18** Αἱ γυναῖκες, ὑποτάσσεσθε τοῖς ἀνδράσιν ὡς ἀνῆκεν ἐν κυρίῳ. **19** Οἱ ἄνδρες, ἀγαπᾶτε τὰς γυναῖκας καὶ μὴ πικραίνεσθε πρὸς αὐτάς. **20** Τὰ τέκνα, ὑπακούετε τοῖς γονεῦσιν κατὰ πάντα, τοῦτο γὰρ εὐάρεστόν ἐστιν ἐν κυρίῳ. **21** Οἱ πατέρες, μὴ ἐρεθίζετε τὰ τέκνα ὑμῶν, ἵνα μὴ ἀθυμῶσιν. **22** Οἱ δοῦλοι, ὑπακούετε κατὰ πάντα τοῖς κατὰ σάρκα κυρίοις, μὴ ἐν ὀφθαλμοδουλίᾳ ὡς ἀνθρωπάρεσκοι, ἀλλʼ ἐν ἁπλότητι καρδίας φοβούμενοι τὸν κύριον. **23** ὃ ἐὰν ποιῆτε, ἐκ ψυχῆς ἐργάζεσθε ὡς τῷ κυρίῳ καὶ οὐκ ἀνθρώποις, **24** εἰδότες ὅτι ἀπὸ κυρίου ἀπολήμψεσθε τὴν ἀνταπόδοσιν τῆς κληρονομίας. τῷ κυρίῳ Χριστῷ δουλεύετε· **25** ὁ γὰρ ἀδικῶν κομίσεται ὃ ἠδίκησεν, καὶ οὐκ ἔστιν προσωπολημψία. **4:1** Οἱ κύριοι, τὸ δίκαιον καὶ τὴν ἰσότητα τοῖς δούλοις παρέχεσθε, εἰδότες ὅτι καὶ ὑμεῖς ἔχετε κύριον ἐν οὐρανῷ.

## Headline Findings

1. **The passage is governed by one word, κύριος ("Lord, master"), and the Greek uses that one word for the earthly masters and for Christ.** `[T]` κύριος ("Lord, master") occurs **nine times in six of these nine verses** (3:18, 20, 22, 23, 24, 4:1). That is nine of its sixteen occurrences in the letter (SBLGNT). The household is placed "in the Lord" (ἐν κυρίῳ, 3:18, 20) and its work done "for the Lord" (ὡς τῷ κυρίῳ, 3:23). Its reward comes "from the Lord" (3:24), and its servants serve "the Lord Christ" (τῷ κυρίῳ Χριστῷ, 3:24), a phrase found nowhere else in the NT. The slave section is framed by the same word in two spheres: "masters according to the flesh" (τοῖς κατὰ σάρκα κυρίοις, 3:22) at its start, and at its end "you too have a Master in heaven" (κύριον ἐν οὐρανῷ, 4:1). The nine verses also hold **nine of the letter's thirty imperatives** (SBLGNT parse). — Surfaced by: Tool 10, Tool 7, Tool 3, Tool 8 (English splits the word into "masters", "Master" and "Lord"), Original Language Observations. *Why it matters:* the code is not a list of roles with a Christian motive added. Every instruction is located under the lordship confessed in 2:6, "as you have received Christ Jesus the Lord". The congregation cannot hear in English that the slave's master and the slave's Lord bear one name.

2. **The household code is 3:17 put to work.** `[T]` for the wording; `[I]` for the function, *moderate–high*. The clause ὃ ἐὰν ποιῆτε ("whatever you do", 3:23) repeats πᾶν ὅ τι ἐὰν ποιῆτε ("whatever you do", 3:17). These are the clause's only two occurrences in the NT, and in both the act is set in relation to "the Lord": "in the name of the Lord Jesus" (3:17), "as for the Lord" (3:23) (SBLGNT). The code opens with no connective at all (3:18), so its tie is to what immediately precedes. It names the slave whom 3:11 placed in the new humanity, where "there is no … slave and free, but Christ is all, and in all". The δοῦλος ("slave") of 3:11 is the δοῦλοι ("slaves") of 3:22 and the δούλοις ("slaves") of 4:1, the only three places in the letter where a slave is a member of the congregation rather than a title for a minister (1:7; 4:7, 12). — Surfaced by: Phase 0.6, Tool 2, Tool 4, Tool 11 Move 4. *Why it matters:* the code is the "whatever you do" of the new humanity in its most ordinary setting, not a separate block of social ethics bolted on after the theology.

3. **The weight falls on the slaves, and they receive the letter's largest promises.** `[T]` The slave section (3:22–25) has 56 of the passage's 116 words. The other four addresses have nine to thirteen words each, and the masters eighteen (SBLGNT). Set against Ephesians, Colossians' slave section is about the same length (Eph 6:5–8, 59 words), while its marriage section is a tenth of Ephesians' (19 words against 189 in Eph 5:22–33). Colossians has compressed everything else and kept the slaves at full length. To the slaves, and only to them in this letter, belong:
   - κληρονομία ("inheritance", 3:24), its only use in Colossians;
   - ἀνταπόδοσις ("reward, recompense", 3:24), its only use in the NT;
   - the fear of the Lord (3:22);
   - the apostle's own stance, "not as men-pleasers" (compare 1 Thess 2:4; Gal 1:10).

   — Surfaced by: Tool 3, Tool 10, Tool 11 (the NT parallels), Tool 7. *Why it matters:* a sermon that spends its time on wives and husbands has preached Ephesians' proportions, not Colossians'. The people Roman law treated as property, and who in Roman law could not inherit `[S]`, are addressed as heirs. The letter is carried by Onesimus, a slave who is "one of you" (4:9) `[T]`/`[I]`.

4. **The passage restrains the superior party at every step, and it holds both slave and master under one impartial Lord.** `[T]`
   - Each of the three superiors is given a command that limits power. Husbands are to love and not be embittered (3:19). Fathers are not to provoke, "so that they will not lose heart" (3:21). Masters are to grant "justice and fairness" (4:1).
   - The final ground is the slaves' own ground, turned round. "Knowing that" (εἰδότες ὅτι) opens the motive for slaves (3:24) and for masters (4:1), and καὶ ὑμεῖς ("you too", 4:1) puts the masters where the slaves already stand.
   - "There is no partiality" (οὐκ ἔστιν προσωπολημψία, 3:25) stands between the two addresses and faces both. Ephesians places it in the address to masters (Eph 6:9).

   — Surfaced by: Tool 3, Tool 4, Tool 5, Tool 10, Difficult Verses (3:25). *Why it matters:* the code does not leave power unaccountable. The master's master is the slave's Lord, and he judges without partiality.

5. **The commands are differentiated, and each comes with its own qualifier.** `[T]`
   - Wives are told ὑποτάσσεσθε ("be subject"), a middle-voice imperative, and are never told to obey. Children and slaves are told ὑπακούετε ("obey", 3:20, 22).
   - Every subordinate command carries a qualifier: "as is fitting in the Lord" (3:18), "for this is well-pleasing in the Lord" (3:20), "fearing the Lord … as for the Lord" (3:22–23).
   - The "in everything" (κατὰ πάντα) given to children and slaves (3:20, 22) is the phrase's only use in Colossians, and the letter has already said who is "all" (3:11).

   — Surfaced by: Tool 7, Tool 8, Original Language Observations, Difficult Verses. *Why it matters:* the passage is not the flat hierarchy it is often preached as, nor the abolition of roles it is sometimes made into. Its own qualifiers set the limits.

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book level.** To keep a young Gentile church walking "in Him" as they began (2:6), by setting before them the Christ in whom all the fullness dwells, so that no rival "philosophy" can persuade them they lack anything. The letter then shows what that sufficiency looks like "in speech, household and work" `[S: overview]`/`[I]`.

**Passage level.** To show the new humanity of 3:10–11 inside the ordinary household. The household consists of three unequal pairs, and in each pair the weaker party is addressed first and directly, as a member of the church. Each relationship is placed under "the Lord", so that obedience and authority alike are exercised as service to Christ and are answerable to him. **The passage's distinctive purpose, against its Ephesian twin, is pastoral pressure on the slave–master relationship** `[T]` for the proportions (Tool 3); `[I]` for the purpose. The letter's own occasion explains why: it is carried by a slave who is "one of you" (4:9) `[T]`, and its companion letter is written to a slave's master `[S: Philemon dig]`.

**What the passage is not doing.** It does not argue for the household order, and it does not ground the order in creation; contrast 1 Cor 11:8–9 and 1 Tim 2:13 `[T]`. It gives no Christ-and-church analogy for marriage, which takes up most of Ephesians' version (Eph 5:23–32) `[T]`. Every ground it gives is "the Lord" `[T]`.

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement.** Four things set the passage up:
- **3:1–4** — the readers have been raised with Christ, who is "seated at the right hand of God", and are to seek "the things above" (τὰ ἄνω, "the things above", 3:1, 2) `[T]`.
- **3:5–11** — they have put off the old self and put on the new, "renewed … according to the image of the One who created him". In that renewal there is "no distinction between Greek and Jew, circumcised and uncircumcised, barbarian, Scythian, slave and free, but Christ is all, and in all" (3:11) `[T]`.
- **3:12–17** — they are to clothe themselves with the virtues of the "chosen of God, holy and beloved". Love is "the bond of perfection" (3:14). Christ's peace is to rule and Christ's word to dwell (3:15–16). Then comes the summary command: **"Whatever you do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks through Him to God the Father" (3:17)** `[T]`.
- **2:16–23** — the rival teaching offered a regimen of food, days and severe treatment of the body; Paul called it "of no value against fleshly indulgence" (2:23) `[T]`.

**Necessity answer.** 3:18–4:1 exists here because 3:11 and 3:17 leave two questions that the letter must now answer.

The first comes from 3:11, which has said that "slave and free" is no distinction "in Christ". What, then, becomes of the household, where slaves and free, wives and husbands, children and fathers live under one roof? The code answers that the household remains, but everyone in it now has the same Lord. Slaves are spoken to as members of the church, and masters are told they have a Master. The δοῦλος ("slave") of 3:11 reappears at 3:22 and 4:1, and nowhere else in the letter as a member of the congregation (1:7; 4:7, 12 use "slave" and "fellow-slave" of ministers) `[T]`.

The second comes from 3:17, which has said "whatever you do … in the name of the Lord Jesus". The code applies that clause where most of life is lived. 3:23 repeats it, ὃ ἐὰν ποιῆτε ("whatever you do"), the only other NT occurrence of the clause (SBLGNT) `[T]`.

**The letter's practical alternative to the rival regimen is not a better regimen but the ordinary household under Christ** `[I]`, *moderate–high*. The asceticism of 2:20–23 treated the body harshly. The code governs how one treats a wife, a child, a slave.

**Implication.** Preached without 3:11 and 3:17, the code becomes a free-standing hierarchy (overview Trap 4). Preached after them, it shows how "Christ is all" (3:11) works out between people who remain unequal in station.

**Revisit after Tool 11.** Move 2 has nothing to track, since no OT source in the letter bears on the household. Move 4 sharpens the positional answer. The passage answers the opening prayer: 1:10's walk "worthy of the Lord, to please Him in all respects", εἰς πᾶσαν ἀρεσκείαν ("to please him in all respects"), returns as εὐάρεστον ("well-pleasing", 3:20) and is reversed in ἀνθρωπάρεσκοι ("men-pleasers", 3:22). It also answers the opening thanksgiving, through the "inheritance" (1:12 κλῆρος, "lot, inheritance", → 3:24 κληρονομία, "inheritance") `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the design, *moderate*. Even the household code is the opening prayer coming true.

#### Wider context

- **Immediately before:** 3:12–17, the "put on", ending with the summary of 3:17 `[T]`.
- **Immediately after:** 4:2–6. The church is to pray with thanksgiving (4:2; compare 3:17's "giving thanks"), and to pray for Paul's open door for "the mystery of Christ", "for which I have also been imprisoned" (δέδεμαι, "I have been bound", 4:3). It is to walk in wisdom towards outsiders (4:5) `[T]`. The prisoner who asks for prayer has just told slaves how to serve. Paul nowhere calls himself a slave in Colossians, but he calls Epaphras and Tychicus his "fellow slaves" (σύνδουλος, "fellow slave", 1:7; 4:7), and Epaphras "a slave of Christ Jesus" (δοῦλος Χριστοῦ, "slave of Christ", 4:12) `[T]`.
- **The section:** 3:1–4:6, the life of the risen. The household (3:18–4:1) sits between the church's inner life (3:12–17) and its face to outsiders (4:2–6) `[T]`.
- **The book:** the code is followed by the envoys (4:7–9). Tychicus comes "with Onesimus, our faithful and beloved brother, who is one of your number" (4:9) `[T]`. The congregation that hears 3:22–4:1 will have Onesimus in the room `[I]`. That he is the slave of Philemon's letter is an inference from the shared names and setting `[I]`/`[S: Philemon dig]`.

### 3. Structure

**Six addresses in three pairs.** In each pair the subordinate party comes first, then the superior `[T]`:

| Verses | Address | Command | Qualifier or motive | Words (SBLGNT) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3:18 | Αἱ γυναῖκες ("wives") | ὑποτάσσεσθε ("be subject") | "as is fitting in the Lord" | 9 |
| 3:19 | Οἱ ἄνδρες ("husbands") | ἀγαπᾶτε ("love"); μὴ πικραίνεσθε ("do not be embittered") | — | 10 |
| 3:20 | Τὰ τέκνα ("children") | ὑπακούετε ("obey") … κατὰ πάντα ("in everything") | "for this is well-pleasing in the Lord" | 13 |
| 3:21 | Οἱ πατέρες ("fathers") | μὴ ἐρεθίζετε ("do not provoke") | "so that they will not lose heart" | 10 |
| 3:22–25 | Οἱ δοῦλοι ("slaves") | ὑπακούετε ("obey") … κατὰ πάντα ("in everything"); ἐργάζεσθε ("work"); [δουλεύετε ("serve")] | "fearing the Lord … as for the Lord … knowing that … for he who does wrong … no partiality" | 56 |
| 4:1 | Οἱ κύριοι ("masters") | παρέχεσθε ("grant") | "knowing that you too have a Master in heaven" | 18 |

**Device.** The device is a fixed form in which every address has three parts: a vocative, a present imperative, and a qualifier or motive. The form is broken once, at the slave address, which swells to four verses `[T]`.

The three pairs follow the scale of the ancient household (marriage, parenthood, ownership) `[S]` for the convention (see Historical Background). Within that scale the length runs from 9–10 words for the marriage pair, to 13 and 10 for children and fathers, to 56 for the slaves. Masters come back to 18 `[T]`. **The passage slows down where the social pressure is greatest** `[I]`.

**The slave–master unit (3:22–4:1) has a frame.** It opens with "masters according to the flesh" (τοῖς κατὰ σάρκα κυρίοις, 3:22) and closes with "a Master in heaven" (κύριον ἐν οὐρανῷ, 4:1). Between the two the word κύριος ("Lord") stands five times for Christ (3:22, 23, 24 twice) `[T]`. "Flesh" and "heaven" are the two spheres of the letter's own argument: compare 3:1–2, "the things above, not … the things that are on earth" `[I]`.

**Paired motives.** εἰδότες ὅτι ("knowing that") opens the motive clause for the slaves (3:24) and for the masters (4:1), and nowhere else in the passage `[T]`. The slave address has two outcomes in balance, reward and repayment (3:24 / 3:25), so the judgment of the Lord faces both ways before the masters are addressed `[T]`/`[I]`.

#### Clause-Flow Layout

```

        so that they do not lose heart (ἵνα μὴ ἀθυμῶσιν).          ← purpose

            fearing (φοβούμενοι) the Lord.                        ← participle: the reason within
    Whatever you do,
        work (ἐργάζεσθε) from the soul,                           ← command

                the reward of the inheritance.                   ← ground (positive)
    The Lord Christ you serve (δουλεύετε).                        ← statement or command (see Difficult Verses)
        For (γάρ) the one who does wrong will receive back the wrong he did,   ← ground (negative)
        and there is no partiality.                              ← principle, facing both ways
Masters,
    grant (παρέχεσθε) to your slaves what is just and equal,      ← command
```

**Central assertions, one per address:**
- Wives: *be subject, as is fitting in the Lord* (command).
- Husbands: *love, and do not be embittered* (command).
- Children: *obey, because it pleases in the Lord* (command with its ground).
- Fathers: *do not provoke* (prohibition with its purpose).
- Slaves: *obey and work as for the Lord, because the Lord rewards and repays without partiality* (command, then truth). The truth half here is as long as the command half, which no other address has `[T]`.
- Masters: *grant justice, because you too have a Master* (command with its ground).

The load-bearing clause of the whole unit is a truth, not a command: **"you too have a Master in heaven"**, with its twin "the Lord Christ you serve" `[I]`.

### 4. Linking Words

- **No connective at 3:18** `[T]`. The code begins by asyndeton. Ephesians, by contrast, hangs its code on a participle, ὑποτασσόμενοι ἀλλήλοις ("being subject to one another", Eph 5:21), and lets the wives' line borrow the verb (5:22 has none) `[T]`. Colossians has no mutual-submission clause and needs no borrowed verb: ὑποτάσσεσθε ("be subject") is its own imperative. **The tie is to 3:17 by content, not by a conjunction** (Tool 2) `[I]`.
- **ὡς ("as")** ×3: ὡς ἀνῆκεν ("as is fitting", 3:18), ὡς ἀνθρωπάρεσκοι ("as men-pleasers", 3:22), ὡς τῷ κυρίῳ ("as for the Lord", 3:23) `[T]`. The first gives a norm, the second a false self-presentation, the third the true one.
- **γάρ ("for")** ×2: at 3:20, grounding the children's obedience in what pleases "in the Lord"; at 3:25, grounding the slave instruction in the Lord's repayment of wrong `[T]`. NA28 has no γάρ ("for") at 3:24b. The majority text adds one, which turns "the Lord Christ you serve" into a reason ("for you serve", KJV) (see Textual Variants) `[T: as the apparatus prints it]`.
- **ἵνα μή ("so that … not")**, 3:21: purpose. The fathers' prohibition exists for the child's sake, ἵνα μὴ ἀθυμῶσιν ("so that they will not lose heart") `[T]`.
- **ἀλλά ("but")**, 3:22: the pivot from eye-service to sincerity of heart `[T]`.
- **καὶ οὐκ ("and not")**, 3:23: "for the Lord and not for men" `[T]`.
- **εἰδότες ὅτι ("knowing that")** ×2, 3:24 and 4:1: causal participles. The slaves' ground and the masters' ground share a form `[T]`.
- **καὶ ὑμεῖς ("you too")**, 4:1: the καί ("too") puts masters alongside slaves — they also have a κύριος ("Lord, master") `[T]`.
- **καὶ οὐκ ἔστιν ("and there is no")**, 3:25: a principle stated in the present indicative, not tied by grammar to either party `[T]`.

### 5. Parallels

The passage is prose, but it uses three kinds of parallel construction that bear on meaning `[T]`:

1. **Paired addresses.** In each pair the second line restrains the authority the first line implies:
   - be subject / love and do not be embittered;
   - obey / do not provoke;
   - obey / grant justice and fairness.

   This is antithetical in function: the superior's line is always a limit, never a licence `[I]`.
2. **Antithetical manner (3:22–23).** "Not with eye-service, as men-pleasers / but with sincerity of heart, fearing the Lord"; "for the Lord / and not for men" `[T]`.
3. **Balanced recompense (3:24–25).** "From the Lord you will receive (ἀπολήμψεσθε) the reward of the inheritance / he who does wrong will receive back (κομίσεται) the wrong which he has done" `[T]`. Two verbs of receiving, one positive and one negative. Ephesians has one positive clause, "whatever good each one does, he will receive (κομίσεται) back from the Lord" (Eph 6:8) `[T]`. Colossians has both halves.

**N/A** — epistle, not narrative. The nearest thing to an authorial aside is the asyndetic statement "the Lord Christ you serve" (3:24), if it is read as an indicative (Difficult Verses).

### 7. Vocabulary

**κύριος ("Lord, master")** `[T]` — *high confidence.* There are nine occurrences, in six of the nine verses; the letter has sixteen in thirteen verses (SBLGNT). The word does two jobs.
- **Earthly owners** twice: τοῖς κατὰ σάρκα κυρίοις ("masters according to the flesh", 3:22) and οἱ κύριοι ("masters", 4:1).
- **Christ** seven times: ἐν κυρίῳ ("in the Lord", 3:18, 20); τὸν κύριον ("the Lord", 3:22); ὡς τῷ κυρίῳ ("as for the Lord", 3:23); ἀπὸ κυρίου ("from the Lord", 3:24); τῷ κυρίῳ Χριστῷ ("the Lord Christ", 3:24); κύριον ἐν οὐρανῷ ("a Master in heaven", 4:1).

**τῷ κυρίῳ Χριστῷ ("the Lord Christ", 3:24) — the two words side by side in that order occur only here in the NT** (SBLGNT) `[T]`. It is the letter's confession, Χριστὸν Ἰησοῦν τὸν κύριον ("Christ Jesus the Lord", 2:6), compressed into a title `[I]`.

The other household codes call the owner δεσπότης ("master, owner"): 1 Tim 6:1–2 twice, Titus 2:9, 1 Pet 2:18. **Only Colossians and Ephesians use κύριος ("Lord, master") for the owner** (Eph 6:5, 9; SBLGNT) `[T]`. That choice is what makes the Lord/master play possible.

**δοῦλος ("slave"), δουλεύω ("to serve as a slave")** `[T]` — *high.* δοῦλοι ("slaves", 3:22), δουλεύετε ("you serve", 3:24), δούλοις ("slaves", 4:1). In the rest of the letter: 3:11 ("slave and free"); 4:12 (Epaphras, "a slave of Christ Jesus"); σύνδουλος ("fellow slave") at 1:7 (Epaphras) and 4:7 (Tychicus) `[T]`. **The verb that tells slaves whom they serve, δουλεύετε ("you serve"), is the slave's own verb**: "you slave for the Lord Christ" `[T]`. The letter has already given the title δοῦλος ("slave") to its most honoured ministers. So the slaves at Colossae are told that their status is the same word as Epaphras's calling `[I]`, *moderate–high*.

**ὑποτάσσω ("to be subject") / ὑπακούω ("to obey")** `[T]` — *high.* Wives receive ὑποτάσσεσθε ("be subject", 3:18), in the middle voice, the letter's only use of the verb. Children and slaves receive ὑπακούετε ("obey", 3:20, 22), the verb's only two uses in the letter (SBLGNT). In the whole NT, ὑπακούω ("to obey") never stands in one verse with γυνή ("wife") or ἀνήρ ("husband"), and no verse commands a wife to obey; the nearest is 1 Pet 3:6, which holds up Sarah, who "obeyed Abraham", as an example `[T]`. Elsewhere in the NT ὑποτάσσω ("to be subject") with ἀνήρ ("husband") occurs at Eph 5:24; Titus 2:5; 1 Pet 3:1, 5 `[T]`. **The distinction between the two verbs is kept in every NT command to wives, and Colossians keeps it** `[T]`. Elsewhere in Paul ὑποτάσσω ("to be subject") describes the Son's own subjection to the Father (1 Cor 15:28) `[T]`. That use rules out the claim that the word in itself implies inferiority, though Colossians does not make the point `[I]`.

**ἀνήκω ("to be fitting")** `[T]` — *high.* ὡς ἀνῆκεν ("as is fitting", 3:18). The verb has three NT uses: Eph 5:4 ("which are not fitting"); Col 3:18; Phlm 8, where Paul could "order you to do what is fitting" (τὸ ἀνῆκον, "what is fitting") (SBLGNT) `[T]`. The imperfect is idiomatic for present propriety `[S]`. The norm is not "fitting in society" but "fitting in the Lord" `[T]`.

**πικραίνω ("to make bitter; passive, to be embittered")** `[T]` — μὴ πικραίνεσθε πρὸς αὐτάς ("do not be embittered against them", 3:19). In Paul it occurs only here; elsewhere in the NT only Rev 8:11; 10:9, 10 (of bitter waters and the bitter scroll) `[T]`. The NASB95's "embittered against" keeps the passive and the inward turn. The ESV's "harsh with" moves it to outward behaviour (Tool 8).

**ἐρεθίζω ("to provoke, irritate")** `[T]` — μὴ ἐρεθίζετε ("do not provoke", 3:21). It has two NT uses. At 2 Cor 9:2 it means "stirred up", in a good sense, by zeal. Here it is negative. **Ephesians uses a different verb, παροργίζετε ("provoke to anger", Eph 6:4)**, and the strongest Western and Alexandrian witnesses bring Colossians into line with it (Textual Variants) `[T]`. In Swete the ἐρεθ- family ("provoke") stands twice with ὑπακούω ("obey"), both in the law of the stubborn son: υἱὸς ἀπειθὴς καὶ ἐρεθιστής, οὐχ ὑπακούων ("a son disobedient and contentious, not obeying", Deut 21:18; again 21:20) `[T]`. Colossians lays the warning on the father, not on the son; see Tool 11 for its weight.

**ἀθυμέω ("to lose heart, be discouraged")** `[T]` — *high.* ἀθυμῶσιν ("they lose heart", 3:21): its only NT use (SBLGNT). The purpose clause makes the child's spirit the measure of the father's conduct `[T]`.

**εὐάρεστος ("well-pleasing") — and the ἀρεσκ- family ("please") across the letter** `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the design, *moderate*. The family has three members in Colossians, each used once:
- ἀρεσκεία ("pleasing", 1:10), its only NT use: "to walk worthy of the Lord, to please him in all respects";
- εὐάρεστον ("well-pleasing", 3:20): obedience "is well-pleasing in the Lord";
- ἀνθρωπάρεσκοι ("men-pleasers", 3:22).

Elsewhere in the NT ἀνθρωπάρεσκος ("men-pleaser") occurs only at Eph 6:6, and Eph 5:10 has εὐάρεστον ("pleasing") too (SBLGNT). The pairing is therefore part of the shared code tradition, not a Colossian invention. **Colossians' distinctive is the 1:10 anchor**: the letter opens by praying that its readers please the Lord in everything (εἰς πᾶσαν ἀρεσκείαν, "to please him in all respects"). The code then names two ways of pleasing — the Lord's way and men's — and gives the "in everything" (κατὰ πάντα) to children and slaves (3:20, 22) `[I]`. Paul uses ἀρέσκω ("to please") with ἄνθρωπος ("man") of his own apostolic stance: "not as pleasing men, but God" (1 Thess 2:4); "if I were still trying to please men, I would not be a slave of Christ" (Gal 1:10) (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Gal 1:10 joins "pleasing men" with δοῦλος Χριστοῦ ("a slave of Christ"), so the slaves are given the apostle's own stance `[I]`, *moderate*.

**ὀφθαλμοδουλία ("eye-service")** `[T]` — *high.* It has two NT uses, Eph 6:6 and Col 3:22, and no known earlier Greek use `[S]` — *moderate on the "no earlier use"; consider BDAG*. The word means work done to be seen. It is set against ἐν ἁπλότητι καρδίας ("with sincerity of heart"). Swete has that phrase in two verses, 1 Chr 29:17 (David's willing offering for the temple) and Wis 1:1 ("seek him in sincerity of heart") `[T]`.

**φοβούμενοι τὸν κύριον ("fearing the Lord")** `[T]` for the wording; `[I]` for the identification, *moderate–high*. The verb with "the Lord" as its object is the Old Testament's own formula. In Swete a form of φοβέομαι ("to fear") is followed directly by (τὸν) κύριον ("the Lord") in **seventy verses**: Sirach 25, Psalms 12, Deuteronomy 7, the Psalms of Solomon 6, and others. The positive control is that Ps 33:10, "fear the Lord, all his saints", was returned `[T]`. In the NT, **Col 3:22 is the only verse in which the verb φοβέομαι ("to fear") takes τὸν κύριον ("the Lord") as its object** (SBLGNT; Rev 15:4 has κύριε, "O Lord", as a vocative) `[T]`. The noun phrase "the fear of the Lord" stands at Acts 9:31 and 2 Cor 5:11, and "the fear of Christ" at Eph 5:21 `[T]`. At 2 Cor 5:11 it follows the judgment seat of Christ, "so that each one may receive back" (κομίσηται, "may receive back", 5:10). Paul joins the fear of the Lord with receiving back from the Lord there too, as Col 3:22–25 does `[T]`/`[I]`. In Colossians "the Lord" is Christ: "the Lord Christ" stands two verses later (3:24) `[T]`. **So the fear of the LORD, the beginning of wisdom in Israel, is here the slave's fear of Christ** `[I]`. The majority text reads τὸν θεόν ("God"), the KJV's "fearing God", which makes the transfer disappear (Textual Variants).

**ψυχή ("soul") and ἐργάζομαι ("to work")** `[T]` — ἐκ ψυχῆς ἐργάζεσθε ("work from the soul", 3:23). Both words occur only here in Colossians (SBLGNT). The NASB95 and ESV render the phrase "heartily". The Greek has "soul" because it has just used "heart" (καρδίας, "of heart", 3:22) `[T]`.

**κληρονομία ("inheritance") — with κλῆρος ("lot") at 1:12** `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the link, *moderate–high*. τὴν ἀνταπόδοσιν τῆς κληρονομίας ("the reward of the inheritance", 3:24) is κληρονομία's only occurrence in Colossians. In Paul it occurs elsewhere only at Gal 3:18 and Eph 1:14, 18; 5:5 (SBLGNT). It answers the opening thanksgiving to the Father "who has qualified us to share in the inheritance of the saints in Light" (εἰς τὴν μερίδα τοῦ κλήρου τῶν ἁγίων, "for the share of the lot of the saints", 1:12) `[T]`. The words are cognate, not the same lemma. **The slaves are told that they are heirs.** In Roman law a slave could hold a *peculium* ("allowance") but could not be an heir in his own right `[S]` — *moderate; consider a background dictionary*. Gal 4:1–7 makes the same move in Paul's own words: "no longer a slave, but a son; and if a son, then an heir" (Gal 4:7) `[T]`. Gal 4:1 and 4:7 are the only two NT verses joining δοῦλος ("slave") with κληρονόμος ("heir") (SBLGNT) `[T]`.

**ἀνταπόδοσις ("repayment, reward")** `[T]` — *high.* This is the word's only NT occurrence (SBLGNT). The cognate verb ἀνταποδίδωμι ("to repay") occurs in six NT verses, three of them of judgment (Rom 12:19; 2 Thess 1:6; Heb 10:30). In Swete the noun stands in fourteen verses (one of them in 1 Enoch). Most are retribution — "the day of recompense" (Isa 34:8; 61:2; 63:4), "repay recompense to the proud" (Ps 93:2). Two are a servant's reward: Ps 18:12 ("your servant keeps them; in keeping them there is great reward") and 2 Sam 19:36 (Barzillai, "your servant", to David) `[T]`. Those two are the only Swete verses joining a δουλ- word ("slave, servant") with ἀνταπόδοσις ("repayment") `[T]`. **The genitive is appositional, "the reward which is the inheritance"** `[I]`, *moderate*. The ESV renders it so: "the inheritance as your reward".

**ἀδικέω ("to do wrong")** `[T]` — ὁ ἀδικῶν … ὃ ἠδίκησεν ("he who does wrong … the wrong which he has done", 3:25). The verb is repeated in two forms. Its only uses in Colossians and Philemon are here and at Phlm 18, εἰ δέ τι ἠδίκησέν σε ("if he has wronged you in any way"), of Onesimus (SBLGNT). The aorist form is the same `[T]`/`[S: Philemon dig]` (independently confirmed here).

**προσωπολημψία ("partiality")** `[T]` — *high.* It has four NT uses: Rom 2:11; Eph 6:9; Col 3:25; Jas 2:1. The related forms are προσωπολήμπτης ("one who shows partiality", Acts 10:34) and προσωπολημπτέω ("to show partiality", Jas 2:9) (SBLGNT). The compound does not occur in Swete. It is a Christian coinage `[S]` from the LXX idiom λαμβάνειν πρόσωπον ("to receive a face", Lev 19:15) and θαυμάζειν πρόσωπον ("to admire a face", Deut 10:17; 2 Chr 19:7) `[T]` for the idiom; `[S]` for the coinage. The scan control confirms the absence: the tool lists this stem as having no match anywhere in Swete.

**τὸ δίκαιον καὶ τὴν ἰσότητα ("what is just and equal")** `[T]` — ἰσότης ("equality") has three NT uses: 2 Cor 8:13, 14, where it is the "equality" between giving and receiving churches, and Col 4:1 (SBLGNT). In Swete it is rare: Job 36:29; Zech 4:7; Pss Sol 17:46 `[T]`. "Equality" is its ordinary sense, and "fairness" or "equity" its extended one `[S]`. The NASB95 ("fairness") and the ESV ("fairly") decide for the extended sense. The KJV's "that which is just and equal" keeps the word (Tool 8) `[unverified — KJV from recall]`.

**Proper-noun inventory.** Χριστός ("Christ", 3:24) is the only name `[T]`. There are no places and no numbers. The roles — wives, husbands, children, fathers, slaves, masters — are generic. There is no canonical freight to load beyond "the Lord Christ".

### 8. Translations

**3:18 ὑποτάσσεσθε ("be subject").** NASB95 "be subject to", ESV "submit to", KJV "submit yourselves unto your own husbands" `[unverified — KJV from recall]`. The KJV's "own" reflects the majority's ἰδίοις ("own"), absent from NA28. The middle voice favours "submit yourselves" `[I]`, *moderate*. None of the three says "obey", and the Greek does not either.

**3:19 μὴ πικραίνεσθε ("do not be embittered").** NASB95 "do not be embittered against them" is closer to the passive and to the root πικρ- ("bitter"). The ESV's "do not be harsh with them" and the NIV84's "do not be harsh with them" turn an inward disposition into outward behaviour `[I]`.

**3:20 εὐάρεστόν ἐστιν ἐν κυρίῳ ("is well-pleasing in the Lord").** NASB95 "well-pleasing to the Lord"; ESV "this pleases the Lord"; KJV "well pleasing unto the Lord". **All three render ἐν κυρίῳ ("in the Lord") as "to the Lord"**, which is the sense of a minority variant (τῷ κυρίῳ, "to the Lord": 0198, 81 and a few others) rather than of NA28 `[T: as the apparatus prints it]`. The Greek sets 3:20's ἐν κυρίῳ ("in the Lord") beside 3:18's ἐν κυρίῳ ("in the Lord"). In the English versions only 3:18 keeps "in the Lord", so the pair is lost.

**3:21 ἐρεθίζετε ("provoke").** NASB95 "exasperate", ESV "provoke", NIV84 "embitter". The NIV84's "embitter" re-uses, in English, the idea of 3:19's πικραίνω ("to be embittered"), which the Greek keeps distinct.

**3:22 τοῖς κατὰ σάρκα κυρίοις ("masters according to the flesh").** NASB95 "your masters on earth", ESV "your earthly masters", KJV "your masters according to the flesh". **The KJV alone keeps σάρξ ("flesh")**, and with it the frame "flesh … heaven" (3:22 / 4:1) and the link to Phlm 16's "in the flesh and in the Lord".

**3:22 δοῦλοι ("slaves").** NASB95 "Slaves"; ESV "Bondservants". It is the same word as 3:11 and 4:1, and the same as Phlm 16 (overview Trap 4) `[T]`.

**3:24 τὴν ἀνταπόδοσιν τῆς κληρονομίας ("the reward of the inheritance").** NASB95 "the reward of the inheritance" (literal); ESV "the inheritance as your reward" (it makes the genitive appositional — a defensible decision, already made for the hearer) `[I]`.

**3:24 τῷ κυρίῳ Χριστῷ δουλεύετε ("the Lord Christ you serve").** NASB95 "It is the Lord Christ whom you serve"; ESV "You are serving the Lord Christ"; NIV84 "It is the Lord Christ you are serving"; KJV "for ye serve the Lord Christ" (with the majority's γάρ, "for") `[unverified — KJV from recall]`. **All the English versions read an indicative.** The form is equally an imperative, "serve the Lord Christ!" (Difficult Verses).

**3:25 καὶ οὐκ ἔστιν προσωπολημψία ("and there is no partiality").** ESV "and there is no partiality" keeps a free-standing clause. NASB95 "and that without partiality" attaches it to the repayment and so narrows it `[I]`.

**4:1 τὸ δίκαιον καὶ τὴν ἰσότητα … παρέχεσθε ("grant what is just and equal").** NASB95 "grant … justice and fairness" keeps the nouns and the verb. ESV "treat … justly and fairly" turns the nouns into adverbs and loses "grant", the middle-voice "supply from yourselves". The KJV's "give unto your servants that which is just and equal" `[unverified — KJV from recall]` keeps ἰσότης ("equality").

**Ancient versions check.** The modern versions agree on two renderings that carry weight. Both were checked.

- **3:24 — the indicative "you serve".** **Translation-tradition split:** the Vulgate reads *Domino Christo servite*, an imperative ("serve the Lord Christ"), against the modern indicative `[S]` — *moderate confidence on the Vulgate wording; recalled, not checked — verify.* For preaching, the indicative states the slave's identity, while the imperative gives a final command. The Greek allows both, and NA28's punctuation (a full stop before the clause) leaves it standing alone.
- **3:22 — "fearing the Lord".** The English versions follow NA28's κύριον ("the Lord"). The KJV's "fearing God" follows the majority reading θεόν ("God"). That is a variant, not a translation choice, and it is reported under Textual Variants.

**Pulpit divergence note (ESV).** The congregation will hear five things the Greek does not say, or will miss five things it does.
1. "Masters", "Master" and "Lord" as different words. Nothing tells them it is one word, nine times.
2. "Earthly masters" (3:22), not "masters according to the flesh": the flesh/heaven frame and the link to Philemon are gone.
3. "Bondservants … serving" (3:22, 24), where the Greek has one root: δοῦλοι … δουλεύετε ("slaves … you slave").
4. "Pleases the Lord" (3:20), where the Greek has "in the Lord", matching 3:18.
5. "Justly and fairly" (4:1), where the Greek has "what is just and equality" and a verb, "grant".

The NASB95 is closer on items 4 and 5. Neither version keeps items 1 to 3.

### 9. Tone and Feel

**Brisk, then grave.** The four family addresses are terse — nine to thirteen words each, imperative and qualifier with nothing added `[T]`. The tone is that of a rule already known being restated with one new element, "in the Lord" `[I]`. At 3:22 the tempo drops: participles, contrasts and two motive clauses, then a warning (3:25). The register moves from instruction to promise and judgment `[T]`/`[I]`. At 4:1 it closes in a single sentence aimed at the household's most powerful members, and its last word is οὐρανῷ ("heaven") `[T]`.

**Soundtrack:** a plain hymn-tune for four verses, then the organ opens up for the slaves, with a single low pedal note under the masters' line. The preacher should not let the passage's brevity on marriage swallow its length on slavery.

### 10. Repetition

Verified by lemma (SBLGNT):

- **κύριος ("Lord, master")** — ×9, in 3:18, 20, 22 (twice), 23, 24 (twice), 4:1 (twice) `[T]`. The passage's Leitwort.
- **ὑπακούετε ("obey")** — ×2, 3:20 and 3:22, the verb's only uses in the letter; with **κατὰ πάντα ("in everything")** ×2, the same two verses, the phrase's only uses in the letter `[T]`. Elsewhere in Paul the words stand together only at Rom 3:2, κατὰ πάντα τρόπον ("in every way") `[T]`.
- **ἐν κυρίῳ ("in the Lord")** — 3:18, 3:20; in the rest of the letter only 4:7 (Tychicus, "fellow slave in the Lord") and 4:17 (Archippus's ministry "in the Lord") `[T]`.
- **εἰδότες ὅτι ("knowing that")** — 3:24 and 4:1 `[T]`.
- **The δουλ- family ("slave")** — δοῦλοι ("slaves"), δουλεύετε ("serve"), δούλοις ("slaves"); three times `[T]`.
- **ἀδικ- ("wrong")** — ἀδικῶν ("doing wrong"), ἠδίκησεν ("he did wrong"); one verse, polyptoton `[T]`.
- **ἄνθρωπ- ("man, human")** — ἀνθρωπάρεσκοι ("men-pleasers", 3:22), ἀνθρώποις ("men", 3:23) `[T]`.
- **καρδία ("heart") / ψυχή ("soul")** — 3:22 / 3:23, the inner person twice in two verses `[T]`.
- **μή ("not")** — four times (3:19, 21 twice, 22). Each superior receives a negative command, and so do the slaves ("not with eye-service") `[T]`.
- **Idea-level repetition:** *reward and repayment* (3:24–25, ἀπολήμψεσθε "you will receive" / κομίσεται "he will receive back"); *the inner disposition* (3:19 not embittered; 3:21 not losing heart; 3:22 sincerity of heart; 3:23 from the soul); *the Lord as audience* (3:20 pleasing in the Lord; 3:22 fearing the Lord; 3:23 as for the Lord). The passage keeps asking who is watching and what is happening inside `[I]`.

### 11. Quotation/Allusion

**Book-level OT source inventory (Move 2 preparation).** The overview's Intertextual Map has no row for this passage, and the letter has no formula quotation `[S: overview]`/`[T]`. No OT source used earlier in Colossians — Ps 110:1 at 3:1, Isa 29:13 at 2:22, Gen 1 at 3:10 — touches the household. Move 2 therefore finds nothing to track. Colossians also **does not quote the fifth commandment** at the children's line. Ephesians does, at the same point: "Honour your father and mother, which is the first commandment with a promise" (Eph 6:2–3, Exod 20:12 / Deut 5:16) `[T]`. That is consistent with the letter's citation-light character `[S: overview]`. It is a comparison with Ephesians, not an absence argument within Colossians.

**The scan (six runs).** I ran the scan over all nine verses (the tool takes one chapter, so the range was supplied directly). It finds **no OT partner**:
- The best is Proverbs 28, median 4. It ranks in the top four in all four window runs, but 16th and 25th as a whole chapter.
- 1 Maccabees 2 has a median of 7.
- Hosea 9 has a median of 8, and Isaiah 63 of 9.
- Deuteronomy 21 has a median of 11, but ranks first in two of the three rare-word runs.
- 1 Chronicles 29 has a median of 13 (Swete) `[T]`.

Read for sense, these partners share the generic vocabulary of family, justice and service. Proverbs 28 shares "love", "wrong", "man", "just", "work", "know", "obey", "soul". 1 Maccabees 2 shares "wife", "children", "father", "slave", "inheritance", "simplicity". Every shared word has its ordinary sense. Two of the passage's distinctive words, ὀφθαλμοδουλία ("eye-service") and προσωπολημψία ("partiality"), have **no match anywhere in Swete** `[T]`. **The scan finds a field — the household and the just judge — not a source.** That agrees with the overview's warning not to manufacture OT allusions in this letter `[S: overview]`.

**No direct quotation and no high-confidence allusion; so the full Citation Triad is not required.** The candidates below are moderate or lower and receive Move 1 only, with one line on their OT-to-OT relations where those are already in the OT itself. All of them are queued for the claim audit.

**2 Chronicles 19:6–7 → 3:22–25** *(low–moderate; a cluster, not a phrase)* `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for any link

*Source context.* Jehoshaphat appoints judges in the fortified cities of Judah (2 Chr 19:5) and charges them:

> "Consider what you are doing, for you do not judge for man (οὐκ ἀνθρώπῳ … ἀλλ᾽ ἢ τῷ κυρίῳ, 'not for man … but for the Lord') but for the LORD … Now then let the fear of the LORD (φόβος Κυρίου) be upon you; be very careful what you do, for the LORD our God will have no part in unrighteousness (ἀδικία) or partiality (θαυμάσαι πρόσωπον, 'admiring a face') or the taking of a bribe." (19:6–7)

He charges the Jerusalem court the same way: "thus you shall do in the fear of the LORD, faithfully and wholeheartedly" (19:9). The paragraph is a royal reform of justice, and the setting is the people turned back "to the LORD, the God of their fathers" (19:4) `[T]`.

*The contact.* Col 3:22–25 has four elements of 19:6–7, though not in its words:
- "fearing the Lord" (3:22);
- "as for the Lord and not for men" (3:23);
- "he who does wrong" (ὁ ἀδικῶν, 3:25);
- "there is no partiality" (οὐκ ἔστιν προσωπολημψία, 3:25), against 19:7's "there is no (οὐκ ἔστιν) … unrighteousness, nor partiality".

The counts:
- A fear-word, κύριος ("Lord"), an ἀδικ- word ("wrong") and a πρόσωπον ("face") idiom stand in one verse of Swete at 2 Chr 19:7 only.
- With an ἄνθρωπ- word ("man") added, within two verses either side, the cluster stands at 2 Chr 19:5–8, Isa 29:21–23, Job 11:13–14, Prov 29:25–27, Jer 40:7 and Jdt 11:3, and only 2 Chr 19 names partiality as a judge's sin.
- "Not for man … but for the Lord" (οὐκ ἀνθρώπῳ … κυρίῳ, "not for man … for the Lord") stands in two Swete verses, 1 Chr 29:1 ("the work is great, for the palace is not for man but for the LORD God") and 2 Chr 19:6 — both in Chronicles.
- The Rahlfs export agrees on 2 Chr 19:6–7 and on 1 Chr 29:1, which adds ἡ οἰκοδομή ("the building") `[T]`.
- 1 Chr 29:17 also has ἐν ἁπλότητι καρδίας ("in sincerity of heart"), the phrase of Col 3:22 `[T]`.

The scan cannot see any of this, because it matches single stems and the partiality idiom has no stem in common with προσωπολημψία ("partiality"): 2 Chronicles 19 ranks at a median of 761, 1 Chronicles 29 at 13 `[T]`.

*Weighing it.*
- *Volume:* four elements, but no shared phrase.
- *Recurrence:* Paul does not quote Chronicles `[S]`.
- *Thematic coherence:* strong — work and judgment done for the Lord, under his fear, before his impartiality.
- *A shared tradition explains it equally well:* Eph 6:5–9 has the same elements ("fear", "as to the Lord and not to men", "no partiality with him").

What survives is that the code's language of the impartial Lord is the Chronicler's language of the impartial LORD. That is a field, and a moderate-or-lower contact. *Low–moderate; queued (C35).*

**Leviticus 25:39–55 → 3:22; 4:1** *(low–moderate; conceptual)* `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for any link

*Source context.* The jubilee law of the Israelite debt-slave:

> "If a countryman of yours becomes so poor with regard to you that he sells himself to you, you shall not subject him to a slave's service (οὐ δουλεύσει σοι δουλίαν οἰκέτου, 'he shall not serve you a servant's slavery'). (Lev 25:39–43; Swete)

The section closes: "For the sons of Israel are My servants; they are My servants whom I brought out from the land of Egypt" (25:55). The Hebrew at 25:43, לֹא־תִרְדֶּה בוֹ בְּפָרֶךְ ("you shall not rule over him with harshness"), uses פֶּרֶךְ ("harshness"), the word of Egypt's oppression at Exod 1:13–14 (WLC) `[T]`. The Swete verse has "the Lord your God" where the Hebrew has "your God" `[T]`.

*The contact.* There is no shared phrase. Two ideas meet:
- **The fear of God is the master's restraint** in Leviticus, and in Colossians it is given to the slave (3:22).
- **The master's slaves belong to another Master** — "they are My servants" (Lev 25:42, 55); "you too have a Master in heaven" (Col 4:1).

Leviticus applies the ownership argument only to Israelite brothers. The same chapter allows chattel slaves from the nations (25:44–46) `[T]`. Colossians applies the "other Master" to every master and slave in the church, with no ethnic boundary — compare 3:11, "barbarian, Scythian, slave and free" `[I]`. The neighbouring Philemon dig, reached independently, already points to Lev 25:39–46 for "brother" with a slave word `[S: Philemon dig]`. *Low–moderate (conceptual); queued (C36).*

**Isaiah 54:17 → 3:24** *(low–moderate; the Hebrew is closer than the Greek)*

*Source context.* The close of the song to barren Zion (Isa 54:1–17), which follows the fourth Servant Song (52:13–53:12):

> "No weapon that is formed against you will prosper … This is the heritage of the servants of the LORD (זֹאת נַחֲלַת עַבְדֵי יְהוָה, 'this is the inheritance of the servants of YHWH'), and their vindication is from Me." (54:17, WLC)

**It is the only verse in the Hebrew Bible in which an inheritance belongs to "the servants (slaves) of YHWH", plural.** The lemma search for נַחֲלָה ("inheritance") with עֶבֶד ("servant, slave") returns twelve verses. In the others the servant is singular (Ps 136:22, "Israel His servant"), or a human master's (Ezek 46:17; Prov 17:2), or the inheritance is God's own (Isa 63:17) (WLC) `[T]`. So the Greek keeps "inheritance", "serve" and "the Lord" but loses "slaves". This is category 1 of the triage — a feature of the Hebrew that the translation does not carry — and the Colossian wording, δουλεύετε ("you serve as slaves"), is closer to the Hebrew than to the Greek `[T]`/`[I]`.

Isa 63:4 has the "day of recompense" (ἀνταπόδοσις, "recompense"), and Isaiah 63 ranks ninth in the scan `[T]`. Paul does not cite Isa 54:17. He does quote the chapter's first verse, Isa 54:1 ("Rejoice, barren woman …"), word for word at Gal 4:27 (SBLGNT; Swete). That is in the argument of the slave woman and the free woman, which ends that "the son of the slave woman shall not inherit (κληρονομήσει, 'inherit') with the son of the free woman" (Gal 4:30) `[T]`. So Isaiah 54, slavery and inheritance are already joined in Paul's own reading of the chapter. There the slave cannot inherit; in Colossians the slaves of Christ do `[I]`. *Low–moderate on Isa 54:17 itself; the Galatians use raises the chapter's availability. Queued (C37).* The link to claim audit 2's C23, Isaiah 49's Servant, is suggestive, not shown `[I]`.

### 12. Genre

**Pauline epistle; paraenesis in the form of a household code.** The household code is a recognisable Hellenistic form: instructions for the three pairs of the household (husband–wife, father–children, master–slaves), descending from the "household management" topic of Greek ethics `[S]` (see Historical Background). Its reading rules:
- (a) Read it as a whole, in its pairs; neither half of a pair can be preached alone.
- (b) Read it as adapted. What differs from the convention is the message. Here that is the direct address to the subordinate, the reciprocal duties of the superior, and "the Lord" in every line.
- (c) Read it as instruction to a church inside an existing social order, not as legislation for one.

The six NT examples (Col 3:18–4:1; Eph 5:21–6:9; 1 Pet 2:18–3:7; Titus 2:1–10; 1 Tim 2:8–15; 6:1–2) share the form and differ in emphasis `[T]` for the texts; `[S]` for the classification.

### 13. Copycat

The commands are direct address to the church and are prescriptive for those addressed `[T]`. Three distinctions matter:

- **The institution is described, not commanded.** Slaves are told how to serve and masters how to rule. Nobody is told to own a slave, and the passage does not ground slavery in God's will, in creation or in Scripture `[T]`. **Regulating an institution is not endorsing it** `[I]`. The letter has already removed the distinction "in Christ" (3:11), and Philemon pushes further on a single case (Phlm 16) `[S: Philemon dig]`.
- **The posture is normative across settings.** Working "as for the Lord and not for men", sincerity of heart, impartiality and justice — the motives are not tied to Roman slavery and transfer to any human authority. The transfer must not erase the particular: these words were first spoken to people who could be beaten and sold `[I]`.
- **The roles of marriage and parenthood** are addressed as continuing relationships of the church's own life. They are not historical accidents in the way slavery is (Difficult Verses) `[I]`, *moderate — the distinction is drawn from Paul's wider teaching, not from this passage, which treats all three pairs in one form*.

### 14. Bible Timeline

This side of the cross, between Christ's enthronement (3:1) and his appearing (3:4) `[T]`. The promises of the passage are future tense. They are not yet paid:
- ἀπολήμψεσθε ("you will receive", 3:24);
- κομίσεται ("he will receive back", 3:25).

The household lives in the overlap. The new humanity exists already (3:10–11), while Roman households, Roman slavery and Roman law continue around it `[I]`. The reader today stands in the same overlap but in a different social order. The timeline caution is two-sided:
- do not apply the slave lines to modern employment as though nothing had changed;
- do not dismiss the code as belonging to a world that has passed, since the Lord and the judgment have not changed (Tool 16).

### 15. Who Am I?

Direct address, in six groups. Every hearer in the original congregation was in at least one group, and many in two — a wife who was also a slave; a father who was also a master `[I]`. Today's hearer may also be in several, and the preacher should let each group hear its own line. No figure in the passage is a type of Christ. The one Lord over all the groups is Christ himself (3:24). **Masters especially are not to hear themselves as the passage's centre; they hear that they too have a Master** (4:1) `[T]`.

### 16. So What?

**The author's intended response.** That every member of the household, in every station, should do what they do as service to the Lord Christ. Those under authority should obey sincerely, in the fear of Christ, without eye-service, and in hope of the inheritance. Those with authority should love, refrain from provoking, and give justice and equality, in the knowledge that they will answer to the same impartial Lord `[I]`.

**Worldview.**
- The household is not a private zone outside the lordship of Christ.
- Ordinary unseen work counts, because the Lord sees it and rewards it.
- Power is held under a higher Master, who judges without partiality.
- The slave is an heir.

**Stop / start.**
- *Stop:* working for the watcher's eye (eye-service); people-pleasing; bitterness towards a spouse; provoking children until they give up; using power without justice; assuming that because no human judge sees, no one does.
- *Start:* doing everything "from the soul, as for the Lord"; a spouse's love that matches the love received (3:12–14); parenting that guards a child's heart; treating those under one's authority with justice and "equality".

**Motivation.**
- The Lord's identity: he is the Lord Christ (3:24).
- His future gift: the inheritance (3:24).
- His impartial judgment (3:25).
- The master's own accountability (4:1).

The motives are not social stability or reputation. The passage gives none of these.

**Four audiences.**
- *For me:* in which relationship am I working for human eyes? In which do I hold power I have forgotten I answer for?
- *For a Christian friend:* the believer in a hard job, or under a harsh authority, who thinks their work does not count — "from the Lord you will receive the reward of the inheritance".
- *For the church community:* the gathered church in which those under authority and those with it listen to the same letter at the same time. The code is spoken in a congregation, not in a private counselling room.
- *For an unbeliever:* the passage's realism about power, and its claim that there is a Master over every master. It is also a fair place to face the question of slavery honestly (Difficult Verses).

**Prayer.** Lord Christ, Master in heaven, you see what no human master sees and you judge without partiality. Teach us to do whatever we do from the soul, as for you. Teach those of us with authority to give what is just and equal. Make us, in every house, heirs who live like heirs.

#### Grammatical and syntactical features

- **Articular nominatives as vocatives** (Αἱ γυναῖκες, "wives"; Οἱ ἄνδρες, "husbands"; and so on) `[T]`. The addressees are named as present hearers. The subordinates are addressed directly, as moral agents, rather than spoken about to the head of the household (Historical Background) `[I]`.
- **Present imperatives throughout** `[T]`: ongoing conduct, not a single act — *moderate on the aspectual weight*.
- **ὑποτάσσεσθε ("be subject")** — middle or passive in form. The middle sense, "subject yourselves", fits the voluntary framing "as is fitting in the Lord" `[I]`, *moderate*.
- **ὡς ἀνῆκεν ("as is fitting")** — imperfect of propriety, translated as present `[S]`, *moderate*.
- **ἵνα μὴ ἀθυμῶσιν ("so that they will not lose heart")** — purpose clause with the present subjunctive `[T]`.
- **φοβούμενοι τὸν κύριον ("fearing the Lord")** — circumstantial participle of cause or manner, beneath "obey" `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **τὴν ἀνταπόδοσιν τῆς κληρονομίας ("the reward of the inheritance")** — genitive of apposition ("the reward which is the inheritance") or source `[I]`, *moderate*.
- **δουλεύετε ("you serve" / "serve!")** — morphologically indicative or imperative. The SBLGNT word index tags it imperative, the English versions take it as indicative, and the Vulgate is recalled as imperative (Tool 8) `[T]` for the ambiguity.
- **ὁ ἀδικῶν ("the one who does wrong")** — generic articular participle: whoever does wrong, slave or master `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **παρέχεσθε ("grant")** — middle: supply from your own side `[S]`, *moderate*.
- **καὶ ὑμεῖς ἔχετε κύριον ("you too have a Master")** — the emphatic pronoun with καί ("too, also") puts masters in the slaves' position `[T]`.

#### Wordplay and sound

- **κύριος ("Lord, master")** — one word for both, nine times. This is the passage's governing play, and it cannot be carried into English (Tool 8) `[T]`.
- **δοῦλοι … δουλεύετε … δούλοις ("slaves … you slave … slaves")** — the slave's noun becomes the verb for serving Christ `[T]`.
- **ἀδικῶν … ἠδίκησεν ("doing wrong … he did wrong")** — polyptoton: the wrong done is the wrong received back `[T]`.
- **ἀπὸ κυρίου ἀπολήμψεσθε ("from the Lord you will receive")** — the preposition of source and the prefix of the verb chime, *apo kyriou apolēmpsesthe*. It is slight and is not pressed `[T]`, *low*.

#### Recommended verification

- The Vulgate at 3:24 (*servite*) and 4:1 (*quod iustum est et aequum*, "what is just and equal") — recalled, not checked.
- BDAG on ἰσότης ("equality"), ὀφθαλμοδουλία ("eye-service") and ἀνταπόδοσις ("reward").
- The Roman law on slaves as heirs: the *peculium*, and testamentary manumission with heirship.
- The ESV Anglicised wording at 3:22–4:1.

### Textual Variants

**3:22 — τὸν κύριον / τὸν θεόν ("the Lord" / "God").**
- **The issue:** whether the slaves fear "the Lord" (Christ) or "God".
- **Manuscript evidence (NA28 apparatus):** θεόν ("God") is read by 𝔓⁴⁶ א² D² K 104. 630, the majority text, d and the Clementine Vulgate. κύριον ("the Lord") is read by א\* A B C D\* F G L Ψ 048. 075. 0278. 33. 81. 365. 1175. 1241ˢ. 1505. 1739. 1881. 2464, it, the Stuttgart and Wordsworth–White Vulgate, the Syriac and Coptic `[T: as the apparatus prints it]`.
- **Most likely original:** κύριον ("the Lord"), on the breadth and age of the evidence. The substitution is explicable as avoiding the "fear the Lord" of slaves next to "masters" (κυρίοις) `[I]` — *high on the reading; moderate on the motive*.
- **Preaching implication:** the KJV's "fearing God" loses the point that the fear of the LORD is directed to Christ. The ESV and NASB95 keep it.

**3:24 — γάρ ("for") before τῷ κυρίῳ Χριστῷ δουλεύετε ("the Lord Christ you serve").**
- **The issue:** whether "you serve the Lord Christ" is a reason ("for you serve") or stands alone (an indicative statement or an imperative).
- **Manuscript evidence:** γάρ ("for") is read by D¹ K L Ψ 075. 104. 630. 1175. 1505, the majority text, the Syriac and Clement. It is absent from 𝔓⁴⁶ א A B C D\* F G 0278. 33. 81. 365. 1241ˢ. 1739. 1881. 2464 `[T: as the apparatus prints it]`.
- **Most likely original:** no γάρ ("for") — *high*. The addition settles the mood question for the KJV.
- **Preaching implication:** without γάρ ("for") the clause can be heard as a command, "Serve the Lord Christ!" Mention it if the sermon leans on "you are serving".

**3:21 — ἐρεθίζετε / παροργίζετε ("provoke" / "provoke to anger").**
- **The issue:** whether the Colossian verb is the Ephesian one.
- **Manuscript evidence:** παροργίζετε ("provoke to anger") is read by א A C D\* F G L 075. 0198. 0278. 33. 81. 104. 365. 1175. 1241ˢ. 1505 and Ambrosiaster. The apparatus marks it as from Eph 6:4. NA28 prints ἐρεθίζετε ("provoke") with 𝔓⁴⁶ᵛⁱᵈ B D¹ K Ψ 630. 1739. 1881. 2464, the majority text and Clement `[T: as the apparatus prints it]`.
- **Most likely original:** ἐρεθίζετε ("provoke"), as the less harmonised reading. **Moderate** — the witnesses for the harmonised reading are strong.
- **Preaching implication:** none for the sense; it bears on the Colossians–Ephesians comparison (Tool 11).

**3:23 — καὶ οὐκ ἀνθρώποις ("and not for men").** Omitted by 𝔓⁴⁶ B 1739 and Ambrosiaster `[T: as the apparatus prints it]`. NA28 prints the words. The omission is early and weighty. If original, the omission removes the "not for men" from Colossians, and Echo #21 then rests on ἀνθρωπάρεσκοι ("men-pleasers", 3:22) alone. *Moderate* that the words are original. The parallel in Eph 6:7 makes harmonisation possible either way.

**Minor units.**
- 3:18 adds ὑμῶν ("your") or ἰδίοις ("own"; KJV "your own husbands").
- 3:19 adds ὑμῶν ("your").
- 3:20 reads τῷ κυρίῳ ("to the Lord") for ἐν κυρίῳ ("in the Lord") in a few witnesses (Tool 8).
- 3:22 κατὰ πάντα ("in everything") is omitted by 𝔓⁴⁶ 075. 0278. 81 — the parallel Eph 6:5 lacks it.
- 3:22 ὀφθαλμοδουλίαις ("eye-services"), plural,, in the majority text and the SBLGNT.
- 3:23 δουλεύοντες ("serving") is added, from Eph 6:7.
- 3:24 λήμψεσθε ("you will receive") for ἀπολήμψεσθε ("you will receive").
- 3:25 παρὰ τῷ θεῷ ("with God") is added in the Western witnesses, from Eph 6:9.
- 4:1 οὐρανοῖς ("heavens"), plural,, in the majority text — as at Eph 6:9.

Several of these are harmonisations to Ephesians. The scribes heard the two codes as one text and smoothed Colossians towards Ephesians `[I]`.

### Historical and Cultural Background

#### Setting

- **Time period:** the 50s–60s AD, if Pauline; the letter's own claim is Paul `[S: overview]`.
- **Location:** Colossae in the Lycus valley of Phrygia (province of Asia), near Laodicea and Hierapolis (2:1; 4:13) `[T]`.
- **Social context:** a church meeting in houses (4:15) `[T]`. The household (οἶκος, "household") — family, slaves, freedmen and dependants — was the basic social and economic unit `[S]`.

#### What the original audience knew

1. **The household-management topic.** Since Aristotle, Greek ethics had discussed the household under three pairs, master and slave, husband and wife, father and children (*Politics* 1.1253b) `[S]` — *high on the topic; check the reference*. Hellenistic Jewish writers used the same scheme to show that Jewish households were orderly: Philo, *Hypothetica* 7.14 and *Decalogue* 165–167; Josephus, *Against Apion* 2.199–210; Pseudo-Phocylides 175–227 `[S]` — *moderate on the exact references*. The Colossians would hear the form as familiar.
2. **The usual direction of address.** The traditional discussions addressed the head of the household about how to rule the others `[S]`. **A code that speaks directly to wives, children and slaves, and gives them a motive of their own, treats them as moral agents and members of the assembly** `[I]`/`[S]` — widely noted.
3. **Slavery in Roman Asia.**
   - Slaves were the property of their masters, could be bought, sold, beaten and bequeathed, and had no legal family. Many worked in households, sometimes in skilled roles `[S]`.
   - Manumission was common, and freedmen often remained clients of former owners `[S]`.
   - A slave could hold a *peculium* ("allowance") but not inherit in his own right `[S]` — *moderate*.
   - Phrygian slaves had a poor reputation in the Roman world `[S: Philemon Logos round 1, Lightfoot]`.
   - Ancient slavery was not race-based in the way of Atlantic slavery `[S]`. That difference must not be used to make it sound benign.
4. **Fathers' power.** The Roman *patria potestas* gave the father legal power over his children for life `[S]`. A command to fathers not to provoke their children "so that they will not lose heart" limits that power from the child's side `[I]`.
5. **Jewish law and wisdom on slaves and masters.** The Torah forbade ruling an Israelite debt-slave "with severity" and grounded the command in the fear of God (Lev 25:43). Job swore that he had not despised his slaves' claims: "what then could I do when God arises? … Did not He who made me in the womb make him?" (Job 31:13–15). Sirach commands, "do not ill-treat a household slave who works faithfully … let your soul love an intelligent slave; do not withhold his freedom" (Sir 7:20–21, Swete) `[T]` for the texts. Jewish members of the congregation (4:10–11) would know that masters answer to God `[I]`.
6. **The letter is carried by a slave.** Onesimus comes with Tychicus as "our faithful and beloved brother, who is one of your number" (4:9) `[T]`. If he is the Onesimus of Philemon, his master's household is in the same congregation `[I]`/`[S: Philemon dig]`.

#### What this changes about how we read

The code does not invent the household order; it inherits a familiar form. **What Colossians adds is the content of every line**:
- the direct address to the weak;
- the limits on the strong;
- "the Lord" in all but one line (3:19, though the husbands' "love" answers 3:12–14);
- the slave's inheritance;
- the master's Master.

Preaching the form as if it were the message misses what the Colossians would have heard as new.

### Original Audience Reception

#### Canonical audience

- **Canonical section:** Pauline corpus, after Ephesians. The canonical reader has just met the fuller Ephesian code, with Christ and the church at its centre, and will hear Colossians' version as a shorter echo `[I]`. The overview's warning applies: read Colossians on its own terms first `[S: overview]`.
- **What the canonical reader knows:** the Gospels, including Jesus as one who serves (Mark 10:45); Paul's "slave of Christ" (Rom 1:1; Gal 1:10); 1 Cor 7:21–23 ("you were bought with a price; do not become slaves of men"); Gal 3:28 `[T]`.
- **Canonical register:** Philemon follows four letters later and makes this code personal `[S: Philemon dig]`.

#### The first hearers

- **Who they were:** a largely Gentile house-church congregation, including wives and husbands, children and fathers, slaves and masters, listening to the letter read aloud (4:16) `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **Their situation:** under pressure from a rival teaching that offered fuller spirituality through regimen and vision (2:16–23) `[T]`.
- **What they brought:** the familiar household form; the expectation that religion would reinforce the household's order; the slaves' expectation of being spoken about, not to `[I]`/`[S]`.

#### Where the passage fits their world

The masters would expect to hear how to rule. They hear instead that they are themselves under a Master and must "grant equality" `[I]`. The slaves would expect to hear that they must obey. They do — but they also hear that their work is for Christ, that their reward is the inheritance, and that wrongdoing (theirs or anyone's) will be repaid without partiality `[I]`. Most strikingly, the slaves are given the passage's longest address `[T]`.

#### Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances

- **Shocking:** to masters — "you too have a Master" (4:1), and a command to supply ἰσότης ("equality") to their slaves `[I]`. To slaves — that they are heirs (3:24) `[T]`.
- **Surprising:** that "the Lord" is in every line; that wives "be subject" but are not told to "obey"; that children are protected from fathers' provocation (3:21) `[T]`.
- **Comforting:** to slaves and to anyone working unseen — "from the Lord you will receive"; to the wronged — the Lord repays without partiality (3:24–25) `[T]`.
- **Disturbing:** to slaves who hoped faith would free them — obey "in everything"; to anyone who does wrong in the household, slave or master — "he who does wrong will receive the consequences" (3:25) `[T]`/`[I]`.

#### What we bring that they didn't

- A post-abolition conscience that hears any regulation of slavery as complicity, or the reverse habit of translating "slave" as "employee" and so softening the text `[I]`.
- Modern egalitarian instincts about marriage, which may make "be subject" heard only as oppression, or a reactive defensiveness which hears it only as a slogan `[I]`.
- Individualism: hearing the code as advice to private households rather than as a word spoken in an assembly where all six groups listen together `[I]`.
- The history of the code's misuse to defend slavery in the nineteenth century `[S]` — *high that it happened; consult a history before citing particulars*.

#### Implication for the sermon

Give the slaves' section the weight the text gives it, and preach its promises (inheritance, reward, the Lord's sight) as well as its commands. Preach 4:1 as the climax, not as a footnote. Name the question of slavery honestly rather than either excusing it or stepping round it (Difficult Verses).

### Biblical-Theological Themes

#### 1. The Lordship of Christ over the household (kingdom)

- **Where this passage sits:** the kingdom of the Son (1:13) worked out in the most ordinary sphere. "The Lord Christ" (3:24) rules the house, and the masters' authority is now derivative (4:1) `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **Before:** the LORD as "Lord of lords" who shows no partiality (Deut 10:17); the LORD as the owner of Israel's slaves (Lev 25:42, 55); the fear of the LORD as the beginning of wisdom `[T]` for the texts.
- **After:** the Lord who will repay each according to works (2 Cor 5:10); the heavenly Master of Eph 6:9; the King of kings and Lord of lords (Rev 19:16) `[T]`.
- **What it contributes:** the household is not autonomous; every authority in it is held from Christ and answerable to him `[I]`.
- **Confidence:** high.

#### 2. Inheritance — from the land to the saints, and to the slave

- **Where this passage sits:** the inheritance promised to Abraham's seed, reframed in Christ as "the share of the lot of the saints in Light" (1:12) and here given explicitly to slaves (3:24) `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **Before:**
  - the land as Israel's allotted inheritance;
  - the LORD as the Levites' inheritance (Num 18:20; Deut 10:9);
  - "the heritage of the servants of the LORD" (Isa 54:17, Hebrew);
  - the wise slave sharing the inheritance among the brothers (Prov 17:2) `[T]`.
- **After:** Gal 4:7, "no longer a slave, but a son … an heir"; Rom 8:17, "heirs of God and fellow heirs with Christ"; 1 Pet 1:4 `[T]`.
- **What it contributes:** the slave's identity before God is "heir", which no Roman law could grant. That is the gospel's answer, already present in the code, to what the code does not yet abolish `[I]`.
- **Confidence:** moderate–high.

#### 3. The impartial judge

- **Where this passage sits:** God's justice "without partiality", applied in a household where partiality was the structure `[I]`.
- **Before:** Lev 19:15; Deut 1:17; 10:17; 16:19; 2 Chr 19:6–7 — the LORD's impartiality as the standard for human judges `[T]`.
- **After:** Acts 10:34; Rom 2:11; Eph 6:9; Jas 2:1, 9; 1 Pet 1:17 `[T]`.
- **What it contributes:** both parties are held under the same court. The slave cannot plead status to excuse wrong, and the master cannot plead status to escape judgment (3:25; 4:1) `[I]`.
- **Confidence:** high on the theme; moderate on any specific OT source.

**Confluence.** The three themes are in **climactic convergence**. They meet in the single figure of "the Lord Christ" (3:24). He is the Lord who rules the household, the Lord from whom the inheritance comes, and the Lord who judges without partiality. The passage names all three in three verses (3:24–4:1) `[I]`.

### Christological Reading

#### Type of connection

NT; Christ is named. The live connection is the **transfer of the OT's κύριος ("Lord") to Christ**. Colossians takes the formula "fear the Lord" (seventy Swete verses, Tool 7) and directs it to the one called "the Lord Christ" two verses later `[T]`/`[I]`, *moderate–high*. Whether Paul expects his readers to hear the LXX formula in φοβούμενοι τὸν κύριον ("fearing the Lord"), or simply uses the church's word for Christ, is not settled by the text. The majority reading "God" shows that some scribes felt the weight `[I]`.

#### How the passage points to Christ

- **Christ as Lord of every station.** The same Lord stands over wives and husbands, children and fathers, slaves and masters (3:18–4:1) `[T]`.
- **Christ as the slave's Master and the master's Master** (3:24; 4:1) `[T]`.
- **Christ as the giver of the inheritance** (3:24, with 1:12) `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **Christ as the impartial judge** (3:25, read with 2 Cor 5:10) `[I]`.

#### Trajectory

- **What the passage anticipates:** the full inheritance (future tense, 3:24); the repayment of every wrong (3:25); the revelation of Christ and his people "in glory" (3:4) `[T]`.
- **What Christ fulfils:** the "Lord of lords" who does not show partiality (Deut 10:17) is now named as Christ `[I]`.
- **What remains (already / not yet):** the household's inequalities remain in this age. The equality the masters are told to grant (4:1) and the equality of status in Christ (3:11) are already real, but not yet complete `[I]`.

#### Moralism check

- **The "be like X" temptation:** "be a good wife / good worker / good parent" — a sermon of roles and duties without the Lord Christ. That is exactly a regimen, which this letter rejects (2:20–23) `[S: overview]`.
- **The gospel grounding:** the household lives from 3:1–17 — raised with Christ, new humanity, "Christ is all" — and towards the inheritance and the judgment `[T]`.
- **Christ as hero:** he is the Master in heaven who gives slaves the inheritance and holds masters to account. The household's dignity comes from him, not from its members' performance `[I]`.

#### Confidence

High on the lordship; moderate–high on the fear-of-the-LORD transfer; moderate on the inheritance link to 1:12.

### Difficult / Contested Verses

#### 3:22–4:1 — Slavery

- **Category:** ethical; apologetic; pastoral.
- **The difficulty:** the passage tells slaves to obey "in everything" and does not tell masters to free their slaves. It has been used to defend slavery `[S]`.
- **Common misreadings:** (a) "the Bible endorses slavery"; (b) "δοῦλος ("slave") just means employee or servant" — softening the word; (c) treating the passage as irrelevant to a world without legal slavery.
- **Rhetorical function in context:** the longest address in the code, framed by two lordships (Tool 3). It gives slaves an identity (heirs, servants of the Lord Christ) and masters an accountability (a Master in heaven, justice, equality). It follows 3:11, which removes "slave and free" as a distinction in Christ `[T]`.
- **The main interpretive options:**
  - (i) The code accepts the institution as a fact of the age and works within it, while sowing principles that dissolve it — 3:11; Gal 3:28; Phlm 16; 1 Cor 7:21 ("if you are able also to become free, rather do that"); 1 Tim 1:10 (slave-traders listed among the lawless) `[S]` for the reading.
  - (ii) The code reflects a conservative accommodation by the church to its society `[S]`.
  - (iii) The code's address to slaves as moral agents and heirs is itself already a subversion of the institution's logic `[S]`/`[I]`.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:**
  - The passage does not command or justify slavery. It regulates conduct within it, and its theological grounds (one Lord; impartiality; inheritance; "equality") stand against the institution's assumptions `[I]`, *moderate–high*.
  - It also does not abolish the institution, and it does tell slaves to obey "in everything". The preacher should name both halves `[T]`.
  - 1 Cor 7:21 is debated in its own right ("rather make use of it") `[S]`.
- **Suggested handling:** address it directly and briefly in the sermon; do not pretend δοῦλος ("slave") means "employee"; apply to employment by analogy, saying so. Be aware that there may be hearers whose families' histories include slavery or modern trafficking.

#### 3:18 — "Wives, be subject to your husbands"

- **Category:** doctrinal (contested among orthodox Christians); pastoral.
- **The difficulty:** the verse has been used to demand obedience and to excuse abuse; it is also heard as offensive in itself.
- **Common misreadings:** "submit" as "obey" (no NT verse commands a wife to obey her husband; 1 Pet 3:6 commends Sarah's obedience as an example — Tool 7); submission as unconditional; or the verse preached without 3:19.
- **Rhetorical function:** the first of three paired commands; the qualifier "as is fitting in the Lord" both motivates and limits; the husband's line immediately restrains (love; not embittered) `[T]`.
- **The main interpretive options:** complementarian (a lasting ordered relationship within marriage, grounded elsewhere in Paul in creation — 1 Cor 11:8–9; Eph 5:23) and egalitarian (a culturally located instruction, its permanent principle the mutual love and service of Eph 5:21) `[S]`.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** Colossians itself gives no grounding beyond "fitting in the Lord", has no mutual-submission clause, and treats marriage in nineteen words `[T]`. **It neither settles the wider debate nor supports either side's maximal claim** `[I]`. Whatever "be subject" means, it is voluntary (middle voice), "in the Lord" (so it cannot require what the Lord forbids), and paired with a love the husbands are to give as they have been loved (3:12, 14, 19) `[I]`.
- **Suggested handling:** name the qualifiers and the pairing; do not preach 3:18 without 3:19; say plainly that the verse gives no warrant for abuse, and that the church's protection of the abused is "fitting in the Lord".

#### 3:20, 22 — "in everything" (κατὰ πάντα)

- **Category:** pastoral; ethical.
- **The difficulty:** is the obedience unconditional?
- **Rhetorical function:** emphasis on wholeheartedness, inside a frame where "in the Lord" and "fearing the Lord" set the ultimate authority `[T]`/`[I]`.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** obedience "in everything" is obedience given "as for the Lord", which cannot include what the Lord forbids (compare Acts 5:29) `[I]`, *high*. The phrase is not a licence for abusive parents or masters.
- **Suggested handling:** acknowledge briefly; ground the limit in the text's own "in the Lord".

#### 3:24 — "You serve the Lord Christ": statement or command?

- **Category:** interpretive (grammatical).
- **The options:** indicative (ESV, NASB95, NIV84; KJV with the majority γάρ, "for") — the slaves' true identity; imperative (the Vulgate, recalled) — the final command of the slave section `[T]`/`[S]`.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** both are grammatically possible. The asyndeton and NA28's punctuation leave the clause standing alone. The indicative fits the surrounding "knowing that"; the imperative fits the passage's string of commands. The theology is the same either way `[I]`, *moderate*.
- **Suggested handling:** preach the identity; mention the command.

#### 3:25 — Who is "the one who does wrong"?

- **Category:** interpretive; pastoral.
- **The options:** (a) the slave who wrongs his master — the γάρ ("for") ties the verse to the slave address, and Phlm 18 uses the same verb of Onesimus; (b) the master who wrongs his slave — "no partiality" is the masters' line in Eph 6:9; (c) anyone — generic participle, standing between the two addresses `[T]`/`[I]`/`[S]`.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** grammar ties 3:25 to the slaves (γάρ), but the generic participle and the principle "no partiality" open it to both. The masters' address follows at once. Reading (c) best fits the passage's balance (Tool 3). *Moderate.*
- **Suggested handling:** let the verse face both ways, as the text does.

#### 4:1 — "Justice and equality" (τὸ δίκαιον καὶ τὴν ἰσότητα)

- **Category:** interpretive; ethical.
- **The options:** "fairness / equity" (NASB95, ESV) — fair treatment within the relationship; "equality" — treat slaves as equals, a sense ἰσότης ("equality") has in its other NT use (2 Cor 8:13–14) `[T]`/`[S]`.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the range includes both; the ordinary sense is "equality". With 3:11 and "you too have a Master", a stronger sense is at least in view `[I]`, *moderate*. The pulpit text has already chosen the weaker sense.
- **Suggested handling:** tell the congregation the word.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: Preaching the code as a free-standing hierarchy

- **What it looks like:** "God's order for the family: wives submit, children obey, workers serve" — a sermon on roles.
- **Why it's wrong:** every line is "in the Lord" or "for the Lord"; every superior is restrained; the code is 3:17 put to work and follows 3:11 (Headline Findings 1, 2, 4). It is also exactly the moralism the letter warns against (2:20–23) `[S: overview]`.
- **The corrective:** preach the Lord before the roles; give the superiors' lines equal weight; end at 4:1.

### Pitfall: Preaching Ephesians' proportions

- **What it looks like:** most of the sermon on marriage, a paragraph on children, a closing application to "work".
- **Why it's wrong:** Colossians gives marriage nineteen words and slaves fifty-six (Headline Findings 3). It has none of Ephesians' Christ-and-church material, and importing it harmonises the two texts.
- **The corrective:** let Colossians set the balance; preach Ephesians 5 when preaching Ephesians.

### Pitfall: "Slaves" as "employees"

- **What it looks like:** "Paul is talking about your relationship with your boss."
- **Why it's wrong:** the word is δοῦλος ("slave"), people owned by other people (overview Trap 4). The ESV's "bondservants" already softens it. The analogy to employment is real but secondary, and making it primary drains the passage of its force for the people it first addressed.
- **The corrective:** say "slave"; preach the first hearers' situation; then draw the analogy and say it is one.

### Pitfall: Using 3:18 against the vulnerable

- **What it looks like:** "submit" heard as "obey whatever you are told", including abuse; or preached without 3:19.
- **Why it's wrong:** the verb is "be subject", not "obey" (no NT verse commands a wife to obey; 1 Pet 3:6 offers Sarah as an example); it is "as is fitting in the Lord"; the husband's line follows at once, and his love answers the love received (3:12, 14, 19) (Difficult Verses).
- **The corrective:** preach the pair together; state that the text gives no cover to abuse.

### Pitfall: "Whatever you do, work heartily" as a productivity motto

- **What it looks like:** 3:23 on the office wall — work hard because God likes hard work.
- **Why it's wrong:** the verse is addressed to slaves, and its ground is that the Lord sees, rewards with the inheritance and judges without partiality (3:24–25). It is the opposite of eye-service, not a call to output `[T]`.
- **The corrective:** keep 3:23 with 3:22–25 and 3:17.

### Pitfall: Skipping 4:1

- **What it looks like:** the sermon ends with the slaves and treats masters as an afterthought.
- **Why it's wrong:** 4:1 closes the frame of the slave address (3:22 "masters according to the flesh" / 4:1 "a Master in heaven"), holds the masters to "equality", and is the passage's load-bearing truth (Tool 3).
- **The corrective:** make 4:1 the climax.
