# Claim Audit: Colossians

**Passages audited:** Col 1:15–20; 1:24–2:5; 2:6–15; with the letter-level claims of the book overview (1:26–4:4; 2:16–3:16; 4:2–18)
**Date:** 21 October 2026
**Version:** 1.0
**Purpose:** The first claim audit on Colossians. It covers the whole queue — fourteen distinct candidates (C1–C8, C10–C15; C9 is C1) — from the book overview (v0.1.1) and the three solo digs (1:15–20 v1.2; 1:24–2:5 v1.1; 2:6–15 v1.1). Trigger 1 had fired (fourteen items, three digs, no audit). Trigger 2 was armed for point-purpose on 1:15–20 by C7 (Headline 3) and C1.
**Primary texts:** SBLGNT with its lemma index (counts); NA28 and its apparatus (Logos export) for Colossians and Ephesians; Swete's LXX (word-beginning and stem-anywhere searches, every hit read for sense); Rahlfs (Logos exports) for Psalms, Proverbs, Isaiah, Genesis and both Greek texts of Daniel; the WLC with its lemma index for the Hebrew.
**Method:** Two blind auditors, run one after the other (Patrick's two-auditor rule of 16 October 2026). Auditor A took the six single-contact claims: wording, rarity, rivals and Greek. Auditor B took the seven field and design claims: block tests, chance baselines and rank stability. Each read a brief, a claims file and the corpus only. Neither saw the digs, the overview, the Logos answers or the other's report. The main session then reproduced the results each verdict rests on (see *Spot checks*).

Warrant tags: `[T]` the text states or shows it · `[I]` a reasonable inference from the text · `[S]` a secondary source or recalled scholarship · `[S: Logos 1]` Patrick's Logos round of 21 October 2026.

## Positional Frame (Phase 0.6)

**What the preceding movement sets up.** The letter opens by moving its readers from "the domain (ἐξουσία) of darkness" into "the kingdom of His beloved Son" (1:13). The confession (1:15–20) then says who the Son is. The apostle's labour (1:24–2:5) says how that confession reached Gentiles who have never seen him. The charge (2:6–15) turns both into a command and a ground `[T]`. Every claim in this queue is a claim that some source, or some structure, helps the letter do that.

**Why the frame matters for the verdicts.** Colossians quotes nothing. It has no formula quotation, and its argument runs on internal repetition (overview, Intertextual Map) `[T]`. So the right question for each OT candidate is whether it adds anything the letter's own repetitions do not already supply. Where an internal chain explains a passage, an external source must earn its place above that chain. Two verdicts below (C3, C15) turn partly on this.

## Claims Audited

### Claim C1: Psalm 68:16 (LXX 67:17) behind Col 1:19 (and 2:9)

**Source:** Overview, Intertextual Map row 4 (moderate–high on wording; function moderate; "first target for the claim audit"); 1:15–20 dig, Tool 11. Load-bearing if a backbone uses the temple theme.
**Claim:** εὐδοκέω ("be pleased") with κατοικέω ("dwell") stands in one verse of Swete (Ps 67:17, with ἐν αὐτῷ) and one verse of the NT (Col 1:19). 1:19 is therefore temple language: what the psalm said of God's chosen mountain, the confession says of the Son.

**Findings.**
- The pair is unique in both corpora, as stated `[T]`: Ps 67:17 in Swete and Col 1:19 in the SBLGNT. Rahlfs has the same wording.
- **Uniqueness alone is at chance level.** εὐδοκ- stands in 85 Swete verses and κατοικ- in 560, so about 1.6 co-occurrences would be expected by chance `[T]` (Auditor A). In a random sample of 300 Pauline verses, 45 % contain some pair of moderately common words that meet in exactly one Swete verse (Auditor A; not re-run by the main session).
- **What lifts the contact above chance is the construction.** In both texts the aorist εὐδόκησεν governs an infinitive of κατοικέω — κατοικεῖν in the psalm, κατοικῆσαι in Colossians — with an ἐν αὐτῷ phrase. That construction stands nowhere else in Swete `[T]`. In Hebrew too, Ps 68:17 is the only verse joining חָמַד ("desire") with שָׁכַן ("dwell") (WLC) `[T]`.
- **The theme is wider than the psalm.** Other texts say the same thing in other words: Hag 1:8 ("build the house, and I will be pleased with it", εὐδοκήσω ἐν αὐτῷ); 3 Macc 2:15–16 (εὐδόκησας of the glory, beside κατοικητήριον, "dwelling place"); Ps 131:13–14 (the Lord chose Zion for his dwelling; Hebrew Ps 132:13–14) `[T]`.
- **Window ranks do not decide.** Ps 67 ranks 13th–216th against 1:15–20, and the stated range reproduces exactly. But Eph 4:7–10, which quotes Ps 67:19 outright, ranks Ps 67 only 7th–30th. A low field rank therefore does not tell against a single-verse contact `[T]`.
- Ps 67:19 is quoted at Eph 4:8, so the psalm was in use in the Pauline circle `[T]`. Eph 4:8's ἀναβάς agrees with Swete against Rahlfs' ἀνέβης.
- 2:9 adds no independent evidence; it repeats 1:19's verb and subject `[T]`. The letter carries the dwelling language on to 3:16, ἐνοικείτω ("let … dwell in you") `[T]` — a link the claim did not note.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | The psalm is quoted at Eph 4:8 |
| Volume | Possible → Strong | The pair is at chance level; the identical construction is unique |
| Recurrence | Possible | 2:9 repeats it; the dwelling verbs continue at 3:16 |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | Dwelling → reconciliation → peace by the cross (1:19–20) |
| Historical plausibility | Strong | Zion texts applied to the Messiah are well within Second-Temple practice `[S]` |
| History of interpretation | Possible `[S]` | Raised in temple readings of Colossians; a minority report |
| Satisfaction | Possible | It explains the unusual "pleased … to dwell" |

**Verdict:** **Confirmed with nuance.** **Moderate–high on wording; moderate on function.**

**Reasoning:** The contact rests on a unique construction, not on a rare pair. The temple function is coherent, but it belongs to a Zion-dwelling tradition (Hag 1:8; Ps 131/132; 3 Macc 2), and Ps 68 is the nearest wording within it, not the only source. For the 1:19 subject question (1:15–20 dig v1.2), the psalm's ὁ θεός as subject of both verbs may now be cited as *support*, at moderate.

**Book-overview action required:** Row 4: keep the ratings. Add "the construction, not the pair, is unique", and add the Zion-dwelling companions. Add ἐνοικέω (3:16) to the dwelling chain. **A sermon may use the temple theme**, framed as "the nearest wording within a wider tradition".

### Claim C2: "The confession is the letter's arsenal" (1:15–20 redeployed in 2:8–15)

**Source:** Overview, Arc Map (design, not baselined); 2:6–15 dig, Headline 2 (first baseline; high on links, moderate–high as design).
**Claim:** The confession's vocabulary is redeployed in the polemic. 2:8–14 shares seven of 1:15–20's 24 rarer words; no stretch outside 2:5–19 shares more than three; 14 of the letter's 90 six-verse passages have a partner sharing seven or eight.

**Findings.**
- **Every named link holds by lemma** `[T]`:
  - ὅτι ἐν αὐτῷ ("for in him") at 1:16, 1:19 and 2:9, and elsewhere in the NT only at 1 John 2:5 and 4:13;
  - πλήρωμα ("fullness") with κατοικέω ("dwell") at 1:19 and 2:9 only in the NT;
  - κεφαλή ("head") with ἀρχή ("rule") and ἐξουσία ("authority") in one NT verse, 2:10;
  - ἀρχή and ἐξουσία as a pair at 1:16, 2:10 and 2:15;
  - σταυρός ("cross") at 1:20 and 2:14;
  - νεκρός ("dead") at 1:18, 2:12 and 2:13.
- **One link fails.** σῶμα ("body") stands in eight verses of the letter (1:18, 22, 24; 2:11, 17, 19, 23; 3:15). It is too common to carry a redeployment argument `[T]`.
- **The baseline needs correcting.**
  - 1:15–20 has 23 rarer words on content words, or 25 counting every word — not 24.
  - 2:8–14 shares seven on either count (tied with 2:9–15), and no stretch away from 2:4–15 shares more than three `[T]`.
  - "Fourteen of ninety" holds only when every word is counted. On content words the figure is seven of ninety. The other high scorers are the epistolary frame (1:1–14 with 1:7–13; 4:7–12 with 1:7–13), not confessions `[T]` (Auditor B).
- **Adjacency does not explain it.** The stretch straight after the confession (1:21–27) shares only three words. The contact skips 1:21–2:7 and lands in 2:8–15 `[T]`.
- **Comparator test.** The same test was run on Phil 2:6–11, Rom 3:21–26, Eph 1:3–14, 1 Cor 15:20–25, 2 Cor 5:16–21, Rom 8:28–33 and Gal 4:1–6. None shows a long-range redeployment of a confession's rarer words at seven against three. Most sit at their letter's median `[T]` (Auditor B).
- **Missed:** κεφαλή, ἀρχή, ἐξουσία and πλήρωμα fall within three verses of one another only at Col 2:8–11 and Eph 1:21–23 `[T]` (bears on C13).

| Criterion (adapted for an internal design claim) | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | Strong | Seven shared rarer lemmas, four of them exclusive pairings |
| Recurrence | Strong | The same lemmas recur at 2:19 |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | The powers are created (1:16), headed (2:10) and stripped (2:15) |
| Comparator / baseline | Strong | Unmatched among seven Pauline confessions |
| Satisfaction | Strong | It explains why 2:9 repeats 1:19's grounding clause |

**Verdict:** **Confirmed with nuance.** **High on the links (σῶμα dropped); moderate–high as a design.**

**Reasoning:** The redeployment is real, long-range, and absent from every comparator. The baseline figures need correcting. Reaching the top band is not by itself a mark of confessional redeployment, because the letter's epistolary frame reaches it too.

**Book-overview action required:** Arc Map, the "arsenal" paragraph: drop σῶμα; state the corrected baseline (23 content-word lemmas; 7 of 90). 2:6–15 dig, Headline 2: the same correction. Note the Eph 1:21–23 cluster in the Neighbours field.

### Claim C3: The hidden-and-revealed sequence 1:26 → 2:3 → 3:3–4 → 4:4

**Source:** Overview, Echo Table #9 (high lexically; design moderate); first-targets list (3).
**Claim:** The hiding words and φανερόω ("make manifest") occur only at these four places, and they move deliberately from God's plan, to Christ's treasures, to the believer's life, to Paul's preaching.

**Findings.**
- **The counts are exact** `[T]`. ἀποκρύπτω ("hide away") stands at 1:26, ἀπόκρυφος ("hidden") at 2:3 and κρύπτω ("hide") at 3:3. φανερόω stands at 1:26, 3:4 (twice) and 4:4.
- **The sequence is largely the mystery thread.** μυστήριον ("mystery") stands at 1:26, 1:27, 2:2 and 4:3, and runs the same course. The αὐτό ("it") of 4:4 is the mystery of 4:3 `[T]`. Three of the four stations belong to it.
- **Spread alone is not evidence.** Five content lemmas of the letter appear in all four chapters, and three of them could be told as a development just as easily: σοφία ("wisdom"), περιπατέω ("walk") and λόγος ("word") `[T]`. Six of the thirteen Pauline letters use the hide and reveal families together. 1 Corinthians and Romans spread them over three or more sections `[T]` (Auditor B).
- **"Once hidden, now manifested" is a Pauline formula.** Mystery with a hiding word stands at 1 Cor 2:7, Eph 3:9 and Col 1:26 `[T]`. Rom 16:25–26 is the closest formal parallel `[S]`. **The corpus's SBLGNT lacks Rom 16:25–27**, so that text could not be checked; check it in the NA28 in Logos.
- **The distinctively Colossian station is 3:3–4.** There the believer's *life* is hidden with Christ in God and will be manifested with him. No Pauline parallel has κρύπτω governing the believer's life `[T]`.

**Verdict:** **Needs reframing.** **High lexically; low–moderate as a deliberate four-station sequence.**

**Reasoning:** The words are where the overview says they are. But the "development" is the letter's mystery thread, which follows a shared Pauline schema, and multi-section spreads that can be narrated are common. The weight belongs to 3:3–4's extension of hiddenness to the believer's life.

**Book-overview action required:** Echo #9: reword it as "the mystery thread (1:26, 27; 2:2; 4:3), with the hidden/revealed vocabulary attached; the distinctive move is 3:3–4". Design: low–moderate. Remove it from the first-targets list as closed.

### Claim C4: καταβραβεύω (2:18) / βραβεύω (3:15) as intended wordplay

**Source:** Overview, Echo Table #17 (high lexically; moderate as wordplay `[I]`).
**Claim:** Each verb occurs only here in the NT. The false umpire is replaced by the true.

**Findings.**
- **Counts and form.** Each verb occurs once in the NT. Both are third-person singular present imperatives (καταβραβευέτω; βραβευέτω) `[T]`.
- **LXX.** Wis 10:12 has ἐβράβευσεν ("he judged the contest"), and 1 Esd 9:14 has the compound συμβραβεύω. Only a stem-anywhere search finds them; a word-beginning search returns nothing, because the augment hides the stem. That was a positive-control failure, caught `[T]`. Wis 10:12 shows the arbiter sense of the simplex in Jewish Greek.
- **Baseline.** Nineteen NT verb pairs consist of a simplex and its compound that are *both* NT hapaxes. Six of the nineteen fall within one book, and **Col 2:18 / 3:15 is the only Pauline case** (Auditor A; not re-run by the main session). With "rare" widened to two NT uses, Gal 3:15/17 (κυρόω / προκυρόω, inside a legal argument) is the comparable deliberate case.
- **New structural evidence.** Colossians has exactly four third-person imperatives `[T]`:
  - 2:16, μή … τις ὑμᾶς κρινέτω ("let no one judge you");
  - 2:18, μηδεὶς ὑμᾶς καταβραβευέτω ("let no one disqualify you");
  - 3:15, ἡ εἰρήνη τοῦ Χριστοῦ βραβευέτω ("let the peace of Christ umpire");
  - 3:16, ὁ λόγος τοῦ Χριστοῦ ἐνοικείτω ("let the word of Christ dwell").

  There are two negative imperatives with human adjudicators and two positive ones with subjects "of Christ", all in the field of adjudication.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | The same letter |
| Volume | Strong | The same root, both hapaxes, the same person, tense and mood |
| Recurrence | Possible | Gal 3:15–17 |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | The false adjudicators answered by Christ's peace and word |
| Historical plausibility | Strong | Athletic imagery in Paul (1 Cor 9:24; Phil 3:14) |
| History of interpretation | Possible `[S]` | Often noted, seldom pressed |
| Satisfaction | Strong | It explains the otherwise unused βραβεύω |

**Verdict:** **Confirmed with nuance — raised.** **Moderate–high as intended wordplay** (high lexically).

**Reasoning:** The rarity baseline and the exact two-against-two imperative frame turn a lexical coincidence into a probable design. The precise sense of καταβραβεύω is debated `[S]`, so a sermon should not hang the reversal on one gloss.

**Book-overview action required:** Echo #17: raise it to moderate–high, and add the imperative frame (2:16, 18 → 3:15, 16) and Wis 10:12. Pulpit Note: the ESV's "disqualify … rule" hides both the umpire and the frame.

### Claim C5 (with C11): Isaiah 45:3 at Col 2:3, and Isaiah 45:14 at Col 1:27

**Source:** Overview, Intertextual Map row 5 (moderate); 1:24–2:5 dig, Tool 11 (C11: Isa 45:14 at 1:27, moderate at most).
**Claims:** θησαυρός ("treasure") with ἀπόκρυφος ("hidden") stands in one verse only at Isa 45:3 and 1 Macc 1:23. The rivals are Sir 1:25, Sir 20:30 and 41:14, and Prov 2:4–6. ἐν σοὶ ὁ θεός ("God is in you") occurs only at Isa 45:14 among Swete's canonical books. מַטְמוֹן ("hidden treasure") joins Prov 2:4 and Isa 45:3.

**Findings.**
- **The counts hold** `[T]`:
  - θησαυρός with ἀπόκρυφος: Isa 45:3 and 1 Macc 1:23 only, and 1 Macc 1:23 is a verbal match only (the temple treasures).
  - ἐν σοὶ ὁ θεός as whole words: Isa 45:14 and Pss Sol 17:1 (there a vocative).
  - מַטְמוֹן: five WLC verses (Gen 43:23; Isa 45:3; Jer 41:8; Job 3:21; Prov 2:4).
- **Wording.** Swete reads θησαυροὺς σκοτινοὺς ἀποκρύφους, ἀοράτους; Rahlfs reads σκοτεινούς, ἀποκρύφους ἀοράτους, with "hidden, unseen" together. The overview's quotation matches neither exactly `[T]`.
- **Translation equivalents.** In Isa 45:3 θησαυρούς renders אוֹצְרוֹת ("treasures"), and ἀποκρύφους ἀοράτους renders מַטְמֻנֵי מִסְתָּרִים ("hoards of secret places") (WLC; Swete). The Hebrew meeting point with Prov 2:4 on מַטְמוֹן therefore comes out once as "treasures" (Prov 2:4) and once as "hidden" (Isa 45:3) in the Greek `[T]`. Colossians' "treasures … hidden" combines the two outcomes.
- **New rival in the same book: Isa 33:6.** אוֹצָר ("treasure") with חָכְמָה ("wisdom") and דַּעַת ("knowledge") stands in one Hebrew verse only, Isa 33:6 (WLC) `[T]`. Swete keeps θησαυροί with σοφία and ἐπιστήμη. It is the only verse in the Hebrew Bible with Col 2:3's triad.
- **Wisdom rivals.**
  - Sir 20:30 and 41:14 are the only Swete verses with wisdom, a hiding word and treasure together `[T]`.
  - Sir 1:25 has the exact noun phrase ἐν θησαυροῖς σοφίας ("in the treasures of wisdom") `[T]`.
  - Prov 2:2–6 carries the fuller field (C10).
- **Isa 45:14 at 1:27.**
  - There is no field support: Isa 45 has a median rank of 122 against 1:26–27 `[T]`.
  - Paul does use the verse elsewhere: 1 Cor 14:25 (προσκυνήσει τῷ θεῷ … ὄντως ὁ θεὸς ἐν ὑμῖν ἐστιν, "he will worship God … God is really among you") `[T]`, a recognised echo `[S]`. That raises availability, but it is God in the church confessed by outsiders, not a reversal.
- **Hebrew, missed.** In Isa 45:3 and 45:15, מִסְתָּרִים ("secret places") and אֵל מִסְתַּתֵּר ("a God who hides himself") share the root סתר and frame 45:14. The LXX loses this `[T]`.

**Verdict (C5, 2:3):** **Confirmed with nuance.** **Moderate as a wisdom-treasury idiom; Isa 45:3 as the source, low–moderate.** Isa 45:3 is the only LXX verse joining "treasures" and "hidden". But the wisdom-and-knowledge content of 2:3 comes from Prov 2:2–6, Isa 33:6 and the Sirach texts. Auditor B would keep the point contact at moderate. Both ratings are recorded; the main session takes the lower *as source* and the higher *as contact*.

**Verdict (C11, 1:27):** **Uncertain — flag for research.** **Low–moderate.** The phrase is real, but slight: function words with a change of referent and no field. 1:27 needs no Isaiah to be understood.

**Book-overview action required:** Row 5: restate it as "the treasury-of-wisdom idiom (Prov 2:2–6; Sir 1:25; 20:30; 41:14; Isa 33:6), with Isa 45:3 the only verse joining treasures and hidden", at moderate. Correct the quotation. Do not add Isa 45:14 at 1:27 as a row; mention it in a note at low–moderate, with 1 Cor 14:25.

### Claim C6: Genesis 1 behind Col 1:15, as distinct from 3:10

**Source:** Overview, Intertextual Map row 2 (high at 3:10; moderate at 1:15); 1:15–20 dig, Tool 11 and Book-Overview Tensions §3 ("add ἀόρατος").
**Claim:** ἀόρατος ("invisible") stands in only three Swete verses, one of them Gen 1:2, so Col 1:15–16 shares it with Genesis. κατ᾽ εἰκόνα ("according to the image") occurs in five LXX verses. 1:15 depends on 3:10 to be heard.

**Findings.**
- **The counts hold** `[T]`:
  - ἀόρατος at Gen 1:2, Isa 45:3 and 2 Macc 9:5.
  - κατ᾽ εἰκόνα at Gen 1:26, 1:27 and 5:1; Sir 17:3; Hos 13:2 (of idols).
  - εἰκών with κτίζω ("create") in one verse: Wis 2:23 only.
  - "Image of God" of Christ: 2 Cor 4:4 and Col 1:15.
- **The ἀόρατος argument fails.** At Gen 1:2 the word describes **the earth** ("the earth was invisible and unformed", rendering תֹהוּ), and no LXX verse calls God ἀόρατος `[T]`. "The invisible God" is not a Genesis phrase.
- **Rivals.** Against 1:15–20 the window ranks are:
  - Ps 88: first in all six runs (C7);
  - Sir 24: median 5;
  - Gen 1: median 37.

  "Firstborn of all creation" is better matched by Ps 88:28 and by the wisdom texts — Wis 7:26 (Wisdom an εἰκών of God's goodness), Prov 8:22 and Sir 24 — than by Genesis `[T]`.
- **3:10 is purely phrasal.** Its field is dominated by the ethnic list, so a window scan cannot see Genesis there at all (median rank 204). But κατ᾽ εἰκόνα with a creator verb and ἄνθρωπος ("man") stands in the LXX only at Gen 1:26–27, 5:1 and Sir 17:3 `[T]`. So the 3:10 link is strong on its phrase. It is also a caution against reading low window ranks as negative evidence.

**Verdict:** **Needs reframing.** **High at 3:10; moderate at 1:15** — on εἰκών, creation and "heaven and earth", secured by 3:10, and not on ἀόρατος.

**Reasoning:** The rating stands but its grounds change. Genesis 1 is one of three converging backgrounds for 1:15–18 — creation, the Davidic firstborn, and wisdom — not the sole one.

**Book-overview action required:** Row 2: drop any ἀόρατος argument, and do not apply the 1:15–20 dig's Tension §3. Add a note on the three converging backgrounds. 1:15–20 dig, Tool 11 (Genesis): withdraw the ἀόρατος line.

### Claim C7: Psalm 89 (LXX 88) as the field partner of Col 1:15–20 — load-bearing

**Source:** 1:15–20 dig, Headline 3 and Tool 11 (moderate–high; "a design claim, measured against chance at six settings but not yet audited"); Book-Overview Tension §1 (raise row 8 from moderate–uncertain to moderate–high).
**Claim:** Ps 88 LXX ranks first of 1,259 Swete chapters in all six runs, sharing six rarer words. πλήρωμα with founding or creating stands only at Ps 88:12. "Firstborn" with a royal king stands in one LXX verse, Ps 88:28. Rev 1:5 combines Ps 88:28 and 88:38. The psalm joins a creation hymn to the royal firstborn.

**Findings.**
- **Reproduced exactly** `[T]`. Ps 88 is first in all six runs. Its margins over second place are 1.21, 1.42, 1.04, 1.21, 1.19 and 1.44; the 8-verse all-words run is a near-tie with Esther 4.
- **Null.** Sixty-five Pauline six-verse passages were scanned (40 drawn at random and 25 confessional). Some chapter was first in all six runs for **four of them (6 %)**. Of those four winners, one was a genuine source (Rom 11:6–11 with Ps 68, which it quotes). One was not a source: for 2 Cor 6:1–6, Esther 4 wins all six while the passage's quoted source, Isa 49, ranks third. The other two were artefacts `[T]` (Auditor B; the 2 Cor 6 case re-run by the main session). **The test separates a strong field from noise. It cannot separate a source from a shared semantic field.**
- **Leave-one-out.** Remove πλήρωμα from the passage, and Ps 88 is first in only two of six runs (ranks 3, 1, 16, 11, 5, 1) `[T]` (reproduced). Remove πρωτότοκος, and the ranks do not change. The winning windows are the creation section (88:6–18), which does not contain 88:28. One caution: the chapter-level runs still credit πρωτοτόκον through the πρωτ- stem of πρωτεύω ("have first place").
- **Sense of the shared words** `[T]`:
  - πλήρωμα at 88:12 is the world's *contents* ("the world and its fullness You founded"), not the divine fullness.
  - θρόνος is David's throne and God's; in Col 1:16 θρόνοι are powers.
  - ἐκκλησία at 88:6 is the heavenly council.
  - εὐδοκία at 88:18 is the noun, against Colossians' verb.
  - Only πρωτότοκος (88:28) matches in sense.
- **Rarity, nuanced** `[T]`:
  - πλήρωμα with founding or creating in one verse: Ps 88:12 only — but Ps 23:1–2 has πλήρωμα with "he founded it" in the next verse.
  - "Firstborn" with "king" in one verse: five Swete verses (2 Sam 13:21; 19:43; 2 Kgs 3:27; 2 Chr 21:3; Ps 88:28). Ps 88:28 is the only one where firstborn status is *conferred over* the kings of the earth.
  - πρωτότοκος with νεκρός ("dead") in the NT: Col 1:18 and Rev 1:5 only. Rev 1:5's combination of 88:28 and 88:38 holds `[S]`.
- **The positive control was overstated.** For Heb 1:1–14, Ps 44 is first in four runs, not six; Ps 88 takes both chapter runs `[T]` (reproduced). Ps 88 is a strong general partner for "Son and firstborn" vocabulary.
- **Rivals.** The royal-messianic texts usually invoked are far away lexically: Ps 2 (median 768), 2 Sam 7 (about 440) and Ps 109 (838). Sir 24 (median 5) is the only steady second `[T]`.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | The Psalter |
| Volume | Possible | Six shared words, but only πρωτότοκος matches in sense |
| Recurrence | Possible | Rev 1:5 reads 88:28 and 88:38 of the risen Christ |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | Creator and royal firstborn in one psalm |
| Historical plausibility | Strong | |
| History of interpretation | Possible `[S]` | Ps 89:27 is regularly cited for "firstborn" at Col 1:15, 18 |
| Satisfaction | Possible | |

**Verdict:** **Needs reframing.** **Moderate** for Ps 88 as the field partner of the whole confession. **Moderate–high** for the point contact Ps 88:28 at Col 1:15, 18, supported by Rev 1:5.

**Reasoning:** The rank is real and beats an arbitrary null at about the 5 % level. But a non-source chapter can win the same way. The result does not survive the loss of πλήρωμα, which is used in a different sense. And the firstborn verse — the claim's strongest evidence — plays no part in the window wins. The contact is the verse, not the field.
