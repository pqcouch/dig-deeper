### Eliphaz (15:1–35)

*Closes with a setumah (ס) in BHS.*

> **15:1** וַ֭יַּעַן אֱלִיפַ֥ז הַֽתֵּימָנִ֗י וַיֹּאמַֽר׃
>
> Then Eliphaz the Temanite responded,

> **15:2** הֶֽחָכָ֗ם יַעֲנֶ֥ה דַֽעַת־ר֑וּחַ וִֽימַלֵּ֖א קָדִ֣ים בִּטְנֹֽו׃
>
> “Should a wise man answer with windy knowledge And fill himself with the east wind?

> **15:3** הֹוכֵ֣חַ בְּ֭דָבָר לֹ֣א יִסְכֹּ֑ון וּ֝מִלִּ֗ים לֹא־יֹועִ֥יל בָּֽם׃
>
> “Should he argue with useless talk, Or with words which are not profitable?

> **15:4** אַף־אַ֭תָּה תָּפֵ֣ר יִרְאָ֑ה וְתִגְרַ֥ע שִׂ֝יחָ֗ה לִפְנֵי־אֵֽל׃
>
> “Indeed, you do away with reverence And hinder meditation before God.

> **15:5** כִּ֤י יְאַלֵּ֣ף עֲוֹנְךָ֣ פִ֑יךָ וְ֝תִבְחַ֗ר לְשֹׁ֣ון עֲרוּמִֽים׃
>
> “For your guilt teaches your mouth, And you choose the language of the crafty.

> **15:6** יַרְשִֽׁיעֲךָ֣ פִ֣יךָ וְלֹא־אָ֑נִי וּ֝שְׂפָתֶ֗יךָ יַעֲנוּ־בָֽךְ׃
>
> “Your own mouth condemns you, and not I; And your own lips testify against you.

> **15:7** הֲרִאישֹׁ֣ון אָ֭דָם תִּוָּלֵ֑ד וְלִפְנֵ֖י גְבָעֹ֣ות חֹולָֽלְתָּ׃
>
> “Were you the first man to be born, Or were you brought forth before the hills?

> **15:8** הַבְסֹ֣וד אֱלֹ֣והַᵃ תִּשְׁמָ֑ע וְתִגְרַ֖ע אֵלֶ֣יךָ חָכְמָֽה׃
>
> “Do you hear the secret counsel of God, And limit wisdom to yourself?

> **15:9** מַה־יָּ֭דַעְתָּ וְלֹ֣א נֵדָ֑ע תָּ֝בִ֗ין וְֽלֹא־עִמָּ֥נוּ הֽוּא׃
>
> “What do you know that we do not know? What do you understand that we do not?

> **15:10** גַּם־שָׂ֣ב גַּם־יָשִׁ֣ישׁ בָּ֑נוּ כַּבִּ֖יר מֵאָבִ֣יךָ יָמִֽים׃
>
> “Both the gray-haired and the aged are among us, Older than your father.

> **15:11** הַמְעַ֣ט מִ֭מְּךָ תַּנְחֻמֹ֣ות אֵ֑ל וְ֝דָבָ֗ר לָאַ֥ט עִמָּֽךְ׃
>
> “Are the consolations of God too small for you, Even the word spoken gently with you?

> **15:12** מַה־יִּקָּחֲךָ֥ לִבֶּ֑ךָ וּֽמַה־יִּרְזְמ֥וּןᵃ עֵינֶֽיךָ׃
>
> “Why does your heart carry you away? And why do your eyes flash,

> **15:13** כִּֽי־תָשִׁ֣יב אֶלᵃ־אֵ֣ל רוּחֶ֑ךָ וְהֹצֵ֖אתָ מִפִּ֣יךָ מִלִּֽין׃
>
> That you should turn your spirit against God And allow such words to go out of your mouth?

> **15:14** מָֽה־אֱנֹ֥ושׁ כִּֽי־יִזְכֶּ֑ה וְכִֽי־יִ֝צְדַּ֗ק יְל֣וּד אִשָּֽׁה׃
>
> “What is man, that he should be pure, Or he who is born of a woman, that he should be righteous?

> **15:15** הֵ֣ן בִּ֭קְדֹשָׁו לֹ֣א יַאֲמִ֑ין וְ֝שָׁמַ֗יִם לֹא־זַכּ֥וּ בְעֵינָֽיו׃
>
> “Behold, He puts no trust in His holy ones, And the heavens are not pure in His sight;

> **15:16** אַ֭ף כִּֽי־נִתְעָ֥ב וְֽנֶאֱלָ֑ח אִישׁ־שֹׁתֶ֖ה כַמַּ֣יִם עַוְלָֽה׃
>
> How much less one who is detestable and corrupt, Man, who drinks iniquity like water!

> **15:17** אֲחַוְךָ֥ שְֽׁמַֽע־לִ֑י וְזֶֽה־חָ֝זִ֗יתִי וַאֲסַפֵּֽרָה׃
>
> “I will tell you, listen to me; And what I have seen I will also declare;

> **15:18** אֲשֶׁר־חֲכָמִ֥ים יַגִּ֑ידוּ וְלֹ֥א כִֽ֝חֲד֗וּ מֵאֲבֹותָֽם׃
>
> What wise men have told, And have not concealed from their fathers,

> **15:19** לָהֶ֣ם לְ֭בַדָּם נִתְּנָ֣ה הָאָ֑רֶץ וְלֹא־עָ֖בַר זָ֣ר בְּתֹוכָֽם׃
>
> To whom alone the land was given, And no alien passed among them.

> **15:20** כָּל־יְמֵ֣י רָ֭שָׁע ה֣וּא מִתְחֹולֵ֑ל וּמִסְפַּ֥ר שָׁ֝נִ֗ים נִצְפְּנ֥וּ לֶעָרִֽיץ׃
>
> “The wicked man writhes in pain all his days, And numbered are the years stored up for the ruthless.

> **15:21** קֹול־פְּחָדִ֥ים בְּאָזְנָ֑יו בַּ֝שָּׁלֹ֗ום שֹׁודֵ֥ד יְבֹואֶֽנּוּ׃
>
> “Sounds of terror are in his ears; While at peace the destroyer comes upon him.

> **15:22** לֹא־יַאֲמִ֣ין שׁ֭וּב מִנִּי־חֹ֑שֶׁךְ וְצָפ֖וּᵃ ה֣וּא אֱלֵי־חָֽרֶב׃
>
> “He does not believe that he will return from darkness, And he is destined for the sword.

> **15:23** נֹ֘דֵ֤ד ה֣וּא לַלֶּ֣חֶם אַיֵּ֑ה יָדַ֓ע׀ כִּֽי־נָכֹ֖ון בְּיָדֹ֣ו יֹֽום־חֹֽשֶׁךְ׃
>
> “He wanders about for food, saying, ‘Where is it?’ He knows that a day of darkness is at hand.

> **15:24** יְֽ֭בַעֲתֻהוּ צַ֣ר וּמְצוּקָ֑ה תִּ֝תְקְפֵ֗הוּ כְּמֶ֤לֶךְ׀ עָתִ֬יד לַכִּידֹֽור׃
>
> “Distress and anguish terrify him, They overpower him like a king ready for the attack,

> **15:25** כִּֽי־נָטָ֣ה אֶלᵃ־אֵ֣ל יָדֹ֑ו וְאֶל־שַׁ֝דַּ֗י יִתְגַּבָּֽר׃
>
> Because he has stretched out his hand against God And conducts himself arrogantly against the Almighty.

> **15:26** יָר֣וּץ אֵלָ֣יוᵃ בְּצַוָּ֑אר בַּ֝עֲבִ֗י גַּבֵּ֥י מָֽגִנָּֽיו׃
>
> “He rushes headlong at Him With his massive shield.

> **15:27** כִּֽי־כִסָּ֣ה פָנָ֣יו בְּחֶלְבֹּ֑ו וַיַּ֖עַשׂ פִּימָ֣ה עֲלֵי־כָֽסֶל׃
>
> “For he has covered his face with his fat And made his thighs heavy with flesh.

> **15:28** וַיִּשְׁכֹּ֤ון׀ עָ֘רִ֤ים נִכְחָדֹ֗ות בָּ֭תִּים לֹא־יֵ֣שְׁבוּ לָ֑מֹו אֲשֶׁ֖ר הִתְעַתְּד֣וּ לְגַלִּֽים׃
>
> “He has lived in desolate cities, In houses no one would inhabit, Which are destined to become ruins.

> **15:29** לֹֽא־יֶ֖עְשַׁר וְלֹא־יָק֣וּם חֵילֹ֑ו וְלֹֽא־יִטֶּ֖ה לָאָ֣רֶץ מִנְלָֽםᵃ׃
>
> “He will not become rich, nor will his wealth endure; And his grain will not bend down to the ground.

> **15:30** לֹֽא־יָס֨וּר׀ מִנִּי־חֹ֗שֶׁךְ יֹֽ֭נַקְתֹּו תְּיַבֵּ֣שׁ שַׁלְהָ֑בֶת וְ֝יָס֗וּר בְּר֣וּחַ פִּֽיו׃
>
> “He will not escape from darkness; The flame will wither his shoots, And by the breath of His mouth he will go away.

> **15:31** אַל־יַאֲמֵ֣ן בַּשָּׁ֣וᵃ נִתְעָ֑הᵇ כִּי־שָׁ֝֗וְא תִּהְיֶ֥ה תְמוּרָתֹֽו׃
>
> “Let him not trust in emptiness, deceiving himself; For emptiness will be his reward.

> **15:32** בְּֽלֹא־יֹ֭ומֹו תִּמָּלֵ֑אᵃ וְ֝כִפָּתֹ֗ו לֹ֣א רַעֲנָֽנָה׃
>
> “It will be accomplished before his time, And his palm branch will not be green.

> **15:33** יַחְמֹ֣ס כַּגֶּ֣פֶן בִּסְרֹ֑ו וְיַשְׁלֵ֥ךְᵃ כַּ֝זַּ֗יִת נִצָּתֹֽו׃
>
> “He will drop off his unripe grape like the vine, And will cast off his flower like the olive tree.

> **15:34** כִּֽי־עֲדַ֣ת חָנֵ֣ף גַּלְמ֑וּד וְ֝אֵ֗שׁ אָכְלָ֥ה אָֽהֳלֵי־שֹֽׁחַד׃
>
> “For the company of the godless is barren, And fire consumes the tents of the corrupt.

> **15:35** הָרֹ֣ה עָ֭מָל וְיָ֣לֹד אָ֑וֶן וּ֝בִטְנָ֗ם תָּכִ֥ין מִרְמָֽה׃  ס
>
> “They conceive mischief and bring forth iniquity, And their mind prepares deception.”

### Job (16:1–17:16)

*Closes with a setumah. Worked verse by verse in the 16:1–17:16 solo dig.*

> **16:1** וַיַּ֥עַן אִיֹּ֗וב וַיֹּאמַֽר׃
>
> Then Job answered,

> **16:2** שָׁמַ֣עְתִּי כְאֵ֣לֶּה רַבֹּ֑ות מְנַחֲמֵ֖י עָמָ֣ל כֻּלְּכֶֽם׃
>
> “I have heard many such things; Sorry comforters are you all.

> **16:3** הֲקֵ֥ץ לְדִבְרֵי־ר֑וּחַ ᵃאֹ֥ו מַהᵃ־יַּ֝מְרִֽיצְךָ֗ כִּ֣י תַעֲנֶֽה׃
>
> “Is there no limit to windy words? Or what plagues you that you answer?

> **16:4** גַּ֤ם׀ אָנֹכִי֮ כָּכֶ֪ם אֲדַ֫בֵּ֥רָה ל֤וּ־יֵ֪שׁ נַפְשְׁכֶ֡ם תַּ֤חַת נַפְשִׁ֗י אַחְבִּ֣ירָה עֲלֵיכֶ֣ם בְּמִלִּ֑ים וְאָנִ֥יעָה עֲ֝לֵיכֶ֗ם בְּמֹ֣ו רֹאשִֽׁי׃
>
> “I too could speak like you, If I were in your place. I could compose words against you And shake my head at you.

> **16:5** אֲאַמִּצְכֶ֥ם בְּמֹו־פִ֑י וְנִ֖יד שְׂפָתַ֣י יַחְשֹֽׂךְ׃
>
> “I could strengthen you with my mouth, And the solace of my lips could lessen your pain.

> **16:6** אִֽם־אֲ֭דַבְּרָה לֹא־יֵחָשֵׂ֣ךְ כְּאֵבִ֑י וְ֝אַחְדְּלָ֗ה מַה־מִנִּ֥יᵃ יַהֲלֹֽךְ׃
>
> “If I speak, my pain is not lessened, And if I hold back, what has left me?

> **16:7** אַךְ־עַתָּ֥ה הֶלְאָ֑נִי הֲ֝שִׁמֹּ֗ותָ כָּל־עֲדָתִֽי׃
>
> “But now He has exhausted me; You have laid waste all my company.

> **16:8** וַֽ֭תִּקְמְטֵנִי לְעֵ֣ד הָיָ֑ה וַיָּ֥קָם בִּ֥י כַ֝חֲשִׁ֗י בְּפָנַ֥י יַעֲנֶֽה׃
>
> “You have shriveled me up, It has become a witness; And my leanness rises up against me, It testifies to my face.

> **16:9** אַפֹּ֤ו טָרַ֨ף׀ וַֽיִּשְׂטְמֵ֗נִי חָרַ֣ק עָלַ֣י בְּשִׁנָּ֑יו צָרִ֓י׀ יִלְטֹ֖ושׁ עֵינָ֣יו לִֽי׃
>
> “His anger has torn me and hunted me down, He has gnashed at me with His teeth; My adversary glares at me.

> **16:10** פָּעֲר֬וּ עָלַ֨י׀ בְּפִיהֶ֗ם בְּ֭חֶרְפָּה הִכּ֣וּ לְחָיָ֑י יַ֝֗חַד עָלַ֥י יִתְמַלָּאֽוּן׃
>
> “They have gaped at me with their mouth, They have slapped me on the cheek with contempt; They have massed themselves against me.

> **16:11** יַסְגִּירֵ֣נִי אֵ֭ל אֶ֣ל עֲוִ֑ילᵃ וְעַל־יְדֵ֖י רְשָׁעִ֣ים יִרְטֵֽנִי׃
>
> “God hands me over to ruffians And tosses me into the hands of the wicked.

> **16:12** שָׁ֘לֵ֤ו הָיִ֨יתִי׀ וַֽיְפַרְפְּרֵ֗נִי וְאָחַ֣ז בְּ֭עָרְפִּי וַֽיְפַצְפְּצֵ֑נִי וַיְקִימֵ֥נִי לֹ֝֗ו לְמַטָּרָֽה׃
>
> “I was at ease, but He shattered me, And He has grasped me by the neck and shaken me to pieces; He has also set me up as His target.

> **16:13** יָ֘סֹ֤בּוּ עָלַ֨י׀ רַבָּ֗יו יְפַלַּ֣ח כִּ֭לְיֹותַי וְלֹ֣א יַחְמֹ֑ול יִשְׁפֹּ֥ךְ לָ֝אָ֗רֶץ מְרֵרָֽתִי׃
>
> “His arrows surround me. Without mercy He splits my kidneys open; He pours out my gall on the ground.

> **16:14** יִפְרְצֵ֣נִי פֶ֭רֶץ עַל־פְּנֵי־פָ֑רֶץ יָרֻ֖ץ עָלַ֣י כְּגִבֹּֽור׃
>
> “He breaks through me with breach after breach; He runs at me like a warrior.

> **16:15** שַׂ֣ק תָּ֭פַרְתִּי עֲלֵ֣י גִלְדִּ֑י וְעֹלַ֖לְתִּי בֶעָפָ֣ר קַרְנִֽי׃
>
> “I have sewed sackcloth over my skin And thrust my horn in the dust.

> **16:16** פָּנַ֣י חֳ֭מַרְמְרֻה מִנִּי־בֶ֑כִי וְעַ֖ל עַפְעַפַּ֣י צַלְמָֽוֶת׃

> **19:21** חָנֻּ֬נִי חָנֻּ֣נִי אַתֶּ֣ם רֵעָ֑י כִּ֥י יַד־אֱ֝לֹ֗והַּ נָ֣גְעָה בִּֽי׃
>
> “Pity me, pity me, O you my friends, For the hand of God has struck me.

> **19:22** לָ֭מָּה תִּרְדְּפֻ֣נִי כְמֹו־אֵ֑ל וּ֝מִבְּשָׂרִ֗י לֹ֣א תִשְׂבָּֽעוּ׃
>
> “Why do you persecute me as God does, And are not satisfied with my flesh?

> **19:23** מִֽי־יִתֵּ֣ן אֵ֭פֹו וְיִכָּתְב֣וּן מִלָּ֑י מִֽי־יִתֵּ֖ן בַּסֵּ֣פֶר וְיֻחָֽקוּᵃ׃
>
> “Oh that my words were written! Oh that they were inscribed in a book!

> **19:24** בְּעֵט־בַּרְזֶ֥ל וְעֹפָ֑רֶתᵃ לָ֝עַ֗ד בַּצּ֥וּר יֵחָצְבֽוּן׃
>
> “That with an iron stylus and lead They were engraved in the rock forever!

> **19:25** וַאֲנִ֣י יָ֭דַעְתִּי גֹּ֣אֲלִי חָ֑י וְ֝אַחֲרֹ֗ון עַל־עָפָ֥ר יָקֽוּם׃
>
> “As for me, I know that my Redeemer lives, And at the last He will take His stand on the earth.

> **19:26** וְאַחַ֣ר עֹ֖ורִֽי נִקְּפוּ־זֹ֑את וּ֝מִבְּשָׂרִ֗י אֶֽחֱזֶ֥ה אֱלֹֽוהַּ׃
>
> “Even after my skin is destroyed, Yet from my flesh I shall see God;

> **19:27** ᵃאֲשֶׁ֤ר אֲנִ֨יᵃ׀ אֶֽחֱזֶה־לִּ֗י וְעֵינַ֣י רָא֣וּ וְלֹא־זָ֑ר כָּל֖וּ כִלְיֹתַ֣י בְּחֵקִֽי׃
>
> Whom I myself shall behold, And whom my eyes will see and not another. My heart faints within me!

> **19:28** כִּ֣י תֹ֖אמְרוּ מַה־נִּרְדָּף־לֹ֑ו וְשֹׁ֥רֶשׁ דָּ֝בָ֗ר נִמְצָא־בִֽיᵃ׃
>
> “If you say, ‘How shall we persecute him?’ And ‘What pretext for a case against him can we find?’

> **19:29** גּ֤וּרוּ לָכֶ֨ם׀ מִפְּנֵי־חֶ֗רֶב כִּֽי־חֵ֭מָה עֲוֹנֹ֣ות חָ֑רֶב לְמַ֖עַן תֵּדְע֣וּן שַׁדּוּן׃ᵃ
>
> “Then be afraid of the sword for yourselves, For wrath brings the punishment of the sword, So that you may know there is judgment.”

### Zophar (20:1–29)

> **20:1** וַ֭יַּעַן צֹפַ֥ר הַנַּֽעֲמָתִ֗י וַיֹּאמַֽר׃
>
> Then Zophar the Naamathite answered,

> **20:2** לָ֭כֵן שְׂעִפַּ֣י יְשִׁיב֑וּנִי וּ֝בַעֲב֗וּרᵃ ח֣וּשִׁי בִֽי׃
>
> “Therefore my disquieting thoughts make me respond, Even because of my inward agitation.

> **20:3** מוּסַ֣ר כְּלִמָּתִ֣י אֶשְׁמָ֑ע וְ֝ר֗וּחַ מִֽבִּינָתִ֥יᵃ יַעֲנֵֽנִי׃
>
> “I listened to the reproof which insults me, And the spirit of my understanding makes me answer.

> **20:4** הֲזֹ֣אתᵃ יָ֭דַעְתָּ מִנִּי־עַ֑ד מִנִּ֤י שִׂ֖ים אָדָ֣ם עֲלֵי־אָֽרֶץ׃
>
> “Do you know this from of old, From the establishment of man on earth,

> **20:5** כִּ֤י רִנְנַ֣ת רְ֭שָׁעִים מִקָּרֹ֑וב וְשִׂמְחַ֖ת חָנֵ֣ף עֲדֵי־רָֽגַע׃
>
> That the triumphing of the wicked is short, And the joy of the godless momentary?

> **20:6** אִם־יַעֲלֶ֣ה לַשָּׁמַ֣יִם שִׂיאֹ֑ו וְ֝רֹאשֹׁ֗ו לָעָ֥ב יַגִּֽיעַ׃
>
> “Though his loftiness reaches the heavens, And his head touches the clouds,

> **20:7** כְּֽ֭גֶלֲלֹו לָנֶ֣צַח יֹאבֵ֑ד רֹ֝אָ֗יו יֹאמְר֥וּ אַיֹּֽו׃
>
> He perishes forever like his refuse; Those who have seen him will say, ‘Where is he?’

> **20:8** כַּחֲלֹ֣ום יָ֭עוּף וְלֹ֣א יִמְצָא֑וּהוּ וְ֝יֻדַּ֗ד כְּחֶזְיֹ֥ון לָֽיְלָה׃
>
> “He flies away like a dream, and they cannot find him; Even like a vision of the night he is chased away.

> **20:9** עַ֣יִן שְׁ֭זָפַתּוּ וְלֹ֣א תֹוסִ֑יף וְלֹא־עֹ֝֗וד תְּשׁוּרֶ֥נּוּ מְקֹומֹֽו׃
>
> “The eye which saw him sees him no longer, And his place no longer beholds him.

> **20:10** בָּ֭נָיו יְרַצּ֣וּ דַלִּ֑ים וְ֝יָדָ֗יו תָּשֵׁ֥בְנָה אֹונֹֽו׃
>
> “His sons favor the poor, And his hands give back his wealth.

> **20:11** עַ֭צְמֹותָיו מָלְא֣וּ עֲלוּמָ֑ו וְ֝עִמֹּ֗ו עַל־עָפָ֥ר תִּשְׁכָּֽב׃
>
> “His bones are full of his youthful vigor, But it lies down with him in the dust.

> **20:12** אִם־תַּמְתִּ֣יק בְּפִ֣יו רָעָ֑ה יַ֝כְחִידֶ֗נָּה תַּ֣חַת לְשֹׁונֹֽו׃
>
> “Though evil is sweet in his mouth And he hides it under his tongue,

> **20:13** יַחְמֹ֣ל עָ֭לֶיהָ וְלֹ֣א יַֽעַזְבֶ֑נָּה וְ֝יִמְנָעֶ֗נָּה בְּתֹ֣וךְ חִכֹּֽו׃
>
> Though he desires it and will not let it go, But holds it in his mouth,

> **20:14** לַ֭חְמֹו בְּמֵעָ֣יו נֶהְפָּ֑ךְ מְרֹורַ֖ת פְּתָנִ֣ים בְּקִרְבֹּֽו׃
>
> Yet his food in his stomach is changed To the venom of cobras within him.

> **20:15** חַ֣יִל בָּ֭לַע וַיְקִאֶ֑נּוּ מִ֝בִּטְנֹ֗ו יֹֽורִשֶׁ֥נּוּ אֵֽל׃
>
> “He swallows riches, But will vomit them up; God will expel them from his belly.

> **20:16** רֹאשׁ־פְּתָנִ֥ים יִינָ֑ק תַּֽ֝הַרְגֵ֗הוּ לְשֹׁ֣ון אֶפְעֶֽה׃
>
> “He sucks the poison of cobras; The viper’s tongue slays him.

> **20:17** אַל־יֵ֥רֶא בִפְלַגֹּ֑ות נַהֲרֵ֥יᵃ נַ֝חֲלֵ֗י דְּבַ֣שׁ וְחֶמְאָֽה׃
>
> “He does not look at the streams, The rivers flowing with honey and curds.

> **20:18** מֵשִׁ֣יב יָ֭גָעᵃ וְלֹ֣א יִבְלָ֑ע כְּחֵ֥ילᵇ תְּ֝מוּרָתֹ֗ו וְלֹ֣אc יַעֲלֹֽס׃
>
> “He returns what he has attained And cannot swallow it; As to the riches of his trading, He cannot even enjoy them.

> **20:19** כִּֽי־רִ֭צַּץ עָזַ֣ב דַּלִּ֑ים בַּ֥יִת גָּ֝זַ֗ל וְלֹ֣א יִבֶנֵֽהוּ׃
>
> “For he has oppressed and forsaken the poor; He has seized a house which he has not built.

> **20:20** כִּ֤י׀ לֹא־יָדַ֣ע ᵃשָׁלֵ֣ו בְּבִטְנֹ֑וᵃ בַּ֝חֲמוּדֹ֗וᵇ לֹ֣א יְמַלֵּֽט׃
>
> “Because he knew no quiet within him, He does not retain anything he desires.

> **20:21** אֵין־שָׂרִ֥יד לְאָכְלֹ֑ו עַל־כֵּ֝֗ן לֹא־יָחִ֥יל טוּבֹֽו׃
>
> “Nothing remains for him to devour, Therefore his prosperity does not endure.

> **20:22** בִּמְלֹ֣אות שִׂ֭פְקֹו יֵ֣צֶר לֹ֑ו כָּל־יַ֖ד עָמֵ֣לᵃ תְּבֹואֶֽנּוּ׃
>
> “In the fullness of his plenty he will be cramped; The hand of everyone who suffers will come against him.

> **20:23** יְהִ֤י׀ לְמַלֵּ֬א בִטְנֹ֗ו יְֽשַׁלַּח־בֹּ֖ו חֲרֹ֣ון אַפֹּ֑ו וְיַמְטֵ֥ר ᵃעָ֝לֵ֗ימֹו בִּלְחוּמֹֽוᵃ׃
>
> “When he fills his belly, God will send His fierce anger on him And will rain it on him while he is eating.

> **20:24** יִ֭בְרַח מִנֵּ֣שֶׁק בַּרְזֶ֑ל תַּ֝חְלְפֵ֗הוּ קֶ֣שֶׁת נְחוּשָֽׁה׃
>
> “He may flee from the iron weapon, But the bronze bow will pierce him.

> **20:25** שָׁלַף֮ וַיֵּצֵ֪א מִגֵּ֫וָ֥ה וּ֭בָרָק מִֽמְּרֹרָתֹ֥ו יַהֲלֹ֗ךְᵃ עָלָ֥יו אֵמִֽים׃
>
> “It is drawn forth and comes out of his back, Even the glittering point from his gall. Terrors come upon him,

> **20:26** כָּל־חֹשֶׁךְ֮ טָמ֪וּן לִצְפּ֫וּנָ֥יו תְּ֭אָכְלֵהוּᵃ אֵ֣שׁ לֹֽא־נֻפָּ֑חᵇ יֵ֖רַעc שָׂרִ֣יד בְּאָהֳלֹֽו׃
>
> Complete darkness is held in reserve for his treasures, And unfanned fire will devour him; It will consume the survivor in his tent.

> **20:27** יְגַלּ֣וּ שָׁמַ֣יִם עֲוֹנֹ֑ו וְ֝אֶ֗רֶץ מִתְקֹומָ֘מָ֥ה לֹֽו׃
>
> “The heavens will reveal his iniquity, And the earth will rise up against him.

> **20:28** יִ֭גֶלᵃ יְב֣וּלᵇ בֵּיתֹ֑ו נִ֝גָּרֹ֗ות בְּיֹ֣ום אַפֹּֽו׃
>
> “The increase of his house will depart; His possessions will flow away in the day of His anger.

> **20:29** ᵃזֶ֤ה׀ חֵֽלֶק־אָדָ֣םᵃ רָ֭שָׁע מֵאֱלֹהִ֑ים וְנַחֲלַ֖ת אִמְרֹ֣ו מֵאֵֽל׃  פ
>
> “This is the wicked man’s portion from God, Even the heritage decreed to him by God.”

### Job (21:1–34)

*Closes with a setumah.*

> **21:1** וַיַּ֥עַן אִיֹּ֗וב וַיֹּאמַֽר׃
>
> Then Job answered,

> **21:2** שִׁמְע֣וּ שָׁ֭מֹועַ מִלָּתִ֑י וּתְהִי־זֹ֝֗את תַּנְח֥וּמֹֽתֵיכֶֽם׃
>
> “Listen carefully to my speech, And let this be your way of consolation.

> **21:3** שָׂ֭אוּנִי וְאָנֹכִ֣י אֲדַבֵּ֑ר וְאַחַ֖ר דַּבְּרִ֣י תַלְעִֽיג׃
>

> **21:4** הֶ֭אָנֹכִי לְאָדָ֣ם שִׂיחִ֑י וְאִם־מַ֝דּ֗וּעַ לֹא־תִקְצַ֥ר רוּחִֽי׃
>
> “As for me, is my complaint to man? And why should I not be impatient?

> **21:5** פְּנוּ־אֵלַ֥י וְהָשַׁ֑מּוּᵃ וְשִׂ֖ימוּ יָ֣ד עַל־פֶּֽה׃
>
> “Look at me, and be astonished, And put your hand over your mouth.

> **21:6** וְאִם־זָכַ֥רְתִּי וְנִבְהָ֑לְתִּי וְאָחַ֥ז בְּ֝שָׂרִ֗י פַּלָּצֽוּת׃
>
> “Even when I remember, I am disturbed, And horror takes hold of my flesh.

> **21:7** מַדּ֣וּעַ רְשָׁעִ֣ים יִחְי֑וּ עָ֝תְק֗וּ גַּם־גָּ֥בְרוּ חָֽיִל׃
>

> **21:8** זַרְעָ֤ם נָכֹ֣ון ᵃלִפְנֵיהֶ֣ם עִמָּ֑ם וְ֝צֶאֱצָאֵיהֶ֗םᵃ לְעֵינֵיהֶֽם׃
>
> “Their descendants are established with them in their sight, And their offspring before their eyes,

> **21:9** בָּתֵּיהֶ֣ם שָׁלֹ֣ום מִפָּ֑חַד וְלֹ֤א שֵׁ֖בֶט אֱלֹ֣והַּ עֲלֵיהֶֽם׃
>
> Their houses are safe from fear, And the rod of God is not on them.

> **21:10** שֹׁורֹ֣ו עִ֭בַּר וְלֹ֣א יַגְעִ֑ל תְּפַלֵּ֥ט פָּ֝רָתֹ֗ו וְלֹ֣א תְשַׁכֵּֽל׃
>
> “His ox mates without fail; His cow calves and does not abort.

> **21:11** יְשַׁלְּח֣וּ כַ֭צֹּאן עֲוִילֵיהֶ֑ם וְ֝יַלְדֵיהֶ֗ם יְרַקֵּדֽוּן׃
>
> “They send forth their little ones like the flock, And their children skip about.

> **21:12** יִ֭שְׂאוּ כְּתֹ֣ףᵃ וְכִנֹּ֑ור וְ֝יִשְׂמְח֗וּ לְקֹ֣ול עוּגָֽב׃
>
> “They sing to the timbrel and harp And rejoice at the sound of the flute.

> **21:13** יְבַלּ֣וּᵃ בַטֹּ֣וב יְמֵיהֶ֑ם וּ֝בְרֶ֗גַע שְׁאֹ֣ול יֵחָֽתּוּᵇ׃
>
> “They spend their days in prosperity, And suddenly they go down to Sheol.

> **21:14** וַיֹּאמְר֣וּ לָ֭אֵל ס֣וּר מִמֶּ֑נּוּ וְדַ֥עַת דְּ֝רָכֶ֗יךָ לֹ֣א חָפָֽצְנוּ׃
>
> “They say to God, ‘Depart from us! We do not even desire the knowledge of Your ways.

> **21:15** מַה־שַׁדַּ֥יᵃ כִּֽי־נַֽעַבְדֶ֑נּוּ וּמַה־נֹּ֝ועִ֗יל כִּ֣י נִפְגַּע־בֹּֽו׃
>
> ‘Who is the Almighty, that we should serve Him, And what would we gain if we entreat Him?’

> **21:16** הֵ֤ן לֹ֣א בְיָדָ֣ם טוּבָ֑ם עֲצַ֥ת רְ֝שָׁעִ֗ים רָ֣חֲקָה מֶֽנִּיᵃ׃
>
> “Behold, their prosperity is not in their hand; The counsel of the wicked is far from me.

> **21:17** כַּמָּ֤ה׀ נֵר־רְשָׁ֘עִ֤ים יִדְעָ֗ךְ וְיָבֹ֣א עָלֵ֣ימֹו אֵידָ֑ם חֲ֝בָלִ֗ים יְחַלֵּ֥ק בְּאַפֹּֽו׃
>
> “How often is the lamp of the wicked put out, Or does their calamity fall on them? Does God apportion destruction in His anger?

> **21:18** יִהְי֗וּ כְּתֶ֥בֶן לִפְנֵי־ר֑וּחַ וּ֝כְמֹ֗ץ גְּנָבַ֥תּוּ סוּפָֽה׃
>
> “Are they as straw before the wind, And like chaff which the storm carries away?

> **21:19** אֱלֹ֗והַּᵃ יִצְפֹּן־לְבָנָ֥יו אֹונֹ֑ו יְשַׁלֵּ֖ם אֵלָ֣יו וְיֵדָֽע׃
>
> “You say, ‘God stores away a man’s iniquity for his sons.’ Let God repay him so that he may know it.

> **21:20** יִרְא֣וּ עֵינָ֣ו כִּידֹ֑ו וּמֵחֲמַ֖ת שַׁדַּ֣י יִשְׁתֶּֽה׃
>
> “Let his own eyes see his decay, And let him drink of the wrath of the Almighty.

> **21:21** כִּ֤י מַה־חֶפְצֹ֣ו בְּבֵיתֹ֣ו אַחֲרָ֑יו וּמִסְפַּ֖ר חֳדָשָׁ֣יו חֻצָּֽצוּ׃
>
> “For what does he care for his household after him, When the number of his months is cut off?

> **21:22** הַלְאֵ֥ל יְלַמֶּד־דָּ֑עַת וְ֝ה֗וּא רָמִ֥ים יִשְׁפֹּֽוט׃

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims, each surfaced by several tools:

1. **The second round contracts. The friends stop speaking to Job and speak about "the wicked man"; Job stops speaking to God and speaks to the friends. The round opens with "the comforts of God" and closes with "How then will you comfort me?"** **New.** `[T]` for the counts; `[I]` for the design
   - **The friends' address to Job collapses** (WLC morphology, second-person forms):
     - round one: Eliphaz 40, Bildad 17, Zophar 33 (4–14 dig);
     - round two: Eliphaz 29 (all in 15:4–17), Bildad 4 (18:2–4, three of them plural), Zophar 1 (20:4).
     - After those lines each friend speaks only of the wicked man in the third person: Eliphaz for 18 of his 34 verses (15:18–35), Bildad for 17 of 20 (18:5–21), Zophar for 25 of 28 (20:5–29). The share grows with each speech.
   - **Job's address to God ends at 17:4.** In 16–17 Job speaks to God eleven times (16:7–8; 17:3–4) (16–17 dig). In chapter 19 there is no second-person singular at all (19 dig). In chapter 21 the only singulars are a friend ("you may mock", 21:3) and the wicked quoted speaking to God ("Depart from us!", 21:14). Job does not address God in his own voice again until 30:20–23.
   - **The round's subject is "the wicked".** רָשָׁע ("wicked") occurs in Job in 26 verses; 9 of them (35%) fall in this round, which holds 17% of the book's verses (15:20; 16:11; 18:5; 20:5, 29; 21:7, 16, 17, 28) (lemma). With עַוָּל ("unrighteous", 18:21) and חָנֵף ("godless", 15:34; 20:5), each friend ends on him: "the company of the godless" (15:34–35), "the dwellings of the unrighteous" (18:21), "the wicked man's portion from God" (20:29).
   - **"Comfort" brackets the round.**
     - תַּנְחוּמִים ("comforts, consolations") occurs in Job only at 15:11 and 21:2 (lemma): Eliphaz, "Are the consolations (תַּנְחֻמוֹת) of God too small for you?" (15:11); Job, "Listen carefully to my speech, and let this be your way of consolation (תַּנְחוּמֹתֵיכֶם)" (21:2).
     - Between and after them, the verb נחם: "Sorry comforters (מְנַחֲמֵי) are you all" (16:2); "How then will you vainly comfort me (תְּנַחֲמוּנִי)?" (21:34).
     - The friends came "to comfort him" (2:11); the family will (42:11) (overview Echo row, extended).
     - The ESV carries the whole chain: "comforts of God" (15:11), "miserable comforters" (16:2), "let this be your comfort" (21:2), "comfort me with empty nothings" (21:34).
   - *Surfaced by:* Structure (the address test), Repetition, Linking Words, Move 4. *High on the data; moderate–high on the design.*

2. **The friends' portrait of the wicked man's end is drawn from the prologue's list of Job's losses — fire from heaven, the sword, the fallen house, the lone survivor, the dead children, the diseased skin, the swallowed wealth — and from Sodom.** **New** as a pattern. `[T]` for each verbal link; `[I]` for the pattern
   - **v1.1 (claim audit 3): a reader-level resonance, not a demonstrated design.** The links are verified. But the portraits beat chance only on a list of elements taken from the portraits themselves; on a list drawn from the prologue they are within chance, and Job 27:13–23 is as dense. The irony is plain to a reader who knows chapters 1–2, and is strongest at 18:13 (skin), 18:19 (no survivor, no offspring) and 20:26 (fire). Sodom is reframed below `[S: audit 3]`.
   - **Fire from God.** "The fire of God fell from heaven (מִן־הַשָּׁמַיִם) … and consumed them (וַתֹּאכְלֵם)" (1:16) → "fire consumes (אָכְלָה) the tents of the corrupt" (Eliphaz, 15:34); "unfanned fire will devour him (תְּאָכְלֵהוּ)" and "the heavens (שָׁמַיִם) will reveal his iniquity" (Zophar, 20:26–27) → "the fire has consumed their remnant" (Eliphaz, 22:20). אֵשׁ ("fire") with אכל ("consume") in Job: 1:16; 15:34; 20:26; 22:20; 31:12 (lemma).
   - **The sword.** The Sabeans and Chaldeans killed the servants "with the edge of the sword" (1:15, 17) → the wicked man "is destined for the sword" (15:22).
   - **The house and tent.** The wind struck the house and it fell on the young people (1:19) → "houses no one would inhabit" (15:28); "He is torn from the security of his tent … There dwells in his tent nothing of his" (18:14–15); "The increase of his house will depart" (20:28).
   - **The survivor.** Four messengers each said "I alone have escaped" (1:15–19) → the wicked man has "no … survivor (שָׂרִיד) where he sojourned" (18:19); "Nothing remains (שָׂרִיד)" (20:21); fire "will consume the survivor (שָׂרִיד) in his tent" (20:26). שָׂרִיד in Job: 18:19; 20:21, 26; 27:15 (lemma).
   - **The children.** Job's sons and daughters died together (1:18–19) → "He has no offspring or posterity among his people" (18:19); "His sons favor the poor" (20:10). Spoken to a bereaved father (sweep).
   - **The skin and the wealth.** Job was struck "from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head" (2:7) → "His skin is devoured" (18:13). God said the Accuser had incited him "to swallow (לְבַלְּעוֹ)" Job (2:3) → the wicked man "swallows (בָּלַע) riches, but will vomit them up" (20:15, 18) (sweep).
   - **Sodom.** Bildad: "Brimstone (גָפְרִית) is scattered on his habitation" (18:15). Zophar: God "will rain (וְיַמְטֵר) it on him" (20:23). The LORD "rained (הִמְטִיר) brimstone (גָּפְרִית) and fire on Sodom" (Gen 19:24); מטר with גָּפְרִית occurs only at Gen 19:24, Ezek 38:22 and Ps 11:6 ("Upon the wicked He will rain snares; fire and brimstone"). Bildad and Zophar each supply half. Ps 11:6 adds "Fire and brimstone and burning wind will be the portion (מְנָת) of their cup" — Zophar's closing "portion" (20:29) and Job's "let him drink of the wrath" (21:20) are its other halves. And Zophar's poison is the Song of Moses' "vine of Sodom" (Deut 32:32–33; Headline 5). The prologue's "fire of God fell from heaven" (1:16) is the fire that fell on Sodom "from the LORD out of heaven" (Gen 19:24); the friends' portraits read Job's disaster as Sodom's. `[T]` for each verse; `[I]` for the composite. *v1.0: moderate.* **v1.1: low–moderate (motif).**
     - **Keep 18:15** as a Sodom-type motif: "brimstone" alone evokes Sodom, through Deut 29:22 [Eng 29:23] as much as Gen 19:24. Rahlfs asterisks the brimstone line.
     - **Read 20:23 with Ps 78:49 as the major partner.** "He sends his burning anger upon …" (יְשַׁלַּח־ב־ חֲרוֹן אַפּוֹ) occurs exactly only at Ps 78:49 and Job 20:23, and the same psalm has God "rain" food and strike Israel "while their food was in their mouths" (78:24–31). Ps 11:6 is a minor partner.
     - **Drop 20:29:** its חֵלֶק ("portion") is not Ps 11:6's מְנָת. 20:29 is a Job-internal refrain with 27:13.
     - **Drop 1:16 ~ Gen 19:24:** the exact אֵשׁ אֱלֹהִים ("fire of God") with "from heaven" and "consume" occurs only at 2 Kgs 1:12 and Job 1:16 `[S: audit 3]`.
   - **What the pattern does.** The friends do not name Job. Their "wicked man" suffers what Job has suffered. The reader who knows the prologue knows why these things happened, and so knows that the portraits are false of Job. `[I]` *Moderate–high.*
   - *Surfaced by:* Move 4 (the opening unit held open), Vocabulary, Tool 11, Who Am I?. *High on the words.* **v1.1: the pattern is a reader-level resonance; design not demonstrated** `[S: audit 3]`.

   - **Eliphaz answers Job's curse and Job's tree.**

     - "The eye of him who sees me will behold me no longer (לֹא־תְשׁוּרֵנִי עֵין רֹאִי) … nor will his place (מְקֹמוֹ) know him anymore" (7:8, 10) → "The eye which saw him sees him no longer, and his place (מְקוֹמוֹ) no longer beholds him (תְּשׁוּרֶנּוּ)" (20:9). שׁור ("behold") with עַיִן ("eye") in one verse only 7:8; 20:9; 24:15.

     - "I was at ease (שָׁלֵו), but He shattered me" (16:12) → "Because he knew no quiet (שָׁלֵו) within him" (20:20).
     - "You have insulted me" (כלם, 19:3) → "the reproof which insults me (כְּלִמָּתִי)" (20:3).

(20:4). עַד ("forever, of old") occurs in Job only at 19:24 and 20:4 (lemma 5703). Job's "I know … forever" is answered with "Do you know … from of old?"
     - Zophar's opening also echoes Eliphaz's vision: "my disquieting thoughts (שְׂעִפַּי)" (20:2) and "like a vision of the night (כְּחֶזְיוֹן לָיְלָה)" (20:8) take the words of 4:13 ("Amid disquieting thoughts (בִּשְׂעִפִּים) from the visions of the night (מֵחֶזְיֹנוֹת לָיְלָה)"). שְׂעִפִּים in Job only 4:13; 20:2; חֶזְיוֹן with לַיְלָה only 4:13; 20:8; 33:15.
   - **Zophar turns the witnesses.** Job called on the earth not to cover his blood (16:18) and named his witness "in heaven" (16:19); he confessed that his Redeemer "will rise (יָקוּם)" (19:25). 4. **Job's last speech of the round answers the three portraits clause by clause, and questions Proverbs and Psalm 1 by quoting them.** **New** except where marked. `[T]` for each verbal link; `[I]` for the pattern
   - **Against Eliphaz:**
     - "Sounds of terror (פְּחָדִים) are in his ears; while at peace (בַּשָּׁלוֹם) the destroyer comes upon him" (15:21) → "Their houses are safe (שָׁלוֹם) from fear (מִפָּחַד)" (21:9). פַּחַד with שָׁלוֹם in one verse: Jer 30:5; Job 15:21; 21:9; 25:2 (lemma).
     - "His wealth (חֵילוֹ) will not endure" (15:29) → "Why do the wicked … become very powerful (גָּבְרוּ חָיִל)?" (21:7).

     - "Your offspring (צֶאֱצָאֶיךָ) [will be] as the grass of the earth" (5:25) → "their offspring (וְצֶאֱצָאֵיהֶם) before their eyes" (21:8). Eliphaz promised Job what the wicked have.
     - "the integrity (וְתֹם) of your ways your hope" (4:6) → "One dies in his full strength (בְּעֶצֶם תֻּמּוֹ, literally 'in the bone of his integrity')" (21:23). תֹּם occurs in Job only at 4:6 and 21:23.

   - **Against Bildad:**
     - "the light of the wicked goes out (יִדְעָךְ) … his lamp (וְנֵרוֹ) goes out above him" (18:5–6) and "calamity (אֵיד) is ready at his side" (18:12) → "How often is the lamp of the wicked put out (נֵר־רְשָׁעִים יִדְעָךְ), or does their calamity (אֵידָם) fall on them?" (21:17). נֵר in Job only 18:6; 21:17; 29:3.
     - "those in the west are appalled (נָשַׁמּוּ) … seized (אָחֲזוּ) with horror" (18:20) → "Look at me, and be astonished (וְהָשַׁמּוּ) … horror takes hold (וְאָחַז) of my flesh" (21:5–6). שׁמם occurs in Job only at 16:7; 17:8; 18:20; 21:5, all in this round. ## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

- **The purpose the book states.** The book states its own purpose only through the frame: the question "Does Job fear God for nothing?" (1:9) and the verdict "you have not spoken of Me what is right as My servant Job has" (42:7). `[T]`
- **This round's place in that purpose.**
  - The friends move from advising Job to describing the end of the wicked. Their portraits reproduce Job's losses (Headline 2), so they say, without naming him, what the Accuser expected and what the reader knows is false: that Job's suffering shows what he is.
  - Job moves from complaint to God to argument with the friends. His appeal to God reaches its height (witness, surety, Redeemer), and then he turns to the evidence of the world (21:29).
  - The round asks the Accuser's question from the other side. Will Job keep fearing God when the doctrine of retribution says he is wicked? And is the doctrine true? (Job 21). `[T]` for the content; `[I]` for the function.
- **For the reader who knows the prologue.** The reader knows the friends' portraits are false of Job, because the reader knows where the fire, the sword and the wind came from (1:12, 16, 19; 2:3). The round tests the reader too: will the reader believe the doctrine or the narrator? `[I]`

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

- **What the preceding movement requires.**
  - Round one ended with Job saying that God destroys man's hope (14:19) and with the one hope he could name, "You will call, and I will answer You" (14:15) (4–14 dig).
  - The friends have not persuaded him. The book needs to show what they do when their advice fails. They harden it into description: the wicked man's end, three times over. `[T]` for the content; `[I]` for the necessity.
- **Why this round is here.**
  - It holds the dialogue's highest confessions (16:19; 17:3; 19:25) inside its harshest portraits (15:20–35; 18:5–21; 20:4–29).
  - It ends with Job taking the doctrine on directly (21), which prepares Eliphaz's turn to open accusation in round three ("Is not your wickedness great?", 22:5) and the round's collapse (25–27).
  - Its last word, "falsehood" (מַעַל, 21:34), is answered at the end of the book by God's verdict on the friends (42:7). `[T]` for the sequence; `[I]` for the function.

#### Surrounding context

- **Before: round one (4:1–14:22).** The argument about hope (4–14 dig).
- **After: round three (22:1–27:23).** Eliphaz quotes Job's 21:14 and 21:16 back to him (22:17–18; overview, Table B); Bildad's last speech is six verses (25:1–6) and repeats 15:14 (25:4); Zophar does not speak; and Job's 27:13 is almost Zophar's 20:29 (overview; BHS cross-reference).

### 4. Linking Words

- **"Because" and "for" in the portraits.** The friends give reasons for the wicked man's end: "Because (כִּי) he has stretched out his hand against God" (15:25); "For (כִּי) he has covered his face with his fat" (15:27); "For (כִּי) he is thrown into the net by his own feet" (18:8); "For (כִּי) he has oppressed and forsaken the poor" (20:19); "Because (כִּי) he knew no quiet within him" (20:20). The cause is always in the man. `[T]` for the connectives; `[I]` for the pattern.
- **"How often?" (כַּמָּה, 21:17).** Job's whole reply turns on one question word. He does not say "never"; he asks "how often?" — an empirical question about a doctrinal claim. `[T]` for the word; `[I]` for the force.
- **"If you say … for you say" (כִּי תֹאמְרוּ, 19:28; 21:28).** Twice Job quotes the friends before answering them: "If you say, 'How shall we persecute him?'" (19:28); "For you say, 'Where is the house of the nobleman?'" (21:28). And at 21:19 he quotes without the formula ("God stores away a man's iniquity for his sons"; NASB95 and ESV supply "You say"). `[T]`
- **"One … another" (זֶה … וְזֶה, 21:23, 25).** "One dies in his full strength … while another dies with a bitter soul" — and then "Together (יַחַד) they lie down in the dust" (21:26). The connectives carry the argument: the two deaths differ; the end is the same. `[T]`
- **"Indeed" and "surely" at Bildad's turns** (גַּם, 18:5; אַךְ, 18:21): the opening and closing of the portrait. `[T]`

### 5. Parallels

- **The portraits' paired lines.** Light // flame (18:5); light // lamp (18:6); roots below // branch above (18:16); west // east (18:20); heaven // earth (20:27). The parallels give the portraits a cosmic completeness: the wicked man's end is total. `[T]`
- **Zophar's food sequence.** Sweet in the mouth // hidden under the tongue (20:12); food in the stomach // venom of cobras within (20:14); swallows riches // vomits them (20:15). Taste becomes poison. `[T]`
- **Job's matched pictures.** "Their houses are safe from fear // the rod of God is not on them" (21:9); "His ox mates without fail // his cow calves and does not abort" (21:10); "One dies in his full strength // another dies with a bitter soul" (21:23, 25). `[T]`
- **The answering verses.** Job's 21 matches the friends' parallels with his own: Bildad's lamp (18:6) against his "How often is the lamp of the wicked put out?" (21:17); Zophar's dust (20:11) against "together they lie down in the dust" (21:26). See Headline 4. `[T]`

### 9. Tone and Feel

- **The friends harden.**
  - Eliphaz opens in irony and alarm ("Were you the first man to be born?", 15:7) and closes in a long, measured portrait (15:20–35).
  - Bildad is stung ("Why are we regarded as beasts?", 18:3) and then cold: the portrait without a single word to Job after 18:4.
  - Zophar is agitated ("my disquieting thoughts make me respond, even because of my inward agitation", 20:2) and then lavish: the most physical of the three portraits, food turning to poison in the belly.
  - `[T]` for the markers; `[I]` for the labels.
- **Job moves from anguish to argument.** 16–17 is lament and appeal; 19 is lament, plea and confession; 21 is the coolest of Job's speeches so far — observation, question and rebuttal, almost without lament (only 21:4–6). "Look at me, and be astonished, and put your hand over your mouth" (21:5) is the voice of a man who has stopped pleading. `[I]`
- **The cruelty is in the details.** The portraits are general in form and particular in content (Headline 2). A bereaved father hears that the wicked man has "no offspring or posterity" (18:19) and that "his sons favor the poor" (20:10); a man with diseased skin hears that "his skin is devoured" (18:13). The sweep's pastoral note stands: name the cruelty. `[I]`
- **The round's emotional centre is in Job's middle speech** — "Pity me, pity me" (19:21) and "My heart faints within me" (19:27) — between the two portraits that do not pity (18; 20). `[I]`

### 11. Quotation / Allusion — with Move 4

The intertexts of 16–17 and 19 are worked in their solo digs; their findings are summarised in Headline 5. This section works the four speeches without solo digs. The significant contacts receive the full Triad.

**Isa 59:1–10 → 15:17–35** *(v1.0: moderate–high. **v1.1: low–moderate (idiom)**, with Isa 33:11–15 as co-source; rank withdrawn* `[S: audit 3]`*)*
- **Move 1 — source context.** Isaiah's indictment of a people whose "iniquities have made a separation between you and your God" (59:2): "They conceive mischief and bring forth iniquity" (59:4); "Their feet run to evil" (59:7); "We hope for light, but behold, darkness" (59:9). The chapter turns at 59:15–20 to the LORD who sees there is no justice and comes as Redeemer ("A Redeemer will come to Zion", 59:20).
- **Move 2 — book usage.** Isaiah is live in Job (Isa 50; 53:9; 38:14; 44:6; 26:19; 30:8 — solo digs and 4–14 dig).
- **Move 3 — OT to OT.** Ps 7:15 [Eng 7:14] has the same idiom ("he conceives mischief and brings forth falsehood"), and its window ranks 8th against 15:17–35. The idiom is shared; Isaiah's chapter shares the most beside it.
- **What it adds.** Eliphaz paints the wicked man with Isaiah's portrait of a sinful people — and Isaiah's chapter ends with the Redeemer who comes, the title Job will use (19:25). `[I]` *Moderate–high on Isa 59:1–10 as a source; the Redeemer link is reader's synthesis.*

**Isa 14:9–23 → 18:5–21; 21:12–13, 26** *(v1.1: moderate for 14:21–22 at 18:17–19 and 14:11 at 21:26; 21:12–13 reassigned to Isa 5:11–14; the tomb contrast at 21:32 low* `[S: audit 3]`*)* **New**

### 12. Genre

- **The forms.** A disputation in poetry, in which each friend combines:
  - a rebuke of Job (15:2–16; 18:2–4; 20:2–3);
  - an appeal to tradition or to the order of the world (15:17–19; 20:4);
  - a **didactic poem on the fate of the wicked** (15:20–35; 18:5–21; 20:5–29), close to the wisdom genre of Proverbs and the Psalter's teaching psalms (Ps 1; 37; 73).
- Job's speeches combine lament (16–17; 19:7–20), legal appeal (16:18–17:5; 19:23–27), confession (19:25–27) and, in 21, **disputation by counter-example** — "Have you not asked wayfaring men?" (21:29).
- `[T]` for the elements; `[I]` for the classification.
- **What the genre forbids.** The friends' poems are true as general wisdom — the lamp of the wicked does go out (Prov 13:9; 24:20). Their error is the genre-switch: a proverb about what usually happens is applied as a verdict on a particular man. Job's 21 is not a counter-doctrine ("the wicked always prosper") but a counter-example ("how often?"). Neither portrait is the book's last word (42:7). `[I]` *High.*

### 13. Copycat

- **The cautions.**
  - The friends' portraits are not offered as a pattern for counsel: their speech is "falsehood" (21:34) and is not "right" (42:7).
  - Job's rebuttal is not offered as a pattern of cynicism about providence: he does not say God never judges; he says the friends cannot read judgement off a man's circumstances.
- **What can be copied.**
  - Listening as comfort: "let this be your way of consolation" (21:2).
  - Honesty about what the world shows, without giving up the confession of 19:25.
  - `[I]`

### 14. Bible Timeline

- **Setting.** Patriarchal (overview). The book's date and composition are not stated `[S]`.
- **Canonical position.** Ketuvim, after the Psalter and before Proverbs (BHS order). The reader comes to this round having read:
  - the Torah's Sodom (Gen 19) and the Song of Moses (Deut 32);
  - the Prophets' oracle on Babylon's king (Isa 14), Isaiah's indictment and Redeemer (Isa 59), Jeremiah's "Why does the way of the wicked prosper?" (Jer 12:1);
  - the Psalter's two ways (Ps 1) and its fire and brimstone (Ps 11:6).
  - Proverbs, which follows Job, keeps the proverb Job questions (Prov 13:9; 24:20). Lamentations, later in the Ketuvim, shares the round's images of judgement (Lam 3; 4). `[T]` for the order; direction of dependence open.
- **The New Testament end of the line.** The question of the round — whether suffering shows guilt — is put to Jesus ("who sinned, this man or his parents?", John 9:2) and answered by him ("Do you suppose that these Galileans were greater sinners …? I tell you, no", Luke 13:2–3). See Christological Reading.

### 15. Who Am I?

- **Like the friends, the reader who explains.** Every reader who has thought, "He must have done something", speaks with Eliphaz, Bildad and Zophar. The portraits are attractive because they are partly true.
- **Like Job, the reader who cannot reconcile what he believes with what he sees.** "Why do the wicked still live, continue on, also become very powerful?" (21:7).
- **Like the friends again, the reader who stops talking to the sufferer** and talks about him (Headline 1).
- **Unlike all of them, the reader who knows the prologue** — and so knows that the fire, the sword and the wind in the portraits were permitted for another reason (1:12; 2:6). `[I]`

### 16. So What?

**Stage 1 — the response the round seeks.** `[I]`
- From the friends (and the reader with them): to stop reading judgement off suffering, to stop describing the wicked man to a man who has lost his children, and to listen.
- From the sufferer: to hold the confession ("I know that my Redeemer lives", 19:25) and the honest observation ("How often?", 21:17) together.
- The book answers the round at its end: the friends are rebuked (42:7), and Job's "Redeemer" is the God who speaks (38:1) and restores (42:10).

**Stage 2.**

| Domain | Application |
|---|---|
| **Worldview** | God judges, but his judgement cannot be read off a person's circumstances in this life. The lamp of the wicked does go out — not always, and not on schedule |
| **Stop** | Comforting with portraits of what happens to the wicked; quoting true proverbs as verdicts; talking about the sufferer instead of to him |
| **Start** | Listening (21:2); pitying (19:21); holding faith and honesty together |
| **Motivation** | The Redeemer Job confessed stood on the earth (Rev 1:17–18). Jesus refused the friends' logic (John 9:2–3; Luke 13:1–5), and he himself was "numbered with transgressors" (Isa 53:12; Luke 22:37) |

**Four audiences.**

| Audience | What the round means |
|---|---|
| **For me** | When others read my suffering as a verdict, God has not given that verdict. My Redeemer lives |
| **For a Christian friend** | Do not describe the end of the wicked to the bereaved. Let your listening be your comfort (21:2) |
| **For the church** | A church can become three friends with a doctrine and no pity. Or it can be the brothers and sisters who come at the end "and comforted him" (42:11) |
| **For an unbeliever** | The Bible itself asks, "Why do the wicked still live?" (21:7), and lets the question stand beside its strongest confession of hope. Its faith does not depend on pretending the world is fair |

**Prayer.** "Listen carefully to my speech" (21:2) — the sufferer's prayer to those around him, and to God, who heard Job (42:9).

- **The address test** (WLC morphology; Headline 1). Second-person forms by speech: Eliphaz 29 singular (15:4–17); Job 16–17, 11 singular (to God, the earth, the pit) and 9 plural (to the friends); Bildad 1 singular (18:4) and 3 plural (18:2–3); Job 19, 17 plural; Zophar 1 singular (20:4); Job 21, 3 singular (21:3, to one friend; 21:14, the wicked quoted to God) and 13 plural. `[T]`

Bildad addresses more than Job: perhaps Job with any who side with him, or Job as one of a class. Then "O you (singular) who tear yourself in your anger" (18:4). `[T]` for the forms; `[I]` for the referent. *Open.*
- **20:4 הֲזֹאת יָדַעְתָּ ("Do you know this?").** BHS: one manuscript and the Greek read הֲלֹא ("Do you not know?"), which the ESV follows ("Do you not know this from of old"). - **20:27 מִתְקוֹמָמָה ("rises up").** The hithpolel of קום (lemma 6965 b), "rise up in hostility" — the stem of the enemies "who rise up against" the psalmist (Ps 17:7; 59:2 [Eng 59:1]). The same verb as 19:25's יָקוּם, in a hostile stem. `[T]` for the form.
- **21:13 וּבְרֶגַע.** רֶגַע I is "a moment, an instant"; a second root רגע ("be at rest") gives "in peace" (ESV, NIV84; NIV84 margin "in an instant"). - **21:16 הֵן לֹא בְיָדָם טוּבָם.** Literally "Behold, not in their hand is their good". As a statement: "their prosperity is not in their hand" (it is God's gift, NASB95, NIV84). As a question: "Is not their prosperity in their hand?" (ESV). Either way the second line follows: "The counsel of the wicked is far from me". `[T]` for the words; *open*.
- **21:19 the unmarked quotation.** "God stores away his iniquity for his sons" has no introductory formula; the NASB95 and ESV supply "You say". The next line answers it: "Let God repay him (יְשַׁלֵּם אֵלָיו) so that he may know it (וְיֵדָע)". `[T]`; the quotation reading `[I]`, *high*.

### Historical and Cultural Background

- **Burial and honour (21:32–33).** "He is carried to the grave (לִקְבָרוֹת), men will keep watch over his tomb (גָּדִישׁ)"; "all men will follow after him, while countless ones go before him". An honoured burial with a procession and a guarded tomb — the opposite of the fate Bildad gave the wicked ("Memory of him perishes", 18:17) and of Isaiah's tyrant "cast out of your tomb" (Isa 14:19). `[T]` for the texts; the customs `[S]`.
- **"Wayfaring men" (21:29).** Travellers bring news of distant places; Job appeals to the widest experience against the friends' tradition ("what wise men have told", 15:18). `[I]`
- **Music (21:12).** Timbrel (תֹּף), harp (כִּנּוֹר) and flute (עוּגָב) are the instruments of celebration; harp and flute together first in Gen 4:21 (Jubal) and in Job only at 21:12 and 30:31. `[T]`
- **Sodom.** For a reader of the Torah, brimstone and fire "rained" from heaven mean Sodom (Gen 19:24; Deut 29:22 [Eng 29:23]). The friends' imagery of the wicked man's end draws on it (Headline 2). `[T]` for the texts.
- **Serpent's venom (20:14–16).** The cobra (פֶּתֶן) and viper (אֶפְעֶה) were the paradigms of deadly poison (Deut 32:33; Isa 11:8; 59:5). `[T]` for the texts.
- **"Portion" and "heritage" (20:29).** חֵלֶק and נַחֲלָה are the words of the land allotment (Num 18:20; Josh 18–19). Zophar uses inheritance language for punishment: the wicked man's "heritage" is God's anger. `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the irony.

### Original Audience Reception

- **What the reader of the Torah and Prophets would hear in the portraits.** Sodom's fire and brimstone; the Song of Moses' vine of Sodom; Isaiah's fallen tyrant; Proverbs' lamp of the wicked. The friends speak the canon's language of judgement, accurately. `[I]`
- **What the reader of the prologue would hear.** That the friends are describing Job (Headline 2), and that they are wrong about him. `[I]`
- **What they would expect of Job 21.** A wisdom reader expects the two ways of Ps 1 to hold. Job quotes Ps 1 and Prov 13:9 and asks "how often?" The reader of the Psalter knows that Ps 73 asks the same question and answers it at the sanctuary ("Then I perceived their end", Ps 73:17). Job does not yet have that answer; the round ends on "falsehood" (21:34). `[I]`

### Biblical-Theological Themes

- **Retribution in this life.** The friends' doctrine is the general truth of the wisdom tradition (Prov 13:9; 24:20; Ps 1:4–6) applied as a particular verdict. Job's counter-example (Job 21) belongs with Ps 73, Jer 12:1–4 and Eccl 7:15; 8:14 ("there are righteous men to whom it happens according to the deeds of the wicked"). The canon holds both, and resolves them in a final judgement (Ps 73:17–20; Dan 12:2; Acts 17:31). `[T]` for the texts; `[I]` for the synthesis.
- **Sodom as a type of judgement.** Gen 19 → Deut 29:22 [Eng 29:23] → Deut 32:32 → Isa 1:9–10 → Lam 4:6 → Luke 17:28–30. The friends make Job's house a Sodom; the book's verdict reverses them. `[I]`
- **The day of wrath.** "The wicked is reserved for the day of calamity; they will be led forth at the day of fury" (21:30): Job grants a day of reckoning while denying that the friends can see it now. Zeph 1:15; Rom 2:5. `[I]`
- **The righteous sufferer counted wicked.** Job is painted as the wicked man by friends who should know better — as the Servant was "numbered with the transgressors" (Isa 53:12). `[I]`

### Christological Reading

- **Within the round:** witness (16:19–21), surety (17:3), Redeemer (19:25). - **What the friends' portraits add.** The Redeemer Job confesses is confessed in the middle of three speeches that paint him as the wicked man. The Servant whom Isaiah sees is likewise counted among the wicked: "His grave was assigned with wicked men … because He had done no violence" (Isa 53:9, already at Job 16:17) and "numbered with the transgressors" (53:12) — words Jesus applies to himself ("this which is written must be fulfilled in Me, 'And He was numbered with transgressors'", Luke 22:37). And the Servant receives a "portion" (אֲחַלֶּק־לוֹ, Isa 53:12) — the word of Zophar's "wicked man's portion" (20:29) and Job's "Does God apportion destruction?" (21:17). `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the typology. *Moderate.*
- **Jesus and the friends' logic.** Asked whether a man born blind or his parents had sinned, Jesus answered, "It was neither that this man sinned, nor his parents" (John 9:2–3). Of the Galileans killed by Pilate and of "those eighteen on whom the tower in Siloam fell and killed them" — a building falling, as on Job's children (1:19) — he asked, "Do you suppose that these Galileans were greater sinners …? I tell you, no" (Luke 13:2–5). The round's question is answered by Christ in the friends' disfavour. `[T]` for the texts.

- **Keep honest.** The round's Christ-ward weight is in Job's speeches (16:19; 17:3; 19:25), not in the friends' portraits. Do not make the portraits allegories of the cross; they are false accusations which the cross, among other things, answers.

**The moralism check.** The trap is "don't be like Job's friends" as the whole sermon. The friends' failure is real, but the sermon's subject is the God whom the friends misrepresent and Job appeals to: the judge who will not be read off circumstances, and the Redeemer who lives. `[I]`

### Difficult / Contested Verses

#### 18:19; 20:10; 21:19 — the wicked man's children

- **The difficulty:** *pastoral.* Bildad tells a bereaved father that the wicked man has "no offspring" (18:19); Zophar that "his sons favor the poor" (20:10); and Job quotes the doctrine "God stores away a man's iniquity for his sons" (21:19).
- **Honest exegesis:** the friends' lines are cruel by application, as the sweep says. Job's reply rejects the transfer of guilt to children: "Let God repay him so that he may know it" (21:19). Compare Ezekiel's answer to the proverb of the sour grapes: the son will not bear the father's iniquity (Ezek 18:20, paraphrased; the Ezekiel export was not staged for this run).
- **Preaching:** name the cruelty; never quote 18:19 or 20:10 near the bereaved as Scripture's word to them.

#### 21:7–16 — "the wicked prosper"

- **The difficulty:** *doctrinal.* Does Job deny that God judges the wicked, against Ps 1 and Prov 13:9?
- **Honest exegesis:** Job's question is "how often?" (21:17), not "never". He grants a day of calamity (21:30) and disowns the counsel of the wicked (21:16). His target is the friends' claim that judgement can be read off a man's circumstances now.
- **Confidence:** high on the grammar of 21:17; moderate on 21:16 (statement or question).

#### 21:23–26 — "Together they lie down in the dust"

- **The difficulty:** *apparent nihilism.* If good and wicked die alike, where is justice?
- **Honest exegesis:** Job states what Ecclesiastes states ("There is one fate for the righteous and for the wicked", Eccl 9:2) and what the friends' portraits deny. He does not deny God's justice; he denies that death sorts the righteous from the wicked. The answer the book gives is not in death but in God's appearing (38–42) and, in the canon, in resurrection (Dan 12:2).
- **Preaching:** do not answer 21:26 too fast. Let it stand beside 19:25, as the round does: "upon the dust" both times.

#### 20:27 — heaven and earth against the wicked

- **The difficulty:** *interpretive.* Is Zophar answering Job's appeal to heaven and earth (16:18–19)?
- **Honest exegesis:** the words are Job's (heaven, earth, "rise"); the use is hostile (the hithpolel "rise up against"). The text does not say Zophar means Job, but the round's sequence (16:18–19 → 19:25 → 20:27) makes the reply audible (Headline 3). *Moderate–high.*

#### 15:14–16 — "man, who drinks iniquity like water"

- **The difficulty:** *doctrinal and pastoral.* Is Eliphaz's doctrine of human sinfulness true?
- **Honest exegesis:** As doctrine it is close to what Scripture says elsewhere (Ps 143:2; Rom 3:10–12; 13–14 dig on 14:4). As a reply to Job it is a misapplication: it makes Job's suffering the proof of a sin the narrator denies (1:1, 8; 2:3). Eliphaz's line is quoted again by Bildad (25:4) and turned on Job by Elihu ("drinks up derision like water", 34:7; sweep).
- **Preaching:** true words, wrong verdict — the round's whole problem in one verse.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: Three portraits and three rebuttals — six sermons in one

- **What it looks like:** "Eliphaz said … Bildad said … Zophar said … and Job answered each."
- **Why it's wrong:** the round's meaning is in the movement — the conversation contracting, the portraits growing, Job's appeal rising and then turning to argument. A tour of six speeches loses it.
- **Corrective:** preach the round's two stories together: the portrait of "the wicked man" that is really Job's losses, and the witness–surety–Redeemer that rises inside it. Let 21 be the answer to the portraits and 19:25 the answer to everything.

### Pitfall: Quoting the friends' portraits as God's word to the suffering

- **What it looks like:** "The lamp of the wicked shall be put out" (18:5) or "the triumphing of the wicked is short" (20:5) offered as comfort or warning.
- **Why it's wrong:** the lines are true as proverbs (Prov 13:9; 24:20) and false as verdicts on Job (42:7). The book quotes them in order to test them.
- **Corrective:** quote them with their speaker, and with Job's "how often?" (21:17).

### Pitfall: "The wicked prosper" as the sermon's conclusion

- **What it looks like:** Job 21 preached as the Bible's realism against naive faith, and left there.
- **Why it's wrong:** Job's question is "how often?", not "never"; he grants a day of calamity (21:30) and disowns the counsel of the wicked (21:16). And the round has already heard 19:25.
- **Corrective:** hold 21:26 ("together they lie down in the dust") beside 19:25 ("He will take His stand on the dust"). The canon finishes the thought (Ps 73:17; Dan 12:2; Acts 17:31).

### Pitfall: Making the friends fools — or monsters

- **What it looks like:** caricaturing the friends' speeches as obviously wrong.
- **Why it's wrong:** they speak Proverbs, Psalm 1, Isaiah and the Song of Moses accurately (Headline 5). Their error is application and pitilessness, and every congregation contains people who reason as they do.
- **Corrective:** let the friends sound wise. Then show what their wisdom does to a bereaved, diseased man.

### Pitfall: Using 18:19, 20:10 or 21:19 near the bereaved

- **What it looks like:** a line about the wicked man's children read without comment.
- **Why it's wrong:** the lines are aimed at a father whose children are dead (sweep). Job rejects the transfer of guilt (21:19).
- **Corrective:** name the cruelty plainly, and be aware of who is in the room.
