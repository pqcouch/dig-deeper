**Extent.** The unit runs from 3:1 to the setumah after 3:33. Three facts fix that boundary:

- it closes the כ (Kaph) stanza;
- the book's arithmetic midpoint (verse 77 of 154) falls between 3:33 and 3:34;
- the unit is bracketed by the root ענה ("afflict"): עֳנִי ("affliction", 3:1) and עִנָּה ("he afflicts", 3:33).

*Alternatives rejected:*

- **3:1–39 or 3:1–42** would keep the wisdom section (3:25–39) whole and reach the turn to "we" (3:40–42). That would gain the answer of 3:42 ("You have not pardoned") but lose the centre as the end of a unit.
- **3:1–24** (the sweep's Unit 3) would end on the hymn, which is the trap the overview names first.

3:34–42 was loaded and read so that the boundary is not drawn blind.

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims that emerged across several tools:

1. **The unit is bracketed by affliction, and it turns a formula of the exodus inside out.**
   - *Bracket.* It opens אֲנִי הַגֶּבֶר רָאָה עֳנִי ("I am the man who has seen affliction", 3:1) and closes כִּי לֹא עִנָּה מִלִּבּוֹ ("for he does not afflict from his heart", 3:33). Between them stands עָנְיִי ("my affliction", 3:19), at the turn.
   - *Formula.* "Seeing affliction" (ראה, "see" + עֳנִי, "affliction") stands in 15 verses of the Hebrew Bible. Elsewhere **it is God who sees, or is asked to see**, the affliction. He sees it at Gen 29:32; 31:42; Exod 3:7; 4:31; Deut 26:7; 2 Kgs 14:26; Ps 31:8; Neh 9:9. He is asked to see it at 1 Sam 1:11; Pss 9:14; 25:18; 119:153; Lam 1:9.
   - *Inversion.* At Lam 3:1 the sufferer is the one who has "seen" it — under God's own rod. Zion has asked "See, O LORD, my affliction" (1:9). The poem answers first with a man who has seen it from the inside, and God does not "see" until 3:59–60.

   *Surfaced by:* Structure, Repetition, Vocabulary, Tool 11, Positional Necessity. `[T]` for the chain and the counts; `[I]` for the inversion as design. *Confidence: high (chain); moderate–high (inversion).*

2. **The assailant of 3:1–16 is "He", never named, and the man describes him in words the psalms use of the wicked and the enemy.**
   - *The count.* Twenty-four finite verbs with God as the implied subject run from 3:2 to 3:16 (third person masculine singular, WLC morphology). The divine name does not appear until 3:18, and then in despair.
   - *The images belong elsewhere to the enemy or the wicked:*

     | Image | Elsewhere | Exclusivity |
     |---|---|---|
     | The lion "lying in wait … in secret places" (3:10) | **the wicked man** of Ps 10:9 (ארב, "lie in wait" + אַרְיֵה, "lion" + מִסְתָּר, "hiding place"); and the wicked of **Ps 17:12** ("a young lion sitting in hiding places") | exclusive to Ps 10:9 and Lam 3:10; אַרְיֵה + מִסְתָּר in three verses (Ps 10:9; 17:12; Lam 3:10) `[S: audit 3]` |
     | "He has made me dwell in dark places like the dead of long ago" (3:6) | spoken of **the enemy** at Ps 143:3 | verbatim but for the order of the first two words |
     | "He has bent his bow" (3:12) | repeats 2:4, where the Lord did it **"like an enemy"** | — |

   - The psalms supplied the man with words for what *the enemy* does; he uses them of God. **Preach this as the man's own choice of words, not as the poet's design.** Claim audit 3 ran a baseline: describing God in an enemy's images is ordinary in these laments (Lam 2:1–8 has 6 such partners of 28; Job 16:7–17 has 2 of 3), and Lam 3:1–16's own rare partners split evenly with passages in which God is the agent. Job 19:6–12 is the closest overall rival `[S: audit 3]`. `[T]` for the wording; `[I]` for the reading. *Confidence: high (the contacts); low–moderate (as design).*

3. **The turn is an act of memory, voiced in the words of Ps 42–43, and 3:31 answers the question of Ps 77 in its own terms.**
   - *The chain.* The verb זנח ("reject") runs from "my soul was rejected from peace" (3:17) to "the Lord will not reject for ever" (3:31). The only other place it occurs in Lamentations is 2:7, "the Lord has rejected his altar".
   - *Ps 77's question answered.* "Will the Lord reject for ever?" (Ps 77:8 [Eng 77:7]) is answered at Lam 3:31: "the Lord will not reject for ever". **זנח + עוֹלָם occurs only at Ps 77:8 and Lam 3:31**, and the Greek of the two is near-identical. "Reject" + חֶסֶד + רַחֲמִים within ±4 verses is also exclusive to Ps 77:8–10 and Lam 3:31–32 `[S: audit 3]`. *Moderate–high.*
   - *The turn's model is Ps 42–43.*
     - Ps 42:7 [Eng 42:6]: עָלַי נַפְשִׁי תִשְׁתּוֹחָח עַל־כֵּן אֶזְכָּרְךָ ("my soul is bowed down within me; **therefore** I remember you").
     - Lam 3:20–21: ותשיח עָלַי נַפְשִׁי … עַל־כֵּן אוֹחִיל ("my soul is bowed down within me … **therefore** I have hope").
     - The refrain of Ps 42–43 is הוֹחִילִי ("hope!", Ps 42:6, 12; 43:5); Lam 3:21, 24, 26 answer with אוֹחִיל ("I will hope").
     - זכר ("remember") + נֶפֶשׁ ("soul") + יחל ("hope") within ±1 verse occurs **only at Ps 42:5, 42:7 and Lam 3:20** `[S: audit 3]`.
     - The "only Ps 44:26" claimed in v1.0 for "my soul is bowed down" was an artefact of the lemma index, which files the related verb שׁחח ("be bowed down") under a separate number (7817) from Lamentations' שׁוח (7743).
     - On the ketiv and in the Greek (καταδολεσχήσει, "will muse"), the partner is Ps 77:4, 7 instead.
   - *Ps 88* supplies the prison of 3:7–8: "and I cannot go out" (וְלֹא אֵצֵא, only Ps 88:9 and Lam 3:7) and "I cry for help … my prayer" (only Ps 88:14 and Lam 3:8) `[S: audit 3]`.
   - *Withdrawn in v1.1:* the claim that 3:17–33 replies to a cluster of four psalms (44, 74, 77, 88). The block's density in that family is not unusual within Lamentations (Lam 1:1–17 scores higher). Also withdrawn: the 3:17–18 "for ever" window, because 3:18's נִצְחִי means "my endurance" `[S: audit 3]`.

   `[T]` for the chains; `[I]` for the reading. *Confidence: moderate–high (Ps 77:8 at 3:31; Ps 42–43 at 3:20–24 on the qere; Ps 88 at 3:7–8).*

4. **God's חֶסֶד ("lovingkindness") and רַחֲמִים ("compassion") open and close 3:22–32, and the unit ends on God's heart.**
   - *The frame.* חַסְדֵי … רַחֲמָיו ("lovingkindnesses … his compassions", 3:22) is answered by וְרִחַם כְּרֹב חסדו ("he will have compassion according to the abundance of his lovingkindness", 3:32). These are **the only two occurrences of חֶסֶד in the book**.
   - *The heart.* It lands on 3:33: "not from his heart". The man brought "this" back "to my heart" (אֶל־לִבִּי, 3:21); the poem ends the unit on what is *not* "from his heart" (מִלִּבּוֹ, 3:33).
   - *Poem 1 answered.* "Though he causes grief" (הוֹגָה, 3:32) answers poem 1's "the LORD has caused her grief **for the multitude** of her transgressions" (1:5). It does so with the only other יגה ("cause grief") + רֹב ("multitude, abundance") in the Hebrew Bible (1:5; 3:32) `[S: audit]`.

   - *v1.1.* The reversed order (חֶסֶד … רַחֲמִים at 3:22; רחם … חֶסֶד at 3:32) is **an observation, not a design**. The pair occurs in either order about equally often elsewhere (11 against 10), and the same reversal within ten verses recurs in Isa 54, Mic 7, Ps 40 and Ps 103 `[S: audit 3]`. The 1:5 → 3:32 tie and the 3:21 ↔ 3:33 "heart" frame stand.

   *Surfaced by:* Structure, Parallels, Repetition, Vocabulary, Move 4. `[T]` for the chains; `[I]` for the frame. *Confidence: high (chains); low–moderate (chiasm as design).* *Synthetic.*

5. **The pulpit text hides three things the Hebrew shows.**
   - **3:22.** The Masoretic text has the poem's first "we": כִּי לֹא־תָמְנוּ ("for **we** are not consumed"). It is the only first-person plural in 3:1–39, and the root תמם ("be finished") returns only at 4:22, "your punishment is **completed**". ESV and NASB95 both follow the emendation (BHS: one manuscript, the Syriac and the Targum), so the congregation hears "never ceases" — not "we", and not the 4:22 link.
   - **3:17 → 3:31.** The ESV renders זנח ("reject") "is bereft" at 3:17 but "cast off" at 3:31, so the link between them cannot be heard. The NASB95 keeps "rejected … reject".
   - **3:21.** The ESV supplies "**But**" and heads the chapter "Great Is Your Faithfulness". The Hebrew has no adversative, and its first word is זֹאת ("this").

   *Surfaced by:* Translations, Original Languages, Textual Variants, Repetition. `[T]` *Confidence: high.*

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book level** (overview v0.2.1, confirmed by the sweep): to give a ruined people ordered words — confession, protest, memory and petition — with which to keep addressing the LORD when he is silent. The ground is that his חֶסֶד ("lovingkindness") is not exhausted and his throne endures (3:22; 5:19). `[I]`

**Passage level.** 3:1–33 makes the book's one sustained *movement*, from "my hope from the LORD has perished" (3:18) to "therefore I have hope in him" (3:24). It does not reach hope by denying the assault. It **remembers the assault in the same words** — wormwood and gall, 3:15, 5 → 3:19 — and finds inside that memory something the assault has not destroyed. Then, in the wisdom of 3:25–33, it teaches how hope is to be held while the affliction lasts: by waiting, silence, dust and the cheek. That posture rests on three grounds about God (3:31–33).

The unit serves the book's purpose by giving the community **a voice that has been through it**, so that "we" can speak at 3:40–47 and in poem 5. `[I]` The man's testimony is the hinge between the city's grief (chs 1–2) and the community's prayer (5).

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement.** Poems 1 and 2 have done three things.

1. **They have asked God to *see*.**
   - "See, O LORD, my affliction" (רְאֵה יְהוָה אֶת־עָנְיִי, 1:9);
   - "See, O LORD, and look" (1:11; 2:20);
   - "See, O LORD, for I am in distress" (1:20).

   No seeing has been reported. `[T]`
2. **They have named God as the agent "like an enemy".**
   - "He has bent his bow like an enemy" (דָּרַךְ קַשְׁתּוֹ כְּאוֹיֵב, 2:4).
   - "The Lord has become like an enemy" (2:5).
   - The Lord "has rejected (זָנַח) his altar" (2:7) and "did not turn back (לֹא־הֵשִׁיב) his hand" (2:8). `[T]`
3. **They have commanded what no one has yet done.** "Cry out … pour out your heart like water … lift up your hands" (2:18–19). `[T]`

Poem 1 has also put Zion's own sentence on record (1:13), with the man's later wording in brackets:

- "from on high he sent fire into my bones (עַצְמֹתַי)";
- "he spread a net for my feet, he **turned me back** (הֱשִׁיבַנִי)";
- "he has **made me desolate** (נְתָנַנִי שֹׁמֵמָה), faint **all the day** (כָּל־הַיּוֹם)". `[T]`

**Necessity answer.** Chapter 3 exists here, at the book's centre, for three reasons.

- **It answers the "See!" of chs 1–2 with a witness.** The first words are "I am the man who **has seen** affliction". The request was that God see; what comes first is a human being who has seen it from inside (Headline 1).
- **It gives the city's sentence a single human voice.** Zion's 1:13 is redistributed across 3:3–11:
  - "all the day" (3:3, 14);
  - "my bones" (3:4);
  - "turns (יָשֻׁב) his hand" (3:3);
  - "he has made me desolate" (שָׂמַנִי שֹׁמֵם, 3:11).

  שֹׁמֵם ("desolate") occurs **only** at 1:13 and 3:11 in the book; the feminine of Zion becomes the masculine of the man. `[T]` for the chain (WLC lemma 8076); `[I]` for the redistribution.
- **It can do what the city could not.** A city cannot "bring back to my heart" (3:21). A man can remember, and so the book's only movement into hope happens in him. It then hands that hope to "us" (3:22 "we are not consumed"; 3:40 "let us"). `[I]`

**Implication.** Without chs 1–2, 3:22–24 is a hymn. **After them, it is the first answer the book gives to a city that has asked God to see, and has been answered so far only by the rod.** The positional answer also explains why the unit ends on God's heart (3:33). The enemy-like conduct of 2:4–8 is real, and the God who is doing it does not do it "from his heart". `[I]`

**Revisited after Tool 11 (v1.1).** The partners of the turn and the centre are laments of the downcast and the rejected: Ps 42–43 (3:20–24), Ps 77 (3:31), Ps 88 (3:7–8). The man speaks as those psalms' speakers spoke, and at 3:31 he gives the answer Ps 77 asked for. `[I]` *(v1.0's "Ps 89 used in both directions" is withdrawn: claim audit 3 finds Ps 89 in Lam 2 and Lam 5, but rates its contact at 3:22 low–moderate `[S: audit 3]`.)*

**Immediate context.** Before: Zion's accusation (2:20–22), ending "my enemy annihilated them" (כִּלָּם, root כלה — the root 3:22 denies of God's compassions). After:

- 3:34–39 continues the wisdom section: injustice the Lord "does not approve"; good and evil from the Most High.
- 3:40–42 turns to "us" and ends, "You have not pardoned".

**The unit's hope is tested nine verses after its last line.**

**Section.** The third poem, of 66 verses: a triple acrostic, each three-verse stanza beginning with the same letter. It is the book's formal centre (the overview's binding device, v0.2.1).

### 3. Structure

**Sections**, read from the text: speaker, addressee, verb-subject, setumot. `[T]`

| Verses | Letters | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3:1 | א | "I am the man who has seen affliction" | **Thesis-heading** for the whole poem: speaker, experience, cause (the rod of his wrath) |
| 3:2–16 | א–ו | **The assault** — "He" | 24 finite verbs with God as subject. Darkness (2, 6), body (4, 16), siege (5), walls and chains (7, 9), prayer shut out (8), beasts (10–11), archer (12–13), mockery (14), bitterness (15) |
| 3:17–18 | ו | **The collapse** — "my soul … I said" | Rejected from peace; "good" forgotten; "my hope from the LORD has perished". **The first divine name (3:18)** |
| 3:19–21 | ז | **The turn: memory** | "Remember" (imperative), "my soul surely remembers", "this I bring back to my heart" → "therefore I have hope" |
| 3:22–24 | ח | **The hymn** — the LORD named three times; first "you" (3:23); first "we" (3:22) | Lovingkindnesses, compassions, mornings, faithfulness, portion → "therefore I have hope in him" |
| 3:25–27 | ט | **Wisdom: "good" ×3** | Good is the LORD; good to wait; good for the man to bear the yoke |
| 3:28–30 | י | **The posture** (jussives) | Sit alone, be silent, mouth in the dust, cheek to the smiter |
| 3:31–33 | כ | **The grounds: three כִּי ("for")** | Not reject forever; though he grieves, he will have compassion; not from his heart |

**Devices.**

- **Inclusio, outer: עֳנִי ("affliction", 3:1) … עִנָּה ("he afflicts", 3:33).** Root ענה; WLC lemmas 6040 and 6031. In ch. 3 the affliction words are 3:1, 3:19 and 3:33 only. `[T]` *Confidence: high (chain); moderate–high (as a deliberate bracket — the setumah after 3:33 and the book's midpoint corroborate).*
- **Inclusio, inner: עַל־כֵּן אוֹחִיל ("therefore I have hope", 3:21, 24).** It frames the hymn, with לוֹ ("in him") added the second time. `[T]` *High.*
- **Frame: חֶסֶד–רַחֲמִים (3:22) ↔ רחם–חֶסֶד (3:32).** It brackets the hymn and the wisdom section together. These are the book's only two חֶסֶד, and the only two compassion-forms outside 4:10. `[T]` for the chain. *High (frame); low–moderate as a designed chiasm, since the pair's order varies freely elsewhere* `[S: audit 3]`.
- **Heart to heart: אֶל־לִבִּי ("to my heart", 3:21) ↔ מִלִּבּוֹ ("from his heart", 3:33).** `[T]` *Moderate–high.*
- **The man, at both ends of the wisdom:** הַגֶּבֶר ("the man", 3:1) and לַגֶּבֶר ("for the man", 3:27), closing on בְנֵי־אִישׁ ("the sons of men", 3:33). גֶּבֶר occurs in the book only in ch. 3 (3:1, 27, 35, 39). `[T]` *High.*

**How the sections relate.**

- *Testimony* (2–18), then *turn* (19–24), then *instruction* (25–30), then *ground* (31–33).
- The voice moves from "he" (2–16) to "I" (17–21), to "you" and "we" (22–23), back to "he" (25–33).
- The direct address to God lasts only three verses: **the only second-person forms in 3:1–39 are זְכָר ("remember!", 3:19) and אֱמוּנָתֶךָ ("your faithfulness", 3:23)** (WLC morphology). `[T]`
- **The man speaks *to* God only at the turn, and speaks *about* him on either side.** `[I]`

**A note on the setumot.** Leningrad prints a setumah after every third verse (3:3, 6 … 33), as BHS prints it. They mark the acrostic stanzas, and the unit's end coincides with one. *Apparatus feature; witness: Leningrad as printed in BHS; reading tradition, not authorial; manuscript spread* `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`.

### 4. Linking Words

The poem's argument runs on a few connectives, and they all cluster at the turn and the centre. `[T]`

| Verse | Hebrew | Function | Referent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3:3 | אַךְ ("surely, only") | restrictive-emphatic | "against me *only* he turns his hand" — the man as the single target |
| 3:8 | גַּם כִּי ("even when") | concessive | prayer itself is blocked |
| 3:18 | וָאֹמַר ("so I said") | consequence of 2–17 | the despair is the *conclusion* of the assault |
| 3:21 | עַל־כֵּן ("therefore") →therefore→ | inferential | hope follows from "this" brought to mind. **What "this" (זֹאת) is, is a crux** (Open Questions): forward to 3:22–23, or back to 3:20 |
| 3:22 | כִּי … כִּי ("indeed … for") | asseverative, then causal | (a) "the LORD's lovingkindnesses — **indeed** we are not consumed"; (b) **←for←** "his compassions do not fail" |
| 3:24 | עַל־כֵּן ("therefore") →therefore→ | inferential | the portion grounds the hope |
| 3:27 | כִּי ("that") | complement | "good … that he bear the yoke" |
| 3:28 | כִּי ("since, when") | causal or temporal | "since he (God) has laid it on him". The ESV's "when" is temporal |
| 3:29 | אוּלַי ("perhaps") | hedge | **the only hedge in the unit**: hope as a possibility, not a possession |
| 3:31–33 | כִּי ×3 ("for") ←for← | grounds | the three reasons for the posture of 3:28–30 |
| 3:32 | כִּי אִם ("but if, though … yet") | concessive-adversative | "though he grieves, **yet** he will have compassion" |

**The decisive finding.** **The jussives of 3:28–30 hang on the three כִּי of 3:31–33.** The man may sit, be silent and give his cheek *because* the Lord will not reject for ever, will have compassion, and does not afflict from his heart. **The posture has no ground of its own; take away 3:31–33 and 3:28–30 is resignation.** `[T]` for the syntax; `[I]` for the reading. *High.*

**ESV at 3:21** supplies "But" (Tool 8). The connective is the translator's.

### 5. Parallels

Parallelism runs through the poem. Five pairs clarify meaning. `[T]`

- **3:4** "He has worn out (בִּלָּה) my flesh and my skin // he has broken (שִׁבַּר) my bones." Synthetic, outside to inside: the assault reaches the frame.
- **3:10** "a bear lying in wait // a lion in hiding." Synonymous. **The pair is God's** (Tool 11: Ps 10:9; Hos 13:7–8).
- **3:22** "the LORD's lovingkindnesses — we are not consumed // his compassions do not fail." Synonymous on the divine side (חֶסֶד // רַחֲמִים, "lovingkindness // compassion"), synthetic on the human side: *we* are not finished **because** *they* are not. The two כִּי make the second line the ground of the first. *High.*
- **3:32** "though he grieves // yet he will have compassion according to the abundance of his lovingkindness." Antithetical. **The asymmetry is the point:** one verb of grief is answered by a verb of compassion *and* a measure, "according to the abundance".
- **3:33** "he does not afflict from his heart // or grieve the sons of men." Synonymous. **The negation and "from his heart" govern both lines**, so the grief of 3:32 is real, but not willed from the heart. `[I]`

**A deliberate break.** 3:5 (בָּנָה עָלַי וַיַּקַּף רֹאשׁ וּתְלָאָה, "he has besieged and encompassed me with gall and hardship") is the only line in 3:1–18 that the NASB95 prints as a single line. BHS flags it as possibly disturbed ("prp …", with the Greek's κεφαλήν μου, "my head"). Not load-bearing.

### 6. Narrator's Comment

**N/A.** The unit is first-person poetry in the man's voice. No narrator stands outside it (contrast 1:1–11, 17; 2:1–19).

### 7. Vocabulary

**Proper-noun inventory:**

- **Divine names:** יְהוָה (3:18, 22, 24, 25, 26) and אֲדֹנָי (3:31).
- **Place and personal names:** none. "The man" (הַגֶּבֶר) is not named.

**No canonical freight from names, but the distribution of the divine names is itself a finding** (Repetition; and gate item e4 below).

1. **עֳנִי / עִנָּה ("affliction" / "he afflicts"; root ענה), 3:1, 19, 33.** In the book, עֳנִי belongs to Zion in poem 1 (1:3, 7, 9) and passes to the man (3:1, 19). The verb returns only at 3:33 and at 5:11 ("they ravished/afflicted the women"). With רָאָה ("see") it is the exodus formula (Headline 1). `[T]` *High.*

2. **הַגֶּבֶר ("the man, the strong man").**
   - It is not the common אִישׁ ("man") or אָדָם ("human"). גֶּבֶר carries the nuance of the able-bodied adult male.
   - In Lamentations it occurs only in ch. 3 (3:1, 27, 35, 39).
   - The poem's first word for its speaker is the word for strength, applied to a man whose strength has perished (3:18 נִצְחִי, "my endurance").

   `[T]` for the distribution (WLC lemma 1397); `[I]` for the irony. *Moderate–high.*
   - **Who he is, is not stated** (Open Questions). The overview and sweep treat him as a representative voice. Nothing in 3:1–33 names Jeremiah.
   - The plural reading at 3:14, עַמִּים ("peoples"), would tilt him toward a corporate figure (Textual Variants).

3. **חַסְדֵי יְהוָה ("the LORD's lovingkindnesses", plural, 3:22) and חֶסֶד at 3:32 (ketiv singular חַסְדּוֹ, "his lovingkindness"; qere plural חֲסָדָיו, "his lovingkindnesses").**
   - These are the only two in the book.
   - The plural construct phrase חַסְדֵי יְהוָה occurs in only four verses: Isa 63:7; Ps 89:2 [Eng 89:1]; Ps 107:43; Lam 3:22 (WLC phrase search; positive control Ps 89:2).
   - חֶסֶד with אֱמוּנָה ("faithfulness") in one verse is a **Psalter pair** (11 verses, all in the Psalms; Ps 89 has 5 of them). Lam 3:22–23 splits it across two verses. `[T]` *High.*

4. **רַחֲמִים / רחם ("compassion" / "have compassion"), 3:22, 32.**
   - The same root returns once more in the book, at its most terrible: רַחֲמָנִיּוֹת ("**compassionate** women", 4:10), who boiled their children (sweep, Unit 6).
   - חֶסֶד with the *verb* רחם occurs only at Isa 54:8, 10 and Lam 3:32. With עוֹלָם ("forever") inside a one-verse window, only Isa 54:8 and Lam 3:31–32 (Tool 11). `[T]` *High.*

5. **זנח ("reject, cast off"), 3:17, 31 (and 2:7).** Of the verb's 20 verses in the Hebrew Bible, 13 are in the Psalms (10) and Lamentations (3). It is the lament verb of rejection (Headline 3). `[T]` *High.*

6. **The vocabulary of hope.** Four roots carry it in 3:18–29:
   - תּוֹחֶלֶת ("hope", 3:18 — perished);
   - יחל ("hope, wait": אוֹחִיל 3:21, 24; וְיָחִיל 3:26);
   - קוה ("wait for", 3:25);
   - תִּקְוָה ("hope", 3:29 — "perhaps").

   **The noun that perished at 3:18 is answered by the verb of the same root three verses later** (תּוֹחֶלֶת → אוֹחִיל). The only קוה elsewhere in the book is the enemies' "this is the day we **waited for**" (קִוִּינֻהוּ, 2:16). `[T]` *High.*

7. **טוֹב ("good").**
   - 3:17 נָשִׁיתִי טוֹבָה ("I have forgotten **good**");
   - 3:25, 26, 27 טוֹב ×3;
   - 3:38 "evil and good".

   **What the man had forgotten (3:17) is what the ט stanza says three times.** The stanza also uses the stock ט-word of alphabetic poetry (Tool 11). `[T]` for the chain; `[I]` for the design. *High (chain).*

8. **שֵׁבֶט עֶבְרָתוֹ ("the rod of his wrath", 3:1).**
   - עֶבְרָה ("fury") occurs in the book only at 2:2 and 3:1: the wrath of poem 2 is the rod of poem 3.
   - The exact pair occurs elsewhere only at Prov 22:8, where the rod of fury belongs to the unjust man and "will come to an end" (יִכְלֶה). That is a verse-exclusive contact with a different referent.

   `[T]` *Low–moderate as an allusion.*

**NASB95 (study) against ESV (pulpit), with the KJV as the historic witness.** Seven divergences bear on findings.

| Verse | NASB95 | ESV | Hebrew | Bearing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3:17 | "My soul has been **rejected** from peace" | "my soul is **bereft** of peace" | וַתִּזְנַח ("and … rejected") | **The ESV loses זנח, so the 3:17 → 3:31 link cannot be heard** (Headline 3). NASB95 keeps "rejected … reject" |
| 3:20 | "Surely my soul remembers / And is bowed down" | "My soul continually remembers it" | ketiv/qere and the *tiqqun* (Textual Variants) | Neither shows the crux |
| 3:21 | "This I recall to my mind" | "**But** this I call to mind" | זֹאת אָשִׁיב ("this I bring back") | **The ESV's "But" is supplied.** It creates a turn the Hebrew leaves to the reader |
| 3:22 | "The LORD's lovingkindnesses indeed **never cease**" | "The steadfast love of the LORD **never ceases**" | כִּי לֹא־תָמְנוּ ("indeed **we** are not consumed") | **Both follow the emendation.** The KJV ("It is of the LORD's mercies that **we are not consumed**") follows the Hebrew *(from memory — KJV not in the corpus; high confidence, check wording)* |
| 3:31 | "will not **reject** forever" | "will not **cast off** forever" | יִזְנַח ("he will reject") | As at 3:17 |

| 3:16 | "cower in the **dust**" | "cower in **ashes**" | בָּאֵפֶר ("in ashes") | The ESV is right. The NASB95 blurs אֵפֶר ("ashes", 3:16) with עָפָר ("dust", 3:29) |

**Also noted:**

- 3:1 "Because of" (NASB95) against "under" (ESV) for בְּ — ESV closer.
- 3:13 "inward parts" (NASB95) against "kidneys" (ESV) — ESV literal, and it keeps the Job 16:13 contact visible.
- 3:28 "Since" (NASB95) against "when" (ESV).

**Ancient versions check.** The moderns agree on three weighty renderings; the ancient versions part company with them on each.

- **3:22 "never cease".**
  - The **Old Greek lacks 3:22–24** (Swete, Rahlfs; BHS "> 𝔊*").
  - The **Syriac and Targum** read תַּמּוּ ("they are finished"), which is the moderns' basis.
  - The **Vulgate** reads *non sumus consumpti* ("we are not consumed") with the MT *(from memory; moderate–high; verify)*.
  - **Translation-tradition split:** ESV and NASB95 side with Syriac and Targum against MT, Vulgate and KJV. Category 2 — see Textual Variants.
- **3:19 "Remember" (imperative).** The Greek has Ἐμνήσθην ("**I** remembered", Swete and Rahlfs). It makes 3:19 the man's own memory, not an appeal to God. Category 2 (vocalisation of זכר); position below.
- **3:33 "afflict".** The Greek has οὐκ ἀπεκρίθη ἀπὸ καρδίας αὐτοῦ ("he did not **answer** from his heart"), reading the same consonants as ענה ("answer"). Category 2 (a different reading of the consonants, not a different *Vorlage*). MT's ענה ("afflict") is preferred: it completes the root bracket with 3:1, 19 and matches the parallel "grieve". *High.*

**Pulpit divergence note (ESV).** The ESV congregation will hear:

- **a hope that is already a turn** ("But this I call to mind");
- **a hymn without its first "we"** ("never ceases", not "we are not consumed");
- **no link between "bereft" (3:17) and "cast off" (3:31)**;
- **a chapter titled from its fourteenth stanza**.

They *will* hear "from his heart" (3:33), which the NASB95 obscures. **The preacher will need to supply what the ESV closes:** read 3:22 aloud in the KJV wording, or name the Hebrew "we", and connect "bereft" and "cast off" in his own words.

### 9. Tone and Feel

**Three registers, in sequence.**

1. **3:1–16: relentless assault.**
   - Short, verb-first lines, nearly every one with God as subject.
   - Bodily violence: flesh, skin, bones, kidneys, teeth.
   - Imagery of entrapment: walls, chains, hewn stone, crooked paths.
   - Imagery of hunting: bear, lion, archer.
   - Sensory poison: wormwood and gall.

   The soundtrack is a drum that does not stop, and the 24 verbs are the drumbeat. **There is no named God and no "you":** the man cannot yet speak *to* the one who is doing this.
2. **3:17–21: collapse and hush.**
   - The voice turns inward: "my soul … I have forgotten … I said".
   - Then the first imperative of the poem, "Remember!", and a quiet resolve: "this I bring back to my heart".
   - The pace slows to almost nothing.
3. **3:22–33: hymn, then wisdom.**
   - 3:22–24 is the only lyrical moment: mornings, newness, "you", "my portion".
   - Then the register cools at once into proverb ("good … good … good"), into jussive counsel ("let him sit, let him be silent"), and into measured theology (three "for"s).
   - **The cooling is deliberate:** the hymn is not allowed to become euphoria. `[I]`

**The single word "perhaps" (3:29)** sets the temperature of the whole second half: hope held with an open hand. **Do not preach this unit triumphantly**, and do not preach its first half as though it were a prologue to be hurried through. `[I]`

**Ps 143:3 → Lam 3:6** *(high — verbatim clause, order of the first two words reversed)* `[T]` `[S: audit]`

> Ps 143:3 הוֹשִׁיבַנִי בְמַחֲשַׁכִּים כְּמֵתֵי עוֹלָם ("he has made me dwell in dark places, like those who have long been dead")
> Lam 3:6 בְּמַחֲשַׁכִּים הוֹשִׁיבַנִי כְּמֵתֵי עוֹלָם

*Move 1 — source context.*

- Ps 143 is a Davidic plea of a servant pursued: "the **enemy** has pursued my soul; he has crushed my life to the ground" (143:3). The subject of "made me dwell" is **the enemy**.
- The psalm's turn follows at once (143:5): "I **remember** (זָכַרְתִּי) the days of old; I meditate on all your doings; I **muse** (אֲשׂוֹחֵחַ) on the work of your hands".
- Then 143:7–8: "do not hide your face … let me hear **in the morning** of your lovingkindness (חַסְדֶּךָ)".

*Move 2 — book usage.* No earlier use of Ps 143 in Lamentations. The verb דכא ("crush", Ps 143:3) appears at Lam 3:34 (לְדַכֵּא), the first line after this unit. `[T]`

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* מַחֲשַׁכִּים ("dark places") occurs in 7 verses, among them Ps 88:7, 19 [Eng 88:6, 18], Ps 143:3 and Lam 3:6. Ps 88 is the other "dark places" psalm, and it is a partner of this unit at 3:8 and 3:17 (below). **Pss 88 and 143 are already a pair:** both are laments of the one made to dwell in darkness like the dead. `[I]`

*What it adds.* Three things.

1. **The subject has changed.** In Ps 143, the enemy does this; in Lam 3:6, "He" does — the God of 2:4–5 "like an enemy" (Headline 2).
2. **The psalm's own sequence runs dark place → remember → morning → חֶסֶד, and Lam 3 runs the same sequence:** dark place (3:6) → remember (3:19–20) → mornings (3:23) → חֶסֶד (3:22). `[I]`
3. *Synthetic, moderate:* the source carries its own turn into Lam 3. Whether by design or by shared idiom, it cannot be shown from the text.

*Move 1 — source context.*

*Move 2 — book usage.* Job is a live source at verse level:

**Ps 119:57 (with 119:49–57) → Lam 3:24 (with 3:19–24)** *(high — חֶלְקִי יְהוָה, "the LORD is my portion", exclusive phrase, 2 verses)* `[T]` `[S: audit]`

*Move 1 — source context.* Ps 119 is the great alphabetic psalm, eight lines to a letter.

| Ps 119 stanza | Content | Lam 3 stanza |
|---|---|---|
| ז (Zayin), 119:49–56 | "**Remember** (זְכֹר) the word to your servant, on which you have **made me hope** (יִחַלְתָּנִי)"; "**This** (זֹאת) is my comfort in my **affliction** (בְעָנְיִי)" (119:50) | ז, 3:19–21: "**Remember** my **affliction** … **this** I bring back to my heart … therefore I **hope**" |
| ח (Heth), 119:57–64 | "**The LORD is my portion** (חֶלְקִי יְהוָה); I have said (אָמַרְתִּי) …" (119:57); "the earth is full of your חֶסֶד" (119:64) | ח, 3:22–24: "**the LORD is my portion**, says my soul" |

*Move 2 — book usage.* No earlier Ps 119 contact in Lamentations. `[T]`

*Move 3 — OT-to-OT.* **The alphabet constrains the vocabulary.** זכר ("remember") is a natural ז-word, and חֶסֶד ("lovingkindness") and חֵלֶק ("portion") natural ח-words. So a shared word in the *same letter-slot* of two acrostics is partly the alphabet's doing.

A **baseline** was run. For each letter, the content lemmas of Lam 3's three-verse stanza were compared with Ps 119's eight-verse stanza at the same letter (WLC, function words excluded):

| Comparison | Mean shared lemmas | Spread |
|---|---|---|
| Same letter | **1.27** | sd 1.42 |
| Different letters (462 pairs) | **0.74** | sd 0.86 |

- **Highest same-letter overlaps:** ר (5: 3:58–60 ↔ 119:153–160), ק (4), **ז (3: זכר "remember", יחל "hope", עֳנִי "affliction")**.
- **In ז only one of the three is forced by the letter.** יחל and עֳנִי are not.

`[T]` for the counts; `[I]` for the reading.

**v1.1 — the alphabet test, corrected** `[S: audit 3]`. Once each line's forced first word is removed, the same-letter excess vanishes (0.68 against 0.60). What survives is **a set of exact phrases found only in these two poems, each at the same letter of both alphabets:**

| Letter | Ps 119 | Lam 3 | Phrase |
|---|---|---|---|
| ח | 119:57 | **3:24** | חֶלְקִי יְהוָה ("my portion is the LORD") |
| פ | 119:136 | **3:48** | פַּלְגֵי־מַיִם … עֵינִי ("streams of water … my eye runs down") |
| ר | 119:154 | **3:58** | רִיב ("plead my cause") + גאל ("redeem"; 4 verses) |

Each phrase begins with its line's forced first word, but what follows is not forced. **The two acrostics are related through exact phrases, not through a ז–ח "slot" correspondence.** *Moderate–high.*

*What it adds.*

- **The turn of Lam 3 shares phrases with Israel's greatest alphabetic prayer** at ח (3:24); the relation runs on at פ and ר (3:48, 58).
- Ps 25:6–8 (also ז–ח–ט) and Ps 145:8–9 (ח–ט: the Exod 34:6 formula, then "the LORD is good … his compassions") show the same stock at the same letters.
- **Direction is open.** The same-letter overlap runs through the whole of Lam 3, not only at the turn.

*Confidence:* high (3:24 ↔ 119:57); moderate–high (the relation of the two acrostics through exact phrases); the ז–ח slot correspondence alone is withdrawn `[S: audit 3]`.

**Addressee check.** Poems 1–2 name the prophets, priests, elders, virgins, children and enemies. Of these:

- **3:28–29 resolves one: the elders' posture of 2:10.**
- No other addressee is resolved here; the prophets return at 4:13. `[T]`/`[I]`

**What the list shows.** **3:1–21 answers poems 1–2 (Zion's sentence, the bow, the hand, the rejection); 3:22–33 plants the end of the book (תמם → 4:22; חָדָשׁ → 5:21; עוֹלָם → 5:19–22).** The unit is the book's hinge in both directions. *Synthetic; each chain verified; moderate–high.*

### 12. Genre

**Hebrew poetry: an alphabetic lament poem**, three verses to each letter, in the first person singular. The reading rules of lament apply: **it gives permission to grieve and does not resolve too fast.**

Two features belong to this poem.

1. **The alphabet constrains the vocabulary** (טוֹב at ט; זכר at ז; חֶסֶד, חֵלֶק at ח). Shared words in the same letter-slot of other acrostics are therefore partly convention (Tool 11 baseline). **Read the hymn and the wisdom as an alphabet's stock words given new weight, not as quotations.** `[I]`
2. **The poem mixes registers inside the lament:** testimony (2–18), petition (19), hymn (22–24), proverb (25–27), counsel (28–30), theology (31–33). **The genre is not "wisdom poem" because 3:25–39 sounds like Proverbs:** the wisdom is spoken from inside the lament, by the man of 3:1. `[I]`

No Gunkel–Mowinckel *Gattung* label is used.

### 13. Copycat

The man is a *voice*, not a narrative character, but his counsel (3:26–30) is framed as prescription ("it is good … let him …").

- **Normative pattern, bounded by its grounds:** waiting for the LORD's salvation (3:25–26). The rest of Scripture commends it (Ps 37:7; 62:2, 6; Isa 30:15; 40:31).
- **Posture before God's discipline, not a rule for human relationships:** "let him give his cheek to the smiter" (3:30). The smiter in context is the one who struck in 3:1–16 — God. **It is not a template for submitting to human violence** (Difficult Verses; Pitfall 3).
- **Descriptive:** "the yoke in his youth" (3:27). It describes a formative discipline, not a command to seek suffering.

### 14. Bible Timeline

- **Where the passage sits:** after the fall of Jerusalem (587/586 BC by near-certain inference; the book names no Babylon), in the time of exile and ruin. *`[I]` for the date.*
- **Where the reader sits:** this side of the cross. The "rod of his wrath" (3:1) has fallen on the Son (Isa 53:5; Rom 3:25–26; 5:9) `[S]`. "Not for ever" (3:31) has been answered by the resurrection and awaits the new creation (Rev 21:4–5, "I make all things new").
- **How that reframes it:** the Christian still hears 3:1–18 as true of present suffering, which is not all punishment (Heb 12:5–11 places discipline inside sonship). But **the ground of 3:31–33 is now historical, not only confessional**: the compassion that "does not fail" has a cross and an empty tomb.
- **Canonical position:** **Ketuvim**, the Megilloth, between Ecclesiastes and Esther (BHS order). The canonical reader of the Writings has the Psalter in the ear — Pss 44, 74, 77, 88, 89, 119, 143 — and Job. The canonical register explains why Lam 3's partners are overwhelmingly in the Psalms and Job (Tool 11). `[I]`
- **Canonical against compositional order:** whether Lam 3 answers Pss 44, 77 and 88, or shares their liturgy, is **open**. The direction of dependence is not resolved here.

### 15. Who Am I?

**The man (הַגֶּבֶר)** has three functions.

- **Representative of the people:**
  - He speaks Zion's sentence (1:13 → 3:11).
  - He moves to "we" (3:22, MT; 3:40–47).
  - He is mocked as Zion is (3:14 ↔ 2:15–16; on the reading עַמִּים, by "the peoples").
- **Example, in a bounded sense:** his waiting and his memory are commended (3:25–26). But his confession (3:42) is ours, not his alone.
- **Type of Christ, by contrast as much as likeness:**
  - Like Christ: under the rod of God's wrath (3:1); mocked "all the day" (3:14); giving his cheek (3:30; Isa 50:6); in the dark among the dead (3:6).
  - Unlike Christ: he confesses transgression (3:42), and his suffering is for sins he shares (Christological Reading).

**We are, first, the people he speaks for**, and the "we" of 3:22 and 3:40. **We are not the man as hero.** The preacher should not invite the congregation to be "the man who turned his memory", as though the turn were his achievement (Pitfall 2).

**God** is both the "He" of the assault and the LORD of the hymn. **The passage will not let the congregation split him into two.** `[T]`

### 16. So What?

**Stage 1 — the author's intended response.** To keep speaking to and about the LORD while his hand is against us. To do so by remembering — not by forgetting the affliction, but by bringing to mind, inside it, his חֶסֶד, his compassions and his faithfulness. And to wait for his salvation in silence and hope, because he will not reject for ever and does not afflict from his heart. `[I]`

**Stage 2.**

| Domain | Application |
|---|---|
| **Worldview** | God may be the one whose hand is against me, and still be the God whose compassions do not fail. **The passage holds 3:1–16 and 3:22–33 together; so must I** |
| **Stop** | Requiring the darkness to make sense before I will speak to God. Speaking of him only in the third person. Reaching for 3:22–23 to skip 3:1–18 |
| **Start** | Remembering on purpose (3:21 "this I bring back to my heart"). Naming the LORD's חֶסֶד in the plural, as "lovingkindnesses" — specific, countable. Waiting silently for his salvation |
| **Motivation** | Not resilience, but three facts about God (3:31–33). **The posture of 3:28–30 has no ground of its own** |

**Four audiences:**

- **For me:** have I preached 3:22–23 to others and never sat in 3:1–18 myself?
- **For a Christian friend:** the one whose prayer is "shut out" (3:8) needs to hear that the man who said so is the man who said "Great is your faithfulness".
- **For the church:** the "we" of 3:22. Hope is spoken in the plural before 3:40 asks us to examine our ways.
- **For an unbeliever:** the God of the Bible does not hide that his hand can be against us. He also says it is not from his heart, and the cross is where that is shown.

**Prayer.**

- "Remember my affliction" (3:19).
- Thanks for compassions new this morning.
- Confession that we forget good (3:17).
- Help to wait in silence for your salvation.

**Grammatical and syntactical features:**

- **3:20 זָכוֹר תִּזְכּוֹר** — infinitive absolute + imperfect. One of the book's six doubled-verb figures; its only partner is Jer 31:20 (זָכֹר אֶזְכְּרֶנּוּ, "I will surely remember him", of God remembering Ephraim) `[S: audit]`.
  - **Parsed as third feminine singular** (soul as subject), it is "my soul surely remembers".
  - **Parsed as second masculine singular** (as the tiqqun tradition implies), it is "**you** will surely remember" — God's remembering, as in Jer 31:20.

  *Moderate; Open Questions.*
- **3:22 כִּי … כִּי.** The first כִּי is best taken as asseverative ("indeed"), the second as causal. The NASB95 "indeed … for" catches it; the ESV drops both. *Moderate–high.*
- **3:28–30.** Jussive (יֵשֵׁב, "let him sit"; וְיִדֹּם, "and let him be silent", WLC morphology) and imperfect with jussive force (יִתֵּן, "let him give"; יִשְׂבַּע, "let him be filled"). These are wisdom counsels, not narrative. *High.*
- **3:32 כִּי אִם** — "but if / though … then". The perfects הוֹגָה … וְרִחַם read as a general truth: "though he has caused grief, he has (also) had compassion". Or with a future sense (NASB95 "will have compassion"). *Moderate.*
- **3:10 אריה** (ketiv) / אֲרִי (qere), "lion". BHS: many manuscripts as qere. No bearing.

**Wordplay and sound.**

- **3:21 / 3:24.** עַל־כֵּן אוֹחִיל — *ʿal-kēn ʾôḥîl* — the refrain is heard twice; the second adds לוֹ (*lô*, "in him").
- **3:23 חֲדָשִׁים לַבְּקָרִים** — *ḥădāšîm labbəqārîm*, "new by the mornings". The plural "mornings" makes the newness repeated, not single.
- **3:17 נָשִׁיתִי טוֹבָה** ("I have forgotten good") and **3:25 טוֹב יְהוָה** ("good is the LORD"): the ט stanza's three טוֹב answer the forgotten good. **This is a lexical recurrence, not a pun.**

**Recommended verification.**

- A lexicon (HALOT, DCH) on זנח with נֶפֶשׁ and the ambiguity of וַתִּזְנַח at 3:17.
- The *tiqqun* at 3:20 in the Masoretic lists.

### Textual Variants

Each entry is classified under the Three-Way Triage. This section carries **category 2** only; category 1 losses are under Tools 7–8.

**3:22 תָּמְנוּ ("we are not consumed") against תַּמּוּ ("they are not finished")**

- **The issue:** MT has a first-person plural perfect of תמם ("be complete"). The emendation reads a third-person plural ("[the lovingkindnesses] are not finished"), which makes the line synonymous with "his compassions do not fail".
- **Evidence:**
  - For תַּמּוּ: one Hebrew manuscript, the Syriac and the Targum (BHS: "l c Ms 𝔖𝔗 תַּמּוּ").
  - For the MT: the rest of the Hebrew tradition; the Vulgate (*non sumus consumpti* — from memory, verify).
  - The Old Greek lacks the verse. BHS records a hexaplaric/Lucianic plus, ἐξέλειπεν με ("it failed me").
- **Weighing:**
  - The emendation smooths the parallelism and removes the only "we" in 3:1–39 — the easier reading.
  - The MT is the harder reading. It has a thematic partner in 4:22 (תַּם עֲוֹנֵךְ, "your punishment is completed"; the only other תמם in the book).
- **Position:** **MT preferred.** *Confidence: moderate–high.*
- **Preaching implication:** name the Hebrew "we", or read the KJV. This is the strongest case in the unit for telling a congregation that their Bible has followed a different reading.

**3:20 ותשיח (ketiv) / וְתָשׁוֹחַ (qere), and the *tiqqun soferim***

- **The issue:**
  - The consonants ותשיח can be read as שׂיח ("muse, complain"). The Greek καταδολεσχήσει ("will muse/babble") read it so — the same Greek verb as Ps 76:4, 7 LXX [MT 77:4, 7].
  - Or as שׁוח, pointed by the qere as וְתָשׁוֹחַ ("and is bowed down").
  - BHS also records a **tiqqun soferim** ("Tiq soph ךָ—ֶ"). The Masoretic tradition says the scribes altered an original נַפְשֶׁךָ ("**your** soul") to נַפְשִׁי ("my soul"). On that tradition the verse read "**You** will surely remember, and **your** soul will bow down over me". In other words, **God's remembering and stooping**.
- **Evidence:** BHS apparatus — many manuscripts as the qere. Lam 3:20 is among the classical lists of the tiqqunim `[S]`. Spread `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`.
- **Weighing:**
  - The tiqqun tradition is a claim about scribal reverence, not a manuscript reading. No extant Hebrew witness reads "your soul".
  - It shows that early readers heard 3:19–20 as addressed to God — "Remember … You will surely remember" — and found the image of God's soul "bowing down" too bold.
- **Position:** the MT (qere) stands as text. The tiqqun and the ketiv are reported as reception. *Confidence: moderate.*
- **Preaching implication:** the preacher may say that ancient readers heard here God himself remembering and stooping. He should not build on it.

**3:14 עַמִּי ("my people") against עַמִּים ("peoples")**

- **Evidence:** a Sebir note (a Masoretic "one would expect"), many manuscripts and the Syriac read עַמִּים (BHS "l c Seb mlt Mss 𝔖").
- **Weighing:** עַמִּים would make the man mocked by the nations, as Zion is (2:15–16; 3:45 "among the peoples"). That supports a corporate reading of the man. עַמִּי ("my people") matches Jer 20:7's private mockery.
- **Position:** undecided; the MT is retained. *Confidence: uncertain.* It bears on the identity question (Open Questions).

**3:32 חסדו (ketiv, singular) / חֲסָדָיו (qere, plural)**

- **Evidence:** the ketiv is read by the Oriental tradition, the Greek and the Targum; the qere by the Occidental tradition and the Vulgate (BHS).
- **Bearing:** the qere makes 3:32 a perfect chiastic answer to 3:22's plural חַסְדֵי. The same ketiv/qere occurs at Ps 106:45 (כְּרֹב חסדו). *Low.*

**3:31 אֲדֹנָי ("Lord") against יהוה ("LORD")**

- **Evidence:** BHS "cf 1,14c": many manuscripts read יהוה for אֲדֹנָי (also at 3:36, 37, 58).
- **Bearing:** the overview's and sweep's "Adonai first at 3:31" is **a Leningrad fact**. The unnamed-He finding (3:1–17) is unaffected.
- **Apparatus feature** `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`; capped at moderate.

**Old Greek omissions: 3:22–24, and 3:29 in some manuscripts** (Swete, Rahlfs; BHS)

- **Category 2.** The ח stanza is required by the acrostic, so its absence in Greek is a loss in transmission or translation, not evidence of a shorter Hebrew. *High.*
- In Vaticanus (Swete) the letter heading Ἥθ (Heth) stands over 3:25, "the Lord is good". **The Greek reader has no hymn at all**, and the ט stanza is labelled ח.

**3:19 MT זְכָר ("remember!") against the Greek Ἐμνήσθην ("I remembered")**

- **The issue:** a different vocalisation of זכר.
- **Position:** the MT imperative is preferred. It is the poem's first address to God, and it parallels 5:1 זְכֹר יְהוָה ("Remember, O LORD"). *Moderate–high.*

**3:33 MT עִנָּה ("afflict") against the Greek "answer"** — see Tool 8. MT preferred. *High.*

**3:17 MT וַתִּזְנַח (ambiguous) against the Greek ἀπώσατο ("he cast off")**

- BHS: the Greek reads וַיִּזְנַח ("and he rejected"); the Vulgate and Syriac have a passive.
- The Greek makes God the subject explicitly, and keeps the 3:17 → 3:31 link (ἀπώσατο … ἀπώσεται). *Category 2, low stakes.*

### Historical and Cultural Background

**Setting.**

- **Time:** after the fall of Jerusalem. *`[I]` for the date.*
- **Place:** a ruined city and a scattered people (1:3; 4:15; 5:2).
- **Political context:** Babylonian conquest and occupation (5:8, "slaves rule over us"). Not named in the book.

**What the original audience knew:**

- **Siege realities behind the imagery.** "He has besieged (בָּנָה עָלַי, 'built against me') and encompassed me" (3:5) is siege language: earthworks built against a city (2 Kgs 25:1; Ezek 4:2). "Walled me in" (3:7, 9) and "made my chain heavy" (3:7, נְחָשְׁתִּי, "my bronze") echo captivity: Zedekiah was bound in bronze fetters (2 Kgs 25:7). `[I]`
- **The Psalter's laments** (44, 74, 77, 88, 89) were known, sung prayers of a people or a king under rejection. To hear their words in Lam 3 is to hear familiar prayers answered. `[I]`
- **The rod as discipline.** שֵׁבֶט ("rod") is the father's and the king's rod of correction (2 Sam 7:14, of David's son: "I will discipline him with the rod of men"; Prov 13:24; 22:15). **The Davidic promise said the son would be chastened with the rod "but my חֶסֶד shall not depart from him" (2 Sam 7:14–15; Ps 89:31–34).** The man under the rod who then confesses חֶסֶד (3:1, 22) walks inside that promise. `[I]` *Moderate.*
- **Morning** as the time of deliverance (Ps 30:6; 46:6; 90:14; 143:8) and of judgement (Zeph 3:5). "New every morning" sits in a known idiom. `[I]`

**What this changes.** The assault is not abstract suffering. It is the siege, the chains and the exile, told as one man's body. And **the hope is not generic optimism. It is the Davidic and covenant "rod … but חֶסֶד" pattern, confessed after the rod has fallen.** `[I]`

### Original Audience Reception

**Canonical audience.**

- **Section:** Ketuvim. The reader arrives exilic or post-exilic, with the Psalter, Job and Proverbs in the ear, and knowing that the promises are unresolved.
- **What they know:** the laments that ask "will he reject for ever?" (Pss 44, 74, 77), and the Davidic psalm that asks "where are your lovingkindnesses?" (Ps 89:50).
- **Register:** Lam 3 answers those questions inside their own canonical neighbourhood.

**The first hearers.**

- **Who:** survivors in the land and in exile.
- **Their situation:** ruin, hunger, occupation, and the silence of God.
- **What they brought:** the expectation of Zion's inviolability (4:12), and the prophets' "peace" (2:14).

**Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances.**

- **Shocking:** God described with the psalms' words for the wicked and the enemy — the lurking lion (3:10), the archer (3:12), the one who makes you dwell with the dead (3:6).
- **Surprising:** that after 3:1–18 the same speaker says "Great is your faithfulness".
- **Comforting:** "we are not consumed" (MT 3:22); "he will not reject for ever" (3:31); "not from his heart" (3:33).
- **Disturbing:** "let him give his cheek to the smiter" (3:30) — the posture of a man who has been struck by God; and "perhaps" (3:29).

**What we bring that they didn't.**

- A therapeutic instinct that hears 3:22–23 as positive thinking.
- A privatised faith that hears "I" and misses the "we" of 3:22.
- A cultural reflex to separate the God who comforts from the God who chastens.

**Candidate Fallen Condition Focus.**

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **What they felt** | That God's hand was against them "all the day" and their prayers were shut out (3:3, 8). That their hope from the LORD had perished (3:18). `[T]` |

### Biblical-Theological Themes

**(1) "Not for ever": God's חֶסֶד and the limit of his anger**

- **Where this passage sits:** the confession of the Exod 34:6–7 God — compassionate, abounding in חֶסֶד, who does not leave the guilty unpunished — after the punishment has fallen.
- **What comes before:**
  - Exod 34:6–7;
  - Ps 30:6 [Eng 30:5] ("his anger is but for a moment");
  - Ps 103:8–9 ("he will not always chide, nor keep his anger for ever");
  - the "reject for ever?" laments (Pss 44, 74, 77);
  - the Davidic "rod … but חֶסֶד" (2 Sam 7:14–15; Ps 89:31–34).
- **What comes after:**
  - Isa 54:7–8 ("for a brief moment … with everlasting חֶסֶד");
  - Mic 7:18–19 ("he does not retain his anger for ever, because he delights in חֶסֶד");
  - in Christ, wrath borne and finished (Rom 5:9; 1 Thess 5:9);
  - "neither death nor life … shall separate us" (Rom 8:35–39).
- **What it contributes:** 3:31–33 gives the theology its most intimate form — **not from his heart**. It is the OT's clearest statement that judgement is God's "strange work" (cf. Isa 28:21), real but not his delight. *Confidence: high.*

**(2) The righteous sufferer and the man under the rod**

- **Where this passage sits:** the voice of Job, Jeremiah and the lament psalms, gathered into one man who also confesses (3:42).
- **What comes before:** Job 16; 19; 30; Jer 20; Pss 22, 88, 143.
- **What comes after:**
  - the Servant who gives his back to the smiters and his cheeks to those who pluck the beard (Isa 50:6);
  - Christ mocked and struck (Matt 26:67; 27:39–44);
  - "he committed no sin … when he suffered, he did not threaten, but continued entrusting himself to him who judges justly" (1 Pet 2:22–23).
- **What it contributes:** **the man of Lam 3 is the pattern's turning-point.** He bears the rod as a sinner among sinners, and so marks out, by contrast, the one who bore it without sin. *Confidence: moderate–high.*

**Confluence: climactic convergence.** The sufferer under the rod (theme 2) is where "not for ever" (theme 1) is spoken. In Christ the two meet in one person. The one who bore the rod of wrath is the one in whom God's compassion does not fail. The cross is the place where "he does not afflict from his heart" is shown to be true at the cost of his own Son. `[I]`

### Christological Reading

**Type of connection: trajectory, plus typology with contrast.**

**How the passage points to Christ.**

- **The man under the rod of his wrath (3:1).** The four typology tests:

  | Test | Result |
  |---|---|
  | Category | Passes: the suffering righteous representative; covenant discipline |
  | NT precedent | Partial: of the same *kind*, not this text. The NT applies the suffering-righteous psalms (22, 69) and Isa 50, 53 to Christ, but never quotes Lam 3 |
  | Escalation | Passes: Christ bears wrath not his own, and finishes it |
  | Authorial pattern | Partial: the poem's shift from "I" to "we" invites a representative reading |

  **Moderate typology.**
- **The cheek to the smiter (3:30).** It shares wording with Isa 50:6 (exclusive) `[S: audit]`. The Gospels' striking of Jesus (Matt 26:67; John 18:22, ῥάπισμα, "a blow") uses Isaiah's word, not Lamentations'. The posture is shared; the dependence runs through Isaiah. *Moderate.*
- **Mocked "all the day" (3:14).** Christ mocked by "his own" (John 1:11; Matt 27:39–44). *Moderate (trajectory).*
- **"Not for ever … he will have compassion" (3:31–32).** The resurrection is God's "not for ever" over his Son (Acts 2:24–27; Ps 16:10). *Trajectory; moderate.*
- **"New every morning" (3:23).** The resurrection morning (Mark 16:2); "the steadfast love" made flesh. *Trajectory, illustrative; low–moderate.*

**Trajectory.**

- **What the passage anticipates:** a compassion that outlasts wrath; a "we" not consumed; salvation for which one may wait in silence (3:26).
- **What Christ fulfils:** he bears the rod (Isa 53:5), is raised (the "not for ever"), and gives the Spirit by whom the church says "Abba" in affliction (Rom 8:15–18).
- **What remains:** the waiting of 3:26 is still the church's posture until all things are new (Rom 8:23–25).

**Contrast — essential.** The man confesses: "**we** have transgressed and rebelled" (3:42). Christ has no sin to confess. **The man is a type of the people for whom Christ suffers before he is a type of Christ.** A sermon that makes "the man" simply Jesus erases 3:42. `[I]`

**Moralism check.**

- **The "be like X" temptation:** "Be like the man — choose to remember, choose hope." That makes the turn a feat of the will.
- **Gospel grounding:** the turn rests on God's חֶסֶד and compassions (3:22) and on three facts about God (3:31–33). The posture of 3:28–30 has no ground of its own.
- **Christ as hero:** the LORD whose compassions do not fail, and the Son in whom that is shown — not the sufferer's capacity to remember.

**Confidence:** high on the trajectory; moderate on the typology; high on the contrast.

### Difficult / Contested Verses

**3:1–16 — God as assailant**

- **Category:** theological, pastoral, apologetic.
- **The difficulty:** God is described as bear, lion and archer, using words the Psalms keep for the wicked.
- **Common mishandling:**
  - softening it ("it only felt like God");
  - reassigning the agency to Babylon (overview trap 3).
- **Rhetorical function:** the testimony that makes the turn costly. 3:32–33 does not retract it: "**though he causes grief**".
- **Honest exegesis:** God is the agent (24 verbs); the poem is said *to* God and *about* God in faith (3:19, 23). Lament is permitted speech.
- **Handling:** acknowledge it plainly; let the text's own resolution (3:31–33) carry the tension rather than explaining it away.

**3:27–30 — "Let him give his cheek to the smiter"**

- **Category:** pastoral landmine (abuse).
- **The difficulty:** these verses can be — and have been — used to counsel victims of abuse to stay and submit.
- **Rhetorical function:**
  - The verses are counsel for a man under **God's** discipline (the smiter of 3:1–16 is God; "since he has laid it on him", 3:28).
  - They are grounded in hope (3:29 "perhaps") and in the three facts of 3:31–33.
  - They are immediately followed by 3:34–36, which declares that the Lord "does not approve" of crushing prisoners, denying justice or subverting a man in his cause. **The next stanza condemns human injustice.**
- **Honest exegesis:** these verses describe a posture before God, not a rule for human relationships. They do not commend submission to human violence, and 3:34–36 explicitly rejects it.
- **Handling:** **address it explicitly** when preaching 3:25–33; read through to 3:36.

**3:33 — "He does not afflict from his heart" against 3:37–38 ("from the mouth of the Most High … both evil and good")**

- **Category:** doctrinal (divine sovereignty and suffering).
- **The difficulty:** if God does not afflict "from his heart", does he afflict at all?
- **Main options:**
  1. **God's will has layers** — what he decrees is not always what he delights in (cf. Ezek 18:23, 32; 33:11).
  2. **"From his heart" means "willingly, gladly"** — the NASB95 sense.
- **Honest exegesis:** the text holds both 3:32 ("though he causes grief") and 3:33 ("not from his heart"). It does not choose between sovereignty and sorrow.
- **Handling:** name both; resist resolving it into either "God didn't do it" or "God enjoys it".

**3:8 — "He shuts out my prayer"**

- **Category:** pastoral (unanswered prayer, depression).
- **Handling:** the man who says this is the man who prays 3:19 and is heard at 3:55–57. **The sentence is part of the prayer, not its end.**

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Preaching 3:22–24 without 3:1–18 ("Great is thy faithfulness")

- **What it looks like:** a sermon, funeral reading or care-home devotion built on 3:22–23 alone, often under the ESV heading.
- **Why it's wrong:**
  - The hymn's words are the ones the assault had erased — hope (3:18 → 3:21), good (3:17 → 3:25), "rejected" (3:17 → 3:31).
  - It is introduced by "remember my affliction … the wormwood and the gall" (3:19).
  - Without 3:1–18 the hymn is a greeting card; with it, it is a confession made in the dark.
- **The corrective:** read at least 3:17–24 aloud. Even in a short devotion, let the hearers hear 3:18 before 3:22.

### Pitfall 2: "Be like the man — choose hope"

- **What it looks like:** "When life is hard, do what Jeremiah did: decide to remember the good."
- **Why it's wrong:**
  - It makes the turn the sufferer's achievement.
  - The text grounds the hope in God's חֶסֶד, compassions and faithfulness (3:22–23), and the posture in three facts about God (3:31–33).
  - It also imports Jeremiah (Pitfall 4).
- **The corrective:** preach the grounds, not the technique. "This I bring back to my heart" is the act; what is brought back is God.

### Pitfall 3: 3:27–30 as counsel to submit to abuse

- **What it looks like:** "Turn the other cheek; bear your yoke; sit in silence" — said to someone suffering at human hands.
- **Why it's wrong:** the smiter is God (3:1–16; "he has laid it on him", 3:28). The next stanza (3:34–36) says the Lord does not approve of crushing prisoners, denying justice or subverting a man's cause.
- **The corrective:** name the misuse from the pulpit. Read to 3:36.

### Pitfall 4: Reading the poem as Jeremiah's diary

- **What it looks like:**
  - "Jeremiah in the cistern" for 3:6 or 3:53–55.
  - Building on 3:12's מַטָּרָא ("target") as if it were Jeremiah's "court of the guard" (מַטָּרָה, Jer 32–39) — a homonym grouped under one lemma in the index.
- **Why it's wrong:**
  - The poem never names its speaker.
  - Its wording is shared with Job, the Psalms and Jer 20 alike.
  - Jeremiah's cistern had no water (Jer 38:6); the man's pit has (3:54).
  - "Target" in Job 16:12 and 1 Sam 20:20 shows the ordinary sense.
- **The corrective:** "the man" is a representative voice. Jer 20:7 supplies a line, not an identity (Tool 11).

### Pitfall 5: "God didn't really do it" (or "God enjoys it")

- **What it looks like:** softening 3:1–16 by appeal to 3:33, or hardening 3:33 by appeal to 3:37–38.
- **Why it's wrong:** the text says both "though he causes grief" (3:32) and "not from his heart" (3:33).
- **The corrective:** preach the tension as the text holds it — the comfort lies in the second clause, not in deleting the first.

### Pitfall 6: Christologising too fast

- **What it looks like:** "This is Jesus speaking from the cross."
- **Why it's wrong:** the man confesses sin (3:42), and the NT never quotes this chapter.
- **The corrective:** reach Christ through the rod of wrath borne, the cheek given (via Isa 50:6), and the "not for ever" of the resurrection. Keep the contrast.

## Open Questions / Uncertainties

1. **3:20 — whose soul, and whose remembering?** The qere ("my soul … bowed down") against the ketiv-as-שׂיח ("my soul muses", the Greek), and the *tiqqun* tradition ("you will surely remember, and your soul will bow down"). The parse of תִּזְכּוֹר (3rd feminine singular or 2nd masculine singular) decides between the man's memory and God's. A Logos pass is needed on the tiqqunim lists and on the major commentaries.
2. **3:21 זֹאת ("this") — forward or back?** Forward, to 3:22–23 (the content recalled)? Or back, to 3:20 (the soul's remembering itself)? The ESV's "But" assumes forward.
3. **3:22 MT against the emendation** — MT preferred here (Textual Variants). The Vulgate's wording needs checking.
4. **3:17 וַתִּזְנַח** — "it was rejected" or "**you** rejected"? Ps 88:15 favours the second person. A lexicon pass is wanted.
5. **3:14 עַמִּי / עַמִּים, and who "the man" is** — individual, representative, king or personified people. The variant tilts the question; the text does not settle it.
6. **Adonai and YHWH spread at 3:31** — many manuscripts read יהוה `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`.
7. **Direction of dependence** with Pss 42–43, 77, 88, 119, 143, Job 16 and 19, Jer 20 and Isa 54: open throughout.
8. *(v1.1: partly answered by claim audit 3 — the ר contact is an exact phrase; see Tool 11.)* **Ps 119's ר-stanza (119:153–160) against Lam 3:58–60** — the highest same-letter overlap (5 lemmas: ראה "see", ריב "plead", גאל "redeem", מִשְׁפָּט "justice"). It belongs to the next dig (3:34–66).
9. **Closed by the corpus this run:**
   - Ps 73 as a partner of 3:22–24 was retired by audit 2. This dig confirms that Ps 119:49–57 leads, and finds the ז-stanza contact (זכר + יחל + עֳנִי).
