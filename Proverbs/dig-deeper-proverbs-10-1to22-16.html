Dig Deeper: Proverbs 10:1–22:16 — Solomon I entire

Translation: ESV (Anglicised) · Hebrew: BHS · Greek: Rahlfs — all three supplied verbatim from Logos, 26 August 2026 Date: 26 August 2026 · Mode: Fresh Exegesis at whole-section scope Book-overview context: In-conversation (v1.2) · Series context: Sermon 5 of 9, preached whole

What this run is, and what it does not replace

Two full solo runs already cover this material — dig-deeper-proverbs-10-1to15-33 and dig-deeper-proverbs-16-1to22-16. This run consumes them; it does not re-derive them. Verse-level exegesis, the cluster maps, the four-way antithesis classification and the individual tool-work all remain in those two reports and remain authoritative for their own halves.

What this run adds is everything that is only visible at 375-verse scale, plus one thing neither earlier run had: the Greek. Solomon I has never been read against the Septuagint in this project, and it yields nineteen verse-units with no Hebrew counterpart.

⚠ And it corrects a figure that had propagated into six documents. See Headline 2.

Warrant tags: [T] derivable from the text · [I] inference · [S] secondary source.

Headline Findings

1 · The received text divides Solomon I into four paragraphs — and the division this project has been using is not one of them. [T] Surfaced by: Structure, and the whole-span marker map, which neither half-run could see. BHS carries four markers in 375 verses: at 10:1 (setting off the superscription), 13:25, 19:9 and 21:30. The resulting paragraphs are:

10:2 → 13:25 115
14:1 → 19:9 162
19:10 → 21:30 80
21:31 → 22:16 (and on to 22:21) 17+

Nothing is marked at 15:33/16:1. The middle paragraph runs straight through it. Preaching Solomon I whole is what the transmitted text does; the two-part division was a modern convenience, and the project’s own two reports were shaped by it.

2 · ⚠ A correction: that middle paragraph is 162 verses, not ninety-one. [T] — direct count from the parsed BHS: chapters 14 (35) + 15 (33) + 16 (33) + 17 (28) + 18 (24) + 19:1–9 (9) = 162. The figure “ninety-one verses” had propagated into six documents — dig-deeper-proverbs-10-1to15-33, dig-deeper-proverbs-16-1to22-16 (twice), dig-deeper-proverbs-30-1to31-9 (twice), proverbs-collections-assessment (twice), proverbs-dig-deeper-macro-synthesis, and proverbs-questions-still-open. All are corrected. (This report first said “three documents”; a residual grep found the other three, which is the argument for running one.) The error was in this project’s favour to fix: a 162-verse paragraph running across the seam is a far stronger datum than a 91-verse one.

3 · The two vocabularies cross, and chapter 16 is the crossing-point — 0 and 11. [T] Counted by verses across all thirteen chapters:

⚠ This is a finding about the Masoretic text, qualified 26 August 2026. The whole-book Greek survey shows that 16:1, 16:3, 16:4 and 16:6 have no Greek counterpart — and all four name YHWH in the Hebrew. So the cluster this headline rests on is, in the Greek tradition, less than half of what it is in the MT. The finding stands for the text we preach from — the ESV and every English version follow the Hebrew — but it must be stated as Masoretic, not as a fact about Proverbs simpliciter. The same survey adds a related figure: chapters 15–16 are the point of sharpest divergence between the two texts anywhere in Solomon I (five Greek pluses in ch. 15, four minuses in 16:1–6). See proverbs-lxx-pluses-survey, §5.

The collection opens sorting people into two columns and arrives at talking about God. At chapter 16 the substitution is total: ṣaddîq reaches zero and YHWH reaches its maximum. Eight of the first nine verses of chapter 16 name him (16:1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9 — only v.8 does not). High confidence — all countable.

And the whole section refines what the half-run could say. The 10:1–15:33 report recorded ṣaddîq “reaching zero.” It reaches zero at chapter 16 and then recovers to a low plateau — 2, 3, 0, 1, 4 across chapters 17–21. So the movement is a steep fall to a single point of total substitution, not a permanent disappearance. [T]

4 · Nineteen Greek verse-units in Solomon I have no Hebrew counterpart — and this project had seen only one of them. [T] Surfaced by: Translations, Textual Variants. 10:4a · 12:11a · 12:13a · 13:9a · 13:13a · 15:18a · 15:27a · 15:28a · 15:29a · 15:29b · 17:6a · 17:16a · 18:22a · 20:9a · 20:9b · 20:9c · 22:8a · 22:9a · 22:14a. Only 22:8a was known here, from the 2 Corinthians 9:7 work. Two of them are theologically weighty enough that a preacher should know they exist — see Tool 8.

5 · The abomination-spine runs the whole length and crosses the old divide. [T] Tôʿēbâ stands at 11:1 · 11:20 · 12:22 · 13:19 · 15:8 · 15:9 · 15:26 · 16:5 · 16:12 · 17:15 · 20:10 · 20:23 · 21:27 — thirteen occurrences spread across the whole section. The 10:1–15:33 run found four of them and called it a spine; the spine is three times longer than that and runs through 15:33/16:1 without a break. High confidence — countable.

The Sixteen Tools

Tools worked in full below are those where whole-section scope changes the answer. Where it does not, the entry points to the half-run that holds the detail rather than repeating it — this is a deliberate economy, not a gap.

1. Author’s Purpose

Book level stands as the overview has it. Section level, and this is what the whole view gives: Solomon I exists to install a two-column world in the reader’s mind and then to dissolve his confidence that he can tell which column he is in. [I] The first half does the installing (81.0% antithetical); the second half does the dissolving (20.4%, and framed on 16:2 ∥ 21:2, “pure in his own eyes… but the LORD weighs”). Read as two sections, that looks like two collections with different styles. Read whole, it looks like one argument with two movements. Moderate–high.

2. Context

Positional Necessity Check

Preceding movement. Chapters 1–9 have finished. The father has fallen silent after 7:27; Wisdom and Folly have both built houses and issued the same invitation to the same person (9:4 ∥ 9:16); and the reader has been left to choose with no vocative in chapter 9 at all.

Necessity answer. Solomon I exists here because the reader has just been left alone. Nine chapters of direct address end; 375 verses of third-person observation begin. [T] One imperative in the first 184 verses and no vocative at all — the book stops speaking to him and starts describing the world he must now navigate. The silence of chapter 9 is not a gap before the collections; it is the doorway into them. [I] Moderate–high.

Implication. A sermon on Solomon I that addresses the congregation throughout in the second person is fighting the text’s own decision. The material is descriptive by design, and the description is the argument.

Immediately before: 9:18, the narrator’s verdict on Folly’s guests. Immediately after: 22:17’s superscription, “the words of the wise” — and the received paragraph runs 21:31 → 22:21, straight across it.

3. Structure

See Headline 1 for the paragraph map. Three further observations belong at whole-section scale.

The superscription is set off by itself. [T] The petuchah falls after mišlê šəlōmōh and before bēn ḥākām, so 10:1a is its own paragraph — the only one-clause paragraph in the section. The transmitted text treats the title as a title.

The internal frame at 10:1b ∥ 15:20 is real, and it does not create a section boundary. [T] bēn ḥākām yəśammaḥ-ʾāb stands verbatim at both. But a frame closing at 15:20 does not require a break at 15:33 — and the Masoretic paragraph runs 14:1 → 19:9 straight through both points. Two true things that pull in different directions, and the honest reading is that 10:1b–15:20 is a bracketed movement inside a larger paragraph, not a collection. [I] Moderate. Surfaced here rather than resolved.

The section does not close on an echo of its opening. 22:16 is about oppressing the poor to enrich oneself; it has no verbal link to 10:1. [T] Solomon I is framed at its head and simply stops at its foot — which is consistent with 21:31 → 22:21 running past it.

N/A at whole-section scale — 375 free-standing bicola have no connective architecture between them. Within-saying connectors are worked in the two half-runs.

5. Parallels

The whole-section antithesis figure is 50.1% — and it describes nothing. [T] 149 of 184 in the first half (81.0%) plus 39 of 191 in the second (20.4%) gives 188 of 375. The average is the one number in this report that should never be quoted, because it conceals precisely the fact that matters: the two halves are formally opposite. A whole-section sermon must show the change, not average it.

Full verse-by-verse classification on the one scheme: see the two half-runs.

8. Translations — and this is where the whole-section run earns its place

ESV, NASB and KJV agree substantively across most of the section; divergences are worked in the half-runs.

Ancient versions check — run for the first time on this material, and it returns nineteen pluses. [T]

The Greek of Solomon I contains nineteen verse-units with no Hebrew counterpart. Two clusters stand out: four in three verses at 15:27a–29b, and three in one verse at 20:9a–c.

The two that a preacher should know exist:

15:27a — ἐλεημοσύναις καὶ πίστεσιν ἀποκαθαίρονται ἁμαρτίαι, τῷ δὲ φόβῳ κυρίου ἐκκλίνει πᾶς ἀπὸ κακοῦ “By almsgiving and acts of faith sins are cleansed; and by the fear of the LORD everyone turns away from evil.”

This is a sentence about almsgiving cleansing sin, in Greek Proverbs, with nothing behind it in the Hebrew. [T] It belongs to the same stream as Tobit 12:9 and Sirach 3:30, and it had a long afterlife. A Reformed preacher will want to know it is there, and to know that it is not in the Hebrew. The classification is category 2 — a substantive plus — and no position is taken here on its Vorlage.

20:9c — μὴ εἴπῃς Τείσομαι τὸν ἐχθρόν, ἀλλὰ ὑπόμεινον τὸν κύριον, ἵνα σοι βοηθήσῃ “Do not say, ‘I will avenge myself on my enemy,’ but wait for the LORD, that he may help you.”

That is Proverbs 20:22 of the Hebrew, relocated to 20:9. [I] Moderate–high. And 20:9a — “of one who speaks evil of father or mother, the lamp shall be put out” — is Hebrew 20:20, likewise relocated. So the 20:9 cluster is substantially made of doublets of verses standing thirteen verses later in the Hebrew. That is a different kind of plus from 15:27a, and the two should not be lumped together.

Others worth a line. 18:22a — “he who casts out a good wife casts out good things; he who keeps an adulteress is foolish and ungodly” — divorce material with no Hebrew counterpart. 17:6a — “the whole world of wealth belongs to the faithful; to the unfaithful, not a penny.” 22:9a — “he who gives gifts gains victory and honour, but he takes away the life of those who possess them.”

Pulpit edition note: Pulpit text matches report default.

9. Tone and Feel

The section’s tone changes at the same place its vocabulary does. [T]/[I] Chapters 10–13 are brisk, binary and impersonal — a world sorted. From chapter 15 the register warms and slows: God is named three times as often, the king appears, and the sayings begin to concede complexity (14:12; 20:9). Soundtrack: a metronome for eleven chapters, then someone leans on the sustain pedal at chapter 16.

10. Repetition

See Tool 7 for the whole-section threads; the half-runs hold the local repetitions.

One whole-section observation. The abomination-formula changes shape at the old divide — tôʿăbat YHWH … rəṣônô four times in the first half (11:1, 11:20, 12:22, 15:8) and not once in the second, which instead has tôʿăbat YHWH gam-šənêhem (17:15, 20:10). [T] The vocabulary persists across 15:33/16:1; the formula does not. That is the sharpest formal evidence that something happens at the seam even though no marker falls there — and preaching the section whole means naming the change rather than using it as a break.

12. Genre

Sentence literature: free-standing bicola, 375 of them, essentially all independent. Reading rule unchanged — generally-true observations, not promises. At this scale the genre’s demand on the preacher is different in kind: no sermon can walk 375 verses, so the sermon must be built on the section’s shape rather than its contents. That shape is Headline 3.

13. Copycat · 14. Bible Timeline · 15. Who Am I?

As the half-runs. One whole-section note under Who Am I?: the reader is offered two columns for eleven chapters and then told at 16:2 and 21:2 that he cannot place himself in them. [T] That is the section’s answer to its own question, and it only becomes visible when the two halves are read together.

16. So What?

Stage 1. The response sought is not a decision but a re-description: to see the world as a place where character and consequence are connected, and then to stop using that connection to grade oneself.

Stage 2. Worldview: the moral order is real, God stands behind it, and the reader is not its auditor. Stop: using Proverbs’ two columns as a sorting device for other people, or for oneself. Start: reading 16:1–9 as the section’s own centre of gravity. Motive: 16:2 — the weighing is God’s, which is a relief before it is a threat.

Four audiences. For me: which column do I assume I am in, and on what evidence? For a Christian friend: anyone using Proverbs as a self-audit. For the church: the two-column world is corporate — 11:10–11 has the city rejoicing. For an unbeliever: the claim that character and outcome are connected is testable, and Proverbs is more honest about its exceptions than its reputation suggests (14:12; 20:9).

Textual Variants

Category 2 — the nineteen Greek pluses. Listed at Headline 4 and worked at Tool 8. No position is taken on the Vorlage of any of them, and the two kinds must be distinguished: relocated doublets (20:9a and 20:9c, which correspond to Hebrew 20:20 and 20:22) are a different phenomenon from free-standing pluses (15:27a, 18:22a) with no Hebrew counterpart anywhere. [I] Moderate–high on the distinction; the individual assignments need checking against a critical apparatus.

Category 1 — translation loss. The tôʿēbâ formula-change and the ṣaddîq/YHWH gradients are features of the Hebrew that no translation carries as a pattern. All counts in this report are counts in the Hebrew.

Category 3 — the NT’s own text. 22:8a → 2 Corinthians 9:7, already established in the 16:1–22:16 run: ἄνδρα ἱλαρὸν καὶ δότην εὐλογεῖ ὁ θεός → ἱλαρὸν γὰρ δότην ἀγαπᾷ ὁ θεός. Three words verbatim, one changed. [T] The most-quoted verse in Christian teaching on giving is a verse of Greek Proverbs, and it is inside this section.

Preaching Pitfalls

1 · Quoting the whole-section antithesis average. 50.1% is arithmetically true and homiletically false. The two halves are formally opposite, and the sermon exists to show the change.

2 · Treating 15:33/16:1 as the sermon’s hinge because the older reports divide there. No marker falls there, and the Masoretic paragraph runs 14:1 → 19:9 through it. The hinge is chapter 16 — but as a crest, not a boundary.

3 · Preaching the two columns and stopping. Eleven chapters of sorting, then 16:2 and 21:2 say the reader cannot sort himself. A sermon that ends at the sorting has ended one movement early.

4 · Addressing the congregation in the second person throughout. The section has one imperative in 184 verses and no vocative. Its descriptive register is a decision, not an accident.

5 · Using the Greek pluses from the pulpit without saying what they are. 15:27a on almsgiving cleansing sin is not in the Hebrew. Mention it only with that stated.

Book-Overview Tensions

One, and it is a correction rather than a tension. Six documents gave the 14:1 → 19:9 paragraph as ninety-one verses. It is 162. [T] Corrected in all six. The finding is strengthened, not weakened.
