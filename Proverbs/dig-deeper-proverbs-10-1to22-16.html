Dig Deeper: Proverbs 10:1–22:16 — the umbrella pass

What this is. A consolidating pass over the two completed solo runs on Solomon’s first collection — dig-deeper-proverbs-10-1to15-33 and dig-deeper-proverbs-16-1to22-16, both 18 September 2026 — reading them side by side for what neither establishes alone. It consumes those runs’ conclusions and does not re-open the sixteen tools.

What this is not. Not Macro-Synthesis Mode. That mode’s prerequisite is a completed sweep across a book, and its ≥3-pericope threshold assumes many runs; here there are two, on one collection, and proverbs-dig-deeper-macro-synthesis already holds the book-level work. The discipline is borrowed and the threshold is adapted: a claim earns a place below only if it is demonstrated in both halves, or if the asymmetry between the halves is itself what the two runs establish. Anything resting on one half alone is named as such and flagged not yet joint.

Primary texts: WLC · Swete · SBLGNT, as cited in the two runs. Study text: NASB95. Pulpit text: NIV84 (first engagement). Date: 18 September 2026. Series context: sermon 5 of nine. One new check was run for this pass — the Greek and Hebrew at the collection’s closing boundary (22:16/22:17); it is declared at the foot. Nothing else here is new exegesis.

Warrant tags are carried forward from the runs unchanged: [T] · [I] · [S].

The thesis in one sentence

Solomon’s first collection is one deposit taught by two methods, and the text itself makes the join: the verse the first half could not resolve is reprinted, word for word, inside the half that names the one who resolves it.

1. One unit, two methods — and 16:25 is the weld

Claim. The two halves are not two collections. They are one body of material in which the teaching method changes at 15:33/16:1, and the change is marked by a quotation rather than a heading.

Converging evidence.

  • 14:12 יֵשׁ דֶּרֶךְ יָשָׁר לִפְנֵי־אִישׁ וְאַחֲרִיתָהּ דַּרְכֵי־מָוֶת (“there is a way which seems right to a man, but its end is the way of death”) is the first half’s own limit-statement, and nothing in chapters 10–15 retrieves the man on that road [T], WLC.
  • 16:25 reprints it consonantally exactly, 68 verses later, inside the material whose recurring sentence is a man plans and YHWH decides [T], WLC. It is the collection’s only whole-verse self-quotation across the seam.
  • The first half asks the question by form — 184 verses of sorting, with two admissions that the sorting fails (13:23; 14:12); the second answers it by form — seven sentences that put the man in the first colon and YHWH in the second [T].

Confidence: strong (converging text-first findings reached independently in the two runs; the quotation is mechanical and reproducible).

Payoff. The sermon does not have to build a bridge between the halves; it has to point at one. Read 14:12 from the first half and 16:25 from the second and the congregation hears the collection re-pose its own problem in the place where the answer stands.

2. Six measures move at one place, and the seventh does not

Claim. The change of method at 15:33/16:1 is not an impression. It is countable on measures taken independently of each other, and one measure’s refusal to move is as informative as the six that do.

Payoff. Two sentences for the pulpit. The book stops sorting people and starts naming who sorts them — and it never stops listening to what they say. The speech row is the one to say aloud, because it shows the collection’s continuity in a table otherwise full of discontinuity.

3. The collection never argues, never addresses anyone, and is barely divided

Claim. Across all 375 verses the material teaches by accumulation, and all three of the means by which a text usually organises a reader are absent.

Converging evidence.

  • No addressee. בְּנִי (“my son”) occurs fifteen times in chapters 1–9 and once in the whole of 10:1–22:16 — at 19:27, where it carries the collection’s odd imperative cease listening [T], WLC. Both runs found the absence independently.
  • No governing connectors. Neither half has a כִּי, לָכֵן or אִם doing structural work; the logic rides on a waw at the caesura — 159 times in the first half, 105 in the second [T].
  • Almost no divisions. WLC, printing the Leningrad layout, marks four places in 375 verses: 10:1 (after the superscription), 13:25, 19:9, 21:30 [T]. A reading tradition, not authorial; spread unchecked in both runs [unchecked — apparatus spread].

Confidence: strong on the vocative and connector findings; moderate on the layout, which rests on one manuscript.

Payoff. This is the evidence for the overview’s trap 4, and it is now countable. A sermon that divides the collection into themed sections is supplying what the manuscript, the vocabulary and the syntax all decline to supply. What the preacher can do instead is teach the form — and three minutes of the table in §2 does it.

4. Both halves supply their own counter-cases, and neither reconciles them

Claim. The collection states how the world usually runs and then, at intervals, says plainly that it does not always run so — and it never harmonises the two.

Converging evidence.

  • First half: 10:3, the LORD will not let the righteous hunger — against 13:23, רָב־אֹכֶל נִיר רָאשִׁים וְיֵשׁ נִסְפֶּה בְּלֹא מִשְׁפָּט (“abundant food is in the fallow ground of the poor, but it is swept away for lack of justice”) [T]. And 14:10, 14:13, two verses conceding that another person’s interior is unreadable, either side of 14:12 — which shares its וְאַחֲרִיתָהּ with 14:13 [T].
  • Second half: 16:4, “even the wicked for the day of evil”, undeveloped [T]; 20:9, the unanswered question; 20:24, “how then can a man understand his own way?” [T].
  • And the second half’s characteristic form concedes rather than asserts: eight verses open טוֹב (“better”), and a comparative lets the unjust income be great and still ranks it second (16:8) [T].

Confidence: strong.

Payoff. The honest sermon is already in the text. The preacher does not have to defend Proverbs against its exceptions, because the collection supplies them — one in each half, in the same voice, without apology. Reading 10:3 and 13:23 aloud together teaches the genre faster than any explanation.

5. The divine name behaves differently in each half, and the difference is the argument

Claim. The name is commoner in the second half and does a different job in each: in the first it classifies, in the second it governs.

Converging evidence.

  • First half — formulaic. 20 verses; twelve of them one of two fixed phrases — six תּוֹעֲבַת יְהוָה (“an abomination to the LORD”) and six of יִרְאַת יְהוָה or fearing him [T]. Chapter 13 has none at all, a 32-verse silence from 12:22 to 14:2 [T].
  • Second half — syntactic. 35 verses, eleven in chapter 16 alone; the recurring sentence is the man/YHWH couplet (16:1; 16:9; 16:33; 19:21; 20:24; 21:1; 21:30–31), and מִן + יְהוָה (“from the LORD”) stands in five of its eight book-wide verses [T].
  • And the two behaviours meet at the seam. The longest consecutive run of verses naming the name anywhere in Proverbs is eight — 15:33 through 16:7 — against a book-wide distribution of 57 runs of one, eight of two, two of three [T]. It begins in the first half and ends in the second.

Confidence: strong (both halves counted independently; the run-length distribution is reproducible in one command).

Payoff. A single sentence carries it: in chapters 10–15 the LORD says what things are; from chapter 16 he says what will happen. And the eight-verse run is the answer to anyone who thinks the seam makes two books — the measures divide there and the name does not.

Claim. The strongest evidence that this is one collection is not the superscription but the vocabulary that runs through the join.

Confidence: strong (every reference verified by lemma or normalised consonantal text in the runs).

Payoff. The last two rows are the sermon. The spine crosses from the frame into the first half as a case study, becomes a universal statement in the second, and ends in a question the book does not answer. A preacher who traces that one line has preached the collection whole without dividing it into sections.

7. Both halves’ outward links run through the Greek — and they go different ways

Claim. Solomon’s first collection is quoted three times in the New Testament, in two letters, and not once from the Hebrew as the corpus holds it.

Converging evidence.

Where The Hebrew The Greek (Swete) The New Testament
Prov 10:12 אַהֲבָה (“love”) covers כָּל־פְּשָׁעִים (“all transgressions”) φιλία (“friendship”) covers the non-contentious — no word for sin 1 Pet 4:8 / Jas 5:20: ἀγάπη … πλῆθος ἁμαρτιῶν — follows the Hebrew’s sense against this Greek witness [T]
Prov 11:31 the righteous is recompensed on the earth εἰ ὁ δίκαιος μόλις σῴζεται (“scarcely saved”) 1 Pet 4:18 quotes the Greek verbatim [T]
Prov 22:8 he who sows iniquity reaps trouble plus: ἄνδρα ἱλαρὸν καὶ δότην εὐλογεῖ ὁ θεός — no Hebrew behind it at all 2 Cor 9:7: ἱλαρὸν γὰρ δότην ἀγαπᾷ ὁ θεός [T]

Confidence: high — NT-authorised, and each wording checked in its own edition.

Payoff. Two things, and a preacher can use either. First, the three-way triage in one chapter of one letter: 1 Peter 4 draws on this collection twice, eleven verses apart, taking the Hebrew’s sense once and the Greek’s once. Second, the correction: “God loves a cheerful giver” is Paul, not Proverbs — the Hebrew of 22:8 says nothing about cheerfulness or giving. Saying so once, lightly, teaches a congregation something true about how the apostles read their Bible.

8. The unit’s start is Masoretic; its end is formal in both languages

Claim. The preaching unit’s two boundaries are marked in different ways, and only one of them is a heading.

Converging evidence.

  • 10:1. The Hebrew has מִשְׁלֵי שְׁלֹמֹה, with a paragraph marker printed between the superscription and the first proverb [T], WLC. Swete has no superscription there at all — the Greek runs out of 9:18’s pluses straight into Υἱὸς σοφὸς εὐφραίνει πατέρα [T], Swete.
  • 15:33. MT’s motto closes the first half; Swete carries it at 16:4, behind the material MT puts at 16:6–8 [T], Swete; [S: BHS, apparatus at 16:6ᵃ].
  • 22:16/17 — checked for this pass. 22:17 is not a superscription in either language. The Hebrew reads הַט אָזְנְךָ וּשְׁמַע דִּבְרֵי חֲכָמִים (“incline your ear and hear the words of the wise”) — an imperative with “the words of the wise” as its object — and Swete has Λόγοις σοφῶν παράβαλλε σὸν οὖς, the same address [T], WLC and Swete. Compare the book’s real headings, which both languages could carry: 10:1, 24:23 גַּם־אֵלֶּה לַחֲכָמִים, 25:1 גַּם־אֵלֶּה מִשְׁלֵי שְׁלֹמֹה [T]. So the collection ends where the form changes — the vocative and the imperative return — not where a title is printed.

Confidence: strong on all three observations; the explanations are open questions.

Payoff. Say once, early, that the sermon’s text is a Masoretic unit at its front end and a formal one at its back. It costs a sentence, it is true, and it inoculates the congregation against the impression that the chapter divisions are doing the work.

What each half has that the other does not

Not every difference is an argument. These are the ones both runs found and neither could have found alone.

Who is judged The righteous and the wicked, by outcome The man himself, by someone competent to weigh him
The court Absent — מֶלֶךְ in 2 verses Present — 12 verses, five of them 16:10–15, and 21:1 puts his heart in another hand
Eden עֵץ חַיִּים three times (11:30; 13:12; 15:4) of four in the book None
Questions Almost none Three in chapter 20 alone (20:6; 20:9; 20:24)
Self-quotation Eight, mostly within the half — including 10:6b = 10:11b, five verses apart Fewer within, but four whole-verse doublets with chapters 25–29, where the first half has none on the same measure

The last row is the one to keep for the next collection: whatever relation the Hezekiah transcription has to Solomon I, it runs through the second half.

Categories that yielded nothing, stated honestly

  • No chiasm, in either half. None was sought and none is claimed; the material does not support one.
  • No diction-driven structure — no pun, no sound-play, no Leitwort. The paronomasia check returned nothing in both runs. The collection’s device is formulaic, not phonetic: a fixed sentence-shape reused with new content.
  • No narrative motif, no type-scene, no character dossier. 375 verses contain one proper noun — Solomon, at 10:1 — plus the divine name [T].
  • No Christological typology. Both runs advanced trajectory and contrast only, and both declined a royal typology at 16:10–15 for want of narrative, escalation and NT precedent.

An honest nil-return in four categories is part of what the pass is for.

What this means for sermon 5

The series plan gives one sermon to 375 verses [S: overview]. The two runs together say what that sermon can actually be, and it is not a survey.

  1. Preach the shape, not a selection. §2’s table is the sermon’s spine, and it can be shown in three minutes.
  2. Let the collection raise its own problem and answer it. 14:12 → 16:25 is the movement, and the text supplies both ends.
  3. Preach one chain across the seam. §6’s last row — 3:7 → 12:15 → 16:2 ‖ 21:2 → 20:9 — is the series’ own spine arriving at its question.
  4. Teach the genre from the verse that most needs it. 22:6 stands nine verses from the end of the unit, among sayings about lending and the poor, and it is the book’s most misused sentence [S: overview, trap 1].
  5. Do not supply the exhortation the collection withholds. No imperative governs either half and there is no addressee; “be the righteous man” is a sermon the text does not preach.
  6. Land on the question, not past it. 20:9 is the collection’s own doorway, and both runs found it. Ask it, leave a silence, then answer it from outside the book.

Open questions, consolidated

Six stand across the two runs; three are the same question asked in two places.

  • Why has the Greek no superscription at 10:1, and why is its order different at 16:1–9? One question, not two — Rahlfs-Hanhart and Göttingen via Logos.
  • What did the Old Greek read at 10:12? Swete has no word for sin; 1 Peter and James do.
  • Is the 22:8 plus pre-Christian, and did Paul meet it as Scripture? A commentary on 2 Corinthians, and the Göttingen apparatus.
  • How widely is the Leningrad paragraph layout of this collection attested? Four marks in 375 verses is either characteristic or idiosyncratic and one manuscript cannot say which [unchecked — apparatus spread].
  • How many verses of the Hebrew Bible carry קֶסֶם? The index returns four and its coverage of the prophets looks thin; the finding rests only on the Torah occurrences.
  • Does עַל־פִּי דַרְכּוֹ at 22:6 mean “the way he should go” or “his own bent”? The versions choose; the Hebrew permits both.
