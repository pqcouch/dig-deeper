## Phase 0 — scope

**A single sermon on one chapter — and the chapter is one sentence.** Twenty-two verses, a single Hebrew syntactic period, no internal seam that would justify splitting it. **No other chapter in Proverbs is built like this.**

**Its place in the series.** This sermon owns ***how is wisdom got?*** Sermon 2 has taken *what the book is for*; sermon 4 takes *who is calling*. **The chapter itself makes the boundary easy to keep** — vv.12 and 16 *announce* the two dangers that chapters 3–7 will develop at length, and this sermon's job is to **name them and hand them forward**, not to preach them.

**One thing the scope decision must not do.** The chapter cannot be preached as a stand-alone unit. **It is an answer** — to 1:28's closed door — **and an announcement** — of the topics chapters 3–7 develop in order. *Both halves of its job are invisible from inside it.* See Keep Honest 3.

## Fallen Condition Focus

> **We want wisdom without the search, or we attempt the search without knowing it is a gift — and either way we do not ask.**

*Anchored in the text.* Three conditions escalating in effort (vv.1–4) — **receive · call out · seek** — of which **the middle one is petition**, and it is the one every sermon on "seeking wisdom" skips. And a כִּי ("for") at v.6 that grounds the whole thing in God's giving. **The chapter is constructed so that neither passivity nor self-reliance survives it.**

*The dig's own candidate, and it says the same thing from the other end:* **we do not know whether wisdom is something given to us or something we have to get, and so we oscillate between passivity and striving.** Either wording will preach; the second is the one to use if the congregation needs the diagnosis before the remedy.

**How this relates to the series FCF.** The series concern is *we cannot certify our own wisdom*. **This chapter supplies the reason it does not matter**: wisdom was never a thing to be certified because it was never a thing to be earned. `[T]` **2:6 is the anti-moralism engine of the entire series** — say so, and the congregation will carry it through the next six sermons.

## Teaching Point

> **The LORD gives wisdom — which is why the search for it is worth everything and earns nothing.**

**Subject:** How is wisdom got?
**Complement:** By a real, strenuous, six-verb search that rests entirely on a prior gift — and the wisdom stored up for the upright was in the end given to people who were not upright.

---

## Purpose Statement

**To move hearers out of both passivity and self-reliance into asking** — pressing the middle verb of the three, which is the one most often skipped because it feels least like effort. To send home a congregation that will search harder *and* claim less.

*Differentiated:* for the **diligent**, that six strenuous verbs still do not constitute a claim on God; for the **passive**, that "the LORD gives wisdom" is not a reason to stop digging but the reason digging works; for **anyone who has concluded from a bad decision that the door is now shut**, that this chapter exists to answer 1:28; for the **outsider**, v.5's inversion — the fear of the LORD is not what you reach *after* you have thought things through, it is the content of the understanding itself. *The dig makes the same point about outsiders: the chapter opens with a condition rather than a command, and what it promises is the knowledge of God.*

---

## Big Question

> **"How would you know if you were actually wise?"**

**This week's answer:** *You would not know by checking. You would know by receiving — "**for** the LORD gives wisdom" (2:6) grounds the search rather than rewarding it.*

*Same words, early. This is the week the question gets its most direct answer, and the congregation should feel the relief of it.*

## Proposed structure — four movements

**The chapter's syntax is the sermon's structure.** Three conditions → *then* → **for** → *then* → two rescues → *so that*. Preaching the grammar is not a technical indulgence here; it is the only way to keep the sermon off the five-step-method reef.

> **⚠ Re-based, 18 September.** The four movements are unchanged — **1–4 · 5–8 · 9–11 · 12–22** — but they now rest on the **syntax**, not on the alphabet.
>
> The alphabetic claim has been checked against the corpus and it is **partly right and not load-bearing**. Five of the six strophes do open on the two letters: **2:5 and 2:9 on א**, the first letter; **2:12, 2:16 and 2:20 on ל**, the twelfth `[T]`, WLC. **2:1 does not** — it opens בְּנִי ("my son"), and אִם ("if") is its second word. And as an *acrostic* the pattern fails outright: checked against the letter each position would require, **only v.12 matches** `[T]`.
>
> **So the hinge at 2:11/2:12 stands, and the syntax is what proves it** — לְהַצִּילְךָ ("to deliver you"), the first of the two rescues, and the point where the chapter stops saying *what you will understand* and starts saying *what you will be delivered from*. **Preach the hinge; do not preach the alphabet.**

| Movement | Text | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **1. If** | **vv.1–4** | Three conditions, escalating: **receive** what is given (v.1) · **call out** for what you lack (v.3) · **dig** for what you want (v.4). Six effortful verbs — *receive, treasure up, incline, call out, raise your voice, seek, search*. **Land on the middle one.** Instruction literature does not usually tell the pupil to *shout for help*; this one does, and it is the term we drop. *Surfaces the FCF.* |
| **2. Then — and *for*** | **vv.5–8** | **The sermon's centre, and it is one word.** *"**Then** you will discern the fear of the LORD"* (v.5) — then the ground: *"**for** the LORD gives wisdom; from His mouth come knowledge and understanding"* (v.6). **The conditions are strenuous *because* he gives, not in order to make him give.** Then note v.7: the son is told to צָפַן ("treasure up, store") the commandments in v.1, and in v.7 **God** צָפַן ("stores up") sound wisdom — the verb's **only two occurrences in the chapter** `[T]`. **The son hides the words; God hides the wisdom; and the answer comes before the son has finished searching.** *(On v.8's חָסִיד ("godly one"): it is the book's only occurrence* `[T]` *— worth saying. Its* **number** *is a Qere, so do not build a corporate point on the plural.)* *Establishes the TP.* |
| **3. Then, again — and the handover** | **vv.9–11** | The **second** *"then you will discern"* (v.9): **צֶדֶק, מִשְׁפָּט and מֵישָׁרִים — "righteousness, justice and equity" — the three words 1:3 said the book was for, two sermons ago.** They co-occur in **exactly two verses of the whole book: 1:3 and 2:9** `[T]`, WLC. **Make the join audible; this is the series' spine becoming visible.** The order is deliberate and it is **vertical before horizontal**: knowing God (v.5), then knowing what is right (v.9). *The shortest movement; do not pad it.* |
| **4. Delivered — and settled** | **vv.12–22** | **The turn of the chapter.** Two rescues, both opened by the identical infinitive לְהַצִּילְךָ ("to deliver you", vv.12, 16) — **and both are from *persons*, not abstractions**, given the same grammatical weight and the same four verses each. *Name them and hand them forward to chapters 3–7; do not preach them.* Then vv.20–22, the land: **covenant sanction, not a promise of a settled life.** The last verb of the chapter is נָסַח ("tear away") — **four occurrences in the whole Hebrew Bible**, one of them Deut 28:63's "you will be torn from the land" `[T]`. Follow the canon's own reframing — **Heb 11:16** relocates the country — *but see Keep Honest 5 before reaching for Matt 5:5.* *Presses the PS.* |

### The Christ-landing

**1 Corinthians 1:30** — *"he became to us wisdom from God, **and righteousness**."*

**The join is exact and it is worth making slowly.** Verse 7 says wisdom is stored up for ***the upright***. **The gospel is that it was given to people who were not upright** — which is why Paul cannot say "wisdom" without immediately saying "and righteousness." **The chapter's own condition is met for us before we meet it.**

**A second landing, and the commentators make it.** Colossians 2:3 puts *all* the treasures of wisdom and knowledge in one place, which is where v.4's digging was always headed. `[S]` **Beale**: *"in 2:2–3 Paul **weds Dn 2 and Pr 2**,"* quoting Prov 2:3–6 in full — and noting that **two Greek versions (Symmachus, Theodotion) read "hidden things" instead of "treasure"** at 2:4, which tightens the link to Col 2:3's ἀπόκρυφοι ("hidden"). **Wright**: Christ "sums up in himself all that the Jews predicated of 'Wisdom' (cf. **Prov. 2:1–8**…)." **Dunn**: the imagery "evokes Jewish wisdom tradition (**Prov. 2:3–6**…)." **So v.4's digging has a canonical address, and the New Testament gives it:** *"in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge."* **The search of vv.1–4 is not called off; it is given a destination.**

**And the destination is a person, which is the chapter's own trajectory.** What the son will *find* is דַּעַת אֱלֹהִים ("the knowledge of God", v.5); what Paul says has shone in our hearts is *"the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Christ"* (2 Cor 4:6). `[T]` **Wisdom in Proverbs terminates in a person, not in a skill set** — and this is the chapter where that is said outright.

*Trajectory, not typology.* There is no person, institution or event here to serve as a type, and the dig advances no typological claim. **The grammar does the theological work: a search that is only possible because a giving is prior is not yet the gospel, but it is the gospel's logic.**

*Secondary, and useful if the effort/gift tension troubles anyone:* **Philippians 2:12–13 has the identical shape** — *work out… **for** it is God who works in you.* Same conjunction, same logic. Read it alongside v.6 and the difficulty dissolves.

*Worth knowing but held as inference:* the *wisdom–understanding–knowledge* triad is **Spirit-given craftsmanship** language elsewhere — Bezalel (Exod 31:1–3), Hiram (1 Kgs 7:14), and Paul's prayer for *"a spirit of wisdom and of revelation in the knowledge of him"* (Eph 1:17). `[I]` *Moderate; no commentator makes this catena. Use as resonance, not as demonstrated allusion.*

## Keep honest

**1 · The five-step method sermon.**
vv.1–4 turned into a programme, with v.6 as an encouraging aside. **Verse 6 is a כִּי ("for") grounding everything before it**, and **there is not one imperative in the chapter** — forty-two verb forms and none of them a command `[T]`, WLC. **Among the paternal addresses of chapters 1–9 this chapter is alone in that**: the imperative counts run 3 · **0** · 11 · 25 · 6 · 12 · 9 · 7 · 9. *(Book-wide it is not unique — chapters 10, 11, 12, 15, 18, 21 and 28 also have none, but those are sentence collections. State the claim within chs 1–9 and it is safe.)*

> **✅ Tested and held, 21 August — against a source that said otherwise.** A Logos return stated that vv.2–4 *"contain direct commands"* and that *"the son is directly commanded."* **Those are the English imperatives, not Hebrew ones.** At 2:1 both verbs — תִּקַּח ("you receive") and תִּצְפֹּן ("you treasure up") — carry the *taw* prefix that makes them second-person imperfects `[T]`. The imperatives would be קַח and צְפֹן, without it. The same holds through vv.2–4, all prefixed and all governed by אִם ("if"). **English versions render a Hebrew conditional imperfect as an imperative because that is how English says it. The translation is right; a grammatical claim drawn from it is not.**
>
> **⚠ But Waltke supplies a qualification the sermon must respect.** *"In the deep structure of meaning, **this casuistic construction is equivalent to an imperatival construction; both aim to persuade**."* `[S]` **He is right. Do not preach "there are no commands here, so relax."** The chapter has six effortful verbs and it is urging the son to act.
>
> **So re-base the anti-moralism on the כִּי, not on the verb-count:** *the conditions are strenuous **because** the LORD gives, not in order to make him give.* **That is the load-bearing sentence. The absence of imperative forms is a supporting observation about form, and it should be offered as one** — though the gap is itself worth a sentence, because it is exactly how the five-step misreading gets in: **an ancient reader heard conditions where a modern reader hears commands.**

**2 · Quoting verse 6 alone.**
*"For the LORD gives wisdom"* as a stand-alone assurance, usually in an opening prayer. **It is not a stand-alone sentence.** Detached from the conditional it grounds, it becomes a remark about divine generosity in general instead of the specific promise that ***this*** search will succeed. **Never quote v.6 without vv.1–5.**

**3 · Preaching the chapter as a stand-alone unit.**
A sermon that begins at 2:1 and ends at 2:22. **Open by reading 1:28–33** — *"they will seek me diligently but will not find me"* — so the congregation hears what 2:3–5 is answering: **קָרָא ("call") and מָצָא ("find"), the same two lemmas, opposite outcome, thirty verses apart** `[T]`. *NIV84 carries this one cleanly — "they will call to me… look for me but will not find me" (1:28) against "if you call out… then you will… find" (2:3, 2:5).* **Close by naming what is coming in chapters 3–7.** That also gives the series its architecture and sets up sermon 4.

**4 · Verse 19 preached as a verdict on individuals.**
*"None who go to her return again"* applied to someone's marriage or someone's past. **The genre is proverbial generalisation about a road's ordinary destination**, and the same book says *"whoever confesses and forsakes them will obtain mercy"* (28:13). **This is the chapter's sharpest pastoral landmine and it should be defused out loud**, not left to be inferred.

**5 · Verses 21–22 preached as a promise of stability — and the Beatitude that does not belong here.**
That the upright can expect a settled and secure life. **These are covenant sanctions**: שָׁכַן ("dwell") + אֶרֶץ ("land") against כָּרַת ("cut off"), closing on נָסַח ("tear away"), Deuteronomy's own verb for expulsion `[T]`. **And do not reach for Matthew 5:5 to soften it.** Psalm 37 says the righteous will יָרַשׁ ("inherit") the land (vv.9, 11, 29) and **Matt 5:5 takes up the psalm's verb** — but **Prov 2:21 has only שָׁכַן ("dwell"), not יָרַשׁ** `[T]`. The shared vocabulary with Psalm 37 is real (שָׁכַן, כָּרַת, תָּמִים) and "inherit" is not part of it. **Name the covenantal register, then bring it through the timeline: the land a Christian holds is not Canaan, but the claim that there is a place to be held or lost is exactly what the chapter is making** — and **Heb 11:16** is the honest relocation.

**6 · The "forbidden woman" of v.16 mis-identified.**
The older "strange woman" invites the idea that her problem is **foreignness**. It is not. The consensus is that אִשָּׁה זָרָה means **estranged from the covenant community, not from the nation** — Waltke's "unchaste wife", "an outsider" who "remains at heart a prostitute". `[S]` **Preaching her as a foreigner imports an ethnic note the text does not carry**, and it will jar in a mixed congregation. **Preach what the text emphasises: her words (אֲמָרֶיהָ, v.16 — the same noun as the father's אֲמָרַי at v.1) and her abandonment (v.17), not her body.** *And the identification question is ancient: the Greek of Swete has no woman at 2:16 at all* `[T]` *— it reads "let evil counsel not overtake you", and vv.17–19 then describe bad advice. One sentence, if it helps; not a point.*

**7 · Do not call Proverbs 2 an acrostic.**
The twenty-two verses are worth mentioning `[T]`; the alphabet is not evidence. **Checked against the corpus, exactly one verse begins with the letter its position would require — v.12** `[T]`. **Five of the six strophe-openings do carry א and ל** (2:5, 2:9; 2:12, 2:16, 2:20), **but 2:1 opens בְּנִי ("my son")**, not א. **If you want a form of words: "the chapter turns at its midpoint, and the syntax shows it."** *The acrostic proper is 31:10–31 — sermon 9 — and the two are worth setting side by side then rather than now.*

**8 · The translation note that costs the chapter's clearest structural signal — and in NIV84 it costs more.**
The two rescues open with **the identical Hebrew infinitive לְהַצִּילְךָ ("to deliver you", vv.12, 16)**, and that identity is what makes them a matched pair and the chapter's programme. **NASB95 keeps it** — "To deliver you… To deliver you". **The ESV weakens it** — "delivering you… So you will be delivered". **NIV84 breaks it outright**: *"**Wisdom will save** you from the ways of wicked men"* (v.12) and *"**It will save** you also from the adulteress"* (v.16) — **a subject the Hebrew has not got, and one form rendered two ways.** **If the sermon makes anything of the pair, read those two half-lines from NASB95 or simply say what the Hebrew has.** One sentence recovers it.

## Anti-moralism gate

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| **"Be like X" or "trust the Christ whom X points to"?** | **Passes.** There is no human exemplar in the chapter and no imperative addressed to the son. The temptation is method rather than imitation, and movement 2 is built to block it. |
| **Is Christ the subject, or an example?** | **Passes.** The hero of Proverbs 2 is the God of vv.6–8 who gives, stores, shields, guards and preserves — **five actions in three verses, all his.** Everything the son does is conditional; everything God does is indicative. |
| **Grounded in Christ's saving work?** | **Passes.** The 1 Cor 1:30 landing is not decorative: it answers the specific problem v.7 creates by storing wisdom **for the upright**. *If the sermon does not name that problem, the landing will feel bolted on.* |

## Pulpit-text check

**Pulpit text: NIV84.** Study text NASB95. Six divergences bear on the chapter; four of them touch this plan.

| What it carries | NIV84 |
|---|---|
| **The TP's hinge (v.6)** | **Carried, conjunction and all** — *"**For** the LORD gives wisdom, and from his mouth come knowledge and understanding."* The sermon's centre is safe in the pulpit text |
| **Movement 1's middle verb (v.3)** | **Carried** — *"and if you **call out** for insight and **cry aloud** for understanding."* The petition survives, and so does the אִם ("if") |
| **Movement 2's צָפַן echo (vv.1, 7)** | **Carried — better than in the study text.** NIV84: *"**store up** my commands within you"* (v.1) and *"He holds victory **in store** for the upright"* (v.7). NASB95 has "treasure" then "stores up", so **the echo is audible in the pulpit and not on the study desk.** *(NIV84's "victory" for תּוּשִׁיָּה is a defensible end of the range — it is close to the Greek's σωτηρίαν, "salvation" — but it is elastic; the word is "sound wisdom" at 3:21 and 8:14.)* |
| **Movement 3's handover (1:3 → 2:9)** | **Carried, and word for word.** NIV84 1:3: *"doing what is **right and just and fair**"*; 2:9: *"Then you will understand what is **right and just and fair**."* **The congregation will hear the join without help.** *NIV84 dissolves the Hebrew nouns into an adjectival phrase — but it dissolves them the same way in both places, which is what the sermon needs. The ESV and NASB95 keep the nouns and also match.* |
| **Movement 4's doubled infinitive (vv.12, 16)** | **Lost, and this is the one that matters.** *"Wisdom will save you…" / "It will save you also…"* — see Keep Honest 8. **Say what the Hebrew has, or read the two half-lines from NASB95** |
| **Movement 4's covenant close (v.22)** | **Carried, and more sharply than NASB95** — NIV84 has *"will be **torn** from it"*, which is nearer נָסַח's use at Deut 28:63 than NASB95's "uprooted". If the Deuteronomy echo is preached, the pulpit text helps |
| **v.17, the covenant** | **Decided in the text.** NIV84: *"the covenant she made **before God**"*, with *"Or covenant of her God"* in a footnote. The Hebrew בְּרִית אֱלֹהֶיהָ is a construct chain and **whose covenant is broken is genuinely open.** **Do not preach the footnote's alternative as though the text said it, and do not preach NIV84's main text as settled.** Give both in a clause |
| **v.13, the root ישׁר** | **Half lost.** NIV84 has *"the **straight** paths"* at v.13 but keeps "upright" at v.7 and v.21; NASB95 keeps "uprightness" at all three. A minor loss, and only worth a word if the sermon traces the root |

**If this sermon is later preached at home from the ESV:** the handover (1:3 → 2:9) is carried; the doubled infinitive is weakened to "delivering you… So you will be delivered"; v.16's "the forbidden woman" needs Keep Honest 6 more than NIV84's "adulteress" does.
