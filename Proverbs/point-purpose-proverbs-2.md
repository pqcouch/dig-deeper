## Phase 0 — scope

**A single sermon on one chapter — and the chapter is one sentence.** Twenty-two verses, a single Hebrew syntactic structure, no internal seam that would justify splitting it. **No other chapter in Proverbs is built like this.**

**Its place in the series.** This sermon owns ***how is wisdom got?*** Sermon 2 has taken *what the book is for*; sermon 4 takes *who is calling*. **The chapter itself makes the boundary easy to keep** — vv.12 and 16 *announce* the two dangers that chapters 3–7 will develop at length, and this sermon's job is to **name them and hand them forward**, not to preach them.

**One thing the scope decision must not do.** The chapter cannot be preached as a stand-alone unit. **It is an answer** — to 1:28's closed door — **and an announcement** — of the four topics chapters 3–7 develop in order. *Both halves of its job are invisible from inside it.* See Keep Honest 3.

---

## Fallen Condition Focus

> **We want wisdom without the search, or we attempt the search without knowing it is a gift — and either way we do not ask.**

*Anchored in the text.* Three conditions escalating in effort (vv.1–4) — **receive · call out · seek** — of which **the middle one is petition**, and it is the one every sermon on "seeking wisdom" skips. And a ***kî*** at v.6 that grounds the whole thing in God's giving. **The chapter is constructed so that neither passivity nor self-reliance survives it.**

**How this relates to the series FCF.** The series concern is *we cannot certify our own wisdom*. **This chapter supplies the reason it does not matter**: wisdom was never a thing to be certified because it was never a thing to be earned. `[T]` **2:6 is the anti-moralism engine of the entire series** — say so, and the congregation will carry it through the next six sermons.

---

## Teaching Point

> **The LORD gives wisdom — which is why the search for it is worth everything and earns nothing.**

**Subject:** How is wisdom got?
**Complement:** By a real, strenuous, six-verb search that rests entirely on a prior gift — and the wisdom stored up for the upright was in the end given to people who were not upright.

---

## Purpose Statement

**To move hearers out of both passivity and self-reliance into asking** — pressing the middle verb of the three, which is the one most often skipped because it feels least like effort. To send home a congregation that will search harder *and* claim less.

*Differentiated:* for the **diligent**, that six strenuous verbs still do not constitute a claim on God; for the **passive**, that "the LORD gives wisdom" is not a reason to stop digging but the reason digging works; for **anyone who has concluded from a bad decision that the door is now shut**, that this chapter exists to answer 1:28; for the **outsider**, v.5's inversion — the fear of the LORD is not what you reach *after* you have thought things through, it is the content of the understanding itself.

---

## Big Question

> **"How would you know if you were actually wise?"**

**This week's answer:** *You would not know by checking. You would know by receiving — "**for** the LORD gives wisdom" (2:6) grounds the search rather than rewarding it.*

*Same words, early. This is the week the question gets its most direct answer, and the congregation should feel the relief of it.*

## Proposed structure — four movements

**The chapter's syntax is the sermon's structure.** Three conditions → *then* → **for** → *then* → two rescues → *so that*. Preaching the grammar is not a technical indulgence here; it is the only way to keep the sermon off the five-step-method reef.

| Movement | Text | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **1. If** | **vv.1–4** | Three conditions, escalating: **receive** what is given (v.1) · **call out** for what you lack (v.3) · **dig** for what you want (v.4). Six effortful verbs — *receive, treasure up, incline, call out, raise your voice, seek, search*. **Land on the middle one.** Instruction literature does not usually tell the pupil to *shout for help*; this one does, and it is the term we drop. *Surfaces the FCF.* |
| **2. Then — and *for*** | **vv.5–8** | **The sermon's centre, and it is one word.** *"**Then** you will understand the fear of the LORD"* (v.5) — and then the ground: *"**for** the LORD gives wisdom; from his mouth come knowledge and understanding"* (v.6). **The conditions are strenuous *because* he gives, not in order to make him give.** Note v.7's verb: he ***stores up*** (*yiṣpōn*) — the same root the son is told to use in v.1, ***treasure up*** (*tiṣpōn*). **The son hides the words; God hides the wisdom; and the promise is that the second is found by doing the first.** *Establishes the TP.* |
| **3. Then, again — and what it is for** | **vv.9–19** | The **second** "then you will understand" (v.9): *righteousness and justice and equity* — **verbatim what 1:3b said the book was for.** The order is deliberate and it is vertical before horizontal: knowing God, then knowing what is right. Then the two rescues, both opened by the identical infinitive *lĕhaṣṣîlĕkā*, "**to deliver you**" (vv.12, 16) — **and both are from *persons*, not abstractions.** *Name them and hand them forward to chapters 3–7.* |
| **4. So that** | **vv.20–22** | The land. **Preach it as covenant sanction, not as a promise of a settled life** — these are Deuteronomy's terms, and the canon records the land being lost. Then follow the canon's own reframing: **Matt 5:5** takes up Psalm 37's identical formula and universalises it; **Heb 11:16** relocates the country. *Presses the PS.* |

### The Christ-landing

**1 Corinthians 1:30** — *"he became to us wisdom from God, **and righteousness**."*

**The join is exact and it is worth making slowly.** Verse 7 says wisdom is *stored up for **the upright***. **The gospel is that it was given to people who were not upright** — which is why Paul cannot say "wisdom" without immediately saying "and righteousness." **The chapter's own condition is met for us before we meet it.** And Colossians 2:3 puts *all* the treasures of wisdom and knowledge in one place, which is where v.4's digging was always headed.

*Secondary, and useful if the effort/gift tension troubles anyone:* **Philippians 2:12–13 has the identical shape** — *work out… **for** it is God who works in you.* Same conjunction, same logic. Read it alongside v.6 and the difficulty dissolves.

## Keep honest

**1 · The five-step method sermon.**
vv.1–4 turned into a programme, with v.6 as an encouraging aside. **Verse 6 is a *kî*-clause grounding everything before it**, and there is **not one imperative addressed to the son in twenty-two verses**. *Preach the grammar.*

**2 · Quoting verse 6 alone.**
*"For the LORD gives wisdom"* as a stand-alone assurance, usually in an opening prayer. **It is not a stand-alone sentence.** Detached from the conditional it grounds, it becomes a remark about divine generosity in general instead of the specific promise that ***this*** search will succeed. **Never quote v.6 without vv.1–5.**

**3 · Preaching the chapter as a stand-alone unit.**
A sermon that begins at 2:1 and ends at 2:22. **Open by reading 1:28–33** — *"they will seek me diligently but will not find me"* — so the congregation hears what 2:4–5 is answering: **same two verbs, opposite outcome, thirty verses apart.** **Close by naming what is coming in chapters 3–7.** That also gives the series its architecture and sets up sermon 4.

**4 · Verse 19 preached as a verdict on individuals.**
*"None who go to her come back"* applied to someone's marriage or someone's past. **The genre is proverbial generalisation about a road's ordinary destination**, and the same book says *"whoever confesses and forsakes them will obtain mercy"* (28:13). **This is the chapter's sharpest pastoral landmine and it should be defused out loud**, not left to be inferred.

**5 · Verses 21–22 preached as a promise of stability.**
That the upright can expect a settled and secure life. **These are covenant sanctions and the canon records their apparent failure.** Preached as a guarantee they will not survive contact with the congregation's experience. See movement 4.

**6 · The "forbidden woman" of v.16 mis-identified.**
The ESV's rendering and the older "strange woman" both invite the idea that her problem is **foreignness**. It is not. The scholarly consensus is that ***ʾiššâ zārâ*** means **estranged from the covenant community, not from the nation** — Waltke's "unchaste wife", "an outsider" who "remains at heart a prostitute"; the term marks someone *outside the circle of a man's proper relations*. `[S]` **Preaching her as a foreigner imports an ethnic note the text does not carry**, and it will jar in a mixed congregation.

**7 · A translation note that costs a structural point.**
**The ESV obscures the *lĕhaṣṣîlĕkā* parallel at vv.12 and 16 that the NASB and KJV keep.** The two rescues open with the identical Hebrew infinitive, and that identity is what makes them a matched pair and the chapter's programme. **If preaching from the ESV, say the Hebrew repeats the word** — one sentence recovers the structure.

## Anti-moralism gate

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| **"Be like X" or "trust the Christ whom X points to"?** | **Passes.** There is no human exemplar in the chapter and no imperative addressed to the son. The temptation is method rather than imitation, and movement 2 is built to block it. |
| **Is Christ the subject, or an example?** | **Passes.** *"The hero of Proverbs 2 is the God of vv.6–8 who gives, stores, shields, guards and watches. Everything the son does is conditional; everything God does is indicative."* |
| **Grounded in Christ's saving work?** | **Passes.** The 1 Cor 1:30 landing is not decorative: it answers the specific problem v.7 creates by storing wisdom **for the upright**. *If the sermon does not name that problem, the landing will feel bolted on.* |
