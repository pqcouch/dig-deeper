## Phase 0 — Scope Decision

**Single sermon**, and the pairing is the series' decision rather than the text's.

30:1 and 31:1 are two separate headings, and 31:1–9 could stand alone as a sermon on a mother, a king and the voiceless. What justifies taking them together is not convenience but three things the fresh dig established: **both headings use מַשָּׂא ("oracle" or "Massa") — the only two occurrences in the book**; both speakers stand outside every institution the book has named; and **עָנִי ("afflicted") and אֶבְיוֹן ("needy") occur twice each in these forty-two verses, at 30:14 and 31:9, and nowhere in the 138 verses of the collection before them** `[T]`. The unit is framed on the poor, and the frame runs from Agur's material into Lemuel's.

**The overlap to name.** 30:1–33 is thirty-three verses and would carry a sermon on its own. If sermon 8 is ever split, the natural line is after 30:33 — and the cost of splitting is the frame: 30:14's predators would lose 31:9's advocate.

**How the forty-two verses are handled.** Four movements touch about eighteen verses. **The six numerical sayings of 30:15–31 are read rather than expounded**, and that is the dig's own reading of them: a man who has just said he does not know proceeds, for twenty-four verses, to number what he has seen and to attach no verdict to any of it. *Read three of them aloud, quickly, and let the flatness do the work.* They are the sermon's evidence, not its outline.

## 1. Fallen Condition Focus

**We want a wisdom we can hold — and what this book finally hands us is a man saying he never got it, and a word we did not produce.**

The first hearers arrived at 30:1 having been promised wisdom in the book's first six verses, given its thesis at 9:10, warned at 3:7 not to certify themselves, shown four men who did it anyway, and then left alone with 138 anonymous cases. **They arrive at Agur expecting a summary.** What they get is a confession that takes 9:10 apart term by term.

The vocabulary makes it exact. **דַּעַת קְדֹשִׁים ("the knowledge of the Holy One") stands in exactly two verses of the entire Hebrew Bible** — 9:10, where it *is* understanding, and 30:3, where Agur says he has not got it `[T]`, whole-corpus search. And **all three of the book's great wisdom nouns occur in this unit once each — חָכְמָה ("wisdom") at 30:3, בִּינָה ("understanding") at 30:2, דַּעַת ("knowledge") at 30:3 — and every one of the three is negated** `[T]`, against thirty-eight, fourteen and thirty-nine verses elsewhere in the book.

That is not a period problem. **It is the position of anyone who has been a Christian long enough to have expected, by now, to have got somewhere.**

*Secondary candidate, if the preacher takes the second half:* **we would rather be told what to think than be handed a list of things nobody understands and a poor man's cause.**

## 2. Teaching Point

> **Proverbs ends its own teaching by handing back what it promised — its last sage has not learned wisdom and cannot name the Holy One — and leaves in its place a question about someone who went up to heaven and came down and a son nobody can name; and the God whose every word is tested has answered that question in a Son who came down, so that wisdom is finally not learned but received.**

**Subject:** what does Proverbs finally do with the wisdom it spent twenty-nine chapters promising?

**Complement:** it hands it back unclaimed, points past itself to a person, and waits — and the New Testament answers with the Son who came down.

**Where it comes from.** Four of the dig's five headlines converge:

- **The revocation.** 9:10 → 30:2–3, the two-occurrence phrase and the three negated nouns `[T]`.
- **The question's shape.** 30:4 asks for **one person who both went up and came down** — and that is the discriminating detail. Swete's Greek reads τίς ἀνέβη εἰς τὸν οὐρανὸν **καὶ κατέβη**; the two nearest rivals do not have it (Deut 30:12 LXX has an ascent only; Bar 3:29 has someone bring *wisdom* down) `[T]`, Swete. John 3:13: **οὐδεὶς ἀναβέβηκεν εἰς τὸν οὐρανὸν εἰ μὴ ὁ ἐκ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ καταβάς, ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου** `[T]`, SBLGNT. **And 30:4's last question — מַה־שֶּׁם־בְּנוֹ, "what is his son's name?" — is answered in the same clause.**
- **The one positive.** 30:5–6: כָּל־אִמְרַת אֱלוֹהַּ צְרוּפָה מָגֵן הוּא לַחֹסִים בּוֹ ("every word of God is tested; he is a shield to those who take refuge in him") — Agur's entire affirmative content, and **the book's only act of quotation**, from Ps 18:31 ‖ 2 Sam 22:31 `[T]`. He changes two things: יְהוָה becomes אֱלוֹהַּ (the divine name of Job, the book immediately before Proverbs in the Tanak), and **כֹּל ("all") moves off the people and onto the word** `[T]`. *A man who cannot vouch for himself can still say that every word is tested.*
- **The last command.** 31:8–9, פְּתַח־פִּיךָ ("open your mouth"), twice `[T]`.

**Why it is not moralism.** The actor throughout is God: he conceals, he tests his word, he shields, he sends. **The payoff is impossible to reach by effort** — a man who tried harder to be humble about his wisdom would simply be the book's fifth self-certifying fool. The only exit is a wisdom given as a person, and the text supplies the question that asks for one. *The gospel grounding is not the incarnation as a fact but the incarnation as an answer: the one who came down is the one who went up again by way of a cross, and that is why the question of 30:4 is good news rather than information.*

## 3. Purpose Statement

> **To move hearers from trying to acquire a wisdom they can hold to receiving one they did not produce** — pressing the capable to say Agur's sentence and mean it; assuring those who already know they have nothing that the book agrees with them and God is not waiting for more; putting the name to the question of 30:4 slowly and late, so that the answer lands as relief rather than as a quiz; and sending out a congregation that has stopped claiming to know, with its mouth open for people who have no voice.

**Differentiated.**

| Audience | The change sought |
|---|---|
| **The long-standing believer** | To stop treating maturity as accumulation. The sermon has worked if the most capable people in the room are the ones who feel 30:2–3 most. |
| **The discouraged believer** | To hear that the book's own last teacher says what they have been ashamed of saying — and that the one thing he still holds is a tested word and a shield, which is what they have too. |
| **The church together** | To take 31:8–9 as the last instruction Proverbs gives before it stops teaching, and to ask what it has actually opened its mouth about. |
| **The unbeliever** | To be given 30:4 as a real question with a real answer, and not to be handed the answer before the question has been felt. |

**The motive, and Agur supplies his own.** It is unusual for this book: not consequence but **reverence**. What he prays against in 30:9 is not ruin but blasphemy — וְתָפַשְׂתִּי שֵׁם אֱלֹהָי ("and I would profane the name of my God") `[T]`. For a Christian congregation the motive under 31:8–9 is that **the one who came down from heaven opened his mouth for people with no standing, and did not open it for himself at his trial.**

## 4. Candidate Big Question

> **If the wisest man in the book says he never got it — what were you hoping to walk out of here with?**

*Candidate.* Two alternatives:

- *(Closer to 30:4, and the better entry if the sermon opens there)* **"Who has been to heaven and back — and why does it matter what his son is called?"**
- *(Blunter, for a congregation that knows the series)* **"What do you do when the teacher tells you he never learned it?"**

The first is recommended. It puts the congregation where 30:2–3 puts them, which is where movements 2 and 3 need them to be.

## 5. Proposed Structure

Four movements. The arc runs FCF → TP → PS, and the whole sermon turns on **not answering movement 2 until movement 3 is over.**

### Movement 1 — The teacher who says he never learned it *(30:1–3)*

**Function:** demolish the expectation the congregation walked in with.

Start at the heading and say what is odd about it. 30:1 uses two words no other heading in Proverbs uses — **מַשָּׂא** and **נְאֻם** — and נְאֻם הַגֶּבֶר ("the utterance of the man") occurs four times in the Hebrew Bible: twice of Balaam, once of David's last words, and here `[T]`. **This is the only place in Proverbs that talks like a prophet.**

Then the confession, and take it in its four steps, because it climbs: **בַּעַר** ("brutish" — the animal level, and the word of Ps 73:22), **בִינַת אָדָם** ("the understanding of a man"), **חָכְמָה** ("wisdom" — the acquired level, which is what the book has been selling since 1:2), **דַּעַת קְדֹשִׁים** ("the knowledge of the Holy One" — the level 9:10 called understanding) `[T]`. *Four denials, each one higher than the last, ending exactly where the prologue ended.*

**Land it on the count.** Two verses in the whole Hebrew Bible have that last phrase, and they say opposite things.

### Movement 2 — The question with nobody to answer it *(30:4)*

**Function:** hold the question open, and be patient.

Four questions — up to heaven and down; the wind in his fists; the waters in his cloak; the ends of the earth. Then two more: **what is his name, and what is his son's name?** Then the taunt: כִּי תֵדָע — *surely you know!* `[T]`

**Do not answer it here.** The verse is built to leave a reader without an answer, and it is preceded by three verses in which a wise man says he has none. *This is the movement where the sermon earns everything else, and the way it earns it is by refusing to move on.* Let it be uncomfortable. If a congregation is looking at each other by the end of this movement, it has worked.

### Movement 3 — What a man who knows nothing can still have *(30:5–9)*

**Function:** establish the Teaching Point's positive content — and then answer movement 2.

Two things, and they are all Agur claims.

**A tested word** (30:5–6). The book's only quotation, and the two changes he makes to it — the name, and where the "all" goes. Then the one prohibition that follows from it: **do not add.** *For a preacher that is not a metaphor.*

**A measured portion** (30:7–9). The only prayer in Proverbs, and it is about money. Two dangers in the right order: satiety first, and it gets the worse consequence — **מִי יְהוָה ("Who is the LORD?"), a question that occurs in exactly two verses of the Hebrew Bible, and the other is Pharaoh's at Exod 5:2** `[T]`. *Agur prays not to become Pharaoh.*

**Then, at the close of this movement, answer 30:4.** The word that is tested has become a person; the one who came down is the one who went up; and the son whose name nobody could say has one. **Say it once, slowly, and do not decorate it.**

### Movement 4 — And then his mouth opens *(31:1–9, with 30:32 and 30:14)*

**Function:** land the Purpose Statement.

Begin at 30:32 — **יָד לְפֶה**, *hand to mouth*: Agur's last word is to shut it. Then the second oracle: a mother, the only one in the book whose teaching is actually given (1:8 promised it and this is the only place it arrives), and the verb is **יסר**, the root of מוּסָר, in its last appearance in the book `[T]`.

Then walk her nine verses to the end: what must not go into a king's mouth (31:4–5), what may be given to the dying (31:6–7 — **preach it with 31:4–5 or not at all**), and twice, **פְּתַח־פִּיךָ** — *open your mouth* — for the mute and for the cause of the afflicted and needy (31:8–9).

**Close on the frame.** 30:14 was a generation whose teeth were swords **לֶאֱכֹל עֲנִיִּים … וְאֶבְיוֹנִים** ("to devour the afflicted … and the needy"); 31:9 is the last line of the unit, **וְדִין עָנִי וְאֶבְיוֹן** ("and plead the cause of the afflicted and needy") — the same two words, and they occur nowhere in the 138 verses before this unit `[T]`. *Predators at one end, an advocate at the other, and the last instruction Proverbs gives before it stops teaching is to be the second.* **And the one who came down from heaven is the one who did exactly that with his mouth, and did not open it for himself when it was his turn.**

## 6. Keep Honest

**1. Do not answer 30:4 in the first five minutes.** It is the besetting sin of this passage. The verse ends with a taunt aimed at a reader who does not know, and it is preceded by three verses of a wise man saying he does not either `[T]`. **A congregation that gets the answer before it has felt the question has been robbed of the only thing that makes the answer worth having.** The structure above holds it to the end of movement 3; hold it there.

**2. Do not preach Agur as a model of humility.** *Be humble like Agur* turns the one honest confession in Proverbs into one more attainment to be graded — which is exactly what 26:12 exists to expose, and the book has already shown four men who did it (26:5, 12, 16; 28:11) `[T]`. And 30:12 is a whole generation that passed its own examination. **He is not being admirable; he is being accurate.** The question is not "are you humble enough?" but "is what he said true of you?"

**3. Do not claim Agur knew about the Son — and know the textual position before someone else finds it.** 30:4 is a question, and its last clause is sarcastic `[T]`. Say what is true: *Proverbs asks a question that includes a son, and the New Testament answers it with one.* **And be ready for this: the singular "his son" is the Masoretic reading. The Greek has "his children" — τοῖς τέκνοις αὐτοῦ — and *BHS* records it** (apparatus 30:4ᶜ) `[S: BHS]`. Anyone who checks a Septuagint will not find the singular. *Saying so makes the point stronger, not weaker.*

**4. Do not let 31:8–9 become a campaign with the confession removed** — and do not be captured by 30:19. On the first: detached from 30:2–4, "speak up for the voiceless" is indistinguishable from any decent secular programme and hands the congregation one more thing to be graded on. **The unit binds them: the man who has stopped claiming to know is the one free to open his mouth for somebody else.** On the second: somebody will ask about עַלְמָה ("maiden") at 30:19 and Isaiah 7:14. The answer is two sentences — **the word stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible and the four contexts are unrelated, and the Greek here does not even render it "maiden" (ἐν νεότητι, "in youth")** `[T]` — and then go back to 30:4, which is where this sermon's Christological weight actually sits, eleven verses earlier.

*The dig lists seven pitfalls; the other three (the "two odd appendices" sermon, 31:6 preached flat, and the pastoral care 31:6 needs) are worth reading before the manuscript.*

## 7. Pulpit-text Note (NIV84)

Four divergences bear on the plan. **Two of them sit on a movement's hinge.**

**A problem — 30:7, and it is movement 3's hinge.** The Hebrew is שְׁתַּיִם שָׁאַלְתִּי מֵאִתָּךְ ("two things I have asked of you") and **names nobody**. NASB95 keeps it that way: *"Two things I asked of You, Do not refuse me before I die."* **NIV84 adds a vocative: *"Two things I ask of you, O LORD."*** `[T]` *What the congregation will hear:* an explicitly addressed prayer with the ambiguity already closed. **The cost is precise: Agur prays for two verses before he names the one he is praying to, and 30:9's מִי יְהוָה is the first time the name appears in his prayer at all.** If movement 3 wants that observation, read 30:7 from the NASB95 or say plainly that the Hebrew has no "O LORD" there.

**A problem — 31:8, and it is movement 4's hinge.** The Hebrew is פְּתַח־פִּיךָ לְאִלֵּם ("open your mouth for the mute"). NASB95 keeps both words. **NIV84: *"Speak up for those who cannot speak for themselves."*** `[T]` The paraphrase is accurate and it loses two things: **אִלֵּם**, and with it the link to Exod 4:11, מִי־יָשׂוּם אִלֵּם ("who makes a man mute?"); and **the mouth**. NIV84 does give "Speak up" at both 31:8 and 31:9, so **the repetition survives and the image does not.** *Movement 4's close depends on the mouth* — hand to mouth at 30:32, open your mouth at 31:8–9, and the mouth Christ opened and did not open. **Read 31:8–9 from the NASB95 at that point.**

**A gift, with a caveat — 30:8.** Hebrew: הַטְרִיפֵנִי לֶחֶם חֻקִּי, "feed me with the food that is my portion" (NASB95). **NIV84: "give me only my daily bread."** `[T]` *What the congregation will hear:* the Lord's Prayer, whether or not the preacher intends it. The conceptual link to Matt 6:11 is real; **the verbal link is not there in either Hebrew or Greek, and NIV84 has made the decision in advance.** Use it — but know that it is the version's decision and not the text's.

**Worth a sentence from the front — 30:1 and 31:1.** Both versions print "oracle"; **both footnote "Massa", and NIV84's footnote at 30:1 also gives the alternative word-division: "I am weary, O God; I am weary, O God, and faint."** `[T]` A congregation reading NIV84 will see it. *BHS* itself directs reading מֶלֶךְ מַשָּׂא ("king of Massa") at 31:1 `[S: BHS]`. **Say it in one sentence and move on:** the consonants are certain, the word-division is not, and nothing in the sermon depends on either reading.

**And one the sermon should state rather than read.** At 30:3 both NASB95 and NIV84 supply the negative in the second colon — "nor do I have the knowledge of the Holy One" — which is the right reading. **But the Hebrew's second colon carries no "not" of its own**, and the Greek read it the other way round: *"God has taught me wisdom, and I know the knowledge of the holy ones"* `[T]`, Swete, with *BHS* reconstructing a Hebrew behind it `[S: BHS]`. No version the congregation holds shows this. *Movement 1 can say it in a sentence, and it makes the confession sharper rather than shakier.*

## 8. Hand-off

The Big Question is a **candidate**; its final wording belongs to the preacher or to a one-sentence-sermon step. The four movements and their verse-ranges are ready for the manuscript.

**Two notes for the manuscript.**

**Order.** This document takes the study order — original script first, English gloss in brackets. **A manuscript inverts it:** the English leads and the Hebrew follows, because those pages are read aloud.

**Which words to say out loud.** This sermon turns on four: **דַּעַת קְדֹשִׁים** ("the knowledge of the Holy One"), **מִי יְהוָה** ("Who is the LORD?"), **פְּתַח־פִּיךָ** ("open your mouth") and the Greek **καὶ κατέβη** ("and came down"). *My recommendation: speak two.* **דַּעַת קְדֹשִׁים in movement 1**, because the whole revocation depends on the congregation hearing that it is the *same phrase* as 9:10 — and the English cannot show that, since 9:10 and 30:3 look like ordinary sentences in every version. **And מִי יְהוָה in movement 3**, because the congregation needs to hear that Agur and Pharaoh ask the identical two words. The other two land in English without help.
