## The three that remain

### 1 · Heim, *Like Grapes of Gold Set in Silver* — **blocked, not unanswered**

> What is Knut Heim's argument in *Like Grapes of Gold Set in Silver: An Interpretation of Proverbial Clusters in Proverbs 10:1–22:16* (BZAW 273, 2001)? How does he identify cluster boundaries, how many clusters does he find, and does he address the division at 15:33/16:1?

**Status: no copy obtainable.** Not in Logos; the monograph is an expensive Berlin series volume.

**How much this now matters: less than it did this morning.** Heim was flagged as the most significant rival account — deliberate arrangement in roughly fifty-nine small clusters rather than two halves. **Fox has since supplied essentially the same caution from inside the library**, and in his own words: there is "no indication that these ever existed as separate collections", "there need be no sharp boundaries between subsections", and the maxims "are not organized in large-scale designs, though there are some thematic clusters." Heim would sharpen the cluster model; he would not change the verdict, because Fox already holds the position that matters for us.

**Worth trying if the chance arises:** an inter-library loan, or Heim's later and more accessible *Poetic Imagination in Proverbs* (2013), which restates the method. Otherwise let it rest — **this no longer holds anything up.**

### 2 ✱ · A Hebrew antithesis count for Proverbs 25–29 — **no one has done it**

> How many sayings in 25:1–27:27, and how many in 28:1–29:27, employ antithetical parallelism?

**Established beyond doubt that no published count exists.** Logos returned the honest answer that it cannot perform original linguistic analysis, and that "to obtain precise Hebrew counts for each section, you would need to conduct a direct analysis of the Hebrew text." Martin's 163-of-183 for 10:1–15:33 has no counterpart for the second Solomonic collection.

**What the literature does supply, all descriptive rather than numerical:**

- **Steinmann** (Concordia): "Proverbs 25–27 have very little antithetical parallelism."
- **Hayes** (New Collegeville): antithetically parallel sayings "predominate in Proverbs 28–29."
- **Morris** (1942): "In 28–29 the antithetic distich prevails."
- **Fox**: "Va has few antithetical couplets; Vb has several."

**Four witnesses to the direction, none to the magnitude.** And my own English proxy has now passed two independent tests — NASB95 returns 32 where ESV returns 33 for 28:1–29:27, and Goldsworthy's "two of fifteen" for 16:1–15 lands in the same range as my figure for chapter 16.

**So the finding is safe and only the precision is missing.** 138 verses; an afternoon's work; a small original contribution rather than a lookup. **Deferred by agreement.** Worth doing before any published or preached claim rests on the *size* of the contrast rather than its direction.

### 3 · Ben Sira and the dating of Proverbs 28–29 — **not returned**

> Is Proverbs 28–29 dated late *because* it shows Torah-piety, while Torah-piety is itself explained as a late development? Does the field notice the circularity?

**Status: asked, no answer returned.** The substantive question about absolute *tôrāh* was well answered — it is read as Israel's covenantal law, and understood as a step toward Ben Sira's explicit equation of wisdom with the law of Moses (Sirach 24:23). **What is missing is the dating argument and whether anyone flags it as circular.**

**Why it is worth one query.** The sweep and the collections assessment both use the *tôrāh* cluster as evidence for the 27:27/28:1 seam. That use is untouched by the dating question — the distribution is a fact whatever its date. But if the chapters are dated late on the strength of their Torah language, and their Torah language is then explained by their late date, a preacher who repeats the dating is repeating a circle. **Low priority; small stakes; easy to check.**

### Settled from the library

The *ʾiššâ zārâ* is **estranged from the covenant community, not ethnically foreign** — Snijders, McKane, NET, Waltke, Kidner; Keil and Delitzsch distinguishing *zārâ* (non-married) from *nokrîyâ* (non-Israelite); Goldingay holding multivalence open but conceding 2:17 points to an Israelite. **Atkinson**, following Christopher Wright, links this to the land sanctions of 2:21–22 by covenant logic. · **Proverbs 14:32**: the library divides **four to one for MT**, with Fox alone against on syntactic rather than theological grounds. · **27:27/28:1** is attested well beyond Ansberry — **Fox calls it "the common division"**, Morris argues it from form, Steinmann and Hayes describe the distribution. · **Van Leeuwen** places the royal block's start at **27:23**, which is a structural version of the hinge reading. · **Snell's** alternative three-part scheme (10:1–14:25; **14:26–16:15**; 16:16–22:16) independently locates the same transition zone the counts identified.

### Withdrawn or corrected

The Proverbs 2 "alphabetic poem" as commonly reported — refuted against the Hebrew, then correctly restated from Waltke at stanza level. · Proverbs 2:22 ∥ Isaiah 53:8 — different verbs, *kārat* against *gāzar*. · My claim that the Greek levels the 1:7 / 9:10 variation — it expands it into two ἀρχή statements. · The road-word count in Proverbs 2 — twelve, not thirteen, and no *nātîb*. · The *lēb* count in 1:8–9:18 — nineteen, not sixteen. · The "hands" count in 31:10–31 — seven, not eight. · The claim that no one dissents from the 15:33/16:1 division.

## What this means for the work ahead

**Nothing outstanding blocks the five planned sweeps.** The scoping recommended in the collections assessment stands, with one change of framing that Fox himself supplies: **the six-unit scheme should be presented as a useful segmentation for reading, not as the recovered structure of separate source-collections.** Fox's own words are the best warrant for that: *"there need be no sharp boundaries between subsections of a collection."*

The remaining agenda is therefore no longer a question list. It is:

1. **Solomon 1A** — 10:1–15:33 (184 vv), reading 15:33–16:9 as a shared hinge
2. **Solomon 1B** — 16:1–22:16 (191 vv)
3. **The Words of the Wise** — 22:17–24:34 (82 vv)
4. **Solomon 2A** — 25:1–27:27 (83 vv), noting Van Leeuwen's alternative break at 27:23
5. **Solomon 2B** — 28:1–29:27 (55 vv)

After those, **chapters 30 and 31:1–9** are the only passages in Proverbs without a solo or section run.
