## Part A — Lookups in your own library (no credits)

### A1 · Fox on the sub-collections ★ HIGHEST PRIORITY

> In *Proverbs 10–31* (Anchor Yale Bible 18B), how does Michael Fox treat the internal structure of the sentence literature? Does he divide the first Solomonic collection at 15:33/16:1, or the Hezekiah collection at 27:27/28:1? If he notes the stylistic shifts but declines to treat them as collection boundaries, what reasons does he give?

- **Why this is first.** The whole collections assessment, and the scoping of the five planned sweeps, rests on whether Ansberry's six-unit scheme is a commonplace or a minority proposal. **Fox is the most authoritative single voice available and the volume is in your library.**
- **Status: an answer exists but must not be used.** A web summary reports that Fox notes both shifts and declines both divisions, preferring "gradients" and "tendencies". That is entirely plausible — and it is **sourced to an Amazon listing and a wiki**. It cannot enter a report as `[S]`.
- **If Fox declines both:** then Fox and Heim, the two strongest voices, are both unpersuaded, and Ansberry's scheme should be framed in the sweeps as *one workable segmentation* rather than as the structure of the sentence literature.
- **If Fox accepts either:** the assessment's confidence on that seam goes back up.

### A2 · Heim's cluster model

> What is Knut Heim's argument in *Like Grapes of Gold Set in Silver: An Interpretation of Proverbial Clusters in Proverbs 10:1–22:16* (BZAW 273, 2001)? How does he identify cluster boundaries, roughly how many clusters does he find, and does he address the proposed division at 15:33/16:1?

- **Why it is new and why it matters.** This is the most significant work I had never heard of, and it is a genuine rival account: the material is deliberately arranged, but the organising unit is a cluster of six or seven sayings, not a half-collection. **If Heim is right, the sweeps of Solomon 1A and 1B should be run cluster by cluster rather than as two blocks** — which would change how they are executed, not merely how they are framed.
- **Ansberry cites Heim** at chapter 4, footnote 7, which makes the tension worth understanding rather than guessing at.
- **Note:** Heim may not be in the library. If not, this becomes a Part C item.

### A3 · Waltke on the structure of Proverbs 2

> In his NICOT commentary on Proverbs, what exactly does Bruce Waltke claim about the 22-verse structure of chapter 2? Does he say the poem is an acrostic, an "alphabetic poem", or something more limited? Does he identify *aleph* and *lamed* as marking the two halves, and if so on what basis?

- **Why it is being asked.** Several sources report Waltke as holding that *every* verse of 2:1–11 begins with *aleph* and *every* verse of 2:12–22 with *lamed*. **I have checked all twenty-two verses against the Westminster Leningrad Codex and that is false** — *aleph* stands at three verses, *lamed* at three, and v.1 begins with *bet*. The sources all appear to be downstream of one compressed summary. **Waltke's actual claim is presumably narrower and may well be right; it should be read at first hand.**
- **Depends on it:** `proverbs-2.md`, where the item still carries a *do not preach until settled* flag.

### A4 · The Septuagint plus at 1:7 in a critical edition

> In Rahlfs-Hanhart or the Göttingen Septuagint, does Proverbs 1:7 read φόβος Κυρίου or φόβος θεοῦ? Is there a significant variant at this point?

- **Why it is here.** I built an observation on the reading *theou* — that it makes the motto sound like general piety rather than covenant loyalty — and had to withdraw it when Brenton turned out to read **Κυρίου**. Some editions do print θεοῦ, so there may be a real variant. **Neither reading should be asserted until an edition is opened.** A small point, but it is the second time this session that a Greek form taken from a summary has proved unreliable.

### A5 · The *ʾiššâ zārâ*

> How is the *ʾiššâ zārâ* / *nokrîyâ* of Proverbs 2:16, 5:3, 7:5 understood — as a literal adulteress, as a foreign woman, as a figure for apostasy or folly, or as deliberately multivalent? Which reading do the major commentaries take?

- **Why it is new.** It was listed under the Fox vol. 1 gap and never asked in its own right. It bears on `proverbs-2.md`, `1-8to9-18.md`, and the whole two-women structure of chapters 1–9 — and Fox vol. 2 will not help, so this needs whatever else the library holds.

## Part B — Study Assistant (credits needed)

### B1 · Does anyone but Ansberry argue for the 27:27/28:1 division?

> Which commentators, apart from Christopher Ansberry, divide the Hezekiah collection (Proverbs 25–29) into two sub-collections at 27:27/28:1? What evidence do they give, and does anyone reject the division?

- **Why it matters.** This is the seam my own evidence supports most strongly — four converging measures — but it is the one attested from a single scholar. The mirror-image question about 15:33/16:1 turned up a substantial body of dissent that I had wrongly reported as absent. **The same question must be asked of this seam before it is trusted.**

### B2 · Proverbs 8:22 — the *qānâ* / 4:5,7 connection

> Does any commentator connect the verb *qānâ* in Proverbs 8:22 ("the LORD *qānâ* me") with the same verb in 4:5 and 4:7, where the son is commanded to "get wisdom"?

- **Status: my own proposal, never tested.** The general debate on 8:22 is now mapped, but this specific question was not answered. If the link holds, it is a striking one — the son is told to acquire what YHWH is said to have acquired.

### B3 · Proverbs 14:32 — a text-critical verdict

> In Proverbs 14:32, do Waltke, Fox, or Longman prefer the MT's *bĕmôtô* ("in his death") or the LXX/Syriac *bĕtummô* ("in his integrity")? Does anyone argue the MT reading gives Proverbs a genuine glimpse beyond the grave?

- **Status: partly answered.** The landscape is clear — most critical scholars prefer "integrity"; a minority retain "death". What is missing is **what your own commentators say**, since this is a verse a preacher will be asked about and the two readings preach very differently.

### B4 · Ben Sira and the *tôrāh* trajectory

> How do scholars describe the relationship between the absolute *tôrāh* in Proverbs 28–29 and Ben Sira's identification of wisdom with the law of Moses (Sirach 24:23)? Is Proverbs 28–29 generally dated late on these grounds, and is that reasoning circular?

- **Status: partly answered**, and the answer was good. What is missing is the **dating argument**, which matters: if chapters 28–29 are dated late *because* they show Torah-piety, and Torah-piety is then explained as a late development, the reasoning is circular. Worth knowing whether the field notices.

## Part C — Needs original work, not a lookup ✱

These two cannot be looked up. No one appears to have done them.

### C1 ✱ · A Hebrew antithesis count for Proverbs 25–29

**Established:** no published count comparable to Martin's 163-of-183 for Proverbs 10:1–15:33 exists for the second Solomonic collection. Carroll's "most of the 376 couplets in 10:1–22:16" is too coarse; Westermann's ~117 righteous/wicked sayings in chapters 10–15 counts themes, not structures.

**What is needed:** a count, in Hebrew, of antithetically parallel sayings in 25:1–27:27 and 28:1–29:27.

**Why it is worth doing.** The collections assessment's strongest quantitative claim currently rests on an English proxy. **That proxy has now survived two tests** — NASB95 returns 32 where ESV returns 33 for 28:1–29:27, and Goldsworthy's independent "two of fifteen" for 16:1–15 lands in the same range as my figure for chapter 16 — so the direction is not in doubt. But a Hebrew count would replace an approximation with a fact, and the material is only 138 verses.

**Feasible in an afternoon**, and it would be a small original contribution rather than a lookup.

### C2 ✱ · Is 375 or 376 the right count for 10:1–22:16?

**The problem:** my sweep verified **375 verses** arithmetically. Carroll refers to **376 couplets**. The gematria argument — that 375 is the numerical value of *Shelomoh* — fails outright if the higher figure is correct.

**What is needed:** a decision on the unit being counted. Verses and couplets are not the same thing, and 10:1a is a superscription rather than a saying, which is exactly the kind of detail that produces an off-by-one.

**Why it matters:** only to stop the gematria being preached as fact. It currently carries a *do not preach* flag and should keep it either way; but the discrepancy is worth resolving, because a claim that depends on an exact number is refuted by an exact number.
