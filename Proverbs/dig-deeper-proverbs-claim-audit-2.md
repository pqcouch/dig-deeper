# Claim Audit 2: Proverbs 1–9

**Passages audited:** Prov 1:1–9:18, with the later texts each claim names. **Twelve claims (P1–P12):** the load-bearing allusion and design claims of `point-purpose-proverbs-1-1to9-18` v2, and the new candidates raised in the two-framework review of that backbone (12 November 2026).
**Date:** 12 November 2026 · **Version:** 1.0
**Purpose:** queue trigger 2 — an unaudited allusion or design about to carry weight in a sermon backbone. The v2 backbone rests on several claims that had been verified as lexical chains but never audited as allusions or designs (Deut 6 behind 3:3 and 7:3; the 1:20–21 ‖ 8:1–3 reprise; the Sheol inclusio; "9:18 the only narrator's comment"). The two-framework review then proposed seven more (Luke 14 through the Greek; Luke 11:49 // Matt 23:34; the ידע leading word; 1:7 ‖ 9:10 at the centre of a triptych; Ps 1–2 as a seam; the two hosts distinguished; 9:1 → 14:1 by the form חָכְמוֹת). This audit decides what each may carry into backbone v3.
**Method:** **two** blind auditors, run one after the other (the rule of 16 October 2026). **Auditor A** took the five single contacts (P1–P4, P12): wording, rarity, rivals, the Greek. **Auditor B** took the seven field and design claims (P5–P11): block tests, permutation baselines and rank stability under at least two settings. Neither saw the dig, the backbone, the review, the earlier audit or the other's report; each had only a brief, the claim as stated, and the corpus. The main session reproduced the decisive results (listed at the end); all reproduced.

**Primary texts:** WLC Hebrew with its lemma index (every count); Swete LXX (observation, all books); the Rahlfs LXX, BHS and the BHS apparatus for Proverbs (Logos exports, the citation of record); SBLGNT for the New Testament. Greek searches are accent-insensitive stem searches, so Greek counts are floors.

Warrant tags: `[T]` the text itself · `[I]` an inference from it · `[S]` a secondary source · `[S: audit]` a verdict of this audit.

**A note on where the claims came from.** Five of the twelve were the backbone's own (P1, P4, P5, P6, P7). Seven were raised in the review of 12 November (P2's Greek argument, P3, P8's triptych, P9, P10, P11b, P12's "same form"). **The review's proposals fared no better than the backbone's**: of the seven, two survive with corrections (P8, P11b), and five need reframing or fall. That is the reason for auditing a reviewer's suggestions before they enter a plan.

## Single contacts (auditor A)

### P1. Deut 6:8–9 and Exod 31:18 behind Prov 3:3; 6:21; 7:3

**Claim.** The father gives his own words the posture of covenant law: at 3:3 and 7:3 he tells the son to קשר ("bind") them and to כתב ("write") them on the לוּחַ ("tablet") of his heart — the verbs of Deut 6:8–9 and the noun of Sinai (Exod 31:18); קשר ("bind") also at 6:21.

**Data.** `[T]`, WLC unless stated.

- **Binding and writing a teaching** occur together, in one verse or a two-verse window, only at **Deut 6:8–9, Deut 11:18–20, Prov 3:3 and Prov 7:3**. Every other window pairing the two verbs is the regnal formula "his conspiracy … is it not written" (1 Kgs 16:20; 2 Kgs 15:15 and nine more).
- **Finger, tablet and writing** — אֶצְבַּע ("finger") + לוּחַ ("tablet") + כתב ("write") — stand together in **three verses only: Exod 31:18, Deut 9:10 and Prov 7:3**. 7:3 קָשְׁרֵם עַל־אֶצְבְּעֹתֶיךָ ("bind them on your fingers") puts on the son's fingers what Sinai's tablets received from the finger of God. The Greek keeps δάκτυλος ("finger") in all three.
- **The tablet of the heart**, לוּחַ לֵב, occurs only at **Prov 3:3, Prov 7:3 and Jer 17:1** (where sin is engraved there — the reverse image).
- **Prov 6:20–22 is the densest Deuteronomy 6 contact of the three**, and the backbone did not use it: commandment, heart, binding, and 6:22 בְּהִתְהַלֶּכְךָ … בְּשָׁכְבְּךָ … וַהֲקִיצוֹתָ … תְשִׂיחֶךָ ("when you walk … when you lie down … when you wake … it will talk with you") against Deut 6:7 and 11:19 ("when you walk … when you lie down and when you rise", with "speak"). Walk + lie down + rise in one verse occur only at Deut 6:7, Deut 11:19 and 1 Sam 3:6.
- **What is bound.** At **3:3** the antecedent of "bind them" is חֶסֶד וֶאֱמֶת ("kindness and truth"), the subject of the preceding clause — not the father's words, which stand at 3:1. At **6:21** it is the father's commandment and the mother's teaching (6:20); at **7:3** the father's commandments and teaching (7:2). The claim holds for 6:21 and 7:3, not directly for 3:3.
- **A textual problem at 3:3.** The line כָּתְבֵם עַל־לוּחַ לִבֶּךָ ("write them on the tablet of your heart") is **absent from Swete and Rahlfs**, and the BHS apparatus marks it as lacking in Vaticanus and Sinaiticus and proposes deleting it as imported from 7:3. MT, NASB95, ESV and NIV84 all have it.
- **Greek.** ἀφάπτω ("fasten") occurs in four Swete verses only: **Deut 6:8, Deut 11:18, Prov 3:3, Prov 6:21**. The Greek Proverbs uses Deuteronomy's verb. LXX Proverbs has no πλάξ ("tablet") anywhere, so 2 Cor 3:3 ("tablets of fleshly hearts") draws on Exodus, Ezekiel and Jer 38:33 LXX, not on the Greek of Proverbs.
- **Rivals.** Deut 11:18–20 is at least as close as Deut 6:8–9 (it alone has קשר with לֵבָב, "heart"). Exod 31:18 and Deut 9:10 tie for the finger. Jer 31:33 is the prophetic companion: תּוֹרָה ("teaching") + כתב ("write") + לֵב ("heart") within two verses occur only at Deut 29:18–20, Jer 31:33, Prov 3:1–3 and Prov 7:2–3.

**Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.** Verbal link **high** (strongest at 7:3 and 6:20–22); intent **moderate–high**. `[S: audit]`
*Wording:* "At 6:21 and 7:3 the father tells the son to קשר ('bind') his commandment and teaching, and at 7:3 to כתב ('write') them on the לוּחַ ('tablet') of his heart. Binding and writing a teaching occur together in the canon only in Deut 6:8–9, Deut 11:18–20 and Prov 3:3 and 7:3; finger, tablet and writing only at Exod 31:18, Deut 9:10 and Prov 7:3; and 6:22 echoes Deut 6:7 — walking, lying down, rising. At 3:3 what is bound is 'kindness and truth', and the tablet line there is absent from the Greek."

### P2. Luke 14:16–24 drawing on Prov 9:1–6, strengthened by the Greek

**Claim.** Wisdom prepares a meal and sends servants to call the simple (9:1–6); Jesus' banquet parable (Luke 14:16–24) reissues it. The Greek strengthens the link: LXX 9:3 has δούλους ("servants") where the Hebrew has נַעֲרֹתֶיהָ ("her maidens"), the δοῦλος of Luke 14:17, 21; and LXX 1:20 puts Wisdom ἐν πλατείαις ("in the streets"), as Luke 14:21 sends the servant εἰς τὰς πλατείας ("into the streets").

**Data.**

- **The servants are confirmed.** LXX 9:3 ἀπέστειλεν τοὺς ἑαυτῆς δούλους ("she sent her servants"), Swete and Rahlfs alike; Luke 14:17 ἀπέστειλεν τὸν δοῦλον αὐτοῦ ("he sent his servant"). In the whole Swete LXX, **9:3–5 is the only scene in which a host sends servants to call guests to a prepared meal.** That is a fact about a scene.
- **The streets argument fails.** In LXX Proverbs 9 the streets belong to **Folly**: 9:14 ἐμφανῶς ἐν πλατείαις ("openly in the streets"), where the Greek renders מְרֹמֵי קָרֶת ("the heights of the city"). For the same Hebrew phrase at 9:3, Wisdom is placed ἐπὶ κρατῆρα ("at the mixing bowl"). Folly too "calls" (9:15) those going "in the ways" — and Luke 14:23 sends the servant "into the roads".
- **No vocabulary signal.** Ranked against Luke 14:16–24 over six settings, Prov 9 stands around **298th of 1,259** Swete chapters (median).
- **Matt 22:1–10 is the closer Synoptic form**: plural servants (ἀπέστειλεν τοὺς δούλους αὐτοῦ, "he sent his servants"), "I have prepared", "everything ready", and τεθυμένα ("slaughtered"), the root of 9:2's θύματα ("sacrificial animals"). Luke has one servant and no slaughter.
- **New, and better for the pulpit:** Luke 7:33–35 // Matt 11:18–19 join bread, wine, eating and drinking with σοφία ("wisdom") within three verses. In Swete, wisdom + bread + wine within five verses is thematic only at Prov 9:1–5 and Eccl 9:1–7.

**Verdict: Needs reframing.** **Low–moderate** as a textual source for Luke 14; **moderate** as a shared scene, stronger for Matt 22 than for Luke. Keep it as the preacher's inference `[I]`. `[S: audit]`
*Wording:* "In the Greek Bible, Prov 9:3 is the only scene in which a host sends servants to call guests to a prepared meal; the Synoptic banquet parables use the same scene (Matt 22:1–10 most closely, Luke 14:16–24 more loosely). The scene is shared; the wording is not."

### P3. Jesus in Wisdom's sending role (Luke 11:49 // Matt 23:34; Matt 11:19 // Luke 7:35)

**Claim.** In Luke 11:49 "the Wisdom of God" says "I will send them prophets"; in Matt 23:34 Jesus says "I send". Jesus therefore does what Wisdom does at Prov 9:3, and is rejected as she is at 1:24–31.

**Data.**

- Luke 11:49 ἡ σοφία τοῦ θεοῦ εἶπεν· Ἀποστελῶ εἰς αὐτοὺς προφήτας καὶ ἀποστόλους ("the Wisdom of God said, 'I will send them prophets and apostles'"); Matt 23:34 ἐγὼ ἀποστέλλω πρὸς ὑμᾶς προφήτας καὶ σοφοὺς καὶ γραμματεῖς ("I send you prophets and wise men and scribes"). The substitution is real: in Matthew, Jesus speaks the line Luke gives to the Wisdom of God.
- **The saying is not in Proverbs.** σοφία ("wisdom") + ἀποστέλλω ("send") in one verse: **none** in Swete. In LXX 9:3 Wisdom sends servants to a meal, and nobody is killed. In 1:24–31 she calls and is mocked (1:30 ἐμυκτήριζον, "they mocked"), but sends no one.
- **The content is the Chronicler's and Jeremiah's.** 2 Chr 24:19 ("he sent prophets to them"), followed by the stoning of Zechariah (24:20–22) — whom Luke 11:51 names; 2 Chr 36:15–16 (sending and mocking); Jer 7:25 ("I sent you all my servants the prophets"); Neh 9:26. The nearest wisdom text is Wis 7:27, where Wisdom "makes friends of God and prophets".
- Matt 11:19 // Luke 7:35 ("wisdom is justified by her works / children") have no verbal source in Proverbs.

**Verdict: Needs reframing.** **Moderate** that the Synoptic tradition lets Jesus speak as "the Wisdom of God"; **low** that the figure is Proverbs' Wisdom of 9:3. `[S: audit]`
**Consequence for the backbone.** The v2 typology test ("NT precedent fails for this figure") **stands**: the Gospels identify Jesus with divine Wisdom in the wider tradition, not with the figure of Prov 8–9.
*Wording:* "Luke 11:49 gives to 'the Wisdom of God' a saying that Matt 23:34 puts in Jesus' own mouth, so in the Gospels Jesus speaks as divine Wisdom. The saying itself comes from the history of the rejected prophets (2 Chr 24; 36; Jer 7), not from Proverbs."

### P4. John 1:1–3 drawing on Prov 8:22–31

**Claim.** The New Testament's language for Christ draws on exactly this register — πάντα δι᾽ αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο ("all things came into being through Him", John 1:3) — without exegeting Prov 8.

**Data.**

- **Shared:** ἀρχή ("beginning": 8:22 ἀρχὴν ὁδῶν αὐτοῦ, "the beginning of his ways"; 8:23 ἐν ἀρχῇ, "in the beginning"), and being "with" God before creation (8:27, 30; John 1:1–2, with a different preposition).
- **Not shared:** λόγος ("word"), πάντα ("all things") of creation, δι᾽ αὐτοῦ ("through him") and γίνομαι ("come into being") are all absent from LXX 8:22–31. **The clause the backbone quotes has no counterpart in Prov 8.** And 8:22 κύριος ἔκτισέν με ("the Lord created me") is the very thing John's contrast — the Word who *was*, the things that *came to be* — excludes.
- **Gen 1 governs.** Against John 1:1–5, Gen 1 ranks about **4th in every setting**; Prov 8 about **184th** (median). ἐν ἀρχῇ in a creation sense: Gen 1:1, Prov 8:23 and Sir 36:20 only.
- **Rivals for "all things through him":** Wis 9:1–2 ("who made all things by your word, and by your wisdom formed man"); Ps 103:24 LXX ("you made all things in wisdom"); Ps 32:6, 9 LXX; Prov 3:19.
- **Nearer Prov 8:22 than John is:** Col 1:15–18 (πρωτότοκος πάσης κτίσεως, "firstborn of all creation"; τὰ πάντα δι᾽ αὐτοῦ … ἔκτισται, "all things were created through him"; ἀρχή) and Rev 3:14 (ἡ ἀρχὴ τῆς κτίσεως τοῦ θεοῦ, "the beginning of God's creation").

**Verdict: Needs reframing.** **Low–moderate** for Prov 8 specifically; **moderate** for the trajectory, stated as a wider word-and-wisdom creation tradition governed by Gen 1:1. `[S: audit]`
*Wording:* "John 1:1–3 is built on Gen 1:1. It shares with Prov 8:22–31 the picture of one who was 'in the beginning' and 'with' God before creation, but its 'all things came into being through him' has no counterpart there; the New Testament's nearest contacts with Prov 8:22 are Col 1:15–18 and Rev 3:14."

### P12. Prov 9:1 → 14:1, with the same rare form חָכְמוֹת

**Claim.** 9:1 חָכְמוֹת בָּנְתָה בֵיתָהּ ("Wisdom has built her house") is planted in the frame and reappears in the field at 14:1 "using the same rare form חָכְמוֹת also found at 1:20".

**Data.**

- **The phrase is unique to the pair.** בָּנְתָה בֵיתָהּ ("she has built her house") occurs **only at 9:1 and 14:1** in the canon (plain and skeletal passes agree). 24:3 ("by wisdom a house is built") is further off.
- **The form is not the same in MT.** חָכְמוֹת ("Wisdom", the plural-form name) stands at **Prov 1:20; 9:1; 24:7; Ps 49:4**. Prov 14:1 is pointed **חַכְמוֹת** ("the wise ones of", the construct of the adjective), as at Judg 5:29 ("the wisest of her princesses"). The consonants are identical; the vowels are not. The BHS apparatus proposes reading 9:1's form at 14:1 and deleting נָשִׁים ("women"), "cf 9,1" — so the identity is an editor's proposal, though MT's singular verb and suffix after a plural subject match 9:1's syntax exactly.
- **The Greek does not read 14:1 as personified Wisdom:** σοφαὶ γυναῖκες ᾠκοδόμησαν οἴκους ("wise women built houses").
- **Baseline.** Across all 168,704 frame–field verse pairs, 9:1/14:1 ranks anywhere from **17th to 221st** depending on setting and tagging. It is one of **19** frame–field pairs sharing a two-word sequence found nowhere else in the canon (others include 6:10–11 // 24:33–34 and 8:35 // 18:22). Unstable rank: not evidence of design.

**Verdict: Needs reframing.** Phrase **high**; "same rare form" **low**; deliberate planting **low–moderate**. `[S: audit]`
*Wording:* "9:1 and 14:1 share 'she has built her house', which occurs nowhere else, and the consonants חכמות; but at 14:1 the Masoretes read the adjective ('the wisest of women'), not Wisdom's name. The echo is one of about twenty word-for-word links between the frame and the later collections."

## Field and design claims (auditor B)

### P5. 1:20–21 ‖ 8:1–3 as a deliberate reprise

**Claim.** The two openings share seven lemmas — רנן ("cry aloud"), קוֹל ("voice"), נתן ("give"), רֹאשׁ ("top"), שַׁעַר ("gate"), פֶּתַח ("entrance"), קרא ("call") — "across two two-verse spans (four verses)", so this is a deliberate reprise.

**Data.**

- **All seven verified — but they need five verses**, 1:20–21 with 8:1–3: three of them (שַׁעַר "gate", פֶּתַח "entrance", רנן "cry aloud") stand only in 8:3.
- **The decisive evidence is a phrase.** תִּתֵּן קוֹלָהּ ("she gives her voice") occurs **only at 1:20 and 8:1 in the canon**. And the seven-lemma set falls within a seven-verse window **nowhere else** in the WLC.
- **The Greek strengthens it.** ὑμνεῖται ("is hymned"), παρεδρεύει ("sits beside") and πύλαις δυναστῶν ("at the gates of rulers") each occur in Swete **only at 1:20–21 and 8:3**.
- **The setting continues**: מְרֹמֵי קָרֶת ("the heights of the city") only at 9:3 and 9:14; חָכְמוֹת ("Wisdom") within the frame only at 1:20 and 9:1 — her first and last entrances — **and at 9:14 Folly takes the setting over.**
- **Baseline.** Seven shared content lemmas put the pair in the top 0.013% of 750,702 span pairs in Proverbs; with the 100 commonest lemmas excluded, only three remain and the pair drops to the top 0.9%, with about 575 frame pairs as good. **The count alone moves with the setting and is not evidence; the unique phrase and window are.**

**Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. Moderate–high.** `[S: audit]`
*Wording:* "1:20–21 and 8:1–3 share seven lemmas and the exact phrase תִּתֵּן קוֹלָהּ ('she gives her voice'), found nowhere else in the canon; the Greek adds three wordings unique to these verses. The setting runs on through 'Wisdom' at 9:1 and 'the heights of the city' at 9:3 — and Folly takes it over at 9:14."

### P6. The speaker map and the "handover"

**Claim.** בְּנִי ("my son") fifteen times, last at 7:1; בָּנִים ("sons") four times as a vocative (4:1; 5:7; 7:24; 8:32); the formula וְעַתָּה בָנִים שִׁמְעוּ־לִי ("now then, sons, listen to me") three times (5:7, 7:24 the father's; 8:32 Wisdom's). The father's last words are at 7:27. Chapter 9 has no vocative: the father is not telling anyone what to do. 9:18 is the only true narrator's comment in 256 verses.

**Data.**

- **The counts are confirmed**, all of them. The formula is exact at 5:7, 7:24, 8:32 and nowhere else; outside Proverbs, Ps 34:12 has לְכוּ־בָנִים שִׁמְעוּ־לִי ("come, sons, listen to me").
- **9:18 is not the only narrator's comment.** 1:20–21 and 8:1–3 are narrated introductions; 9:1–3 and 9:13–15 are narrated; 9:4 and 9:16 carry the narrative frame אָמְרָה לּוֹ ("she says to him").
- **9:18 repeats the father.** וְלֹא־יָדַע כִּי ("and he does not know that") occurs only at **7:23, 9:18 and 28:22**. 7:23 closes the father's own eyewitness story (7:6–23); 9:18 closes the frame; both are chapter-final and followed by a petuchah.
- **Chapter 9 does tell someone what to do.** There is no vocative, but 9:7–12 addresses a "you" with commands: אַל־תּוֹכַח ("do not reprove", 9:8), הוֹכַח ("reprove", 9:8), תֵּן ("give", 9:9), הוֹדַע ("teach", 9:9), with promises and conditions in 9:11–12.
- **The voice of 9:7–12 is blended.** 9:11 כִּי־בִי ("for by me") is Wisdom's (cf. 8:15–16), but its promise is the father's: שְׁנוֹת חַיִּים ("years of life") occurs only at **3:2, 4:10 and 9:11**, the first two in his mouth. The BHS apparatus notes the Greek's different reading at 9:11 and a proposal "by her" — the speaker is a textual question.
- The Greek has a singular υἱέ ("son") in all three formula verses, and adds υἱέ at 9:12.

**Verdict: Needs reframing.** Counts **high**; "the handover" as the speaker map shows it **moderate**; "9:18 the only narrator's comment" and "no one is told what to do" **fail**. `[S: audit]`
*Wording:* "בְּנִי ('my son') runs fifteen times to 7:1; the father's formula 'now then, sons, listen to me' passes to Wisdom at 8:32. Chapter 9 has no vocative and stages the two invitations by narration, while its unmarked middle (9:7–12) still gives commands, and 9:11 puts the father's promise in Wisdom's 'by me'. The frame closes with a narrated verdict (9:18) that repeats the father's own words at 7:23."

### P7. The Sheol inclusio, 1:12 → 9:18

**Claim.** The gang's boast at 1:12 ("let us swallow them alive like Sheol") and Folly's guests "in the depths of Sheol" (9:18) are the frame's first boast and its last fact.

**Data.**

- שְׁאוֹל ("Sheol") occurs four times in Prov 1–9: **1:12, 5:5, 7:27, 9:18** — and three of the four belong to the strange woman's path or house. 9:18 shares with 1:12 only the one word; it shares the same word with 5:5 and 7:27, and רְפָאִים ("the dead, the shades") with 2:18.
- **1:12's source is Korah.** בלע ("swallow") with שְׁאוֹל and "alive" occurs only at Num 16:30–34 and Prov 1:12.
- **Baseline.** Content lemmas whose first frame occurrence lies in the opening speech and last in the closing speech: 5 observed against a chance mean of 4.95 over 2,000 shuffles (p ≈ 0.53); for the two quoted invitations, 2 against 0.91 (p ≈ 0.24). Among frame verse pairs 1:12/9:18 ranks about 460th–800th of 3,926. **The pattern is what chance produces.**

**Verdict: Discard as an inclusio. Low.** `[S: audit]`
*Reframed (moderate):* "Sheol is the frame's death-word. It enters in the gang's boast (1:12, echoing Korah), and thereafter belongs to the woman's house (5:5; 7:27; 9:18); 9:18 closes that refrain with the 'dead' of 2:18."

### P8. 1:7 ‖ 9:10 as the frame's bracket, at the centre of a triptych

**Claim.** 9:10 ("the fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom") answers 1:7 ("the fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge"); this is the frame's main inclusio, stronger than the Sheol pair, and stands at the centre of a triptych 9:1–6 / 9:7–12 / 9:13–18.

**Data.**

- יִרְאַת יְהוָה ("the fear of the LORD") with a "beginning" word in one verse occurs only at **Ps 111:10, Prov 1:7 and Prov 9:10**. 1:7 and 9:10 are the only two in Proverbs, and they swap their terms chiastically (1:7 beginning–knowledge, with wisdom in the second line; 9:10 beginning–wisdom, with knowledge in the second line).
- **It is a formula, with a closer rival.** 9:10a's order matches Ps 111:10 more nearly than 1:7; רֵאשִׁית חָכְמָה ("the beginning of wisdom") occurs only at Ps 111:10 and Prov 4:7. The fear of the LORD recurs inside the frame at 1:29; 2:5; 8:13.
- **But it is the strongest bracket the frame has.** Among all frame verse pairs, 1:7/9:10 ranks 4th–16th under both settings — stable, and far ahead of 1:12/9:18 (P7).
- **The Greek makes the two verses open identically**: LXX 1:7 imports Ps 110:10 LXX almost verbatim (ἀρχὴ σοφίας φόβος θεοῦ, "the beginning of wisdom is the fear of God"). An ancient reader already joined the motto to the Psalm.
- **The triptych is not marked.** WLC and BHS place a petuchah after 9:9 and after 9:18 only, so the Masoretic division is 9:1–9 / 9:10–18. Lexically the outer panels do mirror each other (9:1–6 and 9:13–18 share eleven content lemmas), but nothing marks the middle panel's edges.

**Verdict:** bracket **Confirmed with nuance, moderate–high**; triptych **Needs reframing, moderate**. `[S: audit]`
*Wording:* "1:7 and 9:10 are the frame's strongest bracket — the only two verses in Proverbs that join the fear of the LORD to a 'beginning' word — though the motto is a formula (Ps 111:10 is nearer 9:10a) that recurs inside the frame. 9:10 stands between two mirrored scenes (9:1–6 ‖ 9:13–18) in an unmarked middle; the Masoretic paragraph break falls just before it."

### P9. ידע ("to know") as the frame's leading word

**Claim.** The frame answers "how would you know?" with ידע: 1:2; 1:7; 7:23; 9:13; 9:10; 9:18.

**Data.**

- **Not frequent enough to be a leading word.** The verb stands in 0.039 of the frame's verses, 0.038 of chs 10–31, 0.041 of the canon; it ranks 130th of 181 lemmas for distinctiveness in the frame. דַּעַת ("knowledge") is a register word of the whole book.
- **But "not knowing" is a motif**, and attached to one side: 4:19 (the wicked), 5:6 (the woman), 7:23 (her victim), 9:13 (Folly), 9:18 (Folly's guest) — every negated ידע in the frame.
- **7:23 ‖ 9:18 is a real echo**: an exact clause found three times in the canon (above, P6).
- The root clusters at the frame's two ends (ranked 2nd–4th of comparable lemmas in chs 1 and 9, stable across window sizes), together with the vocabulary of invitation.
- Greek: 7:23 and 9:18 keep the echo; 9:13 becomes "knows no shame".

**Verdict: Needs reframing.** Leading word **low**; motif and echo **moderate**. `[S: audit]`
*Wording:* "Not knowing is the mark of the wrong house — the wicked, the woman, Folly and their victims (4:19; 5:6; 7:23; 9:13; 9:18) — and 9:18 closes the frame with the father's own sentence from 7:23."

### P10. Ps 1 (with Ps 2) as a seam behind Prov 1–9

**Claim.** In BHS order, the frame picks up the seam that opens the Psalter: אַשְׁרֵי ("blessed"), לֵץ ("scoffer"), the two ways, torah, and the royal son (1:1; 4:3–4; Ps 2:7); the messianic thread of the frame runs through the son who receives torah.

**Data.**

- **The best contact is לֵץ ("scoffer")** — almost confined to Proverbs; in the Psalms only at Ps 1:1.
- **The rest is register.** Proverbs' אַשְׁרֵי אָדָם ("blessed is the man") matches Ps 32:2 and 84:6, 13, not Ps 1:1's אַשְׁרֵי הָאִישׁ. The frame's torah is always the **parents'** (תּוֹרַת אִמֶּךָ, "your mother's teaching"; תּוֹרָתִי, "my teaching"), never Ps 1:2's "the LORD's".
- **Ps 2 shares no phrase at all** with Prov 1–9.
- **Ps 34 and Ps 37 are stronger rivals.** Ps 34:12 לְכוּ־בָנִים שִׁמְעוּ־לִי יִרְאַת יְהוָה אֲלַמֶּדְכֶם ("come, sons, listen to me; I will teach you the fear of the LORD") shares the frame's own formula and its motto; Ps 34 and Ps 37 each share eight phrase contacts against Ps 1's three.
- **Block test unstable.** Ps 1 ranks 1st of 159 psalms on one measure, 33rd–44th on a rarity-weighted one, and 6th–93rd among length-matched Psalter windows.

**Verdict:** Ps 1 **Needs reframing, low–moderate**; Ps 2 and the messianic son-line **Discard on this evidence, low**. `[S: audit]`
*Wording:* "Ps 1 shares the two-ways register of Prov 1–9 and its one distinctive word לֵץ ('scoffer'), but it is not closer to the frame than Ps 34, which shares the frame's formula 'sons, listen to me' with the fear of the LORD."

### P11. Chapter 9's two hosts

**Claims in tension.** (a) Both women have a house, are at the high point of the city, set a table, send or call, and say the same four words — so the invitations cannot be told apart. (b) The text distinguishes them, for the reader: what each does, what each serves, where each leads.

**Data.**

- **Shared:** בַּיִת ("house", 9:1, 14); מְרֹמֵי קָרֶת ("the heights of the city", 9:3, 14 — only these two in the canon); קרא ("call", 9:3, 15, 18); לֶחֶם ("bread", 9:5, 17); and the invitation מִי־פֶתִי יָסֻר הֵנָּה ("whoever is simple, let him turn in here"), identical and only at 9:4 and 9:16.
- **Not shared — and this falsifies part of (a):** only Wisdom builds, hews, slaughters, mixes, **sets a table** and **sends** (9:1–3). **Folly sends no one and sets no table.** She sits at her door on a seat (9:14) and calls to passers-by.
- **Speech differs:** Wisdom speaks in imperatives (9:5–6: come, eat, drink, leave, live, walk); Folly makes a proverb-like claim (9:17: stolen water "is sweet").
- **Fare and end differ:** wine (9:5) against water (9:17); וִחְיוּ ("and live", 9:6) against the dead (9:18).
- **Folly borrows:** הֹמִיָּה ("loud", 9:13) is the strange woman's word at 7:11; פֶּתַח ("door") and "the heights of the city" are Wisdom's setting words (1:21; 8:3, 34; 9:3).

**Verdict:** (b) **Confirmed with nuance, moderate–high**; (a) **Needs reframing** — "both set a table" and "both send" are false. `[S: audit]`
*Wording:* "The two invitations open with an identical line, from the same place — so the simple cannot tell them apart by the invitation. The text tells them apart by what follows: Wisdom builds, prepares and sends; Folly sits and calls; one commands, the other claims; one serves wine and leads to life, the other stolen water and the dead. And Folly speaks from Wisdom's own setting."
