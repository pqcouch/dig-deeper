# Repeated Bicola in Proverbs 10–22

*An inventory of verbal repetition across the first Solomonic collection, and a structural hypothesis tested and rejected.*

***

## Summary

This study began with a proposal about the shape of Proverbs 10:1–22:16: that the material before the LORD/king cluster at 16:1–15 sets out a valid description of life as observed, and that the material after it re-reads and qualifies that description, using repeated sayings as the mechanism. The repetitions would then be a reading instruction — enough held constant to trigger recognition, the remainder redirected.

An inventory of repetitions was built from the Hebrew text at lemma level. It found what the proposal predicted: verbatim half-verses reused with pointed alteration, a whole verse repeated across the boundary, and a triad in which "right in his own eyes" migrates from the fool to every man. On the face of it, the proposal looked well supported.

Five tests were then run, each with a stated condition under which it would fail. **All five failed.** The number of repetitions crossing the boundary matches chance (p = 0.70). Their distribution by chapter matches chance. Their distribution across editorial clusters matches chance almost exactly (Herfindahl 0.120 observed against 0.12 expected). The vocabulary either side of the boundary shows no discontinuity — that junction ranks 31st of 57. And the supposed pattern of redirection turns out to be carried entirely by a single verse.

The conclusion is therefore negative, and the negative result is the substantive contribution. **The individual repetitions are real and several are exegetically weighty. What cannot be shown is that they constitute a system.** On four independent measures the material behaves as though position relative to 16:1–15 is irrelevant.

This matters practically. It marks out which claims will bear the weight of exposition — 18:10–12 and 21:2 will — and which will not. "The second half systematically re-reads the first" will not.

***

## Scope and Method

**Source text.** The Westminster Leningrad Codex with Strong's lemma tagging and morphological parsing (OpenScriptures `morphhb`, OSIS XML). No analysis rests on an English translation.

**Procedure.**

1. All 915 verses of Proverbs parsed with per-word lemma and morphology.
2. Each verse split into cola at the *atnach* (֑), the verse-medial disjunctive. 903 of 915 carry one; the remainder were treated as single cola.
3. For each colon a set of **content lemmas** was extracted — nouns, verbs and adjectives only, discarding prepositions, conjunctions, articles and pronominal suffixes. Matching on lemma rather than surface form means morphological variation cannot hide a repetition.
4. Blocks defined as 1A = 10:1–15:33 (368 cola), hinge = 16:1–15 (30 cola), 1B = 16:16–22:16 (352 cola); 750 cola in total.
5. Every colon compared against every other. Overlap scored by Jaccard index on content-lemma sets.

**Thresholds.** 274 pairs share two or more content lemmas; most are noise from the righteous/wicked word-pair. The retained set requires a Jaccard index of at least 0.50 *and* at least three shared lemmas, with exact matches admitted regardless. This yields 18 strong colon-pairs, reducing to 17 distinct verse-pairs.

For the statistical tests a single uniform criterion was applied throughout (three or more shared lemmas, Jaccard ≥ 0.50, excluding pairs within the same verse or within three verses of each other). Under that criterion the corpus contains 35 similar colon-pairs, of which 15 cross the 1A/1B boundary, 8 fall within 1A and 6 within 1B. The two verse-pairs in the inventory not counted among the 15 are 11:20→16:5, which runs 1A to hinge rather than to 1B, and 14:24b/17:12b, which shares only two lemmas.

**Order of work.** The inventory was complete before any secondary literature was opened. Heim's *Like Grapes of Gold Set in Silver* (2001) was read afterwards and is used to confirm, correct or challenge findings already in hand. Where he supplies something the method could not detect, that is marked. Where he challenges the framing, the challenge is recorded rather than absorbed.

**A known blind spot.** Lemma matching cannot see repetition carried by synonym, root-play or syntactic echo. A demonstrated instance appears below at 18:11–12. The inventory is therefore a floor, not a ceiling.

## The Inventory

Snell's taxonomy, transmitted by Heim, gives the standard vocabulary: whole-verse repetitions are category 1 (1.0 for spelling variants only, rising to 1.4 for four or more dissimilar words); half-verse repetitions are category 2 on the same principle; categories 3 and 4 cover half-verses recurring inside whole verses, and syntactically related verses.

### Whole-verse repetition (category 1.0)

One verse repeats in full across the boundary.

> **14:12 = 16:25** — יֵשׁ דֶּרֶךְ יָשָׁר לִפְנֵי אִישׁ / וְאַחֲרִיתָהּ דַּרְכֵי מָוֶת
> "There is a way that seems right to a man, but its end is the way to death."

### Identical single colon (category 2.0)

| 1A | 1B | Shared colon |
|---|---|---|
| 10:15a | 18:11a | הוֹן עָשִׁיר קִרְיַת עֻזּוֹ |
| 11:13a | 20:19a | הלך / רָכִיל / גלה / סוֹד, in inverted order |
| 15:33b | 18:12b | וְלִפְנֵי כָבוֹד עֲנָוָה |
| 10:29b | 21:15b | וּמְחִתָּה לְפֹעֲלֵי אָוֶן |

The reuse at 11:13a / 20:19a is chiastic: 11:13a reads הוֹלֵךְ רָכִיל מְגַלֶּה־סּוֹד, 20:19a reads גּוֹלֶה־סּוֹד הוֹלֵךְ רָכִיל. That is handling, not mechanical duplication.

The pair 10:29b = 21:15b does not appear in lists built from English translations, because the A-cola are rendered very differently: the way of the LORD as a stronghold to the blameless, against justice done as a joy to the righteous. Heim registers the repetition and puts it to exegetical use, arguing against Delitzsch that because the colon recurs identically it must stand on its own rather than depending on an ellipsis of יְהוָה from the first colon.

### Variant repetitions

| 1A | 1B | The alteration |
|---|---|---|
| 12:15a | 21:2a | Subject moves from אֱוִיל (fool) to כָּל־דֶּרֶךְ־אִישׁ (every man) |
| 15:8a | 21:27a | תּוֹעֲבַת יְהוָה → תּוֹעֵבָה; contrast with prayer replaced by *a fortiori* intensification |
| 15:14a | 18:15a | The discerning heart *seeks* (בקשׁ) knowledge → *acquires* (קנה) it |
| 15:13a | 17:22a | A glad heart makes a cheerful *face* → good *healing*; physiological, not perceptual |
| 12:14a | 18:20a | Satisfied with *good* → satisfied in the *belly* |
| 14:27a | 19:23a | Fear of the LORD is a *fountain* of life → *leads to* life |
| 13:3a | 16:17b | Guarding mouth/soul → guarding way/soul |
| 10:12b | 17:9a | Love covers offences → covering offence seeks love |
| 12:2a | 18:22b | Obtaining favour from the LORD, transposed to the marriage saying |
| 11:20a | 16:5a | Crooked of heart → everyone proud of heart (1A to hinge) |
| 14:24b | 17:12b | Shared אִוֶּלֶת and כְּסִיל; weakest in the set |

## Texts That Bear Weight

Four junctures repay exposition. They are readings of particular texts, and nothing in the statistical results below tells against them.

### 18:10–11: a real refuge and an imagined one

10:15a and 18:11a are word-for-word identical. The partner cola diverge:

- **10:15b** — וּמְחִתַּת דַּלִּים רֵישָׁם, the ruin of the poor is their poverty. A symmetrical social observation.
- **18:11b** — וּכְחוֹמָה נִשְׂגָּבָה בְּמַשְׂכִּיתוֹ, and like a high wall *in his imagination*.

The noun מַשְׂכִּית marks the wall as a mental construct. Immediately before it, 18:10 makes the name of the LORD a מִגְדַּל־עֹז into which the righteous runs and is safe. The two verses set a real refuge against an imagined one, using 1A's own vocabulary of fortification to do it.

Heim reads it the same way, describing wealth as a dubious source of security which without the LORD amounts to a figment of the rich person's imagination, and citing Murphy that 18:11 and 10:15 cannot be read without the points made in 18:10 and 18:12. Whybray took 18:10 as a deliberate correction of an older secular saying.

**What the lemma method could not see.** Heim, following Meinhold, notes a root-play binding 18:11 to 18:12: נִשְׂגָּבָה (שׂגב, to be safe, literally to be high) against יִגְבַּהּ (גבה, to be proud, literally to be high). The verses are joined by a pun on height, invisible to lemma matching because the roots differ. Heim treats 18:10–15 as a single editorial cluster on true and false security and the pride that accompanies it.

### The triad 12:15a / 16:2a / 21:2a

- **12:15a** — דֶּרֶךְ אֱוִיל יָשָׁר בְּעֵינָיו, the way of a *fool* is right in his own eyes.
- **16:2a** — כָּל־דַּרְכֵי־אִישׁ זַךְ בְּעֵינָיו, all the ways of a man are *pure* in his own eyes.
- **21:2a** — כָּל־דֶּרֶךְ־אִישׁ יָשָׁר בְּעֵינָיו, every way of a man is *right* in his own eyes.

In 12:15 self-satisfied confidence diagnoses folly — something the reader observes in someone else. By 21:2 it is predicated of everyone, the reader included, with the LORD weighing the לֵב where 16:2 has the רוּחַ.

Snell classifies this as a single three-member variant (category 2.2). Heim notes that Scoralick registered 12:15a→21:2 but missed 16:2, because her text basis stopped at 15:32.

### 16:25 in its new position

The verbatim repetition of 14:12 falls nine verses after 16:2. The wording has not changed; the frame has. In 14:12 the saying is one observation among many. After 16:2 it reads as an instance of a stated rule about the unreliability of self-assessment.

### 20:20–21:4 as a unit

Working from cluster analysis rather than repetition data, Heim reads 21:1–4 as a unit in which the LORD probes the hidden thought of the publicly incontestable hypocrite, so that outward pretence is not enough. He describes the issue as appearance versus real conviction, and takes the sacrifice of 21:3 as public display set against acting justly. That reading is reached independently of anything here.

***

## The Structural Hypothesis

Stated for testing: *the repetitions are positioned and altered so as to mark a two-part structure, in which 16:1–15 functions as a hinge and the material after it re-reads the material before.*

If true, several things should follow. More repetitions should cross the boundary than chance predicts. They should cluster near the hinge rather than scatter. Their alterations should run in a consistent direction, and that direction should be specific to boundary-crossing pairs. And the boundary itself should show up as a break in the material.

Each of these was tested with a failure condition fixed in advance.

***

## The Interiority Control

**Hypothesis.** The 1B member of a repeated saying characteristically adds vocabulary of perception, motive or inner disposition — the axis 16:2 opens, where a man's eyes are set against the LORD's weighing.

**Initial evidence.** Four cases carry the signature clearly: 10:15→18:11 adds מַשְׂכִּית; 12:15→21:2 and 14:12→21:2 add עַיִן, תכן and לִבָּה; 15:8→21:27 adds זִמָּה; 15:33→18:12 adds גבה with לֵב. (These were first counted as five; Heim's demonstration that 18:10–15 is one cluster means 18:11 and 18:12 are one site, not two.)

**The control.** If the tendency belongs to the hinge, it should be absent or weaker in repetitions that stay *within* 1A or *within* 1B. A lexicon of perception, motive and inner-state lemmas was applied uniformly to all three sets.

| | Narrow lexicon | Broad lexicon |
|---|---|---|
| Within 1A | 2/8 = 25% | 25% |
| Within 1B | 2/6 = 33% | 33% |
| Cross-boundary | 5/15 = 33% | 40% |

No differential. Repetitions internal to either half add inner-person vocabulary at the same rate as those crossing the hinge.

**Sensitivity.** Excluding every pair involving 21:2, the rates converge completely: 25%, 20%, 23%. The entire apparent effect was carried by one verse.

**A rescue declined.** The cross-boundary hits use high-salience terms (מַשְׂכִּית, זִמָּה, תכן with עַיִן) while the within-collection hits are generic (נֶפֶשׁ, לֵב). That distinction is real, but the criterion registered in advance was hit *rate*, not hit *quality*. Rewriting the criterion after seeing the numbers is how a thesis becomes unfalsifiable. It stands as a hypothesis for a future test with a pre-committed salience ranking; it cannot save the present claim. Note also that one of the two within-1B hits is 16:25→21:2, part of the same triad, so not independent evidence.

***

## Test 1 — Randomness Baseline

**Question.** How many cross-boundary pairs would be expected if position were irrelevant?

**Method.** The similarity graph is a fixed property of colon content. Colon *positions* were permuted 5,000 times across 10:1–22:16, preserving block sizes and re-applying the adjacency filter, and cross-boundary pairs recounted.

**Failure condition.** If the observed count falls inside the null distribution, the count carries no evidence for the boundary.

**Result.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Observed cross-boundary pairs | 15 |
| Expected | 16.0 (sd 3.0) |
| z | −0.35 |
| P(random ≥ observed) | 0.70 |

The observed count sits fractionally *below* the null mean. Of the 35 similar colon-pairs in the collection, roughly 16 would straddle the boundary by chance given the block sizes. Fifteen do.

**The chapter-density profile, tested the same way:**

| Ch. | Observed | Expected (sd) | P(random ≥ obs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 2 | 1.7 (1.3) | 0.49 |
| 17 | 2 | 2.6 (1.6) | 0.73 |
| 18 | 5 | 2.2 (1.5) | 0.08 |
| 19 | 1 | 2.6 (1.6) | 0.93 |
| 20 | 1 | 2.8 (1.7) | 0.94 |
| 21 | 4 | 2.8 (1.7) | 0.31 |
| 22 | 0 | 1.5 (1.2) | 1.00 |

Only chapter 18 approaches significance, and that is the single cluster 18:10–15, not five independent hits. Chapter 21 is unremarkable at p = 0.31.

**Caveat.** Free permutation destroys local structure, making this a conservative null for questions about *local* clustering. For the question asked — does the boundary matter to the distribution — it is the right test.

***

## Test 2 — Heim's Cluster Grid

**Purpose.** Replace chapter divisions, which are medieval and editorially meaningless, with units derived from the text.

**Method.** Heim's cluster delimitations were harvested from his section headings across chapters 7–10 and verified for coverage.

**Result.** 58 clusters spanning 10:1–22:16, all 375 verses accounted for, no gaps and no duplicate coverage. Mean cluster size 6.5 verses, median 6, range 1–15. Two clusters are interleaved rather than contiguous (7.12 takes 12:13–23 plus 25; 7.13 takes 12:24 plus 26–28).

**An observation, and its correction.** Heim's clusters break at 15:33/16:1 and at 16:15/16:16, with no unit straddling either point — apparently corroborating the hinge from the bottom up, since he derived these boundaries from catchwords, roots, theme and syntax while explicitly declining to work with a 1A/1B division.

This was over-read when first noticed. Of his 58 clusters, 47 begin mid-chapter, so a break at 16:16 was close to guaranteed. That a boundary exists in his scheme says little; whether it is a *strong* boundary is the question Test 4 answers.

***

## Test 3 — Cluster Concentration

**Question.** Do cross-boundary repetitions concentrate in particular editorial clusters?

**Observed distribution.** The 15 pairs land in 10 distinct host clusters of the 25 in 16:16–22:16: 9.9 (18:10–15) hosts three; 9.11 (18:20–24), 10.3 (20:20–21:4) and 8.17 (16:16–30) host two each; six clusters host one.

**Failure condition, fixed in advance.** Concentration must reach p < 0.10 on at least one of three statistics, or the claim fails.

**Result** (4,000 permutations):

| Statistic | Observed | Expected (sd) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distinct host clusters | 10 | 10.86 (1.96) | 0.42 |
| Max pairs in one cluster | 3 | 3.10 (0.94) | 0.74 |
| Herfindahl concentration | 0.120 | 0.12 (0.03) | 0.42 |

Nothing approaches the threshold; the concentration index matches the null mean to two decimal places. A maximum of three pairs in one cluster is precisely the expected maximum for 15 pairs scattered at random across clusters of these sizes.

***

## Test 4 — Thematic Discontinuity

**Question.** Does the boundary show up as a break in the material, independently of repetition counts?

**Method.** For each of the 57 junctions between adjacent clusters, the content-lemma profiles either side were compared by Jaccard index. Because small clusters trivially overlap less, each junction was scored against a size-matched null built from 400 randomly drawn contiguous verse-blocks of the same dimensions. The statistic is a z-score: strongly negative means a sharper break than blocks of that size normally show.

**Prediction, committed before computing.** The 16:15/16:16 junction must rank in the top 3 of 57.

**Result.**

| Rank | z | Junction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | −1.81 | 20:19 / 20:20 |
| 2 | −1.41 | 17:9 / 17:10 |
| 3 | −1.31 | 20:4 / 20:5 |
| 4 | −1.11 | 21:29 / 21:30 |
| 5 | −1.10 | 14:22 / 14:23 |
| … | | |
| **31** | **+0.15** | **16:15 / 16:16** |
| … | | |
| **39** | **+0.53** | **15:33 / 16:1** |

The target junction ranks 31st — dead middle — with a *positive* z, meaning the vocabulary either side is marginally more similar than random blocks of that size. The 15:33/16:1 junction is more continuous still at 39th.

Neither boundary is a thematic discontinuity. Whatever the hinge is, the vocabulary does not break there. This also settles the reinterpretation of Test 2: Heim's boundary at 16:16 is a weak one.

***

## Assessment

Five tests, five negatives:

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Interiority control | No differential; effect carried entirely by 21:2 |
| Cross-boundary count | p = 0.70; observed below the null mean |
| Chapter density | Nothing below p = 0.08, and that a single cluster |
| Cluster concentration | p = 0.42; Herfindahl matches null exactly |
| Thematic discontinuity | Rank 31 of 57; positive z |

Each had a stated way to fail, and each failed. There is no statistical evidence that the distribution of repetition knows anything about a division at 16:1–15.

The failures are consistent with one another, which strengthens the conclusion. It is not a case of one measure disagreeing with three; the material behaves as though position relative to the hinge is irrelevant on every axis examined.

Heim's methodological caution was well founded. He holds that repetitions of vocabulary should bear weight on delimitation only where they occur in sufficient proximity, since over longer stretches coincidental repetition becomes likely, and that larger compositional units should be built from the bottom up out of established smaller ones. The chapter-density argument of the first draft was exactly the error he warned against.

***

## What Survives

**The individual texts.** These were never distributional claims and are untouched by the results above. 14:12 recurs verbatim at 16:25, nine verses after 16:2 supplies "pure in his own eyes." 10:15a recurs at 18:11a with מַשְׂכִּית added and 18:10's מִגְדַּל־עֹז standing beside it — read that way by Murphy, Whybray and Heim, and reinforced by the שׂגב/גבה play into 18:12. The triad 12:15a / 16:2a / 21:2a moves the predicate from the fool to every man. Heim reads 20:20–21:4 as appearance versus real conviction on independent grounds.

These stand or fall on exegesis of those texts. Null models over counts say nothing against them.

**The boundary question, resolved on textual grounds.** 15:33 is fully theocentric and sits awkwardly in 1A. Scoralick, whose analysis stops at 15:32, concluded that it belongs with the second half of the collection, and the evidence she cites is its repetition in 18:12 and 22:4 — the pair listed above. Heim adds that the function of the 15:28–33 cluster carries forward into 16:1–15; Waltke independently places 15:30–33 as the opening of the second half. The boundary can be set at 15:32 with a stated warrant.

**A methodological result.** Repetition inventories built from English translations miss pairs like 10:29b = 21:15b, where the partner cola are rendered very differently. Lemma-level work on the Hebrew is not a refinement here but a requirement.

## Errors Corrected in the Course of the Work

Recorded because the corrections are part of the finding.

1. **15:13 → 17:22** was read as moving from outward appearance to inward effect. The added lemmas are גֵּהָה (healing), יבשׁ (to dry) and גֶּרֶם (bone) — physiological, not perceptual. Reclassified as development.
2. **The interiority count** was stated as five independent cases. Heim's cluster analysis shows 18:11 and 18:12 occupy one site, reducing it to four — before the control removed the basis for the claim altogether.
3. **Chapter-density figures** ("roughly six times denser in chapters 16 and 18") compared small counts without a baseline. Against a baseline the differences dissolve.
4. **Heim's cluster boundary at 16:16** was presented as bottom-up corroboration of the hinge. With 47 of 58 clusters beginning mid-chapter, and Test 4 showing that junction to be unremarkable, the corroboration does not hold.
5. **The 12:15a→21:2a pair** is one arm of a three-member variant including 16:2a; the triad, not the pair, is the unit.

***

## Open Questions

1. **Direction of dependence is not demonstrable.** Everything here reads the final form. Nothing establishes which occurrence was written first.
2. **The blind spot remains.** Repetition carried by synonym, root-play or syntactic echo is invisible to lemma matching; 18:11–12 is a demonstrated instance. Snell's catalogue and Waltke's excursus should be checked for pairs of this kind, which would enlarge the inventory though not obviously change the statistical picture.
3. **A salience-weighted test is still available.** The observation that boundary-crossing pairs use higher-salience motive vocabulary was set aside as post hoc. With a salience ranking committed in advance, it could be tested properly.
4. **Local structure was not tested.** The null models permute freely and so are conservative about local clustering. A block-preserving null would test whether repetitions cluster locally, irrespective of the hinge.
5. **Heim's own follow-up.** He identifies the purpose of variant repetitions as an open question in the 2001 volume and addresses it in *Poetic Imagination in Proverbs* (2013), which should be read before this material is pressed further.

## Appendix: Method Notes

Source: OpenScriptures `morphhb`, Westminster Leningrad Codex with Strong's numbering and morphological parsing, OSIS XML.

Lemma normalisation: compound strings of the form `l/2416 e` split on `/`, numeric components retained, homograph letters preserved (`7389 a` → `7389a`). Only segments whose aligned morphology code begins with N, V or A counted as content lemmas.

Colon division: the first word carrying U+0591 (ETNAHTA) closes colon A; the remainder forms colon B. The 12 verses without an atnach were treated as single units.

Scoring: Jaccard index on content-lemma sets, |A∩B| / |A∪B|.

Null models: colon positions permuted uniformly at random across 10:1–22:16, preserving block and cluster sizes, with the adjacency filter re-applied each iteration. 5,000 permutations for Test 1, 4,000 for Test 3, 400 size-matched draws per junction for Test 4. Random seeds fixed for reproducibility.

Ranges: 1A = 10:1–15:33 (368 cola); hinge = 16:1–15 (30 cola); 1B = 16:16–22:16 (352 cola).
