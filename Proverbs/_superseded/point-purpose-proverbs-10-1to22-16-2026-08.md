# Point & Purpose: Proverbs 10:1–22:16

**Sermon 5 of 9** — *"The Proverbs of Solomon (1)"* · **Translation:** ESV (Anglicised)
**Exegesis consumed:** `dig-deeper-proverbs-10-1to22-16` (whole-section run) · **Series backbone:** `point-purpose-proverbs-series-introduction` (v2)
**Date:** 26 August 2026

---

## Phase 0 — the scope decision

**A single sermon on 375 verses — the longest unit in the series — and the scope is settled, not open.**

The governing rationale is recorded in the series backbone: *people hear the book in far too many fragments, so that they miss the whole point of the book being just that — a book.* **The sermon therefore follows one thread through Solomon I rather than surveying it.**

**Two things make that viable here, and they are peculiar to this unit.**

**First, the received text does the dividing for you.** BHS carries **four markers in 375 verses** — the lowest paragraphing density anywhere in Proverbs, one per ninety-four verses:

| Paragraph | Verses |
|---|---:|
| 10:1 — the superscription, set off alone | 1 |
| 10:2 → 13:25 | 115 |
| **14:1 → 19:9** | **162** |
| 19:10 → 21:30 | 80 |
| 21:31 → 22:16 | 17 |

**A transmitted paragraph of 162 verses is itself the argument for preaching this whole.** The text does not present Solomon I as a set of topics. Anyone dividing it into sub-units is imposing a structure the transmission declines to supply.

**Second, the section has a movement, and the movement is the sermon.** It opens sorting the world into two columns and ends by saying the reader cannot sort himself. That is a single arc, and it cannot be heard in a fragment.

> **⚠ Do not use 15:33/16:1 as the sermon's hinge.** The older half-runs divide there and the project's own habit is to reach for it. **No marker falls there**, and the Masoretic paragraph runs straight through it, 14:1 → 19:9. **Chapter 16 is the crest, not the boundary.**

---

## Fallen Condition Focus

> **We want the moral order to place us — to tell us which kind of person we are — and we cannot read our own position in it.**

**Anchored, and the anchors are the section's own.** `[T]` The collection spends eleven chapters connecting character to consequence, and then breaks the instrument from inside:

- **14:12** — *"There is a way that seems right to a man, but its end is the way to death."*
- **16:2** — *"All the ways of a man are pure in his own eyes, but the LORD weighs the spirit."*
- **20:9** — *"Who can say, 'I have made my heart clean; I am pure from my sin'?"*
- **21:2** — *"Every way of a man is right in his own eyes, but the LORD weighs the heart."*

**This is the series FCF at its sharpest.** The self-assessment thread — *bəʿênāyw*, "in his own eyes" — stands at **12:15 · 16:2 · 21:2**, evenly spaced across the section and crossing the old divide. The congregation arrives wanting the two columns to place them. The section will not do it.

---

## Teaching Point

> **The moral order is real and God stands behind it — and the same collection that spends eleven chapters proving it tells you twice that you are not the one who can read where you stand in it.**

**Subject:** Does the world reward character, and can I tell which side of that I am on?
**Complement:** Yes it does, and no you cannot — because the weighing is the LORD's.

**Why this is one idea and not two.** The two halves are the section's own two movements, and the second is unintelligible without the first. Eleven chapters of sorting are not undercut by 16:2; they are what gives 16:2 its force. **Take away the sorting and "the LORD weighs the spirit" is a platitude; take away 16:2 and the sorting becomes a self-audit.** The sermon needs both, in that order.

---

## Purpose Statement

> **To move the congregation from using Proverbs as a device for grading themselves and others to receiving it as a true description of a world they do not adjudicate** — so that the confident lose the instrument they have been scoring themselves with, the anxious are relieved of a verdict they were never qualified to give, and both are handed to the one who does the weighing.

**Note the direction of the relief.** `[T]` The report's own phrasing: **16:2 is "a relief before it is a threat."** A congregation that has been quietly auditing itself for years is being told to stop, and the reason is not that the standard has been lowered.

**Differentiated:**

- **For the capable** — you have been reading the two columns and placing yourself in the right one. 21:2 says the instrument is broken, and it is your instrument.
- **For the anxious** — you have been reading the two columns and placing yourself in the wrong one. The same verse applies, and it takes the pen out of your hand too.
- **For the church** — the two-column world is corporate, not merely private: **11:10–11**, where the city rejoices. This is not a sermon about private morality.
- **For the enquirer** — that character and outcome are connected is a testable claim about the world, and most people have found it broadly true and painfully uneven. Proverbs says both.

## Candidate Big Question

**The series question, asked in these words in every sermon:**

> **"How would you know if you were actually wise?"**

**This week's form, and its answer:**

> **"Which column are you in — and who told you?"**

> *Answer:* **You would want the sort to place you, and the collection breaks the sort from inside. "All the ways of a man are pure in his own eyes, but the LORD weighs the spirit."**

*Flagged as candidate: final wording belongs to the manuscript stage.*

## Proposed Structure

**Three movements, following the transmitted paragraphs rather than the chapter numbers.**

**1 · The world sorted · 10:2–13:25** *(the first transmitted paragraph, 115 verses)*
*Function: establish the claim, and let it be as bracing as it is.* Antithetical parallelism at its densest in the book; the righteous and the wicked in two columns; the *tôʿēbâ* sayings beginning their run at 11:1 and 11:20. **Preach this as true.** The temptation is to hedge it early because the congregation knows life is not so tidy — but the section's own second movement does that work, and doing it here spoils both.

**2 · The crest · 14:1–19:9, centred on 16:1–9** *(the 162-verse paragraph)*
*Function: establish the Teaching Point.* Something happens across this stretch that the chapter numbers hide: **the righteous/wicked vocabulary thins out as the divine name takes over, and chapter 16 is where they cross — *ṣaddîq* at zero, YHWH at its maximum.** Land on 16:1–9 as the section's own centre of gravity, and on 16:2: *the LORD weighs the spirit.*
*⚠ Say "in the Hebrew".* The chapter-16 divine-name cluster is Masoretic; the Greek lacks 16:1, 3, 4 and 6.

**3 · The auditor disqualified · 19:10–22:16** *(the last two paragraphs, 97 verses)*
*Function: press the response.* 20:9 — *"Who can say, I have made my heart clean?"* — and 21:2, the third and last of the "in his own eyes" sayings. **The section does not resolve into a technique.** It stops at 22:16, on oppressing the poor to enrich oneself, with no echo of its own opening: **Solomon I is framed at its head and simply stops at its foot.** Let the sermon feel that.

---

## Where Christ comes in, and how

**Contrast, and it is structural rather than verbal.** `[I]` There is no messianic verse to reach for here, and reaching for one would be the error. The connection is the shape of the whole:

> **A collection spends 375 verses connecting character to outcome, and then says twice that no one can audit his own character.** That is a gap the section opens and does not close.

**1 Corinthians 1:30 is what closes it** — *"he became to us wisdom from God, righteousness and sanctification and redemption."* Both halves of Solomon I's problem are answered in one clause: **the wisdom the section has been teaching, and the righteous standing it has just told you that you cannot verify.** `[S]`

**Say it in the third movement, not the first.** If Christ arrives before the auditor has been disqualified, he answers a question the congregation has not yet felt. The sermon's whole design is to make 20:9 land before 1 Corinthians 1:30 is offered.

---

## Keep Honest

Five guardrails, from the report's Preaching Pitfalls, all of them specific to this unit.

1. **Do not quote the whole-section antithesis average.** 50.1% is arithmetically true and homiletically false. **The two halves are formally opposite, and the sermon exists to show the change.**
2. **Do not make 15:33/16:1 the hinge.** No marker falls there; the transmitted paragraph runs through it. Chapter 16 is a **crest**, not a boundary — and preaching it as a boundary reintroduces exactly the fragmentation this series is built against.
3. **Do not preach the two columns and stop.** Eleven chapters of sorting, then 16:2 and 21:2. **A sermon that ends at the sorting has ended one movement early** — and will send people home grading themselves, which is the opposite of the purpose.
4. **Do not address the congregation in the second person throughout.** `[T]` The measured figure is **one imperative in the 184 verses of 10:1–15:33, and no vocative** — the descriptive register is a decision, not an accident. The section describes a world; it does not issue commands. A sermon that turns every saying into an instruction has changed its genre.
5. **Do not use the Greek pluses from the pulpit without saying what they are.** Nineteen verse-units in Solomon I have no Hebrew counterpart. **15:27a, on almsgiving cleansing sin, is not in the Hebrew.** Mention it only with that stated.

## Anti-Moralism Gate — checked

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Is the payoff "be like X" or "trust the Christ whom X points to"? | **Trust.** The sermon's design removes the hearer's capacity to grade himself before offering anything, so "be more righteous" is foreclosed by movement 3. |
| Is Christ the subject or merely an example? | **Subject.** 1 Cor 1:30 makes him the actor — *he became to us* wisdom and righteousness. |
| Is the good news grounded in Christ's saving work? | **Yes** — righteousness received rather than assessed, which is precisely the gap Solomon I opens. |

**Passes.** *The specific risk here is the reverse of moralism's usual form: not "try harder", but "score yourself accurately". Movement 3 exists to close that off.*
