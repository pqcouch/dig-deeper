# Point & Purpose: Proverbs 30:1–31:9

**Sermon 8 of 9** — *"The two 'kings'"* · **Translation:** ESV (Anglicised)
**Exegesis consumed:** `dig-deeper-proverbs-30-1to31-9` · **Series backbone:** `point-purpose-proverbs-series-introduction` (v2)
**Date:** 26 August 2026

---

## Phase 0 — the scope decision

**A single sermon on 42 verses** — the shortest unit in the series apart from the preamble, and the most heavily worked by the transmission.

**Nine paragraph markers in forty-two verses — one per 4.7.** `[T]` Against one per 184 in Solomon 1A, that is a **thirty-nine-fold** difference, and it is the steep end of a gradient that runs the length of the book. **The text is telling you where to breathe, and there are nine places.**

**Why Agur and Lemuel belong in one sermon rather than two.** They are two oracles by two non-Israelites, and it would be natural to split them. **The transmitted text argues against it**, and so does the shape:

> **30:32 — *yād lə-peh*, "hand on the mouth."** **פ** **31:8, 9 — *pətaḥ pîkā … pətaḥ pîkā*, "open your mouth… open your mouth."**

**The reversal sits exactly on a paragraph marker.** `[T]` One oracle ends by closing a mouth; the next begins by being told twice to open one. **That is the sermon, and it does not exist if you preach them apart.**

---

## Fallen Condition Focus

> **We want the conditions in which we can go on trusting God, and we cannot tell which conditions those are — so we ask for the ones we want and call it faith.**

**Anchored, and stated by the text itself.** `[T]` **30:8–9** names both failure-modes and **ranks neither**:

> *"Give me neither poverty nor riches… lest I be full and deny you and say, 'Who is the LORD?' or lest I be poor and steal and profane the name of my God."*

Too much and too little are equally dangerous, and Agur does not know which he is being handed. **The same concern extends to power** at 30:32 and 31:4–5 — the king who drinks and forgets what has been decreed.

**Shared entirely with the first hearers.** What differs is that we have a Lord who was **in both conditions** — hungry in the wilderness and refusing to make bread from stones, and rich, becoming poor — and who prayed the prayer through.

---

## Teaching Point

> **The book about getting wisdom ends with a man who says he never got it, who takes his stand on a word he did not write and may not edit — and the last thing wisdom does is open its mouth for people who have no voice.**

**Subject:** How does a book about becoming wise end?
**Complement:** In a confession of ignorance, a given word, a prayer for the conditions to keep fearing God — and a mouth opened for the voiceless.

**Why this is one idea.** The section's epistemology is a single move: **no ascent, a given word, and a prayer.** Everything Agur *knows* he learned from a text he did not write (30:5–6); everything he *does not* know he lists at length (30:2–4, 18–19). The opened mouth of 31:8–9 is not a change of subject — it is what that epistemology produces.

---

## Purpose Statement

> **To bring a congregation that has spent eight weeks learning wisdom to the place where the book's own last sage says he has not learned it** — so that they stop estimating their competence, receive Scripture as given rather than edited, pray for less, and open their mouths for people with no standing.

**Differentiated:**

- **For the capable** — you have been acquiring wisdom for eight weeks. The book's final sage opens by saying *"I have not learned wisdom, nor have I knowledge of the Holy One."* He is not disqualified by it. Neither are you.
- **For the anxious** — 30:8–9 will be, for most hearers, the first time they have heard someone ask God for **less**. It is a prayer you are allowed to pray.
- **For the person with a platform** — 30:32 and 31:4–5. The mouth is the organ this section is about, and power is what makes it dangerous.
- **For the church** — 31:8–9 is specific about who: the mute, *"the sons of passing away"*, the poor and the needy. **Not a general disposition of kindness.**

## Candidate Big Question

**The series question, asked in these words in every sermon:**

> **"How would you know if you were actually wise?"**

**This week's form, and its answer:**

> **"What does wisdom sound like when it finally speaks?"**

> *Answer:* **The wisest man in the book says he does not know — and then receives a word he did not write, and prays. And the last thing the book's wisdom does with a mouth is open it for someone who has none.**

*Flagged as candidate: final wording belongs to the manuscript stage.*

## Proposed Structure

**Four movements, on the transmitted paragraphs, hinged at the mouth.**

**1 · "I have not learned wisdom" · 30:1–6**
*Function: dismantle the eight weeks, then supply what replaces them.* The confession (30:2–3), the unanswerable question (30:4), and the canon-formula (30:5–6): *"Every word of God proves true… do not add to his words."*
> **⚠ Preach 30:1–6 as one paragraph, because that is what it is.** `[T]` The received paragraphing runs **30:1–6 unbroken** and breaks at 30:6/30:7. **This is textual evidence against the standard two-source reading** that makes 30:1–4 a sceptic's manifesto and 30:5–6 an orthodox correction bolted on. The doubt and the confidence are one man's speech.

**2 · "Give me neither poverty nor riches" · 30:7–9**
*Function: press the FCF exactly.* **The only prayer in the book**, and the one place where the sermon should slow right down. Note that both halves have reasons attached, and that neither condition is the safe one.

**3 · The things that are never satisfied · 30:10–33**
*Function: the diagnosis.* **The section's subject is appetite**, carried by three interlocking Hebrew roots the English cannot show as one — √ʾKL "eat/devour", √ŚBʿ "be satisfied", √ŠTH "drink". Devouring the poor (30:14), being devoured by ravens (30:17), the leech's daughters and the four that never say "Enough" (30:15–16), the adulteress who eats and wipes her mouth (30:20). **The movement ends where appetite ends: 30:32 — hand on the mouth.**

**4 · "Open your mouth" · 31:1–9**
*Function: the reversal, and the response.* Across a single paragraph marker the posture flips. Lemuel's mother — **unnamed, and the only extended instruction in the book attributed to a woman** — warns him off wine and women, and then tells him **twice** what his mouth is for: *"Open your mouth for the mute… open your mouth, judge righteously, defend the rights of the poor and needy."* **The book that began with the fear of the LORD (1:7) ends with it looking like an opened mouth.**

## Where Christ comes in, and how

**Trajectory and contrast, with one point of near-direct fulfilment — and it is strong.**

**1 · 30:4, and John 3:13 — the primary route.** `[T]` on the correspondence; `[I]` *moderate–high* on the verdict. Agur asks: *"Who has ascended to heaven and come down? … What is his name, and what is his son's name?"* **John 3:13:** *"No one has ascended into heaven except he who descended from heaven, the Son of Man."*

**The verbal correspondence — ascend / descend — is exact, and John's sentence is formally an answer to a question of exactly this shape.** All four tests pass or nearly pass; the escalation is the striking part: **Agur asks who has *ascended*, and the gospel answers with one who *descended first*.** *Verdict: trajectory, strongly warranted, not direct prophecy.* **Say it that way.**

**2 · 30:8–9 answered in a life.** `[I]` The prayer asks for the conditions in which trust remains possible. **Christ was in both conditions and kept trusting in each** — hungry in the wilderness and refusing to turn stones into bread, and *"though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor"* (2 Cor 8:9). The prayer Agur could only pray, he prayed through.

**3 · 31:8–9 and Jeremiah 22:16.** `[I]` The motive the text supplies is not "because the vulnerable deserve it" but Jeremiah's: *"He judged the cause of the poor and needy… Is not this to know me? declares the LORD."* **Doing this is what knowing God consists of** — which is why the opened mouth is the fear of the LORD arriving at its destination, and why Luke 4 reads as it does.

---

## Keep Honest

**1 · Do not preach 31:8–9 as a standalone social-justice text.** It is the closing move of a book, spoken by a queen mother to a king about the office he holds, and it is grounded in what knowing God consists of. **Lifted out, it becomes a slogan; left in, it is the book's last word about the fear of the LORD.**

**2 · Do not preach "neither poverty nor riches" as an endorsement of the comfortable middle.** 30:8–9 is a prayer about **spiritual danger in both directions**, offered by a man who does not know which way he will be tested. A congregation that hears it as "God approves of moderate prosperity" has heard the opposite of a prayer for dependence.

**3 · Do not preach Agur's humility without knowing that an ancient tradition deleted it.** `[T]` **MT 30:3:** *"I have **not** learned wisdom, **nor** have I knowledge of the Holy One."* **LXX 30:3 makes him affirm it** — God has taught him wisdom. **The Greek reverses the polarity, and the negation is the whole point of the Hebrew.** If you preach the confession, you are preaching a text that some ancient readers found intolerable — and saying so makes the point sharper, not weaker.

**4 · Do not preach the numerical sayings as lists of vices, and do not skip 30:22–23.** The "three things… four" pattern is a wisdom form for teaching observation, not a catalogue of sins. And 30:22–23 — the servant who becomes king, the maid who displaces her mistress — reads as an embarrassment only when applied to other people. **Read on oneself, it belongs with 30:8–9: it is about not being able to survive one's own elevation.**

## Anti-Moralism Gate — checked

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Is the payoff "be like X" or "trust the Christ whom X points to"? | **Trust.** Movement 1 removes competence as the ground before anything is asked; the closing imperative in movement 4 is supplied with Jeremiah's motive — this *is* knowing God — rather than left as a bare duty. |
| Is Christ the subject or merely an example? | **Subject.** 30:4's question has no human answer, and John 3:13 supplies the actor: the one who descended first. Christ answers Agur; he does not model Agur. |
| Is the good news grounded in Christ's saving work? | **Yes** — 2 Cor 8:9, the one who became poor, standing behind a prayer about poverty and riches that no hearer can guarantee for himself. |

**Passes.** *The live risk is movement 4 detaching into activism. The guard is Jeremiah 22:16, and it is in the text.*

## Hand-off

The Big Question and the four movements feed the one-sentence-sermon and manuscript stage.

**Not decided here:** how much of the appetite word-thread to make explicit (three Hebrew roots is one more than most congregations will hold, and the English cannot show them as one), and whether to draw out the link at 31:1 — *"an oracle that his mother **taught** him"* is *yissərattû*, **√יסר, the verb behind 1:8's *mûsar***, so the book's last teacher is doing what its opening verse asked for. Strong, and it closes the frame neatly; but it is a second structural point in a movement that already carries the reversal.
