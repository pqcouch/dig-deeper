## Phase 0 — the scope decision

**A single sermon on 249 verses.** That is four times the length at which this work would normally split a unit into a series, and it is the right call here for one reason: **this is the third visit to the frame, not the first.** The series has already fixed the division of labour, and it holds:

| Sermon | The question it owns | Material that is its alone |
|---|---|---|
| 2 · 1:1–7 | **What is this book for?** | The purpose statement, the named addressee, the motto |
| 3 · ch. 2 | **How is wisdom got?** | The one-sentence chapter; the *kî* of 2:6; the two deliverances |
| **4 · 1:8–9:18** | **Who is calling?** | **The two women and their crossing paths · the three escalating public calls (1:20–21 → 8:1–3 → 9:3) · the father falling silent after 7:27 · chapter 9 as the hinge, with no vocative in it** |

**So this sermon does not survey chapters 1–9. It follows one thread through them** — the voices — and leaves the lecture content to the two sermons that have already done it. *What it is for · how you get it · who is calling.* Kept to those three, they do not compete.

**Consequence for the preparation:** you will read all 249 verses and preach perhaps thirty. The four movements below name the verses that carry the thread; everything else is summarised in transit.

## Fallen Condition Focus

> **We are being talked into things all week, and we assume we can tell a good offer from a bad one by how it sounds. This section says we cannot — because the two invitations in it are word for word the same.**

**Anchored, and countably so.** `[T]` **Proverbs 9:4 and 9:16 are the identical sentence** — *"Whoever is simple, let him turn in here!"* — spoken by Wisdom and by Folly, from houses on the same street, each with a table laid. Around it: **five occurrences of "smooth" speech**, **four quoted seductions**, and a rival rhetoric offered as the only antidote. The section's working assumption about the *pethî*, the simple, is that **the last voice wins**.

This is the shared concern, not an imported felt-need. The first hearers were a generation being handed responsibility in a society whose institutions could be corrupted at every point the book later names. **Ours is a congregation that has been persuaded of several things since Monday.**

## Teaching Point

> **Two voices call the simple from the same street in the same words — and only one of them was there when the world was made, and has since come out into the street in person.**

**Subject:** Who is calling, and how would you know which voice to obey?
**Complement:** Two, indistinguishable by their invitations — and the one that gives life is the Wisdom of God, present at creation and now come to us as a person.

**Why this is the dominant idea and not one of five.** It is where the convergent findings meet: Wisdom takes the father's own vocative formula word for word (8:32 ∥ 5:7, 7:24); the father falls silent after 7:27 and the last two chapters belong to the women; the lectures are indoors and the interludes outdoors, and **chapter 9 fuses the two** — a house *and* a street. The section is built to end with the reader standing between two doors.

---

## Purpose Statement

> **To move the congregation from trying to assess offers by how they sound, to knowing the voice that is speaking** — so that the self-assured stop trusting their own ear, the person currently being talked into something can name which house they are walking towards, and everyone hears that Wisdom's banquet has been reissued and the servants are already out.

**Differentiated:**

- **For the capable** — the sermon's sharpest edge. You are good at reading people. 9:4 and 9:16 are the same sentence; your ear is not the instrument you think it is.
- **For the person being persuaded** — a scheme, an affair, a resentment. Chapter 7 is the most pastorally useful passage in Proverbs for that conversation *precisely because it does not moralise*. Let it do its work without adding to it.
- **For the church** — Wisdom cries in the street (1:20–21; 8:1–3), not only in the house. A congregation that only ever speaks indoors has half her practice.
- **For the enquirer** — 9:4 and 9:16 are a claim about the world that can be tested against experience, and most people have a story about the time they could not tell the difference.

## Candidate Big Question

**The series question, asked in these words in every sermon:**

> **"How would you know if you were actually wise?"**

**This week's form, and its answer:**

> **"Two invitations, word for word the same — whose voice have you been listening to longest?"**

> *Answer:* **You wouldn't know by auditing yourself. Wisdom is not a quality you check; it is a voice you obey — and the way you tell the voices apart is not by how they sound but by who is speaking.**

## Proposed Structure

Four movements, tracking the section's own shape — the three escalating public calls, and the silence that falls between the third and the last.

**1 · The first cry, and nobody stops · 1:8–33**
*Function: surface the need.* The gang's recruitment speech (1:11–14 — *"throw in your lot among us; we will all have one purse"*) set beside Wisdom's first public cry (1:20–33). Two invitations, one street, and the section's opening verdict on the simple: they are already being recruited, and **persuasion is the real battlefield.**

**2 · The father talks — and then stops · 2:1–7:27**
*Function: establish that the human voice runs out.* Move through the lectures at pace, showing they are one cumulative argument rather than ten topics (chapter 2 announced a programme; chapters 3–7 execute it in order). Then the finding that carries the movement: **nineteen vocatives run from 1:8 to 8:32, and the father's last words are at 7:27.** He has said everything he has, and it is not enough to settle the matter. *This is where the sermon refuses to end.*

**3 · The voice that was there first · 8:1–36**
*Function: establish the Teaching Point.* Wisdom cries from the same public places (8:1–3), and then does something the father never could — **she takes his own formula: "And now, O sons, listen to me" (8:32 ∥ 5:7; 7:24)** — and grounds her claim not in experience but in creation (8:22–31). **The answer to "who is calling?" is: someone who was there before the street was.**

**4 · Two doors on the same street · 9:1–18**
*Function: press the response.* Two houses, two tables, two servants sent out, and **the identical sentence at 9:4 and 9:16.** Note what is missing: **chapter 9 has no vocative.** The father is not telling anyone what to do. The choice has been handed over — and the sermon lands where the section lands, at a door.

## Where Christ comes in, and how

**Trajectory, not typology — and the distinction is load-bearing.** `[I]` *High confidence in the verdict.* Running the four tests on *Woman Wisdom as a type of Christ*: theological-category **passes**; NT-precedent passes for the *category* but **fails for this figure** (no New Testament text identifies Woman Wisdom as Christ); escalation **passes**; authorial-pattern **fails**. **Two and a half of four.** The early church read Proverbs 8 with John's *logos* almost unanimously, and the reading is not illegitimate — but it is a canonical reading applied backwards, and pressing it as typology is what hands Arius his argument.

**So preach it as trajectory, in two moves, both in the second half of the sermon:**

1. **The wisdom that calls in the street has been given a name.** Wisdom was present at creation and now stands in public offering herself (8:22–36). The New Testament's claim is that this has come to us as a person, "in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (Col 2:3; cf. 1 Cor 1:24, 30). *Say "the wisdom of God has come out into the street" — do not say "Proverbs 8 is Jesus."*
2. **The banquet has been reissued.** Wisdom builds her house, slaughters her beasts, mixes her wine, sets her table and sends servants to call the simple (9:1–6). Jesus tells of a man who prepared a great banquet, sent his servant to say *"Come, for everything is now ready"*, and — when the invited would not come — **sent him out into the streets to bring in anyone he could find** (Luke 14:21–23). **The invitation in 9:4 is still open, and it is still addressed to the simple.**

**This is what keeps the sermon off moralism.** If the payoff were "listen more carefully", the sermon would have failed on its own terms, because 9:4 and 9:16 are the same sentence and careful listening cannot separate them. **The resolution is not a better ear; it is that one of the two speakers has come out and made herself known.**

## Keep Honest

Four guardrails, from the report's Preaching Pitfalls, chosen for *this* sermon.

1. **Do not stop before chapter 9.** The commonest structural failure with chapters 1–9, and fatal here: chapter 9 is where the two women are finally set side by side and where the vocatives stop. **A sermon on this section that ends at chapter 8 has cut off the ending the section was built for.**
2. **Do not preach from the father's chair.** The pull of ten lectures is towards the preacher as the wise parent addressing the young. But **the father falls silent at 7:27** — the section itself retires that voice before the end. Preach as one of the simple being called, not as the one calling.
3. **Do not allegorise Woman Wisdom.** See above. Trajectory, not typology; and never say Proverbs 8 *is* Christ. The four-test result is two and a half of four, and the Arian history is the reason the distinction matters.
4. **Do not turn chapters 5–7 into a sex talk.** They are in this sermon as the strange woman's journey from abstraction to flesh and back to abstraction — the second voice acquiring a body and then being revealed as Folly at 9:13–18. **Chapter 7 works pastorally because it does not moralise; do not repair that.**

## Anti-Moralism Gate — checked

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Is the payoff "be like X" or "trust the Christ whom X points to"? | **Trust.** The payoff is a door, and the one who opens it is the Wisdom of God who has come. Not "listen harder" — the text forecloses that at 9:4 ∥ 9:16. |
| Is Christ the subject or merely an example? | **Subject.** Movements 3 and 4 make the actor the one who was there at creation and who now sends servants into the street. |
| Is the good news grounded in Christ's saving work? | **Yes** — the reissued banquet (Luke 14:21–23), where the invitation goes out to those who were not looking. |

**Passes.**
