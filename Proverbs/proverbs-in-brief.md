## The book in one line

> **Proverbs will make you competent and then refuse to let you certify it — because wisdom is a gift the LORD gives, and the wisdom he finally gave was Christ.**

**The Big Question, asked in every sermon:** ***"How would you know if you were actually wise?"***

The nine answers below are not nine restatements. The question stays fixed; the answer develops. That is what makes this a series rather than nine sermons on one book.

---

## The spine — eight things the whole book is doing

From `proverbs-dig-deeper-macro-synthesis`. One line each; each is argued there with anchors and confidence.

1. **The transmitted division-points are older than the Masoretes — and they mark genre, not collection.** Where the received text breaks is not where the superscriptions break.
2. **The book gives the reader competence and then systematically denies him the ability to certify it.** 14:12 · 16:2 · 20:9 · 21:2 · 26:12 · 28:11 · 30:2–3.
3. **The frame closes.** At least six verbal welds tie the last chapter back to the first nine.
4. **The speech-ethic runs from restraint to the opened mouth** — and the turn is marked in the transmitted text, not just felt.
5. **Wisdom is progressively subordinated to revelation**, and the last move is a canon-formula (30:5–6).
6. **The sentence collections alternate rather than progress** — four independent formal measures say so.
7. **The addressee appears, disappears, and returns**, and the pattern is structural rather than incidental.
8. **There is almost no typology in Proverbs** — and that is a finding about the book, not a failure of method.

> **⚠ One thing to hold carefully.** Arguments **5** and **6** pull in opposite directions on whether the book *develops*. 5 says wisdom is **progressively** subordinated to revelation; 6 says the sentence collections **alternate rather than progress**.
>
> **They are differently scoped, so they do not strictly contradict** — 5 tracks four pericopes across the whole book, 6 tracks the four Solomonic sub-collections only. But a sermon leaning hard on both at once will sound as though it cannot decide whether Proverbs goes somewhere. **Pick the one your text supports, and say which.**
>
> *(An earlier note in this project put the tension between arguments 1 and 6. That was loose: argument 1 — division-points mark genre — and argument 6 — the sub-collections are formally distinct — actually support each other.)*

**Two gradients worth carrying in your head.** `[T]` Paragraphing gets **denser** as the book goes on — one marker per 94 verses in Solomon I, one per 4.7 in Agur and Lemuel, a twenty-fold change. And the Greek **expands the frame most and 30–31 not at all**, which is the reverse of where it does its rearranging.

## 1 · Introduction — the whole book

**Doing:** planting the question, and showing that the book is designed rather than collected.
**The one finding:** Proverbs is not an anthology that later acquired a frame. It is a designed book, and the design shows in three registers that do not depend on each other.
**Big Question:** *You wouldn't — and that is the point. This book ends in a confession, not a diploma.*
**Christ:** 1 Cor 1:30 — he became to us wisdom from God.
**Trap:** survey sermons drift into lecture. Give it one text to land on.

---

## 2 · Why this book exists — 1:1–7

**Doing:** naming the addressee, before anything is asked of him.
**The one finding:** the preamble is bracketed by a single word-pair — *ḥokmâ ûmûsār*, "wisdom and instruction", at v.2 and again at v.7 — **and the bracket is the argument.**
**Big Question:** *You would start by admitting you are the one it is addressed to. It is written for **the simple**, not the sage — and its motto is the fear of the LORD.*
**Christ:** "something greater than Solomon" (Matt 12:42); the wisdom of God (1 Cor 1:24, 30); "in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (Col 2:3).
**Trap:** preaching 1:7a and never quoting 1:7b — the fear of the LORD as general reverence, with the fools who despise instruction quietly dropped.

---

## 3 · The agenda — chapter 2

**Doing:** setting the posture that everything after it assumes.
**The one finding:** the chapter is **one sentence** — three conditions (*if*, vv.1, 3, 4), *then* (v.5), **for** (v.6), *then* (v.9), **for** (v.10), and two purpose-infinitives of rescue. **v.6 is not an aside; it is what grounds the search.**
**Big Question:** *You would not know by checking. You would know by **receiving** — "**for** the LORD gives wisdom" (2:6) grounds the search rather than rewarding it.*
**Christ:** 1 Cor 1:30 — wisdom received, not achieved.
**Trap:** turning vv.1–4 into a programme — receive, treasure, incline, call out, seek, search — with v.6 as an encouraging aside. That inverts the chapter.

---

## 4 · Two voices in the street — 1:8–9:18

**Doing:** extracting allegiance rather than behaviour.
**The one finding:** **chapter 2 announces a programme and chapters 3–7 execute it in order.** Ten lectures and five interludes; and in chapter 9 Wisdom and Folly call from the same height, both with a house and a table.
**Big Question:** *Wisdom is not a quality you audit; it is **a voice you obey** — and two of them are calling in the same street.*
**Christ:** Wisdom's banquet reissued as an invitation that goes out to those who were not looking (Luke 14:21–23).
**Trap:** each chapter as a topic — money week, sex week, friendship week — with no cumulative argument.
*Greek note:* the frame is the most expanded part of the book, and 9:18a–d gives it a four-verse peroration the Hebrew does not have.

---

## 5 · The Proverbs of Solomon (1) — 10:1–22:16

**Doing:** showing the change across the collection, not averaging it.
**The one finding:** the received text divides Solomon I into **four paragraphs — and none of them is where this project had been cutting.** Across the collection the two vocabularies cross: *ṣaddîq* falls to zero as YHWH peaks at chapter 16.
**Big Question:** *You would want the two-column sort to place you — and the collection breaks it from inside: "there is a way that seems right" (14:12), "who can say, I have made my heart clean?" (20:9), "**all a man's ways are pure in his own eyes**, but the LORD weighs the spirits" (16:2).*
**Christ:** 1 Cor 1:30 — he became to us wisdom from God, **and righteousness**.
**Trap:** quoting the whole-section antithesis average. 50.1% is arithmetically true and homiletically false: **the two halves are formally opposite, and the sermon exists to show the change.**
*⚠ Say "in the Hebrew".* The chapter-16 divine-name cluster is Masoretic — the Greek lacks 16:1, 3, 4 and 6.

---

## 6 · The Words of the Wise — 22:17–24:34

**Doing:** relocating trust, and admitting where the material came from.
**The one finding:** the Masoretic paragraphing here is **fifteen times denser than in Solomon I** — one marker per twelve verses against one per ninety-four. That density is the explanation the project had been missing.
**Big Question:** *You would know by **where your trust is placed**, not by what you have mastered — wisdom borrowed from Egypt is re-founded at 22:19 on trust in the LORD.*
**Christ:** trajectory rather than type, with one strong typological candidate.
**Trap:** verse-by-verse treatment, one proverb per point, each weighed as a generally-true observation. The unit is paragraphed; preach the paragraphs.

---

## 7 · The Proverbs of Solomon (2) — 25:1–29:27

**Doing:** training judgement for when no authority is in the room, then naming the authority.
**The one finding:** **three silences nest inside one another.** The king vanishes for 91 verses (25:7–28:14), the divine name for 65 (25:23–28:4), the righteous/wicked pair for 57 (25:27–27:27). **Chapters 26–27 — fifty-five verses — sit inside all three**, and 26:4–5 is at the centre. The silence ends at a paragraph break: *tôrâ* returns at 28:4, the LORD at 28:5.
**Big Question:** *You would have to judge for yourself — and 25–27 **withholds** the verdict to make you. Then 28–29 hands the standard back, and it is **tôrāh**.*
**Christ:** 25:21–22, the Covenant Code's enemy-duty, is where the NT goes for love of enemies (Rom 12:20; behind Matt 5:44). And **29:23 — honour to the lowly in spirit — is answered in Luke 14:11 and Phil 2:8–9.**
**Trap:** preaching the silence as the message without saying that it ends. The restoration is the point of the withdrawal.

---

## 8 · The two "kings" — 30:1–31:9

**Doing:** unseating the sage at the end of a book about becoming one.
**The one finding:** every block boundary the Greek rearrangement creates falls at a point where the received Hebrew already marks a paragraph — **five seams, five markers.** The most heavily paragraphed unit in the book: one marker per 4.7 verses.
**Big Question:** *The wisest man in the book says **he does not know** — and then receives a word he did not write, and prays.*
**Christ:** 31:8–9 is taken up and intensified by the prophets (Isa 1:17) and by Christ's own programme.
**Trap:** "Proverbs 31:8 — speak up for those who cannot speak for themselves" lifted out as a standalone social-justice text, detached from a king being told what his office is for.

---

## 9 · The closing poem — 31:10–31

**Doing:** letting somebody else say it, and closing the frame.
**The one finding:** Proverbs says **the same thing in the same words** about finding Wisdom (8:35) and finding a wife — and this poem asks that question one last time. The frame closes: at least six verbal welds tie this chapter back to chapters 1–9.
**Big Question:** *You would never say it of yourself. **Somebody else would say it of you** — and the poem's one imperative is "give her praise."*
**Christ:** 1 Cor 1:30 and Col 2:3 — Christ as the wisdom of God, which is what the poem has been circling.
**Trap:** the poem as a standard for Christian womanhood, preached on Mothering Sunday with twenty-two verses turned into a checklist. It is an acrostic about Wisdom embodied, and its own last word is praise.
