# Point & Purpose: Proverbs 22:17–24:34

**Sermon 6 of 9** — *"The Words of the Wise"* · **Translation:** ESV (Anglicised)
**Exegesis consumed:** `dig-deeper-proverbs-22-17to24-34` · **Series backbone:** `point-purpose-proverbs-series-introduction` (v2)
**Date:** 26 August 2026

---

## Phase 0 — the scope decision

**A single sermon on 82 verses**, and for once the length is unremarkable — this is the shortest of the sentence units and sits comfortably inside one sermon.

**What matters here is not scope but register, and the change is abrupt.** `[T]` Last week's unit had **one imperative in 184 verses and no vocative**. This one has:

| | Solomon I (10:1–22:16) | The Words of the Wise (22:17–24:34) |
|---|---|---|
| Vocatives | **none** | **five** (23:15, 19, 26; 24:13, 21) |
| Second person | rare | **38 of 82 verses** |
| Prohibitions | — | **twenty-two *ʾal*-clauses** |
| Motive clauses | — | **twenty-two *kî*-clauses** |
| Paragraph markers | 4 in 375 verses | **7 in 82 — one per twelve** |

**This is the most directly spoken material in the sentence literature, by a wide margin — and the paragraphing is fifteen times denser than Solomon I's.** The preacher who carries last week's method across will flatten it. **Last week described a world; this week addresses a person, and says why each time.**

> **A gift for the series.** Coming straight after Solomon I, the flip in register is itself an argument for preaching the book whole: **the same book changes how it speaks to you, and you only notice if you have just heard the other manner.**

## Fallen Condition Focus

> **We can profit quietly from people who have no advocate — and we can always say we did not realise.**

**Anchored, and not imported.** `[T]` Derived from the collection's own frame:

- **22:22–23** — *"Do not rob the poor… for the LORD will plead their cause."*
- **22:28 and 23:10** — the boundary stone, moved twice; the second time *"do not move the ancient landmark or enter the fields of the fatherless."*
- **23:11** — *"their **Redeemer** is strong; he will plead their cause against you."*
- **24:11–12** — *"Rescue those who are being taken away to death… If you say, 'Behold, **we did not know this**,' does not he who weighs the heart perceive it?"*

**Both halves of the condition are in the text: the quiet profit, and the deniability.** What the first hearers had was the *gōʾēl* as a legal institution with God as its last resort. What we have is a Redeemer who took the role in person.

---

## Teaching Point

> **This collection borrowed the best wisdom the ancient world had, re-founded it on trust in the LORD, and then tested it in the one place it cannot be faked — on behalf of people who have no advocate.**

**Subject:** What makes borrowed wisdom trustworthy?
**Complement:** Not that it works, but that it has been re-grounded on the LORD (22:19) — and it proves itself where no one is watching (22:22–23:11; 24:11–12).

**Why this is one idea.** The collection states its own purpose twice in its opening five verses, and both halves are here: *"that your **trust** may be in the LORD"* (22:19) and *"that you may give a true answer to those who sent you"* (22:21). **Trust, and competence in the service of others.** The sermon is those two clauses.

---

## Purpose Statement

> **To move hearers from holding wisdom as accumulated competence to holding it as trust — and to take away the sentence "I did not realise", so that a congregation able to profit quietly from the unprotected is confronted by the Redeemer who takes their case.**

**Differentiated:**

- **For the capable** — you have acquired a great deal of practical wisdom, much of it from outside the church, and it works. 22:19 does not tell you to discard it. It tells you what it has to be founded on before it is safe.
- **For anyone with power over someone** — an employee, a tenant, a debtor, an elderly relative's paperwork. 23:11 says the other party has counsel, and the counsel is strong.
- **For the church** — 24:11 is corporate before it is individual. **Do not let it become church giving.**
- **For the enquirer** — this is the part of the Bible that most obviously overlaps with the world's own wisdom. Say so, and then say what was done to it.

## Candidate Big Question

**The series question, asked in these words in every sermon:**

> **"How would you know if you were actually wise?"**

**This week's form, and its answer:**

> **"Where is your trust — and would 'I didn't realise' hold?"**

> *Answer:* **You would know by where your trust is placed, not by what you have mastered. Wisdom borrowed from Egypt is re-founded at 22:19 on trust in the LORD — and it is tested at 24:12, where the plea of ignorance is refused.**

*Flagged as candidate: final wording belongs to the manuscript stage.*

## Proposed Structure

**Four movements, following the collection's own frame — trust at the opening, fear at the close — and its one internal seam.**

**1 · Why you are being told this · 22:17–21**
*Function: establish the aim before any content.* The preface states its purpose twice, and neither is mastery: *that your trust may be in the LORD*, and *that you may give a true answer to those who sent you.* **Say here that this material was borrowed** (see Keep Honest 2) — the sermon is stronger for it, not weaker.

**2 · The people who have no advocate · 22:22–23:11**
*Function: press the FCF.* The poor at the gate, the boundary stone moved, the fields of the fatherless — and at 23:11 the word the collection uses **once**: *gōʾēl*, Redeemer. **This is where the sermon's weight falls.**

**3 · "My son, give me your heart" · 23:12–24:22**
*Function: establish the Teaching Point and land Christ.* The direct address at its densest — five vocatives, the wine that bites like a serpent, the envy of sinners refused three times — and then **24:11–12**, the plea of ignorance and the one who weighs hearts. Close on **24:21**, *"Fear the LORD, my son, and the king"*: the collection ends where 1:7 began.

**4 · And these also · 24:23–34**
*Function: a short sting, not a fourth point.* **The transmitted text marks this off** — see below — and it reads as an appendix: partiality in judgement, and the sluggard's field with its broken wall. Keep it brief; the sermon has already landed.

> ### The seam at 24:22 — three witnesses, and they agree
>
> `[T]` Worth ninety seconds from the pulpit if the congregation has been following the series' interest in structure.
>
> 1. **The Hebrew marks it.** 24:22 carries a ***setumah*** — **the only one in this entire project** — falling exactly between the Words of the Wise and *"These also are sayings of the wise."*
> 2. **The Greek breaks there.** The Septuagint's rearrangement interposes 30:1–14 immediately after 24:22.
> 3. **The Greek expands there.** 24:22a–e — five verses on the king's tongue as a sword — is **the longest continuous expansion in the book**, and it sits at the same join.
>
> **Two traditions that arrange this book differently both treat 24:22 as a hinge.** *The third witness is new — see Hand-off.*

## Where Christ comes in, and how

**Trajectory, with one strong candidate — and a resonance that is not a quotation.**

**1 · The Redeemer (23:11) — the primary route.** `[I]` *The strongest Christ-line in the unit.* The *gōʾēl* is the kinsman with both the right and the obligation to take a defenceless relative's case. Proverbs uses the word **once**, and puts it exactly where the fatherless are being defrauded. The trajectory runs through Job 19:25 and Isaiah's Redeemer-title to **Galatians 3:13**. All four tests pass or nearly pass. **Preach it in movement 2:** the people with no advocate turn out to have one, and he is not neutral.

**2 · "We did not know this" (24:11–12) and Matthew 25:44.** `[I]` *Moderate–high, and to be offered as resonance.* **The same excuse, in the same setting, refused the same way** — an obligation to the perishing, a plea of ignorance, and a judge who is not deceived. **The New Testament does not cite Proverbs here.** Say "listen to how Jesus answers the same excuse", not "Jesus is quoting Proverbs."

**3 · "Give me your heart" (23:26).** The teacher asks for the whole person, not compliance. **See Keep Honest 4 before using it** — this line has a trap in it.

## Keep Honest

**1 · Do not preach this as a sentence collection.** Verse-by-verse, one proverb per point, each weighed as a generally-true observation — that is the method Solomon I invites and this unit refuses. **It is paragraphed seven times in 82 verses and it argues.** Preach the paragraphs.

**2 · Do not hide the Egyptian background — and do not count thirty sayings.** `[T]`/`[S]` The material overlaps the *Instruction of Amenemope*, and a series on Proverbs 22–23 that never mentions it is keeping something from the congregation that they can find in any study Bible. **But the two errors come as a pair:** the "thirty sayings" structure is read back from Amenemope's thirty chapters, and **the Hebrew does not deliver thirty units.** Name the borrowing; do not import the enumeration. *What to say: the best wisdom the ancient world had was taken up, and re-founded on the fear of the LORD at 22:19. That is not embarrassment; it is the point.*

**3 · Do not domesticate 24:11.** *"Rescue those who are being taken away to death"* softened into general kindness, or into a line about church giving, removes the verse. It is about people being led away to die, and about the excuse of not having known.

**4 · Do not preach 23:26 in your own voice.** *"My son, give me your heart"* is the teacher speaking. **A preacher who says it as himself is asking the congregation for something no preacher may ask.** Attribute it, and let it point past you.

## Anti-Moralism Gate — checked

**This unit carries the highest moralism risk in the series** — twenty-two prohibitions, five vocatives, thirty-eight verses of second-person address. A sermon here can become a list of instructions almost by accident.

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Is the payoff "be like X" or "trust the Christ whom X points to"? | **Trust** — and the text supplies the guard itself: the stated purpose at 22:19 is *trust*, not conduct, and the prohibitions are given twenty-two motive clauses precisely so they are not bare commands. |
| Is Christ the subject or merely an example? | **Subject.** In movement 2 the actor is the *gōʾēl* who pleads the case — Christ takes the role, he is not offered as a model of taking it. |
| Is the good news grounded in Christ's saving work? | **Yes** — Galatians 3:13, redemption accomplished, standing behind a collection that keeps promising the defenceless an advocate. |

**Passes** — but **the gate needs watching in the manuscript stage more here than anywhere else in the series.** If the sermon's imperatives outnumber its indicatives, it has drifted, however good the individual points.
