# Proverbs — Aide-Mémoire

*For holding the whole in mind while working the detail. From `book-overview-proverbs` v3.0, the nine fresh digs of 18–27 September 2026, the 1:1–9:18 dig v1.1, claim audits 1–2 and the macro-synthesis v2.0. Counts are Hebrew (WLC). Pulpit text assumed: NIV84.*

**In one sentence.** A book that promises to make a young man wise (1:2–6) and whose first instruction about wisdom itself is **"do not be wise in your own eyes" (3:7)**; it argues him into wanting wisdom for nine chapters, hands him five hundred sentences with no addressee and no grader, asks at **20:9** whether anyone can claim a clean heart and never answers, lets its last sage open his mouth to say he never learned wisdom (30:2–3), and closes on a life whose praise is spoken by **other people** (31:28–31). **Pulse: argued for → deposited → withheld → confessed → pronounced from outside; and the verdict never comes from the man himself.**

**Proportions are the argument.** Thirty-one chapters, **915 verses in the WLC and 902 in Swete**. The two sentence collections run to **513 of the 915 — more than half the book — and they address nobody**: no vocative in 10:1–22:16, **0.5% of its verses carrying a second-person form, four paragraph markers in 375 verses**. The frame that teaches a reader how to read them is 256 verses. **Solomon's name stands three times in the whole book, every one in a superscription** (1:1; 10:1; 25:1). **So the bulk of Proverbs has no named hearer and almost no articulation — and the shapelessness is the pedagogy, not a defect of compilation.**

**Nobody inside the book ever passes its test.** *X in his own eyes* governs a self-assessment in **nine verses, one per collection — 3:7; 12:15; 16:2; 21:2; 26:5, 12, 16; 28:11; 30:12** — of which **one is a command and eight are portraits of a man who ignored it**; **26:12 concludes there is more hope for a fool than for him.** The only verdict the book supplies is at the very end and is spoken by somebody else, under a rule it laid down itself at 27:2. The whole rhetoric depends on that gap — and so does the fifth standing trap.

**Its own thesis sentences.**
**1:7** יִרְאַת יְהוָה רֵאשִׁית דָּעַת — the motto, and it is about where wisdom **starts**, not what it earns.
**3:7** אַל־תְּהִי חָכָם בְּעֵינֶיךָ — **the one time the self-assessment formula is a command**; eight case studies follow.
**20:9** מִי־יֹאמַר זִכִּיתִי לִבִּי — *"who can say, 'I have cleansed my heart'?"* **Never answered — and 30:12 reports a generation that says it anyway**, טָהוֹר בְּעֵינָיו and not washed. *The sharpest unadvertised echo in the book.*
**31:30** אִשָּׁה יִרְאַת־יְהוָה — the same two letters-on-the-page as 1:7 and **not the same word**: 1:7 is the noun (3374), 31:30 **the adjective (3373), predicated of a person.** *Make the shift the point.*

**Carry two structural facts.** **Frame and field speak different dialects, and the divine name runs the opposite way from the assumption** — מַעְגָּל ("track") in **seven verses of chs 1–9 and none in 10–31**, against צַדִּיק in 1.6% of the frame's verses and 9.4% of the rest, רָשָׁע 2.7% against 10.6% — **while יְהוָה stands in 7.4% of the frame and 10.3% of the rest.** *Whatever makes chs 1–9 the theological frame, it is not the frequency of the name.* **And two counted gradients turn at 15:33/16:1** — lexical opposition falling **31.5% → 5.2%**, ch. 16 at zero — **while the divine name runs straight across the join: the book's longest consecutive YHWH run is eight verses, 15:33–16:7, straddling the seam.** The MT shows a designed hinge; the Greek does not preserve the MT's order at it.

> **Greek arrangement trap — this book's versification problem.** The Greek relocates whole blocks. **MT 30:1–14 = Swete 24:24–37 · MT 24:23–34 = Swete 24:38–49 · MT 30:15–33 = Swete 24:50–68 · MT 31:1–9 = Swete 24:69–77 · MT 31:10–31 = Swete 29:28–49.** Also **no superscription at all at Swete 10:1**, and **MT 15:33 at Swete 16:4**. Check the edition before calling a Greek reference wrong. *Of the 48 lettered Greek pluses, **none** falls in chs 30–31 — the section the Greek most drastically rearranges is the one it does not expand.*

## Seven movements

**1:1–9:18 · The Long Address** (256 vv.) — purpose statement, motto, paternal speeches, two women who both stand in the street and call. **Nothing here is a proverb; everything is an argument for wanting proverbs.**
**10:1–15:33 · The Two Ways in Two Lines** (184) — the contrast engine: opposition the default form at **31.5%**, **60.3% of verses carrying a character word**, **86.9% waw at the caesura**.
**16:1–22:16 · YHWH, the King, and the Limits of Planning** (191) — opposition collapses to **5.2%**, the name spikes, **12 of Solomon I's 14 king-verses fall here**, and the shape becomes *a man plans, YHWH disposes*.
**22:17–24:34 · The Words of the Wise** (82) — instruction returns sharply: **26.8% of verses carrying אַל, the book's highest by a factor of two**, 48.8% second-person, five vocatives.
**25:1–29:27 · The Hezekiah Transcription** (138) — **the absences are its character**: no מוּסָר, no noun יִרְאָה, no לֵץ (seventeen verses elsewhere), **one דַּעַת (29:7, and it is about the poor)**, six YHWH verses, and **the book's longest divine-name silence, 65 verses, 25:23–28:4.**
**30:1–31:9 · Two Oracles from Outside** (42) — both headed **מַשָּׂא, the only two occurrences in the book**. Agur denies he has wisdom and asks what nobody answers; a queen instructs a king.
**31:10–31 · The Alphabet Closes** (22) — one verse per letter, no superscription, **and no paragraph marker anywhere in it: the longest undivided run in Proverbs.**

**The device:** an anthology of superscribed collections inside three frames the text marks — the **motto** (1:7 → 31:30, restated at 9:10 and 15:33), the **attribution** (Solomon at 1:1, 10:1, 25:1 — "king of Israel" at the first, "king of Judah" at the last), and the **figure** (a woman calling at the gates, 1:20–21; 8:1–3; 9:1–6, answered by a woman whose works praise her in the gates, 31:31). **Seven superscriptions are the only structural signals the book gives in its own voice — and 22:17 is not formatted as one**, sitting inside "incline your ear and hear the words of the wise", with BHS proposing on the Greek's evidence that it be lifted out as a title. **The movements do not build an argument; they stack** — so a series that preaches ch. 15 as following from ch. 12 is preaching a book that is not there.

**Where the Leningrad codex breaks the text.** 52 markers: 50 verse-final פ, one **mid-verse** פ at 10:1 just after the superscription, and the book's **one** ס at 24:22. Density runs **1 per 10.7 verses in chs 1–9 against 1 per 94 in 10:1–22:16**, and **1 per 4.1 in ch. 30 — the most heavily articulated chapter in the book**; ch. 31 has a single marker, at 31:9, exactly where Lemuel ends and the acrostic begins. *Report it as "WLC prints", never as "the Masoretic text".* **And the strongest structural result in the file: six Greek block junctions — after MT 24:22, 30:14, 24:34, 30:33, 31:9 and 29:27 — and every one falls on a Leningrad marker**, the first on the book's only setumah. **Design and rearrangeability are both attested**, so the honest statement is the precise one: *the book we preach is a designed arrangement, and the arrangement is the Masoretic one.* Say it once, early — better from the front than found in a study-Bible footnote.

## The nine units

*⭐ = HIGH: run the full dig before the pulpit.*

**1 · Whole book** ⭐ Introduction: how would you know if you were actually wise?
**2 · 1:1–7** ⭐ The purpose statement and the motto *(bracketed by חָכְמָה וּמוּסָר at 1:2 and 1:7 — three verses in the whole Hebrew Bible, the third 23:23)*
**3 · 2:1–22** The one conditional sentence *(the book's one sentence; 2:6's כִּי is the anti-moralism hinge)*
**4 · 1:8–9:18** ⭐ The long address: two women, two tables *(249 verses, and it contains 8:22–31)*
**5 · 10:1–22:16** ⭐ Solomon's sentences: the world as it usually runs
**6 · 22:17–24:34** The words of the wise: borrowed wisdom, tested
**7 · 25:1–29:27** ⭐ Hezekiah's transcription: wisdom with the name withheld
**8 · 30:1–31:9** ⭐ Two oracles from outside
**9 · 31:10–31** ⭐ The alphabet closes: a life, not a maxim *(raised to HIGH at v3.0)*

**Four notes that change how a unit is preached.** **Unit 5 is one unit with two methods, and the text makes the join itself at 16:25, which reprints 14:12** — the same verse once on each side of the hinge; *use the two-method structure as the sermon's outline, not as a reason to divide it. 375 verses cannot be expounded either way; what a sermon can do is teach how a מָשָׁל works, from the book's own counted shape.* **Unit 7 divides at 27:27 on seven measures** — 25–27 at 4.8% lexical opposition against 28–29's 18.2% — **so preach two movements, not one**; the aggregate 9.4% describes no part of it. *And the withdrawal is datable: the book's last יִרְאַת יְהוָה is 23:17, thirty-four verses before the collection opens.* **Unit 8 carries the series' Christological weight**, on the 9:10 → 30:3 revocation and on 30:4; **31:1's אֲשֶׁר־יִסְּרַתּוּ אִמּוֹ is the only place the book supplies the mother's teaching 1:8 promised.** **Unit 9 is the designed ending, not the shortest slot**, and its best entry is 31:10's מִי יִמְצָא against 20:6's אִישׁ אֱמוּנִים מִי יִמְצָא — the question the book has already asked about a man.

**Matched pairs:** 1:7↔9:10 *(the frame's strongest bracket — the only two verses in Proverbs joining the fear of the LORD to a "beginning" word; 4th–16th of all frame pairs under both settings)* · 1:20↔8:1 *(תִּתֵּן קוֹלָהּ, two verses in the canon)* · 7:23↔9:18 *(וְלֹא־יָדַע כִּי, three verses with 28:22)* · 9:3↔9:14 *(מְרֹמֵי קָרֶת, only these two — Folly sits where Wisdom called)* · 9:4↔9:16 *(the invitation identical, the narrator's frame not)* · 1:28↔2:3–5 · 20:9↔30:12 · 12:4↔31:10↔20:6 · 24:7↔31:8, 9, 26 · 27:2↔31:28, 30, 31 · 6:6↔30:25 *(נְמָלָה, exactly twice in the Hebrew Bible)*. **And three whole bicola occur twice, each crossing a seam: 6:10 ‖ 24:33 · 14:12 ‖ 16:25 · 18:8 ‖ 26:22**, with eleven further half-lines doing the same. *The material circulates across the headings.*

**Compressing?** Six sermons that keep the book: **1:1–7 · 1:8–9:18 · 10:1–22:16 · 25:1–29:27 · 30:1–31:9 · 31:10–31** — but it loses the sharp return of direct address at 22:17, so **one sermon must name it**. A single sermon goes on 20:9 with 30:2–4 and 31:28–31. **Never preach a sentence collection as a self-audit** — a sermon assembled from good advice is 26:12 in the pulpit — and **never budget the shortest slot for 31:10–31.**

## Nine threads

**The New Testament reaches Proverbs through the Greek — and the three exceptions are what make the rule worth stating.** Thirteen uses verified; **every verified *quotation* runs through the Greek and four rest on a Greek reading the Hebrew does not support.** **Heb 12:5–6 ← 3:11–12**, verbatim from Swete, where **the Hebrew has no scourging in it** (וּכְאָב אֶת־בֵּן יִרְצֶה, "as a father the son in whom he delights"); **Jas 4:6 ‖ 1 Pet 5:5 ← 3:34**, where **the Hebrew's first colon is אִם־לַלֵּצִים הוּא־יָלִיץ — *he scoffs at the scoffers*, not *resists the proud***; **1 Pet 4:18 ← 11:31**, where the Hebrew has the righteous recompensed בָּאָרֶץ; **2 Cor 9:7 ← the Greek plus at 22:8, with no Hebrew behind it at all.** Add **Rom 2:6 ← 24:12**, where **in Hebrew the two verses share no lexeme and in Greek four words are identical.** **The three that do not fit:** **1 Pet 4:8 ← 10:12 follows the Hebrew** — *one short epistle, two citations eleven verses apart, going opposite ways*; **2 Pet 2:22 ← 26:11 is not a quotation**, cited as a παροιμία and paired with a non-biblical proverb; **John 3:13 ← 30:4 runs both ways at once.** ***A sermon that preaches Heb 12:6 and Prov 3:12 as the same sentence is preaching two different sentences*** — and the divergences are interpretation, not carelessness. A congregation can follow that in one sentence.

**And the thread above has a resolution.** **27:2's rule — יְהַלֶּלְךָ זָר וְלֹא־פִיךָ, *"let another praise you and not your own mouth"* — is kept at the close, where three of the book's nine הלל verses stand (31:28, 30, 31), every one of them somebody else speaking.** *The series' spine and the answer to its Big Question, and where the gospel enters without being imported.*

**The fear of the LORD frames the book, and at the end stops being a thing and becomes a description of a person.** The noun in construct with the divine name stands in **exactly fourteen verses — 1:7, 1:29, 2:5, 8:13, 9:10, 10:27, 14:26, 14:27, 15:16, 15:33, 16:6, 19:23, 22:4, 23:17** — sits at every structural hinge, is **withdrawn from all 138 verses of 25–29**, is **revoked by Agur in the frame's own words** (דַּעַת קְדֹשִׁים, **two verses in the Hebrew Bible**: 9:10, where it *is* understanding, and 30:3, where he says he has not got it), and returns at **31:30 as the adjective, in four verses of Proverbs.** *Usually preached as a bookend and a slogan. The sweep shows it behaving like an argument: stated, repeated, withheld for five chapters while the reader is made to judge without it, revoked by the last sage, and finally not stated at all but observed in somebody's life. The tracing is the series.*

**Frame and field speak different dialects — and the last movement returns to the frame's.** **Five lemma-verified chains tie the acrostic to personified Wisdom**: **פְּנִינִים** (3:15; 8:11 → 31:10) · **שַׁעַר** (1:21; 8:3 → 31:23, 31) · **שׂחק** (1:26; 8:30–31 → 31:25) · **אשׁר** piel and **תמך**, both in 3:18 *of Wisdom* (→ 31:28, 31:19) · **מצא** (3:13; 8:17, 35 → 31:10's מִי יִמְצָא). **The strongest single result of the sweep, and it governs how sermon 9 is preached.** *The frame is the lens, not the introduction — and the book says so by returning to it at the close.*

**The collection boundaries a preacher plans by are Masoretic.** Five points of Greek disagreement, **each found independently by the run working there** (see the trap above). *The seven headings that give a Proverbs series its shape are not neutral furniture.*

**The book withdraws direct address as it proceeds and hands the reader the cases.** **בְּנִי twenty-two times, fifteen in chs 1–9 and the last of those at 7:1**; after 7:27 the father never addresses the son again, **and chapters 8 and 9 belong to the two women**. **The formula וְעַתָּה בָנִים שִׁמְעוּ־לִי is exact at 5:7 and 7:24 (the father's) and 8:32 (Wisdom's), and nowhere else** — the handover of voice. Then the imperative rate falls **15.9% → 12.0% → 1.8%** across the last three collections, **the vocative retires at 27:11**, the last teaching is given by **two outsiders** whose closing command is to open your mouth *for somebody else*, and **the closing twenty-two verses carry one imperative — תְּנוּ־לָהּ (31:31) — plural, addressed to the audience and not to her.** ***Proverbs teaches judgement by progressively refusing to exercise it for you*** — which turns the collections' apparent shapelessness into a method, and gives the last sermon its grammar.

**The poor are the test case, and the vocabulary turns covenantal at the very end.** The everyday words are spread through the collections — **דַּל fourteen verses, רוּשׁ sixteen**. **The covenant words are not: עָנִי eight, אֶבְיוֹן four — three of those four in the last two chapters — and the pair standing together in exactly three verses, 30:14, 31:9, 31:20**, with **neither word anywhere in the 138 verses of 25–29.** The **poor-and-Maker chain** runs 14:31 → 17:5 → 22:2 → 22:16 (חֵרֵף עֹשֵׂהוּ identical at 14:31 and 17:5), **crosses the 15:33/16:1 seam and lands on Solomon I's closing verse — and 22:22 is the next collection's first prohibition, on the same subject.** **31:20 is the acrostic's hinge: the one verse in the poem where anything leaves the house without being sold.** *The weight is in the shift, and it lands on the book's only two commands to speak for someone with no voice and to pay someone what their work is worth.*

**The book binds itself by repetition rather than citation.** **It quotes an earlier Scripture once in thirty-one chapters** — **30:5**, from Ps 18:31 ‖ 2 Sam 22:31, **and Agur changes exactly two things, both of them the right two: יְהוָה → אֱלוֹהַּ, and כֹּל moved off the people and onto the word.** **Deuteronomy is the one live external source** (the boundary law, the weights, the Shema's bind-and-write, "do not add" at 30:6 taking only that half since גרע is absent, Deut 28:63's נָסַח at 2:22, the satiety sequence at 30:8–9). Otherwise **the collections quote themselves** — eight self-quotations in 10:1–15:33 alone, including **13:14 ‖ 14:27, identical but for the subject** (*the teaching of the wise* / *the fear of the LORD* given the same predicate, thirteen verses apart). **And the doublets are directional: every whole-verse doublet 25–29 shares with Solomon I comes from 16:1–22:16, none from 10:1–15:33.** *The right question of a Proverbs verse is usually not "what Scripture is behind this?" but "where else does the book say this, and what changed?" — and the answer is often one swapped word carrying the whole freight.*

**What the sweep did *not* find, which is part of the report.** **No recurring narrative motifs** — there is no narrative after 7:6–23, so type-scenes have nothing to attach to. **Almost no covenant-historical categories: בְּרִית stands once in the whole book, at 2:17, and it is a covenant being broken.** *A finding, not a gap — the theological work is done by the fear of the LORD and by the created order.* **And no paronomasia of the Torah kind**: the book welds its units by reprinting whole lines across seams.

## Where Christ is

**No messianic prophecy here, and the attempt to find one usually lands on 8:22–31, where it does the most damage.** The weight is an unanswered question, a contrast, and a trajectory — in that order.

**The strongest doorway is a question, and it is the book's own.** **30:4 asks for one person who both went up and came down, and that is the discriminating detail**: Swete reads τίς ἀνέβη εἰς τὸν οὐρανὸν **καὶ κατέβη**, and **the two nearest rivals do not have it** (Deut 30:12 has an ascent only; Bar 3:29 brings *wisdom* down). **John 3:13 matches the shape, and 30:4's last question — מַה־שֶּׁם־בְּנוֹ — is answered in the same clause.** **Carry the caution and never drop it: the singular "his son" is the Masoretic reading, the Greek has τοῖς τέκνοις αὐτοῦ, and BHS records it** — *anyone checking a Septuagint will not find the singular, and saying so makes the point stronger.* With it: **30:9's מִי יְהוָה stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible and the other is Pharaoh's at Exod 5:2; 30:4's מַה־שְּׁמוֹ in two, the other Moses' at Exod 3:13.** *Agur asks Moses' question and prays not to ask Pharaoh's.*

**Then 20:9 and 28:13** — the question nobody answers, and **the book's only verse about confession, carrying the one occurrence of רחם in the whole of Proverbs.** *Concealment was God's glory at 25:2 and is our ruin at 28:13, and the same root does both.*

**Contrast — the book's own test is failed by everyone in it**, and **2:6's כִּי grounds the search rather than rewarding it**: *"**if** you seek her as silver … **then** you will find the knowledge of God, **for** the LORD gives wisdom."* **The book's own syntax forbids the sermon that makes wisdom a wage.** *It supplies the standard and withholds the power; diagnostic before prescriptive.*

**The close supplies a verdict rather than a type.** The acrostic is **neither a picture of Christ nor of the church**; what it stages is **a life assessed, in public, by somebody with the standing to do it** — the shape of Matt 25:21, spoken about work, out loud, by someone else. **And the ground is that the praise in 31:31 is earned and the verdict a Christian waits for is not** (Rom 4:5). *The licensed trajectory is the book's own: personified Wisdom (chs 1–9) → a life (31:10–31) → σοφία in person (1 Cor 1:30; Col 2:3) — abstraction, embodiment, person, with the poem as the middle term.*

**Three refusals, each textual rather than pastoral:** *be like the ant* (answered by 2:6's כִּי) · *Wisdom is Jesus* (identification declined, trajectory kept) · *Proverbs 31 as a wife-template* (answered from the poem's genre, grammar and vocabulary — trap 2).

**Lower confidence — and audit 2 lowered four of these in November.** **John 1:1–3 ← Prov 8:22–31, low–moderate**: λόγος, πάντα of creation, δι᾽ αὐτοῦ and γίνομαι are **all absent from LXX 8:22–31**, **Gen 1:1 governs** (Gen 1 ranks ~4th against John 1:1–5 in every setting, Prov 8 ~184th), and **8:22's ἔκτισέν με is the very thing John's contrast excludes** — ***use Col 1:15–18 and Rev 3:14, which are nearer Prov 8:22 than John is.*** **Luke 14:16–24 ← 9:1–6, low–moderate as a source**: in the whole Swete LXX, 9:3–5 is the only scene where a host sends servants to call guests to a prepared meal, **so the scene is shared and the wording is not**, and **Matt 22:1–10 is the closer Synoptic form**; *the "streets" argument is not to be used — in LXX Prov 9 the streets belong to **Folly** (9:14).* **Luke 11:49 // Matt 23:34, moderate for "Jesus speaks as the Wisdom of God" and not for Proverbs' figure** — the saying's content is the Chronicler's and Jeremiah's, and **σοφία with ἀποστέλλω in one verse occurs nowhere in Swete.** **Ps 1–2 behind the frame: do not use** — Ps 2 shares no phrase at all with Prov 1–9, and **Ps 34:12 carries the frame's own formula and its motto in one verse.**

**The gospel ground.** **Not that Christ helps us keep Proverbs**, but that **he is the one who can say what 20:9 asks and the one whose name answers 30:4** — and that **the verdict has been spoken from outside, over him and over us in him, before anything had been achieved to justify it.** Only then does the book's wisdom become liveable rather than accusing.

## The standing traps

**The promise-mechanism** — a מָשָׁל is a generalisation about how the world usually runs, not a covenant undertaking, and the book supplies its own counter-cases (13:23; 16:33; 21:31); bites at 22:6 above all, then 3:9–10, 10:22 · **Proverbs 31 as a wife-template**, and **the trap now carries its own answer in three textual defences** — **genre** (an acrostic running the alphabet claims *completeness*, not twenty-two requirements), **grammar** (the poem gives her **no command at all**, every verb about her indicative or participial, and gives the audience one), **vocabulary** (it is written in **personified Wisdom's words**, on the five chains above, **so the subject is wisdom-embodied, not wifely performance**); *a useful sentence from the front: "the only person commanded to do anything in this poem is you"* — and **Swete ends the book "give her of the fruit of her *lips*, and let *her husband* be praised in the gates", so the impulse to redirect the praise is two thousand years old and the Hebrew resists it: if a sermon lands on the husband it has quietly taken the Greek's ending** · **8:22–31 as the pre-incarnate Christ** — **קנה at 8:22 belongs to a book-wide chain in which the verb means *acquire*** (1:5; 4:5, 7 ×2; 8:22; 15:32; 16:16; 17:16; 18:15; 19:8; 20:14; 23:23), **so the son is told four times over to קנה wisdom before wisdom says YHWH קָנָנִי**, and **Swete's Κύριος ἔκτισέν με carried the fourth-century Arian argument**; preach the trajectory, not the identification, which is a category error · **preaching the collections as an argument** — four markers in 375 verses, and the headings present the material as **gathered, not composed** (25:1), so a sermon on a "section" of 10–29 must justify its boundaries from something and usually cannot · **moralism** — answered by 20:9 and 30:2–4, and **the highest measured risk is the Words of the Wise (22 "do not"s and 22 grounds in 82 verses), whose own guard is 22:19, re-founding the collection on trust before a single prohibition** · **the rod verses** — 23:13–14 is proverbial hyperbole in a culture of corporal discipline, **and the congregation includes people for whom this was not proverbial** · **the "strange woman" as a person rather than the book's counter-figure** — adultery is the vehicle, the alternative to wisdom is the tenor; **but be exact about the symmetry, because audit 2 falsified part of the usual statement: both women have a house, both are at the heights of the city (מְרֹמֵי קָרֶת, only 9:3 and 9:14) and both say the same four words, but only Wisdom builds, hews, slaughters, mixes, sets a table and sends — Folly sends no one and sets no table**, she sits at her door and calls; *the simple cannot tell them apart by the invitation, and the text tells them apart by what follows* · **31:10–31 as the book's nice afterthought** — **seven of the book's chains make their last appearance in these twenty-two verses** (בטח, תּוֹרָה and חֶסֶד, הלל and מַעֲשֶׂה, הֶבֶל and the adjective יָרֵא), the motto lands here, 27:2 is performed here: **this is the designed ending.**

## Handle with care

**Four text-critical places, and none should be settled from the pulpit.** **3:3c** — כָּתְבֵם עַל־לוּחַ לִבֶּךָ is **absent from Swete and Rahlfs**, marked lacking in Vaticanus and Sinaiticus, and BHS proposes deleting it as imported from 7:3, while NASB95, ESV and NIV84 all print it. ***Build the Deuteronomy case on 7:3 and 6:20–22, not on 3:3*** — which is where it is strongest anyway: **finger, tablet and writing stand together in three verses only (Exod 31:18; Deut 9:10; Prov 7:3)**, and at 3:3 what is bound is **חֶסֶד וֶאֱמֶת**, not the father's words. **1:16** is reported absent from the best Greek witnesses, BHS suspecting it entered from Isa 59:7. **22:20** — Qere שָׁלִישִׁים, Swete τρισσῶς, BHS proposing שְׁלֹשִׁים, "thirty"; **NIV84 settles it as "thirty sayings", so this is the one place in sermon 6 where the preacher must say something.** **31:25/26** are **transposed in Swete into the פ-before-ע order of Lamentations 2–4**, and the Rahlfs/Göttingen check is still owed.

**One correction to carry forward, because it post-dates the overview.** **Claim audit 2 (12 November 2026) is newer than book overview v3.0 (27 September), and the overview has not absorbed it.** **חָכְמוֹת, Wisdom's plural-form name, stands at Prov 1:20; 9:1; 24:7 and Ps 49:4 — and 14:1 is pointed חַכְמוֹת, the construct of the adjective ("the wisest of women"), as at Judg 5:29.** Consonants identical, vowels not; **BHS proposes reading 9:1's form at 14:1, so the identity is an editor's proposal** — *the overview's echo table still counts 14:1 among the four.* **What survives is better: בָּנְתָה בֵיתָהּ occurs only at 9:1 and 14:1 in the whole canon** — though it is one of about twenty word-for-word frame-to-field links and its rank moves from 17th to 221st depending on setting, **so the phrase is high and the design is not.** *Also discarded there: the Sheol inclusio 1:12 → 9:18 (the pattern is what chance produces; 1:12's source is Korah — בלע with שְׁאוֹל and "alive" only at Num 16:30–34 and Prov 1:12). *One method note for the pulpit, and this book taught it as plainly as Job did. **Percentages are measure-specific and are not facts about the book.** The antithesis figures the earlier files carried — "81.0% · 20.4% · 7.3% · 60.0%" — were **not reproducible from the corpus without the criteria that produced them**; two independent measures give 31.5% · 5.2% · 3.6% · 18.2% and 86.9% · 55.6% · 53.7% · 69.8%. **The shape was confirmed three times over — high, low, lowest, high — and the shape is what a congregation can be told.** So: **state the shape, and state the measure alongside any number.** A congregation can be told that two words stand together in only three verses of the Bible, or that one word is absent from a hundred and thirty-eight consecutive verses; that is checkable and true. **They should not be given a percentage as though it were a property of the text, and they should not be told that a statistical rank proves authorial design** — in this book 9:1/14:1 ranked anywhere from 17th to 221st depending on the setting, and that is exactly what the audits exist to catch.*
