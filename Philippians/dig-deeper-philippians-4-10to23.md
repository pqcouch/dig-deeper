## The Passage

> **10** I rejoiced in the Lord greatly that now at length you have revived your concern for me. You were indeed concerned for me, but you had no opportunity. **11** Not that I am speaking of being in need, for I have learned in whatever situation I am to be content. **12** I know how to be brought low, and I know how to abound. In any and every circumstance, I have learned the secret of facing plenty and hunger, abundance and need. **13** I can do all things through him who strengthens me. **14** Yet it was kind of you to share my trouble. **15** And you Philippians yourselves know that in the beginning of the gospel, when I left Macedonia, no church entered into partnership with me in giving and receiving, except you only. **16** Even in Thessalonica you sent me help for my needs once and again. **17** Not that I seek the gift, but I seek the fruit that increases to your credit. **18** I have received full payment, and more. I am well supplied, having received from Epaphroditus the gifts you sent, a fragrant offering, a sacrifice acceptable and pleasing to God. **19** And my God will supply every need of yours according to his riches in glory in Christ Jesus. **20** To our God and Father be glory forever and ever. Amen. **21** Greet every saint in Christ Jesus. The brothers who are with me greet you. **22** All the saints greet you, especially those of Caesar's household. **23** The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit.

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims that emerged across multiple tools:

1. **Paul receives the gift as *koinōnia* framed in two registers at once — commercial ledger ("giving and receiving", "to your credit", "paid in full") *and* cultic sacrifice ("a fragrant offering, a sacrifice acceptable… to God"): their money is *worship*.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary (the ledger and cultic clusters), Tool 11 (the sacrificial allusion), Tool 11 Move 4 (2:17; 2:25–30). Ordinary generosity to gospel work is reclassified as an offering God accepts with pleasure.

2. **"I have learned… to be content" (*autarkēs*) *reframes* a Stoic ideal: contentment is not self-sufficiency but *Christ*-sufficiency — "through him who strengthens me" (v.13); the famous verse is about facing plenty and want, not limitless achievement.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary / Original Languages (*autarkēs*), Difficult Verses, Structure. The scope of 4:13 is set by vv.11–12.

3. **The gift's thanks is *deferred* to the letter's end so it lands inside the theology of contentment, fruit, and worship — the "thankless thanks" refuses patron-client debt and credits the fruit to *their* account (v.17), reframing the whole exchange as gospel partnership.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Phase 0.6 (Positional Necessity Check), Tone, Vocabulary (ledger), Historical Background. The placement is pastoral tact and theology together.

4. **The letter closes as it opened — partnership and joy — an *inclusio* (4:10–20 ↔ 1:3–7), and 4:18 completes the letter's cultic vocabulary system (2:17 → 2:25/30 → 4:18).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure (the inclusio), Tool 11 Move 4. The whole letter is sealed as a gospel-shaped thank-you.

5. **"Caesar's household" (v.22) quietly answers the citizenship theme (3:20) — the gospel has reached the emperor's own staff: the Lord's people inside Caesar's house.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Historical Background, Tool 11 Move 4 (*politeuma*), Vocabulary. The letter ends with a hint that the heavenly commonwealth has an outpost in the imperial court.

## Phase 0.6 — Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement:** the entire letter of *partnership* (1:5, *koinōnia*; the gift) and its **cultic** vocabulary — 2:17 (Paul poured out as a libation) and 2:25/30 (Epaphroditus the *leitourgos* who brought the *leitourgia*). And 4:6–9's "do not be anxious… the God of peace will be with you." `[T]`

**Necessity answer:** the gift is the letter's *occasion*, yet Paul **defers his explicit thanks to the very end** — so that it is received *inside* the theology of contentment, fruit, and worship rather than as a bare transaction. It stands *here*, last, because only after "the surpassing worth of Christ" (3:8) and before "my God will supply your every need" (4:19) can the money be named without cheapening either party. `[T]`

**Implication:** the placement is pastoral tact and theology together — the "thankless thanks" is Paul refusing patron-client debt language and reframing the whole exchange as gospel partnership credited to *their* account (v.17). And it **closes the partnership-and-joy inclusio** (4:10–20 ↔ 1:3–7) and **completes the cultic thread** (2:17 → 4:18).

**Revisit after Tool 11:** Move 2 confirms 4:18 as the completion of the live cultic source begun at 2:17 — so the positional point sharpens: the letter *ends* by naming the gift that occasioned it, but only after teaching the church how to give it (as worship) and how to lack (in contentment).

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book-level** (from the overview): to form a mission-partner church in gospel-worthy steadfastness by the mind of Christ. `[S: overview]`

**Passage-level:** to **thank the Philippians for their gift — but on gospel terms**: received as *koinōnia* and as a sacrifice God accepts, credited to *their* account, without creating patron-client debt; and to **testify to Christ-sufficient contentment** (learned in plenty and want), assuring them that God will supply *their* every need. The thanks is held to the close so it lands inside the letter's theology; then final greetings and grace. `[T]`

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check *(see Phase 0.6 above)*

**Immediately before (4:2–9):** "stand firm… rejoice… do not be anxious… the God of peace will be with you." The "God of peace *with* you" (4:9) flows straight into "my God will *supply* your every need" (4:19). `[T]`

**Immediately after:** the letter ends (greetings, benediction). `[T]`

**Out-of-context flags:** v.13 ("I can do all things") wrenched into achievement/career/athletic triumphalism; v.19 ("supply every need") into prosperity; v.11 ("content") into stoic self-reliance.

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|--------|---------|---------|
| 4:10–13 | Contentment | Joy at their revived care; but Paul has *learned* contentment in every state — "I can do all things through him who strengthens me" |
| 4:14–18 | The partnership and the gift-as-offering | Their kindness; the historical partnership (Macedonia, Thessalonica); "not the gift but the fruit to your credit"; "paid in full… a fragrant offering, a sacrifice acceptable to God" |
| 4:19 | The promise | "My God will supply every need of yours… in Christ Jesus" |
| 4:20 | Doxology | "To our God and Father be glory forever and ever. Amen." |
| 4:21–23 | Greetings + grace | "Greet every saint… especially those of Caesar's household… The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit." |

**Device:** the thank-you *deferred* to the close; the **partnership-and-joy inclusio** (↔ 1:3–7); a doxology (v.20) capping the body before the final greetings. The two "not that… but" turns (vv.11, 17) carefully refuse the wrong frame (hinting for more / seeking the gift). `[T]`

### 4. Linking Words

- **v.11 "Not that (οὐχ ὅτι)… for (γάρ) I have learned"** — Paul heads off a misreading: he is *not* hinting for more. `[T]`
- **v.13 "through (ἐν) him who strengthens me"** — the ground of the contentment. `[T]`
- **v.14 "Yet (πλήν)"** — nevertheless, their kindness was genuinely good. `[T]`
- **v.17 "Not that (οὐχ ὅτι) I seek the gift, but (ἀλλά) I seek the fruit"** — the decisive reframing: their *reward*, not his need, is in view. `[T]`
- **v.18 "I have received full payment (ἀπέχω)"** — a commercial receipt formula: "paid in full." `[T]`
- **v.19 "And (δέ) my God will supply"** — the promise answering their giving. `[T]`
- **v.20 "To my God… be glory"** — the doxology. `[T]`

The paired "not that… but" constructions (vv.11, 17) are load-bearing — Paul refusing both the beggar's frame and the patron's frame.

### 7. Vocabulary

Co-dominant with Tool 11.

- **αὐτάρκης (v.11), "content / self-sufficient"** — a **Stoic** term (the sage needs nothing outside himself); Paul redefines it — his sufficiency is in Christ (v.13), not in himself. `[T, high]`
- **μεμύημαι (v.12), "I have learned the secret / been initiated"** — mystery-cult language ("I have been initiated"), playfully appropriated: contentment is a learned *initiation*, not a native temperament. `[T]`
- **πάντα ἰσχύω ἐν τῷ ἐνδυναμοῦντί με (v.13), "I can do all things in the one who strengthens me"** — the "all things" is bounded by vv.11–12 (facing plenty *and* want), not open-ended achievement. `[T]`
- **The ledger cluster:** *eis logon doseōs kai lēmpseōs* (v.15, "an account of giving and receiving"); *karpon… eis logon hymōn* (v.17, "fruit to your account"); *apechō panta* (v.18, "I have been paid in full" — a receipt formula attested on papyri). `[T, high]`
- **ὀσμὴν εὐωδίας, θυσίαν δεκτήν (v.18)** — the **LXX sacrificial formula** ("a fragrant aroma, an acceptable sacrifice"). `[T, high]`
- **κοινωνέω / συγκοινωνήσαντες (vv.14, 15)** — partnership/sharing — the letter's *koinōnia* thread (1:5). `[T]`
- **χρεία (vv.16, 19), "need"** — their gift met *his* need; God will meet *theirs* (not "greed"). `[T]`

**Proper-noun inventory:** the Lord Jesus Christ; **Macedonia**; **Thessalonica**; **Epaphroditus**; **Caesar**; God the Father. *Note:* Macedonia and Thessalonica are Paul's mission-itinerary markers (Acts 16–17); "Caesar's household" is a historical designation (see Historical Background). None carries an *OT* allusion — the freight is missional-historical, not canonical-hyperlink. `[T]`

### 8. Translations

- **v.13 "I can do all things" (ESV/KJV/NASB)** — *panta ischyō*; all agree, and the danger is the popular *misreading* (context = contentment), not the translation. Note the textual variant (some add "Christ" to "him who strengthens me" — see Textual Variants). `[T]`
- **v.11 "content" (ESV)** — *autarkēs*, the Stoic loanword redefined; "content" is right, but the *self-sufficiency* backdrop (which Paul overturns) is worth naming. `[T]`
- **v.18 "a fragrant offering, a sacrifice acceptable and pleasing to God" (ESV)** — the **LXX cultic formula** (*osmē euōdias, thysia dektē*). Cross-reported to Tool 11. `[T]`

**Ancient versions check *(trigger met at v.18)*.** "A fragrant offering / pleasing aroma" (*osmē euōdias*) is the recurring **LXX** description of an accepted sacrifice — from Noah's burnt offering (Gen 8:21) through the burnt-offering legislation (Exod 29:18; Lev 1:9) to Ezekiel's promise (20:41). **Translation-tradition note:** Paul's wording *is* the Greek OT's sacrificial formula; the church would hear the cult in it. *Confidence high; cross-reported to Tool 11.* `[T]`

**Pulpit edition note:** Pulpit text matches report default (ESV Anglicised); no finding depends on a spelling/idiom difference.

### 9. Tone and Feel

**Grateful yet carefully non-obligated** — warm affection disciplined by a refusal of patronage debt, then rising to doxology, then closing in warm greeting and benediction. The "**thankless thanks**" quality is real: Paul thanks them profusely (v.10, "I rejoiced in the Lord greatly") yet reframes so that they are *not* his patrons and he is *not* their client (vv.11, 17). **Soundtrack:** a gracious, dignified close — heartfelt gratitude held with self-respect, lifted into praise ("glory forever and ever. Amen"), and sealed with grace. `[T]`

### 11. Quotation/Allusion

*Independent candidate-finding:* v.18 "a fragrant offering, a sacrifice acceptable and pleasing to God" → the **LXX sacrificial formula** (*osmē euōdias, thysia dektē* — Gen 8:21; Exod 29:18; Lev 1:9; Ezek 20:41). The ledger idiom (vv.15–18) is *Greco-Roman commercial*, not OT. "My God will supply every need" carries a provision register (Ps 23; Matt 6) but cites no text. **Citation-light apart from the cultic formula.** `[T]`

*Live sources (overview v0.2):* the **sacrificial cult is live** (2:17) — so the aroma-formula here carries an elevated prior and *completes* the thread.

**OT sacrificial formula (Gen 8:21; Exod 29:18; Ezek 20:41 LXX) → 4:18** *(high confidence — the "fragrant offering" allusion)* `[T]`

*Source context (Move 1):* "a pleasing aroma / fragrant offering" (*osmē euōdias*) is the recurring LXX description of an **accepted** sacrifice. It appears first at **Noah's burnt offering** after the flood, which "the LORD smelled… and said… I will never again curse the ground" (Gen 8:21) — God's acceptance inaugurating a new order; then throughout the burnt-offering legislation (Exod 29:18; Lev 1:9, 13); and in **Ezekiel 20:41**, where God promises to accept his *restored people* "as a pleasing aroma." Its function in the source: God's *pleasure in and acceptance of* the offering.

*Book usage (Move 2):* 4:18 **completes the letter's cultic thread** — 2:17 (Paul as libation/drink offering), 2:25/30 (Epaphroditus the *leitourgos* who brought the *leitourgia*), now 4:18 (the gift as *fragrant offering*). The letter has been building a sacrificial vocabulary for gospel partnership, and this is its capstone. **Live source.** `[T]`

*OT-to-OT (Move 3):* the aroma-formula (4:18) and the drink-offering (2:17) belong to the *same* sacrificial system — already a unified frame in the Torah; and Ezek 20:41's application of "pleasing aroma" to the *restored people* links cultic acceptance to the eschatological gathering, so the church's gift stands in that stream. `[I, moderate]`

*What it adds:* the Philippians' **cash gift** to a prisoner is reclassified as **worship God accepts with pleasure** — the highest possible valuation of ordinary generosity. Their money is, before God, what Noah's offering was: a fragrant sacrifice he is pleased to receive. `[T, high]`

### 12. Genre

Pauline **epistolary thanksgiving-for-a-gift + closing greetings + benediction** — a recognised letter-closing form, with the acknowledgment of a gift cast in **commercial/accounting idiom** deployed theologically. Reading rule: read the thanks as *gospel-reframed* (partnership, fruit, worship), not transaction; and read 4:13 and 4:19 strictly *in context* (contentment; need). `[T]`

### 13. Copycat

Largely **N/A** for narrative characters — but Paul's **learned contentment** (vv.11–13) is a *normative posture* of Christ-dependence: it is *learned* ("I have learned the secret"), not a native temperament or a technique, and it is offered for imitation of its *ground* (Christ who strengthens, v.13), not as stoic willpower. Direct address; the church is the generous partner and the recipient of the promise. `[T]`

### 15. Who Am I?

Direct NT epistle address — we are the church addressed: gospel partners and generous givers, recipients of the promise (v.19) and the grace (v.23). **Paul's contentment** is a model (imitated in its ground). **Christ** is the strengthener (v.13), the sphere of supply (v.19), and the grace-giver (v.23). We identify with the Philippians (the partners), not with Paul-the-apostle. `[T]`

### 16. So What?

**Author's intended response:** to give to gospel work as *worship* (a fragrant offering credited to one's own account), and to learn *Christ-sufficient contentment* in every circumstance, trusting God to supply every need — all to God's glory.

**Worldview:** Christian contentment is Christ-sufficiency in every state (neither self-sufficiency nor circumstance-dependence); generosity to gospel work is a sacrifice God receives and credits; God supplies *need* (not greed) "according to his riches in glory in Christ Jesus."

**Behaviour:** *stop* — anxiety about provision, and treating giving as transaction or patronage; *start* — giving to gospel partnership, learning contentment, trusting God to supply.

**Motivation (against moralism):** God's riches in glory in Christ (v.19); his glory forever (v.20); the grace of the Lord Jesus (v.23); the fruit credited to the givers (v.17). Contentment is grounded in "him who strengthens me" (v.13) — dependence, not willpower.

**Four audiences:**
- *For me:* am I content in Christ, or circumstance-dependent? Do I give as worship?
- *For a Christian friend:* the anxious or stretched giver — "my God will supply your every need."
- *For the church:* generosity as worship; contentment as a learned Christ-dependence; partnership as the shape of gospel life.
- *For the unbeliever:* contentment "through him who strengthens me" is unintelligible without Christ — and the gospel has reached even Caesar's household, so no one is beyond it.

**Prayer:** for Christ-sufficient contentment in plenty and want; thanksgiving for gospel partnership; trust in the God who supplies every need; glory to God and the Father forever.

### Textual Variants

**v.13 — "him who strengthens me".** Some manuscripts add "Christ" (*en tō endynamounti me Christō*); the shorter reading (without "Christ") is better attested and likely original — though "Christ" only makes explicit what the context already supplies ("in the Lord", v.10; "in Christ Jesus", v.19). The referent is Christ either way; no doctrinal weight. Otherwise the passage is stable. *Confidence: moderate–high for the shorter reading.* `[T]`

### Historical and Cultural Background

**Setting:** the close of a letter thanking a partner-church for a monetary gift carried by their envoy (Epaphroditus, 2:25), written from imprisonment.

**What the original audience knew that we miss:**
- **Patron-client conventions** — a gift created social *obligation* and debt; a client owed a patron honour and service. Paul's careful reframing (partnership, not patronage; "fruit to *your* account", v.17; "paid in full", v.18) **refuses the client role and the debt**, keeping the relationship gospel-shaped rather than status-bound. `[T/S]`
- **The commercial/accounting idiom** — *logos doseōs kai lēmpseōs* ("account of giving and receiving") and *apechō* ("paid in full") are ordinary business-ledger and receipt terms (the latter attested on papyri); Paul playfully "invoices" their partnership. `[T/S]`
- **Macedonia / Thessalonica** — Paul's early mission (Acts 16–17); the Philippians *uniquely* supported him "in the beginning of the gospel" (v.15). `[T/S]`
- **"Caesar's household" (v.22)** — the imperial *familia*: slaves and freedmen serving in the emperor's administration (not the imperial family); that the gospel had reached *them* is a striking testimony to its advance (cf. 1:13, "the whole imperial guard"). `[T/S]`
- **The *osmē euōdias* formula** — "a pleasing aroma" was the standard language of *accepted* sacrifice in both Jewish (LXX) and, more broadly, ancient cult; applied to the gift, it reads their generosity as worship. `[T/S]`

**What this changes:** the thank-you is neither grovelling nor transactional — it is a dignified, theologically reframed acknowledgment that turns money into worship and reward, and it ends by revealing that the gospel has quietly colonised the heart of Rome.

### Original Audience Reception

**Canonical audience:** the generous partner-church, who sent the gift and would be wondering how Paul received it.

**Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances:**
- *Surprising:* the "thankless thanks" — Paul refuses to be their debtor, and contentment "in want" (v.12) sits oddly with a gift of relief.
- *Comforting:* their gift is a *sacrifice God accepts* (v.18); their account is *credited* (v.17); God will supply *their* every need (v.19).
- *Striking:* the gospel is in *Caesar's household* (v.22).

### Biblical-Theological Themes

**Sacrifice / worship.** The gift as a "fragrant offering… acceptable to God" (v.18) joins and *completes* the letter's cultic vocabulary system (2:17; 2:25/30; 4:18); Christian generosity is worship (cf. Rom 12:1; Heb 13:16). *Confidence:* high. `[T]`

**Provision / the God who supplies.** "My God will supply every need… in glory in Christ Jesus" (v.19) stands in the manna/shepherd provision stream (Ps 23; Matt 6:33), already/not-yet. *Confidence:* high. `[T]`

**Contentment / Christ-sufficiency.** The "learned secret" (vv.11–13) — Christ as the strength in every state — is a new-covenant reframing of wisdom's contentment, grounded in union with Christ rather than in the self. *Confidence:* high. `[T]`

**Confluence — climactic convergence.** The three meet in one circuit of grace the passage names: the gift given as *worship* (a sacrifice God accepts) is met by the *God who supplies* every need of the givers, all crowned in *doxology* (v.20) — with *contentment* the posture that frees both giving and receiving from anxiety. That is the preaching gold: giving is worship, the God who receives the offering is the God who supplies the giver, and contentment in Christ holds the whole in peace — to God's glory.

### Christological Reading

NT passage — Christ named ("him who strengthens me", v.13; "in Christ Jesus", v.19; "the Lord Jesus Christ", v.23). The **ground** register throughout: Christ the strength of contentment, the sphere of God's supply, the giver of grace. No **pattern**/typology in view here (the passage is thanksgiving and closing, not paraenesis-by-example).

**Moralism check.** The temptation is to preach "be content / give generously" as bare exhortation, or 4:13 as self-empowerment. The text forbids it: contentment is grounded in "**him** who strengthens me" (Christ-dependence, not willpower), and gift-and-supply in "**God's** riches in glory **in Christ Jesus**." Preach the Christ who strengthens and the God who supplies, not a motivational slogan. `[T]`

### Difficult / Contested Verses

**v.13 — "I can do all things through him who strengthens me".**
- *Category:* weaponised (the letter's most-misused verse).
- *The difficulty:* torn from context for gym doors, exams, and career/athletic triumph.
- *Rhetorical function:* the *ground* of the contentment just described (vv.11–12).
- *Honest exegesis:* the "all things" is set by vv.11–12 — *facing plenty and hunger, abundance and need*; it means "I can face **any circumstance** through Christ," not "achieve anything I set my mind to."
- *Handling:* name and dismantle the cliché; preach Christ-strengthened contentment.

**v.19 — "my God will supply every need of yours".**
- *Category:* weaponised (prosperity).
- *The difficulty:* used as a wealth-promise.
- *Honest exegesis:* "*need*" (*chreia*), not greed; "according to his riches in glory in Christ Jesus" (God's inexhaustible resources, met *in Christ*); and it is addressed to *generous givers* — the promise follows their sacrifice.
- *Handling:* preach God's sure provision of *need*, not a guarantee of abundance; hold it to its context of costly giving.

**v.11 — "content" (*autarkēs*).**
- *Category:* interpretive.
- *The difficulty:* the word's Stoic origin invites a stiff-upper-lip self-reliance.
- *Honest exegesis:* Paul *overturns* the Stoic sense — his sufficiency is *Christ's* strength (v.13), a learned dependence, not self-possession.
- *Handling:* preach Christ-sufficiency, not stoicism.

**v.17 — "not that I seek the gift, but the fruit to your account".**
- *Category:* pastoral.
- *The difficulty:* can sound like ingratitude or a refusal of the gift.
- *Honest exegesis:* Paul *reframes* the gift so that *their* reward, not *his* need, is in view — a deeper thanks, not a colder one.
- *Handling:* present it as the most generous form of gratitude, not a brush-off.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: "I can do all things" (4:13) as achievement triumphalism

- **What it looks like:** the verse over a gym door, an exam, a career goal — "with God I can accomplish anything."
- **Why it's wrong:** the "all things" is bounded by vv.11–12 — *contentment* in plenty and want.
- **The corrective:** "I can face any circumstance through Christ who strengthens me"; name and dismantle the cliché.

### Pitfall: "My God will supply every need" (4:19) as a prosperity promise

- **What it looks like:** "God will make you wealthy / give you whatever you want."
- **Why it's wrong:** "*need*", not greed; "in glory in Christ Jesus"; addressed to generous givers.
- **The corrective:** preach God's sure supply of *need*, in Christ, held to its context of costly generosity.

### Pitfall: "Content" (4:11) as stoic self-sufficiency

- **What it looks like:** a stiff-upper-lip, need-nothing self-reliance.
- **Why it's wrong:** *autarkēs* is overturned — Paul's sufficiency is *Christ's* strength (v.13).
- **The corrective:** preach Christ-dependent, *learned* contentment, not stoicism.

### Pitfall: Missing the gift-as-worship (4:18)

- **What it looks like:** a sermon that says only "give generously" and stops.
- **Why it's wrong:** their gift is a *fragrant offering, a sacrifice acceptable to God* — worship God smells with pleasure (Gen 8:21; Ezek 20:41).
- **The corrective:** preach generosity as worship God receives and credits (v.17).

### Pitfall: v.17 "not that I seek the gift" as ingratitude

- **What it looks like:** "Paul didn't really want their money."
- **Why it's wrong:** he reframes it so *their* reward, not *his* need, is in view — the deepest thanks.
- **The corrective:** present the reframing as generosity, not coldness.

## Open Questions / Uncertainties

1. **v.13 textual variant** — "him who strengthens me" (shorter, preferred) vs "…me Christ" (added). *Referent is Christ either way.*
2. **v.19 scope** — an unconditional promise, or specifically to generous givers? *Contextually the latter, but grounded in God's character; flag.*
3. **The primary OT locus of *osmē euōdias*** — a *formula* (Gen 8:21 + the burnt-offering legislation + Ezek 20:41), so multiple; high overall, no single "the" source.
4. **The degree of deliberate Stoic engagement in *autarkēs*** — strong redefinition, given v.13.
5. **"Caesar's household" (v.22)** — imperial slaves/freedmen (standard, secure) vs any wider sense.
