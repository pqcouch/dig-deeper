## The Passage

> **1** Paul and Timothy, servants of Christ Jesus, To all the saints in Christ Jesus who are at Philippi, with the overseers and deacons: **2** Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.
> **3** I thank my God in all my remembrance of you, **4** always in every prayer of mine for you all making my prayer with joy, **5** because of your partnership in the gospel from the first day until now. **6** And I am sure of this, that he who began a good work in you will bring it to completion at the day of Jesus Christ. **7** It is right for me to feel this way about you all, because I hold you in my heart, for you are all partakers with me of grace, both in my imprisonment and in the defense and confirmation of the gospel. **8** For God is my witness, how I yearn for you all with the affection of Christ Jesus. **9** And it is my prayer that your love may abound more and more, with knowledge and all discernment, **10** so that you may approve what is excellent, and so be pure and blameless for the day of Christ, **11** filled with the fruit of righteousness that comes through Jesus Christ, to the glory and praise of God.

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims that emerged across multiple tools:

1. **1:1–11 is the letter's overture: the greeting, thanksgiving and prayer *plant* nearly every theme the letter develops — partnership (ch.4), joy (throughout), the day of Christ (2:16; 3:20), discerning love (2:1–4; 4:8), and gospel-suffering (1:12–26) — so this unit is programmatic, not preliminary.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Phase 0.6 (Positional Necessity Check), Tool 11 Move 4 (the opening is the densest planting ground), Author's Purpose. The letter *ends* (ch.4) by doing what it *opens* (ch.1) by praying for.

2. **The thanksgiving is *koinōnia*-shaped: Paul thanks God for their *partnership* in the gospel (v.5), not merely their faith — and *koinōnia* carries a commercial/partnership edge that returns with the gift at 4:15.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Tool 11 Move 4, Historical Background. The gift of chapter 4 is already in view here, without being named.

3. **God's completing work (v.6) is the letter's confidence-thread and controls chapters 2–3 — assurance is God-grounded, not performance-grounded, so the later "work out your salvation" (2:12) is not anxious self-rescue.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary (*epiteleō*), Linking Words, Tool 11 Move 4 (2:13; 3:12–14), Tone. "He who *began*… will *bring to completion*" — the God who starts, finishes.

4. **Paul and Timothy are "servants" (*douloi*), not "apostle" — the letter's ethic is planted in its very first word, anticipating Christ who took "the form of a servant" (2:7).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Tool 11 Move 4. Unique among Paul's undisputed letters as an opening self-designation with no apostolic qualifier.

5. **The opening prayer (vv.9–11) is that love would grow *with knowledge and discernment* — affection disciplined by moral judgement — and this opening petition becomes the closing command ("approve what is excellent", v.10 → "whatever is excellent… think about these things", 4:8).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary (*aisthēsis / dokimazō*), Tool 11 Move 4, Linking Words. Christian love here is not sentiment but a love that *thinks*.

## Phase 0.6 — Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement:** none — this is the book's opening. It *plants* rather than resolves. `[T]`

**Necessity answer:** a Pauline thanksgiving conventionally previews the letter's themes, and this one seeds nearly all of them — partnership (ch.4), joy (throughout), the day of Christ (2:16; 3:20), discerning love (the unity material and 4:8), suffering ("imprisonment", "defence and confirmation of the gospel", v.7). So it must stand *here*, before the argument, as the letter's overture. The greeting also establishes the two poles the letter will hold together — shared servant-identity ("servants… saints… all") and ordered community ("overseers and deacons"). `[T]` / `[I]`

**Implication:** v.6's confidence (God is completing what he began) and vv.9–11's prayer are the *lens* through which every later imperative should be read: because God is completing his work, "work out your own salvation" (2:12) is not anxious self-rescue but the outworking of a God-guaranteed grace. And the singling-out of "overseers and deacons" — unique in a Pauline greeting — anticipates 4:2–3 and the administration of the gift (the leaders likely gathered the collection). This is the letter's densest **planting ground**; Move 4 does most of the work here.

**Revisit after Tool 11:** with no external OT citation (Move 2 nil), the positional weight rests on what this opening *plants* internally — confirming that 1:1–11 is programmatic: the letter is, in large part, the outworking and answering of its own overture.

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book-level** (from the overview): to form a mission-partner church in gospel-worthy steadfastness by the mind of Christ. `[S: overview]`

**Passage-level:** to open the letter by grounding the whole in **gospel partnership, joy, and confidence in God's completing work** — establishing the affectionate, thankful key (a *prison* joy), previewing the themes, and setting the prayer-trajectory (love growing in discernment toward blamelessness at the day of Christ). The thanksgiving is not courtesy but programme: Paul thanks God for the church's *partnership in the gospel*, and prays them toward the maturity the letter will press. `[T]`

---

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check *(see Phase 0.6 above)*

**Immediately before:** nothing — the book's opening. `[T]`

**Immediately after (1:12–26):** Paul's circumstances — "what has happened to me has really served to advance the gospel." The thanksgiving's reference to "my imprisonment… and the defence and confirmation of the gospel" (v.7) sets this up: the church is anxious about Paul, and 1:12–26 answers that anxiety before the thesis of 1:27. `[T]`

**Out-of-context flag:** v.6 ("he who began a good work in you…") is routinely lifted out as a graduation-card verse for personal success, dropping "at the day of Jesus Christ" (God's *persevering* work to consummation) and the corporate, plural "you." The immediate context corrects it: this is churchly assurance, not individual optimism.

---

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|--------|---------|---------|
| 1:1–2 | Greeting | Senders ("servants"), recipients ("saints… with overseers and deacons"), grace-and-peace |
| 1:3–8 | Thanksgiving | One sentence pivoting on "because of your partnership" (v.5); load-bearing truth: "he who began a good work… will bring it to completion" (v.6) |
| 1:9–11 | Intercession | One sentence pivoting on "so that you may approve what is excellent" (vv.9–10); the purpose-chain toward blamelessness at the day and the fruit of righteousness |

**Device:** a standard epistolary overture (prescript → thanksgiving → prayer), but weighted: the thanksgiving and the prayer are both single, purpose-driven sentences whose load-bearing clauses (v.6 truth; vv.9–10 petition) set the letter's confidence and trajectory.

**Clause-flow (vv.9–11):**

```
And it is my prayer                                       ← MAIN CLAUSE (petition)
   that your love may abound more and more,               ← content of the prayer
      with knowledge and all discernment,                 ← the manner: love *with* organs of judgement
   so that you may approve what is excellent,             ← purpose 1 (ἵνα)
   and so be pure and blameless                           ← purpose 2 (result)
      for the day of Christ,                              ← the horizon
   filled with the fruit of righteousness                 ← the accompanying state
      that comes through Jesus Christ,                    ← the source (not self-produced)
      to the glory and praise of God.                     ← the terminus / goal
```

Central assertion: a **petition** — that a *discerning* love would grow toward blamelessness at the day of Christ, bearing Christ-produced fruit, for God's glory. The prayer is Godward (petition), not a to-do list.

### 7. Vocabulary

- **δοῦλοι (v.1), "servants / slaves"** — Paul *and* Timothy, with **no "apostle" qualifier** (unique among the undisputed letters as an opening self-designation). Total belonging; plants → 2:7 (Christ took "the form of a *doulos*") and 2:22 (Timothy "served"). `[T]`
- **ἅγιοι (v.1), "saints"** — set-apart ones; corporate, not an elite class. `[T]`
- **ἐπίσκοποι καὶ διάκονοι (v.1), "overseers and deacons"** — the earliest Pauline naming of these two settled offices, and unusual in a greeting: flag it, don't smooth it (anticipates 4:2–3 / the gift's administration). `[T]`
- **κοινωνία (v.5), "partnership / fellowship / shareholding"** — with a **commercial / business-partnership edge** (*societas*) that returns at 4:15 ("giving and receiving"). The gospel bound Paul and the church as partners in one enterprise. `[T, high]`
- **ἐπιτελέσει (v.6), "will bring to completion / perfect"** — God's completing work; ties to 2:13 ("God who works in you") and 3:12–14. `[T]`
- **ἡμέρα Χριστοῦ (vv.6, 10), "day of Christ"** — the letter's eschatological horizon (2:16; 3:20); the OT "Day of the LORD" now Christ's day (see Tool 11). `[T]`
- **ἐπίγνωσις (v.9), "full knowledge" + αἴσθησις (v.9), "moral perception"** — the latter a NT hapax: love is to grow *with organs of discernment*, not as unbounded sentiment. `[T, high]`
- **δοκιμάζω (v.10), "approve after testing"** + **τὰ διαφέροντα**, "the things that differ / that really matter." `[T]`
- **εἰλικρινεῖς (v.10), "pure"** — etymologically "sun-tested" or "unmixed." `[moderate]`
- **ἐπιποθέω + σπλάγχνα (v.8), "yearn with the affection/bowels"** — visceral affection, and "of Christ Jesus" — Paul loves them with Christ's own tenderness. `[T]`
- **καρπὸς δικαιοσύνης (v.11), "fruit of righteousness"** — possible OT echo (see Tool 11); framed as coming "through Jesus Christ" — Christ-produced, not self-generated. `[T]`

**Proper-noun inventory:** Paul, Timothy, Christ Jesus, **Philippi**, God the Father, the Lord Jesus Christ. *Name-hyperlink (background, cross-reported):* **Philippi** — a Roman *colonia* (Acts 16), the first church founded in Europe, veterans proud of Roman citizenship; freight that detonates at 3:20 ("our citizenship is in heaven"). `[T/S]`

### 8. Translations

- **v.1 "servants" (ESV/NASB/KJV)** — *doulos* is "slave"; the softening loses some of the totality that ties to 2:7. Flag. `[T]`
- **v.6 "he who began a good work in you will bring it to completion"** — stable across versions, but the **"you" is plural** (English hides it). A rendering read individualistically feeds the graduation-card misuse; note the corporate address. `[T]`
- **v.7 "because I hold you in my heart"** — the Greek (*dia to echein me en tē kardia hymas*) is genuinely **ambiguous**: *me* can be the subject ("because I hold *you* in *my* heart") or object ("because *you* hold *me* in *your* hearts"). ESV/NASB take Paul as subject; the reverse is defensible. Flag; the sense of mutual affection survives either way. `[T]`
- **v.10 "approve what is excellent"** — *ta diapheronta* is both "the things that differ" (so: discern between them) and "the things that excel / matter" (so: approve the best). ESV's "approve what is excellent" captures the latter; both nuances are live. `[T]`

**Ancient versions check:** **N/A with reason** — the passage contains no OT citation; the conceptual echoes ("day of Christ", "fruit of righteousness") are not formula quotations of a source text where the moderns agree on a rendering an ancient version disputes. The one weighty Greek-level question (v.7's *me*) is syntactic, not an ancient-version split. Trigger not met.

**Pulpit edition note:** Pulpit text matches report default (ESV Anglicised); no finding depends on a spelling/idiom difference.

### 9. Tone and Feel

**Warm, affectionate, thankful** — a solo cello, not a fanfare. The characteristic joy of the letter is introduced here in a **prison key** (Paul writes in chains, v.7) and as *thankful affection*, not circumstantial cheer: "I thank my God… with joy" (vv.3–4), "I hold you in my heart" (v.7), "I yearn for you all with the affection of Christ Jesus" (v.8). The register is intimate and pastoral; the confidence of v.6 is spoken tenderly, not triumphally. **Soundtrack:** a warm, unhurried prelude — gratitude and longing over a steady ground of assurance. `[T]`

### 10. Repetition

- **πᾶς "all / every" saturates vv.3–8** — "you *all*", "*every* prayer", "*all* of you partakers", "*all* my remembrance": Lightfoot's "studied repetition" of inclusiveness — a quiet instrument of the unity appeal to come. `[T]`
- **"day of (Jesus) Christ" ×2** (vv.6, 10) — brackets the thanksgiving-and-prayer with the eschatological horizon. `[T]`
- **"gospel" (vv.5, 7)** — the partnership and the imprisonment are both "of the gospel." `[T]`
- **"grace" (vv.2, 7)** — grace-and-peace; "partakers of grace." `[T]`
- **"in Christ Jesus / of Christ Jesus / through Jesus Christ"** (vv.1 ×2, 6, 8, 11) — the locative-relational refrain; everything is located in Christ. `[T]`

### 11. Quotation/Allusion

*Independent candidate-finding (from the passage's own vocabulary):* "day of Christ" (vv.6, 10) → the OT "Day of the LORD"; "fruit of righteousness" (v.11) → OT righteousness-fruit language; "began a good work… bring to completion" → possibly the creation motif of God finishing his work. No direct quotation.

*Live sources (overview v0.2):* Isaiah 40–55, Deuteronomy 32, the sacrificial cult — **none cited here.** This is a **citation-light** opening; Move 2 has nothing to track, and the weight falls almost entirely on **Move 4** (internal echo — appropriate, since this is the planting chapter). `[T]`

**"Day of the LORD" → "day of Christ" (vv.6, 10)** *(moderate confidence — conceptual allusion, no verbal quotation)* `[I]`

*Source context (Move 1):* the OT "Day of the LORD" (Joel 2:1; Amos 5:18–20; Zephaniah 1:14; Malachi 4:5) is the day of YHWH's decisive reckoning — judgement and salvation. *What it adds:* Paul names the church's horizon of accountability and hope "the day of **Christ**" — a high-Christology transfer (the Day of *YHWH* becomes the day of *Jesus*) fully consonant with 2:10–11's application of Isaiah 45:23 to Jesus. The church's completion, purity, and fruit are all oriented to *Christ's* day. *Confidence moderate — conceptual; cross-referenced to Biblical Theology and to the 2:10–11 finding.*

**"Fruit of righteousness" (v.11)** *(uncertain — stock OT/wisdom phrase; Move 1 only)* `[I]`

*Source context:* "fruit of righteousness" and cognate images appear in the OT wisdom/prophetic stream (Proverbs 11:30; Amos 6:12; Isaiah 32:17, "the *fruit of righteousness* will be peace"; cf. Hosea 10:12). *What it adds:* if heard, it frames the church's ethical fruit within the OT expectation of righteousness that yields peace and life — but the phrase is somewhat conventional, so weight is low. *Uncertain; do not build on it.*

**Move 4 — internal echo** *(required; the letter's densest planting ground)*

Held open against the whole book, the opening plants:

- *Planted → 2:7, 2:22.* "servants (*douloi*)" (v.1) → Christ took "the form of a *doulos*" (2:7); Timothy "served" (2:22). `[planted]`
- *Planted → 4:2–3.* "overseers and deacons" (v.1) → the leaders / "true companion" and fellow-workers who help the two women (4:2–3), and the administration of the gift. `[planted]`
- *Planted → 4:15; 4:10–20.* *koinōnia* "from the first day until now" (v.5) → "in the beginning of the gospel… partnership in giving and receiving" (4:15); the partnership/joy **inclusio** (1:3–7 ↔ 4:10–20). `[planted — inclusio]`
- *Planted → 2:13; 3:12–14; 3:20–21.* "he who began a good work… will complete it" (v.6) → "God who works in you" (2:13), "I press on" (3:12–14), the transforming Saviour (3:20–21) — the confidence thread. `[planted]`
- *Planted → 2:16; 3:20–21.* "day of (Jesus) Christ" (vv.6, 10) → 2:16; 3:20–21 — the eschatological through-line. `[planted]`
- *Planted → 4:8.* the prayer for love "with knowledge and all discernment… approve what is excellent" (vv.9–10) → "whatever is excellent… think about these things" (4:8): the opening petition becomes the closing command. `[planted — resolved at 4:8]`
- *Planted → 1:12–26; 1:30; 4:4.* "partakers… in my imprisonment" (v.7) → the imprisonment narrative (1:12–26); joy (v.4) → "rejoice… again I will say, rejoice" (4:4). `[planted]`

**Addressee-differentiation:** the greeting names a distinct party — "overseers and deacons" — which the letter later engages specifically at 4:2–3 (the "true companion" and fellow-workers helping Euodia and Syntyche); and "all the saints" plants the inclusive *pas* that the unity appeals cash out.

`Internal: densely planted — this is the letter's densest planting ground; the letter ends (ch.4) by doing what it opened (ch.1) by praying for.`

### 12. Genre

Pauline **epistolary prescript + thanksgiving + intercession** — a Greco-Roman letter opening adapted for gospel purposes ("grace and peace" replacing the conventional "greetings"; God and the Lord Jesus as co-source). Reading rule: the thanksgiving is **programmatic**, not merely polite — Pauline thanksgivings preview the letter's concerns, so read vv.3–11 as a map of what is coming. `[T]`

### 13. Copycat

Largely **N/A** — epistle, no narrative characters to imitate or avoid. ### 14. Bible Timeline

Church age, in the already/not-yet: God's good work *begun* and being *completed* toward the **future "day of Christ"** (vv.6, 10). We stand where the Philippians stood — partners in the gospel between Christ's first coming and his day, dependent on the God who finishes what he starts. "The fruit of righteousness… through Jesus Christ" is this-side-of-the-cross fruit. `[T]`

---

### 15. Who Am I?

Direct NT epistle address — the "you" is **plural** throughout: we identify with "all the saints… at Philippi", the church addressed and prayed for. **God** is the actor (v.6); **Christ** the ground and source (v.11). We do not identify with Paul, though his intercession is a model for how we pray for one another. `[T]`

### 16. So What?

**Author's intended response:** joyful, thankful **confidence in God's completing work**, expressed in gospel partnership and in a love that grows in discernment toward the day of Christ.

**Worldview:** Christian assurance rests on *God's* persevering work (v.6), not on performance; the church is a *partnership* in the gospel, not a collection of individuals; love is meant to *think* (vv.9–10).

**Behaviour:** *start* — partnering in the gospel (prayer, money, suffering, shared labour), interceding for one another with thanksgiving and affection, pursuing a discerning love; *stop* — grounding assurance in one's own performance, or treating joy as circumstance-dependent.

**Motivation (against moralism):** the coming day of Christ, the God who completes what he began (v.6), and the affection of Christ (v.8). The prayer's targets (love, discernment, blamelessness, fruit) are grounded in God's work and come "through Jesus Christ… to the glory of God" (v.11) — gift and doxology, not self-improvement.

**Four audiences:**
- *For me:* do I rest my assurance in God's completing work, or in my own performance?
- *For a Christian friend:* the anxious believer who fears their labour or faith won't last — v.6 is for them.
- *For the church:* partnership and a discerning, growing love — the very things the letter will press toward unity.
- *For the unbeliever:* the "fruit of righteousness" comes *through Jesus Christ*, not by self-effort — righteousness is received and bears fruit, to God's glory.

**Prayer:** thanksgiving for gospel partners; that our love would abound *with* knowledge and discernment; confidence in the God who will complete what he has begun, to the glory and praise of God.

**Grammatical/syntactical features:**

### Historical and Cultural Background

**Setting:** the church at **Philippi**, a Roman *colonia* (Acts 16), the first church founded in Europe — a city of army veterans and their families, intensely proud of Roman citizenship (freight that surfaces at 3:20).

**What the original audience knew that we miss:**
- **"Overseers and deacons"** (v.1) — the naming of settled church officers this early is striking; these leaders very likely gathered and sent the church's gift (ch.4), which is why they are greeted by name. `[T/S]`
- ***koinōnia*** carried the sense of a **business partnership** (*societas*) — a shared enterprise with shared investment and return; the Philippians are Paul's gospel *partners*, not merely well-wishers. `[T/S]`
- **Paul writes in chains** (v.7) — the warm, joyful tone is a prison tone; the original readers, anxious about him, would have felt the poignancy of his confidence. `[T]`

**What this changes:** the greeting is not throat-clearing but the establishment of a *partnership* the whole letter presupposes; the thanksgiving's warmth is costly, spoken from imprisonment.

### Original Audience Reception

**Canonical audience:** gospel-aware, largely Gentile believers, materially generous and personally attached to Paul, anxious about his imprisonment and their own standing.

**Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances:**
- *Surprising:* "servants", not "apostle"; the naming of their own officers in the greeting.
- *Comforting:* v.6 — God will *complete* what he began; Paul's confident affection from prison.
- *Disturbing (gently):* the prayer sets a high trajectory — a love that grows in discernment toward blamelessness at the day of Christ.

### Biblical-Theological Themes

**Day of the LORD → day of Christ.** The OT day of YHWH's reckoning (Joel; Amos; Zephaniah; Malachi) becomes "the day of *Christ*" (vv.6, 10) — a high-Christology transfer consonant with 2:10–11. *Where this sits:* the church's horizon of hope and accountability, now Christ's. *Confidence:* moderate. `[I]`

**The God who completes his work.** From creation's "finished" work (Gen 2:1–2) through the covenant God's faithfulness to the consummation (Rom 8:29–30), the God who *begins* a good work *finishes* it (v.6; 2:13). *Confidence:* high (the theme is textually named). `[T]`

**Koinōnia — partnership in the gospel.** Fellowship as a shared enterprise binds the gift-occasion (ch.4) to the theology of the whole letter (1:5; 4:15). *Confidence:* high. `[T]`

**Confluence — mutual illumination / climactic.** The three converge: the God who **completes** his good work will do so **at the day of Christ**, and the present form of that God-guaranteed work is the church's **partnership** in the gospel. The confidence of v.6 grounds both the partnership (v.5) and the discerning-love prayer (vv.9–11) — assurance is the soil in which partnership and maturing love grow.

### Christological Reading

NT passage — Christ named throughout, chiefly in the **ground** register: the completing God works toward "the day of *Christ*" (v.6); the "fruit of righteousness" comes "*through Jesus Christ*" (v.11); grace and peace flow from "the *Lord* Jesus Christ" (v.2). The one **pattern** seed is *douloi* (v.1), which the hymn will cash out in Christ's servant-form (2:7).

**Moralism check.** The prayer's targets (love, discernment, blamelessness, fruit) can be preached as a self-improvement checklist. The text forbids it: they are the content of *petition to God*, grounded in God's completing work (v.6), and the fruit is Christ-produced ("through Jesus Christ") *to God's glory* (v.11). Preach the God who works and the Christ who bears fruit in his people — not targets to be hit by willpower. `[T]`

### Difficult / Contested Verses

**v.6 — "he who began a good work… will bring it to completion" (perseverance / assurance).**
- *Category:* doctrinal / weaponised.
- *The difficulty:* (a) popular misuse as generic optimism or career-success promise; (b) the Reformed/Arminian question of whether it guarantees *individual* final perseverance.
- *Rhetorical function:* assurance that grounds the letter's imperatives — God is completing his work, therefore the church can obey without anxiety.
- *Honest exegesis:* the confidence is real and God-grounded, addressed corporately ("in *you* all", plural) and oriented to "the day of Christ"; the precise Calvinist/Arminian question lives here — flag without forcing.
- *Handling:* preach the God-grounded assurance and the corporate address; dismantle the graduation-card misuse.

**v.7 — "I hold you in my heart" (grammatical ambiguity).**
- *Category:* apologetic / minor.
- *The difficulty:* the Greek allows "I hold you" or "you hold me."
- *Handling:* note the ambiguity if a hearer is comparing versions; the mutual affection is the point either way.

**v.11 — "fruit of righteousness" (imputed vs imparted).**
- *Category:* doctrinal.
- *The difficulty:* is this justification-fruit or ethical fruit?
- *Honest exegesis:* the phrase names the *ethical produce* of a righteous life, but explicitly "through Jesus Christ" and "to the glory of God" — Christ-sourced and God-glorifying, not self-generated; it does not pit against justification but flows from union with Christ.
- *Handling:* keep the fruit Christ-sourced and God-directed; avoid moralising it into self-produced merit.

**vv.9–11 — the prayer as a moral checklist.**
- *Category:* pastoral / moralism.
- *The difficulty:* preaching the petition's targets as things the hearer must simply achieve.
- *Handling:* preserve it as *prayer* — Godward petition — and ground the targets in v.6's completing God.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: 1:6 as generic optimism / a graduation-card verse

- **What it looks like:** "God has a good plan and will see your dreams through to success."
- **Why it's wrong:** the "good work" is God's *saving* work; the completion is "at the day of Jesus Christ"; the "you" is plural and corporate.
- **The corrective:** preach God's persevering work to the day of Christ as churchly assurance that grounds obedience (cf. 2:12–13), not personal optimism.

### Pitfall: The prayer (vv.9–11) as a self-improvement checklist

- **What it looks like:** "Here are five things to work on: more love, more discernment, more purity…"
- **Why it's wrong:** it is *petition to God*; the fruit comes "through Jesus Christ, to the glory of God."
- **The corrective:** preach it as prayer — ask God for what only God completes (v.6).

### Pitfall: "Joy" as circumstantial happiness

- **What it looks like:** joy presented as a mood available when things go well.
- **Why it's wrong:** this joy is dictated from prison (v.7) and rooted in gospel partnership and God's completing work.
- **The corrective:** preach joy in a prison key — thankful, gospel-grounded, not circumstance-dependent.

### Pitfall: Treating the greeting as skippable preamble

- **What it looks like:** hurrying past vv.1–2 to "get to the content."
- **Why it's wrong:** the greeting plants the letter — "servants", "saints", "all", the named officers, "grace and peace" — and sets the two poles (shared servanthood / ordered community).
- **The corrective:** preach the overture as programmatic; the letter is largely its own opening worked out.

### Pitfall: Moralising "fruit of righteousness" (v.11)

- **What it looks like:** "produce enough good works to be counted righteous."
- **Why it's wrong:** the fruit comes "through Jesus Christ… to the glory of God" — Christ-produced, God-glorifying.
- **The corrective:** keep the fruit Christ-sourced and doxological.
