# Philippians — Research Questions, Round Four

**Compiled:** 15 August 2026 · **Status:** Of the 76 questions the corpus originally raised, **62 are now answered.** These are the **fourteen that remain**, re-aimed once more.

## What this round is for

Round three closed most of the interpretive cruxes. What is left divides cleanly into two very different kinds of thing, and the difference matters more than the count.

**The first kind is a block of ten questions that has now gone three rounds with no research attached at all** — five on textual criticism, five on historical background. These are not hard questions. They went unanswered because each round's effort went, understandably, to the cruxes that were about to be preached. **They are the corpus's only untouched ground, and eight of the ten could be cleared in a single sitting** aimed at the *Lexham Textual Notes*, Keown's EEC, Reumann's Anchor Yale commentary and the Anchor Yale Bible Dictionary — all demonstrably in the library.

**The second kind is four items that have been asked and have not come back properly.** One of them (R19) came back in round four as **uncited general knowledge**, which is precisely the register that question cannot be answered in. The other three are small verifications that would let a hedged claim be stated flatly.

**Priority marks** — ★ load-bearing · • worth having · ○ interest only.

**A note on what has been closed since round three.** Eleven questions were answered in round four: the extent of the confession at 2:10–11 (★), "fear and trembling" at 2:12, the drink offering at 2:17, 1:6 and perseverance, 1:29's divine passive, the pre-Pauline hymn question, Psalm 145 at 4:5 (in part), the Vulgate at 1:19, the *phroneō* version-comparison, the "all of you" density, and the patron-client refusal (★). Two more have been closed since, without research: **the KJV/TR verse order at 1:16–17** (settled by opening a KJV — see the note at the end) and the *phroneō* rendering question. All are folded into the reports, the overview (now v0.7.0) and the macro-synthesis (now v5).

## 1 · Textual criticism — three rounds, no research

*All four remaining questions are aimed at the same two resources. The Lexham Textual Notes answered a question in round one, so it is certainly in the library; Keown's EEC is a full critical commentary. **Ask these four together, in one query if the interface allows it.***

**R1 · The variant at 2:30** •
"What do the *Lexham Textual Notes on the Bible* and Keown's *Philippians* (Evangelical Exegetical Commentary) say about the variant at Philippians 2:30 — παραβολευσάμενος against παραβουλευσάμενος? Which is preferred and on what grounds?"

*Turns on:* the vividness of Epaphroditus's risk — "having gambled with his life" against "not regarding his life". The KJV follows the second reading, so a KJV hearer has a materially different picture. *(Was Q31.)*

**R2 · The variant at 3:3** •
"What do the *Lexham Textual Notes* and Keown's EEC record at Philippians 3:3 — πνεύματι θεοῦ against πνεύματι θεῷ — and which is preferred?"

*Turns on:* whether the Spirit is the **means** of the worship or "in spirit" its **manner**. The ESV footnotes it, so it is visible in the pew. *(Was Q32.)*

**R3 · The longer reading at 3:16** ○
"Do the *Lexham Textual Notes* record a longer Byzantine reading at Philippians 3:16 — 'let us walk by the same rule, let us mind the same thing' — and is it judged an assimilation, and to what?"

*Turns on:* a KJV reader noticing a missing clause. The corpus already records that the KJV expands the verse; what is open is the apparatus's judgement and, if it is an assimilation, to which passage. *(Was Q33.)*

**R4 · "Christ" at 4:13** •
"Do the *Lexham Textual Notes* or Keown's EEC discuss the addition of Χριστῷ at Philippians 4:13, and how is it judged?"

*Turns on:* KJV hearers will know the verse as "through Christ which strengtheneth me", and 4:13 is the most-quoted verse in the letter. The referent is Christ either way — but a preacher correcting the popular misuse of 4:13 should know exactly what the text says before correcting anything. *(Was Q34.)*

**R5 · The manuscript evidence at 1:16–17** — *partly closed; see below.* ○
"How does the apparatus weigh the transposition of Philippians 1:16 and 1:17 — which witnesses reverse the verses, and how decisively is the critical order preferred?"

*Turns on:* the chiasm at 1:15–17. **The KJV/TR half of this question is now settled** (see the closing note), so what remains is only the manuscript judgement — whether the critical order is as secure as the corpus assumes. *(Refines Q30.)*

## 2 · Historical and cultural background — the other untouched block

*Round three answered three of this section's questions — women in Roman Macedonia, "Caesar's household", and provenance — and returned a useful negative on the* cursus honorum *reading of 3:5–6. **Two remain.** Both are aimed at Reumann's Anchor Yale commentary and* Paul in the Greco-Roman World, *both of which the library holds.*

**R7 · Imperial titles** •
"Do Reumann (Anchor Yale), Witherington, or the Logos Research Commentary on Philippians treat κύριος and σωτήρ as **current imperial titles** in a Roman colony of this period? How strongly do they read Philippians 2:11 and 3:20 as counter-imperial — as deliberate polemic, or as resonance a first hearer would feel without Paul intending confrontation?"

*Turns on:* **macro-argument 9**, the counter-imperial citizenship thread, which is one of the twelve and is preached. Round two's answer on the colony supplied Garland and Thielman on 3:20 generally; this asks specifically about **the titles**, which is where the polemic would have to live if it is there. *(Was Q58.)*

**R10 · Prison conditions** ○
"What does *Paul in the Greco-Roman World*, Reumann, or the Anchor Yale Bible Dictionary say about what a prisoner in Roman custody depended on friends and family to supply — food, clothing, money, advocacy? How does that bear on Philippians 1:7 and 4:10–20?"

*Turns on:* making "partakers with me of grace… in my imprisonment" (1:7) **concrete**. If a Roman prisoner ate only what friends brought, then the Philippians' gift is not a courtesy but a lifeline, and the whole of 4:10–20 gains weight. This is interest-only for the exegesis and gold for the pulpit. *(Was Q61.)*

## 3 · Asked, and not properly answered

**R19 · 3:6 "blameless" and the New Perspective** ★
"How is 'as to righteousness under the law, blameless' (Philippians 3:6) treated in the New Perspective debate? **What specifically do Thompson (Paideia), Silva (BECNT) and Wright say about this verse** — is Paul claiming an unblemished external record, a covenant standing, or something else? Please cite the works."

*Turns on:* the report presents the external-record reading with the debate flagged, and 3:4–8 will be preached. **Round four returned an answer explicitly given from general knowledge, with Matera reported as addressing the verse only indirectly.** That is exactly what this question cannot use: the whole point is what the named scholars say. The library demonstrably holds *Justification: Five Views* and Bates — round two's *pistis Christou* answer reached them — so the register is available. **If the sourced answer does not come, the corpus should say the debate is flagged and stop there, rather than characterise positions it has not read.** *(Was Q70; asked twice.)*

**R23 · The Vulgate at 4:7** ★
"How does the Latin Vulgate render φρουρήσει at Philippians 4:7 — with a future indicative (*custodiet*) or with the subjunctive (*custodiat*)? And do the standard editions differ?"

*Turns on:* the corpus currently claims **only** that the Greek is a future indicative (secure) and that the traditional benediction renders it as a wish (verified in round two). **The transmission claim — that the wish-form descends through the Latin — is unsupported and has been deliberately softened in the overview.** A straight look at a Vulgate text settles it in a minute, and would let Pitfall 3 be stated with its history intact instead of hedged. **Note that the parallel question at 1:19 (R24) came back a clean negative** — the Vulgate does *not* preserve the Job 13:16 link — which shows this kind of question is answerable and worth asking. *(Was Q74.)*

**R25 · The 2:5 ellipsis, properly sourced** ★
"What verb do Martin (TNTC), Silva (BECNT), Keown (EEC) and Hawthorne (WBC) each supply in the relative clause at Philippians 2:5 — ὃ καὶ ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ — and how does each one's choice bear on the 'ethical' versus 'kerygmatic' reading of the hymn?"

*Turns on:* **the most consequential translation decision in the letter's thesis section.** ESV "which is yours in Christ Jesus" (the mind you have *because you are in Christ*) against KJV "which was also in Christ Jesus" (the mind Christ *himself* had). The first makes the hymn the ground of the command; the second makes it the model. Round two explained the two options clearly but **from general knowledge, with no commentary cited.** A verse this load-bearing should not rest on an unsourced explanation. *(Refines Q24.)*

**R22 · The reach of Psalm 145** — *half-closed* •
"Does any commentator extend a Psalm 145 background from Philippians 4:5 to **4:19** ('my God will supply every need of yours'), given 145:16 and 145:19 on God satisfying the desire of every living thing?"

*Turns on:* round four **confirmed the 4:5 echo** of Psalm 145:18 — Park, Storms and Pao all attest it, including the prayer-context link to 4:6 that this corpus had proposed independently. **The extension to 4:19 remains unattested and is still the corpus's own.** It is currently held at moderate and kept out of the intertextual table. Either find support or leave it there. *(Was Q40.)*

## 4 · Passage-level questions the corpus has parked

*These were filed in the individual digs and never escalated to a research round. None is load-bearing; each is listed here so that the corpus's open questions live in one place. **Most are lexicon questions that a single BDAG session would clear** — and BDAG is the one resource three rounds have consistently failed to reach, so it may be worth establishing whether the library has it at all before asking any of them.*

**Lexical.** The nuance of *aisthēsis* (1:9, an NT hapax); the sense of *gnōrizō* at 1:22 ("I do not know" against "I do not declare" — Martin contests the corpus's reading here, and Pitfall 6 leans on it); the force of *ephʼ hō* at 3:12 (causal against relative-telic); the precise nuance of *epieikes* at 4:5, where the translation spread is unusually wide.

**Intertextual, held below the line.** Daniel 12 read across the letter (12:3 → 2:15 *phōstēres*; 12:1 → 4:3 "book of life") — **a candidate this corpus generated itself and has never tested**; the Psalm 8:6 echo at 3:21; the strength of the Deuteronomy 30:6 heart-circumcision allusion at 3:3; Isaiah 49:4 against 65:23 as the primary "labour in vain" source at 2:16.

**Historical detail.** Whether *praitōrion* at 1:13 means the guard or the residence; papyrological support for the *apechō* receipt formula at 4:18; how deliberately Paul engages Stoicism at 4:11.

**Genuinely unresolvable, and recorded as such.** The identity of the *syzygos* at 4:3; the cause of Epaphroditus's illness. These are listed only so that no future round spends effort on them.

## Closed since round three, without research

**The KJV/TR verse order at 1:16–17 is settled, and the answer matters for preaching.** The KJV reads **1:16** "The one preach Christ of contention, not sincerely, supposing to add affliction to my bonds" and **1:17** "But the other of love, knowing that I am set for the defence of the gospel" — the **reverse** of the critical text, which has love at v.16 and rivalry at v.17.

So the chiasm the corpus reads at 1:15–17 — envy and strife, then good will, then love, then rivalry, with the **good-motive preachers at the centre** — **exists only in the critical order.** In the KJV it is a plain alternation and the centre is gone. Anyone following a KJV will find the two verses swapped. *(The manuscript judgement behind the critical order is a separate question and stays open as R5.)*

**The *phroneō* rendering question is settled too.** Across seven versions — ESV, NASB95, NIV, KJV, NKJV, CSB, NRSV — **not one uses a single English word throughout**, and **every one of them switches at 4:10**, to "concern" or "care". The one occurrence that describes the Philippians' money is the one occurrence every translation obscures. The corpus's claim that "no version lets a hearer see it" is now verified rather than assumed.
