## The Passage

> **12** I want you to know, brothers, that what has happened to me has really served to advance the gospel, **13** so that it has become known throughout the whole imperial guard and to all the rest that my imprisonment is for Christ. **14** And most of the brothers, having become confident in the Lord by my imprisonment, are much more bold to speak the word without fear. **15** Some indeed preach Christ from envy and rivalry, but others from good will. **16** The latter do it out of love, knowing that I am put here for the defense of the gospel. **17** The former proclaim Christ out of selfish ambition, not sincerely but thinking to afflict me in my imprisonment. **18** What then? Only that in every way, whether in pretense or in truth, Christ is proclaimed, and in that I rejoice. Yes, and I will rejoice, **19** for I know that through your prayers and the help of the Spirit of Jesus Christ this will turn out for my deliverance, **20** as it is my eager expectation and hope that I will not be at all ashamed, but that with full courage now as always Christ will be honored in my body, whether by life or by death. **21** For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain. **22** If I am to live in the flesh, that means fruitful labor for me. Yet which I shall choose I cannot tell. **23** I am hard pressed between the two. My desire is to depart and be with Christ, for that is far better. **24** But to remain in the flesh is more necessary on your account. **25** Convinced of this, I know that I will remain and continue with you all, for your progress and joy in the faith, **26** so that in coming to you again I may share abundantly in Christ Jesus through your boasting in me, because of my being in Christ Jesus.

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims that emerged across multiple tools:

1. **Paul reads his imprisonment through the *gospel's advance*, not his own comfort — the *euangelion*, not Paul, is the subject; he can even rejoice at rivals preaching Christ from bad motives (v.18).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Author's Purpose, Repetition ("Christ" ~9×, "gospel"), Linking Words (the "what then?" verdict of v.18). The chains are read as a pulpit, not a setback.

2. **1:12–26 is *paradigm-setting*, not news-sharing: before commanding the church to stand firm and unafraid (1:27–30), Paul shows fearless, gospel-centred suffering *in himself* — "the same conflict that you saw I had" (1:30) is established here first.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Phase 0.6 (Positional Necessity Check), Tool 2, Tool 11 Move 4 (*prokopē* → 1:27). His example is the premise of his exhortation.

3. **1:19 quotes Job 13:16 LXX verbatim — Paul places himself in the righteous sufferer's confidence of vindication before God; his "deliverance / salvation" (*sōtēria*) is eschatological vindication (he may yet die, v.20), not merely release from prison.** `[T]` for the verbal match; `[I]` for the conscious-invocation framing → **moderate–high** (matching the overview's rating). Surfaced by: Tool 11 (triad), Tool 8 (the LXX wording), Vocabulary (*aischynthēsomai*, "be ashamed" — the honour-shame courtroom).

4. **"To live is Christ, and to die is gain" (v.21) is *grounded*, not sentimental — the *gar* ties it to v.20's "Christ honoured… whether by life or by death"; and "gain" is cashed out as "to depart and *be with Christ*" (v.23) — the intermediate state, "far better", but not yet the resurrection (3:21).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Linking Words, Structure, Vocabulary (*kerdos / analysai*), Bible Timeline. Union with Christ makes both life and death win.

5. **The unit is bracketed by *prokopē* ("advance") — the gospel's advance (v.12) and the church's advance (v.25) — so the frame is itself the theology: God advances his cause *through* his servant's chains.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Structure (inclusio), Repetition. Paul's imprisonment serves both advances.

## Phase 0.6 — Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement:** the thanksgiving and prayer (1:3–11) named "my imprisonment… and the defence and confirmation of the gospel" (v.7) and voiced confidence that "he who began a good work… will bring it to completion" (v.6). The church is anxious about Paul. `[T]`

**Necessity answer:** before Paul can call the church to "stand firm… and not [be] frightened" under their own opposition (1:27–30), he must show them what fearless, gospel-centred suffering *looks like in himself* — so this autobiographical unit must precede the thesis. His example is the premise of his exhortation: "engaged in the same conflict that you saw I had" (1:30) is established *here* first. 1:12–26 is not news-sharing but **paradigm-setting**. `[T]` / `[I]`

**Implication:** the passage exists here to reframe *suffering* (imprisonment advances the gospel, vv.12–14) and *death* ("to die is gain", v.21) before the church is asked to be fearless. It also **plants**: *prokopē* (v.12, 25) → the church's "standing firm… striving side by side" (1:27); "whether by life or death" (v.20) → 2:17 ("even if I am poured out"); "your boasting" (*kauchēma*, v.26) → 2:16 (Paul's boast at the day of Christ).

**Revisit after Tool 11:** the Job 13:16 citation confirms the *wisdom-lament / vindication* register already latent in 1:9–11 and v.20's *aischynthēsomai* — Paul frames his trial as the righteous sufferer's, sharpening why the passage must precede the call to fearless suffering.

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book-level** (from the overview): to form a mission-partner church in gospel-worthy steadfastness by the mind of Christ. `[S: overview]`

**Passage-level:** to **reassure the anxious church about Paul** by reframing his imprisonment as gospel *advance* (not defeat) and his possible death as *gain*, and to **model** the Christ-centred fearlessness he will shortly command (1:27–30). The *gospel*, not Paul, is the subject throughout: he can even rejoice that rivals preach Christ from bad motives (v.18), because the point is that Christ is proclaimed and Christ is honoured (v.20). `[T]`

---

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check *(see Phase 0.6 above)*

**Immediately before (1:3–11):** the thanksgiving/prayer, with its mention of "my imprisonment" (v.7) and its confidence in God's completing work (v.6). `[T]`

**Immediately after (1:27–30):** the thesis — "let your manner of life be worthy of the gospel… standing firm… not frightened… engaged in the same conflict that you saw I had." The church's turn to do what Paul has just modelled. `[T]`

**Out-of-context flags:** v.21 preached as a sentimental epitaph; v.23 ("depart and be with Christ") collapsed into resurrection hope (it is the *intermediate state*, "far better", but not yet the resurrection body of 3:21); v.18 ("Christ is proclaimed" from any motive) misused as indifference to a false *gospel*.

---

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|--------|---------|---------|
| 1:12–18a | Imprisonment advances the gospel | The advance (imperial guard, emboldened brothers, vv.12–14); two groups of preachers, good/bad motive (vv.15–17); the verdict — "Christ is proclaimed" (v.18a) |
| 1:18b–26 | Life or death, and why Paul expects to remain | "I will rejoice"; deliverance; Christ honoured whether by life or death (vv.18b–20); the dilemma "to live is Christ / to die is gain" (vv.21–24); convinced he will remain for their progress and joy (vv.25–26) |

**Hinge:** the double "I rejoice / I will rejoice" (v.18). **Device:** a linear autobiographical reflection framed by an *inclusio of advance* — the gospel's *prokopē* (v.12) and the church's *prokopē* (v.25). The connectors carry the argument's weight (see Linking Words).

**Clause-flow (vv.20–24 — the dilemma):**

```
…Christ will be honoured in my body,                      ← the settled expectation (v.20)
   whether by life or by death.
For to me                                                 ← ground ("for", γάρ)
   to live is Christ,                                     ← the two articular infinitives, in antithesis
   and to die is gain.
If I am to live in the flesh,
   that means fruitful labour for me.
   Yet which I shall choose I cannot tell.
I am hard pressed between the two:                         ← "held/squeezed" from both sides (synechomai)
   my desire is to depart and be with Christ,              ← option A: "far better" (for Paul)
      for that is far better;
   but to remain in the flesh                              ← option B: "more necessary" (for them)
      is more necessary on your account.
```

Central assertion: a **statement of union** ("to live is Christ, and to die is gain") from which a pastoral resolution follows — Paul will *remain* for the church's sake, though presence with Christ is "far better." Both options are *gain*; the tie-breaker is love for the church.

### 7. Vocabulary

- **προκοπή (*prokopē*), vv.12, 25 — "advance / progress"** — a pioneering/military word (cutting a way forward); it brackets the unit (the *gospel's* advance, v.12 ↔ the *church's* advance, v.25). `[T]`
- **πραιτώριον (v.13), "imperial guard / praetorium"** — the praetorian guard (personnel) or the governor's residence (place); either way, the gospel has reached the heart of Roman power (cf. 4:22, "Caesar's household"). `[T/S]`
- **ἀποκαραδοκία (v.20), "eager expectation"** — literally craning the neck, watching intently. `[T]`
- **αἰσχυνθήσομαι (v.20), "be ashamed"** — honour-shame register; the very concern of Job 13 and Isaiah 50's vindication-hope (see Tool 11). `[T]`
- **σωτηρία (v.19), "deliverance / salvation"** — here primarily *eschatological vindication* (he may yet die, v.20), not merely release from prison; the word of Job 13:16 LXX. `[T]`
- **κέρδος (v.21), "gain"** — the ledger word again (cf. 3:7–8; 4:15) — "to die is *gain*." `[T]`
- **ἀναλῦσαι (v.23), "to depart"** — a gentle euphemism for death (loosing a ship's moorings / striking camp). `[T]`
- **"the Spirit of Jesus Christ" (v.19)** — *pneuma Iēsou Christou*, an unusual designation (the Spirit as Christ's own Spirit). `[T]`
- **μεγαλυνθήσεται (v.20), "be magnified / honoured"** — Christ made great "in my body." `[T]`
- **καύχημα (v.26), "ground of boasting"** — planted → 2:16 (Paul's boast at the day of Christ). `[T]`

**Proper-noun inventory:** Christ (Jesus); the praetorium (imperial guard). No OT names or numbers — the one OT engagement is the Job citation (Tool 11), not a name-hyperlink.

### 8. Translations

- **v.19 "deliverance" (ESV/NASB) / "salvation" (KJV)** — *sōtēria*. The KJV's "salvation" preserves the link to **Job 13:16 LXX** (same word) and the eschatological-vindication sense; "deliverance" can be misread as "release from prison." Flag — the rendering bears on the allusion (cross-reported to Tool 11). `[T]`
- **v.13 "imperial guard" (ESV) / "praetorian guard" (NASB) / "palace" (KJV)** — *praitōrion* is the guard (personnel) or the residence (place); ESV/NASB take it as the guard. `[T]`
- **v.23 "depart" (ESV/NASB/KJV)** — *analysai*, a euphemism; "depart" rightly keeps the gentleness. `[T]`

**Ancient versions check *(trigger met at v.19)*.** Paul's "this will turn out for my *sōtēria*" reproduces the **LXX** of Job 13:16 (*touto moi apobēsetai eis sōtērian*) essentially verbatim. The Hebrew (MT) of Job 13:16 carries the sense "this also will be my *salvation/deliverance*, that a godless man will not come before him" — a confidence of *vindication*; the LXX makes the same point with *sōtēria*, the word Paul lifts. **Translation-tradition note:** the allusion is to the *Greek* Job; the "deliverance/salvation" Paul expects is Job's confidence of vindication at the divine bar, not mere acquittal. *Confidence high for the LXX wording; cross-reported to Tool 11.* `[T]`

**Pulpit edition note:** Pulpit text matches report default (ESV Anglicised); no finding depends on a spelling/idiom difference — though the preacher may wish to note the *sōtēria* = Job link where the ESV reads "deliverance."

### 9. Tone and Feel

**Serene, even buoyant — from a prison.** The tonal paradox is itself the message: not stoic resignation but Christ-centred equanimity. The passage moves from calm reportage (vv.12–18) to a rare, intimate window on Paul's inner deliberation (vv.21–24 — "I am hard pressed between the two… my desire is to depart") and closes in warm pastoral resolve (vv.25–26, choosing them over "far better"). **Soundtrack:** a settled, unhurried voice from a cell — untroubled, even glad — with one moment of tender longing before it turns back, for their sake, to the work. A flat or grim delivery misses the astonishing peace of the passage. `[T]`

### 11. Quotation/Allusion

*Independent candidate-finding (from the passage's own vocabulary):* v.19 "this will turn out for my *sōtēria*" → Job 13:16 LXX (verbatim); v.20 "not be at all ashamed" → the honour-shame vindication register (Job; Isa 50:7). "Spirit of Jesus Christ" (v.19) carries no OT allusion.

*Live sources (overview v0.2):* the wisdom-lament register (Job/Psalms) and the vindication motif (Isa 50 / the Servant) are live; Isaiah 40–55 is the letter's chief well.

**Job 13:16 (LXX) → 1:19** *(moderate–high — verbal match high; conscious-allusion framing moderate)* `[T]` verbal · `[I]` framing

*Source context (Move 1):* Job, maintaining his integrity against the friends, resolves to "argue my ways to his face" (13:15) and declares, "this also will be my *salvation* (LXX *sōtēria*), that a godless man shall not come before him" (13:16). His very willingness to bring his case *is* evidence that he is no hypocrite; the "salvation" is his confidence of **vindication at the divine tribunal** in the midst of undeserved suffering. Immediately before, Job risks everything on being heard by God; immediately after, he prepares his case. The verse's function: the righteous sufferer's assurance that God will vindicate him.

*Book usage (Move 2):* the first and only invocation of Job in the letter — but it confirms the *wisdom-lament / vindication* register already latent in 1:9–11 and in v.20's *aischynthēsomai* ("be ashamed"). No earlier Job citation to develop; the register, not a running source, is what carries. `[T]`

*OT-to-OT (Move 3):* Job's vindication-hope stands alongside **Isaiah 50:7–9** — the Servant's "he who vindicates me is near… who will declare me guilty?" — the same honour-shame courtroom Paul's *aischynthēsomai* invokes. Job 13 and Isaiah 50 already converse in the vindication register; and Isaiah 50 is a Servant text, tying this passage's righteous-sufferer motif to the Servant reading of 2:6–11. `[I, moderate]`

*What it adds:* Paul's "deliverance" (*sōtēria*, v.19) is primarily **eschatological vindication**, not release from prison — he says in the very next breath that Christ will be honoured "whether by life or by *death*" (v.20). Paul reads his trial in Job's words — but with a decisive transposition: Job's confidence rested on his own integrity ("a godless man shall not come before him"); Paul's rests on *Christ* ("to me to live is Christ"). The righteous sufferer's vindication has become **Christ himself**.

**Move 4 — internal echo** *(required; positive)*

- *Answers §1:6–7.* "Convinced (*pepoithōs*) I will remain" (v.25) echoes the confidence of v.6 ("I am sure… he… will complete it"); "partakers of grace in my imprisonment" (1:7) is now unfolded. `[answers]`
- *Planted → 1:27.* *prokopē* (v.25, the church's "progress") → their "standing firm… striving side by side for the faith" (1:27). `[planted]`
- *Planted → 2:17.* "whether by life or by death" (v.20) → "even if I am poured out as a drink offering" (2:17). `[planted]`
- *Planted → 2:16.* "your boasting" (*kauchēma*, v.26) → "so that in the day of Christ I may be proud [*kauchēma*] that I did not run in vain" (2:16). `[planted]`
- *Planted → 3:20–21.* "depart and be with Christ" (v.23, the intermediate state) is completed by the *resurrection body* Paul later supplies (3:21) — the letter distinguishes the two horizons. `[planted]`
- *Planted → 4:4.* "I will rejoice" (v.18) → "rejoice in the Lord always" (4:4). `[planted]`

`Internal: found — answers 1:6–7; densely planted toward 1:27, 2:16–17, 3:20–21, 4:4.`

### 12. Genre

Pauline epistle; **autobiographical / apologetic reflection** — Paul's "news" reframed theologically, with a rare stretch of reported inner deliberation (vv.22–24). Reading rule: this is *exemplary* autobiography (Paul offered as model, confirmed at 3:17), but of *trusting Christ in suffering and death*, not of a hero to admire; read vv.21–24 as a window into a Christ-centred mind weighing two goods, not as introspection for its own sake. `[T]`

---

### 13. Copycat

Paul is offered as an **example** (made explicit at 3:17, "imitate me"), but the thing to imitate is the *ground* of his equanimity — "to me to live is Christ" (v.21) — not a stoic pose or a spiritual achievement to envy. His indifference to life-or-death is presented for imitation of its *root* (union with Christ), not as bravado. **Normative pattern** (Christ-centred fearlessness), not a unique event; guard against turning "to die is gain" into a death-wish, which the balancing "to live is Christ… fruitful labour" (vv.21–22) forbids. `[T]`

---

### 14. Bible Timeline

Church age; **"depart and be with Christ" (v.23) is the *intermediate state*** — conscious presence with Christ at death, "far better", but *not yet* the resurrection body (which the letter supplies at 3:21). The preacher must not collapse the two horizons, nor read v.23 as soul-sleep (it is conscious presence "with Christ"). "The Spirit of Jesus Christ" (v.19) locates the passage post-Pentecost. `[T]`

---

### 15. Who Am I?

Direct NT epistle address — Paul writes as an example (3:17 licenses the identification), but **Christ** is the one "honoured in my body" (v.20), Paul's very life (v.21), and the destination of death (v.23) — Christ is the ground and content, not merely a model. We identify with the **church being reassured** and prepared to face "the same conflict" (1:30), not with Paul-the-hero. `[T]`

---

### 16. So What?

**Author's intended response:** to re-read circumstances — imprisonment, opposition, even death — through the *gospel's advance* and *union with Christ*, and so to be fearless and joyful whether living or dying.

**Worldview:** circumstances are re-read by the gospel's progress and by union with Christ in life and death; "to live is Christ, to die is gain." Suffering can be the very means of the gospel's advance.

**Behaviour:** *stop* — measuring life by comfort or outcomes, or fearing loss and death; *start* — living for the gospel's advance and Christ's honour "in my body, whether by life or by death."

**Motivation (against moralism):** "to me to live is Christ" (v.21) — union and love for Christ — and "to depart and be with Christ… far better" (v.23). The courage flows from Christ being one's *life*, not from willpower.

**Four audiences:**
- *For me:* is Christ so much my life that death would be *gain*? What loss or threat am I reading as defeat that God may be using to advance the gospel?
- *For a Christian friend:* the suffering or dying believer — presence with Christ at death is "far better", and their trial may be advancing the gospel.
- *For the church:* a community that reads its opposition as gospel-advance and does not fear — the very posture 1:27–30 will command.
- *For the unbeliever:* "to die is gain" is unintelligible without Christ — an invitation to a life whose gain is a Person.

**Prayer:** that Christ would be honoured in our bodies, whether by life or by death; for courage that does not shame the gospel; that our sufferings would advance it.

**Grammatical/syntactical features:**
- **The articular infinitives of v.21** — *to zēn* ("the to-live") = *Christos*; *to apothanein* ("the to-die") = *kerdos*: life and death each summed in one word, in antithesis. `[T]`
- **The *gar* chain (vv.19, 21)** — grounds the rejoicing (v.19) and the equanimity (v.21). `[T]`
- **Aspect:** future "I will rejoice" (v.18) — settled resolve; present "I am hard pressed" (*synechomai*, v.23) — the ongoing squeeze of the dilemma. `[T]`

**Recommended verification:** the precise force of *praitōrion* (guard vs residence); the strength of the Job 13:16 conscious allusion (commentaries — the verbal match is secure, the intended framing is the judgement call).

### Textual Variants

**v.14** — some manuscripts add "of God" to "the word" ("speak the word *of God*"); the shorter "the word" is likely original. Minor; no doctrinal weight. Otherwise the passage is textually stable. `[T]`

### Historical and Cultural Background

**Setting:** Paul writes under **Roman imprisonment** (house arrest awaiting trial, or stricter custody), chained to a rotation of guards, facing the real possibility of a capital verdict.

**What the original audience knew that we miss:**
- **The praetorium / imperial guard** (v.13) — elite soldiers at the centre of Roman power; that Paul's chains have made Christ "known throughout the *whole* imperial guard" means the gospel has penetrated Caesar's own security apparatus (cf. 4:22, "Caesar's household"). `[T/S]`
- **Honour-shame** — "I will not be *at all ashamed*" (v.20) is public-vindication language; in that culture, a shameful death (a prisoner's execution) would be read as defeat, which Paul flatly denies: Christ will be *magnified* in his body either way. `[T/S]`
- **The rival preachers** (vv.15–17) — Christian preachers exploiting Paul's imprisonment "to afflict" him (perhaps to draw attention or provoke the authorities against him); their fault is *motive* (envy, rivalry), not a false gospel. `[T/I]`

**What this changes:** the passage is not a prisoner's brave face but a considered theological reading of his situation — the gospel is advancing *through* the chains, into the heart of Rome, whatever becomes of Paul.

### Original Audience Reception

**Canonical audience:** an anxious, affectionate church, fearing for Paul's life and for their own standing under opposition.

**Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances:**
- *Surprising:* Paul *rejoices* that rivals preach Christ (v.18); he calls death "*gain*".
- *Comforting:* his imprisonment has *advanced* the gospel; he expects to *remain* "for your progress and joy" (v.25).

### Biblical-Theological Themes

**The righteous sufferer and his vindication.** Paul reads his trial in Job's confidence of vindication (13:16) and the Servant's (Isa 50:7–9) — "I will not be ashamed" (v.20). *Where this sits:* the OT righteous-sufferer motif transposed onto the believer whose vindication is now *Christ*, not his own integrity. *Confidence:* moderate–high (secured by the verbatim Job citation). `[T/I]`

**Union with Christ — in life and death.** "To live is Christ… to die is gain… depart and be with Christ" (vv.21–23): union means both states are gain; death brings conscious presence with Christ ("far better"), the resurrection body awaiting 3:21. *Confidence:* high. `[T]`

**The advance of the gospel through suffering.** *prokopē* (vv.12, 25) — God advances his cause *through* his servant's chains, the pattern of the cross itself and of Acts. *Confidence:* high (textually named). `[T]`

**Confluence — climactic convergence.** The righteous-sufferer's vindication-hope (Job/Isa 50) and union with Christ meet in v.20–21: Paul's confidence of vindication rests not on his own integrity (as Job's did) but on Christ, who is his life — so that whether he lives or dies, Christ is *honoured* and Paul *gains*. The sufferer's "salvation/vindication" **is Christ himself.** That is the preaching gold.

### Christological Reading

NT passage — Christ named throughout, in **two registers**: Christ as **ground** (Paul's life, his gain, his honour; "the Spirit of Jesus Christ") and as **pattern** (fearless, gospel-centred suffering, offered for imitation at 3:17). Typological transposition: Paul stands in the line of the **righteous sufferer** (Job; the Servant of Isa 50), but his vindication is *Christ*, not his own integrity — a gospel reframing of the Job motif (passes the theological-category test [the vindicated-sufferer category] and the NT-precedent test [the Servant applied to Christ and his people]; treat as trajectory/transposition, moderate).

**Moralism check.** The "be like X" temptation is "be brave / stay positive in suffering like Paul." The text forbids it: fearlessness flows from "to me to live is Christ" (v.21) — union and the surpassing worth of Christ (cf. 3:8) — not from willpower or temperament. Christ is Paul's *life*, which is why death is *gain*; preach the Christ who makes both states win, not the technique of courage. `[T]`

### Difficult / Contested Verses

**v.18 — "whether in pretence or in truth, Christ is proclaimed, and in that I rejoice."**
- *Category:* doctrinal / weaponised.
- *The difficulty:* read as indifference to false *doctrine*.
- *Honest exegesis:* the text specifies bad *motive* (envy, rivalry, selfish ambition, vv.15–17), not a false *gospel*; chapter 3 shows Paul is anything but indifferent to a gospel-destroying error (the "mutilators").
- *Handling:* distinguish sharply — rejoice that Christ is truly preached even by flawed people; do not use the verse to excuse false teaching.

**v.21 / v.23 — "to die is gain… depart and be with Christ".**
- *Category:* doctrinal / pastoral.
- *The difficulty:* misused as a death-wish or preached sentimentally; and the *intermediate state* is easily muddled.
- *Honest exegesis:* "gain" is grounded in *union* ("with Christ", v.23) and balanced by "to live is Christ… fruitful labour" (vv.21–22); v.23 is *conscious presence* with Christ at death ("far better"), distinct from the resurrection body of 3:21 and from soul-sleep.
- *Handling:* preach a Christ-centred readiness to die that does *not* despise life; keep the intermediate state and the resurrection distinct.

**v.19 — "this will turn out for my deliverance/salvation" (*sōtēria*).**
- *Category:* doctrinal / lexical.
- *The difficulty:* is it release from prison or eschatological vindication?
- *Honest exegesis:* the Job 13:16 allusion and v.20 ("whether by life or death") tilt decisively toward *vindication* at God's bar, not mere acquittal.
- *Handling:* preach the deeper "salvation" — vindication and Christ's honour — rather than reducing it to "Paul got out of jail."

**vv.22–24 — "which I shall choose I cannot tell".**
- *Category:* apologetic / pastoral.
- *The difficulty:* does Paul contemplate hastening his own death?
- *Honest exegesis:* no — it is a *preference between two goods* (presence with Christ vs fruitful labour), resolved by love for the church (v.24); not a contemplation of suicide.
- *Handling:* name it as the peace of a man for whom both outcomes are gain.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: v.18 as indifference to false doctrine

- **What it looks like:** "Paul didn't care what people preached as long as they preached Christ — so doctrine doesn't matter much."
- **Why it's wrong:** the fault named is bad *motive* (envy, rivalry), not a false *gospel*; chapter 3 shows Paul's fierce zeal against gospel-destroying error.
- **The corrective:** rejoice that Christ is genuinely proclaimed even by flawed messengers; never use the verse to relax doctrinal vigilance.

### Pitfall: "To die is gain" (v.21) as a death-wish or sentimental epitaph

- **What it looks like:** a morbid longing for death, or a funeral-card platitude.
- **Why it's wrong:** "gain" is grounded in *union* ("depart and be *with Christ*", v.23) and balanced by "to live is Christ… fruitful labour" (vv.21–22).
- **The corrective:** preach a readiness to die that flows from Christ being one's life, and that does not despise the labour of living.

### Pitfall: Collapsing the intermediate state (v.23) into the resurrection

- **What it looks like:** "when we die we get our resurrection bodies and it's all consummated."
- **Why it's wrong:** v.23 is *conscious presence with Christ* ("far better"); the resurrection body waits (3:21). Nor is it soul-sleep.
- **The corrective:** keep the two horizons distinct — presence with Christ at death, resurrection at his coming.

### Pitfall: Reducing "deliverance" (v.19) to "release from prison"

- **What it looks like:** "Paul was confident he'd be acquitted and freed."
- **Why it's wrong:** the Job 13:16 echo and v.20 ("whether by life or *death*") make *sōtēria* eschatological vindication, not mere acquittal.
- **The corrective:** preach the deeper salvation — vindication and Christ's honour, whatever the verdict.

### Pitfall: Moralising the passage into "be brave / stay positive"

- **What it looks like:** "have a good attitude in your trials like Paul did."
- **Why it's wrong:** the equanimity flows from "to me to live is Christ" (union), not from temperament or willpower.
- **The corrective:** ground courage in Christ-as-life; the attitude is a fruit of union, not a technique.

### Research verification — 13 August 2026

External research (Logos Research Assistant, concordance and commentary search) addressed **six** of this report's items. This is the most affected of the pericope runs, and one of its live interpretive decisions is now made.

| # | Item | Verdict |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | ***sōtēria* (v.19)** | **Settled — this report's reading is right, and the hedge can go.** "The term 'salvation' does not suggest release from prison but Paul's complete obedience to God"; the Job background pushes the sense toward vindication before the divine bar. Preach it as vindication, not acquittal — and note the consequence the report already drew: Paul's "deliverance" is compatible with his execution. `[S: Park; DeRouchie; Osborne]` |
| 2 | **Job 13:16 framing** | **Confirmed, and upgraded to high.** The phrase τοῦτό μοι ἀποβήσεται εἰς σωτηρίαν occurs nowhere else in Scripture; commentators treat it as a deliberate allusion rather than coincidence, and the *forensic* context of Job 13 (Job "has prepared his case… will defend his ways to his face") is judged to shape Paul's meaning. The overview entry has moved from *moderate–high* to **high**. |
| 3 | ***praitōrion* (v.13)** | **Closed as indifferent.** Martin surveys the options — the emperor's palace, a provincial governor's seat, or (most likely here) the praetorian guard as persons — and concludes that "no decisive evidence for the origin of the epistle can be gained from this verse", which is what this report claimed. Round three adds that the *familia Caesaris* of 4:22 is likewise attested in the provinces, so neither verse settles provenance. `[S: Martin; Flexsenhar]` |
| 4 | **vv.22–24 "which I shall choose"** | **Challenged.** Martin reads *gnōrizō* at v.22 as "I do not **make known**" — *"That is, I dare not reveal my preference"* — rather than "I cannot tell". On that reading Paul is discreet rather than torn. The report's own reading is defensible and the ESV supports it, but it is no longer the only serious option: **say that Paul weighs life and death, which is on the page either way; be careful with "he cannot decide".** `[S: Martin]` |
| 5 | **"Spirit of Jesus Christ" (v.19)** | **Confirmed as genuinely unusual, and connected.** The ascription of the divine Spirit to a figure other than God is "very difficult to find" in ancient Jewish context. And commentators *do* place Phil 1:19 beside **Acts 16:7** ("the Spirit of Jesus"), along with Rom 8:9 and Gal 4:6. That does not make it a Pauline allusion to Luke — but the Macedonian coincidence is noted in the literature, not merely by this corpus. `[S: Hurtado; Luter]` |
| 6 | **v.20 "not be at all ashamed" — a second source** | **New.** Hays observes that "not being ashamed" evokes **Isaiah 50:7–8 LXX**: "I know that I shall not be ashamed, because the one who justifies me is near." So vv.19–20 carry **two** vindication voices — Job's formula and the Servant's posture — in consecutive clauses. Added to the overview's intertextual map at *moderate*; if it holds, the letter's Isaiah 40–55 strand begins here rather than at 2:10. `[S: Hays, via Moyise]` |

**One further note on v.20.** μεγαλύνω ("Christ will be **magnified** in my body") is the LXX's standard verb for magnifying **the LORD** (Ps 34:3; 40:16; 70:4; 2 Sam 7:26), so applying it to Christ is a transference of doxological language reserved for God — a small early instance of the operation that peaks at 2:10–11 with Isaiah 45:23. Confirmed from general knowledge rather than a cited commentary; *moderate–high*, worth noting, not worth headlining.
