## The Passage

> **1** Finally, my brothers, rejoice in the Lord. To write the same things to you is no trouble to me and is safe for you. **2** Look out for the dogs, look out for the evildoers, look out for those who mutilate the flesh. **3** For we are the circumcision, who worship by the Spirit of God and glory in Christ Jesus and put no confidence in the flesh— **4** though I myself have reason for confidence in the flesh also. If anyone else thinks he has reason for confidence in the flesh, I have more: **5** circumcised on the eighth day, of the people of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of Hebrews; as to the law, a Pharisee; **6** as to zeal, a persecutor of the church; as to righteousness under the law, blameless. **7** But whatever gain I had, I counted as loss for the sake of Christ. **8** Indeed, I count everything as loss because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord. For his sake I have suffered the loss of all things and count them as rubbish, in order that I may gain Christ **9** and be found in him, not having a righteousness of my own that comes from the law, but that which comes through faith in Christ, the righteousness from God that depends on faith— **10** that I may know him and the power of his resurrection, and may share his sufferings, becoming like him in his death, **11** that by any means possible I may attain the resurrection from the dead.

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims that emerged across multiple tools:

1. **Paul enacts the hymn: as Christ "counted" (*hēgeomai*) equality-with-God no thing to exploit (2:6), Paul "counts" (same verb, ×3) all his privileges loss — the autobiography is 2:6–8 in Paul's own key, and it is the letter's densest internal echo.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Tool 11 Move 4 (internal echo), Vocabulary, Structure. Chapter 3 is not a new topic; it is the mind of Christ worked out in the apostle's own reckoning.

2. **The polemic reverses circumcision: "we are the circumcision" (v.3); the confidence-in-the-flesh party are the "mutilators" (*katatomē*, v.2) — a bitter pun on *peritomē* — and the covenant boundary-marker is redefined around Spirit-worship and glorying in Christ.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary (the wordplay), Tool 11 (conceptual heart-circumcision allusion), Original Audience. The Judaising boast is turned inside out.

3. **The goal is relational and cruciform — "that I may know him and the power of his resurrection, and may share his sufferings" — with justification (v.9) as the *doorway* to union (v.10), not the destination.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure (the *hina*-chain), Linking Words, Repetition (*gnōnai*), Biblical Theology (confluence). The received righteousness of v.9 is the ground on which the knowing of v.10 stands.

4. **Positional necessity: 3:1–11 is the mind of Christ under attack from *religion* (legalism) — the counterpart to chapter 1's mind of Christ under attack from *suffering*; "put no confidence in the flesh" (v.3) is the negative of "have this mind" (2:5).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Phase 0.6, Tool 2, Tool 11 Move 4. The two "fronts" of the letter meet here from the doctrinal side.

5. **Paul's coarse "*skybala*" (dung/refuse) for his elite pedigree is deliberate shock — the highest religious capital is not merely neutralised but reclassified as *refuse* beside the surpassing worth of knowing Christ.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Tone, Translations. The rhetoric is the theology: nothing competes with Christ, and its true value beside him is rubbish.

## Phase 0.6 — Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement:** 2:5 has commanded "have this mind… which is yours in Christ Jesus", and 2:19–30 has shown it embodied in Timothy and Epaphroditus (who sought others' interests, not their own). 3:1 resumes the joy thread ("rejoice in the Lord"). `[T]`

**Necessity answer:** a *rival* "mind" is now on the field — confidence in the flesh, ethnic/legal boast — which is the exact opposite of the *phronēsis* of 2:3–8. Paul must confront it *here*, and does so by making *himself* the test case: the man with the most flesh-confidence renounces it, embodying the hymn. The abrupt tonal break at v.1b–2 is not a seam but a *pivot* from the models of humility (2:19–30) to the counter-model of pride (the mutilators). Chapter 3 shows the mind of Christ under attack from **religion**, as chapter 1 showed it under attack from **suffering**. `[T]` / `[I]`

**Implication:** "put no confidence in the flesh" (v.3) is the negative of "have this mind" (2:5); and 3:7–8's "count all loss" (*hēgeomai*) is the letter's densest internal echo of the hymn's "did not count equality… a thing to grasp" (2:6, the same verb). The passage exists here to enact, autobiographically, the mind the hymn defined — under the specific pressure of religious pride.

**Revisit after Tool 11:** with no external citation to track (Move 2 nil), the positional weight rests entirely on the internal echo to 2:6 — which confirms that ch. 3 is the hymn *performed*, not a fresh argument.

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book-level** (from the overview): to form a mission-partner church in gospel-worthy steadfastness by the mind of Christ. `[S: overview]`

**Passage-level:** to guard the church against confidence in the flesh (the Judaising boast) by (a) redefining the true "circumcision" — Spirit-worship, glorying in Christ, no confidence in the flesh (v.3) — and (b) modelling the great reversal in Paul's own life: all his covenantal and legal capital "counted loss" for the surpassing worth of knowing Christ. The aim is not merely to win a doctrinal argument about circumcision but to relocate the church's confidence and desire onto Christ, whom Paul pursues even into "the fellowship of his sufferings". `[T]`

---

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check *(see Phase 0.6 above)*

**Immediately before (2:19–30 + 3:1):** the two models of the Christ-mind, and the resumed call to joy. `[T]`

**Immediately after (3:12–21):** "Not that I have already obtained this or am already perfect… I press on" — 3:12 guards the goal of vv.10–11 (knowing him, attaining the resurrection) from a *perfectionist* misreading, and vv.20–21 bring the "resurrection from the dead" (v.11) to its climax in the transforming Saviour. The goal stated in 3:10–11 sets up the correction of 3:12–16. `[T]`

**Out-of-context flags:** 3:2 preached as ethnic abuse (missing the role-reversal); v.9 preached as a bare forensic proof-text severed from its relational goal (v.10); *pistis Christou* (v.9) resolved silently in one direction.

---

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|--------|---------|---------|
| 3:1 | Preface | Joy + warning ("rejoice"; "the same things… safe for you") |
| 3:2–3 | Warning + counter-claim | Three staccato "look out"; "for **we** are the circumcision" (three marks: Spirit-worship, glory in Christ, no confidence in flesh) |
| 3:4–6 | The flesh-confidence catalogue | Seven credentials — four inherited (circumcised 8th day, Israel, Benjamin, Hebrew of Hebrews), three achieved (Pharisee, persecutor, blameless) |
| 3:7–11 | The great reversal | One long sentence: "I counted… loss" governing a purpose-chain toward "gain Christ… be found in him… know him… attain the resurrection" |

**Device:** antithesis — gain/loss reversed; "confidence in the flesh" (×3) set against "in Christ." The hinge is the **"But" (ἀλλά) of v.7**: "whatever gain I had, I counted as loss." The catalogue (vv.4–6) exists to be renounced (vv.7–8); its very completeness is what makes the renunciation weighty.

**Clause-flow (vv.7–11):**

```
But whatever gain I had,                                  ← the pivot ("but", ἀλλά)
   I counted as loss for the sake of Christ.              ← MAIN VERB (aorist: the decisive reckoning)
Indeed, I count everything as loss                        ← present tense: the ongoing reckoning
   because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ…     ← the ground of the reckoning (the treasure)
For his sake I have suffered the loss of all things
   and count them as rubbish (skybala),
      in order that I may gain Christ                      ← purpose-chain (ἵνα) begins
      and be found in him,
         not having a righteousness of my own…            ← the negative
         but that which comes through faith in Christ,     ← the positive: received righteousness (the ground)
      that I may know him                                  ← the relational goal (ἵνα)
         and the power of his resurrection,
         and may share his sufferings,
         becoming like him in his death,
      that… I may attain the resurrection from the dead.   ← the eschatological goal (ἵνα)
```

Central assertion: a **statement of reckoning** ("I count everything loss", vv.7–8) driving a **purpose-chain** whose end is *knowing Christ* and *resurrection*. Justification (v.9) sits *inside* the chain as the ground of the knowing, not as its terminus.

### 4. Linking Words

- **v.1 "Finally" (τὸ λοιπόν)** — transitional "for the rest / moreover", not "in conclusion" (the letter runs on for two chapters). `[T]`
- **v.2 asyndeton** — three "look out" (*blepete*) with no connectors: staccato, hammering warning. `[T]`
- **v.3 "For" (γάρ)** — grounds the warning in the counter-claim: *we* are the true circumcision. `[T]`
- **v.4 "though" (καίπερ)** — concessive: Paul *does* have flesh-grounds, and more than the rivals. `[T]`
- **v.7 "But" (ἀλλά)** — the great reversal; the structural pivot of the passage. `[T]`
- **v.8 "Indeed… more than that" (ἀλλὰ μενοῦνγε καὶ)** — piled particles intensifying from the past reckoning (v.7) to the sweeping present one (v.8). `[T]`
- **vv.8–11 the *hina*-chain** — "in order that I may gain Christ… that I may know him… that I may attain": three purpose clauses driving to the goal. Trace the chain; a dynamic rendering can blur that v.9 is *subordinate to* the knowing of v.10. `[T]`
- **v.9 "not… but" (μὴ… ἀλλά)** — the antithesis of the two righteousnesses (own, from law / from God, through faith). `[T]`

### 5. Parallels

Prose, not Hebrew poetry — but two structuring patterns matter:

- **The credential catalogue (vv.5–6)** moves from *inherited* status (circumcised, Israel, Benjamin, Hebrew) to *achieved* status (Pharisee, zealous persecutor, blameless) — a deliberate escalation from birth to performance, so that the renunciation covers *every* kind of ground. `[T]`
- **The gain/loss antithesis** (vv.7–8) is the governing figure — *kerdos* set against *zēmia*, reversed by the cross.

### 7. Vocabulary

The dominant tool here, with Tool 11 Move 4.

- **κατατομή (v.2) / περιτομή (v.3)** — "mutilation / cutting-down" punning on "circumcision": a deliberate, biting paronomasia. The circumcisers are merely *cutting flesh*; the true *peritomē* is the Spirit-worshipping church. `[T, high]`
- **κύνας (v.2), "dogs"** — the standard Jewish epithet for unclean Gentile outsiders, now turned back on the Judaisers: a pointed role-reversal. `[T]`
- **ἡγέομαι (*hēgeomai*), vv.7, 8 (×2)** — "count / consider / reckon" — the *same verb* as 2:6 (Christ "did not *count* equality a thing to grasp"). The master internal echo; Paul's reckoning mirrors Christ's. `[T, high]`
- **σκύβαλα (v.8), "rubbish / dung / refuse"** — a coarse, deliberately shocking word (table-scraps, excrement). The vulgarity is rhetorical: his pedigree is not merely outweighed but *waste* beside Christ. `[T]`
- **ζημία / κέρδος (loss / gain, vv.7–8)** — commercial ledger language, part of the letter's accounting register (1:21 "to die is gain"; 4:15–18 the accounts). `[T]`
- **δικαιοσύνη… ἐκ θεοῦ ἐπὶ τῇ πίστει (v.9)** — "the righteousness *from God* on the basis of faith", set against "a righteousness of my own from the law". The two righteousnesses. `[T, high]`
- **πίστις Χριστοῦ (v.9)** — "faith in Christ" (objective genitive) or "the faithfulness of Christ" (subjective): a live grammatical crux. See Difficult Verses. *Flag.*
- **γνῶσις / γνῶναι (vv.8, 10), "knowing"** — relational, covenantal knowledge (Hebrew *yādaʿ*), not bare information; "the surpassing worth of *knowing* Christ" is the treasure the reckoning is *for*. `[T]`
- **συμμορφιζόμενος (v.10), "being conformed"** — the *morphē* root of 2:6–7 returns; present tense = ongoing conformity to Christ's death. `[T]`

**Proper-noun inventory:** "Christ Jesus"; "Israel"; "Benjamin"; "Hebrew(s)"; "Pharisee." These are covenant-identity markers deployed as credentials, then renounced. *Name-hyperlink (flagged, low weight):* Paul names his tribe as **Benjamin** — the tribe of the first king Saul and of Paul's own Hebrew name; whether he intends more than pedigree is uncertain (probably a pure status-boast). Cross-reported to Tool 11 at low confidence. `[T]` for the pedigree; `[uncertain]` for any Saul-the-king overtone.

### 8. Translations

- **v.2 "those who mutilate the flesh"** — ESV; **KJV "the concision"; NASB "the false circumcision."** The *katatomē/peritomē* pun is nearly untranslatable; KJV's "concision/circumcision" comes closest to preserving the wordplay. Flag so the preacher can voice the pun. `[T]`
- **v.8 "rubbish"** — ESV / NASB; **KJV "dung."** *skybala* is coarse; the KJV keeps the shock the Greek intends, which "rubbish" softens. The vulgarity is the rhetoric — worth recovering. `[T]`
- **v.9 "faith in Christ"** — ESV renders the objective genitive; the subjective ("faithfulness of Christ") is a live option (see Difficult Verses / Original Languages). Flag. `[T]`

### 9. Tone and Feel

Three registers in eleven verses — and the movement between them is the passage's emotional architecture:

- **Sudden sharpness (vv.2)** — polemical, biting: "dogs… evildoers… mutilators." A brass warning. `[T]`
- **Rising to rapture (vv.7–8)** — the most exultant personal note in the letter: "the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus *my Lord*." A soaring aria. `[T]`
- **Sober and cruciform (vv.10–11)** — quiet, costly: "share his sufferings, becoming like him in his death." A hushed, solemn close. `[T]`

**Soundtrack:** a sharp brass alarm → a soaring love-song → a solemn, kneeling hush. A sermon that stays in one register (only polemic, or only rapture) misses the passage's shape. `[T]`

### 11. Quotation/Allusion

*Independent candidate-finding (from the passage's own vocabulary):* "circumcision / no confidence in the flesh" → the OT heart-circumcision theme (Deut 10:16; 30:6; Jer 4:4; Ezek 44:9); "blameless… under the law" → covenant-nomistic righteousness language; the credential markers (Israel / Benjamin / Hebrew / Pharisee) activate covenant-identity vocabulary but are *redefined*, not cited.

*Live sources (overview v0.2):* Isaiah 40–55, Deuteronomy 32, the sacrificial cult — **none of which is cited in this passage.** This is a **citation-light** pericope; Move 2 has essentially nothing to track, and the interpretive weight falls on **Move 4** (internal echo). This is itself a finding the overview needs confirmed. `[T]`

**Circumcision-of-the-heart (Deut 10:16; 30:6; Jer 4:4) → 3:3** *(moderate confidence — conceptual allusion, no verbal quotation)* `[I]`

*Source context (Move 1):* Deuteronomy repeatedly calls Israel to "circumcise… the foreskin of your heart" (10:16) and promises that **God himself** "will circumcise your heart… so that you will love the LORD your God with all your heart" (30:6) — an interior, Spirit-enabled covenant loyalty that the physical sign only pointed to; Jeremiah 4:4 renews the call. *What it adds:* Paul's "we are the circumcision, who worship by the Spirit of God" (v.3) claims that this promised heart-circumcision is *now fulfilled* in the Spirit-worshipping church — so the physical rite the Judaisers press is not the covenant marker but its outdated shadow. *Book usage (Move 2):* not applicable — no earlier circumcision citation in the letter; this is the letter's only engagement with the theme. *OT-to-OT (Move 3):* the Deuteronomy heart-circumcision promise and the prophetic new-covenant interior renewal (Jer 31; Ezek 36) are already in canonical conversation; Paul stands in that stream. *Confidence moderate — conceptual, cross-referenced to Biblical Theology and Col 2:11 ("the circumcision of Christ").*

**Move 4 — internal echo** *(required; densely positive — the strongest in the letter)*

- *Answers §2:6 — the master echo.* Christ "did not count (*hēgeomai*) equality with God a thing to grasp"; Paul "counts (*hēgeomai*) all things loss." The *morphē* cluster also recurs (*symmorphizomenos*, v.10 ← *morphē*, 2:6–7). Paul is enacting the hymn in his own life. `[answers — the letter's densest internal echo]`
- *Answers §2:5.* "Put no confidence in the flesh" (v.3) is the negative of "have this mind… in Christ" (2:5). `[answers]`
- *Contrasts §1:28.* A second "front" of opponents — now doctrinal (mutilators, v.2) rather than civil (the persecuting opponents of 1:28). `[answers/contrasts]`
- *Planted → 3:12; 3:20–21.* "The power of his resurrection… share his sufferings" (v.10) sets up "not that I have already… attained" (3:12); "attain the resurrection from the dead" (v.11) and *symmorphizomenos* (v.10) plant the transformation resolved at 3:20–21 (*symmorphon… tapeinōseōs*). `[planted — resolved in 3:20–21]`

`Internal: densely found — 3:7–8 is the letter's densest internal echo of the hymn (2:6); the passage is ch. 2 performed autobiographically.`

### 12. Genre

Pauline epistle; **polemical autobiography within paraenesis.** Sub-form: a rhetorical **boast-catalogue** (vv.4–6) in the idiom of ancient self-commendation, deployed *ironically* — Paul boasts precisely in order to renounce the boast. Reading rule: the autobiography is *exemplary* (3:17, "imitate me"), but of the *ground* (counting-all-loss for Christ), not of achievement; read vv.4–6 as the set-up for the reversal of vv.7–8, never as a résumé to admire. `[T]`

---

### 13. Copycat

Paul offers himself as a **normative pattern** of counting-all-loss (made explicit at 3:17, "join in imitating me"). But two guards: (i) v.9's righteousness is *received*, not achieved — so "I count all loss" must not become a new *work* or a spiritual achievement to envy; (ii) we identify with **Paul-the-former-boaster** (the man who renounced his assets), not with **Paul-the-super-apostle** (the credentials themselves, vv.5–6, are descriptive of his past, not prescriptive for us). The imitation is of the *reckoning and the pursuit*, grounded in the gift. `[T]`

---

### 14. Bible Timeline

Church age, in the sharpened already/not-yet: **resurrection *power* now** (v.10) and **resurrection *attainment* future** (v.11); union with Christ's death *now*, conformity *ongoing* (*symmorphizomenos*, present tense). Redemptive-historical shift: "we are the circumcision" places the physical covenant sign *behind* the church — fulfilled in Christ and the Spirit (Col 2:11 "the circumcision of Christ"; Deut 30:6's heart-circumcision now realised). The preacher should not treat circumcision as a live requirement, nor the credentials of vv.5–6 as goods to be regained. `[T]`

---

### 15. Who Am I?

Direct NT epistle address — "**we** are the circumcision" (corporate, plural). We identify with the church being warned against confidence in the flesh, and with **Paul as example** of counting-all-loss (3:17 licenses the identification) — but *not* with Paul-the-achiever, and *not* with the "dogs / mutilators", who are the false teachers (a warning, not a mirror). Christ is the one *known and gained* (v.8), the object of pursuit and worship, not merely an example. `[T]`

---

### 16. So What?

**Author's intended response:** renounce every ground of self-confidence — religious, ethnic, moral — as loss, and pursue the surpassing worth of *knowing Christ*, including the fellowship of his sufferings, resting on the received righteousness of God by faith.

**Worldview:** every ground of confidence apart from Christ is not neutral but *refuse* (*skybala*) beside him; righteousness is God's gift, not a human achievement; to "gain" is to lose everything for the one worth more than all.

**Behaviour:** *stop* — trusting pedigree, performance, or religious credentials as the basis of standing; *start* — counting all such things loss for Christ and actively pursuing knowing him (the present-tense "I count", the straining *hina*-chain).

**Motivation (against moralism):** not "renounce enough and you will earn Christ" but "the *surpassing worth* of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord" (v.8) and the *received* righteousness (v.9) — desire and gift, not duty. Christ is the treasure gained, not the reward for renouncing.

**Four audiences:**
- *For me:* what is my "confidence in the flesh" — the asset I quietly trust? Name it; weigh it against Christ.
- *For the religious/moral person:* their genuine assets (discipline, heritage, record) are *loss* as a basis of standing — a hard, liberating word.
- *For the church:* guard the gospel of received righteousness against every "add circumcision / add performance" pressure; keep v.9 as the doorway to v.10.
- *For the unbeliever:* righteousness cannot be achieved, only received in Christ; the invitation is to be "found in him".

**Prayer:** to know Christ and the power of his resurrection; to count all else loss for his surpassing worth; to be found in him, clothed not in my own righteousness but in the righteousness from God by faith.

> **Verification note (13 August 2026).** This row previously read "aorist (v.7)". The decisive-past-then-continuing-present contrast is also now an *attested* commentary reading (Moore, *Big Greek Idea*; Parsons–Culy–Hall), not merely this report's inference. `[S: Logos, Q28]`

**Grammatical/syntactical features:**
- **The *hina*-chain (vv.8–11)** — three purpose clauses; v.9 (righteousness) is grammatically *subordinate to* the goal of knowing Christ (v.10). `[T]`
- **Aspect:** *ezēmiōthēn* (v.8, aorist, "I suffered the loss") — the decisive past renunciation; *hēgoumai* (v.8, present) — the continuing reckoning. The tense-pairing holds a settled past act and a present, living stance together. `[T]`
- **v.4 concessive *kaiper*** — "though" — grants Paul real flesh-grounds before he renounces them. `[T]`
- **v.9 *heurethō* (aorist passive subjunctive), "be found in him"** — passive: to be *discovered* as belonging to Christ, not to present one's own case. `[T]`

**Recommended verification:** *pistis Christou* (Wallace, *Greek Grammar*; the objective/subjective-genitive debate — O'Brien, Fee, Hays); the strength of the heart-circumcision allusion (commentaries on v.3).

### Textual Variants

**v.3 — "worship by the Spirit of God"** (*pneumati theou latreuontes*) vs a reading "worship God in spirit" (*pneumati theō*). The Spirit-reading (*theou*, genitive) is better attested and preferred by modern editions; it affects whether the verse teaches *Spirit-enabled* worship — it does. Worth a word if a congregation member is comparing versions. *Confidence: moderate–high; the Spirit reading is preferred.* Otherwise no significant variants.

### Historical and Cultural Background

**Setting:** a letter to the church at **Philippi**, a status-proud Roman colony, facing **Judaising missionaries** pressing circumcision and law-observance on Gentile converts (the same threat Paul fights in Galatians).

**What the original audience knew that we miss:**
- **"Dogs"** was the standard Jewish term of contempt for unclean *Gentiles*; Paul's turning it on the *Jewish* teachers is a shocking, deliberate reversal. `[T/S]`
- **Circumcision** was *the* covenant boundary-marker of Jewish identity; to hear it recategorised as "mutilation" (*katatomē*) and reassigned to the Spirit-worshipping church would have been startling and polemical. `[T/S]`
- **Paul's CV (vv.5–6) is a genuine elite pedigree** — "Hebrew of Hebrews" (Aramaic-speaking, not merely Diaspora), Pharisee, zealous enough to persecute — real, enviable religious capital in that world, which is exactly why calling it *skybala* (dung) lands as scandal. `[T/S]`

**What this changes:** the invective of v.2 is not ethnic abuse but a gospel-defining polemic that inverts the honour-map (the "clean" circumcisers are the "dogs"; the "unclean" Gentile believers are the true "circumcision"), and Paul's self-abasement is the costliest possible endorsement of "count it all loss."

### Original Audience Reception

**Canonical audience:** gospel-aware, largely Gentile believers under pressure to secure their standing by adding circumcision and law.

**Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances:**
- *Shocking:* the covenant sign redefined; "dogs" turned on Jewish teachers; Paul trashing his own elite pedigree as dung.
- *Surprising:* the boast-catalogue (which sounds like self-commendation) reversed into total renunciation.
- *Comforting:* righteousness is *received*, not achieved (v.9); the surpassing worth of Christ outweighs every loss.
- *Disturbing:* *every* ground of self-confidence — even genuine religious achievement — named as loss.

### Biblical-Theological Themes

**Circumcision → heart-circumcision → Christ.** From the covenant sign (Gen 17) through the prophetic call for interior circumcision (Deut 10:16; 30:6; Jer 4:4) to its fulfilment in the Spirit-worshipping church and "the circumcision of Christ" (Col 2:11): the physical marker gives way to the Spirit's interior work. *Where this passage sits:* the fulfilment claim — "we are the circumcision" (v.3). *Confidence:* moderate–high (secured by Col 2:11). `[T/I]`

**Justification / the two righteousnesses.** "Not… a righteousness of my own from the law, but… the righteousness from God that depends on faith" (v.9) — the flagship Reformation text, here deployed *pastorally* against confidence in the flesh rather than as an abstract *ordo*. *Confidence:* high. `[T]`

**Union with Christ / resurrection and new creation.** "In him… the power of his resurrection… conformed to his death… attain the resurrection" (vv.9–11) — participation in Christ's death-and-resurrection, already/not-yet, resolved at 3:21. *Confidence:* high. `[T]`

**Confluence — climactic convergence.** These do not merely sit side by side: the received righteousness (v.9, the forensic *ground*) *serves* the relational and participatory goal of knowing Christ and being conformed to his death-and-resurrection (vv.10–11). **Justification is in the service of union.** That is the preaching gold — v.9 is the doorway, v.10 the room.

### Christological Reading

NT passage — Christ named throughout, in **two registers**: Christ as **ground** (the righteousness from God through faith, v.9; "knowing Christ Jesus my Lord", v.8) and as **pattern** (sharing his sufferings, conformed to his death, v.10). Typology in view: **circumcision fulfilled in Christ** (Col 2:11 — trajectory from the OT heart-circumcision longing to the Spirit's work). 

**Moralism check.** The "be like X" temptation is acute: "count everything as loss" preached as a new ascetic *work*, or "be like Paul the great renouncer." The text forbids it — the righteousness is *received* (v.9), the motive is the *surpassing worth* of Christ (desire, not duty, v.8), and the goal is *knowing him* (v.10). Christ is the **treasure gained**, not the reward earned by renunciation. Preach the worth of Christ that makes the loss reasonable, not the loss as the price that buys him. `[T]`

### Difficult / Contested Verses

**v.2 — "dogs… mutilate the flesh" (harsh invective).**
- *Category:* pastoral / apologetic.
- *The difficulty:* the language sounds like ethnic abuse.
- *Rhetorical function:* a gospel-defining polemic with a deliberate role-reversal — the "dogs" epithet (normally for Gentiles) turned on the Judaising teachers; the point is the *error* (a gospel-destroying insistence on circumcision), not ethnicity.
- *Handling:* explain the *katatomē/peritomē* pun and the reversal; anchor in "we are the circumcision" (v.3); voice the polemic as defence of the gospel, not contempt for a people.

**v.9 — *pistis Christou* ("faith in Christ" / "faithfulness of Christ").**
- *Category:* doctrinal / grammatical.
- *The difficulty:* objective genitive (our faith *in* Christ) vs subjective genitive (Christ's own faithfulness) — a live scholarly crux.
- *Honest exegesis:* the immediate context ("that depends on faith", *epi tē pistei*, restating human faith) tilts toward the objective reading, but the subjective is defensible; the passage's thrust (received, not self-generated righteousness) survives either way.
- *Handling:* do not force the crux from the pulpit; preach the agreed thrust — righteousness is God's gift received, not one's own achievement.

**v.6 — "as to righteousness under the law, blameless."**
- *Category:* doctrinal.
- *The difficulty:* apparent tension with Romans 7 and "none is righteous."
- *Honest exegesis:* Paul speaks of *external, observable* law-righteousness — the Pharisee's blameless record — not sinless perfection or the heart's verdict before God; the old-perspective/new-perspective debate lives here.
- *Handling:* name it as a real, attainable *human* record that Paul nonetheless counts loss — which sharpens the point.

**v.11 — "by any means possible I may attain the resurrection."**
- *Category:* doctrinal / pastoral.
- *The difficulty:* the tentative *ei pōs* ("if somehow") can sound like doubt of security or works-salvation.
- *Honest exegesis:* it is *perseverance and humility* language, not doubt — and it deliberately sets up "not that I have already… attained" (3:12); it guards against both a smug and an anxious reading.
- *Handling:* preach humble, pressing-on assurance, not uncertainty of salvation.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: Harsh handling of 3:2 ("dogs… mutilate the flesh")

- **What it looks like:** deploying the invective as raw contempt, or as ethnic abuse of Jewish people.
- **Why it's wrong:** the polemic answers a gospel-destroying error and deliberately *inverts* the "dogs" insult; it is about the false teaching, not ethnicity.
- **The corrective:** explain the *katatomē/peritomē* pun and the reversal; anchor in "we are the circumcision… who put no confidence in the flesh" (v.3).

### Pitfall: Moralising "count everything as loss" into a new work

- **What it looks like:** "be like Paul — give up enough, renounce enough, and you'll gain Christ."
- **Why it's wrong:** the righteousness is *received* (v.9); the motive is the *surpassing worth* of Christ (v.8), not the price of renunciation.
- **The corrective:** preach the worth of Christ that makes the loss reasonable; Christ is the treasure gained, not the reward earned.

### Pitfall: Stopping at v.9 (justification) as the destination

- **What it looks like:** a sermon that ends at "the righteousness from God by faith" and never reaches v.10.
- **Why it's wrong:** the *hina*-chain makes v.9 subordinate to *knowing Christ* (v.10); justification is the doorway, union the room.
- **The corrective:** preach v.9 *toward* v.10 — the received righteousness so that we may know him, share his sufferings, and attain the resurrection.

### Pitfall: "Blameless" (v.6) as sinless perfection

- **What it looks like:** either "Paul claims he was sinless" or "Paul is being ironic/lying."
- **Why it's wrong:** it names *external, observable* law-righteousness — a real Pharisaic record — not the heart's verdict before God.
- **The corrective:** let the genuine attainment stand, so that counting even *that* as loss lands with full weight.

### Pitfall: v.11 ("by any means… attain") as doubt or works

- **What it looks like:** "even Paul wasn't sure he was saved," or "salvation must be attained by effort."
- **Why it's wrong:** *ei pōs* is humble perseverance language, setting up 3:12 ("not that I have already attained").
- **The corrective:** preach pressing-on assurance — certain of Christ, humble about the not-yet.
