# Job: Questions for the Logos Research Assistant, Round 6

**Purpose:** questions for the Research Assistant, drawn from claim audit 3 and the three Sermon 4 digs (16:1–17:16; 19:1–29; 15:1–21:34, all now v1.1). They follow `logos-research-assistant-guidelines` in the Dig Deeper folder. The main aim is to fill the "history of interpretation" criterion, which audit 3 recorded as *not checked*, for the links now rated high or moderate–high, before they enter the book overview.
**Use:**
- Ask one question per turn.
- Set the scope to a *Job commentaries* collection unless the question says otherwise. Where a question names Lamentations, Deuteronomy or the Psalms, widen the scope to include commentaries on that book.
- Paste each answer back with its footnotes, under the question as asked.

**Date:** 7 October 2026

## How the List Is Arranged

The questions are grouped by the template they practise, as in the earlier Job and Matthew lists. Within each group, the first question matters most to the Sermon 4 backbone.

Each question has three parts:

- **Ask:** the wording to type.
- **Why:** the open point it settles, and where the answer would be used.
- **Watch for:** the check from the "Reading the Answer" section of the guidelines that is most likely to matter.

**Owned and not owned.** The earlier rounds found these works in the library:

- **Commentaries:** Keil–Delitzsch, Lange, Barnes, Andersen (TOTC), Ash (*Preaching the Word*), Konkel–Longman (Cornerstone), Walton–Longman and Ortlund.
- **Reference works:** Davison's articles in Hastings' *Dictionary of the Bible*, HALOT, Gesenius, TLOT and the NET notes.

Clines (WBC), Hartley (NICOT) and the Göttingen *Iob* were not found. If the Assistant cites any of those three, check that it is quoting the work and not general knowledge.

**Left out on purpose:**

- counts, word chains and "only here" claims, which the corpus has already settled;
- the prologue's cross-references (including 2 Kgs 1:12 at 1:16, queue #85), which wait with the deferred prologue audit;
- questions answered in rounds 2–5.

## 1. A Named Commentator's View

**1.1 Job 16:9–16 and Lamentations 2**

- **Ask:** What does Delitzsch (Keil–Delitzsch) say about Job 16:9–16, and does he compare it with Lamentations 2:10–17 or Lamentations 3:12–13 and 3:30?
- **Why:** This is the strongest source result of audit 3. All three blind auditors found Lam 2:10–17 behind Job 16:4–16 (rated high on the words), with Lam 3:12–13 and 3:30 completing it. A major commentator who saw it would add the one criterion still unchecked.
- **Watch for:** whether he compares only the gnashing teeth (a common idiom, also Ps 35:16; 37:12) or the rarer items:
  - the gall or liver "poured out on the ground" (16:13; Lam 2:11);
  - sackcloth with dust (16:15; Lam 2:10);
  - the reduplicated "in ferment" (16:16; Lam 2:11).
- **Then ask the same question of:** Andersen (TOTC) and Konkel–Longman (Cornerstone).

**1.2 Job 17:3, "lay down a pledge for me with Yourself"**

- **Ask:** What does Delitzsch say about the meaning of Job 17:3, "Lay down, now, a pledge for me with Yourself; who is there that will be my guarantor?", and does he connect it with Isaiah 38:14 or with the surety sayings of Proverbs (6:1; 17:18; 22:26)?
- **Why:** Audit 3 rated the shared petition עָרְבֵנִי ("be my surety"; Isa 38:14; Job 17:3) moderate, not high. The imperative with "me" is the natural form for anyone asking a guarantor, and 17:3b's "strike hands" is Proverbs' idiom. Delitzsch already joins 16:20 to Isa 38:14 (round 4). Does he do the same for 17:3, and how does he weigh Proverbs?
- **Watch for:** an answer that settles the identity of the guarantor without discussing the wording.

**1.3 Job 19:25, "my Redeemer … at the last"**

- **Ask:** Which commentators on Job 19:25 compare "my Redeemer" and "at the last" with Isaiah 44:6 ("the LORD … his Redeemer … I am the first and I am the last"), and what do they conclude about the identity of the Redeemer?
- **Why:** Audit 3 rated the words moderate–high (גֹּאֵל + אַחֲרוֹן together only at Isa 44:6 and Job 19:25). It treated the Redeemer's identity as a reading, not a result. Round 2 found Andersen, Lange, Delitzsch, Dillmann and Laurin for "God himself"; this asks whether any of them uses Isa 44:6 as evidence.
- **Watch for:** a date assumption ("Isaiah 40–55 is later, so…") doing the work.

**1.4 Job 20:14–16 and Deuteronomy 32:32–33**

- **Ask:** What does Delitzsch say about "the venom of cobras" and "he sucks the poison of cobras" in Job 20:14–16, and does he compare Deuteronomy 32:32–33?
- **Why:** Audit 3 confirmed Deut 32:32–33 behind 20:14–16 at *high* on the words. רֹאשׁ פְּתָנִים ("poison of cobras") occurs only at Deut 32:33 and Job 20:16, and the Greek of Job has the Song's θυμὸς δρακόντων ("wrath of serpents"). With Deut 32:39 at 5:18 and 10:7 (audit 2), the Song would become a live source in both voices.
- **Watch for:** whether he also notices the reversal of Deut 32:13 ("He made him suck honey from the rock"; compare 20:16–17, honey and curds).

## 2. The State of a Debate

**2.1 Job 21:17, "How often is the lamp of the wicked put out?"**

- **Ask:** How do commentators understand the force of Job 21:17–18, "How often is the lamp of the wicked put out?", and do they take it as Job quoting Bildad (18:5–6) and the proverb in Proverbs 13:9 and 24:20?
- **Why:** Audit 3 reframed Job 21 from a "clause-by-clause" reply to a set of pointed rejoinders, of which the quoted lamp proverb is the clearest. The answer bears on how the backbone presents chapter 21.
- **Watch for:** an answer that reasons from the English question mark rather than from כַּמָּה ("how often").

**2.2 Job 21:13, "in a moment" or "in peace"**

- **Ask:** What are the main views on the word translated "in a moment" (NASB95) or "in peace" (ESV) in Job 21:13 (Hebrew רֶגַע), who holds each, and what evidence do they give?
- **Why:** The congregation will hear "in peace" from the ESV. The 15–21 dig favours "in a moment", because the line answers Zophar's "the triumphing of the wicked is short … momentary" (20:5). The dig's v1.0 left this open (Open Question 4).
- **Watch for:** whether the ketiv/qere at the start of the verse (יְבַלּוּ, "wear out", against יְכַלּוּ, "complete") is confused with this question.

**2.3 Job 21:16, statement or question, and whose words?**

- **Ask:** How do commentators read Job 21:16, "Behold, their prosperity is not in their hand; the counsel of the wicked is far from me", and who do they think is speaking — Job, or Job quoting the friends?
- **Why:** This is Open Question 5 of the 15–21 dig. Audit 3 confirmed "the counsel of the wicked" as Ps 1:1's phrase (also 10:3; 22:18), so the answer affects whether Job is claiming Psalm 1 for himself.
- **Watch for:** uncited sentences.

**2.4 Job 18:2–3, Bildad's plurals**

- **Ask:** Whom do commentators think Bildad addresses with the plural verbs in Job 18:2–3 ("How long will you hunt for words? … Why are we regarded as beasts?")?
- **Why:** This is Open Question 3 of the 15–21 dig. The address test counted Bildad's second-person address to Job as four verses, so the plurals affect the count.
- **Watch for:** answers that rest on the English "you", which hides singular and plural.

**2.5 Job 19:26, "from my flesh"**

- **Ask:** What are the main views on מִבְּשָׂרִי ("from my flesh") in Job 19:26 — seeing God "in" the body or "apart from" it — who holds each, and what grammatical arguments do they give?
- **Why:** This is Open Question 1 of the 19 dig, still open. Clines and Hartley are not owned. This asks what Andersen, Delitzsch, Konkel–Longman and the NET notes say.
- **Watch for:** a resurrection conclusion drawn from the KJV's supplied words rather than from the Hebrew.

## 3. Has Anyone Seen This Allusion?

Each of these fills in the "history of interpretation" criterion for an audit-3 verdict. Ask neutrally, and ask whether anyone rejects the link.

**3.1 Lamentations 3:1–9 behind Job 19:6–12**

- **Ask:** Which commentators on Job 19 or on Lamentations 3 compare Job 19:6–12 ("He has walled up my way … He has put darkness on my paths") with Lamentations 3:1–9, and what direction of dependence, if any, do they argue?
- **Why:** Audit 3 rated Lam 3:1–9 at Job 19:6–20 moderate–high (a real result, in the upper tail of chance). It rated the larger "arc" (Lam 3:36, 58–59 against 19:6, 25–27) low.
- **Watch for:** a direction settled only by the date of Lamentations.

**3.2 Isaiah 30:8 behind Job 19:23–24**

- **Ask:** Do commentators connect Job's wish that his words be "written … inscribed in a book … engraved in the rock forever" (Job 19:23–24) with Isaiah 30:8 ("write it on a tablet … inscribe it on a scroll, that it may serve in the time to come as a witness forever")?
- **Why:** Audit 3 confirmed it at moderate–high. "Write", "book" and "inscribe" occur together only in these two verses, and the Greek gives both Isaiah's "book … for ever".
- **Watch for:** answers that discuss only ancient writing materials (background) and not the link.

**3.3 Habakkuk 1:2 behind Job 19:7**

- **Ask:** Which commentators compare Job 19:7 ("Behold, I cry, 'Violence!' but I get no answer; I shout for help, but there is no justice") with Habakkuk 1:2–4, and does anyone see the shared language as a common lament formula instead?
- **Why:** Audit 3 rated the words moderate–high (שׁוע, "cry for help", with חָמָס, "violence", only Hab 1:2 and Job 19:7) and the shared shape low–moderate.
- **Watch for:** the phrase "no justice", which is Isa 59:8's exact wording rather than Habakkuk's.

**3.4 Psalm 102 behind Job 19:20 and 3:24**

- **Ask:** Do commentators connect Job 19:20 ("My bone clings to my skin and my flesh") with Psalm 102:5 [Hebrew 102:6], or Job 3:24 ("my groaning comes at the sight of my food") with Psalm 102:4–5 [Hebrew 102:5–6]?
- **Why:** Audit 3 rated Ps 102 at *high* on the words at 19:20, where the wording "my bone clings" is exact and found only in these two verses. With 3:24 it called Ps 102 a live source (moderate–high).
- **Watch for:** confusion between the Hebrew and English verse numbers of the psalm.

**3.5 Isaiah 14 behind Job 18:17–19**

- **Ask:** Which commentators connect Bildad's wicked man, who has "no name … no offspring or posterity" (Job 18:17–19), with Isaiah 14:20–22 ("I will cut off from Babylon name and survivors, offspring and posterity")?
- **Why:** Audit 3 rated it moderate. נִין + נֶכֶד ("offspring and posterity") occurs only at Gen 21:23, Isa 14:22 and Job 18:19, in a cluster with "name" and "survivor". The rank argument was withdrawn.
- **Watch for:** answers that pick up only the general "memory perishes" theme.

**3.6 Psalm 78:49 behind Job 20:23**

- **Ask:** Do commentators connect Job 20:23 ("God will send His fierce anger on him and will rain it on him while he is eating") with Psalm 78:24–31 and 78:49, where God sent "His burning anger" on Israel "while their food was in their mouths"?
- **Why:** This is new in audit 3 (queue #84, moderate–high). The exact phrase "He sent upon them His burning anger" occurs only at Ps 78:49 and Job 20:23. It replaces Ps 11:6 as the main partner at 20:23.
- **Watch for:** whether the Sodom reading (Gen 19:24; Ps 11:6) is asserted instead, without the wording.

**3.7 Isaiah 5:11–14 behind Job 21:12–14**

- **Ask:** Which commentators compare the feasting wicked of Job 21:11–14 (timbrel, harp, flute; "they go down to Sheol"; "we do not even desire the knowledge of Your ways") with Isaiah 5:11–14?
- **Why:** This is new in audit 3 (queue #81, moderate). Lyre with timbrel within three verses of Sheol occurs only here and in Isa 5, and "knowledge" is refused in both. It replaces Isa 14:11 for 21:12–13.
- **Watch for:** answers that see only a general picture of revelry.

**3.8 Isaiah 59:4 and 33:11 behind Job 15:35**

- **Ask:** Which commentators discuss the parallels between Job 15:34–35 ("they conceive mischief and bring forth iniquity … fire consumes the tents of the corrupt") and Isaiah 59:4, Isaiah 33:11–14 or Psalm 7:14 [Hebrew 7:15], and do they treat it as a shared proverbial idiom?
- **Why:** Audit 3 reverted Isa 59 to low–moderate (a shared idiom) and named Isa 33:11–15 as an equal co-source. Davison (HDB) lists Isa 59:4 (round 2). Does anyone mention Isa 33?
- **Watch for:** inflating words such as "clearly dependent".

**3.9 The friends' portraits and Job's own losses**

- **Ask:** Do commentators observe that the friends' descriptions of the wicked man's fate in Job 18:13–19 and 20:26–28 (skin devoured, no offspring or survivor, fire, the house carried away) match what happened to Job in chapters 1–2, and do they think the friends mean it as an accusation?
- **Why:** Audit 3 found this a reader-level resonance, not a demonstrated design (#75). A commentator who reads it as deliberate accusation would be `[S]` evidence for the friends' intent, not for the author's design.
- **Watch for:** the difference between "the reader hears it" and "the author built it".

## 4. Lexicon, Grammar and Text

**4.1 Job 20:23, the BHS proposal "fire"**

- **Ask:** What does HALOT say about בִּלְחוּמוֹ in Job 20:23 ("while he is eating"), and does it mention the proposal to read a noun meaning "fire" there and at 18:15?
- **Why:** This is Open Question 6 of the 15–21 dig. BHS proposes a noun "fire" (מַבֵּל) at both verses and cross-refers them. If the proposal stood, the Sodom reading of 18:15 would weaken further.
- **Watch for:** an answer drawn from the BHS footnote rather than from the lexicon's own discussion.

**4.2 Job 19:20, "the skin of my teeth"**

- **Ask:** What does HALOT, or Delitzsch, say about the expression "I have escaped only by the skin of my teeth" in Job 19:20, and what are the main proposed meanings?
- **Why:** The 19 dig ties 19:20's "escaped" (וָאֶתְמַלְּטָה) to the messengers' "I alone have escaped" (1:15–19). BHS records a proposal to read "gnawed" for "escaped" (Open Question 6 of the 19 dig).
- **Watch for:** proverbial English explanations without a source.

**4.3 Job 16:8, "it has become a witness"**

- **Ask:** How do Delitzsch and HALOT understand כַּחֲשִׁי ("my leanness") in Job 16:8, and does either note the sense "lie, deception" for the same consonants?
- **Why:** Audit 3 kept Deut 19:16 behind 16:8 at moderate, and auditor A noted that the play on כַּחַשׁ ("lie" or "leanness") fits the false-witness law. The witness's falsehood would be in the word itself.
- **Watch for:** confusion between כַּחַשׁ (noun) and the verb כחשׁ.

## 5. Background

**5.1 Job 21:32–33, the watched tomb**

- **Ask:** What does the background literature say about the custom behind Job 21:32–33 ("men will keep watch over his tomb … the clods of the valley will gently cover him; moreover, all men will follow after him"), and what is meant by the word translated "tomb" (גָּדִישׁ)?
- **Why:** The 15–21 dig tags the burial customs `[S]`. Audit 3 rated the contrast with Isa 14:19 (the tyrant "cast out of your tomb") low, so the custom itself is what the sermon would use.
- **Watch for:** answers drawn from later Jewish practice without saying so.

**5.2 Job 18:15, brimstone on the dwelling**

- **Ask:** Is there evidence from the ancient Near East for scattering sulphur or salt on a destroyed or cursed dwelling, and do commentators on Job 18:15 refer to it?
- **Why:** Audit 3 kept 18:15 as a Sodom-type motif (low–moderate) and noted Deut 29:22 [Eng 29:23]. A known curse or purification practice would be a rival explanation to Sodom.
- **Watch for:** uncited sentences.
