Claim Audit: Philemon

Passages audited: Philemon 7, 12, 20 (the “frame of hearts”, queue item P1); Philemon 13, 16, 21 (the ὑπέρ sequence, queue item P2)
Date: 22 October 2026
Purpose: the short audit of the two design claims from the overview (v0.1.0) and the 1–25 dig (v1.1), run before the Finalise so that neither enters the overview above the rating it can bear
Method: two blind auditors in turn (the house rule of 16 October 2026). Auditor A took the lexical constituents, rivals and Greek senses; auditor B took the design claims and ran the chance baselines, reading A’s report only after designing them. Each had a neutral claims file, a brief, and a corpus bundle (SBLGNT with its word index; Swete’s LXX; the NA28 and NASB95 Philemon exports) and nothing else — no overview, no dig, no commentary. The main session then reproduced the decisive results (Spot checks, below)
Editions: every New Testament count is from the SBLGNT, by lemma; every Greek Old Testament count is from Swete, by accent-stripped word-beginning with each hit read for sense

Warrant tags: [T] the text states or shows it · [I] a reasonable inference from the text · [S] supplied by a secondary source or by tradition.

Claims Audited

Claim P1a: σπλάγχνον and ἀναπαύω together only at Phlm 7 and 20

Source: overview, Structural Arc Map and Echo Table 1 (words high); dig v1.1, Headline 2 and Tool 3. Claim: σπλάγχνον (“heart”) stands in Philemon at vv. 7, 12 and 20 only; ἀναπαύω (“to refresh”) at vv. 7 and 20 only; the two stand together in one verse at Phlm 7 and 20 and nowhere else in the New Testament or in Swete’s LXX.

What was checked. Every NT occurrence of each lemma (σπλάγχνον 11; ἀναπαύω 12) and every verse holding both; the test widened to ἀνάπαυσις (“rest”), ἐπαναπαύομαι (“rest upon”) and συναναπαύομαι (“be refreshed together”), and to a five-verse window. In Swete, σπλαγχν- with its compounds and the augmented ἐσπλαγχν-, against ἀναπαυ-, ἀνεπαυ-, ἀναπεπαυ- and the passive forms.

Result. Confirmed in every part [T]. The only other NT place where the two words fall within five verses is 2 Cor 7:13 and 7:15 (see P1d). In Swete no verse has both, and none has them within three verses. Positive controls: the co-occurrence search returned the two Philemon verses; in Swete, Prov 12:10 and both verses with ἀναπέπαυται (Isa 14:4; Jer 49:10) were returned.

Nuance (auditor A). The Swete absence is weaker evidence than it looks: σπλάγχνον is almost absent from the Greek of the Hebrew-canon books (twice, both in Proverbs) and is chiefly a word of 2 and 4 Maccabees, Wisdom and Sirach, often meaning “entrails”. The NT result is the meaningful one.

Verdict: Confirmed. High.

Book-overview action required: none for the words; add the nuance on Swete where the overview says “nor anywhere in Swete’s LXX”.

Claim P1b: the only singular vocative “brother” in a New Testament letter

Source: dig v1.1, Headline 3; Book-Overview Tension 3. Claim: ἀδελφέ (“brother”, vocative singular) at vv. 7 and 20 is the only singular vocative “brother” in any NT letter.

What was checked. Every token of ἀδελφός parsed vocative singular in the NT; every vocative of the lemma; the other singular addresses in the letters to one person.

Result. Six tokens in the NT — Luke 6:42; Acts 9:17; 21:20; 22:13; Phlm 7; Phlm 20 — so within the letters, Phlm 7 and 20 only [T]. The lemma has 111 vocative tokens in all; every other is ἀδελφοί (“brothers”).

Nuance (both auditors). The rarity is largely genre. Philemon is one of six NT letters written wholly or chiefly to one person, and only in those is a singular address available. What carries information is the choice among singular addresses: Paul calls Timothy τέκνον (“child”, 1 Tim 1:18; 2 Tim 2:1), the Elder calls Gaius ἀγαπητέ (“beloved”, 3 John 2, 5, 11), and Paul calls Philemon “brother” — at the two verses that carry the hearts pair [T]/[I]. “Brother” is not confined to those verses: ἀδελφός also stands at v. 1 (Timothy) and v. 16 (Onesimus), and ἀδελφή (“sister”) at v. 2 [T].

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. High. Measure the rarity against letters to one person, not against all letters.

Book-overview action required: reword the claim in any Finalise to “the only singular vocative ‘brother’ in the New Testament letters — a form available only in letters to one person, where Paul’s other singular address is ‘child’”.

Claim P1c: vv. 7 and 20 as a designed frame, with v. 12 at its centre

Source: overview, Structural Arc Map (“the frame of hearts … the first design claim for the claim audit”) and Echo Table 1 (moderate, design); dig v1.1, Tool 3 and Headline 2 (design moderate, no baseline). Claim: vv. 7 and 20 frame the body of the appeal (vv. 8–19), with v. 12 (“my very heart”) at the centre, so that the letter argues: Philemon has refreshed the saints’ hearts (v. 7); Onesimus is Paul’s heart (v. 12); therefore receiving Onesimus is refreshing Paul’s heart (v. 20).

Baselines (auditor B). Content words only — nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs — with the commonest words set aside (εἰμί, γίνομαι, ἔχω, λέγω, ποιέω, πᾶς, πολύς, adverbial καί, οὐ, μή, νῦν, οὕτως, οἶδα, and the names θεός, κύριος, Χριστός, Ἰησοῦς); a looser list setting aside only three words did not change the ranking. An “exclusive pair” is two content words that two verses share and that stand together in no other verse of the text.

Baseline Question Result
Within Philemon Of all 153 verse pairs at least 8 verses apart, which share an exclusive pair? Three: vv. 1/9 (Παῦλος “Paul” + δέσμιος “prisoner”); vv. 1/16 (ἀγαπητός “beloved” + ἀδελφός “brother”); vv. 7/20 (σπλάγχνον + ἀναπαύω + ἀδελφός — the only pair sharing three, every pairing among them exclusive, both rare words included). vv. 7/20 rank first on every measure
Other short letters Does some verse near the start and some verse near the end, at least a third of the text apart, share an exclusive pair? Yes in every one: 2 John (3 pairs, e.g. vv. 1/13 τέκνον “child” + ἐκλεκτός “elect”); 3 John (2); Jude (3, e.g. vv. 3/20 πίστις “faith” + ἀγαπητός + ἅγιος “holy”); Titus (6, e.g. 1:2 / 3:7 αἰώνιος “eternal” + ζωή “life” + ἐλπίς “hope”). The basic pattern is common and proves nothing alone
25-verse stretches across Paul Of all 1,716 runs of 25 consecutive verses within Romans–Titus, how many reach the level of vv. 7/20 (an end-to-end exclusive pair, including a rare word, with three shared words)? Any end-to-end exclusive pair: 49.5 %; with a rare word: 21.4 %; with three shared words: 12.2 %; all three together: 4.7 % (about 22 distinct verse pairs, several of them recurring lists rather than frames)
Leave one out Does the result survive without σπλάγχνον, ἀναπαύω, or the vocative? As a pair, yes in each case; but without either rare word it falls to the one-in-five background, and without the vocative to the rare-pair level. No single element carries it; the three together do

The rival: Paul’s idiom (P1d). The vocabulary is Paul’s stock language for joy at a refreshed co-worker (2 Cor 7:4–15; see P1d), and 2 Cor 7 shows Paul returning to that idiom at the end of a section (παράκλησις “comfort” + χαρά “joy” at 7:4 and again at 7:13) [T]. Auditor A therefore rated P1c Needs reframing; auditor B, on the baselines, Confirmed with nuance. They agree on the substance: the words are idiom, but their placement is Philemon’s own — half the cluster in the thanksgiving (v. 7), half at the close of the appeal (v. 20) — and the one feature found nowhere else is that ἀναπαύω takes σπλάγχνα as its object, where at 1 Cor 16:18 and 2 Cor 7:13 it takes πνεῦμα (“spirit”) [T]. That switch is what ties vv. 7 and 20 to v. 12’s τὰ ἐμὰ σπλάγχνα (“my very heart”) [I].

What the audit does not support. - “A frame around vv. 8–19.” NA28 paragraphs the letter 1–3 | 4–7 | 8–14 | 15–20 | 21–22 | 23–24 | 25 [T: as NA28 prints it]. On that layout v. 7 closes the thanksgiving and v. 20 closes the appeal: they are matching section-endings, and v. 20 belongs inside the body, not outside it. - “v. 12 at the centre.” With three occurrences, the middle one must fall between the other two; its position proves nothing. Its force is in what it says — Onesimus is Paul’s heart — which is an exegetical point, not a structural one [I]. - The three-step “therefore” (7 → 12 → 20). The vocabulary allows the reading; the counts neither require nor test it [I].

Adjudication. The main session takes auditor B’s verdict, with auditor A’s reframing written into it: the baselines put the pair first of 153 within the letter and in about one 25-verse stretch in twenty across Paul, which is more than chance supplies, and the πνεῦμα → σπλάγχνα switch is a choice made for this letter.

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. Moderate–high that v. 20 deliberately takes up v. 7; low that v. 12 is a structural centre; the “frame around vv. 8–19” is withdrawn.

Restated claim: v. 20 deliberately takes up v. 7 — in Paul’s idiom of refreshment, switched from “spirit” to “heart” and re-addressed with the singular “brother” — so that the thanksgiving and the appeal end on the same note; v. 12 supplies the identification (“that is, my very heart”) that gives v. 20 its point.

Book-overview action required: Structural Arc Map (“the frame of hearts” bullet) and Echo Table 1 — restate as above; drop “frame around vv. 8–19” and “v. 12 at its centre”; raise the rating from moderate to moderate–high for the v. 7 → v. 20 recapitulation; cite the baselines.

Claim P1d: 2 Corinthians 7 as Paul’s own idiom

Source: dig v1.1, Tool 11 (Pauline companions) and Book-Overview Tension 4. Claim: χαρά + παράκλησις + ἀναπαύω in one verse only at 2 Cor 7:13 and Phlm 7; σπλάγχνα + ὑπακοή (“obedience”) in one verse only at 2 Cor 7:15.

Result. Both confirmed [T]. The parallel is wider than the claim stated (auditor A, reproduced): - 2 Cor 7:4 joins πολλή … παρρησία (“great boldness”) with παράκλησις and χαρά — the only NT verse joining παρρησία to either — as Phlm 7–8 runs χαρὰν πολλήν … παράκλησιν … πολλὴν … παρρησίαν [T]. - ὑπακοή falls within three verses of ἀναπαύω only at 2 Cor 7:13–15 and Phlm 20–21 [T]. - 2 Cor 7:15 also has the envoy received, ἐδέξασθε αὐτόν (“you received him”), beside Phlm 17’s προσλαβοῦ αὐτόν (“accept him”) [T] (auditor B). - The five words χαρά, παράκλησις, ἀναπαύω, σπλάγχνον, ὑπακοή fall within three consecutive verses only at 2 Cor 7:13–15 in the NT; in Philemon the cluster is split, four of the five at v. 7, two returning at v. 20, and ὑπακοή at v. 21 [T]. - The NA28 apparatus at Phlm 7: the majority text reads χάριν (“thanks”) for χαράν (“joy”); NA28 and the SBLGNT read χαράν with א A C D and others [T: as NA28 prints it], so the joy link rests on the well-supported text.

Verdict: Confirmed (the facts); its bearing on P1c is written into P1c. High.

Book-overview action required: add 2 Cor 7:4–15 to the overview’s Pauline companions, with the four data above.

Claim P2a: ὑπέρ at vv. 13, 16 and 21

Source: overview, Echo Table 13; dig v1.1, Tool 10 and Tool 11 Move 4. Claim: ὑπέρ stands in Philemon at vv. 13, 16 and 21 only.

Result. Confirmed [T], with the cases now stated:

Verse Phrase Case Sense
13 ὑπὲρ σοῦ (“on your behalf”) genitive on behalf of, in place of
16 ὑπὲρ δοῦλον (“more than a slave”) accusative beyond, more than
21 ὑπὲρ ἃ λέγω (“more than what I say”) accusative beyond, more than

Frequency. Philemon 3 in 334 words (8.98 per 1,000) — the densest rate in Paul — against 97 in 31,966 words in Paul’s other twelve letters (3.03 per 1,000) [T]. At Paul’s rate a text of Philemon’s length would be expected to hold about one; the chance of three or more is about 9 %, and 15 % of 25-verse stretches in Paul hold three or more (auditor B). The count alone is unremarkable.

Verdict: Confirmed. High.

Claim P2b: an escalating ὑπέρ sequence, vv. 13 → 16 → 21

Source: overview, Echo Table 13 (moderate, design; “a claim-audit target”); dig v1.1, Tool 11 Move 4 (“escalates”). Claim: the three uses form an escalation — on your behalf → more than a slave → more than what I say — that hints at the content of v. 21’s “more”.

Result. - v. 13 is a different construction. It is the ordinary genitive “on behalf of” — Paul’s dominant use, about 88 of his 100 tokens — and vv. 16 and 21 are the comparative accusative “beyond” [T]. The referents shift too: Onesimus’s service (v. 13), Onesimus’s status (v. 16), Philemon’s action (v. 21) [T]. - The “beyond” pair is genuinely marked. Accusative ὑπέρ is rare in Paul — ten tokens in the other twelve letters (1 Cor 4:6; 10:13; 2 Cor 1:8; 11:23, adverbial; 12:6; 12:13; Gal 1:14; Eph 1:22; 3:20; Phil 2:9), about 0.3 per 1,000 words [T]. Two in 334 words would occur by chance less than 1 % of the time, and only one other place in Paul has two within 25 verses (2 Cor 11:23 – 12:13) (auditor B; reproduced). - v. 21 uses stock phrasing. ὑπέρ with a relative clause is Pauline idiom: ὑπὲρ ἃ γέγραπται (“beyond what is written”, 1 Cor 4:6), ὑπὲρ ὃ δύνασθε (“beyond what you are able”, 1 Cor 10:13), ὑπὲρ ὃ βλέπει (“more than he sees”, 2 Cor 12:6) [T]. - The three-step chain is an artefact of English. It needs “on your behalf” to be heard as a first “more”; in Greek it is not. The NASB95 itself does not make the chain audible (“on your behalf … more than a slave … even more than what I say”) [T].

Verdict: Needs reframing. High.

Restated claim: vv. 16 and 21 form a marked “beyond … beyond” pair, more than chance would give: Onesimus is “beyond a slave”, and Philemon will do “beyond what I say”. v. 13’s ὑπὲρ σοῦ (“on your behalf”) is a different construction and not part of it.

Book-overview action required: Echo Table 13 — restate as a two-step pair (vv. 16 → 21), moderate–high on the pairing; remove v. 13 and the word “escalates”.

Claim P2c: v. 13 → v. 21 leaning v. 21 towards release to Paul’s service

Source: dig v1.1, Difficult Verses (v. 21: “the text leans slightly to option (c) over (b)”) and Open Question 2. Claim: because v. 13 has already named Paul’s wish to keep Onesimus for service, the link to v. 21 leans “even more” towards release to Paul’s service.

Result. - The ὑπέρ link cannot carry it: vv. 13 and 21 share only a preposition, in two senses (P2b) [T]. - vv. 13–14 do keep the option live, on other grounds. Paul wanted to keep Onesimus (κατέχειν, “to keep”) that he might serve (διακονῇ, “minister”), and declined without Philemon’s consent so that his goodness be voluntary [T]; and ποιέω (“to do”) ties v. 14’s οὐδὲν ἠθέλησα ποιῆσαι (“I did not want to do anything”) to v. 21’s ποιήσεις (“you will do”) [T] (auditor A). - Two links to v. 21 are closer. γράφω (“write”) and λέγω (“say”) stand together only at vv. 19 and 21 in the letter [T], so “what I say” most naturally picks up the requests just made — accept him as me, charge it to me, refresh my heart (vv. 17–20) [I]. And ὑπακοή (“obedience”) at v. 21 joins the letter to Paul’s practice of asking his readers’ obedience to what he writes (2 Cor 2:9; 7:15) [T], which fits the reading of “even more” as a confident closing with no specific content as well as any other [I]. - The alternatives remain equally live: manumission (“both in the flesh and in the Lord”, v. 16); a fuller reception “as me” (v. 17); no specific content [I].

Verdict: Uncertain — flag for research. Moderate. The v1.1 lean is withdrawn: v. 21 does not decide, and vv. 13–14 keep release to Paul’s service one live option among three or four.

Book-overview action required: none in the overview (it did not make the inference); the dig’s Difficult Verses and Open Question 2 to be revised.

Spot checks

All reproduced by the main session from the SBLGNT word index unless stated.

  1. σπλάγχνον (“heart”) stands in eleven NT verses, three of them in Philemon (vv. 7, 12, 20); ἀναπαύω (“to refresh”) in twelve, two in Philemon (vv. 7, 20); the two together only at Phlm 7 and 20.
  2. The vocative singular of ἀδελφός (“brother”) occurs at Luke 6:42; Acts 9:17; 21:20; 22:13; Phlm 7; Phlm 20, and in no other letter.
  3. In Paul’s letters the object of ἀναπαύω is τὸ … πνεῦμα (“the spirit”) at 1 Cor 16:18 and 2 Cor 7:13, and τὰ σπλάγχνα (“the heart”) only at Phlm 7 and 20.
  4. παρρησία (“boldness”) stands in one verse with χαρά (“joy”) or παράκλησις (“comfort”) only at 2 Cor 7:4.
  5. Of the 153 Philemon verse pairs at least eight verses apart, three share an exclusive pair of content words — vv. 1/9, 1/16, 7/20 — and only vv. 7/20 share three (σπλάγχνον, ἀναπαύω, ἀδελφός).
  6. In 2 John, of the fifteen verse pairs at least eight verses apart, vv. 1/13 share τέκνον (“child”) and ἐκλεκτός (“elect”), found together in no other verse of the letter — the basic pattern occurs in a letter where no one proposes a designed frame.
  7. ὑπέρ stands 97 times in 31,966 words in Paul’s letters other than Philemon, and three times in Philemon’s 334 words (vv. 13, 16, 21).
  8. Taking ὑπέρ followed by an accusative, Paul’s other letters give nine verses — 1 Cor 4:6; 10:13; 2 Cor 1:8; 12:6; 12:13; Gal 1:14; Eph 1:22; 3:20; Phil 2:9 — with the adverbial ὑπὲρ ἐγώ (“I more so”) at 2 Cor 11:23 making ten.
  9. At Paul’s rates, the chance of two “beyond” uses in 334 words is under 1 %, and of three or more ὑπέρ of any kind about 9 %.
  10. NA28 paragraphs Philemon 1–3 | 4–7 | 8–14 | 15–20 | 21–22 | 23–24 | 25 (the NA28 export).

Positive controls: the hearts co-occurrence search returned Phlm 7 and 20 before it was run across the NT; in Swete, Prov 12:10 and the two verses with ἀναπέπαυται were returned before the cross-search; the vocative search returned the Luke and Acts tokens before it was asked about the letters; the ὑπέρ total outside Philemon was reached independently by both auditors and the main session (97).
