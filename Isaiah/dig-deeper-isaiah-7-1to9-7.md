## The Passage

The Hebrew is the WLC. The paragraph markers are given as Leningrad prints them: ס (*setumah*, closed) and פ (*petuchah*, open). The NASB95 follows each verse. The extent is set at the Masoretic seams: the unit begins immediately after the *petuchah* that closes chapter 6, and ends at the *setumah* after 9:7 [Heb 9:6]; the refrain-poem that follows begins at 9:8 [Heb 9:7].

> **7:1** וַיְהִ֡י בִּימֵ֣י אָ֠חָז בֶּן־יוֹתָ֨ם בֶּן־עֻזִּיָּ֜הוּ מֶ֣לֶךְ יְהוּדָ֗ה עָלָ֣ה רְצִ֣ין מֶֽלֶךְ־אֲ֠רָם וּפֶ֨קַח בֶּן־רְמַלְיָ֤הוּ מֶֽלֶךְ־יִשְׂרָאֵל֙ יְר֣וּשָׁלִַ֔ם לַמִּלְחָמָ֖ה עָלֶ֑יהָ וְלֹ֥א יָכֹ֖ל לְהִלָּחֵ֥ם עָלֶֽיהָ׃
>
> Now it came about in the days of Ahaz, the son of Jotham, the son of Uzziah, king of Judah, that Rezin the king of Aram and Pekah the son of Remaliah, king of Israel, went up to Jerusalem to wage war against it, but could not conquer it.

> **7:2** וַיֻּגַּ֗ד לְבֵ֤ית דָּוִד֙ לֵאמֹ֔ר נָ֥חָֽה אֲרָ֖ם עַל־אֶפְרָ֑יִם וַיָּ֤נַע לְבָבוֹ֙ וּלְבַ֣ב עַמּ֔וֹ כְּנ֥וֹעַ עֲצֵי־יַ֖עַר מִפְּנֵי־רֽוּחַ׃
>
> When it was reported to the house of David, saying, “The Arameans have camped in Ephraim,” his heart and the hearts of his people shook as the trees of the forest shake with the wind.

> **7:3** וַיֹּ֣אמֶר יְהוָה֮ אֶֽל־יְשַׁעְיָהוּ֒ צֵא־נָא֙ לִקְרַ֣את אָחָ֔ז אַתָּ֕ה וּשְׁאָ֖ר יָשׁ֣וּב בְּנֶ֑ךָ אֶל־קְצֵ֗ה תְּעָלַת֙ הַבְּרֵכָ֣ה הָעֶלְיוֹנָ֔ה אֶל־מְסִלַּ֖ת שְׂדֵ֥ה כוֹבֵֽס׃
>
> Then the LORD said to Isaiah, “Go out now to meet Ahaz, you and your son Shear-jashub, at the end of the conduit of the upper pool, on the highway to the fuller’s field,

> **7:4** וְאָמַרְתָּ֣ אֵ֠לָיו הִשָּׁמֵ֨ר וְהַשְׁקֵ֜ט אַל־תִּירָ֗א וּלְבָבְךָ֙ אַל־יֵרַ֔ךְ מִשְּׁנֵ֨י זַנְב֧וֹת הָאוּדִ֛ים הָעֲשֵׁנִ֖ים הָאֵ֑לֶּה בָּחֳרִי־אַ֛ף רְצִ֥ין וַאֲרָ֖ם וּבֶן־רְמַלְיָֽהוּ׃
>
> and say to him, ‘Take care and be calm, have no fear and do not be fainthearted because of these two stubs of smoldering firebrands, on account of the fierce anger of Rezin and Aram and the son of Remaliah.

> **7:5** יַ֗עַן כִּֽי־יָעַ֥ץ עָלֶ֛יךָ אֲרָ֖ם רָעָ֑ה אֶפְרַ֥יִם וּבֶן־רְמַלְיָ֖הוּ לֵאמֹֽר׃
>
> ‘Because Aram, with Ephraim and the son of Remaliah, has planned evil against you, saying,

> **7:6** נַעֲלֶ֤ה בִֽיהוּדָה֙ וּנְקִיצֶ֔נָּה וְנַבְקִעֶ֖נָּה אֵלֵ֑ינוּ וְנַמְלִ֥יךְ מֶ֨לֶךְ֙ בְּתוֹכָ֔הּ אֵ֖ת בֶּן־טָֽבְאַֽל׃ **ס**
>
> “Let us go up against Judah and terrorize it, and make for ourselves a breach in its walls and set up the son of Tabeel as king in the midst of it,”

> **7:7** כֹּ֥ה אָמַ֖ר אֲדֹנָ֣י יְהוִ֑ה לֹ֥א תָק֖וּם וְלֹ֥א תִֽהְיֶֽה׃
>
> thus says the Lord GOD: “It shall not stand nor shall it come to pass.

> **7:8** כִּ֣י רֹ֤אשׁ אֲרָם֙ דַּמֶּ֔שֶׂק וְרֹ֥אשׁ דַּמֶּ֖שֶׂק רְצִ֑ין וּבְע֗וֹד שִׁשִּׁ֤ים וְחָמֵשׁ֙ שָׁנָ֔ה יֵחַ֥ת אֶפְרַ֖יִם מֵעָֽם׃
>
> “For the head of Aram is Damascus and the head of Damascus is Rezin (now within another 65 years Ephraim will be shattered, so that it is no longer a people),

> **7:9** וְרֹ֤אשׁ אֶפְרַ֨יִם֙ שֹׁמְר֔וֹן וְרֹ֥אשׁ שֹׁמְר֖וֹן בֶּן־רְמַלְיָ֑הוּ אִ֚ם לֹ֣א תַאֲמִ֔ינוּ כִּ֖י לֹ֥א תֵאָמֵֽנוּ׃ **ס**
>
> and the head of Ephraim is Samaria and the head of Samaria is the son of Remaliah. If you will not believe, you surely shall not last.” ’ ”

> **7:10** וַיּ֣וֹסֶף יְהוָ֔ה דַּבֵּ֥ר אֶל־אָחָ֖ז לֵאמֹֽר׃
>
> Then the LORD spoke again to Ahaz, saying,

> **7:11** שְׁאַל־לְךָ֣ א֔וֹת מֵעִ֖ם יְהוָ֣ה אֱלֹהֶ֑יךָ הַעְמֵ֣ק שְׁאָ֔לָה א֖וֹ הַגְבֵּ֥הַּ לְמָֽעְלָה׃
>
> “Ask a sign for yourself from the LORD your God; make it deep as Sheol or high as heaven.”

> **7:12** וַיֹּ֖אמֶר אָחָ֑ז לֹא־אֶשְׁאַ֥ל וְלֹֽא־אֲנַסֶּ֖ה אֶת־יְהוָֽה׃
>
> But Ahaz said, “I will not ask, nor will I test the LORD!”

> **7:13** וַיֹּ֕אמֶר שִׁמְעוּ־נָ֖א בֵּ֣ית דָּוִ֑ד הַמְעַ֤ט מִכֶּם֙ הַלְא֣וֹת אֲנָשִׁ֔ים כִּ֥י תַלְא֖וּ גַּ֥ם אֶת־אֱלֹהָֽי׃
>
> Then he said, “Listen now, O house of David! Is it too slight a thing for you to try the patience of men, that you will try the patience of my God as well?

> **7:14** לָ֠כֵן יִתֵּ֨ן אֲדֹנָ֥י ה֛וּא לָכֶ֖ם א֑וֹת הִנֵּ֣ה הָעַלְמָ֗ה הָרָה֙ וְיֹלֶ֣דֶת בֵּ֔ן וְקָרָ֥את שְׁמ֖וֹ עִמָּ֥נוּ אֵֽל׃
>
> “Therefore the Lord Himself will give you a sign: Behold, a virgin will be with child and bear a son, and she will call His name Immanuel.

> **7:15** חֶמְאָ֥ה וּדְבַ֖שׁ יֹאכֵ֑ל לְדַעְתּ֛וֹ מָא֥וֹס בָּרָ֖ע וּבָח֥וֹר בַּטּֽוֹב׃
>
> “He will eat curds and honey at the time He knows enough to refuse evil and choose good.

> **7:16** כִּ֠י בְּטֶ֨רֶם יֵדַ֥ע הַנַּ֛עַר מָאֹ֥ס בָּרָ֖ע וּבָחֹ֣ר בַּטּ֑וֹב תֵּעָזֵ֤ב הָאֲדָמָה֙ אֲשֶׁ֣ר אַתָּ֣ה קָ֔ץ מִפְּנֵ֖י שְׁנֵ֥י מְלָכֶֽיהָ׃
>
> “For before the boy will know enough to refuse evil and choose good, the land whose two kings you dread will be forsaken.

> **7:17** יָבִ֨יא יְהוָ֜ה עָלֶ֗יךָ וְעַֽל־עַמְּךָ֮ וְעַל־בֵּ֣ית אָבִיךָ֒ יָמִים֙ אֲשֶׁ֣ר לֹא־בָ֔אוּ לְמִיּ֥וֹם סוּר־אֶפְרַ֖יִם מֵעַ֣ל יְהוּדָ֑ה אֵ֖ת מֶ֥לֶךְ אַשּֽׁוּר׃ **פ**
>
> “The LORD will bring on you, on your people, and on your father’s house such days as have never come since the day that Ephraim separated from Judah, the king of Assyria.”

> **7:18** וְהָיָ֣ה ׀ בַּיּ֣וֹם הַה֗וּא יִשְׁרֹ֤ק יְהוָה֙ לַזְּב֔וּב אֲשֶׁ֥ר בִּקְצֵ֖ה יְאֹרֵ֣י מִצְרָ֑יִם וְלַ֨דְּבוֹרָ֔ה אֲשֶׁ֖ר בְּאֶ֥רֶץ אַשּֽׁוּר׃
>
> In that day the LORD will whistle for the fly that is in the remotest part of the rivers of Egypt and for the bee that is in the land of Assyria.

> **7:19** וּבָ֨אוּ וְנָח֤וּ כֻלָּם֙ בְּנַחֲלֵ֣י הַבַּתּ֔וֹת וּבִנְקִיקֵ֖י הַסְּלָעִ֑ים וּבְכֹל֙ הַנַּ֣עֲצוּצִ֔ים וּבְכֹ֖ל הַנַּהֲלֹלִֽים׃
>
> They will all come and settle on the steep ravines, on the ledges of the cliffs, on all the thorn bushes and on all the watering places.

> **7:20** בַּיּ֣וֹם הַה֡וּא יְגַלַּ֣ח אֲדֹנָי֩ בְּתַ֨עַר הַשְּׂכִירָ֜ה בְּעֶבְרֵ֤י נָהָר֙ בְּמֶ֣לֶךְ אַשּׁ֔וּר אֶת־הָרֹ֖אשׁ וְשַׂ֣עַר הָרַגְלָ֑יִם וְגַ֥ם אֶת־הַזָּקָ֖ן תִּסְפֶּֽה׃ **ס**
>
> In that day the Lord will shave with a razor, hired from regions beyond the Euphrates (that is, with the king of Assyria), the head and the hair of the legs; and it will also remove the beard.

> **7:21** וְהָיָ֖ה בַּיּ֣וֹם הַה֑וּא יְחַיֶּה־אִ֛ישׁ עֶגְלַ֥ת בָּקָ֖ר וּשְׁתֵּי־צֹֽאן׃
>
> Now in that day a man may keep alive a heifer and a pair of sheep;

> **7:22** וְהָיָ֗ה מֵרֹ֛ב עֲשׂ֥וֹת חָלָ֖ב יֹאכַ֣ל חֶמְאָ֑ה כִּֽי־חֶמְאָ֤ה וּדְבַשׁ֙ יֹאכֵ֔ל כָּל־הַנּוֹתָ֖ר בְּקֶ֥רֶב הָאָֽרֶץ׃
>
> and because of the abundance of the milk produced he will eat curds, for everyone that is left within the land will eat curds and honey.

> **7:23** וְהָיָה֙ בַּיּ֣וֹם הַה֔וּא יִֽהְיֶ֣ה כָל־מָק֗וֹם אֲשֶׁ֧ר יִֽהְיֶה־שָּׁ֛ם אֶ֥לֶף גֶּ֖פֶן בְּאֶ֣לֶף כָּ֑סֶף לַשָּׁמִ֥יר וְלַשַּׁ֖יִת יִֽהְיֶֽה׃
>
> And it will come about in that day, that every place where there used to be a thousand vines, valued at a thousand shekels of silver, will become briars and thorns.

> **7:24** בַּחִצִּ֥ים וּבַקֶּ֖שֶׁת יָ֣בוֹא שָׁ֑מָּה כִּי־שָׁמִ֥יר וָשַׁ֖יִת תִּֽהְיֶ֥ה כָל־הָאָֽרֶץ׃
>
> People will come there with bows and arrows because all the land will be briars and thorns.

> **7:25** וְכֹ֣ל הֶהָרִ֗ים אֲשֶׁ֤ר בַּמַּעְדֵּר֙ יֵעָ֣דֵר֔וּן לֹֽא־תָב֣וֹא שָׁ֔מָּה יִרְאַ֖ת שָׁמִ֣יר וָשָׁ֑יִת וְהָיָה֙ לְמִשְׁלַ֣ח שׁ֔וֹר וּלְמִרְמַ֖ס שֶֽׂה׃ **פ**
>
> As for all the hills which used to be cultivated with the hoe, you will not go there for fear of briars and thorns; but they will become a place for pasturing oxen and for sheep to trample.

> **8:1** וַיֹּ֤אמֶר יְהוָה֙ אֵלַ֔י קַח־לְךָ֖ גִּלָּי֣וֹן גָּד֑וֹל וּכְתֹ֤ב עָלָיו֙ בְּחֶ֣רֶט אֱנ֔וֹשׁ לְמַהֵ֥ר שָׁלָ֖ל חָ֥שׁ בַּֽז׃
>
> Then the LORD said to me, “Take for yourself a large tablet and write on it in ordinary letters: Swift is the booty, speedy is the prey.

> **8:2** וְאָעִ֣ידָה לִּ֔י עֵדִ֖ים נֶאֱמָנִ֑ים אֵ֚ת אוּרִיָּ֣ה הַכֹּהֵ֔ן וְאֶת־זְכַרְיָ֖הוּ בֶּ֥ן יְבֶרֶכְיָֽהוּ׃
>
> “And I will take to Myself faithful witnesses for testimony, Uriah the priest and Zechariah the son of Jeberechiah.”

> **8:3** וָאֶקְרַב֙ אֶל־הַנְּבִיאָ֔ה וַתַּ֖הַר וַתֵּ֣לֶד בֵּ֑ן וַיֹּ֤אמֶר יְהוָה֙ אֵלַ֔י קְרָ֣א שְׁמ֔וֹ מַהֵ֥ר שָׁלָ֖ל חָ֥שׁ בַּֽז׃
>
> So I approached the prophetess, and she conceived and gave birth to a son. Then the LORD said to me, “Name him Maher-shalal-hash-baz;

> **8:4** כִּ֗י בְּטֶ֨רֶם֙ יֵדַ֣ע הַנַּ֔עַר קְרֹ֖א אָבִ֣י וְאִמִּ֑י יִשָּׂ֣א ׀ אֶת־חֵ֣יל דַּמֶּ֗שֶׂק וְאֵת֙ שְׁלַ֣ל שֹׁמְר֔וֹן לִפְנֵ֖י מֶ֥לֶךְ אַשּֽׁוּר׃ **ס**
>
> for before the boy knows how to cry out ‘My father’ or ‘My mother,’ the wealth of Damascus and the spoil of Samaria will be carried away before the king of Assyria.”

> **8:5** וַיֹּ֣סֶף יְהוָ֔ה דַּבֵּ֥ר אֵלַ֛י ע֖וֹד לֵאמֹֽר׃
>
> Again the LORD spoke to me further, saying,

> **8:6** יַ֗עַן כִּ֤י מָאַס֙ הָעָ֣ם הַזֶּ֔ה אֵ֚ת מֵ֣י הַשִּׁלֹ֔חַ הַהֹלְכִ֖ים לְאַ֑ט וּמְשׂ֥וֹשׂ אֶת־רְצִ֖ין וּבֶן־רְמַלְיָֽהוּ׃
>
> “Inasmuch as these people have rejected the gently flowing waters of Shiloah / And rejoice in Rezin and the son of Remaliah;

> **8:7** וְלָכֵ֡ן הִנֵּ֣ה אֲדֹנָי֩ מַעֲלֶ֨ה עֲלֵיהֶ֜ם אֶת־מֵ֣י הַנָּהָ֗ר הָעֲצוּמִים֙ וְהָ֣רַבִּ֔ים אֶת־מֶ֥לֶךְ אַשּׁ֖וּר וְאֶת־כָּל־כְּבוֹד֑וֹ וְעָלָה֙ עַל־כָּל־אֲפִיקָ֔יו וְהָלַ֖ךְ עַל־כָּל־גְּדוֹתָֽיו׃
>
> “Now therefore, behold, the Lord is about to bring on them the strong and abundant waters of the Euphrates, / Even the king of Assyria and all his glory; / And it will rise up over all its channels and go over all its banks.

> **8:8** וְחָלַ֤ף בִּֽיהוּדָה֙ שָׁטַ֣ף וְעָבַ֔ר עַד־צַוָּ֖אר יַגִּ֑יעַ וְהָיָה֙ מֻטּ֣וֹת כְּנָפָ֔יו מְלֹ֥א רֹֽחַב־אַרְצְךָ֖ עִמָּ֥נוּ אֵֽל׃ **ס**

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims, each reached by more than one tool:

1. **Three named children carry the unit's argument, and 8:18 gathers them.** `[T]` Shear-jashub (שְׁאָר יָשׁוּב, "a remnant will return", 7:3), Immanuel (עִמָּנוּ אֵל, "God with us", 7:14) and Maher-shalal-hash-baz (מַהֵר שָׁלָל חָשׁ בַּז, "swift the spoil, speedy the prey", 8:1–3) are each named at the LORD's direction. 8:18 then says, "I and the children whom the LORD has given me are for signs and wonders (לְאֹתוֹת וּלְמוֹפְתִים) in Israel." The first name is quoted back as an oracle just after the unit closes. The noun שְׁאָר ("remnant") and the verb שׁוּב ("return") stand together in only two verses of the Hebrew Bible, **Isa 10:21 and 10:22**, and 10:21 says the remnant will return "to the mighty God (אֶל־אֵל גִּבּוֹר)" — the title the child receives in 9:6 (WLC). **7:14 cannot be preached rightly with the other two children out of view.**

2. **7:9 turns on a pun on אמן ("be firm, believe"): אִם לֹא תַאֲמִינוּ כִּי לֹא תֵאָמֵנוּ.** `[T]` The root appears in both halves, first as the hiphil ("believe, stand firm"), then as the niphal ("be made firm, endure"). The ESV ("If you are not firm in faith, you will not be firm at all") keeps the single English root. The NASB95 ("If you will not believe, you surely shall not last") keeps the sense and loses the sound. This is **translation loss, not a variant.** The Greek has a third reading: ἐὰν μὴ πιστεύσητε, οὐδὲ μὴ συνῆτε ("if you do not believe, neither will you understand"), which echoes the LXX of 6:9 (οὐ μὴ συνῆτε). Both Greek and Hebrew make the king's survival a question of faith, not of treaty.

3. **Ahaz refuses in the language of Torah, and the prophet calls it wearying God.** `[T]` "I will not ask, and I will not test (וְלֹא־אֲנַסֶּה) the LORD" (7:12) contains the only occurrence of נָסָה ("to test") in Isaiah. It echoes Deut 6:16, "You shall not test (לֹא תְנַסּוּ) the LORD your God, as you tested him at Massah." But the LORD has just *commanded* him to ask (7:11). The answer of 7:13 uses לָאָה ("to weary") twice: wearying men, wearying "my God". **The unbelief is dressed as piety.** `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for the Deuteronomy echo (high confidence: the verb is rare in Isaiah and the object is identical).

4. **Both "therefores" in the unit follow a human refusal.** `[T]` לָכֵן ("therefore") stands in the unit only at 7:14 and 8:7 (WLC). At 7:14 the sign is given *because* Ahaz declined to ask; at 8:7 the River floods *because* "this people has refused the waters of Shiloah" (8:6). Immanuel is announced as the consequence of a no. The sign is grace, but it is not consolation for Ahaz.

5. **"God with us" moves from a name to an address to a confession.** `[T]` עִמָּנוּ אֵל occurs exactly three times, all in this unit (7:14; 8:8; 8:10). At 7:14 it names a child. At 8:8 it addresses the land, "your land, O Immanuel", as the Assyrian flood reaches its neck. At 8:10 it is a clause, "for God is with us" — the reason the nations' plans will fail. The same words are a threat to Judah (the land overrun is *his*) and a defiance of the nations (they cannot win, because *he* is there).

6. **8:21–9:1 [Heb 8:21–23] turns on two opposed roots, קלל ("be light, curse") and כבד ("be heavy, be glorious").** `[T]` The hungry wanderers "curse (וְקִלֵּל) their king and their God" (8:21). Then "in the former time he made light (הֵקַל) the land of Zebulun and the land of Naphtali, and in the latter he has made heavy/glorious (הִכְבִּיד)" (9:1). The verse names the very districts that lay in Assyria's path from the north `[S]`, and promises that the first region humbled will be the first to see the light. The English versions decide between "made heavy" and "made glorious"; the Hebrew holds both senses. Matthew 4:15–16 reads the verse of Galilee. Its Greek is closer to the Hebrew perfect ("have seen") than to the Septuagint's imperative ("see!").

7. **The child of 9:6–7 is set on David's throne in the words of 2 Samuel 7, and the promise is sealed with a phrase the book uses once more.** `[T]` The combination of כִּסֵּא ("throne"), מַמְלָכָה ("kingdom") and כּוּן ("establish") occurs in only three verses of the Hebrew Bible: **2 Sam 7:13, 7:16 and Isa 9:7** (WLC). The closing line, "the zeal of the LORD of hosts will do this" (קִנְאַת יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת תַּעֲשֶׂה־זֹּאת), occurs in only one other place: **Isa 37:32**, the promise that "a remnant" (שְׁאֵרִית) will go out from Jerusalem when Assyria is at the gate. The child-king of Ahaz's unit is guaranteed by the same sentence that delivers Hezekiah's city.

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book level** (per the overview, which this run does not re-derive): the book confronts Judah with the holiness of her God, exposes every rival trust, and holds out what survives the levelling — the stump's shoot, the Servant, the seed. `[S: overview]`

**Passage level.** The unit answers the question chapter 6 leaves open: *what becomes of a people commissioned to be hardened, and of the house of David at their head?* It answers in three movements, each with a sign:

- **Ahaz at the conduit** (7:1–25). Crisis, promise, refusal, and a sign given anyway; then four "in that day" oracles of Assyrian devastation.
- **The tablet, the second child and the flood** (8:1–22). Maher-shalal-hash-baz; the waters of Shiloah refused; the LORD as sanctuary and stone; the teaching sealed among disciples; darkness.
- **Light and the child** (9:1–7). The reversal of 8:22 and the birth announcement for David's throne. `[T]`

The unit opens with an alliance of *armies* (7:1) and closes with "the zeal of the LORD *of hosts*" (9:7). "The LORD of hosts" (יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת) occurs in the unit three times: 8:13 ("him you shall regard as holy"), 8:18 ("who dwells on Mount Zion") and 9:7 (WLC). **Its purpose is to make the house of David, and the reader, choose which hosts to fear.** `[I]`

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement.** Chapter 6 ends with a commission to a ministry of hardening ("Render the hearts of this people insensitive…", 6:10) and with a holy seed in the stump (6:13). Leningrad closes the vision with a *petuchah*. `[T]` *(Gate e4: the paragraph marker is a feature of one manuscript, read in Leningrad as BHS prints it; `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`.)*

The need this creates is a specific one. **A hardening verdict has been passed on "this people", and a holy seed has been promised. Who will be the first to hear, and what does hardening look like in a man?**

**Necessity answer.** The unit exists here to supply the first case. Three features show it:

- **It names the house the vision threatened.** The king is "Ahaz, son of Jotham, son of Uzziah" (7:1), and Uzziah's death dated the vision (6:1). `[T]` The unit begins in the next generation; `[I]` that the sequence is deliberate.
- **It gives the seed a face.** The "holy seed" of 6:13 is unnamed. Here a child is named, and named again: a remnant (Shear-jashub), a presence (Immanuel), a plunder (Maher-shalal-hash-baz), and finally a king (9:6). `[I]`
- **It enacts chapter 6's verbs.** "This people" (הָעָם הַזֶּה), the phrase of 6:9–10, returns at 8:6, 8:11 and 8:12. The prophet is told *not* to walk in their way (8:11). The teaching is then bound and sealed among the disciples (8:16) — the hearing that 6:9 denies the people is given to a smaller circle. `[T]` for the repeated phrase; `[I]` that the unit is working out 6:9–13.

**Implication.** Preached after chapter 6, the unit shows the hardening verdict and the Immanuel promise issued on the same day, to the same man. The sign of 7:14 is not consolation detached from judgement. It is announced *inside* the crisis Ahaz chose by refusing to ask. `[I]`

#### General context

- **Canonical position.** Isaiah heads the Latter Prophets (Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, the Twelve) and follows Kings in the Tanak order. The book's royal narratives therefore stand as prophetic commentary on events the Former Prophets have already told: here, the Syro-Ephraimite war of 2 Kgs 16:5–9. `[T]` for the order; `[I]` for the effect.
- **Historical setting.** Rezin of Aram-Damascus and Pekah of Israel move against Jerusalem. In 2 Kgs 16:7–9 Ahaz appeals to Tiglath-pileser of Assyria, who takes Damascus. `[T]` for the Kings account. Isaiah's text names Rezin, Pekah, "the son of Tabeel" and "the king of Assyria", and says nothing of the appeal. `[T]` The silence matters: **in Isaiah the reader hears the prophet's offer, not the king's embassy.**
- **The meeting place.** "The end of the conduit of the upper pool, on the highway to the fuller's field" (7:3) is the place where the Rabshakeh later stands to demand Hezekiah's surrender (36:2; 2 Kgs 18:17). The phrase "the upper pool" occurs only in these three places (WLC). `[T]` Two kings of David's house are tested at the same spot by the same empire; one refuses a sign, the other prays (37:14–20). `[I]` that the pairing is meant; high confidence.
- **Neighbouring units.** *Before:* the throne-room vision (ch. 6). *After:* the refrain-poem of 9:8–10:4, whose refrain ("For all this his anger is not turned away, and his hand is stretched out still") stands at 9:12, 17, 21 and 10:4 [Heb 9:11, 16, 20; 10:4]. In the whole Hebrew Bible it occurs only there and at 5:25 (WLC). `[T]` **The refrain is broken off at 5:25 and resumed at 9:12, so 7:1–9:7 stands inside a frame of judgement** (see Tool 3).

| C. "In that day" | 7:18–25 | ס after 7:20; פ after 7:25 | Fly and bee (18–19), razor (20), curds and honey (21–22), briars and thorns (23–25) |
| D. The second child | 8:1–4 | ס after 8:4 | Tablet, witnesses, birth, name and meaning |
| E. The waters | 8:5–10 | ס after 8:8 and 8:10 | Shiloah refused; the River rises; the nations shattered |
| F. The fear of the LORD | 8:11–15 | ס after 8:15 | Do not walk in their way; sanctuary and stone |
| G. The sealed teaching | 8:16–22 | ס after 8:18 | Binding, waiting, signs, mediums, darkness |
| H. Light and the child | 9:1–7 | ס after 9:7 | Zebulun and Naphtali, the light, the child, the throne |

`[T]` for the sections and the speakers. Two features of the frame are worth stating:

- **The narrative voice changes.** Chapter 7 speaks of Isaiah in the third person ("the LORD said to Isaiah", 7:3). Chapter 8 is in the first person ("the LORD said to me", 8:1, 5, 11). `[T]` The unit moves from a story about the prophet to the prophet's own testimony — appropriate to a section that ends with witnesses (8:2) and sealed testimony (8:16, 20).
- **The unit is framed by the refrain.** The refrain of judgement, begun at 5:25, is suspended through chapters 6–9:7 and resumes at 9:12. `[T]` for the distribution; `[I]` that the suspension is a frame. This means the Immanuel unit and the throne-room vision are set *inside* an unfinished sequence of judgements on Israel.

**A possible concentric reading — tentative.** It has been suggested that the unit is concentric around 8:1–10 (two kings at the start and at 8:4; the "son of Remaliah" in 7:1–9 and again at 8:6; the conduit of 7:3 answered by the waters of 8:6–8). The text supports the echoes; it does not mark a centre. *Confidence: low.* `[I]` The sure structure is the three-movement one above.

**Boundary note — the closing seam.** The unit runs to 9:7 [Heb 9:6]. The *setumah* there closes the birth announcement, and the next section opens with a new formula, "The Lord sends a word against Jacob" (9:8 [Heb 9:7]). `[T]`

### 4. Linking Words

| Link | Verse | Force |
|---|---|---|
| יַעַן כִּי ("inasmuch as") | 7:5; 8:6 | The charge that grounds the verdict: Aram's plot (7:5); the people's rejection of Shiloah (8:6) |
| לָכֵן ("therefore") | 7:14; 8:7 | The verdict that follows a refusal (see Headline 4) |
| בְּטֶרֶם ("before") | 7:16; 8:4 | The timer: before the boy knows… the two kings' lands / Damascus and Samaria will be plundered |
| וְהָיָה בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא ("and it shall be in that day") | 7:18, 21, 23 (and בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא at 7:20) | Four images of the Assyrian devastation in one paragraph |
| הִנֵּה ("behold") | 7:14; 8:7; 8:18; 8:22 | The four things the unit tells the reader to look at: the young woman, the River the Lord brings, "I and the children", and "distress and darkness" |
| כִּי… כִּי… כִּי ("for… for… for") | 9:4, 5, 6 [Heb 9:3, 4, 5] | Three grounds for the joy of 9:3: the yoke broken, the boot burned, a child born |

**The two "therefores".** `[T]` At 7:14, "therefore" follows Ahaz's refusal, not a request. He had been invited to choose the sign and declined; the Lord now chooses it. At 8:7 the word draws the punishment from the metaphor: those who refuse the quiet waters receive the River in flood. **Both "therefores" are the consequence of a no.**

**The four "beholds".** `[T]` The word הִנֵּה falls four times in the unit (WLC). Read in sequence, the reader is shown a woman with child, the Lord bringing the River, the prophet with his children, and the darkness. `[I]` The sequence is not decorative. The first "behold" and the third are both about children given as signs; the second and fourth are about judgement. The sign stands beside the judgement each time.

**The triple כִּי of 9:4–6.** `[T]` The birth of the child is the *third* reason for joy, not the first. The reader reaches the child by way of the broken yoke and the burned boot. The child is the last ground of rejoicing and the one that guarantees the others ("of the increase of his government and of peace there will be no end", 9:7).

### 5. Parallels

- **Two signs on one timer.** `[T]` 7:16 and 8:4 share the formula כִּי בְּטֶרֶם יֵדַע הַנַּעַר ("for before the boy knows"), which occurs nowhere else (WLC). In both, the verdict is the fall of Damascus and Samaria. Whether the two boys are one child seen twice, or two, is the unit's chief difficulty (see Difficult Verses). The text sets them in parallel; it does not identify them.
- **Two waters.** `[T]` "The waters of Shiloah that go gently" (8:6) against "the waters of the River, mighty and many" (8:7).
- **Two fears.** `[T]` "Do not fear" (אַל־תִּירָא, 7:4) is restated at 8:12–13: "do not fear what they fear… the LORD of hosts, him you shall regard as holy; let him be your fear (מוֹרַאֲכֶם), let him be your dread (מַעֲרִצְכֶם)." The command moves from *do not fear the kings* to *fear the LORD*.
- **Poetic parallelism in 9:2–7.** `[T]` "Walked in darkness // dwelt in the land of deep darkness"; "seen a great light // light has shone on them"; "a child is born to us // a son is given to us". The last pair is synonymous parallelism, and its two passives (יֻלַּד, "is born"; נִתַּן, "is given") name no human agent. `[T]` for the forms; `[I]` that the giver is the LORD, in line with 7:14 ("the Lord himself will give").

### 6. Narrator's Comment

- **7:1** gives the outcome before the story begins: "but he could not fight against it" (וְלֹא יָכֹל לְהִלָּחֵם עָלֶיהָ). The reader knows the siege fails before Ahaz does. `[T]` BHS records the plural יָכְלוּ in 1QIsaᵃ, the versions and 2 Kgs 16:5 (apparatus). `[S: BHS]`
- **7:2** is the narrator's one description of the court: "his heart and the heart of his people shook as the trees of the forest (עֲצֵי־יַעַר) shake before the wind." `[T]` The forest returns in 10:18–19 and 10:34, where the LORD fells Assyria's forest, and is followed by the shoot from Jesse's stump (11:1). The noun יַעַר ("forest") occurs in Isaiah at 7:2, 9:18 [Heb 9:17], 10:18, 10:19 and 10:34 within these chapters (WLC). `[T]` for the occurrences; `[I]` that the trembling trees of 7:2 are meant to be heard against the felled forest and the new shoot. *Moderate confidence.*
- **7:8b** ("within sixty-five years Ephraim will be shattered, so that it is no longer a people") stands in the oracle as a parenthesis. NASB95 brackets it. The text does not say whether it is part of the oracle or a later note. `[T]` that it is parenthetical in sense; its origin is not decidable from the text.
- **8:11** frames the second half of chapter 8 in the prophet's own voice: "the LORD spoke to me with strength of hand (כְּחֶזְקַת הַיָּד), and instructed me not to walk in the way of this people." `[T]` The prophet reports his own formation.
- **8:19–22** shifts from instruction to the disciples to a picture of a hunted, hungry wanderer. `[T]` The darkness is described from inside the experience of the people who refused the teaching.

### 7. Vocabulary

**7a. עַלְמָה — "young woman" (7:14).** `[T]` The word occurs seven times in the WLC: Gen 24:43; Exod 2:8; Isa 7:14; Ps 68:25 [Heb 68:26]; Prov 30:19; Song 1:3; 6:8. In Gen 24:43 it is used of the girl at the well whom the narrator has already called a בְּתוּלָה ("virgin", 24:16). In Exod 2:8 it names Moses' sister. Elsewhere it marks a stage of life. The word denotes a young woman of marriageable age. It is not the Hebrew word that by itself asserts virginity; that is בְּתוּלָה. `[T]` for the occurrences. **The lexica agree.** BDB gives "young woman (ripe sexually; maid or newly married)" (BDB 761). HALOT has "marriageable girl" for Gen 24:43, Exod 2:8 and Ps 68:26, and for Isa 7:14 "young woman (until the birth of her first child)". It reserves the sense "without sexual experience" for בְּתוּלָה (HALOT 836, 167). `[S: BDB; HALOT]` `[revised: logos-research]` HALOT's gloss for 7:14 marks a stage of life that ends with a first birth, not a statement about virginity. Two grammatical features matter more than the noun:

- **The article.** הָעַלְמָה is "*the* young woman": someone definite is in view, whether present at the conduit or known to the hearers. `[T]`
- **The participle.** הָרָה ("pregnant") is an adjective-participle with no verb "to be", followed by the participle וְיֹלֶדֶת ("and bearing"). The Hebrew allows "is pregnant and is about to bear" as well as "will conceive and bear". `[T]` that the forms are ambiguous for time; see Tool 8 for what the English versions do.

**7b. אוֹת — "sign" (7:11, 14; 8:18).** `[T]` The word occurs 79 times in 77 verses of the Hebrew Bible (WLC). In this unit it is offered "deep as Sheol or high as heaven" (7:11), given as a child (7:14), and finally applied to the prophet and his children together, "signs and wonders" (8:18). The pair אוֹת + מוֹפֵת is the Exodus–Deuteronomy pairing for the plagues (e.g. Deut 4:34; 6:22; 26:8; 34:11); in Isaiah it occurs only here and at 20:3, where Isaiah walks barefoot "as a sign and a wonder" against Egypt and Cush (WLC). `[T]` The word widens across the unit from a miracle on request to a living family. `[I]`

**7c. לָאָה — "to weary" (7:13).** `[T]` The verb occurs 19 times in 18 verses (WLC); in Isaiah at 1:14, 7:13 (twice), 16:12 and 47:13. At 1:14 the LORD is "weary of bearing" Judah's festivals. At 7:13 the house of David "wearies men" and "wearies my God". `[T]` for the forms; `[I]` for the force: **Ahaz's pious refusal is of a piece with the festivals of chapter 1 — religious form that exhausts God.**

**7d. Shear-jashub, the remnant and the "mighty God" (7:3; 10:21).** `[T]` The name is a clause (subject + verb), and can be heard two ways: "a remnant will return" (hope) or "only a remnant will return" (threat). The syntax allows both, and 10:22 gives both together ("though your people be as the sand of the sea, a remnant of them will return"). 10:21 completes the sentence: "a remnant will return, the remnant of Jacob, to the mighty God (אֶל־אֵל גִּבּוֹר)". `[T]` אֵל גִּבּוֹר is the second throne-name of the child in 9:6. **The return the first child's name promises is a return to the God whose title the last child bears.** `[I]` *High confidence for the verbal link; moderate for its intended force.* (LXX 10:21: ἐπὶ θεὸν ἰσχύοντα, "to the mighty God".)

**7e. Maher-shalal-hash-baz (8:1, 3).** `[T]` Four words: two near-synonymous pairs ("hasten–spoil", "hurry–plunder"). The name is first *written* on a large tablet (גִּלָּיוֹן, a word found elsewhere only at Isa 3:23, WLC), then *witnessed* by two named men — "faithful witnesses" (עֵדִים נֶאֱמָנִים, 8:2, the root אמן again) — and only then given to the child at birth (8:3). `[T]` **The name is set down as evidence before the event**, so that when Damascus and Samaria fall the record will show the word preceded the deed. `[I]` The faithful witnesses of 8:2 stand against the faithless king of 7:9, in the same root. `[T]` for the root; `[I]` for the contrast.

**7f. שִׁלֹחַ — "Shiloah" (8:6).** `[T]` The name of the gently flowing waters. Its consonants are those of שָׁלַח ("send"); a hearer would hear "the sent [waters]". `[I]` (The Greek spellings Σιλωαμ in some manuscripts and Σιλωα in the Three show the later name, `[S: Swete apparatus]`.)

**7g. 8:14 — sanctuary, stone, rock, trap, snare.** `[T]` The one LORD becomes "a sanctuary (מִקְדָּשׁ), and a stone to strike against (אֶבֶן נֶגֶף), and a rock to stumble over (צוּר מִכְשׁוֹל) to both the houses of Israel, a trap (פַּח) and a snare (מוֹקֵשׁ) to the inhabitants of Jerusalem." The word מוֹקֵשׁ occurs 27 times in the Hebrew Bible and only here in Isaiah (WLC). In the Torah it is the word for what the Canaanites' gods become to Israel (Exod 23:33; 34:12; Deut 7:16). `[T]` **The same LORD is refuge to those who fear him and snare to those who do not**; the vocabulary of idolatry is turned on those who make Assyria their trust. `[I]`

**7h. קֶשֶׁר — "conspiracy" (8:12).** `[T]` The people call everything "conspiracy". The command is not to call it so, and וְאֶת־מוֹרָאוֹ לֹא־תִירְאוּ — "do not fear its fear". The suffix can mean what *the people* fear or the fear *the conspiracy* inspires. `[T]` that it is ambiguous; NASB95 and ESV both take it as "what they fear". The verb *conspire* (קָשַׁר) is the word for a plot against a king; `[I]` the panic of 7:2 has here become a slogan.

**7i. Yoke, staff, shoulder (9:4, 6 [Heb 9:3, 5]).** `[T]` שֶׁכֶם ("shoulder") occurs in the unit at 9:4 and 9:6, and elsewhere in Isaiah at 10:27, 14:25 and 22:22 (WLC). At 9:4 the oppressor's rod lies on the shoulder; at 9:6 the government lies on the child's shoulder. At 10:27 and 14:25 the Assyrian's burden is again removed "from your shoulder"; at 22:22 the key of the house of David is laid "on his shoulder". `[T]` for the five occurrences. **The shoulder that bore the oppressor's yoke is the shoulder on which the child's government rests.** `[I]`

The "burden" of 9:4 (סֻבֳּלוֹ, from the root סבל) begins a second chain. In Isaiah the root occurs in seven verses (eight occurrences, WLC). As a noun ("burden") it stands at 9:4, 10:27 and 14:25, for the burden the LORD *removes*. As a verb ("carry") it stands at 46:4 (twice) and 46:7, the load the LORD *carries* where idols must be carried, and at 53:4 and 53:11, the load the Servant *carries*. `[T]` **The burden lifted from the shoulder here is, by the end of the book, carried by the Servant.** `[I]` *(The sweep counted nine occurrences and put the root at 46:1 and 46:3. The WLC does not support either: see Book-Overview Tensions.)*

**7j. The throne-names (9:6 [Heb 9:5]).** `[T]` פֶּלֶא יוֹעֵץ אֵל גִּבּוֹר אֲבִי־עַד שַׂר־שָׁלוֹם. Three observations from the corpus:

- **"Wonder" and "counsel" belong to the LORD in Isaiah.** The noun פֶּלֶא ("wonder") occurs in Isaiah at 9:6, 25:1 and 29:14. At 25:1 the LORD has "done a wonder, counsels from of old" (עָשִׂיתָ פֶּלֶא עֵצוֹת); at 28:29 the LORD of hosts "makes counsel wonderful" (הִפְלִיא עֵצָה) (WLC). `[T]` Outside this verse, Isaiah uses the pair only of the LORD.
- **The child's counsel answers every other counsel in the unit.** The root יעץ ("to counsel, plan") stands at 7:5 (Aram "has planned evil against you") and at 9:6; the noun עֵצָה at 8:10 ("devise a plan, but it will be thwarted"). `[T]` **The kings plan, the nations plan, and their plans fail; the child *is* the counsel that stands.** `[I]`
- **"Mighty God" is the title of 10:21** (see 7d). `[T]`

The division of the names (four pairs, or a sentence) is discussed under Tool 8.

**7k. The Ketiv forms of 9:3 and 9:7 [Heb 9:2, 6].** `[T]` Two unpointed words in the WLC mark Ketiv readings — the written consonants that Leningrad's margin tells the reader to replace:

- **9:3 [Heb 9:2]:** the Ketiv is לא ("not": "you have multiplied the nation, you have *not* increased the joy"); the Qere is לוֹ ("for it": "you have increased *its* joy"). The English versions follow the Qere. BHS goes further and proposes reading הַגִּילָה ("the rejoicing") in place of הַגּוֹי לֹא (apparatus). `[S: BHS]`
- **9:7 [Heb 9:6]:** the Ketiv is לםרבה, with a final-form mem in the middle of the word; the Qere is לְמַרְבֵּה ("for the increase"). BHS notes that many manuscripts read as the Qere and that 1QIsaᵃ has למ רבה, two words (apparatus). `[S: BHS]`

*Gate e4: both are apparatus features, read in Leningrad; tagged `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`.* Neither affects the sense the congregation hears. The second is often mentioned as a curiosity; it is not a finding.

### 8. Translations

**Study text (NASB95) against the pulpit text (ESV).** Each divergence is classified: **(1)** translation loss — the English cannot carry the Hebrew; **(2)** a textual question — the versions follow different readings; **(3)** the New Testament's own Greek. Each entry ends with what an ESV congregation will hear.

- **7:1.** NASB95: "but could not conquer it". ESV: "but could not yet mount an attack against it". Hebrew: וְלֹא יָכֹל לְהִלָּחֵם עָלֶיהָ ("was not able to fight against it"). *Class 1.* **The congregation hears:** That the attack never got started. The NASB95 implies a siege that failed. The Hebrew is closer to the ESV.
- **7:2.** NASB95: "The Arameans have camped in Ephraim". ESV: "Syria is in league with Ephraim". Hebrew: נָחָה אֲרָם עַל־אֶפְרָיִם ("Aram has settled upon Ephraim"). *Class 1.* **The congregation hears:** An alliance, not an encampment. The verb means "rest, settle"; both are interpretations.
- **7:9.** NASB95: "If you will not believe, you surely shall not last". ESV: "If you are not firm in faith, you will not be firm at all". Hebrew: אִם לֹא תַאֲמִינוּ כִּי לֹא תֵאָמֵנוּ. *Class 1.* **The congregation hears:** The repeated "firm" — the ESV is the better pulpit text here.
- **7:11.** NASB95: "make it deep as Sheol". ESV: "let it be deep as Sheol". Hebrew: הַעְמֵק שְׁאָלָה. *Class 2.* **The congregation hears:** "Sheol". The Masoretic pointing reads שְׁאָלָה as "ask!" ("make the asking deep"); BHS proposes the vowels שְׁאֹלָה, "to Sheol", with Aquila, Symmachus and Theodotion (εἰς ᾅδην) and the Vulgate (apparatus). Both English versions follow the proposal. `[S: BHS]`
- **7:14.** NASB95: "Behold, **a** virgin will be with child". ESV: "Behold, **the** virgin shall conceive". Hebrew: הִנֵּה הָעַלְמָה הָרָה. *Class 1, 3.* **The congregation hears:** The article ("the"), which the Hebrew has and the NASB95 drops. Both versions use "virgin", following the Greek παρθένος and Matthew.
- **7:14.** NASB95: "she will call His name". ESV: "and shall call his name". Hebrew: וְקָרָאת. *Class 2.* **The congregation hears:** The ESV leaves the subject unstated. The Hebrew form can be "she will call" or "you (f.) will call"; 1QIsaᵃ reads וקרא ("he will call"), and the LXX καλέσεις ("you will call") (BHS apparatus).
- **8:1.** NASB95: "write on it… Swift is the booty, speedy is the prey". ESV: "write on it… 'Belonging to Maher-shalal-hash-baz'". Hebrew: לְמַהֵר שָׁלָל חָשׁ בַּז. *Class 1.* **The congregation hears:** A label of ownership. The ESV reads the preposition לְ as "belonging to"; the NASB95 translates the name as a sentence.
- **8:6.** NASB95: "rejoice in Rezin". ESV: "rejoice over Rezin". Hebrew: וּמְשׂוֹשׂ אֶת־רְצִין. *Class 2.* **The congregation hears:** Neither version adopts BHS's proposal to read the verb as "melt (in fear) before" (apparatus, cf. 10:18).
- **8:13.** NASB95: "whom you should regard as holy". ESV: "him you shall honor as holy". Hebrew: אֹתוֹ תַקְדִּישׁוּ. Essentially the same.
- **8:16, 20.** NASB95: "seal the **law**… To the **law** and to the testimony!". ESV: "seal the **teaching**… To the **teaching** and to the testimony!". Hebrew: תּוֹרָה. *Class 1.* **The congregation hears:** "Teaching". Here תּוֹרָה is the prophet's own sealed instruction, so the ESV is closer to the sense.
- **9:1 [Heb 8:23].** NASB95: "later on He **shall make** it glorious". ESV: "in the latter time he **has made** glorious". Hebrew: הִכְבִּיד (perfect). *Class 1.* **The congregation hears:** A completed act.
- **9:2–3 [Heb 9:1–2].** NASB95: "The people who walk… **Will see** a great light… You **shall** multiply". ESV: "The people who walked… **have seen** a great light… You **have** multiplied". Hebrew: רָאוּ, הִרְבִּיתָ (perfects). *Class 1.* **The congregation hears:** Past tenses. The Hebrew perfects can be read as past, or as the "prophetic perfect" of a future certainty; the NASB95 takes the second, the ESV the first. **The preacher should decide which he means and say so**, because an ESV congregation will hear a deliverance already accomplished.
- **9:3 [Heb 9:2].** NASB95: "You shall increase their gladness". ESV: "you have increased its joy". Hebrew: Ketiv לא / Qere לוֹ. *Class 2.* **The congregation hears:** Both follow the Qere (see 7k). The older English tradition that follows the Ketiv ("and not increased the joy") is `[S]`, not checked here.
- **9:6 [Heb 9:5].** NASB95: "Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Eternal Father, Prince of Peace". ESV: "Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace". Hebrew: פֶּלֶא יוֹעֵץ…. *Class 1.* **The congregation hears:** Four titles. The Hebrew can be read as four pairs; the first pair as "a wonder of a counsellor"; or the whole as one long name. The English tradition's comma after "Wonderful" (in older versions) is `[S]`.

**LXX against the Hebrew.** `[T]` for each wording, from Rahlfs (1979) and Swete; the witnesses are named from Swete's apparatus.

- **7:9.** ἐὰν μὴ πιστεύσητε, οὐδὲ μὴ συνῆτε ("if you do not believe, neither will you understand"). The Greek loses the pun on אמן and replaces the second verb with the verb of 6:9 (οὐ μὴ συνῆτε). Whether the translator read a different Hebrew or interpreted is not decidable here.
- **7:14.** ἰδοὺ ἡ παρθένος ἐν γαστρὶ ἕξει καὶ τέξεται υἱόν, καὶ καλέσεις τὸ ὄνομα αὐτοῦ Εμμανουηλ (Rahlfs). For הָעַלְמָה the Greek has **ἡ παρθένος** ("the virgin"). Aquila, Symmachus and Theodotion read **ἡ νεᾶνις** ("the young woman") (BHS apparatus). The Greek tradition varies on two further words, according to Swete's apparatus:
  - Vaticanus has ἐν γαστρὶ **λήμψεται** ("will conceive"), where Sinaiticus, Alexandrinus and Marchalianus have **ἕξει** ("will have [in the womb]").
  - The verb "call" varies: καλέσεις in the main text, καλέσει in Sinaiticus, and **καλέσουσιν** ("they will call") in one later witness.

  The choice of παρθένος is a lexical narrowing made by Jewish translators before the New Testament. It is a fact about how the Greek read the Hebrew, not an error in either direction. *High confidence.*
- **8:14.** The Greek **negates** the Hebrew: "and if you trust in him, he will be to you for a sanctuary, and you will **not** meet him as a stumbling stone (καὶ οὐχ ὡς λίθου προσκόμματι)". Swete's apparatus records that Aquila and Theodotion restore the positive, "and as a stone of stumbling" (εἰς λίθον προσκόμματος). `[T]` This matters for Tool 11. The New Testament's λίθον προσκόμματος καὶ πέτραν σκανδάλου (Rom 9:33; 1 Pet 2:8) agrees with the Hebrew and with the later Greek revisers, not with the Old Greek.
- **9:1 [Heb 8:23].** The Greek has an imperative, "do this first, do it quickly (ταχὺ ποίει), land of Zebulun, land of Naphtali…", and closes with **Γαλιλαία τῶν ἐθνῶν** ("Galilee of the nations"). The Hebrew גְּלִיל הַגּוֹיִם occurs only here (WLC).
- **9:2 [Heb 9:1].** ὁ λαὸς ὁ πορευόμενος ἐν σκότει, **ἴδετε** φῶς μέγα ("people walking in darkness, **see** a great light"): an imperative where the Hebrew has a perfect.
- **9:6 [Heb and LXX 9:5].** In Rahlfs and in Swete's main text (Vaticanus) the four throne-names are absent. The child's name is **Μεγάλης βουλῆς ἄγγελος** ("messenger of great counsel"), followed by "for I will bring peace upon the rulers, peace and health to him". Swete's apparatus shows that the four names *are* present in Greek:
  - Aquila: θαυμαστὸς σύμβουλος, ἰσχυρὸς δυνατός…
  - Symmachus and Theodotion have equivalents.
  - Alexandrinus and a corrector of Sinaiticus add after ἄγγελος "wonderful counsellor, mighty God, ruler, prince of peace, father of the age to come" (θαυμαστὸς σύμβουλος, θεὸς ἰσχυρός, ἐξουσιαστής, ἄρχων εἰρήνης, πατὴρ τοῦ μέλλοντος αἰῶνος).

  `[T]` for the readings as recorded. **The Old Greek's short form falls on the passage's most-quoted verse, and the longer Greek form is a later supplement towards the Hebrew.** The Old Greek still keeps "counsel" (βουλή), the theme Tool 7j traced in the Hebrew. Why the Old Greek has the short form is a question the corpus cannot answer. The library pass found no Septuagint-specialist treatment of it. The one explanation it returned is Margaret Barker's: the translator read the four names as titles of a single angelic figure, possibly under the influence of the Enoch tradition (*Eerdmans Commentary on the Bible*, 2003, 508). `[S: Barker]` `[revised: logos-research]` This is one scholar's proposal, and it is not a consensus. The question stays open (see Open Questions).

### 9. Tone and Feel

The unit moves through five registers, often without transition:

- **Political thriller** (7:1–2): two armies, a report, a trembling court.
- **Pastoral urgency** (7:4): "Take care and be calm, have no fear, do not let your heart be faint", four imperatives to one frightened man.
- **Exasperation** (7:13): "Is it too little for you to weary men, that you weary my God also?" The pronoun shifts from "your God" (7:11) to "my God" (7:13). `[T]` **The prophet no longer calls the LORD the king's God.** `[I]`
- **Menace in the domestic** (7:15–25; 8:6–8): curds and honey, a heifer and two sheep, briars where vineyards stood, a razor hired from beyond the River, a flood up to the neck.
- **Hymnic blaze** (9:2–7), after the bleakest verse of the unit (8:22, "distress and darkness, the gloom of anguish… driven into thick darkness").

**The tonal swing at 8:22–9:1 is the sharpest in the first part of the book.** `[T]` The turn is marked by a single word, כִּי לֹא מוּעָף ("for there will be no gloom", 9:1), which BDB takes as the same word as מְעוּף צוּקָה ("gloom of anguish") in 8:22. BDB glosses מוּעָף "gloom" and gives the 8:22 form as "id.", and Gesenius (GHCLOT) has "darkness" for both. **HALOT dissents.** It glosses מוּעָף in 8:23 as "gleam, lustre", which would make the line "there is no gleam for her who was in anguish", a continuation of the darkness rather than its end. `[S: BDB; GHCLOT; HALOT]` `[revised: logos-research]` The turn to light at 9:2 does not depend on this word, so the tonal swing stands either way. **The verbal link between 8:22 and 9:1 is moderate at best, and the lexica are divided.**

### 11. Quotation/Allusion

#### Old Testament sources within this passage

**Deut 6:16 → Isa 7:12** *(high)*

- **Move 1 — Source context.** "You shall not test (לֹא תְנַסּוּ) the LORD your God as you tested him at Massah" (Deut 6:16) forbids demanding proof from God in unbelief. Exod 17:2, 7 is the Massah story: the people demand water and ask, "Is the LORD among us or not?" (הֲיֵשׁ יְהוָה בְּקִרְבֵּנוּ). `[T]`
- **Move 2 — Book usage.** נָסָה occurs in Isaiah only at 7:12 (WLC). The one appearance of the testing vocabulary is in the mouth of a king who uses it to refuse a sign. `[T]`
- **Move 3 — OT-to-OT.** The irony is sharpened by the Massah question. Israel at Massah asked *whether the LORD was among them*; Ahaz declines a sign, and the sign he is given is **"God with us"**. `[T]` for both phrases; `[I]` that Isaiah's sign answers the Massah question. *Moderate confidence for the Massah link; high for the Deuteronomy echo.*
- **What it adds:** Ahaz's refusal is not humility. It is Scripture used to avoid God's command.

**2 Sam 7:12–16 → Isa 9:7 (and 7:2, 13)** *(high)*

- **Move 1 — Source context.** The LORD promises David a son: "I will establish the throne of his kingdom for ever" (וְכֹנַנְתִּי אֶת־כִּסֵּא מַמְלַכְתּוֹ עַד־עוֹלָם, 2 Sam 7:13); "your house and your kingdom shall be made sure (וְנֶאְמַן) before me for ever; your throne shall be established for ever" (7:16). `[T]`
- **Move 2 — Book usage.** "The house of David" is addressed at 7:2 and 7:13; "the throne of David" at 9:7; "the key of the house of David" at 22:22; and "the sure mercies of David" (חַסְדֵי דָוִד הַנֶּאֱמָנִים) at 55:3, where the adjective is again the niphal of אמן (WLC). `[T]`
- **Move 3 — OT-to-OT.** כִּסֵּא + מַמְלָכָה + כּוּן occur together only at 2 Sam 7:13, 7:16 and Isa 9:7 (WLC). `[T]` And 2 Sam 7:16's verb for "made sure" is **נֶאְמַן — the niphal of אמן, the very form of Isa 7:9b (תֵאָמֵנוּ)**. `[T]` for both forms. **"If you will not believe, you will not be made sure" is the Davidic promise of 2 Sam 7:16 turned into a condition for Ahaz.** `[I]` *High confidence that the vocabulary is shared; moderate that 7:9 is meant to recall 2 Sam 7:16 specifically.*
- **What it adds:** The house of David is offered the stability of its own covenant if it will trust. It refuses; the covenant is kept anyway, through a child.

**Judg 6–8 (Midian) → Isa 9:4** *(high)*

- **Move 1.** "As on the day of Midian" recalls Gideon's victory in which the LORD reduced the army to three hundred so that Israel could not boast "My own hand has saved me" (Judg 7:2). `[T]`
- **Move 2.** Isaiah repeats the reference at 10:26: "as at the slaughter of Midian at the rock of Oreb" (WLC). `[T]`
- **What it adds:** The oppressor's yoke will be broken by the LORD and not by Judah's alliances, the opposite of what Ahaz sought. `[I]`

**Deut 18:10–14 → Isa 8:19** *(high)*

- **Move 1.** Deut 18:11 forbids one "who asks a medium (אוֹב) or a spiritist (יִדְּעֹנִי), or who inquires of the dead (וְדֹרֵשׁ אֶל־הַמֵּתִים)". Isa 8:19 has the same three elements: "inquire (דִּרְשׁוּ) of the mediums and the spiritists… on behalf of the living, of the dead (אֶל־הַמֵּתִים)?" `[T]` Saul at Endor (1 Sam 28) is the narrative case.
- **What it adds:** Consulting the dead is the rival to "the teaching and the testimony" (8:20). `[I]`

**Exod 22:28 [Heb 22:27] → Isa 8:21** *(moderate)*. "You shall not curse (תְקַלֵּל) God, nor curse a ruler of your people." Isa 8:21 has the hungry "curse (וְקִלֵּל) their king and their God". `[T]` for both verbs; `[I]` for the link. *Moderate: the pairing of God and ruler is the shared element.*

### 12. Genre

Mixed: **prophetic narrative** (7:1–17, in the third person; 8:1–4, in the first person), **prophetic oracle** (7:18–25; 8:5–15), **prophetic testimony** (8:16–22) and a **royal birth oracle in hymnic form** (9:1–7). `[T]` The genre markers are the narrative *wayyiqtol* chains of 7:1–3, 7:10–13 and 8:1–5, then the oracular formulae ("thus says the Lord GOD", 7:7; "in that day", 7:18), then the hymn's address in the second person singular to God (9:3–4, "you have multiplied… you have broken"). `[T]`

**Reading implication:** the hymn of 9:2–7 is *addressed to God*, not to Judah. It is an act of praise for a deliverance spoken of as done. The preacher should not turn it into exhortation. `[I]`

### 13. Copycat

**Ahaz** is a negative example, explicitly: he is offered help and refuses it in pious words. **Isaiah and his disciples** are a positive example: they "wait for the LORD", bind up the testimony, and are themselves "signs". `[T]` The copycat line runs to the community of 8:16–18, not to the king.

### 14. Bible Timeline

- **Before:** the promise to David (2 Sam 7); the division of the kingdom ("since the day that Ephraim departed from Judah", 7:17; 1 Kgs 12). `[T]`
- **In the passage's own horizon:** the fall of Damascus and of Samaria to Assyria (7:16; 8:4). 2 Kgs 16:9 records the fall of Damascus; 2 Kgs 17:6 the fall of Samaria. `[T]` for the Kings records.
- **In the book's horizon:** Hezekiah's deliverance (chs. 36–37), the exile (39:6–7), the Servant, and the new creation (chs. 65–66).
- **In the canon's horizon:** the birth of Jesus (Matt 1:23), his ministry in Galilee (Matt 4:15–16), and the stone that causes stumbling (Rom 9:33; 1 Pet 2:8).

The passage stands at the point where the Davidic house first refuses the prophet's word in the book — **the first in a line of royal failures that the book resolves in a king who is not Ahaz.** `[I]`

### 15. Who Am I?

The reader is not Isaiah, nor the child. The reader stands **with the house of David, being offered a sign**: told to "take care and be calm" when every visible power says otherwise, and asked whether he will trust a word he cannot see over a treaty he can. `[I]` A second position is offered at 8:16–18: **one of the disciples**, among whom the teaching is sealed and who wait for a God who hides his face. `[T]`

### 16. So What?

- **Worldview.** Faith is a stance, not a feeling. "Stand firm in faith, or you will not stand at all" — security is given to those who trust the promise, not to those who secure themselves. `[T]`
- **Belief.** The LORD keeps the Davidic promise even when the Davidic king refuses it. The sign is given to the unbelieving (7:14), not withheld from them. `[T]`
- **Behaviour (stop).** The piety that declines God's help because accepting it would mean depending on him; the habit of calling everything "conspiracy" and fearing what everyone fears (8:12). `[T]`
- **Behaviour (start).** Regard the LORD of hosts as holy (8:13); go "to the teaching and to the testimony" (8:20); wait for the LORD when he hides his face (8:17). `[T]`
- **Hope.** The darkness of 8:22 is not the last word; the first land humbled is the first to see light (9:1–2). `[T]`

## Extensions

### Original Language Observations

**The birth-announcement formula (7:14).** `[T]` The three words הָרָה וְיֹלֶדֶת בֵּן ("pregnant and bearing a son") occur in only four verses of the Hebrew Bible: **Gen 16:11** (the angel of the LORD to Hagar), **Judg 13:5 and 13:7** (the angel to Samson's mother) and **Isa 7:14** (WLC, skeletal match). Of these, only Gen 16:11 and Isa 7:14 go on to וְקָרָאת שְׁמוֹ ("and you/she will call his name"). In Gen 16:11 the form is plainly the second person feminine ("you will call his name Ishmael"), addressed to the mother. `[T]` Three consequences follow:

- 7:14 uses a **set annunciation formula**, not a description of an ordinary birth. In each of the other three cases the child's birth is a sign of the LORD's intervention in a crisis. `[T]` for the formula; `[I]` for the shared function.
- The form וְקָרָאת matches Gen 16:11, which favours reading it as "**you (the mother) will call**" or "**she will call**". **The mother names the child**, as Hagar names Ishmael. `[I]` *High confidence.*
- In Gen 16:11 the name explains itself: "for the LORD has heard (שָׁמַע)". Isaiah's "God with us" works the same way. The name is a sentence about what God has done. `[T]`

**The participle of 7:14.** `[T]` The phrase הָרָה וְיֹלֶדֶת has no verb "to be". In Gen 16:11 and Judg 13:5 the same words follow הִנָּךְ ("behold, you"). Hagar *is* already pregnant (16:4). Samson's mother is *not yet* pregnant: she is barren (13:3), and the angel adds "you shall conceive" (וְהָרִית). So the formula itself does not decide whether the conception is past or future. The ESV's "shall conceive" and the NASB95's "will be with child" are both possible readings, and neither is required. `[T]`

**7:9 — hiphil and niphal of אמן.** `[T]` תַאֲמִינוּ is the hiphil ("you believe, take a firm stand"); תֵאָמֵנוּ is the niphal ("you will be made firm, established"). The niphal is the verb of 2 Sam 7:16 (וְנֶאְמַן בֵּיתְךָ, "your house shall be made sure") (see Tool 11). Both verbs are plural: the address is to the house of David, not to Ahaz alone. `[T]` **"You" in 7:9 is plural — the whole royal house.**

**7:13–14 — the shift to the plural.** `[T]` "Hear now, O house of David… Is it too little *for you* (מִכֶּם, plural)… the Lord himself will give *you* (לָכֶם, plural) a sign." The sign is addressed to the dynasty, not to the man. `[T]` This keeps the sign's horizon open beyond Ahaz's lifetime. `[I]`

**9:6 — who calls the name?** `[T]` וַיִּקְרָא שְׁמוֹ is active, "and he/one called his name", with no subject expressed. BHS proposes the passive וַיִּקָּרֵא ("and his name was called"), following the versions (apparatus). `[S: BHS]` The English "his name will be called" follows the versions. The Hebrew as it stands leaves the namer unnamed; in context the giver of the child (9:6, נִתַּן, "is given") is God. `[I]`

**9:6 — אֵל גִּבּוֹר.** `[T]` The exact phrase occurs only at Isa 9:6 and 10:21 (WLC). With the article and a further adjective it is the title of the LORD in Deut 10:17 (הָאֵל הַגָּדֹל הַגִּבֹּר, "the great, the mighty God"), and the same phrase stands in Jer 32:18 and Neh 9:32. `[T]` At 10:21, in the next chapter, אֵל גִּבּוֹר is unambiguously the LORD to whom the remnant returns. **Inside Isaiah, the title the child bears is God's.** `[T]` *High confidence.*

### Historical and Cultural Background

- **The Syro-Ephraimite coalition.** Aram-Damascus and Israel move against Judah, by the account of 2 Kgs 16:5, to replace Ahaz with "the son of Tabeel" (Isa 7:6). The common reconstruction is that they meant to force Judah into an anti-Assyrian alliance, around 734–732 BC. `[S]` The text itself gives the plot (7:6) and the outcome (7:1) but not the motive.
- **Ahaz's appeal.** 2 Kgs 16:7–9 says Ahaz sent silver and gold from the temple to Tiglath-pileser with the words "I am your servant and your son." `[T]` for Kings. Isaiah never records the embassy. **It lets the reader see the prophet's offer, and leaves the king's alternative to be inferred from 8:6–8.** `[I]`
- **The conduit of the upper pool (7:3).** Its exact location is debated. `[S]` The text uses it as a stage on which two kings of David's house face the same empire (7:3; 36:2). `[T]`
- **"Sixty-five years" (7:8).** No single date fits easily, and the reckoning is disputed. One proposal links it to the resettlement of Samaria under Esarhaddon (Ezra 4:2) `[S]`. The preacher need not resolve it. The point is the text's own: Ephraim will cease to be a people. `[T]`
- **Curds and honey (7:15, 22).** The phrase חֶמְאָה וּדְבַשׁ occurs only at 7:15 and 7:22 (WLC). In 7:21–22 the text explains it: a man keeps a heifer and two sheep, the vineyards have become briars, and "everyone who is left in the land will eat curds and honey". `[T]` **In context it is the food of a depopulated land, not of luxury.** The child of 7:15 grows up among the survivors of the devastation. `[T]` *(Readings of the phrase as royal or paradisal food are `[S]` and are not supported by 7:21–22.)*
- **Mediums (8:19).** Consultation of the dead was forbidden in Torah (Deut 18:11) but practised (1 Sam 28; 2 Kgs 21:6). `[T]` Words such as "chirp" and "mutter" (הַמְצַפְצְפִים וְהַמַּהְגִּים) describe the sound of necromancy. `[S]`
- **Zebulun and Naphtali (9:1).** These northern territories lay on Assyria's route south and were annexed in the 733–732 BC campaign (2 Kgs 15:29 names Galilee and "all the land of Naphtali"). `[T]` for Kings; dating `[S]`.

### Original Audience Reception

- **The house of David in Ahaz's day** heard a promise conditional on faith (7:9), a sign that accompanied judgement (7:14–17), and a warning that the ally they wanted would become the flood (8:7–8). They would have felt the 7:2 tremor and the relief of a treaty. *Candidate FCF:* the pull to secure ourselves by a deal we can see rather than a promise we cannot. `[I]`
- **The disciples of 8:16** heard that the word was being sealed among them. They were told to wait for a God who was hiding his face (8:17), and that they themselves were "signs". `[T]` They are the book's first explicit audience for its *future* (see 30:8). `[I]`
- **The later readers whom the book addresses from chapter 40** heard 9:1–7 after the exile. The child on David's throne was still to come, because 39:8 left the throne occupied by a complacent king, and no later king in the book fills it. `[I]` *(The overview's Christological section makes the same observation `[S: overview]`.)*

### Biblical-Theological Themes

### Christological Reading

**The trajectory the text itself builds.** `[T]` The unit links three moments:

- a sign-child named "God with us" whose name is first a promise (7:14), then the owner of the land (8:8, "your land, O Immanuel"), then a confession (8:10);
- a child born and given, whose titles in Isaiah belong elsewhere only to the LORD (פֶּלֶא and עֵצָה, 25:1 and 28:29; אֵל גִּבּוֹר, 10:21) (9:6);
- a throne established in the words of 2 Sam 7, without end, by the LORD's own zeal (9:7).

**The double horizon.** `[T]` 7:16 sets a timer within Ahaz's lifetime: "before the boy knows to refuse evil and choose good, the land whose two kings you dread will be forsaken". Whatever the sign is, it must have meaning in the 730s BC. 8:8 and 9:6–7 then push the child's identity beyond any ordinary child: an ordinary child does not own the land, and no king of Judah is called אֵל גִּבּוֹר or given an endless government. **The text itself creates the tension that Matthew resolves**, rather than Matthew importing it. `[I]` *High confidence that the tension is in the text; the resolution is a canonical reading.*

**Matthew's reading.** Matthew 1:23 follows the Greek παρθένος and adds the translation "God with us". `[T]` It treats Jesus as the fullness of the sign: the child in whom the name is literally true. `[I]` The preacher can hold both: the sign spoke to the house of David in Ahaz's day, and it is fulfilled in the son of David born of Mary. *(How Matthew uses fulfilment language elsewhere — e.g. 2:15 with Hos 11:1 — supports a typological, not merely predictive, reading `[S]`.)*

**The stone and the LORD of hosts.** `[T]` 1 Pet 3:15 puts "Christ" where Isa 8:13 has "the LORD of hosts", and Rom 9:33 and 1 Pet 2:8 identify Christ as the stone of 8:14. **The New Testament reads the unit's "LORD of hosts" — the one to be feared, the sanctuary and the stone — as Christ.** `[T]` for the NT forms; `[I]` for the significance.

**Hebrews 2:13.** `[T]` The prophet's words "I will put my trust in him" and "Behold, I and the children God has given me" (Isa 8:17–18, LXX) are given to the Son. The writer places Christ in Isaiah's role: the one who waits for God and stands with the children as a sign.

### Difficult / Contested Verses

**7:14 — who is the child, and who is the young woman?** *Category:* apologetic, doctrinal.

- *The text's own constraints:* `[T]`
  - "the young woman" is definite;
  - the sign is for the house of David (plural);
  - the timer of 7:16 runs within Ahaz's lifetime;
  - the child is linked to the land (8:8) and, by the shared formula and 10:21, to the child of 9:6.
- *Proposals:* `[S]`
  1. a wife of Ahaz, the child being Hezekiah (chronology is disputed);
  2. the prophet's wife, the child being Maher-shalal-hash-baz (but 8:3 gives a different name);
  3. a young woman present at the conduit, unidentified;
  4. a direct and only messianic prediction (hard to square with 7:16);
  5. a sign with an immediate referent that the book itself enlarges into the child of 9:6, fulfilled in Christ.
- *Handling:* Name the double horizon honestly. A sermon that denies the eighth-century referent will not survive 7:16; one that denies the Matthean referent will not survive Matthew 1. The text supports proposal 5 best, because 8:8 and 9:6 are themselves the "enlargement". `[I]`

**7:14 — "virgin".** The Hebrew word does not itself assert virginity; the Greek does. Say that plainly. Matthew's claim about Mary rests on his own narrative (1:18–25), not only on the Hebrew word of Isa 7:14. `[T]` for Matthew's narrative. A preacher can affirm the virgin birth without overclaiming the Hebrew.

**7:8 — sixty-five years.** See Historical Background. Do not build anything on the arithmetic.

**8:14 — the LORD as a trap.** *Category:* ethical, doctrinal. The text says the same LORD is sanctuary and snare. The snare is not arbitrary. It is the consequence of refusing to fear him (8:13) and of trusting what he forbids (8:12). `[T]`

**9:1 — "contempt" and "glory".** Some read the second verb as "he made heavy" — that is, the oppression grew worse — rather than "made glorious". `[S]` HALOT's range for the hiphil of כבד includes both "make heavy" and "honour, cause to be honoured". `[S: HALOT]` Childs (OTL, 2001, 79–80) reads the pair as a contrast in time: "first he brought into contempt… later he made glorious". He takes the two perfects as two *qualities* of time, judgement and redemption, continuing the theme that runs from chapter 7. `[S: Childs]` `[revised: logos-research]` The contrast of "former time" and "latter time", the antonym pair קלל/כבד, and the light of 9:2 all favour "made glorious". `[I]` *High confidence.*

**9:6 — "Mighty God" applied to a human king.** It has been argued that the titles are a royal coronation formula, not a claim to deity. `[S]` Inside Isaiah, אֵל גִּבּוֹר is the LORD's title (10:21); פֶּלֶא with עֵצָה describes only the LORD (25:1; 28:29); and the government is endless (9:7). `[T]` The text does not supply a weaker sense. **The difficulty is not the Hebrew. It is how a child of David can bear God's titles — which is the question the New Testament answers.** `[I]`

**9:6 — "Everlasting Father".** *Category:* doctrinal (a weaponised verse). It is sometimes used to blur the Son and the Father. The phrase אֲבִי־עַד ("father of perpetuity") describes the king's relation to his people, a father for ever — compare 22:21, where Eliakim is "a father to the inhabitants of Jerusalem". `[T]` for 22:21. It is not a statement about the relations within the Godhead. `[I]`

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: Preaching 7:14 as an isolated Christmas proof-text

**Why tempting:** It is the verse Matthew quotes, and the season demands it.
**Why wrong:** The text sets the sign among three named children, on a timer within Ahaz's lifetime (7:16), as the consequence of a refusal (לָכֵן). Lifted out, it loses the very thing that makes it good news: God gives the sign to an unbelieving house, and keeps David's promise when David's son will not trust it.
**Better approach:** Preach 7:1–17 with 8:8–10 and 9:6–7 in view: the sign, the land that belongs to Immanuel, the child on the throne. Then let Matthew 1 show who the child is.

### Pitfall: Overclaiming עַלְמָה

**Why tempting:** To defend the virgin birth.
**Why wrong:** The Hebrew word does not by itself assert virginity; the Greek παρθένος does. An overclaim invites an easy rebuttal and makes the doctrine look as if it hangs on one word.
**Better approach:** Say what the Hebrew says, what the Greek did with it, and that Matthew's account of Mary (1:18–25) is his own testimony, not a deduction from a lexicon.

### Pitfall: Treating Ahaz's refusal as admirable humility

**Why tempting:** "I will not test the LORD" is Deut 6:16, and Jesus quotes it (Matt 4:7, Οὐκ ἐκπειράσεις κύριον τὸν θεόν σου) `[T]`.
**Why wrong:** The LORD has commanded him to ask (7:11). To refuse a command in the words of a commandment is unbelief, and the prophet calls it "wearying my God" (7:13).
**Better approach:** Show how Scripture can be used to avoid God, and contrast Hezekiah at the same pool (36:2; 37:14–20).

### Pitfall: Reading 7:9 as "faith is a feeling of confidence"

**Why tempting:** The English "firm in faith" sounds psychological.
**Why wrong:** The verb is the root of *amen* and of the covenant word נֶאְמַן (2 Sam 7:16). It means a stance on a promise, made by the whole house (plural "you").
**Better approach:** Faith is taking one's stand on what God has said. Ahaz's alternative was not doubt but a treaty.

### Pitfall: Preaching 9:2–7 as exhortation

**Why tempting:** The congregation wants an application.
**Why wrong:** The hymn is addressed to God in the second person ("you have multiplied… you have broken", 9:3–4). It is praise for a deliverance spoken of as accomplished.
**Better approach:** Let the congregation join the praise. The application is the reversal itself: "the people who walked in darkness".

### Pitfall: "Everlasting Father" as a Trinitarian puzzle

**Why tempting:** The title sounds as if the Son is called the Father.
**Why wrong:** אֲבִי־עַד describes a perpetual fatherly rule over a people, as Eliakim is "a father to the inhabitants of Jerusalem" (22:21).
**Better approach:** One sentence of explanation is enough. Do not build a doctrine of the Trinity on it, in either direction.

### Pitfall: Moving the past tenses into the future (or the reverse) without noticing

**Why tempting:** The ESV congregation hears "have seen", the NASB95 reader "will see".
**Why wrong:** The Hebrew perfects allow both. The preacher who does not choose will confuse the hearers between a promise and a report.
**Better approach:** Say once that the prophet speaks of the future as done, because the zeal of the LORD of hosts will do it (9:7).
