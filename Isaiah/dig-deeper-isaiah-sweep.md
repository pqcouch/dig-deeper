## How this sweep was run

**Sweep mode, not 28 solo runs.** Per the skill's Multi-Passage Sweep rule, each pericope receives *proportional* depth: Headline Findings, the Positional Necessity Check, Move 4 (internal echo — required for every passage), and the tools that actually surface something for that unit. N/As are more frequent than in a solo run and are given with reasons. **For any ⭐ HIGH-weight unit, run a full solo dig-deeper before pulpit preparation** — this sweep is series-planning and overview-auditing material, not sermon preparation.

**Passage quotation.** The house sweep format quotes each pericope in full. That is possible for an eleven-unit letter; it is not for a 66-chapter book, where the full text would run to some 37,000 words of Scripture before a word of analysis. Each unit therefore carries an **anchor quotation** — the verses the findings actually turn on — with the full range named. Where a finding depends on exact wording, the wording is quoted. `[T]`

**Text-first order of work.** The Hebrew was swept for the lexical and structural data (roots, distributions, Masoretic *petuhot*/*setumot*) before the overview's threads were re-read, and before any commentary knowledge was brought in. Where a finding was reached independently and the overview agrees, that agreement is noted as earned; where a finding originates in the overview, it is tagged `[S]`. **No commentary, study guide, lecture transcript or prior dig-deeper report on Isaiah was in context at any point in this run.**

## Phase 0.5 — Book-overview active layer

The four active threads extracted from `book-overview-isaiah.md` v0.1.1 and held in peripheral vision throughout. These orient the exegesis; they do not drive it.

### Thread 1 — Presenting situation

A book attributed to Isaiah son of Amoz as one *vision* (1:1), addressing **three audience horizons the book itself lets show**: eighth-century Judah under Assyrian pressure; a people addressed as already in Babylon from 40:1; and a canonical reader with Deuteronomy 32 in the ear. The book explicitly instructs that its words be sealed for later readers (8:16; 30:8), so **the multiple horizons are a textual datum, not a critical reconstruction** `[T]`. The single pressure: the Holy One of Israel has reared children who rebelled (1:2), and he is both the one who must judge them and the only one who can save them.

### Thread 2 — Intertextual map, with **confirmed live sources for Move 2**

This is the operative input for Tool 11 Move 2. Sources the overview establishes as **live** (≥2 uses), meaning further echoes from them carry an elevated prior:

**A standing caution the overview itself flags:** Isaiah uses **no citation formula anywhere** — no "as it is written". Every link is allusion, tested by verbal distinctiveness, not asserted from formula. `[T]`

### Thread 3 — Christological trajectory

Direct prophecy (7:14; 9:6–7; 11:1–10; 52:13–53:12); typology through the *ʿebed* and *ʾāšām* categories; the trajectory in which **the singular Servant's "seed" (53:10) becomes the plural "servants"** from 54:17 onwards; the anointed herald of 61:1–3; and the *contrast* of kings who could not do it (Ahaz 7:12; Hezekiah 39:8).

### Thread 4 — Preaching traps (watch for passage-level manifestations)

The two-books instinct; 53 as a free-standing tract; 6:8 as a missionary appeal; skipping 13–23 and 34; stopping at 66:23; 2:4 as a political slogan.

## Whole-book orientation (worked independently; reconciled at Phase 5.5)

### Author's purpose at book level

The book exists **to make Judah — and every later reader it seals its words for — reckon with the holiness of the God who reared them**, so that every rival trust is exposed as nothing and the only thing left standing is what God himself brings out of the felled stump. `[T]`/`[I]` Every pericope below serves that; the per-unit "Author's purpose" is stated only where the unit's contribution is not obvious.

### The Masoretic section markers as a structural control

An unusual asset for this book: BHS carries the Masoretic *petuḥot* (open, major) and *setumot* (closed, minor) throughout, and they were swept before any structural claim was made. `[T]` The heaviest *petuḥot* fall at **12:6, 27:13, 35:10, 39:8, 44:23, 45:17, 48:16, 50:11, 65:12**. They corroborate the overview's seven movements at 12/13, 27/28, 35/36 and 39/40, and they will be cited per unit where they settle a boundary question. **This is a text-internal control on structure that most Isaiah series never use.**

### Book-level repetition to track (Tool 10, running across all units)

## Pericope 1 — Isaiah 1:1–31 — Children who rebelled: the lawsuit opens ⭐ HIGH

**Anchor (ESV).** ¹ The vision of Isaiah the son of Amoz… ² Hear, O heavens, and give ear, O earth; for the LORD has spoken: "Children have I reared and brought up, but they have rebelled against me…" ⁴ Ah, sinful nation… They have despised the Holy One of Israel… ¹¹ "What to me is the multitude of your sacrifices?"… ¹⁸ "Come now, let us reason together, says the LORD: though your sins are like scarlet, they shall be as white as snow"… ²¹ How the faithful city has become a whore… ²⁹ For they shall be ashamed of the oaks that you desired; and you shall blush for the gardens that you have chosen… ³¹ and both of them shall burn together, with none to quench them.

**Headline findings.**

1. **Isaiah 1:2 quotes Deuteronomy 32:1 with the verbs inverted — an allusion marker, not a loose echo.** `[T]` (Isaiah side) / `[S]` (Deuteronomy wording, not supplied). Deut 32:1 runs *haʾăzînû ha-šāmayim … wə-tišmaʿ hā-ʾāreṣ* (**give ear**, heavens … **hear**, earth); Isa 1:2 runs *šimʿû šāmayim wə-haʾăzînî ʾereṣ* (**hear**, heavens … **give ear**, earth). **The two verbs are swapped.** Inverted citation is a recognised Hebrew signal that a source is being invoked deliberately rather than coincidentally. Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Tool 11 Move 1, Parallels. *High confidence on the inversion; moderate on the convention's force.* Consequence: **chapter 1 is not "a general call to repentance" — it is a formal covenant lawsuit convened under Moses' own witnesses.**

2. **The chapter offers cleansing (v.18) *before* it has executed any judgement — and it is the offer, not the indictment, that the rest of the book has to make good.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Linking Words, Positional Necessity. This is why "1–39 = judgement, 40–66 = comfort" collapses at the first chapter.

3. **קְדוֹשׁ יִשְׂרָאֵל, the book's spine-title, has its first occurrence here (1:4) — and it appears in an accusation, not a doxology.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Repetition (book level). The title is introduced as the thing Judah *despised*; every later comfort is addressed to people who did that.

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* none — the book's opening; it plants rather than resolves. *Necessity:* the striking positional fact is what is **not** here. The call-vision (ch. 6) is not the book's opening; the indictment is. `[T]` The reader therefore meets the charge sheet before he meets the prophet's commission — so that when 6:5 says "I am a man of unclean lips **and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips**", the reader has already been shown exactly which people, in what condition. *Implication:* ch. 1 is deliberately staged as the evidence that makes ch. 6's hardening-commission intelligible rather than arbitrary. `[I]`

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** Five movements, and the Masoretic markers corroborate: superscription (v.1); the lawsuit and the sick body (vv.2–9, *setumah* at v.9); the cult rejected and cleansing offered (vv.10–20, *setumot* at vv.17 and 20); the city's dross and refining (vv.21–26, *petuḥah* at v.23 — the heaviest break inside the chapter, splitting the lament over the city from the LORD's "Therefore"); the two destinies (vv.27–31, *setumah* at v.31). `[T]`
- **Linking words.** v.24 **לָכֵן** *"Therefore"* is the hinge of the chapter — and what it introduces is not annihilation but **smelting** (v.25 "I will smelt away your dross as with lye"). The judgement is metallurgical, aimed at restoration (v.26 "*Afterward* you shall be called the city of righteousness"). Preachers routinely take v.24's "Ah, I will get relief from my enemies" as the chapter's terminus; the connector says it is the means to v.26. `[T]`
- **Vocabulary / Hebrew.** **פָּשַׁע** *pāšaʿ* (v.2) — not moral lapse but **treaty-breach, rebellion by a vassal**; its participle closes the book at 66:24 `[T, high]`. **הוֹי** (v.4) — the funeral-lament particle used as prophetic indictment; first of 22 `[T]`. **אֵילִים** (v.29) "oaks/terebinths" and **גַּנּוֹת** "gardens" — the vocabulary of the fertility cult, and both recur decisively (see Move 4) `[T, high]`. **סִיגִים** *sîgîm* "dross" (v.22, 25) — the refining metaphor, which is *not* the same as the burning of v.31: two different fire-images, one purgative and one terminal, and the chapter holds them apart `[T]`.
- **Parallels.** v.3 is emblematic parallelism with a sting: ox and donkey *know* their owner; "Israel does not know". The animals are the standard of comparison, and Israel falls below it. `[T]`
- **Tone.** Prosecution, then astonishing offer, then lament ("How the faithful city…", v.21 — the *ʾêkâ* of Lamentations), then verdict. **Four registers in thirty-one verses.** Do not preach it in one key.
- **Translations / ancient versions.** **Translation-tradition split, category 1 and 2 both present.** LXX 1:1 renders עַל as **κατά** — *against* Judea, resolving an ambiguity the Hebrew leaves open (interpretive, not textual). More strikingly, LXX 1:2 has **Ἄκουε, οὐρανέ** — *singular* "hear, O heaven" — against MT's plural שִׁמְעוּ שָׁמַיִם, which weakens the Deut 32 double-witness structure in Greek. Category: **1 (translation/interpretive loss)**, not a variant. *Moderate confidence.* `[T]`
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Deut 32:1 → 1:2** *(high on the verbal inversion)* — *Move 1:* the Song of Moses summons heaven and earth as the covenant's legal witnesses and proceeds to indict a people who "dealt corruptly… children" (Deut 32:5) — the same charge, the same relational term. *Move 2:* first use of Deut 32 in the book; it establishes the register the *ṣûr* "Rock" motif will keep alive (8:14; 26:4; 30:29; 44:8; 51:1). `[T]` *Move 3:* Deut 32 is itself already reworking Genesis (creation, the nations' allotment), so invoking it activates a chain, not a verse. `[I]` *What it adds:* the reader is told, in the first line of poetry, what kind of document this is.
  - **Gen 19 (Sodom) → 1:9–10** *(high)* — *Move 1:* Sodom is the canonical limit case of a city God does not spare. *What it adds:* v.9 says Judah escaped Sodom's fate only by a remnant; v.10 then addresses her *as* Sodom. **The compliment and the insult are one sentence apart.** `[T]`
  - **Move 4 — internal echo.** Exceptionally dense; this chapter is the book's densest planting ground. `Internal: planted —` **פָּשְׁעוּ בִי** (v.2) → 43:27 → **66:24** (only three occurrences); **וְאֵין מְכַבֶּה** (v.31) → **66:24** *"their fire shall not be quenched"*; the **oaks** (v.29) → 57:5 → **61:3** *"oaks of righteousness"*; the **gardens** (v.29) → 65:3; 66:17; **בְּחַרְתֶּם** *"you have chosen"* (v.29) → 65:12; 66:3–4; **אֶנָּחֵם** (v.24) → 57:6 (only two occurrences of this form); the **eat/be-eaten** alternative (vv.19–20) → 65:13; the **faithful city** renamed (v.26) → 62:12; **survivors** (v.9) → the stump of 6:13 and the remnant of 10:20–22; the summons to **hear the word of the LORD** (v.10) → 66:2, 5 *"trembles at my word"*. *High confidence on all of these; each is a verbal, not thematic, link.* `[T]`
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Prophetic covenant lawsuit in poetry. Pre-exilic Judah under the Assyrian shadow; the reader is this side of the cross, where the scarlet-to-snow offer of v.18 has been made good at cost. **Who am I?** — not the prophet; **the defendant**. Copycat **N/A** — no narrative characters.
- **So what?** Worldview: religious activity can be simultaneously commanded by God and hated by God (vv.11–15) when it is severed from justice (v.17). Behaviour — stop: the assumption that attendance and offering constitute standing. Start: v.17's five imperatives, every one of them about someone else's welfare. Motivation: v.18, which precedes the imperatives in force even where it follows them in the paragraph.
- **Original audience / FCF.** *They felt:* the confusion of a people whose worship was diligent and whose worship was rejected. *Candidate FCF:* **the fear that our religion may not be reaching God at all** — answered not by more religion but by the cleansing of v.18. *Shared/differs:* shared; they had no cross to point to, and we do. `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** vv.11–15 — God's rejection of the sacrificial system he himself instituted. *Rhetorical function:* this sits inside an indictment, before an offer; it is **prosecutorial hyperbole in service of v.17**, not a repeal of Leviticus. Handle by naming the function; a flat "God hates ritual" reading is both wrong and pastorally corrosive.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Pericope 2 — Isaiah 2:1–4:6 — The mountain, the levelling, and the branch

**Anchor (ESV).** 2:¹ The word that Isaiah the son of Amoz saw concerning Judah and Jerusalem. ² It shall come to pass in the latter days that the mountain of the house of the LORD shall be established as the highest of the mountains… ⁴ and they shall beat their swords into plowshares… ¹² For the LORD of hosts has a day against all that is proud and lofty, against all that is lifted up—and it shall be brought low… 4:² In that day the branch of the LORD shall be beautiful and glorious…

**Headline findings.**

1. **The unit is built as a deliberate collision: the highest mountain (2:2) against everything else that is high being brought low (2:12–17).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Repetition (רָם / נִשָּׂא / גָּבֹהַּ saturate 2:11–17), Linking Words. Zion is exalted **by** the levelling, not despite it — and this establishes the book's governing pulse at its second unit.

2. **2:1 carries a second superscription — the only other one before 13:1 — which marks 2:1–4:6 as a bounded sub-vision, not a continuation.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Context. Preachers who run 1:1–2:5 together are crossing a seam the text itself marks.

3. **"In that day" (בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא) fires six times in 2:11–4:2 alone** `[T]` — the densest cluster of the book's 45. Surfaced by: Repetition. It is the unit's structuring refrain, and it turns at 4:2 from threat to promise without changing the formula.

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* ch. 1's lawsuit has established rebellion, rejected worship, and a promised refining. *Necessity:* the lawsuit needs a verdict with a horizon. 2:2–4 supplies the *destination* (nations streaming to Zion for Torah), 2:6–4:1 supplies the *demolition* required to get there, and 4:2–6 supplies the *remnant* who arrive. The unit exists here because ch. 1's smelting metaphor (1:25) demands to be shown at scale. `[I]` *Implication:* 2:2–4 must not be preached as a free-standing peace vision — the text immediately follows it with what God does to human height.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** Four movements: the mountain and the nations (2:2–5); the day against all that is high (2:6–22, *petuḥot* at 2:4 and 2:22 bracketing it precisely); the stripping of Jerusalem's leadership and its women (3:1–4:1, *setumot* at 3:12, 15, 17; 4:1); the branch and the canopy (4:2–6, *petuḥah* at 4:6 closing the unit). `[T]` The Masoretic *petuḥah* at 2:4 is significant: **the Masoretes closed the section after "neither shall they learn war anymore"**, making 2:5 the opening of the next paragraph rather than the tail of the vision.
- **Repetition.** The levelling vocabulary is relentless: *gaʾăwâ*, *rām*, *niśśāʾ*, *gābōah*, *šāp̄ēl* — applied to cedars, oaks, mountains, hills, towers, walls, ships (2:13–16). Seven categories, each "lofty and lifted up". Then the refrain twice, verbatim: **"and the LORD alone will be exalted in that day"** (2:11, 17). `[T]`
- **Vocabulary.** **צֶמַח יְהוָה** (4:2) "the branch of the LORD" — the first of the book's botanical messianic terms, before the *ḥōṭer* and *nēṣer* of 11:1 `[T]`. Note it is paired with "the **fruit of the land**", so the referent may be agricultural abundance rather than a person; the book will resolve the ambiguity at 11:1. *Moderate confidence on a messianic reading here — flag it, don't assert it.*
- **Tone.** Vision, then terror (2:10, 19, 21 — three near-identical flights into rock and dust), then satire (3:18–23's inventory of finery, twenty-one items listed to be swept away), then tenderness (4:5–6).
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Micah 4:1–3 ‖ Isaiah 2:2–4** — a near-verbatim doublet. **Direction of dependence is not determinable from the text, and the Micah wording was not supplied to this run.** `[S]`, flagged to Open Questions. Do not preach a direction.
  - **Exod 13:21–22 → 4:5** *(high)* — *Move 1:* the cloud by day and fire by night are the wilderness-guidance signs of the exodus. *Move 2:* **first exodus allusion in the book**; it establishes Exodus as live, which the sweep will find running to 63:11–14. `[T]` *What it adds:* the restored Zion is described as a *wilderness camp* — God's presence over it, and a **סֻכָּה** (booth, 4:6) for shade. That last word answers 1:8, where the daughter of Zion was left "like a **booth** in a vineyard" — a shelter of abandonment becomes a shelter of protection. **This is a verified internal echo the overview did not have.** `[T, high]`
  - **Move 4 — internal echo.** `Internal: answers §1:8` — the **סֻכָּה** reversal above. `Internal: planted —` 2:12–17's רָם וְנִשָּׂא → 6:1; 52:13; 57:15 (the book's pulse-phrase, planted here in its negative form). `Internal: planted —` 3:14 "you have devoured **the vineyard**" → 5:1–7; 27:2–6.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Prophetic poetry with an embedded oracle of salvation. Copycat **N/A** — no narrative characters. **Who am I?** — among "all that is lifted up".
- **So what?** Worldview: every human height is scheduled for demolition, including the respectable ones (towers, walls, ships — infrastructure, security, trade). Behaviour — stop: 2:22, "Stop regarding man, in whose nostrils is breath."
- **Original audience / FCF.** *They felt:* the pull of alliance and prosperity (2:6–8: silver, horses, idols — the three things Deut 17:16–17 forbids a king). *Candidate FCF:* **the instinct to secure ourselves by what we can see and count.** `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** 3:16–4:1 — the humiliation of the daughters of Zion, including "the LORD will lay bare their secret parts" (3:17). *Category:* ethical/pastoral. Handle with care in a mixed congregation; note that the passage's target is the *ruling class* generally (3:1–15 is addressed to elders and princes) and that ch. 4 ends with the same daughters cleansed (4:4). Do not preach 3:16–24 as a passage about women's dress.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Pericope 3 — Isaiah 5:1–30 — The vineyard that failed, and six woes ⭐ HIGH

**Anchor (ESV).** ¹ Let me sing for my beloved my love song concerning his vineyard… ⁴ What more was there to do for my vineyard, that I have not done in it? ⁷ For the vineyard of the LORD of hosts is the house of Israel… and he looked for justice, but behold, bloodshed; for righteousness, but behold, an outcry! ⁸ Woe to those who join house to house… ²⁰ Woe to those who call evil good and good evil…

**Headline findings.**

1. **The song is a legal trap: the hearers are invited to judge (v.3) before they learn they are the defendant (v.7).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Tone, Author's Purpose. The rhetorical form *is* the theology — self-condemnation elicited before identification.

2. **v.7 turns on an untranslatable double pun that the ESV cannot carry.** `[T, high]` God looked for **מִשְׁפָּט** *mišpāṭ* (justice) but found **מִשְׂפָּח** *miśpāḥ* (bloodshed); for **צְדָקָה** *ṣədāqâ* (righteousness) but found **צְעָקָה** *ṣəʿāqâ* (an outcry). Each pair differs by a single consonant. Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Original Languages, Translations. **Category 1 — translation loss, not a variant.** This is the single most quotable Hebrew observation in the first movement.

3. **The six woes (5:8, 11, 18, 20, 21, 22) are the first instalment of a series the book completes at 28–33 — and the refrain "his hand is stretched out still" ties 5:25 to 9:12, 17, 21 and 10:4.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Repetition, Structure, Move 4. **The unit is structurally incomplete on purpose.**

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 2:1–4:6 has promised a purged Zion and a branch. *Necessity:* the promise raises the question the vineyard song answers — *what exactly went wrong with a people God had every reason to expect fruit from?* v.4's "What more was there to do?" is the book's most direct statement that the failure is not God's. The song exists here, immediately before the throne-vision, so that ch. 6's commission to harden falls on a people already shown to be inexcusable. `[I]` *Implication:* 5 and 6 are a pair. Preaching 6 without 5 makes the hardening look arbitrary.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** Two halves the Masoretes divide precisely: the song and its interpretation (vv.1–7, *setumah* at v.7); the woe-series and the summoned nation (vv.8–30, *setumot* at vv.19, 20, 23; *petuḥot* at v.10 and the chapter's end at v.30). `[T]`
- **Vocabulary / Hebrew.** **כֶּרֶם** *kerem* (vineyard) — 8× in this chapter of the book's 15 `[T]`. **שִׁירַת דּוֹדִי** (v.1) "my love song" — the unit opens in the register of a wedding song and turns into an indictment; the genre-switch is the ambush. **הוֹי** ×6.
- **Parallels.** v.7 is a double antithetical parallel whose two halves are bound by sound rather than sense (see Headline 2).
- **Tone.** Love song → courtroom → six hammer-blows → the terrifying long crescendo of vv.26–30, which ends the chapter in darkness with no relief: *"And if one looks to the land, behold, darkness and distress; and the light is darkened by its clouds."* **Do not resolve this chapter.** The book will, six chapters later.
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Deut 32:32–33 (the vine of Sodom) → 5:1–7** *(moderate)* — *Move 1:* the Song of Moses already uses a corrupt-vine image for Israel. *Move 2:* Deut 32 is a **live source** established at 1:2, so this echo carries an elevated prior. `[I]` Note also that 5:24 makes the accusation explicit in Deuteronomic terms: "they have **rejected the torah of the LORD** of hosts, and have **despised the word of the Holy One of Israel**".
  - **Move 4 — internal echo.** `Internal: answers §3:14` ("you have devoured the vineyard"). `Internal: planted —` the vineyard → **27:2–6**, where the same owner says *"I, the LORD, am its keeper… I have no wrath"* — the identical image with the opposite verdict, and one of the strongest reversals in the book *(high)*. `Internal: planted —` "his hand is stretched out still" (5:25) → 9:12, 17, 21; 10:4 — a refrain that spans two pericopes and is invisible if 5 and 9–10 are preached apart *(high)*. `Internal: planted —` v.26's **נֵס** *"signal for nations far away"* → 11:10, 12; 13:2; 18:3; 49:22; 62:10 — the raised banner starts here as a summons to *invaders* and ends as a summons to *the exiles' return*. **A whole-book reversal the overview's map records only in part.** `[T]`
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Juridical parable embedded in prophetic poetry — closest OT analogue to Nathan's parable (2 Sam 12). **Who am I?** — the vineyard, and the panel of judges in v.3, simultaneously. Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: privilege increases culpability (v.4). Behaviour — the six woes are diagnostic: land-grabbing (v.8), drunkenness (v.11), brazenness about sin (v.18), moral inversion (v.20), self-satisfied cleverness (v.21), corrupt courts (v.23). Five of the six are about **what the powerful do to the poor**.
- **Original audience / FCF.** *They felt:* prosperous and therefore blessed. *Candidate FCF:* **the assumption that fruitfulness in what we can measure means fruitfulness in what God asked for.** `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** v.20's "call evil good and good evil" is heavily weaponised in contemporary polemic; note that its immediate context (vv.22–23) makes it about **judicial corruption for a bribe**, not about cultural disagreement generally. Preachers should say so before applying it.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Pericope 4 — Isaiah 6:1–13 — Holy, holy, holy — and the stump that remains ⭐ HIGH

**Anchor (ESV).** ¹ In the year that King Uzziah died I saw the Lord sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up… ³ "Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts; the whole earth is full of his glory!" ⁵ "Woe is me! For I am lost; for I am a man of unclean lips…" ⁸ "Whom shall I send…?" Then I said, "Here I am! Send me." ⁹ And he said, "Go, and say to this people: 'Keep on hearing, but do not understand…'" ¹³ And though a tenth remain in it, it will be burned again, like a terebinth or an oak, whose stump remains when it is felled. The holy seed is its stump.

**Headline findings.**

1. **The chapter's opening phrase, רָם וְנִשָּׂא, is the exact phrase 2:12–17 has just said God will destroy in every human instance — and it is applied here to God's throne.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Repetition (book level), Move 4, Positional Necessity. The reader has been taught the phrase two units earlier in order to feel it here. It will be applied once more, to the Servant (52:13), and once more to God dwelling with the crushed (57:15). **This four-point chain is the book's spine, and it starts as a threat.**

2. **"Here I am, send me" is the answer to a question asked *after* atonement (v.7), and the commission it accepts is a commission to harden (vv.9–10).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Linking Words, Author's Purpose. Both halves are routinely amputated.

3. **The one clause of hope in the chapter — "the holy seed (זֶרַע קֹדֶשׁ) is its stump" — is the seed of the book's whole botanical programme.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Move 4. Stump → 11:1 shoot; seed → 53:10; 65:9. *High confidence.*

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* ch. 5 has established a vineyard God could do no more for, and closed in unrelieved darkness. *Necessity:* the hardening commission of vv.9–10 is only *just* if the people are already inexcusable — which chs. 1 and 5 have demonstrated at length. **The vision is placed fifth, not first, precisely so that it is not read as the book's beginning but as its verdict.** `[T]` (the placement) / `[I]` (the purpose). *Implication:* the famous "Here am I" is spoken by a man who has just been shown the throne *and* the sentence. Any sermon that ends at v.8 has stopped one verse before the passage's difficulty.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** Vision (vv.1–4) → confession and cleansing (vv.5–7) → commission (vv.8–10) → "How long?" and the answer (vv.11–13). *Petuḥah* at v.13 closes the unit cleanly. `[T]`
- **Vocabulary / Hebrew.** **קָדוֹשׁ ×3** (v.3) — the only threefold repetition of an attribute in the Hebrew Bible; Hebrew has no superlative, so trebling is the superlative `[T, high]`. **נִדְמֵיתִי** (v.5) "I am lost/undone/silenced" — the root can carry both *perish* and *be silent*, and the man whose problem is his **lips** is silenced. *Moderate confidence on the double sense; worth a lexicon check.* **רִצְפָּה** (v.6) "burning coal" — from the altar, i.e. **the coal is a sacrificial by-product; the cleansing is atonement-derived, not mystical** `[T]`.
- **Linking words.** v.5 **כִּי** ×3 stacked — "**for** I am lost; **for** I am a man of unclean lips… **for** my eyes have seen the King". The third *kî* is the ground of the first two: seeing God is what makes the uncleanness lethal. `[T]`
- **Tone.** Awe that tips instantly into terror, then the shock of gratuitous cleansing, then the flat bleakness of vv.9–13. Do not let the seraphim's music set the tone for the whole chapter.
- **Translations / ancient versions.** **Category 1/3 divergence, and it matters.** MT vv.9–10 are **imperatives addressed to the prophet**: הַשְׁמֵן / הַכְבֵּד / הָשַׁע — "*Make* the heart of this people fat, *make* their ears heavy, *blind* their eyes." LXX turns them into an **indicative about the people**: ἐπαχύνθη γὰρ ἡ καρδία — "*for* the heart of this people *has grown* fat… and they *closed their own eyes*." The Greek relocates the agency from God to the people. **This is the form quoted at Matt 13:14–15 and Acts 28:26–27** — so it belongs to **category 3 (the NT's own text)** and stands as a fact about how the NT reads Isaiah, whichever text is prior. *High confidence.* `[T]` **Preaching consequence:** a sermon that quotes Matthew's version to soften Isaiah's has quietly swapped texts. Name both.
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Move 4 — internal echo.** `Internal: answers §2:12–17` — רָם וְנִשָּׂא (see Headline 1) *(high)*. `Internal: answers §1:1` — "in the year that King Uzziah died" names the first of the four kings of 1:1, anchoring the vision inside the superscription's frame *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: planted —` the blinding and deafening of v.10 → **29:18; 32:3; 35:5; 42:7, 18–20; 43:8**, where sight and hearing are restored *in instalments across the whole book* rather than at one stroke *(high)*. `Internal: planted —` "the holy **seed** is its stump" → 11:1; 53:10; 65:9 *(high)*.
  - **Exod 33:20 → 6:5** *(moderate)* — the "no one may see me and live" principle is what makes v.5 a death-sentence rather than a devotional moment. Exodus is a **live source** (established at 4:5). `[I]`
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Prophetic call-vision (throne-room theophany). **Who am I?** — decisively **not** Isaiah. The reader is "this people" (v.9), the one being hardened; identification with the volunteering prophet is the classic Moses-is-me error here. Copycat: Isaiah's "send me" is **descriptive of a unique prophetic commission**, not a normative pattern — the content of what he is sent to do settles that.
- **So what?** Worldview: God's holiness is not an attribute among others but the thing that makes a man's ordinary speech lethal. Motivation: the coal precedes the commission.
- **Original audience / FCF.** *They felt:* a king had died and the throne was empty; the vision says the throne was never empty. *Candidate FCF:* **the vertigo of a leadership vacuum, and the deeper vertigo of standing before a holiness we are not fit for.** `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** vv.9–10 — divine hardening. *Category:* doctrinal + apologetic. *Rhetorical function:* the commission's content, following an indictment and preceding a "how long?" that receives a *limited* answer (v.13's stump). The passage does not teach hardening as God's first move; it is judicial, following chs. 1–5. Name the options (judicial hardening / ironic commission / prophetic foreknowledge stated as purpose) and do not resolve from the pulpit without saying you are choosing.
- **Textual variants.** v.13's final clause **זֶרַע קֹדֶשׁ מַצַּבְתָּהּ** is absent from the LXX at this point. *Category 2 — substantive variant.* The clause is present in 1QIsaᵃ `[S]`. Given that the whole botanical programme of the book hangs on it, this is worth knowing before preaching it. *Moderate confidence; verify in the BHS apparatus and NET notes.*

## Pericope 5 — Isaiah 7:1–9:7 — Immanuel: two kings, two signs, one child ⭐ HIGH

**Anchor (ESV).** 7:⁹ "If you are not firm in faith, you will not be firm at all." ¹² But Ahaz said, "I will not ask, and I will not put the LORD to the test." ¹⁴ Therefore the Lord himself will give you a sign. Behold, the virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel. 8:¹⁴ And he will become a sanctuary and a stone of offense and a rock of stumbling… ¹⁶ Bind up the testimony; seal the teaching among my disciples. ¹⁸ Behold, I and the children whom the LORD has given me are signs and portents in Israel… 9:² The people who walked in darkness have seen a great light… ⁶ For to us a child is born, to us a son is given.

**Headline findings.**

1. **The unit is built around three named sign-children, and the text says so.** `[T, high]` Shear-jashub ("a remnant shall return", 7:3), Immanuel ("God with us", 7:14), Maher-shalal-hash-baz ("quick to the plunder", 8:3) — and then 8:18 gathers them: *"Behold, I and **the children** whom the LORD has given me are **signs and portents** in Israel."* Surfaced by: Vocabulary (names), Structure, Repetition. **The names are the argument.** A series that preaches 7:14 alone has taken one child out of a set of three.

2. **7:9 is a pun the English cannot carry, and it is the hinge of the whole Ahaz encounter.** `[T, high]` *ʾim lōʾ taʾămînû kî lōʾ tēʾāmēnû* — "if you will not **be-firm**, you will not **be-firmed**": both verbs from **אמן**, the root behind *amen* and *faith*. ESV's "firm in faith… firm at all" preserves the sense and loses the sound. **Category 1 — translation loss, not a variant.** Surfaced by: Original Languages, Translations, Vocabulary. It is picked up at 28:16 (*ha-maʾămîn*).

3. **Ahaz is the first test case of chapter 6's hardening — a man offered a sign "deep as Sheol or high as heaven" who piously declines it.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Positional Necessity, Tone, Narrator's Comment (7:2's "the heart of Ahaz… shook as the trees of the forest shake"). His refusal is dressed as reverence (7:12 quotes Deut 6:16). **Unbelief here speaks in Scripture.**

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 6:9–13 has commissioned a ministry of hardening and left one clause of hope (the stump). *Necessity:* the very next unit supplies the **first hearer**, and he hardens exactly as commissioned — while the promise of a child is given anyway. The unit exists here to show that the hardening verdict and the Immanuel promise are issued to the same man on the same day. `[T]` *Implication:* 9:6–7's child is not consolation detached from judgement; it is announced *inside* the Assyrian crisis Ahaz created by refusing the sign.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** Three panels, Masoretically marked: Ahaz at the conduit (7:1–25, *petuḥot* at 7:17 and 7:25); Maher-shalal-hash-baz and the stone (8:1–22, *setumot* at 8:4, 8, 10, 15, 18); light in the darkness and the child (9:1–7, *setumah* at 9:6). `[T]`
- **Vocabulary / Hebrew.** **עַלְמָה** *ʿalmâ* (7:14) with the **article** — "*the* young woman", a definite figure in view, not "a virgin" generically. The word denotes a young woman of marriageable age; **בְּתוּלָה** *bətûlâ* is the term that specifies virginity. `[T, high]` **עִמָּנוּ אֵל** — appears three times (7:14; 8:8; 8:10), and 8:10's is not a name but a *sentence*: "for **God is with us**". The name is unpacked into a confession. `[T]`
- **Translations / ancient versions.** **Category 1 and 3 both live.** LXX 7:14 renders *ʿalmâ* by **παρθένος**, "virgin" — a lexical narrowing made two centuries before the New Testament, by Jewish translators, and it is the form Matthew 1:23 quotes. Report it as a **fact about how the Greek read the Hebrew**, not as an error in either direction. *High confidence.* Note also MT **וְקָרָאת** (7:14) — a form readable as "**she** shall call" or "**you** (f.s.) shall call"; LXX has καλέσεις, "**you** shall call"; 1QIsaᵃ reads וקרא, "**he** shall call" `[S]`. *Category 2, minor.* Nothing doctrinal turns on it, but a congregant comparing versions will notice.
- **Linking words.** 8:6–7 **לָכֵן** — "*Because* this people has refused the waters of Shiloah that flow gently… **therefore** the Lord is bringing up against them the waters of the River." The punishment is the metaphor's own logic: refuse the quiet stream, receive the flood. `[T]`
- **Tone.** Political thriller (7:1–2), pastoral urgency ("Be careful, be quiet, do not fear", 7:4), then the eerie domesticity of curds and honey, then oracular menace (8:6–8), then — without transition — the blaze of 9:2. **The tonal swing at 8:22/9:1 is the sharpest in the first movement.**
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Deut 6:16 → 7:12** *(moderate–high)* — Ahaz quotes the Torah's "you shall not put the LORD your God to the test" to justify refusing a sign the LORD has just commanded him to ask for. *What it adds:* Scripture misapplied is the form his unbelief takes. `[I]`
  - **Move 4 — internal echo.** `Internal: planted —` 7:9's **אמן** → **28:16** *"the one who believes (ha-maʾămîn) will not be in haste"*, the inscription on the foundation stone *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: planted —` 8:14's **stone of offence / rock of stumbling** → 28:16's *tested stone, precious cornerstone* — the same image, judgement and salvation, and the book holds both *(high)*. `Internal: planted —` 8:16 *"seal the teaching among my disciples"* → **29:11** (the sealed book no one can read) → **30:8** (write it on a tablet "for the time to come as a witness for ever") — **a three-point self-referential thread about the book's own transmission, and it is a Move 4 finding the overview did not have** *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: planted —` 9:2's light in darkness → 42:6–7; 49:6; 58:10; **60:1–3** *(high)*. `Internal: answers §6:13` — Shear-jashub's name (7:3) is the stump-clause made into a boy's name, and 10:21–22 will quote the name back as an oracle *(high)*.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Prophetic narrative + oracle + hymnic announcement. **Who am I?** — not Isaiah, not the child; the reader stands with the house of David being offered a sign. Copycat: Ahaz is a **negative example**, explicitly.
- **So what?** Worldview: faith is not a feeling but a stance — *stand firm or you will not be made firm*. Behaviour — stop: the piety that declines God's offered help because accepting it would mean depending on him.
- **Original audience / FCF.** *They felt:* two armies at the gate and a superpower offering terms. *Candidate FCF:* **the pull to secure ourselves by a deal we can see rather than a promise we cannot.** `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** 7:14 — the *ʿalmâ*/παρθένος question, and the double horizon (a child in Ahaz's own day, 7:16, and the child of 9:6). *Category:* apologetic + doctrinal. Handle by naming the double horizon honestly; a sermon that denies the eighth-century referent will not survive 7:16, and one that denies the Matthean referent will not survive Matthew.
- **Textual variants.** 7:14 (above). Also **8:12–13**: 1 Pet 3:14–15 quotes these verses with "the Lord" applied to Christ — worth knowing, though not a variant. `[S]`

## Pericope 6 — Isaiah 9:8–10:34 — The rod I send, the rod I break

**Anchor (ESV).** 9:¹² For all this his anger has not turned away, and his hand is stretched out still. 10:⁵ Woe to Assyria, the rod of my anger; the staff in their hands is my fury! ⁷ But he does not so intend… ¹⁵ Shall the axe boast over him who hews with it…? ²⁰ In that day the remnant of Israel… will lean on the LORD, the Holy One of Israel, in truth. ³³ Behold, the Lord GOD of hosts will lop the boughs with terrifying power.

**Headline findings.**

1. **The refrain "his anger has not turned away, and his hand is stretched out still" occurs five times — 5:25; 9:12, 17, 21; 10:4 — and it is a structural bracket that spans two pericopes.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Repetition, Structure, Move 4. It means 5:25–30 and 9:8–10:4 are one composition interrupted by 6:1–9:7. **Any series that preaches 5 and 9–10 weeks apart severs a refrain the text uses to bind them.**

2. **The same idiom is then turned outward at 14:26–27 — "this is the hand that is stretched out over all the nations."** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Move 4, Repetition. The hand stretched out against Israel becomes the hand stretched out over the world. This is how the book universalises its indictment, and it is a verbal link, not a thematic one.

3. **10:5–19 is the book's clearest statement of the double agency problem: Assyria is God's rod *and* Assyria is culpable — "but he does not so intend" (v.7).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Linking Words, Author's Purpose, Tone. The axe-and-saw parable of v.15 is the argument's compression.

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 9:1–7 has announced a child on David's throne with government and peace without end. *Necessity:* the announcement is immediately followed by the demonstration that the *present* Davidic arrangement is under judgement and the *present* instrument of that judgement (Assyria) is itself doomed. The unit exists here to prevent 9:6–7 being read as a description of Hezekiah or any king then living. `[I]` *Implication:* the boughs lopped at 10:33–34 are what make 11:1's shoot-from-a-stump necessary. **10:33–34 and 11:1 are one sentence in two chapters.**

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** Four strophes closed by the refrain (9:8–12; 13–17; 18–21; 10:1–4), then the Assyria oracle (10:5–19), the remnant oracle (10:20–27), and the invader's itinerary (10:28–34). Masoretic *setumot* at 9:12, 20; 10:4, 11, 23, 32, 34 — corroborating the strophe divisions exactly. `[T]`
- **Repetition.** The refrain ×5 (above). Also **מַטֶּה / שֵׁבֶט** (rod/staff) — 10:5, 15, 24, 26; the rod passes from God's hand to Assyria's and back. `[T]`
- **Vocabulary.** **שְׁאָר יָשׁוּב** (10:21) — Isaiah's son's name (7:3) quoted back as an oracle: *"A remnant shall return, the remnant of Jacob, to the mighty God."* `[T, high]` **אֵל גִּבּוֹר** in 10:21 is the same title given to the child at 9:6 — the only two occurrences in the book. **The remnant returns to the one the child is named after.** *High confidence; a verified link.*
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Route as allusion — 10:28–32.** The invader's march is given as an **itinerary of eleven named places** (Aiath, Migron, Michmash, Geba, Ramah, Gibeah, Gallim, Laishah, Anathoth, Madmenah, Gebim, Nob) closing at "the mount of the daughter of Zion". *Weight:* moderate — the sequence is a real advance-route, and the naming is the terror. **Proper-noun inventory run; the load-bearing name is Nob** (10:32), the priestly city Saul destroyed (1 Sam 22) `[S]` — a possible outstanding-word resonance, flagged *uncertain* and routed to Open Questions rather than preached.
  - **Exod 14 / Judg 7 → 10:26** *(high)* — "as when he struck **Midian** at the rock of Oreb. And his staff will be over the sea, and he will lift it as he did in **Egypt**." Two deliverance-precedents named in one verse. *Move 2:* Exodus is a live source (4:5); this is its second explicit use, and it now functions as *precedent for rescue* rather than *pattern of presence*. `[T]`
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §5:25` — the refrain *(high)*. `Internal: answers §7:3` — Shear-jashub *(high)*. `Internal: planted —` 10:33–34's lopped boughs → 11:1 *(high)*; "the hand stretched out" → 14:26–27 *(high)*.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Prophetic oracle cycles. **Who am I?** — Israel under the rod, and (uncomfortably) Assyria in its self-congratulation (10:13, "By the strength of my hand I have done it"). Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: God uses instruments that do not know they are instruments, and holds them accountable for the intent they *did* have. Motivation for the fearful: 10:24–27, "be not afraid of the Assyrians".
- **Original audience / FCF.** *Candidate FCF:* **the terror of being at the mercy of a power that does not know God — and the harder thought that God is using it.** `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** 10:5–15 — divine use of a wicked agent. *Category:* doctrinal/apologetic. The passage itself supplies the framework (v.7 distinguishes God's purpose from Assyria's intent); use it rather than importing a systematic solution.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Pericope 7 — Isaiah 11:1–12:6 — A shoot from the stump; the wells of salvation ⭐ HIGH

**Anchor (ESV).** 11:¹ There shall come forth a shoot from the stump of Jesse, and a branch from his roots shall bear fruit. ² And the Spirit of the LORD shall rest upon him… ⁶ The wolf shall dwell with the lamb… ⁹ for the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the LORD as the waters cover the sea. ¹⁰ In that day the root of Jesse… ¹¹ the Lord will extend his hand yet a second time to recover the remnant… 12:² "Behold, God is my salvation… for the LORD GOD is my strength and my song, and he has become my salvation."

**Headline findings.**

1. **12:2 is a near-verbatim quotation of Exodus 15:2, the Song of the Sea — the first movement of the book ends by putting Israel's oldest song in the mouth of the redeemed remnant.** `[T, high]` *"The LORD God is my strength and my song, and he has become my salvation"* ‖ Exod 15:2 *"The LORD is my strength and my song, and he has become my salvation."* Surfaced by: Tool 11 Move 1, Vocabulary, Structure. **This is the strongest single verbal allusion in chapters 1–12, and the *petuḥah* at 12:6 shows the Masoretes treating it as the movement's close.**

2. **11:1's stump is 6:13's stump — the same word, the same felling, and the answer to the same darkness.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Move 4, Vocabulary, Positional Necessity. 10:33–34 has just lopped the forest; 11:1 grows one shoot from what is left.

3. **11:11's "a **second time**" (שֵׁנִית) is the book's first explicit new-exodus claim.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Biblical Theology. It is completed in 11:16: "a highway from Assyria… **as there was for Israel when they came up from the land of Egypt.**"

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 10:33–34 has felled the forest. *Necessity:* the shoot has nowhere to come from unless the tree is down first. The unit exists here because the book has spent ten chapters establishing that nothing standing will survive — so that the messianic hope is unmistakably **resurrection-shaped rather than reformist**. `[T]` *Implication:* preach 11:1 without 10:33–34 and the shoot becomes a nice image; preach them together and it is the only living thing in a clear-felled landscape.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** The shoot and his rule (11:1–9, *petuḥah* at 11:9); the root as banner and the second gathering (11:10–16, *petuḥah* at 11:10 — the Masoretes marked a break *before* v.10, making "in that day" the opening of a new paragraph); the two-stanza doxology (12:1–6, *petuḥah* at 12:6 closing the movement). `[T]`
- **Vocabulary / Hebrew.** **חֹטֶר** (shoot) and **נֵצֶר** (branch, 11:1) — two rare botanical terms in one verse; **נֵצֶר** returns at **60:21** ("the branch of my planting") applied to *the people* `[T]`. **גֶּזַע** (stump) — the felled trunk. **נֵס** (11:10, 12) "banner/signal" — see the reversal noted at 5:26.
- **Translations / ancient versions.** 11:2–3 lists six Spirit-endowments in Hebrew, with **"the fear of the LORD" appearing twice** (end of v.2, start of v.3). LXX renders the second occurrence **εὐσεβείας** (piety), producing **seven** — the source of the sevenfold-Spirit tradition. *Category 1 — an interpretive rendering, not a variant.* *High confidence.* `[T]` At **11:10**, LXX has the root of Jesse *rising to rule* the nations and the nations *hoping* in him, where MT has the nations *inquiring of* him — and **Rom 15:12 quotes the Greek**. *Category 3.*
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Exod 15:2 → 12:2** *(high — near-verbatim)* — *Move 1:* the Song of the Sea is Israel's first hymn, sung on the far shore after the deliverance, and its function there is the *response* to a completed rescue. *Move 2:* Exodus has appeared at 4:5 (cloud and fire) and 10:26 (the staff over the sea); this third use moves from **presence** to **precedent** to **the song itself**. The book is tracking Exodus 13–15 in order. `[I]` *(a Move 2 pattern worth testing in a solo dig)* *Move 3:* Exod 15 and Gen 1 are already in canonical conversation (waters divided, dry land appearing) `[I]`. *What it adds:* chapter 12 is not a generic doxology — it is the remnant of 11:11 singing the song of the first exodus after the second.
  - **Gen 3:14–15 → 11:8** *(moderate)* — the nursing child playing over the cobra's hole, and the weaned child's hand on the adder's den, reverse Eden's enmity without naming it. The reversal is made explicit only at **65:25**, which re-cites this passage and adds *"dust shall be the serpent's food."* `[T]` for the re-citation; `[I]` for the Genesis intent here.
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §6:13` (stump) *(high)*; `answers §10:33–34` (the felling) *(high)*; `answers §1:2` — 11:9's *"the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the LORD"* answers 1:3's *"Israel does not know, my people do not understand"*, the same root **ידע** *(high)* `[T]` — **a verified reversal the overview did not have**. `Internal: planted —` 11:6–9 → 65:25 *(high)*; 11:10's **נֵס** → 49:22; 62:10 *(high)*; 11:16's highway → 19:23; 35:8; 40:3; 62:10 *(high)*.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Messianic oracle + eschatological vision + hymn. **Who am I?** — the redeemed remnant of ch. 12, singing. Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: the coming king judges "not by what his eyes see" (11:3) — the opposite of every court condemned in 1:23; 5:23; 10:1–2. Behaviour — start: 12:4, "make known his deeds among the peoples".
- **Original audience / FCF.** *Candidate FCF:* **the longing for a ruler who will actually judge rightly for the poor** (11:4), in a book that has documented courts that will not. `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** 11:6–9 — literal or figurative? Name the options; note that 65:25 re-uses it in a new-creation frame, which is the book's own commentary.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Pericope 8 — Isaiah 13:1–14:27 — Babylon: the taunt over a fallen star

**Anchor (ESV).** 13:¹ The oracle concerning Babylon which Isaiah the son of Amoz saw. ⁶ Wail, for the day of the LORD is near… ¹⁹ And Babylon… will be like Sodom and Gomorrah when God overthrew them. 14:⁴ you will take up this taunt against the king of Babylon… ¹² "How you are fallen from heaven, O Day Star, son of Dawn!" ²⁴ The LORD of hosts has sworn: "As I have planned, so shall it be"… ²⁶ This is the purpose that is purposed concerning the whole earth, and this is the hand that is stretched out over all the nations.

**Headline findings.**

1. **14:26–27 turns the first movement's refrain outward: "this is **the hand that is stretched out** over all the nations."** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Repetition, Move 4. Five times the outstretched hand was against Israel (5:25; 9:12, 17, 21; 10:4); here it covers the earth. **The oracles-against-the-nations block is not a digression — it is the same hand, widened.**

2. **13:1 carries the book's third and last superscription, and it names Isaiah again — over an oracle about Babylon.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Context. Whatever one makes of it historically, **the text itself attributes the Babylon material to the same seer as chapters 1 and 2**, which is a datum a series should state plainly rather than avoid.

3. **13:19 measures Babylon by Sodom — the same standard applied to Judah at 1:9–10.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Move 2, Move 4. Judah was *spared* Sodom's fate; Babylon *receives* it.

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* chs. 1–12 have indicted Judah, announced a shoot, and closed with a doxology. *Necessity:* an indictment confined to Judah invites the objection that God is parochial. The *maśśāʾ* block exists here to demonstrate that the standard applied to Zion is the standard applied to the world — and it opens with **Babylon**, not Assyria, which is the power that will actually take Judah away (39:6). `[I]` *Implication:* the book names its final enemy long before the narrative reaches it.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** The day of the LORD against Babylon (13:1–22, *petuḥah* at 14:23 closing the whole Babylon block); the taunt-song (14:1–23, *setumah* at 14:2, 14:32); the Assyrian coda and the universal purpose (14:24–27, *petuḥah* at 14:27). `[T]`
- **Vocabulary.** **מַשָּׂא** *maśśāʾ* (13:1) — "oracle/burden", the heading of ten units through ch. 23 `[T]`. **הֵילֵל בֶּן־שָׁחַר** (14:12) — "Shining One, son of Dawn"; **the Latin *Lucifer* is the Vulgate's rendering, not a Hebrew name** `[T, high]`.
- **Tone.** Cosmic dread (13:10, sun and moon darkened), then the extraordinary literary set-piece of the taunt — Sheol stirring to greet the arriving king (14:9–11), maggots for a bed. **This is satire, and satire of a specific kind: the deflation of a claim to deity.**
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Gen 19 → 13:19** *(high)* — *Move 1:* Sodom is the overthrow with no survivors. *Move 2:* Sodom is a **live source** (1:9–10; 3:9); its third use inverts the first — Judah escaped it, Babylon does not. `[T]`
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §5:25 etc.` — the outstretched hand *(high)*. `Internal: answers §2:12–17` — 14:13–14's *"I will ascend… I will set my throne on high"* is the levelling-oracle's boast put in a named mouth *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: planted —` 14:1's *"sojourners will join them"* → 56:3–8 *(moderate)*.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Oracle against a nation + taunt-song (*māšāl*). **Who am I?** — not Babylon's king, though the ambition described is ordinary. Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: 14:24–27 is the book's most compressed statement of divine purpose — *planned, therefore it stands; stretched out, therefore who will turn it back?*
- **Difficult verses.** 14:12–15 — the traditional reading as Satan's fall. *Category:* doctrinal + apologetic. The passage's stated referent is "the king of Babylon" (14:4). Preachers may note the later application; they should not present it as the text's subject without saying so. Also **13:16** (infants dashed) — an ethical landmine; name it rather than reading past it.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Pericope 9 — Isaiah 14:28–20:6 — Oracles: Philistia, Moab, Damascus, Cush, Egypt

**Anchor (ESV).** 16:⁵ then a throne will be established in steadfast love, and on it will sit in faithfulness in the tent of David one who judges and seeks justice… 17:⁷ In that day man will look to his Maker, and his eyes will look on the Holy One of Israel. 19:²⁴ In that day Israel will be the third with Egypt and Assyria, a blessing in the midst of the earth, ²⁵ whom the LORD of hosts has blessed, saying, "Blessed be **Egypt my people**, and **Assyria the work of my hands**, and **Israel my inheritance**."

**Headline findings.**

1. **19:24–25 gives Egypt and Assyria the two most loaded covenant titles in Israel's vocabulary — "my people" and "the work of my hands" — and puts Israel third.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Tone, Author's Purpose. **This is arguably the most startling sentence in the Old Testament**, and it sits inside a block series routinely skip.

2. **A Davidic throne appears inside a *Moab* oracle (16:5).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Move 4, Vocabulary (*ḥesed*, *ʾōhel Dāwid*). Refuge for Moab's outcasts is grounded in the character of a coming Davidic ruler — the promise of 9:7 and 11:4 restated as a *Gentile's* asylum.

3. **The prophet's own grief is written into the oracles — "my heart cries out for Moab" (15:5), "I drench you with my tears" (16:9), "my inner parts moan like a lyre" (16:11).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Tone, Narrator-voice. These are not gloating texts, and a series that treats them as such misreads their register.

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 14:24–27 has declared a purpose over "the whole earth" and a hand stretched over "all the nations". *Necessity:* the claim requires itemisation. These oracles are that itemisation, and they run in a deliberate arc from **Philistia (a neighbour) → Moab (kin) → Damascus (an ally) → Cush/Egypt (a superpower)**, ending not in destruction but in a highway of shared worship (19:23). `[I]` *Implication:* the block's destination is 19:23–25, and a series that samples the oracles at random will never reach it.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** Six *maśśāʾ* units (14:28 Philistia; 15:1 Moab; 17:1 Damascus; 18:1 Cush; 19:1 Egypt) plus the sign-act narrative of ch. 20. Masoretic *setumot* cluster densely: 16:6, 14; 17:3, 6, 11, 14; 18:3, 7; 19:15, 17, 18, 23, 25; 20:2, 6. `[T]` **The four *setumot* inside 19:16–25 mark five successive "in that day" panels** — the escalation is structurally signposted.
- **Repetition.** **בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא** ×6 in 19:16–24, each opening a further stage: terror → five cities → an altar → the LORD known → a highway → the threefold blessing. `[T]`
- **Vocabulary.** **עַמִּי** (19:25) "my people" applied to **Egypt**; **מַעֲשֵׂה יָדַי** "the work of my hands" applied to **Assyria** — both phrases used elsewhere in the book of *Israel* (e.g. 29:23; 60:21; 64:8). `[T, high]` This is not a loose ecumenism: it is the deliberate transfer of Israel's own titles.
- **Translations / ancient versions.** **19:18** — MT **עִיר הַהֶרֶס** "City of **Destruction**"; LXX **Πόλις-ασεδεκ**, "City of **Righteousness**". A single-consonant difference (*heres* / *ṣedeq*), and the two readings are opposites. *Category 2 — substantive variant.* 1QIsaᵃ reads עיר ההרס `[S]`. *Moderate confidence; the ESV and LXX cannot both be preached here.* Flag it if quoting the verse.
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Exod 7–12 → 19:1–15** *(moderate–high)* — the Nile dried, the counsellors confounded, the LORD riding on a cloud into Egypt. *Move 2:* Exodus is live (4:5; 10:26; 12:2); this fourth use runs the plague-pattern **against** Egypt and then **reverses** it: 19:20 promises Egypt "a saviour and defender" who will "deliver them" when they cry out — **exactly the vocabulary of Exod 2:23–25 and 3:7–8, now with Egypt as the oppressed party.** `[T]` for the vocabulary; `[I]` for the deliberateness. *This is the sweep's strongest Move 2 finding so far.*
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §2:6` — 2:6 condemned Judah for striking hands with **בְּנֵי נֵכָר**; 19:25 blesses foreigners as God's people *(moderate)*. `Internal: planted —` 16:5's Davidic throne → 55:3 *(moderate)*; 19:23's highway → 35:8; 40:3; 62:10 *(high)*.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Oracles + one prophetic sign-act (ch. 20). **Copycat:** Isaiah walking naked and barefoot three years (20:2–3) is **descriptive and unique** — a commanded sign-act, not a model of ministry. Say so before anyone asks.
- **So what?** Worldview: God's purposes for the nations are not merely punitive. Behaviour — the block resists both triumphalism about enemies and despair about their power.
- **Original audience / FCF.** *They felt:* small, and surrounded. *Candidate FCF:* **the temptation to define ourselves by who our enemies are, when God intends to bless them.** `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** 20:2–4 — the naked prophet, and the humiliation of captives. Handle briefly and plainly; it is a sign-act, and its offensiveness is its function.
- **Textual variants.** 19:18 (above).

## Pericope 10 — Isaiah 21:1–23:18 — Oracles: the watchman, Jerusalem, Shebna, Tyre

**Anchor (ESV).** 21:⁸ "Upon a watchtower I stand, O Lord, continually by day…" ⁹ And he answered, "Fallen, fallen is Babylon; and all the carved images of her gods he has shattered to the ground." 22:¹ The oracle concerning the valley of vision… ¹³ "Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die." ²² And I will place on his shoulder the key of the house of David. He shall open, and none shall shut; and he shall shut, and none shall open.

**Headline findings.**

1. **Jerusalem is given a *maśśāʾ* heading of her own (22:1) and placed in the middle of the oracles against the nations.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Structure, Author's Purpose, Positional Necessity. "The valley of vision" is Jerusalem, and she is being processed on the same docket as Moab and Tyre. **This is the block's most important structural fact and the answer to anyone who thinks 13–23 is a digression.**

2. **22:13 — "Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die" — is quoted verbatim by Paul at 1 Corinthians 15:32.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Move 1, Quotation. In Isaiah it is the reaction of a city that has just been delivered and celebrates instead of repenting (22:12–14); in Paul it is the logic of a world without resurrection. **The two uses illuminate each other.**

3. **22:22's "key of the house of David" is the only place in the book where authority is described as a key, and it is given to a servant named Eliakim — whose tenure the very next verses say will fail (22:25).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Linking Words, Tone. Any office in this book that is not the Servant's collapses.

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* the oracles have run outward from Philistia to Egypt and arrived at a highway of shared worship (19:23–25). *Necessity:* the sequence now turns back on Jerusalem, so that the reader who has been enjoying the nations' comeuppance is handed a *maśśāʾ* with his own address on it. `[I]` *Implication:* 22:1–14 is the hinge of the whole nations-block, and its placement is the argument.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** Four units: the wilderness of the sea / Dumah / Arabia (21, *setumot* at 21:10, 12, 15, 17; *petuḥah* at 21:5); the valley of vision (22:1–14, *setumah* at 22:14 — a *petuḥah* in the Masoretic tradition, marking the heaviest break in the chapter); Shebna and Eliakim (22:15–25, *setumah* at 22:25); Tyre (23, *setumot* at 23:14; *petuḥah* at 23:18). `[T]`
- **Vocabulary.** **צֹפֶה** *ṣōp̄eh* (21:6, 8) "watchman" — the office reappears at **52:8** ("the voice of your watchmen") and **56:10** ("his watchmen are blind"), and 62:6 ("I have set watchmen on your walls"). `[T]` **Four occurrences, and they run the whole arc: the watchman who sees Babylon fall, the watchmen who see the LORD return, the watchmen who are blind, the watchmen who never keep silent.** A whole-book thread the overview did not record.
- **Tone.** 21:3–4 is the prophet's own physical collapse under the vision — "my loins are filled with anguish… I am dismayed so that I cannot see". **The seer who cannot see.** Note the irony against 6:1's "I saw".
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: planted —` 21:9's *"Fallen, fallen is Babylon"* → 47:1–15 (Babylon's dethronement in full) *(high)*. `Internal: answers §7:3` — 22:9–11's frantic waterworks at *"the lower pool"* recall Ahaz at *"the upper pool"* (7:3) and Hezekiah's envoys at the same conduit (36:2); **three water-engineering scenes at the same location, and in each the question is whether the city will look to the LORD** (22:11: "But you did not look to him who did it") *(moderate–high)* `[T]` — **a Move 4 finding the overview did not have.** `Internal: planted —` 22:22's key → the Eliakim–Shebna reversal is itself answered at 36:3 and 37:2, where both men reappear in office. *(high)* `[T]`
  - **1 Cor 15:32; Rev 3:7; Rev 14:8, 18:2** — three NT uses drawn from this unit `[S]`. Note them; do not build the exegesis on them.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Oracles + a court narrative (22:15–25). **Who am I?** — the city celebrating on the rooftops (22:1–2, 13). Copycat: Shebna is a **negative example** with a named verdict.
- **So what?** Worldview: deliverance that produces partying rather than repentance is itself judged (22:12–14). Behaviour — stop: the relief that skips the reckoning.
- **Difficult verses.** 22:14 — "Surely this iniquity will not be atoned for you until you die." *Category:* doctrinal/pastoral. Note its position: it is a verdict on a specific generation's specific response, not a general doctrine of unforgivable sin; but do not soften it into nothing.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Pericope 11 — Isaiah 24:1–23 — The earth emptied: the cosmic assize ⭐ HIGH

**Anchor (ESV).** ¹ Behold, the LORD will empty the earth and make it desolate… ⁵ The earth lies defiled under its inhabitants; for they have transgressed the laws, violated the statutes, **broken the everlasting covenant**. ¹⁸ For the windows of heaven are opened, and the foundations of the earth tremble. ²³ Then the moon will be confounded and the sun ashamed, for the LORD of hosts reigns on Mount Zion.

**Headline findings.**

1. **The chapter is written in flood vocabulary: "the everlasting covenant" (v.5) and "the windows of heaven are opened" (v.18) are both Genesis flood-narrative terms.** `[T, high]` *bərît ʿôlām* is the Noahic covenant's own designation (Gen 9:16); *ʾărubbôt* "windows/floodgates" of heaven is Gen 7:11. Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Move 1, Biblical Theology. **The judgement of the earth is being narrated as an un-flooding — and the promise not to flood again (54:9) will later be the ground of comfort.**

2. **v.2's six-fold levelling — people/priest, slave/master, maid/mistress, buyer/seller, lender/borrower, creditor/debtor — is a merismus for the whole social order.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Parallels, Repetition, Structure. Every pair names a *power differential*, and every one is cancelled.

3. **The word "earth" (אֶרֶץ) occurs sixteen times in twenty-three verses.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Repetition. The chapter's subject is not Judah, not Babylon, but the ground itself.

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* ten oracles have judged the nations one by one, and 22 has judged Jerusalem among them. *Necessity:* an itemised list invites the question *is that all of them?* Chapter 24 answers by removing the list and judging the earth. It exists here as the **generalisation the block has been building toward**, and it is why 24–27 cannot be detached from 13–23. `[I]` *Implication:* the "Isaiah Apocalypse" is not a separate genre-block dropped in; it is the *maśśāʾ* series' conclusion.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** Emptying (vv.1–13); a fragment of song and the prophet's dissent (vv.14–16); terror and the pit (vv.17–20); the LORD enthroned (vv.21–23). Masoretic *setumot* at 24:15, 20; *petuḥah* at 24:23 closing the chapter. `[T]`
- **Repetition.** **אֶרֶץ** ×16; the sound-play of v.16–17 in Hebrew — *bōgĕdîm bāgādû ûbeged bôgĕdîm bāgādû* ("the traitors have betrayed, with betrayal the traitors have betrayed"), then *paḥad wāpaḥat wāpāḥ* ("terror and the pit and the snare"). **Two consecutive verses of dense alliteration that no translation carries. Category 1 — translation loss.** *High confidence.* `[T]`
- **Tone.** Desolation with a strange interruption: vv.14–16a break into *praise* from the ends of the earth, and the prophet immediately refuses to join it — *"But I say, 'I waste away, I waste away. Woe is me!'"* (v.16b). **The dissonance is deliberate and is the chapter's most preachable feature.**
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Gen 7:11; 9:16 → 24:5, 18** *(high)* — *Move 1:* the flood narrative's covenant is God's promise *never to do this again by water*; the "windows of heaven" are its mechanism. *Move 2:* Noah becomes a **live source** here and is used explicitly at **54:9** — where the same covenant is invoked as the guarantee that God's anger will not return. **The book uses the flood twice, once as threat and once as promise, and the second cancels the first.** `[T]` *Move 3:* Gen 9's *bərît ʿôlām* and the creation account are already linked (the flood as de-creation) `[I]`. *What it adds:* chapter 24 is de-creation language, which is why 65:17's new creation is its answer.
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §1:2` — "Hear, O heavens… give ear, O earth" summoned the witnesses; ch. 24 puts them in the dock *(moderate–high)*. `Internal: planted —` 24:23's *"the LORD of hosts reigns on Mount Zion"* → 52:7's *"Your God reigns"* *(high)*; 24:21's punishment of "the host of heaven" → 27:1's Leviathan *(moderate)*.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Prophetic-apocalyptic poetry. **Reading rule: vision, not video** — the sun and moon "ashamed" is not astronomy. Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: the earth itself is implicated in human covenant-breaking (v.5) — creation is not a neutral stage.
- **Original audience / FCF.** *Candidate FCF:* **the sense that the world's disorder is more than the sum of its politics.** `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** v.5's "everlasting covenant" — which covenant? Options: Noahic (the vocabulary), Sinai (the "laws… statutes"), or a general creation-order covenant. The vocabulary points to Noah; the parallel terms point to Sinai; **the honest answer is that the verse holds both**, which is the point of judging *the earth* by *Israel's* categories.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Pericope 12 — Isaiah 25:1–27:13 — The feast on the mountain; death swallowed up ⭐ HIGH

**Anchor (ESV).** 25:⁶ On this mountain the LORD of hosts will make for all peoples a feast of rich food… ⁷ And he will swallow up on this mountain the covering that is cast over all peoples… ⁸ **He will swallow up death forever**; and the Lord GOD will wipe away tears from all faces. 26:¹⁹ Your dead shall live; their bodies shall rise. 27:¹ In that day the LORD… will punish Leviathan the fleeing serpent… ² "A pleasant vineyard, sing of it! ³ I, the LORD, am its keeper."

**Headline findings.**

1. **27:2–6 re-sings 5:1–7 with the verdict reversed — same owner, same vineyard, opposite outcome.** `[T, high]` 5:1 *"Let me sing… my love song concerning his vineyard"*; 27:2 *"A pleasant vineyard, sing of it!"* 5:6 *"I will make it a waste… briers and thorns shall grow up"*; 27:4 *"Would that I had thorns and briers to battle! I would march against them."* Surfaced by: Move 4, Repetition, Structure. **The thorns that were the judgement become the thing God fights on the vineyard's behalf.** This is the single most complete internal reversal in chapters 1–39.

2. **25:8's "he will swallow up death forever" uses the same verb (בִּלַּע) as v.7's swallowing of the shroud — and the LXX reverses the subject.** `[T, high]` MT: God swallows death. LXX: **κατέπιεν ὁ θάνατος ἰσχύσας** — *death, having prevailed, swallowed*. **Category 2 — substantive divergence**, and 1 Cor 15:54 follows neither exactly (κατεπόθη ὁ θάνατος εἰς νῖκος, closer to the Hebrew sense). *High confidence on the divergence; the direction of dependence is unsettled — say so.*

3. **26:19 is one of the Old Testament's few unambiguous resurrection statements, and the LXX makes it more explicit still.** `[T]` MT *"Your dead shall live; their bodies shall rise"*; LXX ἀναστήσονται οἱ νεκροί, "**the dead shall rise**". Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Translations, Biblical Theology.

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* ch. 24 has emptied the earth and enthroned the LORD on Zion. *Necessity:* an enthronement requires a banquet, and a de-created world requires an answer to death. 25–27 supplies both, in that order, and then returns to the vineyard image the book opened its indictment with — so that the movement closes by *reversing its own first accusation*. `[I]` *Implication:* the *petuḥah* at **27:13** closes movement two, and the last thing before it is a trumpet gathering the lost from Assyria and Egypt (27:13). **The block ends where 11:11–16 said it would.**

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** Thanksgiving (25:1–5); the feast and the swallowing of death (25:6–12, *petuḥah* at 25:5 and 25:8 — **the Masoretes marked a break immediately after "he will swallow up death forever"**, isolating it) `[T]`; the song of the strong city (26:1–21, *setumot* at 26:10, 11, 19, 21); Leviathan, the vineyard, and the trumpet (27:1–13, *setumot* at 27:1, 6, 11, 12; *petuḥah* at 27:13). `[T]`
- **Vocabulary / Hebrew.** **בִּלַּע** *billaʿ* (25:7, 8) — "swallow up", used twice in two verses of *the covering* and *death*; the same verb described Sheol's appetite at 5:14 (*"Sheol has enlarged its appetite and opened its mouth"*). **The mouth that swallowed Jerusalem's nobility is itself swallowed.** *High confidence — a verified verbal reversal.* `[T]` **לִוְיָתָן** (27:1) with **נָחָשׁ בָּרִחַ / נָחָשׁ עֲקַלָּתוֹן** — "fleeing serpent / twisting serpent".
- **Parallels.** 26:19's four-line build (dead live / bodies rise / dwellers in dust awake and sing / earth gives birth to the dead) is climactic parallelism — each line more explicit than the last.
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Move 4 — the vineyard.** See Headline 1. `Internal: answers §5:1–7` *(high)*.
  - **Move 4 — Sheol's mouth.** `Internal: answers §5:14` *(high)*, and `answers §14:9–11` (Sheol stirring to greet the king) *(moderate)*.
  - **Creation-combat → 27:1** *(moderate–high)* — Leviathan/Rahab is a **live source** (30:7; 51:9). *Move 2:* here the sea-monster is *punished*; at 51:9 the same defeat is invoked as the ground for a plea. `[T]` *What it adds:* the chaos-power is not merely symbolic scenery; the book treats its defeat as an act still to come.
  - **Gen 3 → 27:1** *(moderate)* — **נָחָשׁ** *naḥaš* is the Eden word. Together with 65:25's explicit Gen 3:14 citation, the book brackets its serpent-material.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Apocalyptic-prophetic poetry + a re-sung parable. **Who am I?** — a guest at a feast prepared for "**all peoples**" (25:6). Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: death is a defeated enemy in the future tense — the OT's own "not yet". Motivation: 25:9's *"we have waited for him"* is the appropriate posture, not activity.
- **Original audience / FCF.** *They felt:* mortality as the one thing no covenant had addressed. *Candidate FCF:* **the shroud over all peoples — the universal fact no religion had removed.** `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** 26:14 vs 26:19 — "they are dead, they will not live" and "your dead shall live" within five verses. The distinction is between *the oppressors* and *God's people*; note it before someone else does.
- **Textual variants.** 25:8 (above) — a real divergence with NT consequences.

## Pericope 13 — Isaiah 28:1–29 — The tested cornerstone against the covenant with death ⭐ HIGH

**Anchor (ESV).** ¹⁰ "For it is precept upon precept, precept upon precept, line upon line, line upon line, here a little, there a little." ¹⁵ Because you have said, "We have made a covenant with death, and with Sheol we have an agreement"… ¹⁶ therefore thus says the Lord GOD, "Behold, I am the one who has laid as a foundation in Zion, a stone, a tested stone, a precious cornerstone, of a sure foundation: 'Whoever believes will not be in haste.'" ²¹ to do his deed—strange is his deed! and to work his work—alien is his work!

**Headline findings.**

1. **28:10 is not a doctrine of Bible study; it is mockery of the prophet's speech reproduced as baby-talk.** `[T, high]` The Hebrew is *ṣaw lāṣāw ṣaw lāṣāw, qaw lāqāw qaw lāqāw, zəʿêr šām zəʿêr šām* — monosyllables imitating a drunk mimicking a preacher, or the sound of a foreign tongue. v.11 then says God will answer them "by people of strange lips and with a foreign tongue" — **the gibberish they invented becomes the language of their judgement.** Surfaced by: Original Languages, Repetition, Linking Words, Tone. **Category 1 — translation loss.** The popular use of "line upon line" as a method of doctrine is the exact inverse of the verse.

2. **28:16's "whoever believes will not be in haste" answers 7:9's "if you are not firm, you will not be firm" — the same root אמן, twenty-one chapters apart.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Move 4, Vocabulary. **The wordplay pressed on Ahaz has become the inscription on the foundation stone.**

3. **The chapter ends with a farmer parable (vv.23–29) whose point is that God's judgement is *measured* — you do not thresh dill with a sledge.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Genre, Tone. It is the tonal counterweight to vv.14–22 and is almost always cut.

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 24–27 has judged the earth and promised the feast; the *petuḥah* at 27:13 closed it. *Necessity:* the cosmic horizon now collapses back to a very local one — a drunken northern aristocracy and a Jerusalem leadership doing deals. The unit exists here to show that people who have been shown the end of the world will still make a covenant with death for the sake of a policy. `[I]` *Implication:* the six *hôy*-woes of 28–33 are the book's second woe-series (the first was 5:8–23), and they are aimed at **false trust** rather than social injustice. The two series are complementary, not repetitive.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** Ephraim's crown (vv.1–6); priests and prophets reeling (vv.7–13); the scoffers' covenant and the stone (vv.14–22); the farmer parable (vv.23–29). *Setumot* at 28:2, 11 (in some traditions), 22; *petuḥah* absent within the chapter — the Masoretes ran it as one block. `[T]`
- **Vocabulary / Hebrew.** **אֶבֶן בֹּחַן פִּנַּת יִקְרַת מוּסָד מוּסָּד** (v.16) — five terms piled on one stone: *tested*, *cornerstone*, *precious*, *foundation*, *founded*. `[T]` **מוּסַר** / **מוּסָד** wordplay is audible in Hebrew.
- **Translations / ancient versions.** **28:16** — MT **לֹא יָחִישׁ** "will not be in haste/panic"; LXX **οὐ μὴ καταισχυνθῇ**, "will not be **put to shame**", apparently reading the root as *bôš*. **Rom 9:33, Rom 10:11 and 1 Pet 2:6 all quote the Greek.** *Category 3 — the NT's own text; it stands whichever reading is prior.* *High confidence.* `[T]` **Preaching consequence:** if you quote Romans on this verse and then read the ESV, you have read two different sentences. Say so.
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §7:9` — the אמן link *(high)*. `Internal: answers §8:14` — the stone that was "a stone of offence and a rock of stumbling" is now "a tested stone, a precious cornerstone": **the same stone, and the book has given both faces** *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §5:11, 22` — the drunkards of ch. 5 reappear as drunken priests and prophets *(moderate–high)*. `Internal: planted —` v.21's "strange… alien" work → 45:7; 55:8–9 *(moderate)*.
  - **2 Sam 5:20; Josh 10:10–12 → 28:21** *(moderate)* — "as on Mount Perazim… as in the Valley of Gibeon". **Route/name allusion:** both are places where the LORD fought *for* Israel; here he will fight *against* her, which is what makes the deed "strange". **The freight is carried entirely by the place-names — no verbal quotation at all.** *Weight: moderate; the load-the-story rule was applied to the association but the source narratives were not re-read in this run — flagged.* `[I]`
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Woe-oracle + parable. **Who am I?** — the scoffers who rule "this people in Jerusalem" (v.14). Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: policies are covenants, and some of them are with death. Behaviour — stop: managing risk by arrangements that assume God will not act.
- **Difficult verses.** vv.7–8's vomit-covered tables — vivid and deliberately disgusting; do not sanitise. v.10's popular misuse (above) — correct it explicitly if you preach the chapter.
- **Textual variants.** None significant beyond the 28:16 rendering.

## Pericope 14 — Isaiah 29:1–30:33 — Sealed books, smooth words, and the God who waits

**Anchor (ESV).** 29:¹¹ And the vision of all this has become to you like the words of a book that is sealed… ¹³ "Because this people draw near with their mouth and honor me with their lips, while their hearts are far from me…" ¹⁶ Shall the potter be regarded as the clay…? 30:⁸ And now, go, write it before them on a tablet and inscribe it in a book, that it may be for the time to come as a witness forever. ¹⁰ who say to the seers, "Do not see"… ¹⁵ "In returning and rest you shall be saved; in quietness and in trust shall be your strength." But you were unwilling. ¹⁸ Therefore the LORD waits to be gracious to you.

**Headline findings.**

1. **29:11 and 30:8 complete the book's self-referential thread begun at 8:16 — a sealed testimony, an unreadable book, and a written witness "for the time to come".** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Move 4, Vocabulary, Structure. **The book keeps telling you that it is a book, and that its readers are later than its hearers.** This is the textual ground for the overview's "the book anticipates being read after the events it describes", and this sweep reached it independently.

2. **30:15 is the book's most compressed statement of what faith looks like — four nouns, no verbs of achievement: *šûbâ wā-naḥat… hašqēṭ û-biṭḥâ* (returning, rest, quietness, trust) — and it is immediately refused.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Linking Words, Tone. The refusal ("But you were unwilling") is part of the verse's meaning and is regularly cropped out of the quotation.

3. **30:18's "**Therefore** the LORD waits to be gracious to you" is a connector doing extraordinary work: the *therefore* follows a refusal.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Linking Words. Grace is grounded in the very obstinacy that should have ended it.

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 28 has offered a tested stone to people who made a covenant with death. *Necessity:* the alternative to the stone has to be named, and it is **Egypt** (30:1–7; 31:1–3). The unit exists here because a book that has just laid a foundation must show what people build on instead. `[I]` *Implication:* 29–30 are the diagnosis for which 28:16 was already the cure.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** Ariel besieged and blinded (29:1–14, *setumot* at 29:8, 12, 14); the potter and the reversal (29:15–24, *setumot* at 29:21); the Egyptian alliance (30:1–17, *setumot* at 30:5, 11; *petuḥah* at 30:14); the waiting God and the coming rain (30:18–33, *setumot* at 30:18, 26, 33). `[T]`
- **Vocabulary / Hebrew.** **אֲרִיאֵל** (29:1, 2, 7) — the name plays on "lion of God" and "altar hearth" (cf. Ezek 43:15), and 29:2 exploits it: *"she shall be to me like an Ariel"* — the city becomes the altar it houses. *Moderate–high confidence; the double sense is widely recognised.* `[T]` for the repetition; `[I]` for the pun's force.
- **Translations / ancient versions.** **29:13** — MT *"their fear of me is a commandment taught by men"*; the LXX renders it *"in vain do they worship me, teaching as doctrines the commandments of men"*, and **Matthew 15:8–9 / Mark 7:6–7 quote the Greek**. *Category 3.* *High confidence.* `[T]` A sermon on hypocrisy that quotes Jesus quoting Isaiah is quoting the Septuagint.
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §8:16` — the sealed testimony *(high)*. `Internal: answers §6:9–10` — 29:18's *"the deaf shall hear the words of a book, and out of their gloom the eyes of the blind shall see"* is the first instalment of the reversal of the hardening commission *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: planted —` 29:16's potter/clay → **45:9** (*"Woe to him who strives with him who formed him… shall the clay say to him who forms it"*) and **64:8** (*"we are the clay, and you are our potter"*) — **three potter passages, one in each of the book's thirds, and the third turns the image into a prayer** *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: planted —` 30:7's *"Rahab who sits still"* → 51:9's *"was it not you who cut Rahab in pieces?"* *(high)*.
  - **Gen 3 / Deut 32 conceptual → 30:9** "lying children, children unwilling to hear the **torah** of the LORD" — Deut 32 is a live source; the phrase *bānîm* + *torah* is Deuteronomic. `[I]`
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Woe-oracles + oracle of salvation. **Who am I?** — those who say "speak to us smooth things" (30:10). Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: the alternative to trust is always a *specific* alternative with a budget attached (30:6's caravan of treasure to Egypt). Motivation: 30:18.
- **Original audience / FCF.** *They felt:* the reasonableness of an Egyptian alliance. *Candidate FCF:* **preferring an arrangement we can inspect to a promise we can only wait for.** `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** 30:33 — Topheth prepared "for the king", with fire and sulphur. Note its use in later hell-imagery `[S]`; the passage's own referent is Assyria.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Pericope 15 — Isaiah 31:1–33:24 — Egypt is flesh; a king will reign in righteousness

**Anchor (ESV).** 31:¹ Woe to those who go down to Egypt for help… ³ **The Egyptians are man, and not God, and their horses are flesh, and not spirit.** 32:¹ Behold, a king will reign in righteousness… ¹⁵ until the Spirit is poured upon us from on high. 33:¹⁴ "Who among us can dwell with the consuming fire? Who among us can dwell with everlasting burnings?" ¹⁷ Your eyes will behold the king in his beauty. ²² For the LORD is our judge; the LORD is our lawgiver; the LORD is our king; he will save us.

**Headline findings.**

1. **31:3 is the book's sharpest antithesis, and it is built on two Hebrew word-pairs: *ʾādām* / *ʾēl* and *bāśār* / *rûaḥ*.** `[T, high]` "The Egyptians are **man** and not **God**, and their horses are **flesh** and not **spirit**." Surfaced by: Parallels, Vocabulary, Original Languages. The pairing of *bāśār* with *rûaḥ* occurs only twice in the book — here, and at **61:1** where the Spirit rests on the anointed one `[T]`. **The category Egypt lacks is the category the Servant possesses.**

2. **33:14's terrified question — "Who among us can dwell with the consuming fire?" — is chapter 6 asked by the congregation.** `[T, high]` Isaiah cried "Woe is me, for I am lost" before the thrice-holy God; here "the sinners in Zion are afraid; trembling has seized the godless" and ask the same question. Surfaced by: Move 4, Tone, Vocabulary. **And unlike ch. 6, the answer given (33:15–16) is a description of character, not a coal from the altar** — which is precisely the gap the Servant will fill.

3. **32:15's "until the Spirit is poured upon us from on high" is the book's first promise of an outpouring, and it is the hinge that turns the ruin of 32:9–14 into the righteousness of 32:16–20.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Linking Words (*ʿad*, "until"), Structure, Biblical Theology.

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 30 has named Egypt as the alternative to trust and closed with God waiting to be gracious. *Necessity:* the woe-series needs its theological ground stated, and 31:3 states it in one line. The unit then exists to move from *why the alternative fails* (31) to *what the real king looks like* (32:1–8) to *who can survive God's presence* (33:14–16) — the last of which is the question chapters 40–66 exist to answer. `[I]` *Implication:* the *petuḥah*-marked close of the movement is not here but at **35:10**; 31–33 and 34–35 belong together as the movement's conclusion.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** Woe on the Egyptian alliance (31); the righteous king and the complacent women (32:1–20, *setumot* at 31:3, 9; 32:20); the last *hôy* and the answer to the fire (33:1–24, *setumot* at 33:1, 6, 12). `[T]`
- **Repetition.** **בָּטַח** *bāṭaḥ* "trust" saturates this stretch — 30:12, 15; 31:1; 32:9, 10, 11, 17, 18 — eight occurrences in three chapters, out of 23 in the whole book. `[T, high]` **The block is an extended argument about the object of trust**, and it is about to be tested by a siege (see Pericope 17).
- **Vocabulary.** **מוֹקְדֵי עוֹלָם** (33:14) "everlasting burnings" — the phrase occurs only here `[T]`. **חֹקֵק / מְחֹקְקֵנוּ** (33:22) "lawgiver" — a triple divine title (judge, lawgiver, king) that anticipates the collapse of every human office in the book.
- **Tone.** 32:9–14's address to "you women who are at ease" is the second time the book targets complacency through the women of Zion (cf. 3:16–24); the register is warning, not contempt — the same women are included in the Spirit's outpouring at v.15.
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §6:1–7` — 33:14's question *(high)*. `Internal: answers §1:15–17` — 33:15's answer (*"who despises the gain of oppressions, who shakes his hands, lest they hold a bribe"*) is 1:15–17's indictment turned into a portrait *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: planted —` 32:3's *"the eyes of those who see will not be closed, and the ears of those who hear will give attention"* → 35:5; 42:7; 43:8 *(high)*. `Internal: planted —` 32:15's Spirit-outpouring → 44:3 (*"I will pour my Spirit upon your offspring"*) → 59:21; 61:1 *(high)* `[T]`.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Woe-oracles + royal oracle + liturgy of entrance (33:14–16). **Who am I?** — the sinner in Zion asking 33:14's question. Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: the decisive question is not *is this ally strong?* but *is this ally flesh or spirit?* Behaviour — stop: 31:1's *"but do not look to the Holy One of Israel or consult the LORD"* names the actual sin as an omission.
- **Original audience / FCF.** *Candidate FCF:* **wanting help that is visible, and discovering that visibility is exactly what disqualifies it.** `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** 33:14–16 — read as a works-based entrance requirement, this contradicts 1:18. *Rhetorical function:* it is an entrance liturgy, a *description of the citizen*, and the book has not yet said how anyone becomes one. Flag the gap rather than filling it prematurely.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Pericope 16 — Isaiah 34:1–35:10 — Edom's ruin and the Way of Holiness

**Anchor (ESV).** 34:¹ Draw near, O nations, to hear… ⁴ All the host of heaven shall rot away, and the skies roll up like a scroll… ¹⁶ **Seek and read from the book of the LORD**: Not one of these shall be missing. 35:⁵ Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf unstopped; ⁶ then shall the lame man leap like a deer… ⁸ And a highway shall be there, and it shall be called the Way of Holiness. ¹⁰ And the ransomed of the LORD shall return and come to Zion with singing.

**Headline findings.**

1. **34 and 35 are a matched pair — the same landscape, twice, with the vegetation reversed.** `[T, high]` 34:13 *"Thorns shall grow over its strongholds, nettles and thistles in its fortresses. It shall be the haunt of jackals"*; 35:7 *"in the haunt of jackals, where they lie down, the grass shall become reeds and rushes."* Surfaced by: Structure, Repetition, Vocabulary. **The jackals' lair is the hinge word.** Preaching 35 without 34 removes the contrast that gives it force.

2. **34:16's "Seek and read from the book of the LORD" is the fourth and last of the book's self-references.** `[T, high]` With 8:16 (seal the testimony), 29:11–12 (the sealed book), and 30:8 (write it on a tablet for the time to come), the book has now told the reader four times that it is a written document intended to be checked later. Surfaced by: Move 4, Vocabulary, Structure. **ספר occurs fifteen times in Isaiah; the cluster at 29:11–12, 30:8, 33:18 and 34:16 is deliberate.**

3. **35:5–6 is the first full reversal of 6:9–10 in the book — eyes opened, ears unstopped — and it is quoted by Jesus in answer to John the Baptist.** `[T]` for the reversal; `[S]` for the Matt 11:5 / Luke 7:22 use. Surfaced by: Move 4, Biblical Theology.

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 28–33 has demolished false trust and left a congregation asking who can survive God's fire. *Necessity:* the movement must end somewhere, and it ends with a **road**. 34 delivers the verdict on the nations one last time; 35 builds the way home. The *petuḥah* at **35:10** is one of the heaviest in the book, and it closes movement three before the narrative block. `[T]` *Implication:* 35 is not a stray oracle of comfort — it is the destination of everything from ch. 28, and it is the text 40:3 will pick up.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** Universal summons and the sword on Edom (34:1–17, *setumah* at 34:17); the wilderness rejoicing and the highway (35:1–10, *setumah* at 35:2, *petuḥah* at 35:10). `[T]`
- **Vocabulary.** **דֶּרֶךְ הַקֹּדֶשׁ** (35:8) "the Way of Holiness" — the phrase occurs only here `[T]`. **מְסִלָּה** (35:8) "highway" — 11:16; 19:23; 33:8; 35:8; 40:3; 49:11; 59:7; 62:10: **an eight-point whole-book thread, and 33:8 is its low point (*"the highways lie waste; the traveller ceases"*) two chapters before its restoration.** *High confidence — a verified sequence.* `[T]`
- **Tone.** 34 is the book's most physically repellent chapter (stench, blood, sulphur, owls and hyenas); 35 is its most exhilarating. **Preach them in one sitting or the contrast is lost.**
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Gen 19 → 34:9** *(moderate–high)* — "her streams shall be turned into pitch… her land shall become burning pitch" is Sodom's chemistry. Sodom is a **live source** (1:9–10; 3:9; 13:19); this is its fourth use, and its target is now Edom. `[T]`
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §6:10` — 35:5–6 *(high)*. `Internal: answers §33:8` — the ruined highway restored *(high)*. `Internal: planted —` 35:8's Way of Holiness → 40:3; 62:10 *(high)*; 35:10's *"everlasting joy shall be upon their heads… sorrow and sighing shall flee away"* → **51:11, where the line recurs verbatim** *(high)* `[T]` — **a verbatim self-quotation across the book's supposed seam, and one of the strongest single-book arguments in the text.**
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Oracle of judgement + oracle of salvation, as a diptych. **Who am I?** — "the ransomed of the LORD" walking the road (35:10). Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: the road home is *holy* before it is *safe* — "the unclean shall not pass over it" (35:8) precedes "no lion shall be there" (35:9).
- **Difficult verses.** 34:1–17 as *ḥerem*-style judgement language. *Category:* ethical. Note that it is poetry against a symbol of implacable hostility, not a military instruction; and that ch. 35 is what it is *for*.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Pericope 17 — Isaiah 36:1–37:38 — The Rabshakeh's question: on what do you rest your trust? ⭐ HIGH

**Anchor (ESV).** 36:⁴ "Thus says the great king, the king of Assyria: **On what do you rest this trust of yours?** ⁵ … In whom do you now trust, that you have rebelled against me? ⁶ Behold, you are trusting in Egypt, that broken reed of a staff… ⁷ But if you say to me, 'We trust in the LORD our God'… ¹⁵ Do not let Hezekiah make you trust in the LORD…" 37:¹⁶ "O LORD of hosts, God of Israel, enthroned above the cherubim, you are the God, you alone, of all the kingdoms of the earth." ³⁵ "For I will defend this city to save it, for my own sake and for the sake of my servant David."

**Headline findings.**

1. **The verb בָּטַח "trust" occurs **eight times in chapter 36 alone** — more than a third of its twenty-three occurrences in the whole book — and every one is on the lips of the Assyrian.** `[T, high]` (36:4 ×2, 5, 6 ×2, 7, 9, 15.) Surfaced by: Repetition, Vocabulary, Author's Purpose. **The enemy preaches the book's own sermon.** Chapters 28–33 argued at length about the object of trust; the Rabshakeh now puts the argument to Judah in the field, in her own language, and gets it exactly right about Egypt (36:6) and exactly wrong about the LORD.

2. **The Rabshakeh switches languages deliberately (36:11–13) so that the people on the wall will hear.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Narrator's Comment, Historical Background, Tone. He is not negotiating with a delegation; he is preaching to a congregation over the heads of its leaders. **This is the only sustained *counter-sermon* in the book.**

3. **The narrative is the book's structural hinge and it is placed out of strict chronological order** — Hezekiah's illness and the Babylonian embassy (38–39) belong earlier in his reign than the Sennacherib crisis `[S]`, yet stand after it. `[T]` for the order; `[S]` for the chronology. Surfaced by: Structure, Positional Necessity. **The arrangement is the argument: Assyria is answered, and then Babylon is invited.**

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* three movements of poetry have argued that trust in Egypt is flesh, that the Holy One is the only refuge, and that a road home exists. *Necessity:* an argument about trust has to be *tested*, and prose is where testing happens. The narrative exists here to convert the book's central claim from oracle into event — and to do it at the same conduit where Ahaz refused his sign (7:3; 36:2). `[T]` *Implication:* **Ahaz and Hezekiah are staged at the identical location facing the identical question, and they answer differently.** That is the strongest positional finding in the book's first half, and it is carried entirely by a repeated geographical detail.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** The first embassy and speech (36:1–22); Hezekiah's first enquiry and Isaiah's reply (37:1–7); the letter and Hezekiah's prayer (37:8–20); Isaiah's oracle against Sennacherib (37:21–35, *setumot* at 37:32, 35); the deliverance and Sennacherib's death (37:36–38, *setumah* at 37:38). `[T]`
- **Narrator's Comment.** Sparse but decisive: 36:21's *"But they were silent and answered him not a word, for the king's command was, 'Do not answer him.'"* — the narrator supplies the reason, so that silence reads as obedience rather than defeat. `[T]` And 37:36–38's flat report of 185,000 dead and a king murdered at worship in his own god's temple is the narrative's verdict without a word of comment.
- **Vocabulary.** **בָּטַח** ×8 (above). **חֵרֵף / גִּדֵּף** "mock/revile" — Sennacherib's crime is named as *verbal*: he "mocked the living God" (37:4, 17, 23–24). `[T]` The chapter is a contest of speeches, and God answers the speech.
- **Tone.** Political menace with an edge of obscenity (36:12), then the extraordinary quietness of 37:14 — *"Hezekiah received the letter… and read it; and Hezekiah went up to the house of the LORD, and spread it before the LORD."* **A man reading his enemy's letter to God is the tonal centre of the whole book's first half.**
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §7:3` — the conduit *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §22:20–25` — Eliakim and Shebna, whose promotion and fall were oracled in ch. 22, both appear here in office (36:3, 22; 37:2), **Shebna demoted from "over the household" to "secretary" exactly as 22:19 said** *(high)* `[T]` — **a fulfilment the narrative does not flag and most series never notice.** `Internal: answers §10:5–19` — Assyria the rod is punished for the boast of 10:13, and 37:24's boast is nearly the same speech *(high)*. `Internal: answers §31:5` — *"Like birds hovering, so the LORD of hosts will protect Jerusalem"* is delivered at 37:33–35 *(high)*.
  - **2 Kgs 18–19 ‖ Isa 36–37** — a near-verbatim parallel `[S]`; the wording was not supplied to this run. Flagged to Open Questions; a solo dig should compare them, since the differences are where Isaiah's own emphasis shows.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Historical narrative inside a prophetic book. **Copycat:** Hezekiah's spreading the letter before the LORD is a **normative pattern** — the narrator commends it and the outcome vindicates it — but the 185,000 is a **unique event**, not a template for expecting military deliverance. Label both.
- **So what?** Worldview: the taunt "has any of the gods of the nations delivered his land?" (36:18) is answered not by argument but by an event. Behaviour — start: 37:14's practice of putting the threatening document in front of God.
- **Original audience / FCF.** *They felt:* besieged, and told plausibly that their faith was a delusion. *Candidate FCF:* **hearing our faith described accurately by someone who despises it, and having no answer but the God it rests on.** `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** 36:10 — the Rabshakeh claims the LORD sent him. *Category:* apologetic. Note that chapter 10 has already said something like it is true (Assyria *is* the rod), which makes the claim harder, not easier — and that his error is not the claim but the conclusion he draws from it.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Pericope 18 — Isaiah 38:1–39:8 — Fifteen years, a treasury shown, and peace in my days ⭐ HIGH (the hinge)

**Anchor (ESV).** 38:¹ "Set your house in order, for you shall die, you shall not recover." ⁵ "I have heard your prayer; I have seen your tears. Behold, I will add fifteen years to your life." ¹⁷ Behold, it was for my welfare that I had great bitterness; but in love you have delivered my life from the pit of destruction, for you have cast all my sins behind your back. 39:⁶ "Behold, the days are coming, when all that is in your house… shall be carried to Babylon." ⁷ And some of your own sons… shall be **eunuchs** in the palace of the king of Babylon. ⁸ Then Hezekiah said to Isaiah, "The word of the LORD that you have spoken is good." For he thought, "There will be peace and security in my days."

**Headline findings.**

1. **The book's best king ends his section on a shrug about his grandchildren, and the *petuḥah* at 39:8 makes it the heaviest structural break in Isaiah.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Structure, Tone, Narrator's Comment, Positional Necessity. **This is the hinge — and it turns on complacency, not catastrophe.**

2. **39:7's "eunuchs" (סָרִיסִים) is one of only three occurrences of the word in Isaiah, and the other two are 56:3 and 56:4 — where the eunuch is promised "a name better than sons and daughters".** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Move 4, Vocabulary. **The curse pronounced in the last verse of the book's first half is repealed, specifically and by name, seventeen chapters later.** This sweep confirms the overview's finding independently and adds the observation that **39:7 is the last oracle before 40:1** — the comfort begins precisely where the curse landed.

3. **Hezekiah's psalm (38:9–20) is the only first-person poem in the book attributed to someone other than the prophet, and its theology is the book's in miniature: "you have cast all my sins behind your back" (v.17).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Genre, Structure, Vocabulary.

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 36–37 has vindicated trust in the LORD against Assyria. *Necessity:* if the book ended there, the answer to the Rabshakeh would be "trust works, and Jerusalem is safe" — which is the Zion-inviolability doctrine the book spends its second half dismantling. 38–39 exists here to show that **the king who was right about Assyria was careless about Babylon**, so that the deliverance of ch. 37 cannot be generalised into a guarantee. `[I]` *Implication:* the reader arrives at 40:1 knowing that comfort is required because a *delivered* city is still going into exile. **This is why "Comfort, comfort" is not a non-sequitur.**

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** Illness and reprieve (38:1–8, *setumot* at 38:3, 8); the writing of Hezekiah (38:9–20); the two appended notes (38:21–22, out of sequence — a deliberate ragged edge); the embassy and the oracle (39:1–8, *petuḥah* at 39:8). `[T]`
- **Vocabulary.** **סָרִיס** (39:7) — 3× in the book (39:7; 56:3, 4) `[T, high]`. **שָׁלוֹם וֶאֱמֶת** (39:8) "peace and security/truth" — Hezekiah's last words, and the book will spend three panels saying **אֵין שָׁלוֹם לָרְשָׁעִים** (48:22; 57:21). *Moderate–high confidence on the link; the vocabulary is common but the placement is pointed.* `[I]`
- **Narrator's Comment.** 39:8b is the clearest authorial intrusion in the book: *"For he thought, 'There will be peace and security in my days.'"* **The narrator tells us what the king was thinking, and it is not to his credit.** `[T]` Compare Gen 25:34's "So Esau despised his birthright" — a single sentence that reframes an episode.
- **Tone.** 38 is intimate and frightened; 39 is breezy and disastrous. The tonal drop between "I moan like a dove" (38:14) and "he showed them his treasure house" (39:2) is the point.
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: planted —` 39:7 → 56:3–5 *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §22:1–14` — a Jerusalem that celebrates after deliverance instead of repenting; 39:2's *"Hezekiah welcomed them gladly"* is the same posture in a single king *(moderate–high)* `[T]`. `Internal: planted —` 39:6's Babylon → 13:1–14:23; 46–47 *(high)*. `Internal: answers §38:17's "you have cast all my sins behind your back"` → 43:25; 44:22 (*"I have blotted out your transgressions like a cloud"*) *(moderate)*.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Narrative + an embedded individual psalm. **Copycat:** Hezekiah's prayer in illness is a **normative pattern**; the fifteen years and the retreating shadow are **unique events**. His display of the treasury is a **negative example** with a named consequence.
- **So what?** Worldview: the sin that ends the first half of Isaiah is not idolatry or injustice but *showing off*, followed by relief that the bill falls due after one's own death. Behaviour — stop: measuring God's goodness by whether the trouble lands in our lifetime.
- **Original audience / FCF.** *They felt:* the immense relief of a city spared. *Candidate FCF:* **taking a reprieve as a guarantee, and being content with peace that expires with us.** `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** 38:1 vs 38:5 — a divine sentence reversed by prayer. *Category:* doctrinal. Name the options (a conditional announcement; an accommodated anthropomorphism; a genuine contingency) and do not resolve without saying you are choosing.
- **Textual variants.** 38:21–22's placement is disturbed in the parallel at 2 Kgs 20:7–8 `[S]`. *Category 2, minor.* Worth a note only if preaching the chapter closely.

## Pericope 19 — Isaiah 40:1–31 — Comfort, comfort: the word that stands ⭐ HIGH

**Anchor (ESV).** ¹ Comfort, comfort my people, says your God. ² Speak tenderly to Jerusalem… that her warfare is ended, that her iniquity is pardoned… ³ A voice cries: "In the wilderness prepare the way of the LORD"… ⁶ All flesh is grass… ⁸ The grass withers, the flower fades, but the word of our God will stand forever. ⁹ Go on up to a high mountain, O Zion, herald of good news… ²⁷ Why do you say, O Jacob… "My way is hidden from the LORD"? ³¹ but they who wait for the LORD shall renew their strength.

**Headline findings.**

1. **40:3's punctuation is the textbook case of translation-as-interpretation, and the Masoretic accents settle the Hebrew.** `[T, high]` MT reads *qôl qôrēʾ* with a **zaqef qaton** (a disjunctive accent) on *qôrēʾ*, and the parallelism is **בַּמִּדְבָּר ‖ בָּעֲרָבָה** ("in the wilderness ‖ in the desert") — so the Hebrew is *"A voice cries: **In the wilderness** prepare the way."* LXX reads **φωνὴ βοῶντος ἐν τῇ ἐρήμῳ** — *"a voice of one crying **in the wilderness**"* — moving the phrase from the road to the crier. **All four Gospels follow the Greek.** *Category 3 — the NT's own text.* Surfaced by: Translations, Original Languages, Parallels. *High confidence.*

2. **The chapter is built on three unanswered questions and one answered one.** `[T]` "What shall I cry?" (v.6) is answered. "Who has measured the waters…?" (v.12), "To whom then will you liken God?" (v.18, repeated v.25), "Have you not known? Have you not heard?" (v.21, repeated v.28) are rhetorical. Surfaced by: Structure, Repetition, Tone. **The chapter argues by interrogation, not assertion** — which is why it lands on the exhausted rather than lecturing them.

3. **"Comfort" (נחם) has appeared only three times in chapters 1–39 (1:24; 12:1; 22:4) and appears fourteen times from here.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Repetition (book level), Vocabulary. The verb's distribution is a genuine hinge-marker — **and 1:24's occurrence is the *hostile* sense ("I will vent myself on my enemies"), which 57:6 will pick up.** The same root does both jobs.

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 39 has announced Babylon and closed on a king content with peace in his own days. *Necessity:* the comfort is addressed to the generation Hezekiah shrugged about. **40:1's "Comfort" is spoken to the eunuchs of 39:7.** `[T]` (the adjacency) / `[I]` (the addressee identification). *Implication:* the *petuḥah* at 39:8 is the loudest silence in the book, and 40:1 breaks it. Preach 40 as a standalone and it is warm but weightless; preach it after 39:8 and it is grace arriving exactly where the verdict fell.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** The heavenly commission (vv.1–11: three voices — "a voice cries" v.3, "a voice says" v.6, "herald of good news" v.9); the incomparability disputation (vv.12–26); the application to the exhausted (vv.27–31). Masoretic *setumot* at 40:2, 5, 8, 11, 16, 24, 26; *petuḥah* at **40:31** closing the chapter. `[T]` **The seven *setumot* in vv.1–26 are unusually dense — the Masoretes heard this as a sequence of distinct oracular units, not one flowing poem.**
- **Vocabulary / Hebrew.** **נַחֲמוּ נַחֲמוּ** — the doubled imperative (v.1) `[T]`. **צָבָא** (v.2) "warfare/hard service" — the word for military service and for the temple duty roster; "her warfare is ended" carries a note of *discharge*. **כִּפְלַיִם** (v.2) "double" — whether double *punishment* or the *matching* of sin and payment is genuinely contested; flag it. *Moderate confidence.*
- **Parallels.** vv.6–8 is a three-line build with a hinge: grass/flower (twice) against "the word of our God" — the antithesis is the chapter's spine and is quoted at 1 Pet 1:24–25 `[S]`.
- **Translations / ancient versions.** 40:3 (above). Also **40:5**: MT *"all flesh shall see **it** together"* (the glory); LXX *"all flesh shall see **the salvation** (τὸ σωτήριον) of God"* — and **Luke 3:6 quotes the Greek.** *Category 3.* *High confidence.* `[T]`
- **Tone.** Herald's proclamation → the flatness of "all flesh is grass" → the soaring interrogation of vv.12–26 → the tenderness of vv.29–31. **Four registers again, and the last is the pastoral one.**
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Exod / new exodus → 40:3–5** *(high)* — *Move 1:* a way through the wilderness is the exodus itinerary. *Move 2:* Exodus is a **live source** running 4:5 → 10:26 → 12:2 → 19:20 → here; **this is its fifth use and the first in which the wilderness road is *built for a return* rather than remembered from a departure.** `[T]` *What it adds:* the chapter opens the book's central new-exodus argument, which 43:16–19 will make explicit and 52:12 will complete.
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §35:8–10` — the highway and the ransomed returning; **35:10 and 51:11 are verbatim identical**, and 40:3 sits between them *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §6:9–10` — "Have you not heard?… Have you not known?" (vv.21, 28) is the hardening vocabulary turned into an appeal *(moderate–high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §1:2` — "**my people**" (40:1) restores the relational term the book opened by putting in question *(moderate)*.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Prophetic proclamation + disputation. **Who am I?** — Jacob saying "my way is hidden from the LORD" (v.27). Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: the argument of vv.12–26 is not that God is big but that he is *incomparable* — the idol-craftsman passage (vv.19–20) is placed inside the cosmology to make the point by contrast. Motivation: v.31's strength is given to those who *wait*, not those who try.
- **Original audience / FCF.** *They felt:* forgotten by a God whose city had fallen. *Candidate FCF:* **the suspicion that our situation has escaped God's notice** (v.27) — answered by creation, not by reassurance.
- **Difficult verses.** v.2's "double for all her sins". *Category:* doctrinal. Options: excess punishment; the equivalent/matching payment; the folded-over document. Do not preach retributive excess without noting the alternatives.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Pericope 20 — Isaiah 41:1–43:28 — Who declared it? The trial of the gods

**Anchor (ESV).** 41:⁸ But you, Israel, my servant, Jacob, whom I have chosen, the offspring of Abraham, my friend… ²¹ Set forth your case, says the LORD; bring your proofs… ²² Tell us the former things, what they are… 42:¹ Behold my servant, whom I uphold… ¹⁹ **Who is blind but my servant, or deaf as my messenger whom I send?** 43:¹⁰ "You are my witnesses," declares the LORD… ¹⁸ "Remember not the former things… ¹⁹ Behold, I am doing a new thing." ²⁷ Your first father sinned, and your mediators **transgressed against me**.

**Headline findings.**

1. **42:19 is the block's hardest verse and its structural key: the servant God has just commissioned (42:1–4) is immediately identified as blind and deaf.** `[T, high]` *"Who is blind but my servant, or deaf as my messenger whom I send?"* Surfaced by: Structure, Repetition, Move 4, Author's Purpose. **Israel-as-servant is the servant who cannot do the job** — which is why the book must go on to a servant who can (50:5, "the Lord GOD has opened my ear, and I was not rebellious"). Any reading of the servant material that stops at "the servant is Israel" has to account for 42:19.

2. **43:27's "your mediators **transgressed against me**" (פָּשְׁעוּ בִי) is the middle of only three occurrences of the phrase in the book — 1:2, 43:27, 66:24.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Repetition (book level), Move 4. The book's opening charge is restated at its exact centre, inside the trial scene, and will be the last thing said at 66:24.

3. **The "former things / new things" disputation term (רָאשֹׁנוֹת) is confined to 41–48 and then reappears only at 65:16–17 to be cancelled.** `[T, high]` 41:22; 42:9; 43:9, 18; 46:9; 48:3 — then *"the former troubles are forgotten"* (65:16) and *"the former things shall not be remembered"* (65:17). Surfaced by: Repetition, Move 4. **The argument that God can be trusted because he declared the former things ends by declaring the former things forgotten.**

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 40 has asserted God's incomparability against idols and addressed a people who think their way is hidden. *Necessity:* an assertion of incomparability invites the question *prove it* — and 41:21 stages exactly that: a courtroom in which the gods are summoned to produce predictive evidence. The unit exists here because 40:12–26's rhetorical questions have to be converted into a formal contest. `[I]` *Implication:* the Servant passages are introduced **inside a trial**, as God's exhibit, not as a devotional interlude.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** The summons of the coastlands and the stirring from the east (41:1–20, *petuḥot* at 41:16, 20); the trial of the idols (41:21–29, *petuḥah* at 41:29 — **the Masoretes closed the section immediately before 42:1**, so the first Servant passage opens a new paragraph) `[T]`; the Servant and the new song (42:1–17, *petuḥot* at 42:4, 42:9; *setumah* at 42:13, 17); the blind servant (42:18–25, *petuḥah* at 42:25); "fear not" and the new thing (43:1–21, *setumot* at 43:10, 13, 15, 21); the lawsuit resumed (43:22–28, *setumot* at 43:24, 28). `[T]`
- **Repetition.** **רִאשֹׁנוֹת / אַחֲרֹנוֹת** and the "who declared it?" formula — 41:22, 26; 42:9; 43:9, 12; 44:7–8; 45:21; 46:10; 48:3–5. **Nine occurrences of one argument.** `[T]` Also **אַל־תִּירָא** "fear not" — 41:10, 13, 14; 43:1, 5; 44:2 — six times, always to a named addressee.
- **Vocabulary.** **עֶבֶד** — 41:8, 9; 42:1, 19; 43:10, all **singular** and all in this block; the singular runs unbroken to 53:11 `[T, high]`. **תּוֹלַעַת** (41:14) "worm" — one of only two occurrences (the other is 66:24) `[T]`.
- **Translations / ancient versions.** **42:1** — MT has an **anonymous** *hēn ʿabdî*, "Behold my servant"; **the LXX inserts the names**: Ιακωβ ὁ παῖς μου… Ισραηλ ὁ ἐκλεκτός μου. The Greek has decided the identification the Hebrew leaves open. **Matt 12:18 quotes 42:1–4 in a form *without* the names.** *Category 1 interpretive rendering with category 3 consequences.* *High confidence.* `[T]` **This is the single most consequential LXX divergence in the servant material and it belongs in any series on the Servant Songs.** Also **42:4**: MT *"the coastlands wait for his **torah**"*; LXX *"in his **name** the nations shall hope"* — quoted at Matt 12:21. *Category 3.*
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Gen 12–22 (Abraham) → 41:8** *(high)* — *Move 1:* "Abraham my friend" (*ʾōhăbî*, "the one who loved me / whom I loved") invokes the promise-bearer chosen without merit. *Move 2:* Abraham is a **live source** (29:22); this is its second use and the first to ground *servanthood* in Abrahamic election. It runs on to 51:2 and 63:16. `[T]`
  - **Exod 14–15 → 43:16–19** *(high)* — *Move 1:* "who makes a way in the sea, a path in the mighty waters, who brings forth chariot and horse" is the Red Sea, described in the participles of a creed. *Move 2:* Exodus's **sixth** use in the book, and the decisive one: having recited it, God says *"Remember not the former things… behold, I am doing a new thing."* **The book cites the exodus in order to tell Israel to stop looking at it.** `[T, high]` *Move 3:* Exod 14–15 and Gen 1 are already linked (waters divided, dry ground) `[I]`. *What it adds:* the new exodus is not a repetition but a supersession, and the book says so in the same breath as the citation.
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §6:9–10` — the blind-and-deaf servant of 42:18–20 is the hardened people of ch. 6, now given the title *servant* *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §1:2` — 43:27 *(high)*. `Internal: planted —` 41:14's "worm" → 66:24 *(moderate)*; 42:6's "a covenant for the people, a light for the nations" → 49:6, 8 *(high)*.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Prophetic disputation / trial speech (*rîb*) + servant oracle + salvation oracle. **Who am I?** — the witnesses (43:10), and uncomfortably the blind servant (42:19). Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: God's claim rests on a testable record — *he said it before it happened*. Behaviour — start: 43:10's witness-role, which is a legal function before it is an evangelistic one.
- **Original audience / FCF.** *Candidate FCF:* **being asked to trust a God whose last recorded act was letting our city fall.** The trial format meets that head-on. `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** 45:7 (anticipated here) and 42:19's blind servant. Also 43:3–4's "I give Egypt as your ransom" — a hard exchange-image; name it.
- **Textual variants.** None significant beyond 42:1.

## Pericope 21 — Isaiah 44:1–48:22 — Idols made of firewood; a shepherd named Cyrus

**Anchor (ESV).** 44:¹⁶ Half of it he burns in the fire… ¹⁷ And the rest of it he makes into a god… and prays to it and says, "Deliver me, for you are my god!" ²⁸ who says of **Cyrus**, "He is my shepherd"… 45:¹ Thus says the LORD to **his anointed, to Cyrus**… ⁷ I form light and create darkness… ²³ To me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall swear allegiance. 46:¹ Bel bows down, Nebo stoops… their idols are… **a burden to the weary beast**. ³ … you who have been **borne** by me from before your birth, **carried** from the womb. ⁴ I will **carry** and will save. 48:²² "There is no peace," says the LORD, "for the wicked."

**Headline findings.**

1. **46:1–4 is a single sustained pun on two Hebrew roots, and it is the funniest and sharpest thing in the book.** `[T, high]` The verbs **נשא** (lift/bear) and **סבל** (carry a load) are applied first to idols — *"your carried things (נְשֻׂאֹתֵיכֶם) are **loaded** (עֲמוּסוֹת), a burden (מַשָּׂא) to the weary beast"* (46:1) — and then to Israel: *"you who have been **loaded** (הַעֲמֻסִים) from the belly, **carried** (הַנְּשֻׂאִים) from the womb… I will bear (אֶשָּׂא), I will **carry** (אֶסְבֹּל)"* (46:3–4). **עמס occurs exactly twice in the whole book, and both are here.** Surfaced by: Original Languages, Repetition, Structure, Parallels. **Category 1 — translation loss.** *High confidence; verified by sweep.* **You carry your god, or your God carries you.**

2. **That same root סבל reappears only at 53:4 and 53:11 — "he has **borne** our griefs… he shall **bear** their iniquities".** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Move 4, Original Languages. סבל occurs nine times in Isaiah: 9:3; 10:27; 14:25 (the yoke God *removes*), 46:1, 3, 4, 7 (the load God *carries*), 53:4, 11 (the load the Servant *carries*). **A three-stage argument in one root, and it is invisible in English.** *This is the sweep's strongest single lexical finding.*

3. **Cyrus is named twice (44:28; 45:1) and given two titles Israel reserved for its own — "my shepherd" and "his anointed" (מְשִׁיחוֹ).** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Tone, Author's Purpose. **A foreign king is the only non-Israelite in the Hebrew Bible called the LORD's *māšîaḥ*.** The two *hôy*-woes that immediately follow (45:9, 10 — the only woes in chapters 40–66 apart from 55:1) are aimed at people who object.

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 41–43 has staged a trial in which the gods failed to produce predictive evidence. *Necessity:* the verdict requires an exhibit, and the exhibit is a name — **Cyrus**, announced before he acts. The block exists here because the trial's whole logic ("who declared it beforehand?") is worthless without a specific prediction on the record. `[I]` *Implication:* the idol-satire of 44:9–20 is not a digression but the *cross-examination* of the losing party, and 46:1–4 is its verdict delivered as comedy.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** Five sub-units, Masoretically marked: Jacob chosen and the idol-satire (44:1–23, *petuḥot* at 44:5 and **44:23** — a heavy break); Cyrus and the Creator's rights (44:24–45:25, *petuḥot* at **45:13, 45:17**; *setumot* at 45:7, 8, 9, 10); Bel and Nebo (46, *setumot* at 46:2, 4, 7, 11, 13); Babylon dethroned (47, *setumot* at 47:3, 7, 15); the final appeal and departure (48, *petuḥah* at **48:16**, *setumot* at 48:2, 11, **48:22**). `[T]`
- **Vocabulary / Hebrew.** **עמס / סבל / נשא** (above). **מְשִׁיחוֹ** (45:1). **תֹּהוּ** (45:18) — *"he did not create it *tōhû*"*, the Genesis 1:2 word, in the mouth of the Creator defending the point of the world `[T, high]`.
- **Tone.** 44:9–20 is **satire**, and it must be preached as satire: a man fells a tree, cooks his dinner over half of it, roasts his meat, warms himself, says "Aha, I am warm" — and worships the other half. v.20's verdict is devastating and quiet: *"He feeds on ashes."* Do not preach this passage solemnly; the tone is the argument.
- **Translations / ancient versions.** **45:1** LXX retains τῷ χριστῷ μου Κύρῳ — "to my christ, Cyrus" — without embarrassment. `[T]` **45:23** is quoted at **Rom 14:11** and alluded to at **Phil 2:10–11**, where the knee-bowing due to the LORD is rendered to Jesus `[S]`; a Christological datum of the first order, but note it as NT usage, not as this passage's claim.
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Gen 1:2 → 45:18** *(high)* — *Move 1:* *tōhû* is the pre-creation void. *Move 2:* Genesis is a **live source**; this use turns the creation account into an argument about *purpose* — the world was formed **לָשֶׁבֶת**, "to be inhabited". `[T]` *What it adds:* the anti-idol case is grounded not in God's power but in his intention.
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §29:16` — 45:9's *"Woe to him who strives with him who formed him… Shall the clay say to him who forms it, 'What are you making?'"* is the potter image escalated into a woe *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: planted —` 46:1–4's carrying → 53:4, 11 *(high)*. `Internal: answers §13:1–14:23` — ch. 47's dethroned "virgin daughter of Babylon" delivers what 13–14 announced *(high)*. `Internal: planted —` 48:22's refrain → 57:21 *(high)*. `Internal: answers §1:25` — 48:10's *"I have refined you… in the furnace of affliction"* is the smelting of 1:25 reported as accomplished *(moderate–high)* `[T]`.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Disputation + satire + royal oracle + taunt. **Who am I?** — the one who carries his god, or is carried. Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: the difference between an idol and God is directional — you carry the one, the other carries you, "even to your old age and grey hairs" (46:4). Behaviour — stop: 48:4's stiff neck; start: 48:20's "Go out from Babylon".
- **Original audience / FCF.** *They felt:* the plausibility of Babylonian religion, whose gods were visibly magnificent. *Candidate FCF:* **the exhaustion of maintaining what was supposed to sustain us.** 46:1's *"a burden to the weary beast"* is the FCF in one image. `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** **45:7** — "I form light and create darkness, I make well-being and create calamity (רָע)." *Category:* doctrinal + apologetic. *Rhetorical function:* it sits inside the Cyrus oracle, addressed to a Persian dualist context, and its point is monotheistic exclusivity, not a theodicy. Name the options (privative/permissive; calamity as judicial disaster rather than moral evil; deliberate polemic against dualism) and do not resolve without saying so.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Pericope 22 — Isaiah 49:1–50:11 — A light to the nations; the Servant who was not rebellious ⭐ HIGH

**Anchor (ESV).** 49:¹ Listen to me, O coastlands… The LORD called me from the womb… ³ And he said to me, "You are my servant, Israel, in whom I will be glorified." ⁴ But I said, "I have labored in vain…" ⁶ "It is too light a thing that you should be my servant to raise up the tribes of Jacob… I will make you as a light for the nations, that my salvation may reach to the end of the earth." ¹⁴ But Zion said, "The LORD has forsaken me"… ¹⁵ "Can a woman forget her nursing child…?" 50:⁵ The Lord GOD has opened my ear, and I was not rebellious; I turned not backward. ⁶ I gave my back to those who strike…

**Headline findings.**

1. **50:5 is the direct answer to 42:19: the servant who is blind and deaf is replaced by a servant whose ear God opens and who "was not rebellious".** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Move 4, Vocabulary, Structure. *"The Lord GOD has opened my ear, and I was not rebellious (לֹא מָרִיתִי); I turned not backward."* **The verb מרה is the covenant-rebellion word; the servant of ch. 50 is defined precisely by not doing what ch. 1 said the children did.** *This is the sweep's clearest demonstration that the servant material is an argument, not a set of independent poems.*

2. **49:3 calls the servant "Israel" and 49:5–6 sends him *to* Israel — the tension is on the page and the text does not resolve it.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Vocabulary, Linking Words. Preachers must state the tension rather than smooth it: whatever the servant is, he both *is* Israel and *has a mission to* Israel.

3. **The Masoretes closed a section at 49:6 (*setumah*) and at 50:11 (*petuḥah*) — so the text's own markers give 49:1–6 and 50:4–11 as units, the second a chapter longer than the conventional "Third Servant Song".** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Structure. **The delimitation question the overview raised is settled here in the text's favour: 50:10–11 belongs with the song, and it is where the song is *applied*.**

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 48:22 has closed the first panel with "no peace for the wicked", after Babylon has fallen and the exiles have been told to leave. *Necessity:* release from Babylon does not deal with the blindness of 42:19 or the transgression of 43:27. The unit exists here because the political problem has been solved and the covenantal one has not. `[I]` *Implication:* the servant material intensifies exactly where the exile ends — which is why reading 40–55 as "the return from exile" flattens it.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** The servant's call and commission (49:1–6, *setumah* at 49:6); the restoration of Zion (49:7–26, *setumot* at 49:13, 23, 26; *petuḥah* at 49:21); the servant's obedience and its application (50:1–11, *setumah* at 50:3, *petuḥah* at **50:11**). `[T]`
- **Vocabulary / Hebrew.** **מָרָה** (50:5) "rebel" — the Deuteronomic covenant verb; **לִמּוּדִים** (50:4) "those who are taught / disciples" — and the same word appears at **8:16** ("seal the teaching among my **disciples**") and **54:13** ("all your children shall be **taught** by the LORD"). **Three occurrences, and they run from the sealed book to the taught Servant to the taught people.** *High confidence — verified.* `[T]`
- **Parallels.** 49:15's emblematic parallel — a nursing mother, then the surpassing: *"Even these may forget, yet I will not forget you."* The concessive is the point.
- **Tone.** Public commission → private despair ("I have laboured in vain", 49:4) → expansion of the commission → Zion's complaint → maternal tenderness → the Servant's beaten face. **The alternation between the Servant's voice and Zion's is the unit's structure.**
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §42:19` — the opened ear *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §8:16` — *limmudim* *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §40:27` — Zion's *"the LORD has forsaken me"* (49:14) restates Jacob's *"my way is hidden from the LORD"* (40:27), and receives the mother-image as answer *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §5:26, 11:10, 11:12` — 49:22's *"I will raise my signal (נֵס) to the peoples"* completes the banner-reversal: the signal that summoned invaders now summons the nations to *carry Israel's children home* *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: planted —` 50:6's beaten back and spat-upon face → 52:14; 53:3–5 *(high)*.
  - **Exod / new exodus → 49:9–11** *(moderate–high)* — prisoners released, feeding on the roads, no hunger or thirst, mountains made a road: exodus and wilderness provision combined. Exodus is a **live source**. `[T]`
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Servant oracle (first person) + salvation oracle to Zion. **Who am I?** — Zion, complaining (49:14). **Not** the Servant; 50:10's *"Who among you fears the LORD and obeys the voice of **his servant**?"* explicitly distinguishes the hearer from the Servant. *This verse is the passage's own answer to the Who-Am-I question and settles it.* `[T]`
- **So what?** Worldview: the Servant's discouragement (49:4) is recorded and then *answered by an enlargement of the task*, not by relief from it. Motivation: 50:7's flint-set face rests on 50:9's "the Lord GOD helps me".
- **Original audience / FCF.** *They felt:* forsaken and forgotten (49:14). *Candidate FCF:* **the conviction that God has moved on from us** — answered by a mother's inability to forget, and then surpassed. `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** 50:6's voluntary submission to abuse. *Category:* pastoral. In a congregation containing abuse survivors, this must not be preached as a general model of endurance; the Servant's silence is unique and vicarious, and 50:8–9 makes clear he expects vindication, not annihilation.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Pericope 23 — Isaiah 51:1–53:12 — Awake, awake — and the Servant crushed for many ⭐ HIGH

**Anchor (ESV).** 51:² Look to **Abraham** your father and to **Sarah** who bore you… ⁹ Awake, awake, put on strength, O arm of the LORD… Was it not you who cut **Rahab** in pieces…? ¹¹ And the ransomed of the LORD shall return… 52:⁷ How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him who brings good news… ¹³ Behold, my servant shall act wisely; he shall be **high and lifted up**, and shall be exalted. 53:⁴ Surely he has **borne** our griefs and **carried** our sorrows… ⁵ he was **crushed** for our iniquities… ¹⁰ when his soul makes an offering for guilt, he shall see his **offspring**… ¹¹ he shall **bear** their iniquities.

**Headline findings.**

1. **52:13's נָשָׂא does double duty, and both meanings are load-bearing in the same passage.** `[T, high]` At 52:13 the Servant is *"**high and lifted up** (יָרוּם וְנִשָּׂא)"* — the phrase used of God's throne at 6:1 and of God himself at 57:15, and pronounced doomed for every human instance at 2:12–17. At 53:4 and 53:12 the same root means *"he has **borne** our griefs… he **bore** the sin of many"*. Surfaced by: Original Languages, Repetition (book level), Move 4, Parallels. **The Servant is lifted up by bearing.** *This is the sweep's headline finding for the whole book.*

2. **53:10's אָשָׁם is the technical Levitical guilt-offering, applied to a person — and it is one of only two rare cultic-legal borrowings in the book's last third (the other is דְּרוֹר at 61:1).** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Biblical Theology, Move 1. The LXX removes it: **ἐὰν δῶτε περὶ ἁμαρτίας** — *"if **you (pl.)** give an offering for sin"* — transferring the offering from the Servant to the hearers, and rendering *"it pleased the LORD to **crush** him"* as *"the Lord wills to **cleanse** him of his blow"*. **Category 2 — substantive divergence, and the most theologically consequential in the book.** *High confidence on the divergence; the priority question is not settled and the report does not settle it.*

3. **51:11 reproduces 35:10 verbatim.** `[T, high]` *"And the ransomed of the LORD shall return and come to Zion with singing; everlasting joy shall be upon their heads; they shall obtain gladness and joy, and sorrow and sighing shall flee away."* Surfaced by: Repetition, Move 4. **A word-for-word self-quotation across the book's supposed seam** — and one of the strongest text-internal arguments for reading Isaiah as one volume.

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 50:11 has closed with those who kindle their own fire lying down in torment; 49–50 has established a Servant with an opened ear who is struck and not rebellious. *Necessity:* the book has now shown (i) a people who cannot see, (ii) a Servant who can, and (iii) no mechanism connecting them. 52:13–53:12 supplies the mechanism, and it supplies it as **substitution** — the pronouns do the work: *our* griefs, *our* sorrows, *our* transgressions, *our* iniquities, *our* peace, *we* are healed, *we* like sheep, the iniquity of *us all*. `[T]` *Implication:* the passage exists *here* because everything before it has established the need and nothing before it has met it.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** Three "listen to me" appeals (51:1, 4, 7, *setumot* at 51:2, 3, 6, 8); three "awake, awake" cries — to the arm of the LORD (51:9), to Jerusalem (51:17), to Zion (52:1) — **the direction reverses: God is told to wake, then the city is** `[T, high]`; the herald and the departure (52:7–12, *setumot* at 52:10, 12); the Servant (52:13–53:12, framed by *setumot* at **52:12 and 53:12** — **the Masoretic markers isolate exactly the unit every modern edition prints**, the only one of the four Servant passages where the text's own boundaries and scholarly convention coincide). `[T]`
- **Repetition.** The threefold **עוּרִי עוּרִי / הִתְעוֹרְרִי** (51:9, 17; 52:1). The pronoun-drumbeat of 53:4–6. **רַבִּים** "many" — 52:14, 15; 53:11, 12 (×2): five occurrences framing the passage. `[T]`
- **Vocabulary / Hebrew.** **אָשָׁם** (53:10); **דָּכָא** "crush" (53:5, 10) — and the same root at **57:15**, where the High and Lofty One dwells *"with him who is of a contrite (דַּכָּא) and lowly spirit"*. **The God who dwells with the crushed is the God who crushed the Servant.** *High confidence — verified.* `[T]` **סבל / נשא** (see Pericope 21, Headline 2).
- **Translations / ancient versions.** Beyond 53:10: **52:15** MT **יַזֶּה** *"he shall **sprinkle** many nations"* (the priestly *nazah*); LXX **θαυμάσονται** *"many nations shall **marvel**"* — the atonement note vanishes. *Category 2.* **53:4** MT *"our **sicknesses**… our **pains**"*; LXX *"our **sins**"* — and **Matt 8:17 quotes it in the Hebrew sense, against the Greek**. *Category 3, inverted — here the NT sides with the Hebrew.* **53:8** LXX *"in his humiliation his judgement was taken away"* is quoted verbatim at **Acts 8:33**. **53:11** LXX adds **φῶς**, *"to show him **light**"*, supported by 1QIsaᵃ and 1QIsaᵇ `[S]` — *category 2, and the Qumran support is strong*. *High confidence on all; the priority questions are individually unsettled.*
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Gen 12–22 → 51:2** *(high)* — *Move 1:* "Look to Abraham your father and to **Sarah who bore you**; for he was but **one** when I called him." The point is *smallness*: one childless man. *Move 2:* Abraham's third use (29:22; 41:8; here), and this one sets up **54:1's "Sing, O barren one, who did not bear"** three chapters later — **the barren matriarch of 51:2 becomes the barren city of 54:1.** *High confidence; a verified Move 2 → Move 4 chain the overview did not have.* `[T]`
  - **Exod 14–15 / creation-combat → 51:9–10** *(high)* — Rahab, the dragon, the sea dried, the depths made a way. Both live sources, fused in one verse.
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §35:10` — verbatim at 51:11 *(high)*. `Internal: answers §6:1` and `§2:12–17` — 52:13's רָם וְנִשָּׂא *(high)*. `Internal: answers §40:9` — 52:7's מְבַשֵּׂר completes the herald commissioned at 40:9 *(high)*. `Internal: answers §12:2 / Exod 12:11` — 52:12's *"you shall **not** go out in haste"* deliberately reverses the first exodus's *"in haste"* *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: planted —` 53:10's **זֶרַע** → 54:17; 59:21; 65:9 *(high)*.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Servant oracle framed by divine speech (52:13–15; 53:11b–12) with a confession in the middle (53:1–11a). **Who am I?** — decisively **"we"**: the confessing group who "esteemed him not". Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: the passage's grammar is substitutionary before its theology is. Motivation: 53:5's "with his wounds we are healed".
- **Original audience / FCF.** *Candidate FCF:* **"we esteemed him not" — the discovery that what we dismissed was what was carrying us.** `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** 53:10's "it was the will of the LORD to crush him". *Category:* doctrinal + pastoral. Name the LXX divergence (above) so the preacher is not ambushed; note that the Hebrew is unambiguous and that 53:11's "he shall see and be satisfied" is part of the same sentence.
- **Textual variants.** 53:10, 53:11 (above) — both worth knowing before preaching.

## Pericope 24 — Isaiah 54:1–57:21 — Sing, O barren one; come, everyone who thirsts ⭐ HIGH

**Anchor (ESV).** 54:¹ "Sing, O barren one, who did not bear…" ⁹ "This is like the days of **Noah** to me…" ¹⁷ This is the heritage of the **servants** of the LORD… 55:¹ "Come, everyone who thirsts, come to the waters…" ³ I will make with you an everlasting covenant, my steadfast, sure love for **David**. ¹¹ so shall my word be that goes out from my mouth; it shall not return to me empty. 56:⁵ I will give in my house and within my walls a monument and a name better than sons and daughters… ⁷ for my house shall be called a house of prayer for all peoples. 57:¹⁵ For thus says the One who is **high and lifted up**… "I dwell in the high and holy place, and also with him who is of a contrite and lowly spirit." ²¹ "There is no peace," says my God, "for the wicked."

**Headline findings.**

1. **54:17 is the turn: עַבְדֵי יְהוָה, "the *servants* of the LORD" — plural — and the singular עַבְדִּי never returns.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Repetition (book level), Move 4, Structure. The singular ran from 41:8 to 53:11; 53:10 said the Servant would "see **seed**"; and the very next chapter's closing line hands the inheritance to a plural. **The book's own resolution, executed in its morphology, and it happens at exactly the point 53:10 predicted.**

2. **55:1 opens with הוֹי — the funeral-lament particle that has introduced twenty-one woes — used as a *gracious* summons: "Ho, everyone who thirsts!"** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Repetition (book level), Tone, Genre. It is the second-last הוֹי in the book. **The form of the woe is turned inside out.**

3. **56:3–8 repeals two specific exclusions the book itself created: the eunuch of 39:7 and the foreigner of 2:6.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Move 4, Vocabulary. סָרִיס occurs 3× in Isaiah — 39:7, 56:3, 56:4 — and 39:7 is the curse. **The last words before "Comfort, comfort" are answered by name.**

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 53:12 has ended with the Servant bearing the sin of many and making intercession for transgressors. *Necessity:* an atonement that produces no people is incomplete. 54 supplies the people (a barren woman with more children than the married), 55 supplies the invitation, 56 supplies the membership rules — and they are the *widest* in the Old Testament. `[I]` *Implication:* **the eunuch and the foreigner are admitted immediately after the Servant is crushed, and in that order.** The sequence is the argument.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** The barren city (54, *setumot* at 54:8, 10, 17); the free invitation (55, *setumot* at 55:5, 13); the inclusion of outsiders and the failure of watchmen (56, *setumot* at 56:2, 3, 5, 9); the idolatry indictment and the dwelling of the High One (57, *setumot* at 57:14, **57:21**). `[T]` **The *setumah* at 57:21 closes the second nine-chapter panel exactly as 48:22 closed the first.**
- **Vocabulary / Hebrew.** **עֲבָדַי / עַבְדֵי** plural — 54:17; 56:6; 63:17; 65:8, 9, 13, 14, 15; 66:14 `[T, high]`. **דַּכָּא** (57:15) — see Pericope 23. **רָם וְנִשָּׂא** (57:15) — the fourth and last occurrence, and note the counterfeit at **57:7**: Zion's bed set *"on a high and lofty mountain"* uses the same phrase seven verses earlier. **The chapter puts the true and the false exaltation side by side.** *High confidence — verified.* `[T]`
- **Repetition.** נחם — 57:5's *"you who burn with lust (הַנֵּחָמִים) among the oaks"* and 57:6's *"Shall I relent (אֶנָּחֵם) for these things?"* use the same root that opens 40:1. **And אֶנָּחֵם occurs only twice in the book: 1:24 and 57:6.** *High confidence — verified.* `[T]`
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Gen 6–9 (Noah) → 54:9** *(high — explicit)* — *Move 1:* the oath after the flood is God's unilateral, unconditional promise never to repeat it. *Move 2:* Noah's second use; the first (24:5, 18) was the *threat* of un-creation. **The flood is used first as what God will do and then as what he swore never to do again — and the second cancels the first.** `[T, high]` *Move 3:* Gen 9's *bərît ʿôlām* is already in conversation with the creation account `[I]`. *What it adds:* the permanence of the covenant of peace (54:10) rests on an oath the reader can check.
  - **2 Sam 7 → 55:3** *(high)* — *Move 1:* the Davidic covenant is a promise to a dynasty. *Move 2:* David's sixth and final naming in the book (7:2, 13; 9:6; 16:5; 22:9, 22; 29:1; 37:35; 38:5; 55:3 — ten occurrences of the name). *What it adds:* **the covenant is made "with you" plural** — the dynastic promise is transferred to the people. This is the Davidic strand's destination.
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §51:2` — the barren Sarah becomes the barren city *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §39:7` and `§2:6` — eunuch and foreigner *(high)*. `Internal: answers §40:8` — 55:11's word that does not return empty completes 40:8's word that stands for ever; **the two verses bracket the panel** *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §1:29` — 57:5's **oaks** *(high)*. `Internal: answers §1:24` — 57:6's אֶנָּחֵם *(moderate–high)*. `Internal: answers §6:1` — 57:15's רָם וְנִשָּׂא *(high)*.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Salvation oracle + invitation + torah of admission + indictment. **Who am I?** — the barren, the thirsty, the outsider, and (in ch. 57) the idolater. Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: 55:8–9's "my thoughts are not your thoughts" is *not* a general statement about divine inscrutability — its context is v.7's abundant pardon, and it means **God forgives more readily than you would**. Preaching it as mystery inverts it. Behaviour — start: 55:6, "Seek the LORD while he may be found."
- **Original audience / FCF.** *They felt:* ineligible — childless, mutilated, foreign. *Candidate FCF:* **the fear that whatever God is doing, there is a reason we are not in it.** 56:3 puts the fear in direct speech. `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** 57:1–2's "the righteous man perishes… he enters into peace" — a rare OT statement about death as mercy; note it, it is pastorally useful. 55:8–9 (above) — correct the popular misuse.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Pericope 25 — Isaiah 58:1–59:21 — The fast I choose; the arm that brought salvation

**Anchor (ESV).** 58:⁶ "Is not this the fast that I choose: to loose the bonds of wickedness… ⁷ Is it not to share your bread with the hungry…? ⁹ Then you shall call, and the LORD will answer; you shall cry, and he will say, **'Here I am.'**" ¹² you shall be called the repairer of the breach, the restorer of streets to dwell in. 59:² but your iniquities have made a separation between you and your God… ¹⁶ He saw that there was **no man**, and wondered that there was **no one to intercede**; then his own **arm** brought him salvation, and his righteousness upheld him. ¹⁷ He put on righteousness as a breastplate, and a helmet of salvation on his head. ²⁰ "And a Redeemer will come to Zion."

**Headline findings.**

1. **59:16's "no one to intercede" (מַפְגִּיעַ) uses the same rare verb as 53:6 and 53:12 — "the LORD laid on him the iniquity of us all" and "he makes intercession for the transgressors".** `[T, high]` The hiphil of פגע occurs five times in Isaiah (47:3; 53:6, 12; 59:16; 64:4), and three of them are these. Surfaced by: Original Languages, Move 4, Vocabulary. **59:16 says there was no intercessor; ch. 53 has already supplied one.** *A verified lexical link across the panels, and one of the sweep's best findings.*

2. **58:9's "you shall cry, and he will say, 'Here I am' (הִנֵּנִי)" is God returning Isaiah's own answer from 6:8.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Move 4, Vocabulary. The chain runs 6:8 (the prophet says it to God) → 58:9 (God says it to a praying people) → **65:1 (God says it doubled, הִנֵּנִי הִנֵּנִי, to a nation that never asked)**. **The direction of the word reverses across the book.**

3. **58 defines fasting entirely in terms of other people's bodies — bonds, yokes, bread, the homeless poor, the naked, your own flesh — and only then promises light, healing and answered prayer.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Repetition, Linking Words (the *ʾāz* "then" of vv.8, 9, 10, 14 — four times, each conditioning a promise on the preceding practice).

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 57:21's *"no peace for the wicked"* has closed the second panel. *Necessity:* the third panel opens by asking what a restored people actually *do* — and answers with a rebuke of religion that looks devout (58:2's "they seek me daily and delight to know my ways"). The unit exists here because the invitation of 55 and the inclusion of 56 have to be shown at street level. `[I]` *Implication:* 58 is the answer to 1:11–17 in the same terms — **the book's first indictment and its last ethical instruction are the same instruction.**

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** The fast (58:1–14, *setumah* at 58:14); the indictment (59:1–8); the corporate confession, in the first person plural (59:9–15a); the divine warrior (59:15b–21, *setumah* at 59:21). `[T]` **The switch from "they" to "we" at 59:9 is the unit's pivot** — the prophet joins the accused.
- **Linking words.** 59:1–2's **הֵן… כִּי אִם** — *"Behold, the LORD's hand is not shortened… **but** your iniquities have made a separation."* The verse exists to forestall exactly one objection. `[T]`
- **Vocabulary / Hebrew.** **מַפְגִּיעַ** (59:16). **סָמַךְ** "uphold" (59:16) — the verb used of the Servant at **42:1** (*"my servant, whom I uphold"*) `[T]`. **גֹּאֵל** (59:20) "Redeemer" — the *gōʾēl* thread, 26 occurrences in the book, concentrated in 41–63.
- **Tone.** 58 is exasperated and practical; 59:1–8 is forensic; 59:9–15a is one of the OT's great corporate confessions (*"we grope for the wall like the blind… we growl like bears and moan like doves"*); 59:15b–21 is theophany. **Four registers, and the confession is the one preachers skip.**
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §6:8` — הִנֵּנִי *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §53:6, 12` — the intercessor *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §1:11–17` — the fast that God chooses restates the worship God rejects *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §42:1` — סמך *(moderate–high)*. `Internal: planted —` 59:17's armour → 63:1–6, where the same warrior appears with stained garments *(high)*; 59:21's *"my Spirit… and my words… in the mouth of your **seed** and your seed's seed"* → 61:1; 65:9, 23; 66:22 — **the Servant's seed given the Servant's Spirit and words** *(high)* `[T]`.
  - **Rom 11:26 quotes 59:20–21** `[S]`; note it, do not build on it.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Prophetic disputation + confession + theophany. **Who am I?** — the fasters of 58:3 and the confessors of 59:12. Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: unanswered prayer is diagnosed (59:2) rather than mystified. Behaviour — 58:6–7's six infinitives are the most concrete list of instructions in the book.
- **Original audience / FCF.** *They felt:* religiously diligent and divinely ignored (58:3). *Candidate FCF:* **doing everything we know to do and getting no answer.** `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** 58:13–14's sabbath conditions, and the whole chapter's "if… then" structure, read as merit. *Rhetorical function:* these are covenant-relationship terms inside a book that has already established grace (1:18; 43:25; 53); the conditionality is about *reality*, not *earning*. Say so.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Pericope 26 — Isaiah 60:1–62:12 — Arise, shine; the year of the LORD's favour ⭐ HIGH

**Anchor (ESV).** 60:¹ Arise, shine, for your light has come… ³ And nations shall come to your light… ²¹ Your people shall all be righteous… the **branch** of my planting, the work of my hands. 61:¹ The Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me, because the LORD has anointed me… he has **sent me**… ² to proclaim the year of the LORD's favor… ³ that they may be called **oaks of righteousness**, the planting of the LORD. 62:² and you shall be called by a **new name**… ⁶ On your walls, O Jerusalem, I have set watchmen… ¹⁰ Go through, go through the gates; prepare the way for the people; build up, build up the **highway**… lift up a **signal** over the peoples.

**Headline findings.**

1. **61:3's "oaks of righteousness" (אֵילֵי הַצֶּדֶק) redeems the word for the idolatrous oaks of 1:29 and 57:5 — the three occurrences in that sense are the whole arc.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Move 4, Repetition. **The tree of the fertility cult becomes the name of the people God plants.** *Verified by sweep; confirms the overview's v0.1.1 addition and is here reached from the text.*

2. **61:1's שְׁלָחַנִי, "he has sent me", is one of three occurrences of the form — 6:8, 48:16, 61:1.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Move 4. **The anointed herald speaks in the prophet's own commissioning words from ch. 6, with the Spirit and the anointing added.** The book's last prophetic voice is its first one, escalated.

3. **60:21's נֵצֶר "branch" is applied to *the people*, and it is one of only three occurrences — 11:1 (the messianic shoot), 14:19 (the Babylonian king cast out "like a loathed branch"), 60:21 (the people as God's planting).** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Move 4. **The word that names the Messiah and the word that names the discarded tyrant are the same word, and it lands finally on the redeemed community.**

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 59:20 has promised that "a Redeemer will come to Zion", and 59:21 has put God's Spirit and words in the mouth of a seed for ever. *Necessity:* 61:1 is the immediate cash-value of 59:21 — *"The **Spirit** of the Lord GOD is upon me… he has put his **words** in my mouth"* is precisely what 59:21 just promised. The unit exists here because the covenant of 59:21 requires a speaker. `[T]` *Implication:* **61:1 should be read as the fulfilment of 59:21, not as an isolated Servant Song** — and this is a positional finding the fourfold-Servant-Song framework tends to obscure, because 61 is not usually counted among them.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** Zion's light and the nations' tribute (60, *setumah* at 60:22); the anointed herald and the reversal of mourning (61, *setumah* at 61:9); the new name and the highway (62, *setumot* at 62:9, 12). `[T]`
- **Repetition.** **פאר** (glory/beautify) saturates the unit — 60:7, 9, 13, 19, 21; 61:3, 10 — and 61:3's **פְּאֵר** ("a beautiful headdress") instead of ashes puns on the root. `[T, moderate–high]`
- **Vocabulary / Hebrew.** **דְּרוֹר** (61:1) — the **Jubilee release-word of Leviticus 25:10**, one of only two rare cultic-legal borrowings in the book's last third (with אָשָׁם at 53:10) `[T, high]`. **אֵילֵי הַצֶּדֶק** (61:3). **נֵצֶר** (60:21).
- **Translations / ancient versions.** **61:1** — MT ends *"and to the bound, **פְּקַח־קוֹחַ**"* (an opening — of prison, most naturally); LXX renders **καὶ τυφλοῖς ἀνάβλεψιν**, *"and **recovery of sight to the blind**"*. **Luke 4:18 quotes the Greek.** *Category 3.* *High confidence.* `[T]` **Preaching consequence:** the sermon "Jesus read Isaiah 61 in the synagogue" is quoting a form the Hebrew does not have at that clause. Note it; it is not a problem, but a preacher should not be surprised by it. Note also that **Luke stops mid-verse**, before 61:2b's "day of vengeance" `[S]`.
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Lev 25:10 → 61:1** *(high)* — *Move 1:* the Jubilee *dərôr* is a **legal reset**: land returned, debts cancelled, slaves freed, once a generation. *Move 2:* the Levitical cult is a **live source** by way of 53:10's *ʾāšām*; these are the book's only two technical borrowings and they sit eight chapters apart in the same argument — **guilt-offering, then release.** `[T]` *Move 3:* Lev 25 and the exodus are already linked (the Jubilee is grounded in "I am the LORD your God who brought you out of Egypt", Lev 25:38) `[I]`. *What it adds:* the anointed one's programme is not general benevolence but a specified legal act.
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §1:29` — the oaks *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §6:8` — שלחני *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §11:1` and `§14:19` — נצר *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §59:21` — Spirit and words *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §21:6–12 / 52:8 / 56:10` — 62:6's watchmen who "never keep silent" complete the four-stage watchman thread: the one who sees Babylon fall, the ones who see the LORD return, the ones who are blind, the ones who never rest *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §5:26; 11:10, 12; 49:22` — 62:10's **נֵס** and **מְסִלָּה** together, the last occurrence of each *(high)*.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Salvation oracle + anointed-herald oracle + Zion oracle. **Who am I?** — the mourners of 61:2–3, and the watchmen of 62:6 who are told to give God no rest. Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: 61:3's exchanges (a headdress for ashes, oil of gladness for mourning, a garment of praise for a faint spirit) are the book's tidiest statement of reversal. Behaviour — start: 62:6–7's insistent prayer.
- **Original audience / FCF.** *They felt:* small, dark, unnamed. *Candidate FCF:* **needing a new name because the old one is a description of what went wrong** (62:4's Forsaken and Desolate). `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** 60:12 — "the nation and kingdom that will not serve you shall perish". *Category:* ethical/apologetic. It sits inside a passage about nations *streaming in*; note the tension rather than choosing one half.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Pericope 27 — Isaiah 63:1–65:16 — The winepress trodden alone; "Here I am, here I am"

**Anchor (ESV).** 63:³ "I have trodden the winepress alone, and from the peoples no one was with me"… ⁵ I looked, but there was **no one to help**; I was appalled, but there was **no one to uphold**; so my own **arm** brought me salvation, and my **wrath** upheld me. ¹¹ Then he remembered the days of old, of **Moses** and his people… ¹⁶ For you are our Father, though Abraham does not know us… 64:⁸ But now, O LORD, you are our Father; we are the **clay**, and you are our **potter**. 65:¹ I said, "**Here I am, here I am**," to a nation that was not called by my name. ¹³ "Behold, my **servants** shall eat, but you shall be hungry."

**Headline findings.**

1. **63:5 is a near-verbatim reprise of 59:16 with one word changed — and the change is the point.** `[T, high]` 59:16: *"his own arm brought him salvation, and **his righteousness** (צִדְקָתוֹ) upheld him."* 63:5: *"my own arm brought me salvation, and **my wrath** (חֲמָתִי) upheld me."* The rare hitpolel of שמם ("was appalled") occurs only in these two verses in the whole book. Surfaced by: Original Languages, Repetition, Move 4. **The same solitary warrior, once upheld by righteousness and once by wrath.** *Verified by sweep; a first-class finding.*

2. **65:1's doubled הִנֵּנִי הִנֵּנִי completes the chain from 6:8 and 58:9 — and completes it by inverting the addressee.** `[T, high]` Isaiah said it to God; God said it to a praying people (58:9); here God says it twice **"to a nation that was not called by my name"**, that "did not ask" and "did not seek". Surfaced by: Move 4, Vocabulary, Tone.

3. **63:11 names Moses — the only two occurrences in the book that refer to the lawgiver (63:11, 12) — and does so inside a prayer that asks where the exodus God has gone.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Move 1, Move 2. **The book's most sustained Exodus passage is a complaint that the Exodus is not happening.**

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 60–62 has promised light, a herald, a new name and a rebuilt highway. *Necessity:* promises of that magnitude, unfulfilled, produce a specific spiritual crisis — and 63:7–64:12 is that crisis, prayed. The unit exists here because the book will not let its own most glorious chapters stand without the prayer of people who have not yet seen them. `[I]` *Implication:* **63:7–64:12 is the book's largest single prayer, and its position immediately after 60–62 is a pastoral decision.** A series that runs 60–62 straight into 65's new creation removes the honest lament between them.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** The winepress (63:1–6, *setumah* at 63:6); the great prayer (63:7–64:12, *setumah* at 64:11); the divine answer, dividing servants from rebels (65:1–16, *setumot* at 65:7, **65:12** — a *petuḥah*, the last heavy break in the book). `[T]`
- **Vocabulary / Hebrew.** **הִנֵּנִי** (65:1). **חֹמֶר / יֹצֵר** (64:8) — the third potter passage (29:16; 45:9; 64:8), and **the only one in which the clay speaks to the potter in prayer rather than in complaint** `[T, high]`. **אָבִינוּ** "our Father" — 63:16 (×2) and 64:8: **three occurrences, all in this prayer, and the only ones in the book** `[T, high]`. *Verified by sweep.* **The book's only address to God as Father is in its darkest prayer.**
- **Tone.** Blood-spattered theophany (63:1–6) → historical recital (63:7–14) → raw complaint (*"Why, O LORD, do you make us wander from your ways?"*, 63:17) → the extraordinary *"Oh that you would rend the heavens and come down"* (63:19) → confession (64:5–7) → the last line of the prayer, unanswered: *"Will you restrain yourself at these things, O LORD? Will you keep silent, and afflict us so terribly?"* (64:12).
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Exod 14–15; 32–34 → 63:11–14** *(high)* — *Move 1:* the recital names Moses, the sea, the shepherd of the flock, the Spirit "in the midst of them", the guiding right arm, the beast going down into the valley. *Move 2:* **Exodus's final and fullest use in the book** (4:5 → 10:26 → 12:2 → 19:20 → 40:3 → 43:16 → here). Every earlier use was assertion; this one is *interrogation* — "**Where** is he who brought them up out of the sea? **Where** is he who put in the midst of them his Holy Spirit?" (63:11). `[T, high]` *What it adds:* **the book's new-exodus argument ends in a question.** That is a finding the overview's intertextual map did not carry, and it materially affects how 65–66 must be read: as an *answer*.
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §59:16` — the doublet *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §6:8 / 58:9` — הנני *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §29:16 / 45:9` — the potter *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §1:29` — 65:3's *"sacrificing in gardens"* *(high)*. `Internal: answers §1:2` — 65:2's *"I spread out my hands all the day to a rebellious people"* is the reared-children charge in a new image *(moderate–high)*. `Internal: answers §41:8–9; 42:1; 44:1` — 65:9's **בְחִירַי** "my chosen" is **plural**, where 42:1's was singular *(high)* `[T]`.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** Theophany + communal lament + oracle of division. **Who am I?** — the praying community of 63:7–64:12, and then one of the two groups of 65:13–15. Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: the book models complaint addressed *to* God as an act of faith, and places it after its greatest promises rather than before them. Behaviour — start: praying 63:19.
- **Original audience / FCF.** *They felt:* that God had gone quiet after promising everything. *Candidate FCF:* **holding promises we have not seen kept, and saying so to God rather than about him.** `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** 63:1–6's winepress imagery, and **63:17's "Why, O LORD, do you make us wander from your ways and harden our heart?"** *Category:* doctrinal + apologetic. Note that this is the *congregation's* language about 6:9–10, and that the book lets them say it. Do not correct the prayer.
- **Textual variants.** **63:9** — MT (Qere) *"in all their affliction he was afflicted, and the angel of his presence saved them"*; LXX **οὐ πρέσβυς οὐδὲ ἄγγελος, ἀλλʼ αὐτὸς κύριος** — *"**not** an envoy nor an angel, but the Lord himself saved them"*. **The Greek denies the mediation the Hebrew affirms.** *Category 2 — substantive divergence, and there is a Ketiv/Qere issue in the Hebrew as well (לֹא / לוֹ).* *Moderate confidence; genuinely unsettled — present the options and do not resolve.* This is worth knowing before preaching the verse.

## Pericope 28 — Isaiah 65:17–66:24 — New heavens, new earth — and the fire not quenched ⭐ HIGH

**Anchor (ESV).** 65:¹⁷ "For behold, I create new heavens and a new earth, and the **former things** shall not be remembered"… ²⁵ The wolf and the lamb shall graze together… and **dust shall be the serpent's food**. 66:¹ "Heaven is my throne, and the earth is my footstool"… ² "But this is the one to whom I will look: he who is humble and contrite in spirit and **trembles at my word**." ⁸ Shall a land be born in one day? ²¹ And some of them also I will take for **priests and for Levites**. ²³ From new moon to new moon… all flesh shall come to worship before me. ²⁴ "And they shall go out and look on the dead bodies of the men who have **rebelled against me**. For their **worm** shall not die, their fire shall **not be quenched**."

**Headline findings.**

1. **The book's last verse closes four distinct verbal circuits opened in its first chapter, and every one is lexical rather than thematic.** `[T, high]` **פֹּשְׁעִים בִּי** (66:24 ‖ 1:2, only three occurrences with 43:27); **לֹא תִכְבֶּה** ‖ 1:31's **וְאֵין מְכַבֶּה**; **תּוֹלַעְתָּם** ‖ 41:14's **תּוֹלַעַת** (the only two); and the **gardens** of 66:17 ‖ 1:29. Surfaced by: Move 4, Repetition, Vocabulary. **This is the strongest single-composition argument the text itself supplies.**

2. **65:25 re-cites 11:6–9 and adds one clause that is not in the original: "and dust shall be the serpent's food."** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Move 4, Move 1, Vocabulary. The addition is **Genesis 3:14**, and it is the only place in Isaiah the serpent's curse is quoted. **The book's final vision is its own earlier vision with Eden's sentence appended** — the one thing the peaceable kingdom of ch. 11 had left unsaid.

3. **66:2 and 66:5 name the true remnant by a single participle — חָרֵד, "he who trembles at my word" — and these are its only two occurrences in this sense.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Repetition, Move 4. **The book that opened by summoning heaven and earth to hear (1:2) ends by identifying the people who actually did.**

**Positional Necessity Check.**
*Preceding movement:* 63:7–64:12 has prayed *"Oh that you would rend the heavens and come down"* and ended unanswered; 65:1–16 has divided servants from rebels. *Necessity:* the prayer asked for the heavens to be torn open; the answer is that God will make new ones. **65:17's *"behold, I create new heavens"* is the reply to 63:19's *"would that you would rend the heavens"* — same noun, opposite verb.** `[T]` *Implication:* the new creation is not an appendix; it is the answer to the book's largest prayer, and preaching it detached from 63–64 removes the question it answers.

**Tools (proportional).**

- **Structure.** New creation (65:17–25, *setumah* at 65:25); the temple relativised and the true worshipper named (66:1–6, *setumah* at 66:4); Zion in labour (66:7–14, *setumot* at 66:9, 11); fire, gathering, and the last verse (66:15–24). `[T]` The absence of a closing marker after 66:24 is itself notable — **the book does not have a tidy end-mark.**
- **Repetition.** **בָּחַר** (choose) — 66:3's *"These have **chosen** their own ways"* and 66:4's *"I also will **choose**"*, answering 1:29's *"the gardens that you have **chosen**"* and 65:12's *"you **chose** what I did not delight in"*. `[T, high]` Four occurrences of one verb across the book's frame.
- **Vocabulary / Hebrew.** **חָרֵד** (66:2, 5). **שָׁמַיִם חֲדָשִׁים וְאָרֶץ חֲדָשָׁה** (65:17; 66:22). **רָאשֹׁנוֹת** (65:16, 17) — the disputation term of chs 41–48, here cancelled `[T, high]`.
- **Tone.** Exuberant creation-poetry (65:17–25), then the astringent 66:1–6, then obstetric urgency (66:7–9), then maternal comfort (66:10–14 — *"as one whom his mother comforts, so I will comfort you"*), then fire, then the rubbish tip. **The final tonal drop is deliberate and must not be smoothed.**
- **Quotation/Allusion.**
  - **Gen 3:14 → 65:25** *(high)* — *Move 1:* the serpent's curse is the first sentence pronounced after the fall, and it is the only one Genesis does not later mitigate. *Move 2:* Genesis is a **live source** running 45:18 → 51:3 → here; **and 65:17/66:22's new heavens and new earth complete the Gen 1 arc while 65:25 completes the Gen 3 one.** `[T, high]` *Move 3:* Gen 1 and Gen 3 are of course already one narrative `[T]`. *What it adds:* the book's end is not restoration to Sinai but reversal of Eden — which is why the last enemy named is a serpent and the last image is a worm.
  - **Move 4.** `Internal: answers §1:2, 1:29, 1:31` *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §11:6–9` *(high)*. `Internal: answers §41:14` — the worm *(moderate–high)*. `Internal: answers §63:19` — the rent heavens *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §6:1` — 66:1's *"Heaven is my throne"* returns to the throne-vision, now with the temple explicitly relativised (*"what is the house that you would build for me?"*) *(high)* `[T]`. `Internal: answers §56:6–7` — 66:21's *"some of **them** also I will take for priests and for Levites"* takes the foreigner-inclusion of ch. 56 one decisive step further into the priesthood *(high)* `[T]`.
  - **Mark 9:48 quotes 66:24 verbatim in its LXX form** `[S]`.
- **Genre / Timeline / Who am I? / Copycat.** New-creation oracle + prophetic disputation + eschatological vision. **Who am I?** — one of the two groups of 66:5, and the question the book leaves the reader with. Copycat **N/A**.
- **So what?** Worldview: the new creation includes building houses and planting vineyards (65:21) — it is not disembodied. Behaviour — the book's final ethical instruction is a posture: **trembling at his word** (66:2).
- **Original audience / FCF.** *Candidate FCF:* **wanting an ending, and being given one that has two doors.** `[T/I]`
- **Difficult verses.** **66:24** — the final image. *Category:* ethical + pastoral + doctrinal. *Rhetorical function:* it is the closing bracket of an inclusio with 1:31, not a systematic doctrine of hell; but it is also the verse Jesus quotes about Gehenna `[S]`. Handle with both facts in view. Also **65:20**'s "the young man shall die a hundred years old" — a genuinely difficult verse for any eschatology; name the interpretive options (hyperbolic longevity; a millennial reading; poetic idiom for the removal of premature death) rather than resolving quietly.
- **Textual variants.** None significant.

## Cross-Passage Convergent Findings

These emerge only from reading the twenty-eight units in sequence. None is visible from a single pericope, and several are invisible in English.

### 1. נָשָׂא does double duty, and both meanings land on the Servant

The root **נשא** means both *lift up* and *carry a burden*, and Isaiah uses both senses as running threads that converge in one passage.

| Sense | Occurrences worked | Where they converge |
|---|---|---|
| **Lifted up** | 2:12–17 (everything רָם וְנִשָּׂא brought low); 6:1 (the Lord's throne); 57:7 (the counterfeit high place); 57:15 (the Lord again) | **52:13** — *"my servant… shall be **high and lifted up** (יָרוּם וְנִשָּׂא)"* |
| **Bearing** | 46:1, 3, 4 (idols carried / Israel carried) | **53:4, 12** — *"he has **borne** our griefs… he **bore** the sin of many"* |

**The Servant is lifted up by bearing.** *High confidence; verified by sweep.* `[T]` This is the sweep's single most important finding, and it is the sharpest available statement of the overview's "high brought low, low raised high" pulse.

### 2. סָבַל runs a three-stage argument across the whole book

The root occurs nine times: **9:3; 10:27; 14:25** (the *yoke/burden God removes* from Israel) → **46:1, 3, 4, 7** (the *load God carries*, against idols that must be carried) → **53:4, 11** (the *load the Servant carries*). *High confidence; verified.* `[T]` **Three pericopes minimum, and the movement is unmistakable: God takes the burden off, then carries the people, then the Servant carries what the people owe.**

### 3. The new-exodus argument ends in a question, not a triumph

Move 2 tracked Exodus across seven uses: **4:5** (cloud and fire) → **10:26** (the staff over the sea) → **12:2** (the Song of the Sea quoted) → **19:20** (the plague-pattern *reversed for Egypt*) → **40:3** (the road built for a return) → **43:16–19** (cited, then superseded: *"remember not the former things"*) → **63:11–14** (*"**Where** is he who brought them up out of the sea?"*). `[T, high]`

**The book's controlling paradigm ends in interrogation**, and is answered not by a second exodus but by **65:17's new creation**. This materially reframes the standard "40–55 = new exodus" summary and is offered to the overview as a correction (see Book-Overview Tensions).

### 4. The הִנֵּנִי chain reverses direction

**6:8** (the prophet answers God) → **58:9** (God answers a praying people) → **65:1** (God says it **doubled** to a nation *that never asked*). `[T, high]` Three pericopes; the trajectory is from human response to divine initiative, and it terminates at the point of least deserving.

### 5. The intercessor problem is posed after it has been solved

**53:6** (*"the LORD **laid on him** (הִפְגִּיעַ) the iniquity of us all"*) and **53:12** (*"he **makes intercession** (יַפְגִּיעַ) for the transgressors"*) precede **59:16** (*"he wondered that there was **no one to intercede** (מַפְגִּיעַ)"*). Three of the verb's five occurrences. `[T, high]` The reader who has met the Servant hears 59:16 as a statement about everyone *else*.

### 6. The singular Servant becomes the plural servants exactly where 53:10 said he would

**עַבְדִּי** singular runs 41:8 → 53:11 unbroken. 53:10 promises *"he shall see **seed**"*. **54:17** — the next chapter's closing line — has **עַבְדֵי יְהוָה**, plural, and the singular never returns (56:6; 63:17; 65:8, 9, 13, 14, 15; 66:14). `[T, high]` Confirms the overview independently, and adds the observation that **65:9's בְחִירַי "my chosen" is the plural of 42:1's singular בְּחִירִי** — the whole servant-vocabulary pluralises together, not just one word.

### 7. The blind-and-deaf sentence of 6:10 is reversed in instalments, not at a stroke

**6:10** → **29:18** → **32:3** → **35:5–6** → **42:7** → **42:18–20** (the servant himself is blind) → **43:8**. `[T, high]` Seven pericopes. **The reversal is spread deliberately across the whole book**, which is why no single passage can be preached as "the answer to Isaiah 6".

### 8. Four smaller threads, each spanning three or more pericopes

| Thread | Sequence | What the sequence does |
|---|---|---|
| **The book's self-reference** | 8:16 (seal the testimony) → 29:11–12 (the sealed book) → 30:8 (write it for the time to come) → 34:16 (seek and read from the book of the LORD) | The book tells the reader four times that it is a document to be checked later `[T, high]` |
| **The potter** | 29:16 (the clay's absurd complaint) → 45:9 (a woe on the clay) → 64:8 (*"we are the clay, and you are our potter"* — in prayer) | The image travels from rebuke to woe to petition `[T, high]` |
| **The watchman** | 21:6–12 (sees Babylon fall) → 52:8 (sees the LORD return) → 56:10 (blind, silent dogs) → 62:6 (never keep silent) | Four occupants of one office, and the last is the reversal of the third `[T, high]` |
| **The banner and the highway** | נֵס: 5:26 (summons invaders) → 11:10, 12 → 13:2 → 18:3 → 49:22 (summons nations to carry Israel home) → 62:10. מְסִלָּה: 11:16 → 19:23 → 33:8 (ruined) → 35:8 → 40:3 → 49:11 → 62:10 | Both end together at 62:10, and both reverse their first sense `[T, high]` |

### 9. Two verbatim self-quotations across the book's supposed seam

**35:10 = 51:11**, word for word. And **40:8's word that "will stand forever"** is completed by **55:11's word that "shall not return to me empty"**, bracketing the first panel. `[T, high]` **A composition that quotes itself verbatim across 39/40 is not two books stitched together at that point.**

### 10. The Ahaz–Hezekiah staging is carried by one repeated location

**7:3** (Ahaz "at the end of the conduit of the upper pool on the highway to the Washer's Field"), **22:9–11** (frantic waterworks at the lower pool: *"But you did not look to him who did it"*), **36:2** (the Rabshakeh stands "by the conduit of the upper pool on the highway to the Washer's Field" — **the identical phrase as 7:3**). `[T, high]` Three pericopes, one spot, three responses. **The narrative makes its comparison entirely through a survey reference.**

## Headline Findings — whole book

The five claims this sweep would stand behind most firmly, each surfaced by multiple tools across multiple pericopes.

1. **Isaiah's central Christological statement is carried by a Hebrew pun that no translation can render: the Servant is נִשָּׂא — *lifted up* (52:13, with 2:12–17; 6:1; 57:15) and *bearing* (53:4, 12).** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Original Languages, Repetition (book level), Move 4, Parallels, across pericopes 2, 4, 23, 24. **Exaltation and substitution are the same word.**

2. **The book is bound to itself by verbal, not thematic, links — and they cross the 39/40 seam repeatedly and in both directions.** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Repetition, Move 4, across every pericope. The evidence: קְדוֹשׁ יִשְׂרָאֵל 12/13; 35:10 = 51:11 verbatim; 39:7's eunuch repealed at 56:3–5; 6:10's sentence reversed in seven instalments spanning both halves; the נשא, סבל, פגע, נס and מסלה chains. **Whatever a series concludes about composition, it cannot preach these as two books without cutting the wires.**

3. **The book's answer to its own opening indictment is not a return to the exodus but a new creation — and it says so by ending its exodus thread in a question (63:11) and its Genesis thread in a citation of Gen 3:14 (65:25).** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Move 1, Move 2, Move 4, Biblical Theology, across pericopes 19, 20, 27, 28.

4. **The Servant material is an argument with a problem, a diagnosis and a solution, and 42:19 is the diagnosis: "Who is blind but my servant?"** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Structure, Move 4, Vocabulary, across pericopes 20, 22, 23, 24. Israel-as-servant fails; the Servant of 50:5 has an opened ear and "was not rebellious"; 53:10's seed becomes the servants of 54:17. **Preaching 53 without 42:19 removes the reason 53 is necessary.**

5. **Every human office in the book collapses, and the collapses are narrated: Ahaz refuses (7:12), Shebna is demoted exactly as oracled (22:19 → 36:3), Eliakim's peg gives way (22:25), Hezekiah shrugs (39:8), the watchmen go blind (56:10), and "there was no one to intercede" (59:16).** `[T, high]` Surfaced by: Narrator's Comment, Move 4, Structure, across pericopes 5, 10, 17, 18, 24, 25. **The vacancy is the argument.**

## Preaching Pitfalls — whole book

### Pitfall: preaching Isaiah as two books

- **What it looks like:** A series on "Isaiah 1–39: the God who judges" and, months later, "Isaiah 40–66: the God who comforts". Or a sermon on 40:1 that treats it as a fresh start.
- **Why it's wrong:** 1:18 offers cleansing before any judgement is executed; 65:12 pronounces judgement in the last chapters. The title *Holy One of Israel* splits 12/13. 51:11 quotes 35:10 verbatim. 56:3–5 repeals 39:7 by name.
- **The corrective:** Preach 36–39 as the **hinge**, and make the eunuch of 39:7 the person 40:1 is addressed to. State the 12/13 count from the pulpit once; it does more work than an argument.

### Pitfall: "Here am I, send me" as a recruitment text

- **What it looks like:** A missions Sunday on Isaiah 6:8, ending with an appeal.
- **Why it's wrong:** The "here am I" follows atonement (6:6–7) and precedes a commission to *harden* (6:9–13). The chapter is placed fifth, after two chapters of evidence, so that the sentence is just.
- **The corrective:** Preach 6:1–13 whole, and let 6:13's stump — not 6:8 — be the note it ends on.

### Pitfall: preaching Isaiah 53 first, or alone

- **What it looks like:** A standalone Good Friday sermon on 53:4–6, or a "Servant Songs" mini-series that starts at 52:13.
- **Why it's wrong:** The passage is the fourth movement of an argument that begins by calling *Israel* the servant (41:8), then calls that servant blind (42:19), then gives a servant an opened ear (50:5). And it is followed immediately by the servants plural (54:17). Preached alone, it becomes a proof-text rather than a resolution.
- **The corrective:** Four sermons minimum, in order — 42:1–4; 49:1–6; 50:4–11; 52:13–53:12 — with 42:18–20 preached as part of the first.

### Pitfall: cutting 13–23, 34, or 66:24

- **What it looks like:** A series that jumps 12 → 24, or 33 → 36, or ends the final sermon at 66:23.
- **Why it's wrong:** 22:1–14 puts Jerusalem on the nations' docket and is the hinge of the whole block; 19:24–25 calls Egypt "my people"; 34 is what makes 35 legible; 66:24 closes four verbal circuits with chapter 1 and is the verse Jesus quotes.
- **The corrective:** Preach 13–23 as **two** sermons, not eleven or none. Preach 34–35 in one sitting. End the series on 66:24 and say why.

### Pitfall: 55:8–9 preached as divine inscrutability

- **What it looks like:** "God's ways are higher than ours" deployed at a funeral or after a disaster.
- **Why it's wrong:** The context is v.7 — *"let him return to the LORD… for he will **abundantly pardon**"*. The "higher thoughts" are God's readiness to forgive, which is greater than ours. The verse is about mercy, not mystery.
- **The corrective:** Read v.7 with vv.8–9 every time. There are better texts for inscrutability (45:15; 64:12).

### Pitfall: preaching the LXX and the Hebrew as if they were one text

- **What it looks like:** A sermon that quotes Matthew 13 on Isaiah 6:10, or Romans 9:33 on Isaiah 28:16, or Luke 4 on Isaiah 61:1, and then reads the ESV as though it said the same thing.
- **Why it's wrong:** At each of those points the New Testament is quoting the Greek, and the Greek differs materially from the Hebrew. 6:10's imperatives become indicatives; 28:16's "not be in haste" becomes "not be put to shame"; 61:1's "opening to the bound" becomes "recovery of sight to the blind".
- **The corrective:** Name it in one sentence — *"the apostle is quoting the Greek Old Testament here, which reads…"* — and move on. It costs fifteen seconds and prevents a congregant discovering it alone with a study Bible.

## Book-Overview Tensions

Surfaced, not resolved. Five items for the Finalise pass.

### 1. The new-exodus summary needs correcting *(extension, and a real correction)*

The overview's intertextual map says Exodus supplies "the controlling paradigm of 40–55: the return is a **new exodus**, deliberately outdoing the old". The sweep's Move 2 tracking found that **the Exodus thread does not end in 40–55 and does not end in triumph**: its fullest and final use is **63:11–14**, and it is a *question* — *"Where is he who brought them up out of the sea? Where is he who put in the midst of them his Holy Spirit?"* The paradigm is then answered not by a second exodus but by **65:17's new creation**, which is itself the reply to 63:19's *"Oh that you would rend the heavens"*. **The overview should say that the book's exodus argument terminates in interrogation and is answered by new creation.**

### 2. The preaching-units division splits a composition the text binds *(tension)*

The overview's units 3 (5:1–30) and 6 (9:8–10:34) separate material bound by a **five-fold refrain** — *"For all this his anger has not turned away, and his hand is stretched out still"* (5:25; 9:12, 17, 21; 10:4). `[T, high]` The refrain means 5:25–30 and 9:8–10:4 are one composition interrupted by 6:1–9:7. **Either preach them adjacently, or name the refrain in both sermons.** The overview's unit table should carry the note.

### 3. Isaiah 61 has a positional home the overview did not identify *(extension)*

The overview added ch. 61 to the solo-dig list at v0.1.1 on the strength of the oaks and *sent-me* chains. The sweep adds the **positional** finding: **61:1 is the cash-value of 59:21** — *"my Spirit… and my words… in your mouth"* (59:21) is exactly what 61:1 claims. `[T]` **61 is the fulfilment of the covenant two chapters earlier**, and this is obscured by the fourfold-Servant-Song framework, which does not count 61 among them.

### 4. The echo table can be extended by at least a dozen verified rows *(extension)*

Beyond the four added at v0.1.1, this sweep verified: 1:8 → 4:6 (**סֻכָּה**, booth of abandonment → booth of shelter); 1:3 → 11:9 (**ידע**, "Israel does not know" → "the earth full of the knowledge of the LORD"); 5:26 → 49:22 (**נֵס** reversed); 8:16 → 29:11 → 30:8 → 34:16 (the book's self-reference); 22:19 → 36:3 (**Shebna's demotion fulfilled, unflagged by the narrative**); 21:6 → 52:8 → 56:10 → 62:6 (watchmen); 29:16 → 45:9 → 64:8 (potter); 46:1–4 → 53:4, 11 (**סבל**); 53:6, 12 → 59:16 (**פגע**); 59:16 → 63:5 (near-verbatim, righteousness → wrath); 63:19 → 65:17 (rent heavens → new heavens); 51:2 → 54:1 (barren Sarah → barren city); 35:10 = 51:11 (verbatim); 40:8 → 55:11 (the word).

### 5. The microscript's pulse can be stated more sharply *(refinement, not correction)*

The overview names the pulse "high brought low, low raised high". The sweep would state it lexically: **נָשָׂא does both jobs, and the Servant is lifted up by bearing (52:13 with 53:4, 12).** `[T, high]` That is the same claim with the evidence attached.

**Everything else in the overview the sweep touched, it confirmed** — including the 12/13 distribution, the 1↔66 inclusio, the servant/servants shift, the eunuch reversal, the רָם וְנִשָּׂא chain, the oaks chain, and the Masoretic delimitation of the Servant passages (the *petuḥot* at 41:29, 42:4 and 42:9 confirm 42:1–4; the *setumot* at 52:12 and 53:12 confirm 52:13–53:12 exactly).
