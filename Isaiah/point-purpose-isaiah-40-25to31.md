# Point & Purpose: Isaiah 40:25–31

**Source exegesis:** `dig-deeper-isaiah-40-1to31.md` (25 October 2026) — its Headline Findings 3 and 1, Tools 3, 7, 10, 11 and 16, the candidate Fallen Condition Focus, the Christological Reading and the Preaching Pitfalls
**Study text:** NASB95
**Pulpit text:** KJV (Authorised Version)
**Setting:** care home visit — a short talk of about 8–12 minutes
**Date:** 26 October 2026 *(revised the same day: pericope widened from 40:27–31 to 40:25–31; pulpit text changed from the ESV to the KJV)*

## Scope Decision

**Single talk, Isaiah 40:25–31.** The wider unit is better than 27–31, and the text's own markers show why.

- **It is two of Leningrad's paragraphs, whole.** A *setumah* falls after v.24 and another after v.26, and a *petuchah* follows v.31. Verses 25–26 and 27–31 are the chapter's last two paragraphs. `[T — in Leningrad; the paragraph markers are a reading tradition and their manuscript spread has not been checked]`
- **It contains both of the chapter's closing "sayings".** "Saith the Holy One" (v.25, יֹאמַר) is followed at once by "Why sayest thou, O Jacob" (v.27, תֹאמַר). Both are imperfects of אָמַר ("to say"). **God speaks, then Jacob answers him with a complaint.** (Report, Tool 3.)
- **The answer no longer needs a doorway verse.** In the earlier plan v.26 had to be read in to make v.28 work. Now it belongs to the text being preached.

**Reading:** Isaiah 40:25–31 (KJV).

---

## Fallen Condition Focus

**The suspicion that our situation has slipped out of God's notice, and that we have run out of strength to keep trusting him.**

*Anchored in the text:* "My way is hid from the LORD, and my judgment is passed over from my God" (v.27). The paragraph then names exhaustion three times (vv.29–31). `[T]`

*Shared:* the exiles felt forgotten by a God whose city had fallen. Care home residents may feel forgotten by family, by church and by a world that has moved on, and they live with bodies that have run out of strength.

*Differs:* we wait this side of the cross, for the return of the one Isaiah's voice announced. The iniquity of 40:2 has been paid in Christ, not only "served".

---

## Teaching Point

**The Holy One, who has no equal, names every star and never tires; he has not lost sight of his people, and he gives his own strength to those who have none left as they wait upon him.**

- **Subject:** When God's people feel forgotten and worn out, has God lost track of them?
- **Complement:** No. The incomparable Holy One misses nothing in his creation (vv.25–26), does not faint (v.28), and gives power to the faint who wait upon him (vv.29–31).

**Why this TP — the evidence in the report:**

- **"Not one faileth" is the answer to "my way is hid".** In v.26 God "calleth them all by names", and נֶעְדָּר means "is missing". Number + name + call stands in only one other verse of the Hebrew Bible, Ps 147:4. There the God who counts the stars is also the one who "gathereth together the outcasts of Israel" (147:2). (Report, Tool 11.)
- **The tirelessness passes to the tired.** The pairing of יָעֵף ("faint") and יָגַע ("weary") is ordered God (v.28) → the youths (v.30) → those who wait (v.31). v.31 reverses v.28's order, so what is true of God becomes true of them. (Report, Tool 3.)
- **The gift goes to the strengthless.** It is given לְאֵין אוֹנִים ("to them that have no might", v.29) — the same אַיִן ("nothing") the chapter uses of the nations (v.17). The difference is that here God fills the nothing. (Report, Tool 10.)
- **The posture is Isaiah's own.** Jacob, "hid" and "wait" come together only at 8:17 and 40:27–31. In 8:17 the prophet himself waits for the LORD "that hideth his face from the house of Jacob". (Report, Headline 3.)

## Purpose Statement

**To lift tired, often overlooked believers from "God has forgotten me" to waiting upon him in hope.** The lift comes by showing them a Holy One with no equal, who has never lost a single star, never tires, and gives his strength to people who have none. The talk has three aims:

- **For the believer who feels forgotten:** to hear vv.26 and 28 as God's answer to their own sentence (v.27), not as a rebuke.
- **For the one who has nothing left:** to know that the promise is for "them that have no might" (v.29). Weakness is the qualification, not the disqualification.
- **For those who cannot see any use in their days:** to see waiting upon the LORD as an act of faith God honours, not time wasted. The aged Simeon spent his last years "waiting for the consolation of Israel", and was given Christ to hold (Luke 2:25–30).

## Proposed Structure

About 8–12 minutes: three movements of roughly three minutes each, and a short close.

| Movement | Verses | Working title | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | vv.25–26 | **"To whom then will ye liken me?"** — the Holy One who misses nothing | Establish who is speaking before Jacob speaks. Nothing compares with him. He brings out the stars "by number" and calls each "by names", and "not one faileth" — none is missing. **If not one star goes missing, not one of his people does.** |
| **2** | vv.27–28 | **"Why sayest thou, O Jacob?"** — our complaint and his answer | Surface the FCF. God has spoken (v.25); now Jacob speaks, and says the very thing many residents feel: "my way is hid from the LORD". Name it gently. Then give God's reply: "the everlasting God… fainteth not, neither is weary." **He has not stopped attending.** |
| **3** | vv.29–31 | **"He giveth power to the faint"** — strength for those who have none | The response. Even the youths "utterly fall" (v.30): human strength is not the answer at any age. The promise is for "them that have no might" (v.29). "They that wait upon the LORD" (v.31) — who look for him, as Isaiah did in 8:17 — receive his strength. The order of v.28 comes back in v.31: "not be weary… not faint". |
| **Close** | Luke 2:25–30 | **An old man who waited** | The Christ-centred landing. Simeon, old and "waiting for the consolation of Israel", took the child "up in his arms" and said: "Lord, now lettest thou thy servant depart in peace… for mine eyes have seen thy salvation." **Waiting upon the LORD is never wasted, because he comes.** Close by praying v.31 over them. |

**On the close for this audience.** Simeon's words in the KJV are the Nunc Dimittis. Residents brought up on the Book of Common Prayer may know it by heart from Evensong. If they do, the close lands in words they already own; if not, it still stands. `[I]` — check the residents' background before relying on it.

**Hinge sentence** (for the manuscript to sharpen): *"You may feel forgotten; you are not lost. The Holy One who never fainteth gives his power to them that have no might — and he gives it to those who wait upon him."*

## Christ-centred Check (gate)

1. **"Be like X" or "trust the Christ X points to"?** The talk does not say "be like the eagle" or "be like Simeon". Simeon shows what waiting looks like, and the point is the Christ he received. **Passes.**
2. **Is God the subject?** In every movement God is the actor: he has no equal, names the stars, does not faint, gives power. The hearers receive. **Passes.**
3. **Is the good news grounded in saving work?** Yes: 40:2's iniquity paid (in Christ, 53:10), and the God who "comes" (40:9–10) came in Christ (Luke 2). The close carries it. **Passes**, provided the close is not cut for time. **If time runs short, shorten Movement 1, never the close.**

## Keep Honest

- **Not a promise of physical renewal.** "They shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint" must not be preached to people in wheelchairs as if their legs will be restored now. The strength promised is strength to keep trusting and to go on. The text's own picture is of exiles going home, not athletes. (Report: Preaching Pitfalls.)
- **Vv.25–26 are not a lecture on God's attributes.** The report warns against preaching "to whom will ye liken" as abstract philosophy. The question exists to answer v.27. Keep Movement 1 short and pointed towards Jacob.
- **The complaint is answered gently, not scolded.** "Why sayest thou…?" is a rebuke, but in a care home many hearers will be grieving, not grumbling. Name the feeling honestly first, as the text does by quoting it. Then give v.28.
- **"Wait upon" is not resignation.** קָוָה ("wait, hope, look for") is hopeful expectancy, looking for someone who is coming. It is not filling time until the end. For these hearers the distinction is everything.
- **Don't build on "exchange".** The report notes that יַחֲלִיפוּ (KJV "renew") can mean "exchange", but rates it moderate. Leave it at "renew".

## Pulpit-text Note (KJV)

The KJV carries this passage well, and better than the ESV at one point. Its archaic words need explaining at four points where the plan leans on them.

- **"Not one faileth" (v.26).** נֶעְדָּר means "is missing, is lacking". A modern ear hears "fails" as "breaks down", and that loses the hinge of Movement 1. **Say: "not one faileth — not one is missing."**
- **"My judgment is passed over from my God" (v.27).** מִשְׁפָּט here is Jacob's *right*, his case. A modern ear hears "judgment" as condemnation, which turns the complaint upside down. **Say: "my judgment — my cause, my case — is passed over."**
  - *A gain over the ESV:* the KJV uses "judgment" for מִשְׁפָּט at v.14 too ("the path of judgment"). So the KJV hearer can catch what the ESV hides: **Jacob accuses of overlooking his "judgment" the God whom no one ever taught "the path of judgment".** Use it only if time allows.
- **The chiasm is audible.** The KJV renders יָעֵף consistently as "faint" and יָגַע as "weary": v.28 "fainteth not, neither is weary" → v.30 "faint and be weary" → v.31 "not be weary… not faint". **Read v.31 slowly so the reversal is heard.**
- **"Power" and "strength" do not match the Hebrew.** In v.29, "he giveth **power** to the faint" is כֹּחַ, and "he increaseth **strength**" is a different word, עָצְמָה. In v.31, "renew their **strength**" is כֹּחַ again. **The KJV hearer will pair v.29's "strength" with v.31's "strength", but the real repetition is v.29's "power" with v.31's "strength".** If the talk makes the "same strength" point, say: "the power he gives is the strength they renew."
- **Isaiah 8:17 in the KJV:** "I will wait upon the LORD, that hideth his face from the house of Jacob, and I will look for him."
  - Two different Hebrew verbs stand behind "wait upon" and "look for". **The word that matches 40:31's "wait upon" is 8:17's "look for"** (קָוָה).
  - The English happens to sound identical ("wait upon the LORD" in both verses). That is fine to use in a talk. Just don't say it is the same word.
