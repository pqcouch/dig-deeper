# Point & Purpose: Isaiah 40:27–31

**Source exegesis:** `dig-deeper-isaiah-40-1to31.md` (25 October 2026) — its Headline Findings 3 and 1, Tools 3, 10, 11 and 16, the candidate Fallen Condition Focus, the Christological Reading and the Preaching Pitfalls
**Study text:** NASB95
**Pulpit text:** ESV (Anglicised)
**Setting:** care home visit — a short talk of about 8–12 minutes
**Date:** 26 October 2026

---

## Scope Decision

**Single talk, Isaiah 40:27–31.** The five verses are one paragraph: Leningrad closes it with a *petuchah* after v.31, and it opens with the chapter's only quoted human speech (v.27). The paragraph has its own complaint, answer and promise, so it carries a short talk without flattening.

**One overlap to manage.** The answer in v.28 ("Do you not know? Have you not heard?") leans on everything vv.12–26 have said, and v.26 is the nearest and most usable piece of it: God calls every star by name, "not one of them is missing". **Read 40:26–31 aloud, and preach 27–31.** Verse 26 then stands as the doorway sentence. In a care home that is all the cosmology the talk needs.

---

## Fallen Condition Focus

**The suspicion that our situation has slipped out of God's notice, and that we have run out of strength to keep trusting him.**

*Anchored in the text:* Jacob says it aloud: "My way is hidden from the LORD, and the justice due me escapes the notice of my God" (v.27). The paragraph then names exhaustion three times (vv.29–31). `[T]`

*Shared:* the exiles felt forgotten by a God whose city had fallen. Residents of a care home may feel forgotten by family, by church and by a world that has moved on, and they live with bodies that have run out of strength. The concern rhymes almost exactly.

*Differs:* we wait this side of the cross, for the return of the one Isaiah's voice announced. The iniquity of v.2 has been paid in Christ, not only "served".

## Teaching Point

**The everlasting Creator, who never tires and has never lost sight of his people, gives his own strength to those who have none left, as they wait for him.**

- **Subject:** When God's people feel forgotten and worn out, has God lost track of them?
- **Complement:** No. He is the everlasting God who does not grow weary (v.28), and he gives strength to the faint who wait for him (vv.29–31).

**Why this TP — the evidence in the report:**

- **The question and the answer are verbally matched.** The answer to "hidden from the LORD" (v.27) is the God who "does not faint or grow weary" (v.28). The chiasm of יָעֵף ("faint") and יָגַע ("weary") — God (v.28) → the youths (v.30) → the waiters (v.31) — puts God's tirelessness inside the waiters at the end. (Report, Tool 3.)
- **The gift goes to the strengthless.** It is given לְאֵין אוֹנִים ("to him who has no might", v.29), the same אַיִן ("nothing") the chapter has used of the nations (v.17) and the rulers (v.23). The one difference is that here God fills the nothing. (Report, Tool 10.)
- **The posture is Isaiah's own.** Jacob, hidden and waiting come together only at 8:17 and 40:27–31. In 8:17 the prophet himself waits for the LORD "who is hiding his face from the house of Jacob". Israel is given the prophet's posture. (Report, Headline 3.)

---

## Purpose Statement

**To lift tired, often overlooked believers from "God has forgotten me" to waiting on him in hope.** The lift comes by showing them a God who has never lost a single star, never tires, and gives his strength to people who have none. The talk has three aims:

- **For the believer who feels forgotten:** to hear v.28 as God's answer to their own sentence (v.27), not as a rebuke.
- **For the one who has nothing left:** to know that the promise is for the faint, not the fit (v.29). Their weakness is the very qualification, not a disqualification.
- **For those who cannot see any use in their days:** to see waiting on the LORD as an act of faith that God honours, not wasted time. The aged Simeon spent his last years "waiting for the consolation of Israel" and was given Christ to hold (Luke 2:25–30).

## Proposed Structure

**Reading:** Isaiah 40:26–31 (ESV). About 8–12 minutes; three movements of roughly three minutes each, and a short close.

| Movement | Verses | Working title | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | v.27 (with v.26 as the doorway) | **"My way is hidden from the LORD"** — the words we say | Surface the FCF. Let the hearers recognise their own sentence in Jacob's. Then set it against v.26: the God who counts and names every star, so that "not one of them is missing". **If not one star is missing, not one of his people is.** |
| **2** | v.28 | **"Have you not known?"** — the God we forget | The truth. He is the everlasting God (אֱלֹהֵי עוֹלָם, "God of eternity" — a title found only here in the Hebrew Bible). He is Creator of the ends of the earth. He does not faint or grow weary, and his understanding cannot be searched out. **He has not stopped attending.** God answers the complaint by showing who he is. |
| **3** | vv.29–31 | **"He gives power to the faint"** — the strength he gives | The response. Even the youths fall (v.30): human strength is not the answer at any age. The promise is for "him who has no might" (v.29). "They who wait for the LORD" (v.31) are those who hope in him as Isaiah did in 8:17. They receive strength, and the order of v.28 comes back in v.31: what is true of God becomes true of them. |
| **Close** | Luke 2:25–30 | **An old man who waited** | The Christ-centred landing. Simeon, old and waiting "for the consolation of Israel", took the child in his arms and said, "my eyes have seen your salvation." The God of 40:9, "Behold your God!", came in person. **Waiting on the LORD is never wasted, because he comes.** End by praying v.31 over them. |

**Hinge sentence** (for the manuscript to sharpen): *"You may feel forgotten; you are not lost. The God who never tires gives his strength to people who have none — and he gives it to those who wait for him."*

## Christ-centred Check (gate)

1. **"Be like X" or "trust the Christ X points to"?** The talk does not say "be like the eagle" or "be like Simeon". Simeon shows what waiting looks like, and the point is the Christ he received. **Passes.**
2. **Is God the subject?** In every movement God is the actor: he names the stars, does not tire, gives power. The hearers receive. **Passes.**
3. **Is the good news grounded in saving work?** Yes: v.2's iniquity paid (in Christ, 53:10), and the God who "comes" (40:9–10) came in Christ (Luke 2). The close carries it. **Passes**, provided the close is not cut for time. **If time runs short, cut from Movement 1, never from the close.**

---

## Keep Honest

- **Not a promise of physical renewal.** "They shall mount up with wings like eagles; they shall run and not be weary" must not be preached to people in wheelchairs as if their legs will be restored this side of the resurrection. The strength promised is strength to keep trusting and to go on. The text's own picture is of exiles going home, not athletes. (Report: Preaching Pitfalls, the resilience misreading.)
- **The complaint is answered gently, not scolded.** v.27's "Why do you say…?" is a rebuke, but in a care home many hearers will be grieving, not grumbling. Name the feeling honestly first, as the text does by quoting it. Then give v.28.
- **Don't jump from v.27 to v.31.** The eagles mean nothing without v.28. The strength is his before it is theirs.
- **"Wait" is not resignation.** In 8:17 and here, קָוָה ("wait, hope") is hopeful expectancy, looking for someone who is coming. It is not filling time until the end. Make this plain: for these hearers the distinction is everything.
- **Don't build on "exchange".** The report notes that יַחֲלִיפוּ (ESV "renew") can mean "exchange", but rates it moderate. The ESV hearers will hear "renew". Leave it there.

## Pulpit-text Note (ESV)

The ESV carries this paragraph well, with three things to know:

- **The chiasm is audible in the ESV.** It renders יָעֵף consistently as "faint" and יָגַע as "weary": v.28 "does not faint or grow weary" → v.30 "shall faint and be weary" → v.31 "not be weary… not faint". **Read v.31 slowly, so the reversal is heard.**
- **"Power" and "strength" are one word.** The ESV's "gives **power** to the faint" (v.29) and "renew their **strength**" (v.31) both render כֹּחַ ("strength"). The congregation will not hear that the strength renewed is the power given. If the talk makes the point, say it: "the same strength".
- **8:17 in the ESV has two verbs.** If you quote 8:17, it reads "I will wait for the LORD… and I will hope in him". The word that matches 40:31's "wait" is **"hope"**, the second verb. Quote the whole verse and stress "hope in him". Otherwise the link will sound like "wait = wait" and miss that this waiting is hope.

The NASB95's "the justice due me" (v.27) is "my right" in the ESV. Nothing in this plan rests on the difference.
