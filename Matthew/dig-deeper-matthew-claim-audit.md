# Claim Audit: Matthew

**Passages audited:** 22 claims from `Matthew/book-overview-matthew.md` (Draft v0.1.0, 1 October 2026). They are every non-quotation allusion the overview rates above *moderate*, and its three structural or canonical claims. All are listed in the project's `claude/claim-audit-queue.md` (Matthew #1–22).
**Date:** 1 October 2026

**Revised:** 8 October 2026, v1.1. Searches are now stated in words: the original-language words in their own script with a gloss, the edition searched, and the result; word-index numbers, search commands and script names have been removed. No verdict, count, rank or rating has changed (toolkit amendments round 8).
**Purpose:** To test the overview's above-moderate allusions before any of them is handed to a dig or a sermon. Under the standing rule, both triggers had fired: 22 claims were queued, and 19 of them had entered an overview above *moderate* without being audited.
**Primary texts:** NA28 text and apparatus (Logos export); SBLGNT with the MorphGNT index (Matthew, Mark, Luke, and the whole New Testament where a claim reached beyond the Gospels); Swete LXX; Rahlfs–Hanhart exports, including Old Greek and Theodotion Daniel; WLC with `find.py`; BHS apparatus exports for Exodus and Deuteronomy.
**Study text:** NASB95 · **Pulpit text:** none — no sermon is in view.

## Method

The method is the Mark audit's (29 September 2026). There were **three independent auditors**, each a fresh subagent with no access to the overview or to the old Matthew sweep. They were given the claims as proposals to break, not to defend. They split the claims three ways:

- **Torah and structure:** T1–T10.
- **Prophets and the passion:** P1–P6.
- **Writings and the canon:** W1–W6.

Each auditor worked only from the device corpus, and did six things for every claim:

1. Read the Matthew verse in the NA28/SBLGNT, the Synoptic parallel where there was one, and the source in the WLC, in Swete **and** in Rahlfs.
2. Took a frequency baseline for every shared word or phrase, in Swete and in the New Testament.
3. Searched for the strongest rival source.
4. Ran a random-passage sanity test where it was useful.
5. Scored the claim on the Hays criteria.
6. Gave a verdict.

No commentaries and no web sources were used, so "History of Interpretation" is recorded as *not checked* throughout. Every search was accent-insensitive, Hebrew was checked by lemma, and every absence was preceded by a positive control.

**The main session then re-ran the evidence each verdict turns on.** Twenty spot checks were made. **Nineteen reproduced exactly. One auditor's correction was itself corrected:** P1, Tobit's ἀπήγξατο ("he/she hanged himself/herself"), is discussed there.

Abbreviations: OG = Old Greek; Th = Theodotion; MT = the Hebrew (WLC). Swete's numbering is given where it differs.

## Claims Audited

**Torah**

### Claim 1 (T1): Exod 4:19 → Matt 2:20

**Source:** Intertextual Map, Torah; rated *high*. It carries the Moses/exodus typology of Matt 2.
**Claim:** τεθνήκασιν γὰρ οἱ ζητοῦντες τὴν ψυχὴν τοῦ παιδίου ("for those who sought the child's life are dead", 2:20) is Exod 4:19 LXX, τεθνήκασιν γὰρ πάντες οἱ ζητοῦντές σου τὴν ψυχήν ("for all who sought your life are dead").

**Evidence.**

- τεθνήκασι(ν) ("they are dead") occurs 3 times in Swete (Exod 4:19; 1 Sam 4:17; 4 Macc 12:4), and in the New Testament only at Matt 2:20.
- ζητοῦντ- ("seeking") with τὴν ψυχήν ("the life") occurs in 10 Swete verses. Only Exod 4:19 has the verb, γάρ ("for"), "those seeking" and "the life" together.
- The plural, though only Herod has died, is Exodus' plural.

**Strengthened by two new findings:**

- **Rahlfs Exod 4:19 opens with a plus:** μετὰ δὲ τὰς ἡμέρας τὰς πολλὰς ἐκείνας ἐτελεύτησεν ὁ βασιλεὺς Αἰγύπτου ("after those many days the king of Egypt died"). Swete's text lacks it (reproduced). In Rahlfs, therefore, a king's death comes directly before τεθνήκασιν ("they are dead"), which is the order of Matt 2:19–20: Τελευτήσαντος δὲ τοῦ Ἡρῴδου … τεθνήκασιν γάρ ("when Herod died … for they are dead").
- **Exod 2:15:** ἐζήτει ἀνελεῖν Μωυσῆν· ἀνεχώρησεν δὲ Μωυσῆς ("he sought to kill Moses; but Moses withdrew") stands beside Matt 2:13–14, ζητεῖν … ἀπολέσαι … ἀνεχώρησεν ("to seek … to destroy … he withdrew"). ἀνεχώρ- ("withdrew") occurs in 7 Swete verses (reproduced).

**Strongest rival:** 3 Kgdms 11:40 / 12:24c–d (the LXX plus) — Jeroboam in Egypt until Solomon dies. Same plot, no shared wording.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Availability | Strong | Torah, LXX |
| Volume | Strong | Six-word sequence, a rare perfect |
| Recurrence | Strong | Exod 2:15 and the Rahlfs 4:19 plus in the same narrative |
| Thematic Coherence | Strong | A child-killing king, flight, return |
| Historical Plausibility | Strong | — |
| History of Interpretation | Not checked | No commentaries |
| Satisfaction | Strong | Explains the odd plural |

**Verdict:** **Confirmed** (strengthened).

**Book-overview action required:** Keep *high*. Add Exod 2:15 (2:13–14) and the Rahlfs plus at Exod 4:19 to the row. Cite **Rahlfs** for the "king died" sequence: Swete lacks it.

### Claim 2 (T2): ἀνέβη εἰς τὸ ὄρος ("he went up into the mountain") — 5:1 and 14:23

**Source:** Intertextual Map, Torah; rated *moderate–high*.
**Claim:** The phrase evokes Moses' ascents (Exod 19:3; 24:18; 34:4), said to be its only Swete occurrences.

**Evidence.**

- **The count holds for the exact form.** The aorist ἀνέβη εἰς τὸ ὄρος ("he went up into the mountain") occurs 3 times in Swete and in Rahlfs, all Moses at Sinai. But the Hebrew of Exod 19:3 has no "mountain" (וּמֹשֶׁה עָלָה אֶל־הָאֱלֹהִים, "and Moses went up to God"); the Greek supplies it.
- **Any form of ἀναβαίνω ("to go up") + εἰς τὸ ὄρος ("into the mountain")** gives 25 Swete verses, 17 of them in Exodus–Deuteronomy.
- **It is also ordinary Gospel Greek:** Mark 3:13 ἀναβαίνει εἰς τὸ ὄρος ("he goes up into the mountain"); Luke 9:28 ἀνέβη εἰς τὸ ὄρος προσεύξασθαι ("he went up into the mountain to pray").
- **At 14:23** Matthew changes Mark 6:46 ἀπῆλθεν ("he went away") to ἀνέβη ("he went up"). That is a Matthean preference, but the scene is prayer, and nothing Sinaitic follows.
- **At 5:1** Torah follows ("it was said … but I say"), so the setting does the work the words cannot.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Availability | Strong | — |
| Volume | Possible | Common words; only the exact form is rare |
| Recurrence | Possible | Other mountain scenes, but in different wording |
| Thematic Coherence | Strong (5:1) / Weak (14:23) | Torah-giving follows 5:1 only |
| Historical Plausibility | Strong | — |
| History of Interpretation | Not checked | — |
| Satisfaction | Possible | — |

**Verdict:** **Confirmed with nuance** for 5:1 (moderate, carried by the setting). **Needs reframing** for 14:23: Matthean idiom, no Sinai allusion.

**Book-overview action required:** Lower to *moderate*. Drop 14:23 from the row. Restate the count as "the exact aorist form, 3 times in Swete, all of Moses".

### Claim 3 (T3): Gen 4:24 → Matt 18:22

**Source:** Intertextual Map, Torah; rated *high*.
**Claim:** ἑβδομηκοντάκις ἑπτά ("seventy times seven" / "seventy-seven times") is Gen 4:24 LXX. Lamech's limitless vengeance is reversed into limitless forgiveness.

**Evidence.**

- Gen 4:24 is the only occurrence in Swete and in Rahlfs, and Matt 18:22 the only one in the New Testament.
- Gen 4:24 has Matthew's two steps: ἑπτάκις ("seven times"), then ἑβδομηκοντάκις ἑπτά ("seventy times seven"). Peter offers the first and Jesus answers with the second.
- The Q parallel, Luke 17:4, has only ἑπτάκις ("seven times"). The escalation is Matthew's.
- The Hebrew is שִׁבְעִים וְשִׁבְעָה ("seventy and seven", that is 77). "Seventy-seven" is the safer English.

**Rivals:** Lev 25:8–10 (sevens, and ἄφεσις, "release") and Dan 9:24. Neither has the multiplicative adverb. Weak.

### Claim 4 (T4): Gen 37:20 → Matt 21:38

**Source:** Intertextual Map, Torah; rated *moderate–high*.
**Claim:** δεῦτε ἀποκτείνωμεν αὐτόν ("come, let us kill him") quotes the words of Joseph's brothers.

**Evidence.**

- δεῦτε ἀποκτείνωμεν ("come, let us kill") occurs in Swete only at Gen 37:20. ἀποκτείνωμεν ("let us kill") occurs only at Gen 37:20 and 37:26, both in the Joseph story.
- **New:** Gen 37:24, καὶ λαβόντες αὐτόν ("and taking him"), is the only Swete occurrence of that phrase (reproduced), and it lies behind Matt 21:39, καὶ λαβόντες αὐτὸν ἐξέβαλον ("and taking him, they threw him out").
- **New:** Matthew's ἰδόντες τὸν υἱόν ("seeing the son", 21:38), which Mark lacks, matches Gen 37:18, "they saw him … to kill him".

**Nuance:** Mark 12:7 already has δεῦτε ἀποκτείνωμεν αὐτόν ("come, let us kill him"). The echo is **inherited from Mark**. Matthew strengthens it: his "cast out, then killed" order (21:39) follows Gen 37:24, against Mark's "killed, then cast out".

**Rival:** Naboth's vineyard (3 Kgdms 20 Swete = 1 Kgs 21 MT) — a vineyard, an inheritance, a man taken outside and killed. It has the theme without the words.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Availability | Strong | — |
| Volume | Strong | Unique phrase |
| Recurrence | Possible | — |
| Thematic Coherence | Strong | The beloved son killed by "brothers" / tenants |
| Historical Plausibility | Strong | — |
| History of Interpretation | Not checked | — |
| Satisfaction | Possible–Strong | — |

**Verdict:** **Confirmed with nuance.**

**Book-overview action required:** Keep *moderate–high*. Note that it is inherited from Mark and strengthened by Matthew (21:38 ἰδόντες ("seeing"), and the 21:39 order following Gen 37:24). Name Naboth as a thematic rival.

### Claim 5 (T5): Num 27:17 → Matt 9:36

**Source:** Intertextual Map, Torah; rated *moderate–high* for the phrase, *uncertain* for the Joshua link.
**Claim:** ὡσεὶ πρόβατα μὴ ἔχοντα ποιμένα ("like sheep without a shepherd") echoes Num 27:17, ὡσεὶ πρόβατα οἷς οὐκ ἔστιν ποιμήν ("like sheep that have no shepherd"). The successor is named at Num 27:18: Ἰησοῦς ("Joshua").

**Evidence.**

- **Decisive:** ὡσεὶ πρόβατα ("like sheep") occurs in Swete only at Num 27:17 and Ps 79:2 (reproduced).
- **Matthew changed Mark 6:34's ὡς ("as") to ὡσεί ("like")** (reproduced), and the NA28 records no ὡς variant at 9:36. Matthew's edit moves the line towards Numbers.
- The Hebrew of Num 27:17 and of 1 Kgs 22:17 is identical (כַּצֹּאן אֲשֶׁר אֵין־לָהֶם רֹעֶה, "like a flock that has no shepherd"). The case for Numbers therefore rests on the **Greek**.
- **The Joshua link** is stronger than the overview allowed. Num 27:15–23 is the commissioning of a leader who will "go out and come in before them". Matthew follows 9:36 immediately with workers sent into the harvest and the commissioning of the Twelve (9:38; 10:1–5). The same handover recurs at Deut 31:2, 14.

**Rivals:** 2 Chr 18:16 ὡς πρόβατα οἷς οὐκ ἔστιν ποιμήν ("as sheep that have no shepherd"), the same clause with ὡς ("as"); 3 Kgdms 22:17; Ezek 34:5 (the weakest verbally).

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Availability | Strong | — |
| Volume | Strong | ὡσεί ("like") narrows it to Numbers, and it is Matthew's change |
| Recurrence | Possible | Shepherd material at 10:6; 15:24; 26:31, from other sources |
| Thematic Coherence | Strong | A leader to be appointed; a commissioning follows |
| Historical Plausibility | Strong | — |
| History of Interpretation | Not checked | — |
| Satisfaction | Strong (phrase) / Possible (Joshua) | — |

**Verdict:** **Confirmed with nuance.**

**Book-overview action required:** Raise the phrase to *high*, giving the reason: Matthew's ὡσεί ("like") against Mark's ὡς ("as"). Raise the Joshua link from *uncertain* to *moderate*, resting on the commissioning context, not on the name alone.

### Claim 6 (T6): Deut 18:15 → Matt 17:5

**Source:** Intertextual Map, Torah; rated *moderate–high*.
**Claim:** ἀκούετε αὐτοῦ ("hear him") echoes αὐτοῦ ἀκούσεσθε ("you shall hear him"), the prophet like Moses, with Moses present on the mountain.

**Evidence.**

- αὐτοῦ ἀκούσεσθε ("you shall hear him") occurs in Swete only at Deut 13:4 and 18:15. The imperative ἀκούετε αὐτοῦ ("hear him") does not occur in Swete.
- The words already stand in Mark 9:7. Acts 3:22 and 7:37 read Deut 18:15 Christologically, so the New Testament itself makes the link.
- **Matthew's own Sinai additions carry the case** `[T]`:
  - 17:2 ἔλαμψεν τὸ πρόσωπον αὐτοῦ ("his face shone"), which Mark lacks; compare Exod 34:29–30, Moses' face glorified.
  - 17:6 ἐφοβήθησαν σφόδρα ("they were greatly afraid"); compare Exod 34:30 ἐφοβήθησαν ("they were afraid").
  - 17:1 μεθʼ ἡμέρας ἕξ ("after six days"); compare Exod 24:16, the cloud covering Sinai six days, and the call on the seventh.
  - 17:5 ἐπεσκίασεν ("overshadowed"). Its Swete parallels are Exod 40:29 (Swete; MT 40:35), the cloud overshadowing the tent, and Ps 139:8 (reproduced).

**Rival (formal only):** 1 Macc 2:65 αὐτοῦ ἀκούετε ("hear him") — Mattathias naming Simon. It has the same words and a weak theme.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Availability | Strong | — |
| Volume | Possible | Two very common words |
| Recurrence | Strong | Moses present; wording from Exod 24 and 34 |
| Thematic Coherence | Strong | — |
| Historical Plausibility | Strong | — |
| History of Interpretation | Strong within the NT (Acts 3:22; 7:37) | Commentaries not checked |
| Satisfaction | Strong | — |

**Verdict:** **Confirmed with nuance.** The case rests on the Sinai setting, which Matthew strengthens, more than on the two words.

**Book-overview action required:** Keep *moderate–high*. Merge the row with the Exod 34:29–35 row, and add Exod 24:16 and Exod 40:29 Swete. Note that the words come from Mark and the Sinai colouring is Matthew's.

### Claim 7 (T7): Deut 21:6–9 → Matt 27:24–25

**Source:** Intertextual Map, Torah; rated *moderate–high*.
**Claim:** Pilate's hand-washing and "I am innocent of this blood" (27:24), and "his blood on us" (27:25), allude to Deut 21:6–9.

**What holds:**

- Deut 21:6 νίψονται τὰς χεῖρας ("they shall wash their hands").
- Deut 21:7, ἀποκριθέντες ἐροῦσιν … τὸ αἷμα τοῦτο ("answering they shall say … this blood"). τὸ αἷμα τοῦτο ("this blood") occurs in Swete **only** at Deut 21:7 (reproduced).
- Matt 27:25 ἀποκριθεὶς πᾶς ὁ λαὸς εἶπεν ("all the people answered and said").

**What does not:** the LXX of Deut 21:8–9 has αἷμα ἀναίτιον ("guiltless blood"), not ἀθῷος ("innocent"). The link to Matthew's word runs only through the Hebrew, דָּם נָקִי ("innocent blood"; checked in the WLC).

**Closer sources for Pilate's sentence** (all reproduced):

- **Susanna Th 1:46:** Ἀθῷος ἐγὼ ἀπὸ τοῦ αἵματος ταύτης ("I am innocent of this woman's blood"). This is almost Matthew's line.
- **2 Sam 3:28:** Ἀθῷός εἰμι ἐγὼ … ἀπὸ τῶν αἱμάτων ("I am innocent … of the blood").
- **Ps 25:6 and 72:13 (LXX numbering):** washing hands ἐν ἀθῴοις ("in innocence").

**Closer co-texts for 27:25:**

- **Deut 27:25:** a curse on anyone who takes a bribe to strike αἵματος ἀθῴου ("innocent blood"), καὶ ἐροῦσιν πᾶς ὁ λαός ("and all the people shall say"). This is the only Swete verse joining πᾶς ὁ λαός ("all the people") with innocent blood, and it fits Judas' silver (27:3–4) as well. The Hebrew דָּם נָקִי ("innocent blood") is gated at Deut 27:25.
- **Jer 26:15 MT (Swete 33:15):** "innocent blood upon you", at a prophet's trial.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Availability | Strong | — |
| Volume | Possible | Ritual plus τὸ αἷμα τοῦτο ("this blood"), but no ἀθῷος ("innocent") |
| Recurrence | Strong | The blood chain: 23:35; 27:4, 6, 8 |
| Thematic Coherence | Strong | — |
| Historical Plausibility | Possible | A Gentile performing a Deuteronomic rite |
| History of Interpretation | Not checked | — |
| Satisfaction | Possible | — |

**Verdict:** **Needs reframing.** Deut 21:6–9 is the **ritual background** (moderate–high). Pilate's words come from several texts, and Deut 27:25 is the stronger single co-text for 27:25.

**Book-overview action required:** Reframe the row: Deut 21:6–9 as the ritual (moderate–high); Susanna Th 46 and 2 Sam 3:28 as the declaration formula; Deut 27:25 and Jer 26:15 for 27:25. Because Susanna stands outside the Tanak, flag it in the "outside the Tanak" note.

### Claim 9 (T9): Deut 31:1 / 32:45 → Matt 26:1

**Source:** Intertextual Map, Torah; rated *moderate*.
**Claim:** The fifth formula, ἐτέλεσεν … πάντας τοὺς λόγους τούτους ("finished … all these words"), echoes the end of Moses' speeches.

**Evidence.**

- Swete and Rahlfs Deut 31:1: συνετέλεσεν Μωυσῆς λαλῶν πάντας τοὺς λόγους τούτους ("Moses finished speaking all these words").
- The WLC of Deut 31:1 reads וַיֵּלֶךְ ("and he went"). But **the BHS apparatus (31:1 note a–a) records QG וַיְכַל מֹשֶׁה לְדַבֵּר ("and Moses finished speaking")** (reproduced). The "finished" reading has Qumran and Greek support. This is an apparatus feature: it is reported as BHS prints it, and its manuscript spread has not been checked in Logos `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`.
- At 32:45 the relation is the reverse. The WLC has "all these words" (וַיְכַל … אֶת־כָּל־הַדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה, "and he finished … all these words"); Swete (ἐξετέλεσεν, "finished") and Rahlfs (συνετέλεσεν, "finished") lack them.

**Baselines:**

- πάντας τοὺς λόγους τούτους ("all these words") occurs in 25 Swete verses, so the phrase alone is weak.
- "Finish / cease speaking" formulae are many. For Moses: Num 16:31; Exod 34:33; Deut 31:1, 24. For others: Gen 49:33 (Jacob); 1 Kgs 8:54; Jer 43:1 MT.
- Matthew's simplex ἐτέλεσεν ("finished") is used in the New Testament only in the five formulae `[T]`.

**Thematic support:** after Deut 31:1–2 comes Moses' approaching death; after Matt 26:1 comes the passion announced (26:2). Only 26:1 has πάντας ("all").

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Availability | Strong | — |
| Volume | Possible | The phrase is common; the compound verb is a synonym |
| Recurrence | Strong | The formula comes five times |
| Thematic Coherence | Strong | Farewell, then death |
| Historical Plausibility | Strong | — |
| History of Interpretation | Not checked | — |
| Satisfaction | Possible–Strong | — |

**Verdict:** **Confirmed with nuance.** For 26:1 only.

**Book-overview action required:** Raise from *moderate* to *moderate–high* for 26:1. Cite Deut 31:1 LXX with the BHS QG note (tagged apparatus-unchecked), and Deut 32:45 MT. Add Gen 49:33 (Jacob finishing his charge to his twelve sons) as a candidate for 11:1 (see the new candidates below).

### Claim 10 (T10): The five discourses as five books of Moses

**Source:** Structural Arc Map, contested readings; `[I]`, *moderate*.
**Claim:** The five formula-closed discourses evoke the Pentateuch and cast Jesus as a new Moses.

**What the text supports:**

- The fifth formula's Deuteronomic resonance (Claim 9).
- The Sinai colouring of 5:1 (Claim 2).
- The Genesis opening, βίβλος γενέσεως ("book of the genesis").

**What it does not support:**

- The formula is not Mosaic. ἐτέλεσεν ("finished") is not in the LXX at all. Καὶ ἐγένετο ὅτε ("and it happened when") occurs 12 times in Swete, never in the Torah.
- The first instance has a Q precursor (Luke 7:1 ἐπειδὴ ἐπλήρωσεν πάντα τὰ ῥήματα αὐτοῦ, "when he had completed all his words").
- The discourses do not map onto Genesis–Deuteronomy. 5–7 is Sinai, not Genesis; 10 and 18 have no Leviticus or Numbers wording; only 24–25 → Deuteronomy's farewell works.
- The count of five depends on how 23–25 are divided.
- Fivefold groupings were common (the Psalter, 1 Enoch).
- Moses is named 7 times in Matthew, never in connection with the discourses.

**Verdict:** **Needs reframing.**

**Book-overview action required:** Restate: *low* as "five books of Moses". *Moderate* as "Moses-like teaching, framed by a Sinai-coloured opening (5:1) and a Deuteronomy-coloured close (26:1)". Keep it `[I]`.

**Prophets and the passion**

### Claim 11 (P1): 2 Sam 17:23 → Matt 27:5

**Source:** Intertextual Map, Former Prophets; rated *moderate–high*.
**Claim:** ἀπήγξατο ("he hanged himself") echoes Ahithophel, David's counsellor who went over to his enemy. It is said to occur in Swete only at 2 Sam 17:23 and Tob 3:10.

**Evidence.**

- 2 Sam 17:23 (Swete and Rahlfs, checked in the second book of the Rahlfs Samuel file): ἀπῆλθεν εἰς τὸν οἶκον αὐτοῦ … καὶ ἀπήγξατο καὶ ἀπέθανεν ("he went away to his house … and hanged himself and died"). Matt 27:5: καὶ ἀπελθὼν ἀπήγξατο ("and going away he hanged himself").
- In the New Testament, ἀπάγχομαι ("to hang oneself") occurs only at Matt 27:5 (index lemma ἀπάγχομαι).
- The Hebrew חָנַק ("to strangle") occurs only at 2 Sam 17:23 and Nah 2:13 (a lion). It is the Hebrew Bible's only self-hanging.
- **The overview's statement stands, and the auditor's correction is itself corrected.** The auditor reported that Tobit has only the infinitive, so the finite ἀπήγξατο ("he/she hanged himself/herself") is unique to 2 Sam 17:23. The main-session check found that **Sinaiticus Tobit 3:10 does have ἀπήγξατο**, in reported speech: Sarah imagines people saying of her, αὐτὴ ἀπήγξατο ("she hanged herself"). She then decides against it. Tobit BA has only the infinitive. So ἀπήγξατο ("he/she hanged himself/herself") occurs in Swete at 2 Sam 17:23 and Tobit (S) 3:10, and **only 2 Sam 17:23 narrates a hanging that happened.**
