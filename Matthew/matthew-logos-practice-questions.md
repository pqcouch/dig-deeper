# Matthew: Practice Questions for the Logos Research Assistant

**Purpose:** Open questions from the Matthew work (book overview v0.2, the whole-book sweep, claim audits 1 and 2, and the assessment of the first Logos answers), written for the Research Assistant. They follow `logos-research-assistant-guidelines` in the Dig Deeper folder.
**Use:** Ask one question per turn. Set the scope to a *Matthew commentaries* collection unless the question says otherwise. Paste each answer back with its footnotes, under the question as asked.
**Date:** 2 October 2026

---

## How the List Is Arranged

The questions are grouped by the template they practise. Within each group, the first question matters most to the work.

Each question has three parts:

- **Ask:** the wording to type.
- **Why:** the open point it settles, and where the answer would be used.
- **Watch for:** the check from the "Reading the Answer" section of the guidelines that is most likely to matter.

Questions about counts, word chains or manuscript witnesses are left out on purpose. The corpus has already answered those.

## 1. A Named Commentator's View

**1.1 Matthew 27:9–10, "Jeremiah"**

- **Ask:** What does Davies and Allison (ICC) say about why Matthew 27:9 attributes the quotation to Jeremiah, and which passages of Jeremiah do they think are in view?
- **Why:** The first round returned only popular works. The corpus now points to Jeremiah 19 (innocent blood; the potter's vessel; burial "for lack of a place", the last in the Hebrew only). Does a major commentary agree?
- **Watch for:** whether Jeremiah 19, or only Jeremiah 18 and 32, is named.
- **Then ask the same question of:** France (NICNT) and Hagner (WBC).

**1.2 Matthew 23:35, the canon-span**

- **Ask:** What does France (NICNT) say about whether "from the blood of Abel to the blood of Zechariah" in Matthew 23:35 refers to the first and last books of the Hebrew canon?
- **Why:** The canon-span reading is rated low–moderate, and Dunbar is so far the only supporting source.
- **Watch for:** an argument that rests only on the Baba Bathra order of the books.

**1.3 Matthew 28:18–20 and 2 Chronicles 36:23**

- **Ask:** Apart from Beale, which commentators on Matthew discuss a link between Matthew 28:18–20 and 2 Chronicles 36:23, and does any of them reject it?
- **Why:** The audit proposes raising the link from low–moderate to moderate. A second scholar, or one who rejects it, would settle the rating.
- **Watch for:** inflating words such as "structurally intentional".

## 2. The State of a Debate

**2.1 Matthew 16:18–19, the keys**

- **Ask:** What are the main explanations of "the keys of the kingdom" in Matthew 16:19, who holds each, and do any of them connect it with Isaiah 22:22?
- **Why:** The sweep found the "key" in the Hebrew of Isaiah 22:22 only. The passage also sits under Trap 5.
- **Watch for:** answers that settle the Petrine-office question without textual argument.

**2.2 Matthew 27:51–53, the raised saints**

- **Ask:** What are the main ways commentators understand the raising of the saints in Matthew 27:51–53, who holds each view, and what evidence do they give?
- **Why:** This is an open question in the sweep. Unit 54 is a ⭐ unit.
- **Watch for:** uncited sentences.

**2.3 Matthew 25:31–46, "the least of these my brothers"**

- **Ask:** What are the main views on who "the least of these my brothers" are in Matthew 25:40, who holds each, and what evidence from Matthew itself do they use?
- **Why:** Trap 7. The sweep added the ξένος ("stranger") chain to 27:7, but the question of identity is still open.
- **Watch for:** whether the answer argues from Matthew's own wording (10:42; 18:6, 10; 28:10) or from theology alone.

**2.4 Matthew 2:23, "He shall be called a Nazarene"**

- **Ask:** What are the main explanations of the fulfilment formula "He shall be called a Nazarene" in Matthew 2:23, and which Old Testament texts does each appeal to?
- **Why:** The sweep found Ναζωραῖος ("Nazarene") only at 2:23 and 26:71. The source of the formula itself has not been examined.
- **Watch for:** answers that reason from the English word "Nazarene".

## 3. Has Anyone Seen This Allusion?

Each of these fills in the "history of interpretation" criterion, which the audits recorded as *not checked*.

**3.1 Matthew 8:23–27 and Jonah 1**

- **Ask:** Which commentators discuss a link between the storm in Matthew 8:23–27 and Jonah 1:4–6, what wording do they point to, and does anyone reject it?
- **Why:** The audit raised this to moderate–high. The phrase "great … in the sea" and the form ἐκάθευδεν ("was sleeping") are in Matthew's own changes to Mark.

**3.2 Matthew 22:1–14 and Zephaniah 1:7–8**

- **Ask:** Do any commentators connect the wedding feast and the man without a wedding garment in Matthew 22:1–14 with Zephaniah 1:7–8?
- **Why:** The audit raised this to moderate–high. The phrase "clothed in a garment" occurs in Swete only at Zephaniah 1:8.

**3.3 Matthew 12:20, "to victory"**

- **Ask:** How do commentators explain "until He leads justice to victory" in Matthew 12:20, where Isaiah 42:3 has "to truth", and does anyone mention Habakkuk 1:4?
- **Why:** The audit rated Habakkuk 1:4 moderate, on the strength of the Hebrew לָנֶצַח ("for ever"), which the Greek translators render εἰς νῖκος ("to victory").
- **Watch for:** an explanation that rests only on the English.

**3.4 Matthew 21:12–20 and Hosea 9**

- **Ask:** Which commentators connect the cleansing of the temple and the cursing of the fig tree in Matthew 21:12–20 with Hosea 9:10–17, and what do they make of the link?
- **Why:** This is new candidate N8, and the Mark audit found the same link for Mark 11.

**3.5 Matthew 28:3 and Daniel 7:9**

- **Ask:** Do commentators connect the angel's clothing "white as snow" in Matthew 28:3 with the Ancient of Days in Daniel 7:9?
- **Why:** The audit rated this moderate–high. The wording matches Theodotion's Greek of Daniel and is Matthew's own change to Mark.

**3.6 Matthew 27:24 and Susanna 46**

- **Ask:** Do commentators compare Pilate's words "I am innocent of this man's blood" in Matthew 27:24 with Susanna 46, and what do they conclude?
- **Why:** Three auditors met this independently. It is rated moderate.

**3.7 Matthew 26:15 and Exodus 21:32**

- **Ask:** Do commentators connect the thirty pieces of silver in Matthew 26:15 with the price of a slave in Exodus 21:32 as well as with Zechariah 11:12?
- **Why:** New candidate N1.

**3.8 Matthew 5:39 and Isaiah 50:6**

- **Ask:** Which commentators connect "whoever slaps you on your right cheek" in Matthew 5:39 with Isaiah 50:6, and do they link it to the slapping of Jesus in Matthew 26:67?
- **Why:** The audit raised this to moderate–high.
- **Watch for:** whether they note that Luke 6:29 has different wording.

## 4. Grammar and Text

**4.1 Matthew 28:19, "go"**

- **Ask:** How does Wallace's *Greek Grammar beyond the Basics* classify the aorist participle πορευθέντες ("having gone") in Matthew 28:19, and does it carry imperative force?
- **Why:** Harris and Naselli have answered already. Wallace is the grammar the overview names, and Trap 6 is to be reworded at Finalise.

**4.2 Matthew 1:7–10, Asaph and Amos**

- **Ask:** What does Metzger's *Textual Commentary* say about the readings Asaph and Amos in Matthew 1:7–10?
- **Why:** The witnesses are known from the NA28 apparatus. The reasoning behind the committee's decision is not.

**4.3 Matthew 21:44**

- **Ask:** What does Metzger's *Textual Commentary* say about Matthew 21:44, and why did the committee print it?
- **Why:** The verse is bracketed in NA28, but the evidence for omitting it is thin (D, 33, Old Latin, Sinaitic Syriac, Origen).

**4.4 Matthew 16:2b–3**

- **Ask:** What does Metzger's *Textual Commentary* say about Matthew 16:2b–3?
- **Why:** The omission has strong support (Sinaiticus, Vaticanus). This matters before unit 32 is preached.

**4.5 Matthew 27:16–17, "Jesus Barabbas"**

- **Ask:** What does Metzger's *Textual Commentary* say about the reading "Jesus Barabbas" in Matthew 27:16–17?
- **Why:** This is a bracket in a ⭐ unit (53).

**4.6 Matthew 17:20, "little faith"**

- **Ask:** What does Metzger's *Textual Commentary* say about ὀλιγοπιστίαν ("little faith") and ἀπιστίαν ("unbelief") in Matthew 17:20?
- **Why:** The "little faith" chain in the overview depends on the NA28 reading.

**4.7 Deuteronomy 6:13 in the Septuagint (only if you own the Göttingen *Deuteronomium*)**

- **Ask:** What does the Göttingen Septuagint apparatus record at Deuteronomy 6:13 for προσκυνήσεις ("you shall worship") and μόνῳ ("only")?
- **Why:** Codex Alexandrinus agrees with Matthew 4:10. Is it alone, and does the Göttingen apparatus treat the reading as an assimilation to the New Testament?

**4.8 Isaiah 7:14 and Hosea 6:6 in the Septuagint (only if you own Ziegler's *Isaias* and *Duodecim prophetae*)**

- **Ask:** What does the Göttingen apparatus record at Isaiah 7:14 for ἕξει ("will have in the womb") and λήμψεται ("will conceive")?
- **Then ask:** What does it record at Hosea 6:6 for ἤ ("rather than") and καὶ οὐ ("and not")?
- **Why:** This tests the sweep's third headline: wherever Swete and Rahlfs differ, Matthew agrees with Rahlfs.

## 5. Background

**5.1 Matthew 17:24–27, the temple tax**

- **Ask:** What was the half-shekel temple tax in first-century Judaism, and what evidence do commentators give for linking it with Exodus 30:11–16?
- **Why:** The audit rated Exodus 30 high as the institution behind the passage, but only moderate for the "ransom" reading.

**5.2 Matthew 1:3, Tamar**

- **Ask:** What evidence do commentators give for Tamar's ethnicity in Matthew 1:3, and do any of them note that Genesis 38 does not state it?
- **Why:** Tension 7 in the sweep.
- **Watch for:** an ethnicity stated as fact, with no source given.
