# Claims file — Auditor A (single contacts: wording, rarity, rivals, Greek)

Each claim is stated as the proposing reports state it, counts included. Test each.

## A1 — Psalm 68:16 (LXX 67:17) behind Colossians 1:19 (and 2:9) [queue C1]
- Col 1:19 ὅτι ἐν αὐτῷ εὐδόκησεν πᾶν τὸ πλήρωμα κατοικῆσαι. Ps 67:17 LXX τὸ ὄρος ὃ εὐδόκησεν ὁ θεὸς κατοικεῖν ἐν αὐτῷ ("the mountain in which God was pleased to dwell").
- Stated: εὐδοκέω ("be pleased") with κατοικέω ("dwell") occurs in **one verse in the whole of Swete's LXX — Ps 67:17**, which also has ἐν αὐτῷ; and in **one verse of the NT — Col 1:19**. No rival found. Rahlfs agrees with Swete at 67:17.
- Stated: Ephesians quotes Ps 67:19 at Eph 4:8, so the psalm was in use in this circle.
- Stated: the whole-LXX scan ranks Ps 67 only 13th–216th against 1:15–20, so it is a single-verse contact, not a field partner. 2:9 ("in Him all the fullness of Deity dwells bodily") adds no evidence of its own; it is mediated through 1:19.
- Stated function: 1:19 is temple language — what the psalm said of God's chosen mountain-dwelling, the confession says of the Son.
- Current rating: **moderate–high on wording; moderate on function**. Load-bearing if a sermon uses the temple theme.
- Also test: does any other LXX verse join εὐδοκέω (or εὐδοκία) with another dwelling verb (κατασκηνόω, οἰκέω, ἐνοικέω, σκηνόω)? Is there an NT or LXX rival for "pleased … to dwell"?

## A2 — καταβραβεύω (2:18) / βραβεύω (3:15) as intended wordplay [C4]
- 2:18 μηδεὶς ὑμᾶς καταβραβευέτω ("let no one keep defrauding you of your prize / disqualify you"); 3:15 ἡ εἰρήνη τοῦ Χριστοῦ βραβευέτω ("let the peace of Christ rule").
- Stated: each verb occurs only here in the NT (SBLGNT). Claim: a deliberate reversal — the false umpire replaced by the true. Rated high lexically; **moderate as intended wordplay**.
- Test: confirm the counts; both are third-person imperatives — confirm; is there a baseline (how often does Paul place a compound and its simplex verb, each an NT hapax or rare, in one letter, and does the second use plausibly answer the first)? Any LXX use of either verb (Swete)?

## A3 — Isaiah 45:3 at Col 2:3, and Isaiah 45:14 at Col 1:27 [C5 + C11]
- Col 2:3 ἐν ᾧ εἰσιν πάντες οἱ θησαυροὶ τῆς σοφίας καὶ γνώσεως ἀπόκρυφοι. Isa 45:3 (Swete) θησαυροὺς σκοτεινούς, ἀποκρύφους, ἀοράτους.
- Stated: θησαυρός ("treasure") with ἀπόκρυφος ("hidden") in one verse: **Isa 45:3 and 1 Macc 1:23 only** (Swete). Isa 45:3 also has ἀόρατος (cf. Col 1:15–16).
- Stated rivals: Sir 1:25 ἐν θησαυροῖς σοφίας ("in the treasures of wisdom" — the exact noun pair); Sir 20:30 and 41:14 ("hidden wisdom and an unseen treasure"); Prov 2:4–6. Rated **moderate** (a wisdom idiom; no single source).
- Stated: Isa 45:14 ὅτι ἐν σοὶ ὁ θεός ἐστιν ("surely God is in you"); the exact Greek ἐν σοὶ ὁ θεός occurs in Swete's canonical books **only at Isa 45:14** (also Pss Sol 17:1 in another sense). Col 1:27 τοῦ μυστηρίου τούτου ἐν τοῖς ἔθνεσιν, ὅ ἐστιν Χριστὸς ἐν ὑμῖν. Claim: a reversal — the nations once confessed God "in" Israel; now Christ is "in" the Gentiles. Rated **moderate at most, synthetic**.
- Stated: the Hebrew word מַטְמוֹן ("hidden treasure") occurs in five WLC verses — Gen 43:23; Job 3:21; Prov 2:4; Isa 45:3; Jer 41:8 — so Prov 2:4 and Isa 45:3 meet on the word Colossians spells as "treasures … hidden".
- Stated: Paul quotes Isa 45:23 at Rom 14:11 and echoes it at Phil 2:10–11, so the chapter was in his hand.

## A4 — Genesis 1 behind Col 1:15, as distinct from 3:10 [C6]
- Col 1:15 εἰκὼν τοῦ θεοῦ τοῦ ἀοράτου, πρωτότοκος πάσης κτίσεως; 1:16 τὰ ὁρατὰ καὶ τὰ ἀόρατα. Col 3:10 κατ᾽ εἰκόνα τοῦ κτίσαντος αὐτόν.
- Stated: ἀόρατος ("invisible") stands in **only three verses of Swete — Gen 1:2; Isa 45:3; 2 Macc 9:5**. κατ᾽ εἰκόνα occurs in five LXX verses: Gen 1:26, 1:27, 5:1; Sir 17:3; Hos 13:2 (idols). Rival in wording: Wis 2:23 shares κτίζω with εἰκών where Genesis has ποιέω. Genesis LXX uses ποιέω, not κτίζω.
- Stated: in the NT, Christ is called "the image of God" only at 2 Cor 4:4 and Col 1:15; εἰκών occurs in Colossians only at 1:15 and 3:10.
- Claim: Gen 1 at 3:10 is **high**; at 1:15 **moderate**, and 1:15 depends on 3:10 to be heard. The whole-LXX scan ranks Genesis 1 14th–52nd against 1:15–20.

## A5 — Daniel 2 (Theodotion and Old Greek) at Col 1:26–27; 2:2–3 [C12]
- Col 1:26 τὸ μυστήριον τὸ ἀποκεκρυμμένον ἀπὸ τῶν αἰώνων … νῦν δὲ ἐφανερώθη; 1:27 οἷς ἠθέλησεν ὁ θεὸς γνωρίσαι …; 2:2–3 ἐπίγνωσιν τοῦ μυστηρίου τοῦ θεοῦ … θησαυροὶ τῆς σοφίας καὶ γνώσεως ἀπόκρυφοι.
- Stated: μυστήριον ("mystery") with γνωρίζω ("make known"): **three verses in Swete — Theodotion's Daniel 2:28, 29, 30**; 2:30 also has σοφία. μυστήριον with σοφία within three verses: **Wisdom 6:20–22 and the two Greek texts of Daniel 2 only** (Swete). Theodotion Dan 2:22 has ἀπόκρυφα ("hidden things"). Colossians uses φανερόω, not Daniel's ἀποκαλύπτω. The Old Greek has the same vocabulary (ranked lower). Rahlfs Theodotion 2:23 adds καὶ νῦν.
- Claim: Daniel 2 supplies the shape (a hidden purpose for the ages, disclosed by God, about his kingdom, beyond the wise of the nations). Rated **moderate**.
- Also test: rivals (Wisdom 6; Sirach; 1 Enoch in Swete's corpus; Qumran is outside the corpus — say so); and the claim that Theodotion is a later recension `[S]`.

## A6 — Mark 7:1–23 behind Col 2:8 and 2:16–22 [C15]
- Col 2:8 κατὰ τὴν παράδοσιν τῶν ἀνθρώπων; 2:16 ἐν βρώσει καὶ ἐν πόσει; 2:21–22 μὴ ἅψῃ μηδὲ γεύσῃ … κατὰ τὰ ἐντάλματα καὶ διδασκαλίας τῶν ἀνθρώπων.
- Stated: παράδοσις ("tradition") with ἄνθρωπος ("man") in one verse: **Mark 7:8 and Col 2:8 only** in the NT (SBLGNT). Mark 7 is about washings — βαπτισμός at 7:4, its only Gospel use — and about food, and quotes Isa 29:13 (7:7). ἔνταλμα ("commandment") occurs in only three NT verses: Matt 15:9; Mark 7:7; Col 2:22. ἔνταλμα with διδασκαλία: one LXX verse, Isa 29:13; Colossians reorders the wording.
- Claim (synthetic): three contacts — tradition of men (2:8), Isa 29:13 (2:22), the food question — point to the Jesus tradition about human tradition behind Paul's polemic. Rated **moderate**.
- **Synoptic check required:** does the evidence single out **Mark** 7, or equally Matthew 15:1–20 (the parallel), or simply a shared Jesus tradition / shared use of Isa 29:13? State what is Mark's own.
