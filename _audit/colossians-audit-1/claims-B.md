# Claims file — Auditor B (field and design claims: block tests, chance baselines, rank stability)

Each claim is stated as the proposing reports state it, counts included. Test each, with a null.

## B1 — "The confession is the letter's arsenal" (Col 1:15–20 redeployed in 2:8–15) [queue C2]
- Stated links (SBLGNT, by lemma): ὅτι ἐν αὐτῷ ("for in Him") in Paul's letters only at Col 1:16, 1:19, 2:9 (the two other NT uses are in 1 John); πλήρωμα ("fullness") with κατοικέω ("dwell") in the NT only at Col 1:19 and 2:9 — and each word occurs in Colossians only at those two verses; κεφαλή ("head") with ἀρχή and ἐξουσία in one NT verse only, Col 2:10; ἀρχαί and ἐξουσίαι created (1:16), headed (2:10), stripped (2:15) — their only three appearances as a pair in the letter; σταυρός ("cross") only 1:20 and 2:14 in the letter; νεκρός only 1:18, 2:12, 2:13; σῶμα 1:18 → 2:17, 2:19; κεφαλή 1:18 → 2:10, 2:19.
- Stated chance baseline: of 1:15–20's **24 rarer words** (each in at most four verses of the letter), the seven-verse stretch **2:8–14 shares seven**, the most of any stretch; **no stretch outside 2:5–19 shares more than three**; but across the letter's **ninety six-verse passages, fourteen have some partner stretch sharing seven or eight**.
- Rating: **high on the specific links; moderate–high as a design**.
- Test: reproduce the baseline; then ask whether the result is what any dense doctrinal passage would show against its nearest neighbour (e.g. run the same test for other Pauline confession/argument pairs: Phil 2:6–11 against Phil 2–3; Rom 3:21–26 against Rom 4–5; Eph 1:3–14 against Eph 1–3), and whether adjacency alone (2:8–14 is only two verses after 1:15–20's section ends? — check) explains it.

## B2 — The hidden-and-revealed sequence 1:26 → 2:3 → 3:3–4 → 4:4 [C3]
- 1:26 τὸ μυστήριον τὸ ἀποκεκρυμμένον … νῦν δὲ ἐφανερώθη; 2:3 θησαυροὶ … ἀπόκρυφοι; 3:3–4 ἡ ζωὴ ὑμῶν κέκρυπται … φανερωθῇ … φανερωθήσεσθε; 4:4 ἵνα φανερώσω αὐτό.
- Stated: the hiding words (ἀποκρύπτω, ἀπόκρυφος, κρύπτω) and φανερόω ("to reveal/manifest") stand in Colossians **only at these places**. Claim: the pair moves from God's plan (1:26), to Christ's treasures (2:3), to the believer's life (3:3–4), to Paul's preaching (4:4) — a deliberate sequence. Rated **high lexically; design moderate**.
- Test: counts; the baseline (how often does a pair of word-families in Paul's letters spread over four sections of a letter in an order that can be narrated as a development?); rivals in other Pauline letters (1 Cor 2:7; Rom 16:25–26; Eph 3:5, 9).

## B3 — Psalm 89 (LXX 88) as the field partner of Col 1:15–20 [C7] — load-bearing (a Headline)
- Stated: the whole-LXX scan of the passage's 25 content stems ranks **Ps 88 LXX first of 1,259 Swete chapters in all six runs** (chapters, 8-verse and 16-verse windows; all words and rarer words). It shares six rarer words, cognates merged: πρωτότοκος ("firstborn", 88:28), πλήρωμα (88:12), κτίζω (88:13), θρόνος, ἐκκλησία (88:6), εὐδοκία (88:18, noun cognate of εὐδοκέω).
- Stated: πλήρωμα with founding or creating: only Ps 88:12–13 among the 13 Swete verses with πλήρωμα. πρωτότοκος with βασιλεύς ("king") in a royal sense: one LXX verse, Ps 88:28. πρωτότοκος with νεκρός: two NT verses, Col 1:18 and Rev 1:5, and Rev 1:5 combines Ps 88:38 ("the witness … faithful") and 88:28 ("firstborn … kings of the earth"). The psalm itself joins a creation hymn (89:5–18) with the royal firstborn (89:19–37). Rahlfs agrees with Swete on every load-bearing word. Positive control: Heb 1:1–14 ranks Ps 44 LXX first.
- Rating: **moderate–high — a design claim, measured against chance at six settings but not audited**.
- Test above all: **a null for "first in all six runs"**. Run the scan on a set of arbitrary Pauline passages of about six verses (say 30–50, drawn across the letters) and report how often *some* LXX chapter ranks first in all six runs, and how far ahead the winner usually is. Report Ps 88's margin over second place in each run for Col 1:15–20. Read the matched words for sense. Rivals: Ps 2; 2 Sam 7; Isa 9; 1 Chr 29; Sir 24; Prov 8.

## B4 — Isaiah 45 as a candidate live source (1:15–20 with 2:3) [C8]
- Stated: Isaiah 45 ranks **7th–23rd** of 1,259 against 1:15–20 (shares ἀόρατος 45:3, κτίζω 45:7–8, heaven and earth, and 45:7 "making peace and creating evil"), but only about **100th** (median) against 1:24–2:5. Point contacts at 2:3 (θησαυρός + ἀπόκρυφος) and 1:27 (ἐν σοὶ ὁ θεός, 45:14). Claim: Isaiah 45 used twice in the letter → candidate live source. Rated **moderate, synthetic**.
- Test: rank stability; null; whether two point contacts in a letter beat chance for a 25-verse prophetic chapter.

## B5 — Proverbs 2:1–6 behind Col 2:2–4 (with 1:28) [C10]
- Stated ranks of Prov 2 against 1:24–2:5 (63 stems, supplied directly because the scan cannot cross 1:29 → 2:1): chapters 12th / 4th; 8-verse windows 6th / 11th; 16-verse windows 2nd / 6th; median 6. Daniel 2 (Theodotion) median 14; Isaiah 45 median ~100. Higher-ranking chapters (2 Macc 9; Esther 4; 2 Macc 1; Sirach 30) are common vocabulary.
- Stated rarity (Swete): ἐπίγνωσις ("knowledge") and θησαυρός ("treasure") within six verses of each other in **one passage only, Prov 2:1–5**; καρδία, σύνεσις, θησαυρός and σοφία together within six verses: **Prov 2:1–6 and Sir 1:23–25 only**. 1:28 νουθετοῦντες ~ Prov 2:2 νουθέτησιν. Rahlfs agrees on the load-bearing words.
- Stated sequence: Prov 2 moves from treasure-wisdom (2:1–6) to deliverance "from the man who speaks nothing trustworthy" (2:12) — the same sequence as Col 2:2–3 → 2:4 ("so that no one will delude you with persuasive argument").
- Rating: **moderate**.
- Test: the ranks (the scan works within a chapter only — you will need to build the cross-chapter run yourself with the same method, or run 1:24–29 and 2:1–5 separately and say so); a null for the sequence argument (how many wisdom chapters move from seeking wisdom to protection from a deceiver?); rivals Sir 1; Wis 6–7; Bar 3; Job 28.

## B6 — Ephesians 3:1–13 as the parallel to Col 1:24–2:5 (and Eph 2:1–16 to Col 2:6–15) [C13]
- Stated: of the 68 words in Col 1:24–2:5 left when the commonest function words are set aside, **24 recur in Eph 3:1–13**; eleven other Pauline passages of similar length share **9–18** (1 Cor 2:1–13 and 2 Cor 4:1–13 highest at 18). Eph 3:13 ("my afflictions on your behalf") is the nearest NT parallel to Col 1:24.
- Stated: of 58 such words in Col 2:6–15, **16 recur in Eph 2:1–16**, against 4–12 for eleven other Pauline passages (Eph 1:15–23 at 12; Rom 6:1–14 at 9).
- Claim: a measured parallel; direction of dependence open.
- Test: replace the eleven hand-picked comparison passages with **every** Pauline passage of similar length (sliding windows across all thirteen letters, excluding Colossians), and report where Eph 3:1–13 and Eph 2:1–16 fall in that distribution. Note anything that bears on direction, without resolving it.

## B7 — Col 1:24–2:5 and 4:2–18 as a frame for the letter's personal material [C14]
- Stated: 4:2–18 shares more of 1:24–2:5's rarer vocabulary (words in at most four verses of Colossians) than any other seventeen-verse stretch: **thirteen**, against **eleven** for the next independent stretch (1:7–23). Rated **moderate (design)**; each individual link high (ἀγωνίζομαι only 1:29 and 4:12; τέλειος only 1:28 and 4:12; πληροφορ- 2:2 → 4:12; παρακαλέω with καρδία only 2:2 and 4:8; Λαοδίκεια 2:1; 4:13, 15, 16; μυστήριον 4:3).
- Test: reproduce; null (how often does a letter's personal/closing section share most with its earlier autobiographical section — check Phil, Rom, 1 Thess); is the margin 13 vs 11 meaningful?
