# Claim audit — Colossians — Auditor B (field and design claims B1–B7)

*Blind audit. Sources read: the common brief, the claims file for B1–B7, and the primary-text corpus only (SBLGNT with its lemma index; Swete's LXX; the Rahlfs-Hanhart exports of Psalms, Proverbs and Isaiah from Logos). Anything from my own knowledge of scholarship is tagged [S] and carries no verdict on its own. British English throughout.*

## How the tests were run (in words)

- **New Testament counts** are by SBLGNT lemma. "Content words" means nouns, verbs and adjectives; "all words" means every lemma. Where a claim's count depends on the setting, both are reported.
- **Swete searches** are on accent-free word-beginnings, with every hit read for sense. For verbs I also searched the stem anywhere in the word (so that augmented and compounded forms such as ἀποκρύψω or ἐκτίσας are caught). One correction to my own working is worth recording: a first pass for the "hide" family that left out the ψ-stem (κρυψ-) missed Wis 6:22 (ἀποκρύψω) and Prov 2:1 (κρύψῃς). Every hide-family result below comes from the corrected search, and the positive controls returned the expected verses.
- **Whole-LXX field scans.** The house scan ranks every Swete chapter, and every 8- and 16-verse window, against an NT passage in six runs: chapters, 8-verse windows and 16-verse windows, each scored once on all words and once on rarer words. Where a passage crosses a chapter break (Col 1:24–2:5), I fed the same scoring method the combined word-list of both halves, and also ran each half separately.
- **Null distributions** were built from passages chosen arbitrarily (a fixed random draw of 40 six-verse passages across the thirteen letters) and from a second set of 25 confessional or dense doctrinal six-verse passages. Within-letter and cross-letter nulls were built with sliding windows.

## B1 — "The confession is the letter's arsenal" (1:15–20 redeployed in 2:8–15)

### Reproducing the stated links (SBLGNT, by lemma)

- **ὅτι ἐν αὐτῷ ("for in him")**: Col 1:16, 1:19, 2:9. The only other NT instances are 1 John 2:5 and 4:13. **Holds exactly.**
- **πλήρωμα ("fullness") with κατοικέω ("dwell") in one verse**: Col 1:19 and 2:9 only in the NT. In Colossians each word occurs only at these two verses. **Holds exactly.**
- **κεφαλή ("head") with ἀρχή ("rule") and ἐξουσία ("authority") in one verse**: Col 2:10 only. **Holds by verse.** The claim misses one thing. Allow a three-verse span and add πλήρωμα, and the four words cluster in only two places in the NT: Col 2:8–11 and **Eph 1:21–23**. This bears on B6.
- **ἀρχή and ἐξουσία as a pair in Colossians**: 1:16, 2:10, 2:15. **Holds.** Note that ἀρχή alone also stands at 1:18, and ἐξουσία alone at 1:13 ("the authority of darkness").
- **σταυρός ("cross")**: 1:20 and 2:14 only. **Holds.**
- **νεκρός ("dead")**: 1:18, 2:12, 2:13. **Holds.**
- **κεφαλή**: 1:18, 2:10, 2:19. **Holds.**
- **σῶμα ("body")**: the stated chain 1:18 → 2:17, 2:19 is **incomplete**. σῶμα stands in eight verses of the letter: 1:18, 1:22, 1:24, 2:11, 2:17, 2:19, 2:23, 3:15. It is too common in the letter to carry a redeployment argument, so it should be dropped from the link list.

### Reproducing the stated baseline

- **Rarer words of 1:15–20** (lemmas found in at most four verses of the letter): **23** on content words, **25** on all words. The claim says 24. The two extra lemmas on the all-words setting are εἴτε ("whether") and πρό ("before").
- **The stretch 2:8–14 shares seven** on either setting: κατοικέω, κεφαλή, νεκρός, πλήρωμα, σταυρός, ἀρχή, ἐξουσία. It ties with 2:9–15 as the best stretch. **Holds.**
- **"No stretch outside 2:5–19 shares more than three"**: essentially holds. Stretches beginning at 2:4 share five. Every stretch beginning before 2:4 or after 2:15 shares three or fewer. The best independent stretch away from chapter 2 is 3:5–11, with three.
- **"Fourteen of the ninety six-verse passages have a partner stretch sharing seven or eight"**: **holds only on the all-words setting.** On content words the figure is **seven of ninety**. The other high scorers are not confessions. They are the letter's epistolary frame: 1:1–6 with 1:7–13; 1:8–13 and 1:9–14 with 1:1–7; and 4:7–12 with 1:7–13, the Epaphras and fellow-servant language. So scoring seven puts 1:15–20 in the top 8–16 % of the letter's passages, but it is not the only kind of passage that gets there.

### Adjacency

1:15–20 ends at 1:20. Whether its unit is taken to close at 1:20 or at 1:23, 2:8 begins 15–16 verses later. The stretch right after the confession (1:21–27) shares only three rarer words (κτίσις "creation", ἀποκαταλλάσσω "reconcile", ἐκκλησία "church"). **Adjacency does not explain the result.** The contact jumps over 1:21–2:7 and lands in 2:8–15.

### Comparator test: do other dense passages redeploy themselves like this?

I ran the same test on other Pauline confession and argument passages. Each was a six-verse source passage, compared with every non-overlapping seven-verse stretch of its own letter. "Rarer" meant at most four verses of the letter, or a threshold scaled to the letter's length for the long letters.

- **Phil 2:6–11**: best partner shares 4 (Phil 3:18–4:3). That is the letter's median; 69 of 99 passages do as well.
- **Rom 3:21–26**: best partner shares 2 on the fixed threshold and 5 on the scaled one (Rom 5:6–12, the expected partner). It sits below the median.
- **Eph 1:3–8 / 1:9–14**: best partners share 4–6. That is the median; about half the letter's passages do as well.
- **1 Cor 15:20–25**: 7–9 on the scaled threshold, but its partners lie inside the same chapter (15:26 and 15:50). This is the ordinary adjacency pattern that Colossians does not show.
- **2 Cor 5:16–21, Rom 8:28–33, Gal 4:1–6**: at or below their letters' medians.

None of the comparators shows what Colossians shows: a long-range redeployment, eight or more verses away, of a confession's rarer vocabulary, about twice as strong as the next independent partner (7 against 3).

### Hays criteria

These are not strictly applicable, because this is an intra-letter design claim and not an allusion. In their place: Volume **Strong** (seven shared rarer lemmas, four of them unique pairings); Recurrence **Strong** (the same lemmas recur at 2:19); Thematic coherence **Strong** (2:10 and 2:15 visibly re-use 1:16's powers as created, headed and stripped); Satisfaction **Strong**.

### Verdict

**Confirmed with nuance.** Recommended rating: **high** on the specific links (σῶμα excepted); **moderate–high** as a design. The redeployment is real, long-range and unmatched among the comparator confessions. Two corrections are needed. The "fourteen of ninety" figure holds only on an all-words count; on content words it is seven. And the other high scorers are the epistolary frame, so being in the top band is not by itself a mark of confessional redeployment. The σῶμα chain should be dropped, and the Eph 1:21–23 cluster noted.

## B2 — The hidden-and-revealed sequence 1:26 → 2:3 → 3:3–4 → 4:4

### Counts (SBLGNT, by lemma; positive control: each search returned its known verse)

- **Hide family in Colossians**: ἀποκρύπτω ("hide away") 1:26; ἀπόκρυφος ("hidden") 2:3; κρύπτω ("hide") 3:3. No other member of the family appears.
- **φανερόω ("make manifest")**: 1:26, 3:4 (twice, in one verse), 4:4. φανερός and φανέρωσις do not occur.
- **Both statements hold exactly.**

### What the claim misses inside the letter

- **μυστήριον ("mystery")** stands at 1:26, 1:27, 2:2 and 4:3. It runs the **same narratable path** as the claimed sequence: God's plan (1:26), "Christ in you, the hope of glory" (1:27), "God's mystery, Christ" (2:2), Paul's preaching (4:3). It co-occurs with the reveal-word at 1:26 and, across the verse break, at 4:3–4. The 4:4 αὐτό ("it") refers to that mystery.
- **γνωρίζω ("make known")** at 1:27 ("God willed to make known"), and again at 4:7 and 4:9 (Tychicus and Onesimus will "make known" everything), continues the thread into the letter's close.
- **So three of the four stations belong to the mystery thread.** The one station that is distinctively Colossian is **3:3–4**: the believer's life hidden with Christ in God and manifested with him. I found no Pauline parallel in which κρύπτω governs the believer's life.

### Null: how often does a pair of word-families run through a whole letter?

- **Within Colossians**: of the 51 content lemmas found in three to eight verses, five occur in all four chapters. Three of those carry meaning that could be narrated as a development just as easily: σοφία ("wisdom") at 1:9, 1:28, 2:3, 2:23, 3:16, 4:5; περιπατέω ("walk") at 1:10, 2:6, 3:7, 4:5; λόγος ("word") at 1:5, 1:25, 2:23, 3:16–17, 4:3, 4:6. A four-station spread that can be told as a story is therefore common in this letter.
- **Across Paul**:
  - 1 Corinthians spreads the hide-words and reveal-words over three separate sections: 2:7 (ἀποκρύπτω, of God's wisdom in a mystery); 4:5 (κρυπτός with φανερόω, of the Lord's coming); 14:25 (κρυπτός with φανερός, of the secrets of the heart).
  - Romans pairs them at 2:16 and 2:28–29, with φανερόω at 1:19 and 3:21.
  - 2 Corinthians pairs them at 4:2, and uses φανερόω in nine verses.
  - Ephesians pairs them at 5:12–14.
  - 1 Timothy pairs them at 5:25.
  - So six of the thirteen letters use the two families together somewhere, and two of them (1 Cor and Rom) spread them over three or more sections.

### Rivals

- **1 Cor 2:7 and Eph 3:9** are the only NT places outside Colossians where a hide-word stands within two verses of both μυστήριον and σοφία. The triad (mystery, wisdom, hidden) is found at 1 Cor 2:7, Eph 3:9–10, Col 1:26 and Col 2:3, and nowhere else in the NT.
- **Rom 16:25–26** ("a mystery kept silent … but now manifested") is the closest formal parallel to Col 1:26 [S]. **The corpus's SBLGNT lacks Rom 16:25–27**, so I could not count it. It needs checking in NA28 from Logos.
- The "once hidden, now revealed" form at 1:26 is a recurring Pauline revelation-schema (1 Cor 2:7–10; Eph 3:5, 9; 2 Tim 1:9–10; Titus 1:2–3) [S, Dahl and others].
- **LXX**: Wis 6:22 ("I will not hide mysteries from you", with σοφία) is the only Swete verse besides Dan 2:47 Old Greek and Tob 12:7 and 12:11 that joins μυστήριον to a hide-word, and the only one that also has σοφία.

### Verdict

**Needs reframing.** Recommended rating: **high** lexically; **low–moderate** for a "deliberate four-station sequence".

- The counts are exact. But the claimed development is largely the letter's μυστήριον thread, and that thread is already a shared Pauline schema.
- Multi-section spreads that can be told as a story are common, both in this letter and in 1 Corinthians and Romans.
- Reframe it as "the mystery thread, with the hidden/revealed vocabulary attached". The distinctive Colossian move is the extension of hiddenness to the believer's life in 3:3–4, and that deserves the weight.

## B3 — Psalm 88 LXX (89 MT) as the field partner of Col 1:15–20 (Headline)

### Reproduction

- The scan finds **25 content stems** in 1:15–20. Two have no Swete match at all (ἀποκαταλλάσσω "reconcile" and κυριότης "dominion"). **Holds.**
- **Ps 88 ranks first in all six runs. Holds exactly.**
- The six rarer words shared in the chapter run are ἐκκλησία, εὐδοκία, θρόνος, κτίζω, πλήρωμα and πρωτότοκος. **Holds.**

**Ps 88's margin over second place in each run:**

| Run | Ps 88 score | Second | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chapters, all words | 21.6 | Dan 3 OG, 17.9 | 1.21 |
| Chapters, rarer words | 18.5 | Sir 24, 13.0 | 1.42 |
| 8-verse, all words | 19.7 | Esth 4, 19.0 | 1.04 |
| 8-verse, rarer words | 16.8 | 1 Chr 29, 13.9 | 1.21 |
| 16-verse, all words | 20.7 | 1 Chr 29, 17.4 | 1.19 |
| 16-verse, rarer words | 17.8 | Sir 24, 12.4 | 1.44 |

Mean ratio: 1.25. The 8-verse all-words run is a near-tie.

### Reading the matched words for sense (Swete; Rahlfs agrees on every word)

- **πλήρωμα** (88:12): "the world and its *fullness* you founded". Here the word means the world's contents, not the divine fullness that dwells in Christ (Col 1:19). It is the same word in a different sense.
- **κτίζω** (88:13, and again 88:48, ἔκτισας): God creating north and sea, and creating humankind. Sense compatible.
- **θρόνος** (88:5, 15, 30, 37, 45): David's throne and God's. In Col 1:16 θρόνοι are angelic powers. Different referent.
- **ἐκκλησία** (88:6): "in the assembly of the holy ones", the heavenly council. Col 1:18: the church. Related, but not the same.
- **εὐδοκία** (88:18): "in your favour our horn is exalted". This is the noun; Col 1:19 has the verb εὐδόκησεν ("God was pleased"). A weak cognate.
- **πρωτότοκος** (88:28): "I will make him firstborn, high above the kings of the earth". **Strong.**
- The all-words run also credits Ps 88:50 ἀρχαῖα ("of old") to ἀρχή ("rule"). That is a false friend, and it affects only the all-words runs.

### Rarity claims

- **πλήρωμα in Swete**: 13 verses. **Holds.**
- **πλήρωμα with founding or creating in one verse**: Ps 88:12 only. **Holds.** But Ps 23:1–2 has πλήρωμα, then "he founded it on the seas" (ἐθεμελίωσεν) in the very next verse, so the claim is verse-bound.
- **Missed rival**: the Col 1:19 pair itself, πλήρωμα with κατοικ- ("dwell" or "inhabitants"), occurs in **Ps 23:1, Ps 97:7, Jer 8:16 and Jer 29:2**, always as "the earth and its fullness, and those who dwell in it". Ps 88 lacks κατοικέω.
- **πρωτότοκος with βασιλ- ("king") in one verse**: five Swete verses — 2 Sam 13:21; 2 Sam 19:43; 2 Kgs 3:27 (the firstborn "who would reign in his place"); 2 Chr 21:3 ("the kingdom he gave to Jehoram, for he was the firstborn"); Ps 88:28. **Needs nuance**: two others are royal-succession contexts. Ps 88:28 is the only verse where "firstborn" is a status conferred over the kings of the earth.
- **πρωτότοκος with νεκρός in the NT**: Col 1:18 and Rev 1:5 only. **Holds.**
- **Rev 1:5**: "the faithful witness, the firstborn of the dead, the ruler of the kings of the earth". It combines Ps 88:38 ("the witness in heaven is faithful") with 88:28 ("firstborn … kings of the earth"). **Holds** [S, widely recognised].
  - Witness with faithful in Swete: nine verses, among them Prov 14:5 and 14:25, Isa 8:2, Jer 49:5, Ps 88:38.
  - Only Ps 88 has firstborn and kings of the earth in the same chapter.

### Positive control (Heb 1:1–14)

**Overstated.** Ps 44 ranks first in four of the six runs (all four window runs). **Ps 88 ranks first in both chapter runs.** The control works in part: a quoted source surfaces. But it also shows that Ps 88 is a strong general partner for "Son and firstborn" vocabulary (Heb 1:6 πρωτότοκος).

### The null for "first in all six runs"

- **Arbitrary set**: 40 randomly drawn six-verse Pauline passages. **Three of 40** had one chapter first in all six runs:
  - 2 Cor 6:1–6 → Esther 4, mean ratio 1.36;
  - Col 3:12–17 → Esther 8, mean 1.16;
  - Rom 11:6–11 → Ps 68, mean 1.79.
- **Confessional set**: 25 passages, including Phil 2:6–11, Eph 1:3–14 in two halves, Rom 3:21–26, 1 Cor 15:20–29, 2 Cor 4:1–6 and 5:16–21, 1 Tim 3:11–16, Titus 2–3, Col 2:9–14. **One of 25**: Eph 1:3–8 → Odes 14, mean 1.29.
- **Combined: 4 of 65 passages (6 %)** produce a six-of-six winner.
- **Mean winner's ratio over second place** across all 65 passages: median 1.11, interquartile range 1.07–1.15, 90th percentile 1.19. Only four passages reach 1.25 or more. Ps 88 for Col 1:15–20, at 1.25, sits on that line.
- **What the four six-of-six winners are:**
  - **Rom 11:6–11 / Ps 68**: a genuine quotation source (Rom 11:9–10 quotes Ps 68:23–24).
  - **2 Cor 6:1–6 / Esther 4**: *not* a source. It is a vocabulary-of-affliction match (θλῖψις, στενοχωρία, βοήθεια, νηστεία) with a margin larger than Ps 88's. Meanwhile the passage's **actual quoted source, Isa 49 (2 Cor 6:2 quotes Isa 49:8), ranks third**.
  - **Eph 1:3–8 / Odes 14**: an artefact. Odes 14 is the Christian *Gloria*, a Christian text inside Swete's LXX.
  - **Col 3:12–17 / Esther 8**: an artefact of false friends (εἰς credited to εἷς; ἐνοικειῶται credited to ἐνοικέω; καλλίστη).
- **Conclusion from the null**: six-of-six with a clear margin happens about once in twenty Pauline passages. In the null, one such winner in four was a real source. The test can tell a strong field contact from noise. It cannot tell a source from a shared semantic field.

### Leave-one-out stability (a test the claim did not run)

- **Remove πλήρωμα** from the passage and Ps 88 is first in **only two of six** runs (ranks 3, 1, 16, 11, 5, 1).
- **Remove both πλήρωμα and πρωτότοκος** and it is first in one of six.
- **Remove only πρωτότοκος** and nothing changes. The winning windows (Ps 88:6–18, the creation section) do not contain 88:28 at all.
- So the six-of-six result rests largely on the one word whose sense differs most from Colossians, and the royal-firstborn verse contributes nothing to the window wins.

### Rivals (median rank across the six runs)

- Sir 24: 5.
- 1 Chr 29: 8.
- Isa 45: 9.
- Gen 1: 37.
- Wis 9: 53.
- Prov 8: 109.
- Isa 9: 132.
- 2 Sam 7 and 1 Chr 17: about 435–445.
- Ps 2: 768.
- Ps 109: 838.
- **None challenges Ps 88**, and the royal-messianic texts usually invoked (Ps 2, 2 Sam 7, Ps 109) are nowhere near it lexically. Sir 24 (wisdom created first and enthroned in the assembly) is the only steady second.

### Hays criteria

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Psalter |
| Volume | Possible | six shared words, but three differ in sense; one rare word (πρωτότοκος) carries the real weight |
| Recurrence | Possible | Ps 88:28 and 88:38 used in Rev 1:5; Col's own use otherwise unconfirmed |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | creator plus royal firstborn in one psalm |
| Historical plausibility | Strong | |
| History of interpretation | Possible [S] | Ps 88:28 is regularly cited for "firstborn" in Col 1:15/18 and Rev 1:5; less often as the field of the whole hymn |
| Satisfaction | Possible | |

### Verdict

**Needs reframing.** Recommended rating: **moderate** for "Ps 88 as the field partner of the whole hymn"; **moderate–high** for the point contact Ps 88:28 ~ Col 1:15, 18.

- The rank reproduces exactly and beats an arbitrary-passage null at about the 5 % level.
- But the null shows that a non-source chapter (Esther 4) can win this way, and the result does not survive the loss of πλήρωμα, a word used in a different sense.
- The firstborn verse, the claim's strongest evidence, plays no part in the window wins.
- State it as: "Ps 88 is the strongest single LXX field partner of Col 1:15–20, measured above chance but fragile under leave-one-out; its weight-bearing contact is 88:28 (firstborn above the kings of the earth), confirmed as early-Christian reading by Rev 1:5."

## B4 — Isaiah 45 as a candidate live source (1:15–20 with 2:3)

### Ranks

- **Against 1:15–20**: 18, 23, 9, 7, 8, 7 in the six runs (median 9). The stated "7th–23rd" **holds**.
  - Within that list Isa 45 stands fifth to seventh among the chapters with a median of 10 or better.
  - The others in that band are Sir 17, Wis 18 and Sir 38, which nobody proposes as sources.
  - So Isa 45's rank lies in the band that non-sources also reach.
- **Against 1:24–2:5** (combined): 99, 133, 155, 102, 87, 33 (median 102). "About 100th" **holds**.
- **2:1–5 alone**: median 69.
- **1:24–29 alone**: median 202.

### The point contacts (Swete; Rahlfs agrees)

- **θησαυρός ("treasure") with ἀπόκρυφος ("hidden") in one verse**: Isa 45:3 and 1 Macc 1:23 only. This is a genuinely rare contact with Col 2:3.
  - **Widening the search to the hide-stem anywhere in the word** adds Sir 20:30 and Sir 41:14 ("hidden wisdom and invisible treasure, what profit in either?"). These are the only Swete verses holding wisdom, a hide-word and treasure together.
  - Within a three-verse span, Wis 7:12–14 joins them: "I do not hide her wealth; for she is an unfailing treasure".
  - Isa 45:3 has the exact adjective, ἀποκρύφους. The sapiential texts have σοφία, which Col 2:3 also has ("treasures of wisdom and knowledge, hidden").
- **Isa 45:3 also has ἀοράτους ("invisible")** (cf. Col 1:16, ἀόρατα). This is a real second link from the same verse.
- **The 1:27 contact** (Isa 45:14 ἐν σοὶ ὁ θεός, "God is in you" ~ Col 1:27 Χριστὸς ἐν ὑμῖν, "Christ in you") is **weak**: different subject, and a preposition-plus-pronoun phrase. The recognised NT use of Isa 45:14 is 1 Cor 14:25 ("God is really among you") [S].
- **Isa 45:7** ("making peace and creating evil") against Col 1:20 εἰρηνοποιήσας ("making peace"): ποιέω plus εἰρήνη is common, so weak on its own.

### Null: are two point contacts in a letter unusual for a chapter of this size?

I counted, for every Swete chapter, the separate places in Colossians where a verse shares a rare pair of content words with it. A rare pair is one found in at most 3, 5 or 10 Swete verses; three settings.

- **Chapters of 20–30 verses**: two or more separate contact points in **39 of 436 (9 %)** at the strictest setting, **54 of 436 (12 %)** at the middle one, **101 of 436 (23 %)** at the loosest.
- **Isa 45 scores five points** at the strict and middle settings, which puts it among the top 9–13 chapters of 1,259.
- **But three of the five come from one false friend**. Isa 45:1 calls Cyrus "my anointed" (τῷ χριστῷ μου), and that word is credited to Χριστός at Col 1:27, 3:1 and 4:3.
  - Isa 45:1 also has "right hand", "nations" and "I will open doors". These produce apparent contacts with Col 3:1 (Christ at God's right hand), 1:27 (among the nations) and 4:3 (God opening a door for the mystery of Christ).
  - Without the Cyrus title, Isa 45 has **two** contact points (1:15/16 through "invisible", and 2:3), which puts it in the top ~12 % of comparable chapters. That is suggestive, not exceptional.

### Hays criteria

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | |
| Volume | Possible | 2:3 rare pair; ἀόρατος |
| Recurrence | Weak | Isa 45 in Paul at Rom 14:11 / Phil 2:10–11 (45:23) and 1 Cor 14:25 (45:14) [S], not in Colossians otherwise |
| Thematic coherence | Possible | hidden treasures given; the sole creator |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | |
| History of interpretation | Weak [S] | Col 2:3 is usually read against Proverbs, Sirach or wisdom texts more than Isa 45:3 |
| Satisfaction | Possible | |

### Verdict

**Uncertain — flag for research.** Recommended rating: **low–moderate** as a "live source used twice"; **moderate** for the single point contact Isa 45:3 ~ Col 2:3.

- The field rank is in the non-source band.
- The count of two contacts is within the top eighth of comparable chapters, but not beyond chance.
- The 1:27 contact is weak.
- The sapiential rivals (Sir 20:30 and 41:14; Wis 7:13–14) compete directly for 2:3.

## B5 — Proverbs 2:1–6 behind Col 2:2–4 (with 1:28)

### Ranks

The combined passage was built by joining the two halves' word-lists under the same method: **63 stems**.

- **Prov 2: 12, 4, 6, 11, 2, 6 — median 6. Reproduces exactly.**
- **Daniel 2 (Theodotion)**: median 14. Holds.
- **Isa 45**: median 102. Holds.
- **Chapters ranking above Prov 2 by median**: 2 Macc 9 (3), Esther 4 (4), 2 Macc 1 (5). Sirach 30 is at 7. These are common-vocabulary matches (θλῖψις, χαίρω, φανερόω, παρακαλέω). **Holds.**
- **Separately**: 2:1–5 alone puts Prov 2 at median 6, level with Daniel 2 (Theodotion) at 6 and behind Daniel 9 (Theodotion) at 5. 1:24–29 alone puts it at median 30. So the contact is concentrated in 2:1–5, as the claim implies.
- **Wisdom 7 (unnamed rival)**: median 10 on the combined passage and **median 5 on 1:24–29 alone**. It shares ἀποκρύπτω (7:13), πλοῦτος ("wealth", 7:8, 11, 13; cf. Col 1:27, 2:2), σοφία, ἐνέργεια ("working", 7:17, 26; cf. Col 1:29), δόξα and γενεά.

### Rarity claims (Swete; Rahlfs agrees on the load-bearing words of Prov 2:1–6 and 2:12)

- **ἐπίγνωσις ("knowledge") with θησαυρός ("treasure") within six verses**: one passage, Prov 2:1–5. **Holds.** I ran it on the noun forms only, because a word-beginning search for ἐπιγνωσ- also catches future verbs such as ἐπιγνώσεσθε. Positive control: the search returned Prov 2.
  - Caution: the noun ἐπίγνωσις occurs in only **eight Swete verses**, so the collocation's rarity mostly reflects how rare the noun is.
- **καρδία, σύνεσις, θησαυρός and σοφία within six verses**: Prov 2:1–6 and Sir 1:23–25 only. **Holds.**
- **Supporting results not stated in the claim:**
  - νουθετ- ("admonish/admonition") with σοφ- ("wisdom") in one Swete verse: **Prov 2:2 only**. In the NT, νουθετέω with σοφία in one verse: **Col 1:28 and 3:16 only**. This firmly supports the 1:28 link.
  - Prov 2:1 also has κρύψῃς ("hide [my commandment] with yourself"). So Prov 2:1–6 holds a hide-word, treasure, wisdom, understanding, heart and knowledge, all of which Col 2:2–3 has.
  - Prov 2:5 ἐπίγνωσιν θεοῦ ("knowledge of God") ~ Col 2:2 ἐπίγνωσιν τοῦ μυστηρίου τοῦ θεοῦ ("knowledge of the mystery of God"). But ἐπίγνωσις θεοῦ also occurs in Hos 4:1 and 6:6, and Col 1:10 has it too.
- **Rival phrase**: Sir 1:25 ἐν θησαυροῖς σοφίας ("in the treasures of wisdom") is the closest verbal match to Col 2:3's θησαυροὶ τῆς σοφίας.

### Null for the sequence argument (wisdom sought, then protection from a deceiver)

Reading Proverbs 1–9 in Swete, the same move appears in at least five chapters:

- **1:2** (knowing wisdom) → **1:10–11** ("let not ungodly men lead you astray … if they urge you").
- **2:1–6** → **2:12** ("the man who speaks nothing trustworthy").
- **5:1–2** (attend to my wisdom) → **5:3** (the harlot's lips drip honey).
- **6:20–23** (keep your father's commands) → **6:24** ("to guard you from … the slander of a strange tongue").
- **7:1–4** (hide my commandments; call wisdom your sister) → **7:5** ("to keep you from the strange woman, if she assails you with flattering words").

Prov 7:5, λόγοις τοῖς πρὸς χάριν ("with words meant to please"), is arguably closer to Col 2:4's πιθανολογία ("plausible argument") than Prov 2:12 is. Prov 7:1 also has a hide-word (κρύψον). **The sequence is generic to Proverbs 1–9 and does not single out chapter 2.**

### Other rivals (median rank, combined passage)

- Wis 7: 10.
- Wis 6: 85; but Wis 6:22 joins σοφία, a hide-word and μυστήρια in one verse.
- Sir 1: 76.
- Prov 8: 177.
- Sir 24: 262.

- Job 28: 502.
- Bar 3 and Job 28 are lexically remote and can be set aside. Wis 6–7 is a live co-candidate.

### Verdict

**Confirmed with nuance.** Recommended rating: **moderate**, with the lexical case nearer moderate–high.

- The ranks and rarity claims reproduce exactly, and two unstated results strengthen the lexical case: admonition with wisdom is unique to Prov 2:2 in the LXX, and the hide-word at Prov 2:1.
- The sequence argument should be dropped: the move is generic to Proverbs 1–9, and Prov 7:5 fits Col 2:4 at least as well.
- Wis 6:22–7:17 should be named as a co-candidate field for 1:26–2:3.

## B6 — Ephesians 3:1–13 as the parallel to Col 1:24–2:5, and Eph 2:1–16 to Col 2:6–15

### Reproduction

- **Source words**: Col 1:24–2:5 has **68** content lemmas (nouns, verbs and adjectives). **Matches the stated 68.** Col 2:6–15 has **59** (stated 58).
- **Shared counts on my content-word definition**:
  - Eph 3:1–13 shares **27** (stated 24).
  - 1 Cor 2:1–13: **20** (stated 18).
  - 2 Cor 4:1–13: **21** (stated 18).
  - For Col 2:6–15: Eph 2:1–16 **20** (stated 16), Eph 1:15–23 **16** (stated 12), Rom 6:1–14 **10** (stated 9).
- **The ordering holds throughout.** The absolute numbers run consistently 2–4 higher than stated, which probably reflects a different stop-list. Report the definition used.

### Sliding windows across all twelve other Pauline letters

**Col 1:24–2:5:**

| Window length | Windows | Median | 90th pct | 99th pct | Windows ≥ Eph 3:1–13's 27 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 verses | 1,814 | 11 | 16 | 22 | 3 (all in Eph 3) |
| 13 verses | 1,790 | 13 | 17 | 24 | 9 (all in Eph 3) |

- The best window is **Eph 3:7–17** (27) at 11 verses and **Eph 3:7–19** (29) at 13 verses. The parallel extends into Paul's prayer, 3:14–19: "according to the riches of his glory", Christ dwelling in hearts, "that you may be filled".
- The next independent windows are also Ephesian (1:13–23 at 24; 2:22–3:10 at 23).
- The best non-Ephesian window is **2 Cor 4:6–16** (21), then 1 Thess 2:13–3:3 (21), Rom 11:33–12:7 (20) and 1 Cor 2:1–11 (20).
- **Eph 3 is at the very top of the distribution, at both lengths.**

**Col 2:6–15:**

| Window length | Windows | Median | 90th pct | 99th pct | Windows ≥ Eph 2:1–16's 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 verses | 1,826 | 7 | 10 | 14 | 0 (max 19) |
| 16 verses | 1,754 | 9 | 12 | 19 | 15 |

- **But the best window is not Eph 2:1–16.** It is **Eph 1:20–2:6** (19) at 10 verses and **Eph 1:19–2:11** (23) at 16 verses.
- That span takes in Eph 1:20–23 (raised and seated above every ἀρχή and ἐξουσία; head; fullness) and 2:1–6 (dead in trespasses; made alive with; raised with). The parallel centres on **Eph 1:20–2:6**, not on 2:1–16.
- The next non-Ephesian window is Rom 5:13–6:7 (15).

### A letter-level null (not stated in the claim)

- I took every fifth 10-verse window of Colossians (18 windows). **16 of 18** find their best Pauline partner in Ephesians, and they do so in broadly the same running order (Col 1 → Eph 1; Col 1:21–2:5 → Eph 3; Col 2:7–15 → Eph 1:20–2:6; Col 3 → Eph 4–5; Col 3:18–4:1 → Eph 5:22–6:9; Col 4:4–8 → Eph 6:15–24).
- The two exceptions are the opening (Philippians 1:1–10, tied with Ephesians 1) and the greetings (Philemon).
- So "Eph 3:1–13 beats the other Pauline passages" is **one instance of the letter-wide Colossians–Ephesians relationship** [S, long recognised], not a separate finding.

### Bearing on direction (noted, not resolved)

- **Eph 3:13** "my afflictions on your behalf" (θλίψεσίν μου ὑπὲρ ὑμῶν) is the nearest verbal parallel to Col 1:24 (παθήμασιν ὑπὲρ ὑμῶν … θλίψεων). θλῖψις with ὑπέρ and "you" in one verse occurs at 2 Cor 1:8, 2 Cor 7:4, Eph 3:13, Col 1:24 and 2 Thess 1:4. Only Eph 3:13 and Col 1:24 make it Paul's affliction *for* the readers. 2 Cor 1:6 is close in thought.
- **μυστήριον**: Ephesians defines the mystery as Gentile co-heirship (3:6); Colossians as "Christ in you" (1:27) and "Christ" (2:2).
- **Col 2:9–10 and Eph 1:21–23** are the only two NT places where κεφαλή, ἀρχή, ἐξουσία and πλήρωμα fall within three verses of one another.
- **παρακαλέω with καρδία** ("comfort hearts") in one verse: Acts 11:23, Eph 6:22, Col 2:2, Col 4:8 and 2 Thess 2:17. Col 4:8 and Eph 6:22 belong to the near-verbatim Tychicus notices.
- None of these settles direction. Each can be read either way.

### Hays criteria

These apply only loosely, because this is a literary-relationship claim. Volume **Strong**; Recurrence **Strong** (letter-wide); Thematic coherence **Strong**; Historical plausibility **Strong**; History of interpretation **Strong** [S]; Satisfaction **Strong**.

### Verdict

**Confirmed with nuance.** Recommended rating: **high** for Col 1:24–2:5 ↔ Eph 3:1–19; **moderate–high** for Col 2:6–15 ↔ Eph 1:20–2:10.

- On a full distribution, both parallels sit at the 99th percentile or above. The hand-picked comparators were not misleading about Eph 3.
- The Col 2:6–15 partner should be re-located to Eph 1:20–2:6, or Eph 1:19–2:11 at a 16-verse length.
- Both results should be presented as instances of the letter-wide parallel, not as independent measured findings.

## B7 — Col 1:24–2:5 and 4:2–18 as a frame for the letter's personal material

### Reproduction

- **Rarer vocabulary of 1:24–2:5** (lemmas in at most four verses of Colossians): 53 on content words, 59 on all words.
- **4:2–18 shares 13. Holds.**
- **Next independent seventeen-verse stretch, 1:7–23**: 11 on all words (**holds**), 10 on content words.
- **The 13 shared lemmas**: Λαοδίκεια, βλέπω, γνωρίζω, διάκονος, λέγω, μυστήριον, οἶδα, παρακαλέω, πληρόω, τέλειος, φανερόω, ἀγωνίζομαι, ἐκκλησία. About five are generic (λέγω "say", οἶδα "know", βλέπω "see", ἐκκλησία, διάκονος).
- **The individual links (SBLGNT):**
  - ἀγωνίζομαι ("strive"): 1:29 and 4:12 only. **Holds.** The noun ἀγών stands at 2:1, so the family runs 1:29, 2:1, 4:12.
  - τέλειος ("mature"): 1:28 and 4:12 only. **Holds.**
  - παρακαλέω with καρδία: 2:2 and 4:8 only in the letter. **Holds.**
  - Λαοδίκεια: 2:1; 4:13, 15, 16. **Holds.**
  - μυστήριον: 4:3. **Holds.**
  - **πληροφορ-** ("full assurance"), 2:2 → 4:12: true as a word-family (the noun πληροφορία at 2:2; the verb πληροφορέω at 4:12). But it is **not** among the 13 lemma matches. The lemma that is counted is πληρόω (1:25; 4:17; also 1:9 and 2:10).

### Is the 13 against 11 margin meaningful?

- **Within the letter**: for each of the 85 eleven-verse passages of Colossians, I compared its best seventeen-verse partner with its next independent partner.
  - On content words, a margin of two or more occurs in **53 of 85** passages; three or more in **33 of 85**.
  - On all words, two or more in **41 of 85**.
- **A two-point margin is therefore at about the median, and is not evidence.**
- What *is* unusual is the long range. The only other passages whose best partner shares 13 or more are opening-section passages paired with an adjacent stretch (1:1–9 with 1:21–23, and similar).
- The 1:23–26 → 4:2 pairing is the letter's one strong long-range pairing at this level, apart from the B1 confession pairing.

### Cross-letter null (closing section against the earlier autobiographical section)

| Letter | Closing section | Autobiographical section | Rank of the autobiographical section among partner windows |

| Romans | 15:14–33 | 1:8–17 | **1st to 5th of 392** — the same frame |

So the device is real in Colossians, and in Romans [S: the "apostolic parousia" frame, Funk]. It is not a general Pauline habit.

### Verdict

**Confirmed with nuance.** Recommended rating: **moderate** as a design (unchanged); **high** for the individual links.

- The frame reproduces, and the cross-letter null shows it is a recognisable but optional Pauline device, shared with Romans.
- Drop the "13 against 11" margin as evidence: a margin of that size is ordinary in the within-letter null.
- Correct the πληροφορ- link: it holds as a word-family link, but it is not one of the 13 lemma matches.

## New candidates

- **Wisdom 6:22–7:17 for Col 1:26–2:3** (moderate, needs a dig).
  - Wis 6:22 is the only Swete verse that joins σοφία, a hide-word and μυστήρια ("I will not hide mysteries from you").
  - Wis 7:13–14 has "I do not hide her wealth (πλοῦτος); she is an unfailing treasure".
  - Wis 7:17 and 7:26 have ἐνέργεια.
  - Wis 7 has median 5 against 1:24–29 and 10 against 1:24–2:5.
  - Rahlfs for Wisdom is not in the exports; check it in Logos before citing.
- **Sirach 20:30 and 41:14, and Sirach 1:25, for Col 2:3** (low–moderate). Sir 20:30 and 41:14 read "hidden wisdom and invisible treasure". Sir 1:25 reads "in the treasures of wisdom" (ἐν θησαυροῖς σοφίας), the closest phrase match to θησαυροὶ τῆς σοφίας.
- **Psalm 23 LXX for Col 1:19** (low). "The earth and its fullness, the world and all who dwell in it", with "he founded it" in the next verse. It carries the Col 1:19 pair πλήρωμα with κατοικ-, which Ps 88 lacks, though "dwell" there means "inhabitants".
- **Eph 1:20–2:6 as the Ephesian counterpart of Col 2:6–15** (see B6). It is the true lexical partner and the place of the four-word head/rule/authority/fullness cluster.
- **Isa 45:1–3 for Col 4:3** (low, for the record only). Isa 45:1–3 combines "my anointed", "right hand", "I will open doors" and "hidden, invisible treasures". Col 4:3 asks God to "open a door … to speak the mystery of Christ". Door-opening is an ordinary Pauline idiom (1 Cor 16:9; 2 Cor 2:12), and χριστός in Isa 45:1 is Cyrus, so this should not be pressed. It would matter only if an early reading of Isa 45:1 as "my Christ, the Lord" were in view [S, Barnabas 12:11].
- **Corpus gap**: the corpus's SBLGNT lacks Rom 16:25–27, the closest formal rival for Col 1:26. Check it in NA28 from Logos.
