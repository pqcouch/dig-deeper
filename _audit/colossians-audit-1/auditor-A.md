# Claim audit — Colossians — Auditor A report

**Scope.** Six single-contact claims (A1–A6): wording, rarity, rivals and Greek. Everything below was tested blind against the primary-text corpus only: Swete's LXX (searched on accent-free word-beginnings, with every hit read for sense), the SBLGNT with its lemma index, the WLC Hebrew with its lemma index, and the Logos exports (Rahlfs for Psalms, Isaiah, Genesis and both Greek texts of Daniel; NA28 Colossians text and apparatus; NA28 Ephesians). Anything from my own knowledge of scholarship is tagged [S] and carries no verdict on its own.

**A note on method that affects every Swete count.** My first positive control failed, and that failure matters for the whole audit. A word-beginning search for βραβ- ("umpire") found nothing in Swete. Yet Wisdom 10:12 has ἐβράβευσεν ("he judged the contest"). The augment hides the stem at the start of the word. I therefore re-ran every verb search as a search for the stem *anywhere in the word*, then read each hit for sense. That recovered Wis 10:12 and 1 Esd 9:14, and it also recovered the augmented forms of γνωρίζω ("make known") in Theodotion's Daniel 2:28–29, which a plain word-beginning search also misses. Every count below that involves a verb was made in this second way.

**Chance baselines used throughout.**

(a) *Pair expectation.* For any two words, the number of Swete verses holding both that we would expect by chance is (verses with word 1 × verses with word 2) ÷ 30,533, the number of verses in Swete.

(b) *How common is a "unique" contact?* I drew 300 verses at random from ten Pauline letters (Romans to Philemon, without the Pastorals). For each I took its nouns, verbs and adjectives, leaving out very common words such as "God", "be" and "say". Each content word had to occur in between 20 and 600 Swete verses. I then asked whether some pair of these words occurs together in exactly one Swete verse. **135 of the 300 verses (45 %) have at least one such pair.** So the bare fact that two words in a Colossians verse meet in only one LXX verse is common. It is not evidence by itself.

(c) *Window ranks.* I ranked each Colossians passage against every Swete chapter and every 8- and 16-verse window, using all words and then rarer words only. I calibrated these ranks against two known contacts and thirteen arbitrary passages; the results are under A1 and A4.

## A1 — Psalm 68:16 (LXX 67:17) behind Colossians 1:19 (and 2:9)

### What I tested
- εὐδοκέω/εὐδοκία ("be pleased", "good pleasure") with κατοικέω ("dwell") in one verse, across Swete and the SBLGNT; then within two verses.
- εὐδοκέω/εὐδοκία with every other dwelling verb: κατασκηνόω ("encamp, dwell"), οἰκέω ("dwell"), ἐνοικέω ("dwell in"), σκηνόω ("tabernacle").
- Ps 67:17 and 67:19 in Rahlfs; Eph 4:8 in the SBLGNT.
- The Hebrew of Ps 68:17. Hebrew rivals: חָמַד ("desire") with יָשַׁב ("sit, dwell") or שָׁכַן ("dwell"); רָצָה ("be pleased") with either; and אָוָה ("desire") with מוֹשָׁב ("dwelling").
- The window ranks of Ps 67 against Col 1:15–20, and a calibration against Eph 4:7–10, which quotes Ps 67:19 outright.

### Results
- **εὐδοκέω with κατοικέω, one verse.** Swete: Ps 67:17 only. SBLGNT: Col 1:19 only. *Holds exactly.* The positive control was Ps 67:17 itself, which the search returned. Ps 67:17 has ἐν αὐτῷ ("in it"); Col 1:19 has ἐν αὐτῷ ("in him"). Both have εὐδόκησεν followed by an infinitive of κατοικέω: κατοικεῖν in the psalm, κατοικῆσαι in Colossians.
- **Chance expectation for the pair.** εὐδοκ- occurs in 85 Swete verses and κατοικ- in 560. We would expect **about 1.6** co-occurrences by chance, and we observe 1. The rarity of the pair is therefore *at chance level*. What lifts the contact above chance is the shared construction: the aorist εὐδόκησεν governing an infinitive of the same dwelling verb, with an "in him/in it" phrase. That construction is found nowhere else in Swete.
- **Other dwelling verbs.**
  - With κατασκηνόω in one verse: Ps 67:17 only. The same verse has κατασκηνώσει ("will dwell") in its second half.
  - With ἐνοικέω: Sir 25:16 only, "I would rather dwell with a lion … than dwell with an evil wife". This is irrelevant.
  - With οἰκέω and its derivatives, every hit was read for sense: 1 Chr 29:3 ("my delight in the house of my God"); Ps 50:20 (εὐδοκία with οἰκοδομηθήτω, "be built", of Jerusalem's walls); Hag 1:8 ("build the house, and I will be pleased with it", εὐδοκήσω ἐν αὐτῷ); Isa 62:4 ("the Lord was pleased in you", with a pun on Οἰκουμένη, "inhabited"). Tob 5:17 and Ps 101:15 are false matches.
  - With σκηνόω: none.
  - Within two verses of κατοικέω: Jdt 15:8/10, Isa 62:4–5 and 3 Macc 2:15–16. 3 Macc 2:16 reads "since you were pleased (εὐδόκησας) [to set] your glory among your people Israel, you sanctified this place", beside κατοικητήριον ("dwelling place") in 2:15.
- **Rival "pleased … dwell" texts.** None matches the construction. But a group of Zion and temple texts says the same thing in other verbs: Hag 1:8 (εὐδοκέω of the house); 3 Macc 2:15–16 (εὐδοκέω of the glory in the sanctuary); Ps 131:13–14 ("the Lord chose Zion, preferred it for his dwelling … here I will dwell", with αἱρετίζω, "prefer", in place of εὐδοκέω); and Ps 73:2 ("Mount Zion, in which you dwelt", κατεσκήνωσας ἐν αὐτῷ). So the *theme* of a dwelling God has chosen is widespread. The *wording* with εὐδοκέω and an infinitive of κατοικέω is Ps 67:17 alone.
- **New Testament.** No rival for "pleased … to dwell". The nearest is 2 Cor 5:8, εὐδοκοῦμεν … ἐνδημῆσαι ("we are pleased … to be at home with the Lord"), which uses a different verb and has a human subject. Colossians itself carries the dwelling verbs on: κατοικέω at 1:19 and 2:9, and ἐνοικέω at 3:16 ("let the word of Christ *dwell in* you"). The claim did not note 3:16.
- **Rahlfs against Swete at 67:17.** The wording agrees. Rahlfs punctuates the relative clause as part of the question ("… why do you look askance, you curdled mountains, at the mountain in which God was pleased to dwell?"). That makes no difference to the wording. *Holds.*
- **Eph 4:8 and Ps 67:19.** Eph 4:8 opens Ἀναβὰς εἰς ὕψος ("having ascended on high"). Swete's Ps 67:19 also reads ἀναβάς; Rahlfs reads ἀνέβης ("you ascended"). Ephesians therefore agrees with the Vaticanus-type text against Rahlfs at that word, which is a small nuance the claim missed. Eph 4:8 also departs from both Greek texts ("he gave gifts to men" where the psalm has "you received gifts"). The psalm was demonstrably in use in this circle, and that holds whatever one concludes about the authorship of Ephesians [S].
- **Hebrew.** Ps 68:17 reads הָהָר חָמַד אֱלֹהִים לְשִׁבְתּוֹ ("the mountain God desired for his dwelling") … יִשְׁכֹּן ("he will dwell").
  - חָמַד with יָשַׁב in one verse: Ps 68:17 and Song 2:3 (a lover sitting in the shade).
  - חָמַד with שָׁכַן: Ps 68:17 only.
  - רָצָה with either dwelling verb: none.
  - The Hebrew rival is Ps 132:13–14, with אָוָה ("desire") and מוֹשָׁב ("dwelling"), and Ps 132:14 also with יָשַׁב.
  So in Hebrew, too, Ps 68:17 is the unique *desire + dwell* verse of its own wording. Ps 132 is the thematic twin.
- **Window ranks of Ps 67 against Col 1:15–20.** Chapters, all words: 17th. Chapters, rarer words: 94th. 8-verse windows, all words: 40th. 8-verse windows, rarer words: 216th. 16-verse windows, all words: 13th. 16-verse windows, rarer words: 55th. Median 55. *The stated range "13th–216th" holds exactly.*
- **Calibration.** Eph 4:7–10 quotes Ps 67:19 outright, yet ranks Ps 67 only between 7th and 30th (median 15). Its top partner is 1 Macc 10, at rank 1 or 2 in every setting, which is a vocabulary artefact. So a window rank is a poor detector even of a quotation in a short passage. Ps 67's modest rank does not tell against the contact. It does, however, confirm that the contact sits in one verse and is not a field.
- **Col 2:9** (κατοικεῖ πᾶν τὸ πλήρωμα τῆς θεότητος σωματικῶς, "all the fullness of deity dwells bodily") has no εὐδοκέω. *I agree that it adds no independent evidence.* It repeats 1:19's subject and verb.
- **NA28.** There is no variant at 1:19. The NA28 text equals the SBLGNT here.

### Corrections to stated counts
None of substance. The range of Ps 67 ranks and the one-verse uniqueness (Swete; SBLGNT) hold exactly. Add that the uniqueness of the pair is *not* beyond chance (about 1.6 expected). The construction, not the pair, carries the weight.

### Hays
| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Ps 67:19 is quoted at Eph 4:8 |
| Volume | Possible→Strong | The pair alone is at chance level; the identical "εὐδόκησεν + infinitive of κατοικέω + ἐν αὐτῷ" construction is unique in Swete |
| Recurrence | Possible | 2:9 repeats the verb and subject; 3:16 continues the dwelling language; the psalm recurs in the Pauline circle at Eph 4:8 |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | 1:19–20 moves from dwelling to reconciliation and peace "through the blood of his cross"; temple logic |
| Historical plausibility | Strong | A Zion text applied to the Messiah is well within Second Temple practice [S] |
| History of interpretation | Possible [S] | Noted by a minority of commentators (it has been raised in temple-themed readings of Colossians) [S] |
| Satisfaction | Possible | It makes sense of the unusual "pleased … to dwell" with πλήρωμα as subject |

### Verdict
**Confirmed with nuance.** Recommended rating: **moderate–high on wording; moderate on function.** The contact is a single verse with a unique construction. It is not a rare pair: on the null the pair is unremarkable, and its weight lies in the identical syntax. The temple function is coherent but not specific to Ps 68, because Hag 1:8, Ps 131 and 3 Macc 2 carry the same "chosen dwelling" idea. A sermon may use the temple theme, provided it presents Ps 68 as the nearest wording within a wider Zion-dwelling tradition, not as the sole source.

### Missed
The dwelling sequence in Colossians: κατοικέω at 1:19 and 2:9, then ἐνοικέω at 3:16. Hag 1:8 and 3 Macc 2:16 as εὐδοκέω-temple rivals. Ps 131/132 as the thematic twin in both Greek and Hebrew. The agreement of Eph 4:8 with Swete's ἀναβάς against Rahlfs.

## A2 — καταβραβεύω (2:18) / βραβεύω (3:15) as intended wordplay

### What I tested
- The NT counts and morphology of both verbs (SBLGNT lemma index).
- Both verbs in Swete, by word-beginning and then by stem-anywhere search (see the method note).
- **A baseline.** I searched the whole SBLGNT for every verb pair made up of a simplex and its prepositional compound, with both verbs rare in the NT. I asked how often both fall in one book, and how often in Paul.
- Every third-person imperative in Colossians, to test whether the two uses sit in a designed frame.

### Results
- **Counts.** καταβραβεύω occurs once in the NT (Col 2:18) and βραβεύω once (Col 3:15). Cognates: βραβεῖον ("prize") at 1 Cor 9:24 and Phil 3:14. *Holds exactly.*
- **Morphology.** Both are third-person singular present active imperatives: καταβραβευέτω and βραβευέτω. *Confirmed.*
- **NA28.** No variant bears on either verb. The NA28 text equals the SBLGNT.
- **LXX (Swete).** καταβραβεύω: none. βραβεύω: Wis 10:12, "in a hard contest she judged him the victor" (ἀγῶνα ἰσχυρὸν ἐβράβευσεν αὐτῷ). Also the compound συμβραβεύω in 1 Esd 9:14 ("assisted as arbiters"). A word-beginning search returns *nothing*. The stated "any LXX use?" would have been wrongly answered "none" without the stem-anywhere search. Wis 10:12 shows the athletic-arbiter sense of the simplex in Jewish Greek, which supports reading 3:15 as "let peace *umpire*", not merely "rule".
- **Baseline: compound and simplex in one book.**
  - In the NT, 19 verb pairs (a simplex and its compound) have *both* members as NT hapaxes. Six of the 19 fall in a single book: Luke three times, Acts twice, and Colossians once. **Colossians is the only Pauline letter with such a pair.**
  - If "rare" means at most two NT uses each, 52 pairs qualify and 15 fall in one book. Three of those 15 are in the Pauline corpus: Gal 3:15/3:17 κυρόω/προκυρόω ("ratify"/"ratify beforehand"), 2 Tim 3:6/4:3 σωρεύω/ἐπισωρεύω ("heap up"/"heap up for oneself"), and Col 2:18/3:15.
  - Of the two comparators, Galatians is plainly deliberate. It is part of a legal argument, and the same verse group adds ἀκυροῖ ("annuls", 3:17), a third member of the family. 2 Timothy looks incidental.
  - So the pattern is rare in Paul. When it occurs inside an argument, it can be intended.
- **The imperative frame.** Colossians has exactly four third-person imperatives:
  - 2:16, μή … τις ὑμᾶς κρινέτω ("let no one judge you");
  - 2:18, μηδεὶς ὑμᾶς καταβραβευέτω ("let no one disqualify you");
  - 3:15, ἡ εἰρήνη τοῦ Χριστοῦ βραβευέτω ("let the peace of Christ umpire");
  - 3:16, ὁ λόγος τοῦ Χριστοῦ ἐνοικείτω ("let the word of Christ dwell").

  The first two are negative, with "you" as object and human adjudicators as subject. The second two are positive, with "of Christ" subjects. The pairing is exact, two against two, and both pairs belong to the adjudication field: judge, disqualify, umpire. That is structural evidence for design which the claim did not cite.

### Corrections to stated counts
None. Add the LXX evidence (Wis 10:12; 1 Esd 9:14) that the stated test called for.

### Hays (internal echo; adapted)
| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | The same letter |
| Volume | Strong | The same root, both NT hapaxes, the same person, tense and mood |
| Recurrence | Possible | Paul does play on compound and simplex elsewhere (Gal 3:15–17) |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | The false adjudicator (2:16–18) is answered by Christ's peace and word (3:15–16) |
| Historical plausibility | Strong | Athletic and arbitration imagery is common in Paul (1 Cor 9:24; Phil 3:14) |
| History of interpretation | Possible [S] | Several commentators note the link; few press it as design [S] |
| Satisfaction | Strong | It explains the odd choice of βραβεύω where the NT never uses it otherwise |

### Verdict
**Confirmed with nuance; recommend raising it.** Recommended rating: **moderate–high as intended wordplay** (high lexically). The baseline shows that a both-hapax compound/simplex pair is rare in Paul: Colossians is the only case. The four third-person imperatives form an exact negative/positive frame, which turns a lexical coincidence into a probable design. The precise sense of καταβραβεύω ("disqualify", "rule against" or "rob of the prize") is debated [S], so a sermon should not hang a reversal on one gloss.

### Missed
The 2:16/2:18 → 3:15/3:16 imperative frame. Wis 10:12 for the arbiter sense. The Galatians 3 comparator.

## A3 — Isaiah 45:3 at Col 2:3, and Isaiah 45:14 at Col 1:27

### What I tested
- θησαυρός ("treasure") with ἀπόκρυφος ("hidden") in one verse and within one verse either side (Swete). The same with any hiding word (κρύπτω and its family, by stem anywhere).
- θησαυρός with σοφία ("wisdom"), and with σοφία *and* γνῶσις ("knowledge") within two verses.
- θησαυρός with ἀόρατος ("unseen").
- The wording of Isa 45:3, 45:14 and 45:15 in Swete and Rahlfs.
- The exact phrase ἐν σοὶ ὁ θεός ("God is in you"), as whole words, across Swete. The NT echoes of Isa 45.
- WLC: מַטְמוֹן ("hidden treasure"); אוֹצָר ("treasure") with חָכְמָה ("wisdom") and דַּעַת ("knowledge"); Isa 45:3 and 45:15 in Hebrew.
- Window ranks against Col 2:2–3 and Col 1:26–27.

### Results — Col 2:3 and Isa 45:3
- **θησαυρός with ἀπόκρυφος, one verse.** Swete: Isa 45:3 and 1 Macc 1:23 only. *Holds.* It also holds within one verse either side. The positive control was Isa 45:3, which was returned.
  - 1 Macc 1:23 is Antiochus taking "the hidden treasures which he found" from the temple. The match is verbal only.
  - By chance about 0.09 co-occurrences would be expected; 2 are observed.
  - With any hiding word (stem anywhere), the list adds Sir 20:30 and 41:14 (σοφία κεκρυμμένη, "hidden wisdom", with θησαυρὸς ἀφανής). Note that Sirach's "unseen treasure" is ἀφανής, not ἀόρατος.
- **Wording correction (Isa 45:3).** Swete prints θησαυροὺς σκοτινοὺς ἀποκρύφους, ἀοράτους ἀνοίξω σοι. That is σκοτινούς, with the comma *after* ἀποκρύφους. Rahlfs prints θησαυροὺς σκοτεινούς, ἀποκρύφους ἀοράτους, with the comma *before*. The claim's "σκοτεινούς, ἀποκρύφους, ἀοράτους" matches neither exactly. In Rahlfs "hidden, unseen" go together as one description. The wording point stands either way.
- **θησαυρός with ἀόρατος:** Isa 45:3 only. **θησαυρός with σοφία, one verse:** Sir 1:25, 20:30, 41:14 and **Isa 33:6**. The claim did not name Isa 33:6.
- **θησαυρός, σοφία and γνῶσις within two verses.** Prov 2:4–6 is the true triad: θησαυρούς 2:4, ἐπίγνωσιν θεοῦ ("knowledge of God") 2:5, and σοφίαν with γνῶσις 2:6. Three other hits are generic: Jer 10:12–14, its doublet Jer 28:15–17, and Theodotion's Dan 1:2–4 (temple treasury; youths skilled in wisdom). Note that Prov 2:5 has ἐπίγνωσις θεοῦ, and Col 2:2 has ἐπίγνωσιν τοῦ μυστηρίου τοῦ θεοῦ. Prov 2:2–6 therefore matches four Colossians words in three verses: σύνεσις ("understanding") 2:2, ἐπίγνωσις, θησαυροί, and σοφία with γνῶσις.
- **New rival in Isaiah itself: Isa 33:6.**
  - Swete: "salvation is in treasures (ἐν θησαυροῖς); wisdom and understanding (σοφία καὶ ἐπιστήμη) … these are treasures (θησαυροί) of righteousness."
  - In the WLC it is stronger still. אוֹצָר with חָכְמָה and דַּעַת in one verse: **Isa 33:6 only** in the Hebrew Bible ("wisdom and knowledge … the fear of the LORD is his treasure"). The positive control was the verse itself, which was returned.
  - Within two verses, the same three lemmas meet also at Jer 10:12–14, Jer 51:15–17 and Dan 1:2–4. These are generic, as in the Greek.
  - So the exact Col 2:3 triad of *treasures, wisdom and knowledge* stands in one Hebrew verse, and that verse is in Isaiah.
- **מַטְמוֹן.** Five WLC verses: Gen 43:23; Isa 45:3; Jer 41:8; Job 3:21; Prov 2:4. *Holds* (here in Tanak order). Also, מַטְמוֹן with חָכְמָה and דַּעַת within three verses: Prov 2:2–5 only.
  - **Nuance.** In Isa 45:3 the Greek θησαυρούς renders אוֹצְרוֹת ("treasures"). מַטְמֻנֵי מִסְתָּרִים ("hoards of secret places") is rendered by ἀποκρύφους ἀοράτους. In Prov 2:4 θησαυρούς renders מַטְמוֹנִים.
  - So Prov 2:4 and Isa 45:3 do meet on מַטְמוֹן in Hebrew. In Greek, though, the word comes out once as "treasures" and once as "hidden". Colossians' "treasures … hidden" combines both outcomes. That suits a reader of the Hebrew, or of both Greek verses together. It is not a single Greek source.
- **Hebrew, missed.** Isa 45:15 reads אֵל מִסְתַּתֵּר ("a God who hides himself"). It shares the consonantal root סתר ("hide") with מִסְתָּרִים in 45:3. Within Isa 45 (one book, read as a whole), "hidden hoards" (45:3) and "the God who hides himself" (45:15) frame the confession "God is in you" (45:14). The LXX loses this: at 45:15 it reads "you are God, and we did not know". It bears on any hidden/revealed reading of Col 1:26–2:3.

That is a sense-artefact and needs discounting. **No single text dominates.** Daniel 2, Proverbs 2 and Isaiah 45 are all within the top ten.

### Results — Col 1:27 and Isa 45:14
- **ἐν σοὶ ὁ θεός as whole words.** Swete: Isa 45:14 and Pss Sol 17:1 only. In Pss Sol 17:1 ὁ θεὸς ἡμῶν is a vocative ("in you, O our God, our soul will boast"). *Holds.* A substring search falsely adds Gen 49:25, 1 Chr 12:18 and Bel 37 ("…helped you, God"). These were checked and discarded.
- **Rahlfs Isa 45:14** has the same clause, ὅτι ἐν σοὶ ὁ θεός ἐστιν, and adds καὶ ἐροῦσιν ("and they will say") before "there is no God besides you". Hebrew: אַךְ בָּךְ אֵל ("surely God is in you").
- **Window ranks of Isa 45 against Col 1:26–27:** median 122, with a spread of 57 to 202. **There is no field support** for Isa 45 behind 1:26–27. The contact rests on a three-word phrase and a change of referent.
- **Missed, and positive for the claim's availability: 1 Cor 14:25.** The outsider "will fall on his face and worship God (προσκυνήσει τῷ θεῷ), declaring that God is really among you (ὄντως ὁ θεὸς ἐν ὑμῖν ἐστιν)". Isa 45:14 has προσκυνήσουσίν σοι … ὅτι ἐν σοὶ ὁ θεός ἐστιν: worship by outsiders, and the confession "God is in you". This is a widely recognised echo [S], and the wording supports it: προσκυνέω, θεός, ἐν + pronoun, ἐστιν. The same verse has τὰ κρυπτά … φανερά ("the hidden things … made manifest"). So Paul elsewhere applies Isa 45:14 to the church, within a hidden-manifest frame. That raises availability. But in 1 Cor 14:25 it is still *God* in the church, confessed by outsiders, not a reversal. The "reversal" (Gentiles now indwelt by Christ) remains the claimant's synthesis.
- **Isa 45 in Paul's hand.** Rom 14:11 quotes Isa 45:23 ("every knee shall bow …"), prefaced by "as I live, says the Lord", which comes from elsewhere in Isaiah [S]. Phil 2:10–11 echoes it. As a calibration, the window ranks of Isa 45 against Phil 2:6–11 run from 2nd to 5th (median 3): the scan does find a recognised allusion when the vocabulary is shared. Add 1 Cor 14:25 (Isa 45:14) [S]. *The chapter was in Paul's hand: holds, and is stronger than stated.*

### Verdict
- **Col 2:3 / Isa 45:3: Confirmed with nuance.** Recommended rating: **moderate** for the claim as framed (a wisdom idiom with no single source). Isa 45:3 is the only LXX verse to join "treasures" and "hidden" of what God gives. But the wisdom-and-knowledge content comes from Prov 2:2–6 and Isa 33:6. Isa 33:6 is the only Hebrew verse with treasure, wisdom and knowledge together, and it lies within the same book. Isa 45:3 *as source* is low–moderate. Isaiah as a whole (33:6 with 45:3) is moderate.
- **Col 1:27 / Isa 45:14: Uncertain — flag for research.** Recommended rating: **low–moderate** (below the stated "moderate at most"). 1 Cor 14:25 shows that Paul used the verse. But the Colossians contact is too slight to carry a reversal reading without independent support.

## A4 — Genesis 1 behind Col 1:15, as distinct from 3:10

### What I tested
- ἀόρατος ("invisible") anywhere in Swete, by stem anywhere, with every hit read for sense. ὁρατός ("visible") likewise.
- κατ᾽ εἰκόνα ("according to the image") as a phrase in Swete and the SBLGNT.
- εἰκών with κτίζω ("create"), augmented forms included. εἰκών near ὁρατός or ἀόρατος. πρωτότοκος ("firstborn") with κτίζω.
- "Image of God" in the SBLGNT.
- Rahlfs Gen 1:2.

### Results
- **ἀόρατος.** Swete: Gen 1:2, Isa 45:3 and 2 Macc 9:5. *Holds exactly.*
  - In Gen 1:2 it describes **the earth**: "the earth was invisible and unformed", rendering תֹהוּ. Rahlfs is identical.
  - In 2 Macc 9:5 it describes God's "unseen blow". In Isa 45:3 it describes treasures.
  - **No LXX verse calls God ἀόρατος.** "The invisible God" (Col 1:15) is therefore not a Genesis phrase. The Gen 1:2 contact is a shared adjective with a different referent. ὁρατός hits are almost all ὁρᾶτε ("see!") or δόρατ- ("spear"); the true ὁρατός cases (Job 34:26; 37:21; 2 Sam 23:21; 1 Chr 11:23) are irrelevant. The LXX never pairs ὁρατός with ἀόρατος.

The "creator" of Col 3:10 (τοῦ κτίσαντος) therefore has precedent and does not weaken the Genesis link. Wis 2:22, the preceding verse, has "they did not know the mysteries (μυστήρια) of God", a small bridge to A5.

- **Window ranks of Gen 1 against Col 1:15–20.** Chapters, all words: 20th. Chapters, rarer words: 14th. 8-verse windows, all words: 24th. 8-verse windows, rarer words: 52nd. 16-verse windows, all words: 49th. 16-verse windows, rarer words: 37th. Median 37. *"14th–52nd" holds exactly.* Against the shorter Col 1:15–18 the range is 11th–34th, median 24.

Two of the thirteen have a partner at rank 1 in all six settings: Phil 4:4–9 with 4 Macc 1 (a virtue list), and 2 Cor 5:1–6 with Wis 9 (the earthly tent). So a top rank in every setting is not rare in itself. Ps 88 differs from those genre matches because its shared words include the rare, title-bearing πρωτότοκος, together with creation, fullness and thrones, which are Col 1:15–20's own vocabulary. The firstborn of Ps 89:27 is a standard proposal for Col 1:15 and 1:18 (and Rev 1:5) [S]. It is a *Davidic-royal* rival to Gen 1 for "firstborn of all creation".
- **Wisdom rivals.**

  | Text | Median rank against Col 1:15–20 | Spread |

  | Wis 7 | 101 | |
  | Prov 8 | 109 | |

  Sir 24 ranks high on ἀρχή ("beginning"), ἐκκλησία, ἐξουσία ("authority"), θρόνος and κτίζω. Wis 7:26 ("she is … an image (εἰκών) of his goodness") is the closest verbal parallel for εἰκών *of God* attributed to a mediator. Prov 8:22 ("the Lord created (ἔκτισεν) me as the beginning (ἀρχήν) of his ways") supplies ἀρχή and κτίζω. These rank low on the scan but are the standard wisdom background [S]. The thesis that Col 1:15–18 expounds בְּרֵאשִׁית ("in the beginning") through Prov 8:22's רֵאשִׁית is a known scholarly proposal [S]. It is a strong phrase: κατ᾽ εἰκόνα with a creator verb and ἄνθρωπος (3:9), which in the LXX stands only in Gen 1:26–27, 5:1 and Sir 17:3. But the field-rank method cannot see it. That is a further caution against reading low window ranks as negative evidence.

## A5 — Daniel 2 (Theodotion and Old Greek) at Col 1:26–27 and 2:2–3

### What I tested
- μυστήριον ("mystery") with γνωρίζω ("make known") in one verse (augmented forms included).
- μυστήριον with σοφία within three verses. μυστήριον with hiding words, revealing words and φαίνω-family words ("appear, make manifest") within two verses. μυστήριον with αἰών ("age").
- The distribution of μυστήριον across Swete. 1 Enoch in Swete.
- Rahlfs for both Greek texts of Daniel at 2:22–30. Aramaic רָז ("mystery") with הוֹדַע ("make known") in the WLC.
- NT parallels to the revelation schema. Window ranks against Col 1:26–27 and 2:2–3.

### Results
- **μυστήριον with γνωρίζω, one verse.** Swete: Theodotion Dan 2:28, 2:29 and 2:30 only. *Holds.* A word-beginning search finds only 2:30, because 2:28 and 2:29 have ἐγνώρισεν. The positive control needed the stem-anywhere search. Chance would give about 0.05 co-occurrences; 3 are observed, all in one passage.
  - The Aramaic agrees. רָז with the verb יְדַע ("know"; haphel "make known") in one verse: Dan 2:28, 2:29, 2:30 and 4:6. Theodotion's γνωρίζω tracks הוֹדַע, where the Old Greek uses δηλόω ("show").
- **μυστήριον with σοφία within three verses.** Wis 6:20–24 (σοφία in 6:22 itself), the Old Greek Dan 2:18–30 and Theodotion Dan 2:18–30. *Holds* for the noun σοφία. If the adjective σοφός ("wise") is admitted, Theodotion Dan 4:3/4:6 and the "wise men" verses of 2:47–48 join. Theodotion 2:30 has σοφία in the same verse as μυστήριον. *Holds.*
- **Theodotion Dan 2:22** reads ἀποκαλύπτει βαθέα καὶ ἀπόκρυφα ("he reveals deep and hidden things"). *Holds.* The Old Greek has σκοτεινά ("dark things"). Compare Col 2:3, ἀπόκρυφοι.
- **Correction on φανερόω against ἀποκαλύπτω.** The claim says that Colossians uses φανερόω and not "Daniel's ἀποκαλύπτω". That is true of Theodotion only. The **Old Greek** uses ἐκφαίνω ("bring to light, make manifest") at 2:19 (ἐξεφάνθη), 2:30 (ἐξεφάνθη) and 2:47 (ὁ ἐκφαίνων μυστήρια κρυπτά, "who brings hidden mysteries to light"). That is the φαίνω family, closer to φανερόω than ἀποκαλύπτω is. The Old Greek 2:47 also has κρυπτά ("hidden"). **The two Greek texts divide the Colossians vocabulary between them:** Theodotion supplies γνωρίζω and ἀπόκρυφα; the Old Greek supplies "made manifest" and "hidden". Neither has both halves.
- **καὶ νῦν ("and now") at Dan 2:23.** Rahlfs Theodotion has καὶ νῦν ἐγνώρισάς μοι ("and now you have made known to me"). *Holds.* Swete's Theodotion lacks νῦν. Swete's Old Greek *has* καὶ νῦν ἐσήμανάς μοι ("and now you have shown me"). So the "now" of disclosure belongs to both Greek traditions; only Swete's Theodotion lacks it. Compare Col 1:26, νῦν δὲ ἐφανερώθη ("but now has been made manifest").
- **Distribution.** μυστήριον stands in 32 Swete verses, all in late books: Wisdom 4, Sirach 4, Judith 1, Tobit (both texts) 4, 2 Maccabees 1, Old Greek Daniel 7, Theodotion Daniel 8, 1 Enoch 3. Any μυστήριον contact will therefore fall in one of these books.
- **Rivals.**
  - **Wis 6:22** (Swete): "what wisdom is and how she came to be I will declare, and *I will not hide mysteries from you* (οὐκ ἀποκρύψω ὑμῖν μυστήρια), but will trace her from the beginning of creation (ἀπ᾽ ἀρχῆς γενέσεως) and *set the knowledge of her in the open* (θήσω εἰς τὸ ἐμφανὲς τὴν γνῶσιν αὐτῆς)". This verse alone has mysteries, hiding, wisdom, knowledge, "from the beginning" and "into the open" (the φαίνω family again). It matches the movement of Col 1:26 and 2:3 as closely as Daniel does, and in a wisdom setting. It ranks low on the scan (median 75 against 1:26–27) because its vocabulary is spread out.
  - **Wis 2:22**: "they did not know the mysteries of God", next to the image text at 2:23.
  - **1 Enoch (Swete).** μυστήριον occurs at 1 En 9:6, 10:7 and 16:3. **1 En 9 is the top window partner for Col 1:26–27**, with a median rank of 4 (1–7). Its shared words are holy, ages, generations, glory, God, mysteries and "manifest" (φανερά, 9:5). But in Enoch the "mysteries of the age (τὰ μυστήρια τοῦ αἰῶνος)" are *illicitly* revealed by Azazel (9:6), and in 16:3 by the Watchers. That is a revelation scene of the opposite kind. It is a genre match, not a source.
  - **Ps 144 (LXX).** It ranks second to fifth on doxological words: "to make known (γνωρίσαι) to the sons of men … the glory (δόξα) of the majesty of your kingdom; your kingdom is a kingdom of all the ages (αἰώνων) … in every generation (γενεᾷ)" (144:12–13). It has no μυστήριον. It shows that "make known … glory … ages … generations" is ordinary liturgical register. That weakens any argument from those words alone.
  - **Sirach** (22:22; 27:16–17: a friend's secrets) and **Tobit** 12:7, 11 ("it is good to hide a king's secret, but to reveal the works of God") use μυστήριον for secrets, not eschatological disclosure.
  - **Qumran** רָז texts are *outside the corpus*. I could not test them. From memory they are the closest Semitic parallels for "mystery" as hidden divine purpose [S].
- **Paul's own schema (the NT rival).**
  - μυστήριον with ἀποκρύπτω ("hide"): 1 Cor 2:7, Eph 3:9 and Col 1:26 (SBLGNT). Eph 3:9 has the identical "hidden from the ages" (τοῦ ἀποκεκρυμμένου ἀπὸ τῶν αἰώνων). 1 Cor 2:7 joins wisdom, mystery, hidden and "before the ages".
  - μυστήριον with γνωρίζω: Eph 1:9, 3:3, 6:19 and Col 1:27.
  - μυστήριον with φανερόω: Col 1:26 and 1 Tim 3:16.
  - Rom 16:25–27 (mystery kept silent for long ages, now manifested, made known to the nations) is *not present in the corpus's SBLGNT layer*. It is cited from memory [S].

  So Col 1:26–27 is a Pauline formula with its own NT recurrence. Daniel 2 would be background to the formula, not the immediate source of Colossians' wording.
- **Window ranks.**

  | Against | Theodotion Dan 2 | Old Greek Dan 2 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Col 1:26–27 | median 10 (6–19); 6–10 in the rarer runs | median 18 (11–79) |
  | Col 2:2–3 | median 3 (1–9) | median 24 |

  Against 2:2–3, Theodotion Dan 2 is the strongest single partner: it is first among chapters on all words. The claim's "Old Greek ranked lower" *holds*.
- **Theodotion as a later recension [S].** The historical Theodotion is late second century AD. But "proto-Theodotionic" readings of Daniel appear in the NT (for example Heb 11:33, "shut the mouths of lions") and in first-century writers [S]. So a Theodotion-type Daniel is *possibly* available to Paul but not secure. That is why the Old Greek's φαίνω vocabulary matters for the claim: it does not depend on Theodotion.

But the Old Greek, not only Theodotion, supplies "manifest" and "hidden". Wis 6:22 is an equal rival for the hidden-to-open movement in a wisdom setting. Col 1:26's wording is a recurring Pauline formula (1 Cor 2:7; Eph 3:9).

## A6 — Mark 7:1–23 behind Col 2:8 and 2:16–22

### What I tested
- παράδοσις ("tradition") with ἄνθρωπος ("man") in one verse (SBLGNT). κατὰ τὴν παράδοσιν ("according to the tradition") as a phrase.
- βαπτισμός ("washing") in the NT. ἔνταλμα ("commandment") in the NT and in Swete. ἔνταλμα with διδασκαλία ("teaching") in Swete and the NT. διδασκαλία with ἄνθρωπος.
- βρῶσις with πόσις ("eating", "drinking"). ἅπτω with γεύομαι ("touch", "taste") in the NT and in Swete.
- Isa 29:13 in Swete and Rahlfs.
- Synoptic comparison of Mark 7:1–23 with Matt 15:1–20 at each contact. Pauline rivals.
- The NA28 apparatus at Col 2:12 and 2:22.

### Results
- **παράδοσις with ἄνθρωπος, one verse:** Mark 7:8 and Col 2:8 only. *Holds.* The positive control was Mark 7:8, which was returned. Both read τὴν παράδοσιν τῶν ἀνθρώπων, with identical articles. Chance would give 0.8; 2 are observed. The pair is unremarkable as a pair. The *exact phrase* is the point, and it occurs only here.
- **κατὰ τὴν παράδοσιν:** Mark 7:5 ("… of the elders"), Col 2:8 ("… of men") and 2 Thess 3:6 (the apostolic tradition).
- **βαπτισμός:** Mark 7:4, **Col 2:12**, Heb 6:2 and 9:10. "Only Gospel use" *holds*. But the claim did not note that Colossians itself uses βαπτισμός at 2:12, *in the sense of Christian baptism* ("buried with him in baptism"). NA28 prints βαπτισμῷ with 𝔓⁴⁶, א², B, D*, F and G; many witnesses read βαπτίσματι. Mark 7:4's "washings of cups and pots" is a different sense. **This is not a link.** If anything, Col 2:12 tells against reading βαπτισμός as a Mark 7 contact.
- **ἔνταλμα:** three NT verses: Matt 15:9; Mark 7:7; Col 2:22. *Holds.* In Swete, ἔνταλμα occurs in four verses: Job 23:11, 23:12; Isa 29:13; Isa 55:11. ἔνταλμα with διδασκαλία: Isa 29:13 only. *Holds.*
- **Word order: the decisive synoptic test.**
  - Swete and Rahlfs Isa 29:13: διδάσκοντες ἐντάλματα ἀνθρώπων καὶ διδασκαλίας ("teaching commandments of men and teachings").
  - Mark 7:7 and Matt 15:9, identically: διδάσκοντες διδασκαλίας ἐντάλματα ἀνθρώπων ("teaching as teachings commandments of men").
  - Col 2:22 (SBLGNT and NA28): κατὰ τὰ ἐντάλματα καὶ διδασκαλίας τῶν ἀνθρώπων.

  **Colossians keeps the LXX order** (ἐντάλματα first, then καί διδασκαλίας). It moves only "of men" to the end, where it governs both nouns. The Gospels instead put διδασκαλίας first and drop καί. So the claim's "Colossians reorders the wording" needs correcting. Colossians departs from the *Gospel* form back towards the *LXX* form. **On this evidence Col 2:22 is not dependent on the Synoptic form of the quotation.** It draws on Isa 29:13 directly, or on a tradition closer to the LXX.
- **διδασκαλία with ἄνθρωπος:** Matt 15:9, Mark 7:7, Col 2:22 and **Eph 4:14** ("every wind of teaching … by the trickery of men"). ἐντολή with ἄνθρωπος: Mark 7:8 and **Titus 1:14** ("Jewish myths and commandments of men"), besides two irrelevant cases.
- **βρῶσις with πόσις, one verse:** John 6:55, Rom 14:17 and Col 2:16. The food contact with Mark 7 is thematic, not verbal. Mark 7:19 has βρώματα ("foods"). Colossians uses βρῶσις, as Rom 14:17 does.
- **ἅπτω with γεύομαι:** Col 2:21 only (NT). Swete: no real case. The two stem hits, ἥψησεν ("boiled", Gen 25:29) and θάψω ("bury", Tob 2:4 Sinaiticus), are false.
- **What is Mark's own, against Matt 15:1–20?**
  1. **"The tradition of men" (Mark 7:8).** Matthew has only "the tradition of the elders" (15:2) and "your tradition" (15:3, 6). *This is the one wording contact that singles out Mark.*
  2. βαπτισμοί (7:4) and the explanatory aside on washings (7:3–4). These are Mark's own, but not a Colossians contact (see above).
  3. "Making all foods clean" (7:19). Mark's own, but Colossians has no βρῶμα and no καθαρίζω ("cleanse").

  **Shared with Matthew:** the Isa 29:13 citation (in an identical form); "commandment of God" against human tradition; the "what enters the mouth" saying (Matt 15:11, 17, 20; Mark 7:15, 18–19).
- **Pauline rivals** for the same polemic, with no Gospel needed:
  - Titus 1:14–15: "commandments of men", then "all things are pure to the pure".
  - Rom 14:14: "nothing is unclean in itself", *in the Lord Jesus*. That last phrase is often taken as an appeal to dominical tradition [S].
  - Rom 14:17: "the kingdom of God is not food and drink (βρῶσις καὶ πόσις)".
  - Rom 14:20: "all things are clean".
  - Gal 1:14: Paul's own "traditions of my fathers".

  Rom 14 is the closest Pauline parallel on food, and it shares βρῶσις καὶ πόσις with Col 2:16.

### Corrections to stated counts
- All counts hold.
- Correct "Colossians reorders the wording": Colossians keeps the LXX order and connective; the Gospels reorder it.
- Add Col 2:12 (βαπτισμός = baptism), which removes βαπτισμός as evidence.

### Hays (Mark 7 specifically)
| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Possible [S] | A pre-Markan Jesus tradition is plausible; written Mark is uncertain at Colossians' date [S] |
| Volume | Possible | "The tradition of men" (exact, Mark only); Isa 29:13 is shared with Matthew and in LXX order in Colossians |
| Recurrence | Possible | Rom 14:14 "in the Lord Jesus" [S]; Titus 1:14–15 |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | Human tradition, food and purity |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | |
| History of interpretation | Possible [S] | |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Isa 29:13 plus Paul's own Rom 14 explain most of it |

### Verdict
**Needs reframing.** Recommended rating: **moderate** for "Paul's polemic draws on the Jesus tradition about human tradition and food, with Isa 29:13". **Low–moderate** for *Mark* specifically.
- The evidence does not single out Mark 7 over Matt 15. The only Mark-specific wording is "the tradition of men" (Mark 7:8 = Col 2:8).
- The Isa 29:13 contact at 2:22 follows the LXX order, not the Gospel order. That points to the LXX directly.
- βαπτισμός should be dropped, because Col 2:12 uses it for baptism.

The honest frame is a shared dominical tradition, plus Paul's own reading of Isa 29:13 (LXX), with Mark 7:8 as the nearest wording for 2:8.

## New candidates

1. **Psalm 89 (LXX 88) behind Col 1:15–20.** It ranks first in every window setting and supplies the firstborn title (88:28), creation, "fullness", thrones and "assembly". It is a Davidic-royal rival to Genesis 1 for "firstborn of all creation", and also relevant to 1:18 ("firstborn from the dead"). It should be audited in its own right.
2. **Isaiah 33:6 behind Col 2:3.** The only Hebrew verse uniting treasure, wisdom and knowledge (WLC), within the same book as 45:3. The LXX keeps treasures and wisdom.
3. **Isa 45:3 with 45:15 in Hebrew.** "Hoards of secret places" (מִסְתָּרִים) and "a God who hides himself" (מִסְתַּתֵּר) share the root סתר and frame 45:14. This matters for any hidden/revealed reading of Col 1:26–2:3, and it is lost in the LXX.
4. **Wisdom 6:22 behind Col 1:26 and 2:3.** Mysteries not hidden, from the beginning, wisdom and knowledge set in the open. It is an equal rival to Daniel 2 for the movement of the passage.
5. **1 Cor 14:25 as Paul's own use of Isa 45:14.** It strengthens availability for A3 (1:27), while showing that Paul's known use is not a reversal.
6. **The adjudication frame of Col 2:16–18 and 3:15–16.** Two negative and two positive third-person imperatives, carrying κρίνω ("judge"), καταβραβεύω ("disqualify"), βραβεύω ("umpire") and ἐνοικέω ("dwell"). This is a structural basis for A2, and it links to the dwelling language of 1:19 and 2:9.
7. **Haggai 1:8 and 3 Maccabees 2:15–16** as εὐδοκέω-temple texts beside Ps 67:17 for the temple function of 1:19.
8. **Prov 2:2–6 behind Col 2:2–3.** It shares four Colossians terms, including ἐπίγνωσις θεοῦ ("knowledge of God", Prov 2:5) answering Col 2:2's ἐπίγνωσιν τοῦ μυστηρίου τοῦ θεοῦ. It ranks a median of 7 against Col 2:2–3. It is stronger than the Sirach texts named in the claim, which rank in the 200s.
