# Claims under audit — Philemon (two design claims)

Text: Philemon 1–25. Substrate: SBLGNT with its word index (`corpus/greek-nt-sblgnt/` and `_index/18-Phlm.tsv`); NA28 for the printed text and apparatus (`corpus/logos-exports/02-New-Testament/18-Philemon-NA28*.txt`); Swete's LXX (`corpus/greek-lxx-swete/`) for the Greek Old Testament. NASB95 for English reference only.

The claims are stated as a proposing run stated them. Test them; do not assume them.

## P1 — the "frame of hearts"

**P1a (lexical).** σπλάγχνον ("heart, inward parts") occurs in Philemon at vv. 7, 12 and 20 only; ἀναπαύω ("to refresh") at vv. 7 and 20 only. The two lemmas stand together in one verse at Phlm 7 and Phlm 20 and **nowhere else in the New Testament**, and **nowhere in Swete's LXX**.

**P1b (lexical).** Both v. 7 and v. 20 carry the vocative ἀδελφέ ("brother"); these are claimed to be the only singular vocative "brother" in any New Testament letter.

**P1c (design).** vv. 7 and 20 form a deliberate frame around the body of the appeal (vv. 8–19), with v. 12 ("that is, my very heart", of Onesimus) at its centre, so that the letter argues: Philemon has refreshed the saints' hearts (v. 7); Onesimus *is* Paul's heart (v. 12); therefore to receive Onesimus is to refresh Paul's heart (v. 20). The proposing run rated the words high and the *design* moderate, with no chance baseline.

**P1d (rival, raised by the proposing run against itself).** 2 Cor 7:13–15 shares χαρά ("joy") + παράκλησις ("comfort") + ἀναπαύω in one verse (claimed: only 2 Cor 7:13 and Phlm 7 in the NT), and σπλάγχνα + ὑπακοή ("obedience") in one verse (claimed: only 2 Cor 7:15). If the vocabulary is simply Paul's idiom of joy at refreshed spirits, does that weaken P1c?

## P2 — the ὑπέρ escalation

**P2a (lexical).** ὑπέρ occurs in Philemon at vv. 13, 16 and 21 only: ὑπὲρ σοῦ ("on your behalf", v. 13), ὑπὲρ δοῦλον ("more than a slave", v. 16), ὑπὲρ ἃ λέγω ("more than what I say", v. 21).

**P2b (design).** The three uses form an escalating sequence, from what Onesimus did "on your behalf" (v. 13), to what he now is, "more than a slave" (v. 16), to what Philemon will do, "more than what I say" (v. 21), and the sequence hints at the content of v. 21's "more".

**P2c (inference drawn from it).** Because v. 13 has already named Paul's wish to keep Onesimus for service, the v. 13 → v. 21 link leans the "even more" of v. 21 towards **release of Onesimus to Paul's service**, rather than towards manumission or simple better treatment.

The proposing run rated P2b and P2c moderate (design), with no baseline.
