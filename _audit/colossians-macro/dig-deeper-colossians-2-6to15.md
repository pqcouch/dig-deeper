## Headline Findings

1. **2:6–7 is the letter's first command and its thesis, and it is built out of the opening chapter.** `[T]` περιπατεῖτε ("walk", 2:6) is the first imperative of the letter's argument: 1:24–2:5 has none. One command governs four participles — rooted, being built up, established, overflowing — which describe the walking and are not further commands (Tool 3). Each element answers 1:3–23: "walk … the Lord" (περιπατέω with κύριος, 1:10 → 2:6); "just as you learned from Epaphras" (καθὼς ἐμάθετε, 1:7) → "just as you were taught" (καθὼς ἐδιδάχθητε, 2:7); the stability participles with "the faith" (τῇ πίστει, 1:23 → 2:7); thanksgiving (1:3, 12 → 2:7). περιπατέω ("walk") occurs in the letter only at 1:10, 2:6, 3:7 and 4:5 (SBLGNT, by lemma). *Surfaced by:* Tool 3, Tool 4 (οὖν, "therefore"), Tool 11 Move 4, Tool 10. The hinge of the letter is a call to keep going the way they began. *High confidence.*

2. **The polemic answers the rival with the confession's own words.** `[T]` The ground-clause ὅτι ἐν αὐτῷ ("for in Him") stands in Paul's letters only at 1:16, 1:19 and 2:9 (SBLGNT; the two other NT uses are in 1 John). πλήρωμα ("fullness") with κατοικέω ("dwell") stands in the NT only at 1:19 and 2:9. κεφαλή ("head") with ἀρχή ("rule") and ἐξουσία ("authority") stands in one NT verse only, 2:10. σταυρός ("cross") occurs in the letter only at 1:20 and 2:14; νεκρός ("dead") only at 1:18, 2:12 and 2:13 (all SBLGNT, by lemma). **Chance baseline, run here for the first time** (Book-Overview Tensions): of 1:15–20's 24 rarer words, the seven-verse stretch 2:8–14 shares seven, more than any other stretch of the letter; outside 2:5–19 no stretch shares more than three. *Surfaced by:* Tool 10, Tool 11 Move 4, Tool 2. The overview's "arsenal" claim stands on its specific links. As a measured design it is real, and — on claim audit 1's comparator test — unmatched among Pauline confessions: Phil 2:6–11, Rom 3:21–26, Eph 1:3–14, 1 Cor 15:20–25, 2 Cor 5:16–21, Rom 8:28–33 and Gal 4:1–6 show nothing like it. **Corrected baseline (v1.2):** 1:15–20 has 23 rarer words on content words (25 on all words), not 24. Seven or more shared is reached by 7 of the letter's 90 six-verse passages on content words (14 on all words), and the other high scorers are the letter's epistolary frame, not confessions. Adjacency does not explain it: 1:21–27 shares only three `[S: audit 1]`. *High confidence on the links; moderate–high on the design (confirmed with nuance).*

3. **"Made complete in Him" (2:10) is spelt out as union, not addition.** `[T]` In ten verses ἐν αὐτῷ ("in Him") stands five times (2:6, 7, 9, 10, 15) — five of the letter's eight — and ἐν ᾧ ("in whom/which") twice (2:11, 12). Then three verbs compounded with σύν ("with"): συνθάπτομαι ("buried with", 2:12), συνεγείρω ("raised with", 2:12), συζωοποιέω ("made alive with", 2:13), with σὺν αὐτῷ ("together with Him") (SBLGNT). συνθάπτομαι occurs in the NT only here and Rom 6:4; συζωοποιέω only here and Eph 2:5. *Surfaced by:* Tool 10, Tool 7, Tool 4, Christological Reading. The rival offers something more. Paul answers that what the Colossians have is Christ himself, with whom they have died and risen. *High confidence.*

4. **The cross deals with everything the rival claims to manage — guilt, the record against us, and the powers.** `[T]` 2:13–15 runs three participle clauses into the cross: "having forgiven us all our transgressions" (2:13); "having blotted out the certificate of debt … nailing it to the cross" (2:14); "having stripped off the rulers and authorities … triumphing over them" (2:15). ἀρχαί and ἐξουσίαι ("rulers and authorities") are **created** through Christ at 1:16, **headed** by him at 2:10 and **stripped and paraded** at 2:15 — their only three appearances as a pair in the letter. 1:13's "domain (ἐξουσία) of darkness" is the fourth use of ἐξουσία (SBLGNT). χαρίζομαι ("to forgive") occurs only at 2:13 and 3:13. *Surfaced by:* Tool 3, Tool 10, Tool 11 Move 4, Biblical Theology. Who is the subject of 2:15, and whether ἐν αὐτῷ means "in Him" or "in it" (the cross), are open questions (Difficult Verses). The scope of the victory is not. *High confidence on the structure; moderate on the subject of 2:15.*

5. **The warning is defined by its standard: three "according to"s, one "not".** `[T]` "According to the tradition of men, according to the elements of the world, and **not** according to Christ" (2:8). Both phrases recur at the end of the polemic and frame it: τὰ στοιχεῖα τοῦ κόσμου ("the elements of the world") at 2:8 and 2:20, the letter's only two uses; "of men" at 2:8 and 2:22 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. And the "tradition" (παράδοσις) of 2:8 is set against what they "received" (παρελάβετε) at 2:6 — the pair Paul uses elsewhere for the handing-on of the gospel itself (1 Cor 11:23; 15:3; 2 Thess 3:6, SBLGNT) `[T]`/`[I]`. The only other NT verse with "tradition" and "men" together is Mark 7:8 `[T]`, where Jesus sets "the tradition of men" against God's command in a dispute about food and washing, which is the subject of Col 2:16–22. *Surfaced by:* Tool 4, Tool 3, Tool 10, Tool 11 (Mark 7). The question to ask of any teaching is not "is it deeper?" but "whose is it — human tradition, or Christ?" *High confidence on the links. v1.2: the Mark 7 echo is reframed by claim audit 1 — **moderate** for a shared dominical tradition about human tradition and food; **low–moderate** for Mark 7 specifically. "The tradition of men" (Mark 7:8) is the one Mark-only contact. Col 2:22 keeps the LXX order of Isa 29:13 against the Gospels' order `[S: audit 1]`.*

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: Preaching against a reconstructed "Colossian heresy"

- **What it looks like:** a lecture on first-century Phrygian syncretism, Jewish mysticism or Gnosticism, then its modern equivalent.
- **Why it's wrong:** the passage names standards, not a system — "the tradition of men", "the elements of the world", "not according to Christ" (2:8). The reconstruction is `[S]` (overview Trap 2).
- **The corrective:** preach the test the text gives — *is this according to Christ?* — and let the congregation recognise their own "more".

### Pitfall: Turning the indicatives into tasks

- **What it looks like:** "We must circumcise our hearts, put off the flesh, nail our sins to the cross, triumph over the powers."
- **Why it's wrong:** every verb in 2:11–15 has God or Christ as its agent; the readers are circumcised, buried, raised, made alive and forgiven. The passage has two commands and eight indicatives.
- **The corrective:** preach what has been done, then the two commands as the response — walk in him; don't be carried off.

### Pitfall: Making 2:11–12 the sermon's battleground for baptism

- **What it looks like:** a sermon that becomes a defence of infant baptism (circumcision → baptism) or of believer's baptism (through faith).
- **Why it's wrong:** the passage's point is union with Christ in his death and resurrection, received through faith and signified in baptism. It does not explain the relation of circumcision and baptism.
- **The corrective:** say what the text says — circumcised *in Christ*, buried *with him* in baptism, raised *through faith* — and keep the debate for another setting.

### Pitfall: "The law was nailed to the cross" — so God's moral will is gone

- **What it looks like:** using 2:14 to dismiss any moral obligation.
- **Why it's wrong:** what is nailed is the *certificate of debt* "against us", not righteousness; the letter goes straight on to "put to death" and "put on" (3:5–17).
- **The corrective:** preach the debt cancelled and its claim on us ended.

### Pitfall: Leaving the powers in the first century — or preaching fear of them

- **What it looks like:** either silence about "rulers and authorities", or a sermon that makes the congregation more afraid of spiritual powers.
- **Why it's wrong:** the passage names the powers in order to show them defeated, stripped and paraded.
- **The corrective:** name them — and name them as beaten. The fear is what the rival sells; the triumph is what Christ gives.

### Christological Reading

*NT passage.* Christ is named three times (2:6, 8, 11) and referred to as "Him" throughout `[T]`. The passage's Christological claims:

1. **Christ is the full dwelling of God** — "in Him all the fullness of Deity dwells bodily" (2:9), the strongest statement of Christ's deity in the letter `[T]`.
2. **Christ is the sphere of the believer's life** — "in Him" five times (Headline 3) `[T]`.
3. **Christ's death and resurrection are the believer's** — circumcised in his circumcision, buried and raised with him, made alive with him (2:11–13) `[T]`.
4. **Christ's cross is the place of forgiveness and of victory** (2:14–15) `[T]`.
5. **Christ is head of every power** (2:10) — the creator of 1:16, now their head and conqueror `[T]`.

**Typology in view:** circumcision (Gen 17) → "the circumcision of Christ". Tests: theological category (covenant sign) — pass; NT precedent (Rom 2:29; Phil 3:3) — pass; escalation (made without hands; the whole body of the flesh, not a part) — pass; authorial pattern (Deut 30:6 already points inward) — pass. **High confidence as typology**, whichever reading of the genitive is taken.

**Moralism check.**

- *The "be like X" temptation:* turning 2:11–15 into tasks — "circumcise your heart", "put off the flesh", "triumph over the powers".
- *The gospel grounding:* every verb in 2:11–15 has God or Christ as agent; the readers receive (Tool 15).
- *Christ as hero:* the triumph is his. The passage's two commands (2:6, 8) stand on eight indicatives (Tool 3).
