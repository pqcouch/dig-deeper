## Headline Findings

1. **"Put to death therefore" is the letter's answer to the regime it has just rejected — and the "members" to be killed are named by the list that follows, not by the limbs of the body.** `[T]`/`[I]` Surfaced by: Positional Necessity, Linking Words, Translations, Move 4. οὖν ("therefore", 3:5) draws on "you died" (3:3) and repeats 3:2's "on earth" (τὰ ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς) `[T]`. Two verses earlier the rules were "of no value against fleshly indulgence" (2:23); now the flesh is dealt with — by death, not regulation `[T]`/`[I]`. "The members which are on earth" are set in apposition to five accusatives — immorality, impurity, passion, evil desire, greed — so the "members" are those practices `[T]`. The 2:16–23 dig reached the same reading `[S: dig]` (reached independently). **The NASB95 restructures the verse ("consider the members of your earthly body as dead to immorality …"), softening the imperative into a reckoning and turning the list into what one is dead *to*** (Tool 8). *High.*

2. **Two lists of five, moving from the body and possessions to the tongue — and the second list begins with the word for God's wrath.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Repetition, Vocabulary. 3:5: πορνεία, ἀκαθαρσία, πάθος, ἐπιθυμία κακή, πλεονεξία (sexual immorality, impurity, passion, evil desire, greed); 3:8: ὀργή, θυμός, κακία, βλασφημία, αἰσχρολογία (anger, wrath, malice, slander, foul speech), "from your mouth", followed by "do not lie to one another" (3:9) `[T]`. 3:12 will answer with five virtues `[T]`. ὀργή ("wrath, anger") stands in Colossians only at 3:6 ("the **wrath** of God is coming") and 3:8 ("put away … **anger**") (SBLGNT) `[T]`: **the anger believers are to put off is the very word for the wrath that comes on these things** `[I]`. *High on the words.*

3. **Greed is called idolatry — and the first list ends where idolatry begins.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Tool 11 (Eph 5:5; Wisdom 14). πλεονεξία ("greed") is defined: ἥτις ἐστὶν εἰδωλολατρία, "which is idolatry" `[T]`. Ephesians makes the same identification of the greedy person (πλεονέκτης, ὅ ἐστιν εἰδωλολάτρης, Eph 5:5); the two nouns stand together within a verse only at Col 3:5, and the agent nouns at 1 Cor 5:10, 11 and Eph 5:5 (SBLGNT) `[T]`. The Greek Bible's Wisdom of Solomon traces sexual immorality to idolatry ("the devising of idols was the beginning of fornication", Wis 14:12) — the same field, approached from the other end `[T]`/`[I]`. *High on the identification; moderate on the field.*

4. **The old self has been stripped off and the new put on — done, as Christ stripped off the powers — and the new self is being renewed after the image of the Creator.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Original Languages, Tool 11 (Gen 1:26–27), Move 4. ἀπεκδυσάμενοι … ἐνδυσάμενοι ("having stripped off … having put on", aorist participles, 3:9–10) — ἀπεκδύομαι stands in the NT only at 2:15 (Christ "stripped off" the rulers and authorities) and 3:9 (SBLGNT) `[T]`; the 2:6–15 dig recorded the pair `[S: dig]`. The new self is "being renewed" (present passive) "into knowledge, according to the image of the One who created him" (κατ᾽ εἰκόνα τοῦ κτίσαντος αὐτόν) — Gen 1:27, κατ᾽ εἰκόνα θεοῦ ἐποίησεν αὐτόν ("according to the image of God he made him"), rated high by the overview and confirmed high at 3:10 by claim audit 1 `[S: audit 1]`. εἰκών ("image") and κτίζω ("to create") stand in Colossians only at 1:15–16 and 3:10 (SBLGNT) `[T]`: **the image in which the new humanity is renewed is the Son who is "the image of the invisible God"** `[I]`, moderate–high. *High on the words.*

5. **In the renewed humanity the letter's own divisions are named and dissolved, and "Christ is all" takes up the confession's "all things".** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Vocabulary, Tool 11 (Gal 3:28; 1 Cor 12:13), Move 4. The list begins "Greek and Jew" — the reverse of Gal 3:28 and 1 Cor 12:13, which begin with the Jew `[T]`. It adds "circumcision and uncircumcision" (the letter's own words at 2:11, 13), "barbarian, Scythian" (Σκύθης, its only NT occurrence; a byword for savagery in 2 Macc 4:47 and 3 Macc 7:5), and "slave, free" (who will be addressed at 3:22–4:1 and embodied in Onesimus, 4:9) `[T]`. It has no "male and female"; some Western witnesses add it from Gal 3:28 `[T]`. And it ends ἀλλὰ [τὰ] πάντα καὶ ἐν πᾶσιν Χριστός — the τὰ πάντα ("all things") created, held and reconciled in him (1:16, 17, 20) `[T]`. *High on the words; moderate–high that the ordering is deliberate.*

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: A list without a ground

- **What it looks like:** a sermon on ten sins to avoid.
- **Why it's wrong:** the commands rest on "you died" (3:3) and "having put off … having put on" (3:9–10). Without them the sermon becomes the rule-keeping the letter has just rejected (2:20–23; overview moralism check).
- **The corrective:** preach the ground at least as long as the commands.

### Pitfall: "Consider yourself dead" as the whole response

- **What it looks like:** following the NASB95, "reckon yourself dead to these things" as an act of the mind.
- **Why it's wrong:** the Greek is an aorist imperative, "put to death"; the members are the practices themselves.
- **The corrective:** call for decisive action — the ESV's "put to death" is closer.

### Pitfall: "Old self / new self" as a civil war inside the believer

- **What it looks like:** "Your old nature and your new nature are fighting; feed the right one."
- **Why it's wrong:** the old self has been stripped off (aorist); the passage describes two humanities, the old and the renewed, not two selves within.
- **The corrective:** preach identity and renewal: you are the new; become what you are.

### Pitfall: Ending at 3:9

- **What it looks like:** a sermon that stops at the sins and the lying.
- **Why it's wrong:** the passage ends on the renewed image and "Christ is all, and in all".
- **The corrective:** make 3:10–11 the climax.

### Pitfall: 3:11 as a slogan for erasing every distinction — or as a merely inward truth

- **What it looks like:** using 3:11 against the household instructions; or reducing it to "we're all equal before God" with no practical effect.
- **Why it's wrong:** the letter keeps the categories and addresses them (3:18–4:1), while denying them any power to rank in Christ, and acts on it (4:9).
- **The corrective:** preach the social reality within the church: who sits where, who is listened to, who is called brother.

### Christological Reading

**Type of connection:** NT; Christ is the image after which the new self is renewed (3:10 with 1:15, moderate–high) and the "all and in all" (3:11) `[T]`/`[I]`. Gen 1's image-bearer finds its original and its goal in him: typology passes the theological-category test (image, creation), the NT-precedent test (Rom 8:29; 2 Cor 4:4), the escalation test (Adam bore the image; Christ is it) and partly the authorial-pattern test (Genesis's image language returns in 5:1 and 9:6) — *moderate–high*.

**Moralism check:**
- **The "be like X" temptation:** "kill these ten sins" as self-improvement.
- **The gospel grounding:** "you died" (3:3); "having stripped off … having put on" (3:9–10); "being renewed" (passive) `[T]`.
- **Christ as hero:** he is the image, the one in whom the new humanity is made, and "all" `[T]`.

**Confidence:** *high* on the Christ-centredness.
