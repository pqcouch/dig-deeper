## Headline Findings

1. **The unit is bracketed by Paul's joy and by Paul's own flesh, and everything inside the bracket is a minister's labour "for you".** `[T]` χαίρω ("to rejoice") occurs in the letter only at 1:24 and 2:5 — the first and last verses of the unit (SBLGNT, by lemma). Between them σάρξ ("flesh") is used three times of Paul's own body: suffering "in my flesh" (1:24), unseen "in the flesh" (2:1), absent "in the flesh" (2:5). ὑπέρ ("on behalf of") stands three times (twice at 1:24, once at 2:1). *Surfaced by:* Tool 3 (structure), Tool 10 (repetition), Tool 7 (vocabulary), Tool 9 (tone). The passage is not autobiography for its own sake. It is the stranger in chains telling a church that has never seen him that his suffering and striving are for them. *High confidence.*

2. **The mystery is a person, and the unit names him three times, each time more closely: "Christ in you, the hope of glory" (1:27); "God's mystery — Christ" (2:2); "in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (2:3).** `[T: as NA28 prints it]` NA28 reads τοῦ μυστηρίου τοῦ θεοῦ, Χριστοῦ ("the mystery of God, Christ") at 2:2 with 𝔓⁴⁶ and B. The apparatus records seven other readings, and every one of them reads as an attempt to explain the short text (Textual Variants). μυστήριον ("mystery") occurs in Colossians only at 1:26, 1:27, 2:2 and 4:3 (SBLGNT). *Surfaced by:* Tool 7, Tool 10, Tool 4 (the relatives ὅ, ὅν, ἐν ᾧ that chain the identifications), Textual Variants, Original Audience. The content of the secret is not a doctrine or a technique. It is Christ, now open to Gentiles, and already holding every treasure the rival teaching will claim to add (2:8, 23). *High confidence on the reading as printed; the variant is the one place the headline rests on a textual decision.*

3. **Colossians 1:24 is about a minister's suffering for the church, not about any shortfall in the atonement — and the letter's own vocabulary shows it.** `[T]`/`[I]` In Colossians ὑπέρ ("on behalf of") occurs in six verses — 1:7, 1:9, 1:24, 2:1, 4:12, 4:13 — and every one describes a minister (Epaphras or Paul) praying, suffering or labouring for churches. It is never used of Christ's death (SBLGNT). θλῖψις ("affliction") is predicated of Christ nowhere else in the New Testament: of its 43 verses, only Col 1:24 joins it to Christ as his own (Rom 8:35 and Phil 1:17 have both words, but the afflictions there are the believer's and Paul's) `[T]`. The reconciliation has already been stated in finished, aorist terms (1:20, 22) `[T]`. And the phrase "in my flesh … for the sake of His body" answers "in the body of His flesh" at 1:22 `[T]`: Christ reconciled in his body of flesh; Paul suffers in his flesh for Christ's body. The two other New Testament uses of ἀναπληρόω ("to fill up") with ὑστέρημα ("what is lacking") — 1 Cor 16:17 and Phil 2:30 — both mean supplying what someone's absence leaves lacking `[T]`. *Surfaced by:* Tool 7, Tool 10, Tool 2, Tool 11 (Pauline usage), Difficult Verses. Which of the live readings of "what is lacking in Christ's afflictions" is right remains open (Difficult / Contested Verses). The absence reading has a named proponent, Garland, and on the evidence of Phil 2:30 it survives Beale's grammatical objection to it (v1.1) `[S: Logos 1]`. The atonement reading is ruled out by the letter's own grammar of ὑπέρ and its finished verbs. **v1.3:** the three commentaries now supplied in full — Dunn, O'Brien and Wright — all read the verse by the messianic woes joined to Christ's sharing in his body's sufferings, and none takes the absence reading `[S: Logos 2]`. *High confidence that the verse is not about atonement; moderate on the positive reading, with a slight lean to the woes-and-body reading (v1.3).*

4. **The passage turns the opening prayer into Paul's labour, and the letter's closing hands the same labour to other men.** `[T]` The prayer of 1:9 — "filled with the knowledge (ἐπίγνωσις) of His will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding (σύνεσις)" — returns in Paul's struggle at 2:2: "the full assurance (πληροφορία) of understanding (σύνεσις), resulting in a true knowledge (ἐπίγνωσις) of God's mystery". σύνεσις ("understanding") occurs in the letter only at 1:9 and 2:2. Then 4:12 gives Epaphras "always labouring earnestly (ἀγωνιζόμενος) for you (ὑπὲρ ὑμῶν) in his prayers, that you may stand perfect (τέλειοι) and fully assured (πεπληροφορημένοι)". ἀγωνίζομαι ("to strive") occurs only at 1:29 and 4:12, and τέλειος ("complete, mature") only at 1:28 and 4:12. παρακαλέω ("to encourage") with καρδία ("heart") stands only at 2:2 and 4:8, where Tychicus is sent "that he may encourage your hearts" (all SBLGNT, by lemma). *Surfaced by:* Tool 11 Move 4, Tool 10, Tool 2. Paul's apostolic agony for a church he has not seen is carried, in the closing names, by the church's own founder and envoys. **Synthetic as a design:** the closing block 4:2–18 shares more of this unit's rarer vocabulary than any other seventeen-verse stretch of the letter (thirteen words that occur in at most four verses of Colossians; the next independent stretch, 1:7–23, shares eleven). The design is *moderate*; each individual link is *high*. **v1.2:** the 13-against-11 margin is not itself evidence: a margin of two or more is ordinary in the letter, occurring for 41–53 of its 85 eleven-verse passages. What is unusual is the long range — apart from the confession's redeployment in chapter 2, this is the letter's only long-range pairing at this level. And Romans has the same frame (15:14–33 with 1:8–17), though Philippians, 2 Corinthians and 2 Timothy do not. The πληροφορ- link (2:2 → 4:12) holds as a word family but is not one of the thirteen lemma matches `[S: audit 1]`.

5. **The unit is the hinge that turns the confession into the warning.** `[T]`/`[I]` "I say this so that no one (μηδείς) will delude you with persuasive argument" (2:4) is the first warning in the letter, and it is restated four verses later: "See to it that no one (μή τις) takes you captive through philosophy and empty deception" (2:8). Before either warning, the passage has placed "all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" in Christ (2:3). σοφία ("wisdom") runs 1:9 → 1:28 → 2:3 → 2:23, where the rival rules have only "the appearance of wisdom" `[T]`. And the warning is softened by joy: Paul rejoices to see their "good discipline and the stability of your faith" (2:5). *Surfaced by:* Phase 0.6, Tool 4 (three ἵνα clauses), Tool 11 Move 4, Tool 9. The polemic of 2:6–23 is prophylactic, aimed at a church in good order, and it is set up by the claim that nothing is left outside Christ for anyone to sell them. *High confidence on the links; the "prophylactic" reading is `[I]`, moderate–high.*

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: 1:24 as a deficient atonement — or explained away in a sentence

- **What it looks like:** "Paul says Christ's sufferings were not enough", or the opposite, a hurried "of course it doesn't mean that" with nothing said about what it does mean.
- **Why it's wrong:** the letter has just said the reconciliation is finished (1:20, 22). The word for "afflictions" is never used of the cross. And Colossians uses "on behalf of" only for ministers labouring for churches. But the verse is not empty: it says something true and costly about the church's sufferings and the minister's.
- **The corrective:** say what it cannot mean and why, in one breath; then preach what it does mean — Christ shares his people's afflictions, and Paul bears a share *for* the church. Use 1:22 as the key: Christ reconciled in the body of his flesh; Paul suffers in his flesh for Christ's body.

### Pitfall: "Christ in you" as a private spiritual experience

- **What it looks like:** a sermon on the indwelling Christ in the individual heart, using 1:27 as a slogan.
- **Why it's wrong:** the verse is about the *Gentiles*: "this mystery among the Gentiles, which is Christ in you" — and the "you" is plural. What is astonishing is not that Christ lives in a heart but that God's long-hidden purpose has reached the nations.
- **The corrective:** preach the plural. The mystery is Christ in the midst of a former-pagan congregation, their hope of glory (3:4).

### Pitfall: "Mystery" preached as "something we cannot understand" — or as secret depth for the advanced

- **What it looks like:** "God's ways are a mystery", or "there are deeper truths for the mature".
- **Why it's wrong:** the mystery has been "manifested" (1:26), "made known" (1:27), "proclaimed" to "every man" (1:28). It is a secret God has told, to everyone.
- **The corrective:** define the word as Paul does — a purpose hidden and now disclosed — and give its content: Christ.

### Pitfall: Paul the hero, or the preacher as Paul

- **What it looks like:** a sermon on the heroic sufferings of the apostle, or a minister applying 1:24–29 to himself ("this is what ministry costs me").
- **Why it's wrong:** the stewardship was given "to me for you" (1:25); the congregation is the "you". The labour is "according to His working" (1:29). And the passage's method of ministry is handed to the whole church at 3:16.
- **The corrective:** let the congregation hear themselves as the people for whom Paul suffered, and then hear 3:16 — they are now to admonish and teach one another in all wisdom.

### Pitfall: 2:4–5 as a scolding of a failing church

- **What it looks like:** "The Colossians were being led astray, and Paul is warning them."
- **Why it's wrong:** Paul rejoices "to see your good discipline and the stability of your faith" (2:5). The warning is preventative.
- **The corrective:** preach the warning as a friend's care for a church in good order — and so as a word for a healthy congregation, not only for a troubled one.

### Christological Reading

*NT passage:* Christ is named in five of its eleven verses (SBLGNT) `[T]`. Four Christological claims are made:

1. **Christ is the content of the mystery** — "Christ in you" (1:27); "God's mystery, Christ" (2:2). Not a doctrine about him, but he himself `[T]`.
2. **Christ is the treasury** — "in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (2:3). The confession's "in Him" (1:16, 17, 19) is now the location of all wisdom `[T]`.
3. **Christ suffers in his body** — "the afflictions of Christ … for the sake of His body, which is the church" (1:24) `[T]`. The risen Lord who reconciled all things (1:20) still shares his people's afflictions `[I]`.
4. **Christ's power works in his servant** — "according to His working, which works mightily within me" (1:29); that working is the power that raised him from the dead (2:12) `[T]`/`[I]`.

**Typology in view:** none claimed. Daniel 2 supplies a revelation-pattern, not a type.

**Moralism check.**

- *The "be like X" temptation:* "Be like Paul — rejoice in suffering, labour harder, strive in prayer." The passage does describe things that become patterns (Copycat). But the passage's subject is what Christ has done through Paul *for them*.
- *The gospel grounding:* the mystery has been manifested; Christ is in them; the power is his; the goal is that they be presented complete "in Christ" (1:28), which is God's work before it is Paul's (1:22).
- *Christ as hero:* the minister labours "according to His working" (1:29). Paul is the instrument, not the protagonist.

**Confidence:** high on the four claims as stated by the text; moderate on the "Christ suffers in his body" reading of 1:24.
