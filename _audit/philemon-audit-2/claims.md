# Claims under audit — Philemon (six claims)

Text: Philemon 1–25. Substrate: SBLGNT with its word index (`corpus/greek-nt-sblgnt/`, `_index/`); NA28 text and apparatus for Philemon (`corpus/logos-exports/02-New-Testament/`); Swete's LXX (`corpus/greek-lxx-swete/`, verse numbering as Swete prints it); the WLC Hebrew of the Torah with its word index, and of Job (`corpus/hebrew-wlc/`). NASB95 for English reference only.

The claims are stated as a proposing run stated them, with its ratings. Test them; do not assume them.

## P3 — name-play on Onesimus

(a) Ὀνήσιμος ("Onesimus") means "useful, profitable"; v. 11's τόν ποτέ σοι ἄχρηστον νυνὶ δὲ … εὔχρηστον ("formerly useless to you, but now useful") plays on the name. (b) v. 20's ἐγώ σου ὀναίμην ("let me benefit from you"), from ὀνίνημι ("to benefit"), its only NT occurrence, plays on the name again, two verses after Paul's offer to repay. (c) A further pun is proposed in v. 11 on χρηστός ("useful") / Χριστός ("Christ"), said to have been pronounced alike. Ratings: (a) and (b) high; (c) moderate–uncertain.

## P4 — the Torah's restitution law behind vv. 18–19

Paul's pledge ἐγὼ ἀποτίσω ("I will repay", v. 19), after εἰ δέ τι ἠδίκησέν σε ἢ ὀφείλει ("if he has wronged you in any way or owes you anything", v. 18), echoes the Covenant Code's restitution law, where ἀποτίνω ("to make restitution") is the key verb: claimed 30 verses in Swete, 15 of them in Exod 21:19–22:17 (Swete numbering); with a word of wrongdoing (ἀδικ-) in the same verse, three (Exod 22:9; Lev 6:5; Ezek 33:15). The chapter opens with the slave law (Exod 21:2–6). Rival: the commercial promissory-note idiom of the papyri. Rating: uncertain–moderate.

## P5 — the "forever slave" contrast behind vv. 15–16

ἵνα αἰώνιον αὐτὸν ἀπέχῃς, οὐκέτι ὡς δοῦλον ("that you would have him back forever, no longer as a slave") contrasts with the Torah's permanent slave — Exod 21:6, δουλεύσει αὐτῷ εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα ("he shall serve him for ever"); Deut 15:17, ἔσται σοι οἰκέτης εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα ("he shall be your servant for ever") — and with Lev 25:39, the brother who is not to be made to serve as a slave. Claimed: a δουλ- word with αἰών in one verse, 18 verses in Swete; ἀδελφός with a δουλ- word in the Torah, only Lev 25:39 and Deut 15:12. Rival in wording: Job 40:23 (Swete) δοῦλον αἰώνιον, of Leviathan. Rating: uncertain (a reader's contrast).

## P6 — Col 4:9 as the public counterpart of Phlm 16

Col 4:9 calls Onesimus τῷ πιστῷ καὶ ἀγαπητῷ ἀδελφῷ, ὅς ἐστιν ἐξ ὑμῶν ("the faithful and beloved brother, who is one of your number") — the status Phlm 16 asks Philemon to grant, ἀδελφὸν ἀγαπητόν ("a beloved brother"). If the letters were delivered together, Colossians states in public what Philemon asks in private. Rating: high on the words; moderate on reading the two together.

## P7 — the freewill offering behind v. 14

ἵνα μὴ ὡς κατὰ ἀνάγκην τὸ ἀγαθόν σου ᾖ ἀλλὰ κατὰ ἑκούσιον ("so that your goodness would not be, in effect, by compulsion but of your own free will"). In the LXX τὸ ἑκούσιον ("the voluntary thing") is the freewill offering (Lev 7:6 Swete; 23:38; Num 15:3; 29:39; Ezra 3:5). Claimed: καθʼ ἑκούσιον ("as a freewill offering") occurs once in Swete, at Num 15:3; a ἑκουσ- word with an ἀγαθ- word in one verse once, at Ps 53:8 Swete [Eng 54:6], "Willingly I will sacrifice to You … for it is good". Rival: κατὰ ἑκούσιον as ordinary Greek idiom for "voluntarily". Rating: uncertain–moderate.

## P8 — "in the flesh and in the Lord" against "masters according to the flesh"

Phlm 16, καὶ ἐν σαρκὶ καὶ ἐν κυρίῳ ("both in the flesh and in the Lord"), is set against the household codes' τοῖς κατὰ σάρκα κυρίοις ("the masters according to the flesh", Col 3:22; Eph 6:5). Claimed: σάρξ ("flesh") with κύριος ("Lord, master") in one verse, in Colossians, Ephesians and Philemon, only at Col 3:22, Eph 6:5 and Phlm 16. In the codes the slave has a master "according to the flesh"; in Philemon the slave is a brother "in the flesh and in the Lord". Rating: words moderate–high; the contrast as intended, moderate.
