# Claim audit 1 — Obadiah — Auditor B: field and design claims

**Editions.** Hebrew counts: Westminster Leningrad Codex (WLC), by lemma, Hebrew versification. Greek OT: Swete, with Rahlfs read wherever the two might differ; Swete's Jeremiah oracle against Edom is 29:8–23 and Rahlfs' is 30:1–16 (both Hebrew 49:7–22). Greek NT: SBLGNT. BHS apparatus cited where a reading matters. Anything from memory is tagged [S] and carries no verdict.

**Terms.** "Content word" follows the stated method: a lemma found in 1,500 verses or fewer. "Rare pair" means two content words in one verse, a combination found in five verses or fewer of the Hebrew Bible. Canon size: 23,213 verses (WLC). "Expected" means the verse count that the two words' separate frequencies would produce if they fell independently.

## B1. Jeremiah 49 and Obadiah

**Tested.** Chapter ranking at four rarity ceilings; attachment of each pair; subsets of Obadiah; exact phrases; two-pass diff; Greek of both; a null for how often any short prophetic passage has such a partner.

**Counts reproduced.** All hold.
- Jer 49 ranks first of 862 chapters of ten verses or more: 74 pairs in 39 verses, 1.897 per verse. Ezek 31 is second at 0.278.
- The 74 pairs attach to Obad 1 (13), 2 (4), 3 (26), 4 (13), 5 (17) and 8 (1), and to none in vv. 10–21.
- On the Jeremiah side they fall at 49:7, 9, 14, 15, 16 and, once, 49:20.
- The first place is stable. With the ceiling at three, five, ten or twenty verses, Jer 49 stays first (1.64, 1.90, 2.13, 2.51 against second places of 0.19, 0.28, 0.41, 0.67).
- For Obad 10–14 alone Jer 49 falls to 483rd of 862; for 15–21 to 467th; for 10–21 to 506th.
- מִי יוֹרִדֵנִי אָרֶץ ("Who will bring me down to earth?"): Obad 3 only, plain and skeletal. בֵּין כּוֹכָבִים ("among the stars"): Obad 4 only, plain and skeletal. Control: the same search for מִשָּׁם אוֹרִידְךָ ("from there I will bring you down") returned Jer 49:16 and Obad 4.
- יַעֲקֹב ("Jacob", 319 verses) and צִיּוֹן ("Zion", 153 verses) are absent from Jer 49. Control: Edom is found at 49:7, 17, 20, 22, and Israel at 49:1, 2.
- Order holds: Obad 1, 2, 3–4 = 49:14, 15, 16; Obad 5, 6 = 49:9, 10; Obad 8 = 49:7. This is a clean inversion of three blocks.

**Corrections and nuance.**
- "None to vv. 10–21" is true of rare pairs only. Single words do cross: אֵיד ("calamity"), Jer 49:8 and Obad 13; אָח ("brother"), Jer 49:10 and Obad 10, 12; שָׁתָה ("drink"), Jer 49:12 and Obad 16. These matter for B5 and B9.
- "Obadiah alone has the taunt" holds for the wording, not the form. Jer 49:4 gives Ammon מִי יָבוֹא אֵלָי ("Who will come to me?"), and Jer 21:13 has "Who will come down against us?". The closer rival for Obad 3–4's pluses is Isa 14:13, 15: "you said in your heart", "above the stars", "you will be brought down". It is the only verse with כּוֹכָב ("star") and לֵבָב ("heart"), with the descent two verses on.

**Diff, pass one (consonantal).**
- Obad 1–4 (50 words) against Jer 49:14–16 (38 words): 24 words in order.
- There are three runs of four or more words: מֵאֵת יְהוָה וְצִיר בַּגּוֹיִם ("from the LORD, and an envoy among the nations"), הִנֵּה קָטֹן נְתַתִּיךָ בַּגּוֹיִם בָּזוּי ("behold, small I have made you among the nations, despised") and קִנֶּךָ מִשָּׁם אוֹרִידְךָ נְאֻם יְהוָה ("your nest, from there I will bring you down, declares the LORD").
- Obad 5 against Jer 49:9: four words in order, plus the thieves clause transposed.
- Obad 6 against Jer 49:10: only עֵשָׂו ("Esau") is identical. נֶחְפְּשׂוּ ("are searched out") and חָשַׂפְתִּי ("I have stripped bare") have the same three consonants in a different order. מַצְפֻּנָיו ("his treasures", a word found only here) stands for מִסְתָּרָיו ("his hiding places").
- Obad 8 against Jer 49:7: two shared roots, in different syntax.

**Diff, pass two (waw, yod and final he deleted).**
- Obad 1–4 rises only from 24 to 25 words; Obad 5 from four to six.
- Spelling fullness cuts both ways. Jeremiah is the fuller in "sent" and "gleanings", Obadiah in "leave". Orthography gives no direction.
- What separates the two texts is wording, not spelling: this is composition or reworking, not copying.

**Evidence on direction.**

*Pointing to Obadiah as the reworked form:*
1. In Jer 49:16 "your nest" is the object of "make high". In Obad 4 "make high" has lost its object, and the words "and if among the stars set" (which BHS notes are absent from Jer 49:16) carry שִׂים ("set"), a passive participle that BHS would emend or delete. That pointed form with "your nest" occurs only at Num 24:21 and Obad 4. The plus looks built from other wording (see B3).
2. עָלֶיהָ ("against her", Obad 1) is the only third feminine singular suffix in the book. In Jeremiah, feminine בָּצְרָה ("Bozrah") stands in the preceding verse, 49:13.
3. "If plunderers by night, how you are destroyed" (Obad 5) interrupts its verse; BHS asks whether to transpose it or delete it as a gloss from Jer 49:9.

*Pointing to Jeremiah as the reworked form:*
1. Jer 49:7–22 is demonstrably a compilation. Jer 49:19 shares 24 of 27 words with 50:44, 49:20 shares 20 of 24 with 50:45, 49:18 shares 12 of 15 with 50:40, and 49:22 shares 8 of its 17 with 48:41.
2. Jeremiah's plus תִּפְלַצְתְּךָ ("your horror") occurs nowhere else.
3. Obadiah's first-person plural (שָׁמַעְנוּ "we heard", וְנָקוּמָה "let us rise") holds together; so does Jeremiah's singular, however.
4. Obadiah's rhetorical הֲלוֹא ("would they not?") makes a clearer argument than Jeremiah's flat לֹא ("not"), which BHS proposes to correct.

*Neutral:* a block inversion can be made in either direction. Both books also reuse material elsewhere (for Obadiah's side, see Joel 3:5 under New candidates).

**Greek.**
- The translators worked separately. For צִיר ("envoy") Obadiah has περιοχήν ("an enclosing", an odd rendering), Jeremiah ἀγγέλους ("messengers"). For קָטֹן ("small"), ὀλιγοστόν ("least") against μικρόν ("small"). For בָּזוּי ("despised"), ἠτιμωμένος ("dishonoured") against εὐκαταφρόνητον ("contemptible"). For זָדוֹן ("arrogance"), ὑπερηφανία ("pride") against ἰταμία ("recklessness"). For "bring down", κατάξω ("I will bring down") against καθελῶ ("I will pull down").
- The shorter Greek Jeremiah still has all the overlap, and lacks the taunt and the stars. The overlap is therefore not a late addition to the longer Hebrew Jeremiah, and the pluses were already distinct.
- Greek Obadiah's ἤκουσα ("I heard") against the Hebrew "we heard" agrees with Jeremiah. BHS records this. It may be harmonisation or a different Vorlage; it cannot decide the matter.

**Baseline.** I took 120 arbitrary 21-verse stretches of the Latter Prophets and ranked partner chapters outside each stretch's own book. The median top density is 0.33; only 8% reach 1.0. Every stretch at or above 1.0 belongs to a known literary parallel: Isa 37 with 2 Kgs 19, Jer 52 with 2 Kgs 25, Isa 2 with Mic 4, Jer 48 with Isa 16, and Jeremiah's own Edom oracle with Obadiah. So the density places Obad–Jer 49 in the class of real parallels, not of shared vocabulary.

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Counts verified | All exact |
| Baseline | Density reached only by documented parallels; stable at every ceiling |
| Sense | Same oracle, same target; but Obadiah's sentence in v. 5 is a question where Jeremiah asserts |
| Alternative explanation | Common source: fully open. The synonym swaps at Obad 6 suit reworking or a shared tradition better than copying |

**Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. Rating: high for shared material.** The field result is one of the sturdiest in the book. Evidence on direction exists on both sides and does not sum to a verdict, so "open" stands. [S] Scholarship has argued all three positions: that Jeremiah depends on Obadiah, the reverse, and a common source.

**Missed.** Obadiah's pluses against Jeremiah are not random: star and "set" point to Num 24, and "search" to Amos 9:3. It is one modest reason to think Obad 4 and 6 are the worked forms, at least in those words.

## B2. Amos 9:12 as a designed handoff

**Tested.** Each stated contact, with expected counts; by-book rates with intervals; arbitrary 73-verse stretches as a null; all 66 book pairings in the Twelve; order reshuffling; closing-chapter to opening-chapter seams; the Greek order.

**Counts reproduced.** All hold.
- יָרַשׁ ("possess") with אֱדוֹם ("Edom") in one verse: Amos 9:12 only.
- הִסְגִּיר ("hand over") with גָּלוּת ("exiles"): Amos 1:6, 9 only, both also naming Edom.
- אֵשׁ ("fire") with יוֹסֵף ("Joseph"): Amos 5:6, Obad 18.
- מִשָּׁם אוֹרִידֵם ("from there I will bring them down"): Amos 9:2 only.
- By-book rates: Joel first (8 pairs, 73 verses, 10.96 per 100); Jeremiah second (109 in 1,364, 7.99); Amos eleventh (4 in 146, 2.74); Jonah sixteenth (1 in 48, 2.08).

**Corrections and sense.**
- "Only" flatters the Amos 9:12 pair. "Possess" (204 verses) and "Edom" (93 verses) would be expected together in 0.82 verses by chance. One is what chance gives.
- Obadiah never puts the two words together: Obad 19 has "possess" with עֵשָׂו ("Esau"), which also occurs at Deut 2:12, 22 and Josh 24:4. The cognate noun in Num 24:18, יְרֵשָׁה ("possession") with Edom, is an equal rival, and it is part of the B3 cluster. No scan can see this link, since it is not a pair within any verse of Obadiah.
- Amos 1:6, 9: in Amos other nations hand exiles over *to* Edom; in Obad 14 Edom does the handing over, and the object is שְׂרִידָיו ("his survivors").
- Amos 1:11 is not unique for brother with Edom: Gen 32:4, Num 20:14, Amos 1:9, 1:11. Num 20:14's אָחִיךָ יִשְׂרָאֵל ("your brother Israel") is the nearest in form to Obad 10's אָחִיךָ יַעֲקֹב ("your brother Jacob").
- Fire with Joseph (expected 2.88, since Joseph fills Genesis) proves little as a pair. "House of Joseph" with fire and אָכַל ("consume") is sharper: Amos 5:6 and Obad 18 only. The sense is an inversion: fire against Joseph in Amos, Joseph as flame in Obadiah. This is the best Amos link.
- Amos 9:2's closest relative is Jer 49:16 and Obad 4, which share "from there I will bring you down" verbatim. Obad 6's חפשׂ ("search"), however, differs from Jeremiah's word and is at Amos 9:3, in that very passage. That small datum favours contact.

**Chance baselines.**
- *Adjacency.* I normalised each pair's shared rare pairs by the product of the two books' lengths. The eleven adjacent pairs have a mean rank of 34.7 of 66, against 33.5 by chance; 60% of 20,000 random orders of the Twelve do as well or better. Adjacent books share no more than non-adjacent ones. Amos–Obadiah ranks 46th of 66.
- *Seams.* Comparing each book's last chapter to each other book's first, the adjacent seams average 63.6th of 132 (chance 66.5). Amos 9 into Obadiah is high at 6th, but Joel 4 into Obadiah is 2nd and Nah 3 into Obadiah is 4th. Obadiah's shortness also lifts it in many pairings.
- *Rarer words.* By lemmas in 50 verses or fewer, normalised, Amos is seventh of eleven partners; Joel first.

**Is Joel the densest partner, and do short books distort it?**
- Joel holds first place, with limits. The 95% intervals overlap heavily: Joel 4.7–21.6 per 100; Nahum 1.3–18.7; Malachi 1.1–15.9; Habakkuk 1.1–15.7; Amos 0.7–7.0. Short books are distorted: Nahum, Malachi and Habakkuk reach third to fifth on three pairs each.
- Against a real null Joel still stands out. Arbitrary 73-verse stretches of Isaiah, Jeremiah (without ch. 49) and Ezekiel have a median of 3 pairs, and only 0.8% reach 8.
- But some of Joel's eight are weak. One involves only the negative particle; the two from Joel 3:5 count one verse twice; "stubble" with "fire" is idiom. About five independent contacts remain (lots at 4:3; repayment on the head at 4:4, 7; Zion with escape at 3:5; go up and judge at 4:12; strangers and Jerusalem at 4:17), giving a rate near 6.8.
- Leave-one-out: dropping Joel 3:5 leaves 8.2 per 100, still first.
- Jeremiah's second place is wholly Jer 49. Without it, 39 pairs and 2.94 per 100 put Jeremiah about ninth.

**Missed.** Joel 4:19 is the only verse with חָמָס ("violence") and "Edom", and it adds "the sons of Judah". Compare Obad 10 ("violence") and Obad 12 ("sons of Judah"). In Swete's order Joel stands directly before Obadiah; in the Hebrew order Amos does. So two successive books close on Edom, and a handoff theory that chooses Amos depends on the order assumed.

| Hays | Rating |
|---|---|
| Availability | Possible; depends on the shape and order of the Twelve |
| Volume | Weak for Amos 9:12 alone; Possible for Amos 5:6 and 9:2–3 |
| Recurrence | Possible: Amos has six of the Twelve's Edom verses |
| Thematic coherence | Strong |
| Historical plausibility | Possible |
| History of interpretation | Possible [S]: catchword readings of Amos–Obadiah are familiar in Twelve studies |
| Satisfaction | Possible |

**Verdict: Needs reframing. Rating: low–moderate as a designed handoff; moderate as a set of contacts.** The order is a fact and Amos's closing does name Edom, but adjacency has no general lexical effect in the Twelve, the key pair is at chance level, and Joel's closing has at least as strong a claim. Say "Amos ends with Edom and Obadiah is next; a reader will connect them", not "Obadiah unfolds that clause".

## B3. The Num 24:17–21 cluster

**Tested.** Phrases and counts; 19,492 five-verse windows across the canon, scored on Obadiah's rarer words; leave-one-out on the triple; the pointed form; the Greek.

**Counts reproduced.** All hold.
- כּוֹכָב מִיַּעֲקֹב ("a star from Jacob") and אֱדוֹם יְרֵשָׁה ("Edom a possession"): one verse each, plain and skeletal.
- שִׂים בַּסֶּלַע קִנֶּךָ ("set your nest in the rock"): Num 24:21, found by the skeletal pass only (the source spells the conjunction differently).
- שָׂרִיד ("survivor") with יַעֲקֹב ("Jacob"): Num 24:19, Obad 18 only (expected 0.38).
- Scan rank: 66th of 922 at a five-verse floor; 58th of 862 at the ten-verse floor.

**The chapter scan understates it.** The scan works verse against verse and divides by chapter length, so it cannot see a tight cluster spread over five verses.
- Scored by window on Obadiah's rarer words (in 150 verses or fewer), Num 24:17–21 ranks 13th of 19,492. Only overlapping windows of Jer 49 (ten) and Job 39 (two) stand above it. It is the third independent place in the canon after Jeremiah's Edom oracle and Job's eagle.
- Without the proper names Edom, Esau, Jacob and Seir, it ranks 18th.
- Star, nest and survivor in one five-verse window: Num 24:17–21 only.
- Leave-one-out, any two of the three within five verses: nest with survivor, only there; star with survivor, only there; star with nest, only there and in Obadiah itself. No one word carries it.
- Independence would predict about 0.002 such windows for this triple, though that triple was chosen after seeing the texts.

**Sharpest datum.** The pointed form שִׂים ("set") with "your nest" occurs only at Num 24:21 and Obad 4. This is the grammatical oddity BHS would emend, and Jer 49:16 does not have it. Hab 2:9 has "set" and "nest" (with an infinitive) and מָרוֹם ("height", cf. Obad 3), so "set a nest" is shared by three verses, but the form is shared by two.

**Greek (Swete) strengthens it.**
- Num 24:17 ἄστρον ἐξ Ἰακώβ ("a star from Jacob") with Obad 4 ἄστρων ("stars").
- Num 24:18 reads Ἠσαύ ("Esau") for Seir, alongside Ἐδώμ ("Edom") and κληρονομία ("inheritance"); Obadiah's Greek has Esau with κατακληρονομήσουσιν ("they shall inherit", v. 19).
- Num 24:21 ἐὰν θῇς ἐν πέτρᾳ τὴν νοσσιάν σου ("if you set your nest in the rock") with Obad 4 ἐὰν … θῇς νοσσιάν σου ("if … you set your nest"). Only these two verses have "if", "set" and "nest". The Greek of Jer 29:17 has the nest, but neither "if" nor "set".
- Num 24:19 ἀπολεῖ σωζόμενον ("will destroy the one being saved"); Obad 8 ἀπολῶ ("I will destroy"); Obad 14 ἀνασῳζομένους ("those escaping").

**Sense.** The transfers are real but bold. The star in Numbers is a ruler from Jacob; in Obadiah stars are Edom's false height. The nest belongs to the Kenite, not Edom, and Num 24:21–22 itself threatens that nest-builder. שָׂרִיד ("survivor") in Num 24:19 is from a city; in Obad 18 from the house of Esau. The hiphil of אָבַד ("destroy") is ordinary (26 occurrences), and on its own would not count. Num 24:19's וְיֵרְדְּ ("and he shall rule") looks like Obad 3–4's "bring down" but is another root: a sound-play at most.

| Hays | Rating |
|---|---|
| Availability | Strong (Torah), though the date of the Balaam oracles is argued [S] |
| Volume | Possible to Strong: low per word, high in concentration |
| Recurrence | Possible: Edom's dispossession recurs, the Balaam wording does not |
| Thematic coherence | Strong: Edom, Jacob, possession, no survivor |
| Historical plausibility | Possible |
| History of interpretation | Weak to Possible [S]: Num 24:17–18 and Edom is an old connection; reading Obad 4 and 18 from it is not, as I recall, standard |
| Satisfaction | Strong: it explains the odd שִׂים ("set") |

**Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. Rating: moderate–high** (raised above what a 66th-place scan rank suggests). The cluster survives leave-one-out and beats nearly every window in the canon; the Greek agrees independently. Direction is unmarked, and part of the rock-and-nest wording is already in Jer 49:16, so the case rests on the star, the form "set" and the survivor. Say "shares wording with", not "quotes".

## B4. The three-block structure

**Tested.** Reproduced the score; ranked cuts raw and by standard deviations from each cut's own shuffle; varied the word threshold; the same test on 600 arbitrary 21-verse stretches of the Latter Prophets, and on every whole chapter of 15–30 verses in the Twelve; the boundary claim against all boundaries.

**Counts reproduced.** All hold.
- Shared words: 4 (1–9 with 10–14), 4 (1–9 with 15–21), 3 (10–14 with 15–21), total 11.
- 20,000 shuffles: mean 30.3; lowest found 15; none at 11.
- Of 91 cuts (each block at least three verses) this one is second lowest; the lowest is 1–3 | 4–6 | 7–21 at 9, which is merely the effect of two tiny blocks.
- No content word crosses 14|15 within three verses either side.

**Corrections.**
- By the fair measure the result is better than stated: distance from each cut's own shuffle ranks 1–9 | 10–14 | 15–21 **first** of 91 (about 5 standard deviations). Neighbours 1–9 | 10–15 and 1–7 | 8–14 follow.
- The result depends somewhat on the filter: with the threshold at 300 or 800 it stays second; at 3,000, or with no filter, it falls to tenth and eighth.
- "Content words" still include אַתָּה ("you") and עַד ("until").

**Does verse-shuffling overstate cohesion? Yes, greatly.** Shuffling destroys the local clustering every text has, so the null is far too easy.
- Among arbitrary 21-verse stretches of the Latter Prophets, each given its own best balanced cut (blocks of five or more), 49% reach better than 1 in 200 by the same shuffle, and 38% are never matched in 200 shuffles.
- "None in 20,000" therefore tells us little.
- The proper comparison is the ratio of observed to expected. Obadiah's is 0.36. Against arbitrary stretches' best cuts, this sits at the 5th percentile (share at or below: 5%).
- Same result for whole chapters of 15–30 verses in the Twelve: of 39, only Amos 2 (0.32, oracles against nations) goes lower. Joel 4 is 0.44. Isa 34 has an even lower raw score on unconstrained cuts.

So the honest odds are about 1 in 20, not less than 1 in 20,000: a real but ordinary-sized signal of oracular composition.

**The 14|15 boundary.**
- Zero crossing within three verses is not rare: 311 of 3,785 such boundaries (8.2%) in the Latter Prophets and Obadiah, and 71 of about 715 in the Twelve.
- The result is partly made by the filter. יוֹם ("day", 1,930 verses) is excluded as too common, yet it is the book's hinge: vv. 8, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, with v. 14 בְּיוֹם צָרָה ("in the day of distress") meeting v. 15 יוֹם־יְהוָה ("the day of the LORD"). Allow that one word and the boundary is crossed by the key term.
- v. 15 therefore works as a join rather than a clean break.
- 9|10 is also soft: the Greek, Syriac and Latin join מִקָּטֶל ("by slaughter") to v. 10 (BHS: perhaps rightly), as Swete shows with διὰ τὴν σφαγήν ("because of the slaughter").
- Verse-level testing cannot address the view [S] that v. 15a and v. 15b belong to different units.
- WLC has no paragraph marks inside Obadiah, so the Masoretic layout neither supports nor opposes.

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Counts verified | All exact; first of 91 by the fair measure |
| Baseline | Shuffle null invalid; realistic baseline about the 5th percentile |
| Sense | Sentence, crime, Day: coherent by content, independently of counts |
| Alternative explanation | Topic change alone produces vocabulary change; this measures topic blocks, not authorial design |

**Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. Rating: moderate–high.** The division is the best available by vocabulary and fits the content. The stated odds and the "no word crosses" claim should be dropped or restated, since יוֹם ("day") crosses and joins the second and third blocks.

## B5. The talion mirror

**Tested.** All crossing vocabulary; a null for how much any 21-verse prophetic stretch recurs across a same-position block; sense of each link.

**Counts reproduced.**
- כָּרַת ("cut off") vv. 9, 10, 14; אָבַד ("destroy") vv. 8, 12; שָׂרִיד ("survivor") vv. 14, 18; פָּלִיט ("fugitive") v. 14; פְּלֵיטָה ("escape") v. 17.
- 10–14 with 15–21: Jerusalem, survivor, Jacob: three.
- The stated chance mean holds: 8.02 in 20,000 shuffles, with three or fewer in 1.6% of them.

**Do the pointed links beat chance? By count, no.**
- Only seven content words of vv. 10–14 recur elsewhere in the book: survivor, destroy, cut off, Jacob, Jerusalem, שָׁלַח ("send"), and אַתָּה ("you"). A random five verses of Obadiah share 14.2 on average. Only 0.6% do as poorly as this.
- Among words in 400 verses or fewer, four cross; in Latter Prophets 21-verse stretches the median is seven and 88% have four or more.
- So the links are not surplus. They are nearly all the crossing vocabulary the block has. Calling them "pointed" is a claim about sense only.
- Four chosen families recurring together occurs in no other Latter Prophets stretch (of 1,004 windows, none has even three), but the families were picked from Obadiah, so this is not independent evidence.

**By sense they are unequal.**

| Link | Crime | Sentence | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| כָּרַת ("cut off") | v. 14, Edom cuts off Judah's fugitives (active) | vv. 9, 10, Edom is cut off (passive) | Real mirror |
| שָׂרִיד ("survivor") | v. 14, Edom hands over his survivors | v. 18, no survivor for Esau | Real mirror; best of the four. "Hand over" with "survivor": Obad 14 only |
| פָּלִיט / פְּלֵיטָה ("fugitive" / "escape") | v. 14 | v. 17, escape on Zion | Reversal in the victim's favour, not talion on Edom; different lemmas |
| אָבַד ("destroy") | v. 12, Judah's day of ruin: Edom rejoices at it | v. 8, the LORD destroys Edom's wise | Weak: different subject, object and act |

The filter also hides real echoes: בּוֹא ("come", too common for the filter), thieves "come" to Edom (v. 5) and Edom "comes" in at the gate (vv. 11, 13); שָׁלַח ("send"), allies send Edom away (v. 7) and Edom reaches out (v. 13).

**The claim does not need counts, because the text says it.**
- כַּאֲשֶׁר עָשִׂיתָ יֵעָשֶׂה לָּךְ ("as you have done, it will be done to you", v. 15) is the book's own statement, and v. 16 repeats the "as … so" form.
- Comparable wording: Lev 24:19, Judg 1:7, Jer 50:15, 29.
- גְּמֻלְךָ יָשׁוּב בְּרֹאשֶׁךָ ("your deed will return on your head") has its nearest partner at Joel 4:4, 7.

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Counts verified | Exact |
| Baseline | Fails: fewer crossings than chance and than typical oracles |
| Sense | Two real mirrors, one reversal, one weak |
| Alternative explanation | Standard judgement vocabulary repeats in any such oracle; but two links are specific |

**Verdict: Needs reframing. Rating: high that talion is the stated principle; low–moderate as lexical design.** Base it on v. 15a and illustrate it with cut off and survivor. Do not call פְּלֵיטָה ("escape") talion or lean on אָבַד ("destroy"), and do not speak of a whole-book mirror.

## B6. Edom as frame of the Twelve

**Tested.** Distribution of Edom and rival nations by book; rank of Malachi's chapters against Obadiah; each pair; "stubble" as idiom; Greek of Mal 3:19.

**Counts reproduced.**
- זֵד ("arrogant") with קַשׁ ("stubble"): Mal 3:19 only. With the cognate זָדוֹן ("arrogance"): none, as the claim implies.
- אָח ("brother") with עֵשָׂו ("Esau") and יַעֲקֹב ("Jacob") outside Genesis: Mal 1:2 only.

**Strengthening item.** אָח ("brother") with יַעֲקֹב ("Jacob") in one verse outside Genesis, Esau not required: Obad 10 and Mal 1:2 only. This is the real link. (Hos 12:3–4 has it across two verses, without Esau's name.)

**Distribution (verses with Edom, Esau, Teman or Seir).** Hosea 0, Joel 1, Amos 6, Obadiah 7, Jonah 0, Micah 0, Nahum 0, Habakkuk 1 (Teman, a theophany and no judgement), Zephaniah 0, Haggai 0, Zechariah 0, Malachi 3.
- Not a frame: the Twelve's opening book has none, and Obadiah is fourth.
- What it is: a cluster at books 2–4, a gap of seven books, then Malachi.
- Rival nations spread more widely: Egypt in seven books (Hosea to Zechariah), Assyria in five (Hosea to Zechariah).
- What is distinctive: of Edom and thirteen other nations and places tested, Edom is the only one named in Malachi at all.

**Malachi as partner.** Malachi has 3 rare pairs with Obadiah (5.45 per 100, interval 1.1–15.9). Mal 1 ranks 14th of 862 chapters, on 2 pairs; Mal 3 ranks 449th with none.

**Mal 3:19 fails.**
- קַשׁ ("stubble", 16 verses) is burned or set near fire in nine verses, including Exod 15:7, Isa 5:24, 33:11, 47:14, Joel 2:5, Nah 1:10. That is close to idiom.
- The arrogant of Mal 3:15, 19 are not Edom: nothing in Mal 3 names it, and in 3:15 they are the ones the speakers themselves call blessed.
- Jer 50:31–32 (arrogance named, fire set) and Isa 13:11 are as close by subject. Only the shared cognate pulls it towards Obad 3.
- Yet the Greek moves another way. Swete's Mal 4:1 (Hebrew 3:19) reads πάντες οἱ ἀλλογενεῖς ("all the foreigners") and καλάμη ("stubble"). Obad 18 has καλάμην ("stubble") and Obad 11 ἀλλογενῶν ("foreigners"). Those two words in one verse: Mal 4:1 only. The Hebrew cognate link disappears in Greek and a foreign-nation link appears.

**Verdict: Needs reframing. Rating: moderate–high for Obad 10 with Mal 1:2–4; low for "frame" and Mal 3:19.** Drop the word "frame". Say: Edom clusters early and returns as the only nation in the last book, where the brother theme recurs in wording found nowhere else outside Genesis.

## B7. The land left undone

**Tested.** Name sets by chapter; rare-pair scans of vv. 19–20 at several ceilings; each "possess" pairing with expected values; rivals; syntax.

**Counts reproduced.** Josh 13:2 has הַנִּשְׁאֶרֶת ("that remains") and Philistines; 13:6 Sidonians and "I will dispossess". Josh 16:10 and Judg 1:29 are as stated; Judg 1:21 Benjamin; Zarephath is Sidon's at 1 Kgs 17:9.

**Baseline.** Eleven names from vv. 19–20 (with Esau) were checked chapter by chapter.
- 2 Chr 28 and Judg 1 lead with five each.
- Josh 13 has three (joint 27th). Josh 16 has two (about 42nd). Jer 17, 32, 33 have three each.
- Scan on vv. 19–20 alone, with rarity set at five, ten and twenty verses: Josh 16 first at each ceiling (2, 3 and 5 pairs in a 10-verse chapter); Judg 1 sixth, eighth, second; Jer 32 second, third, sixth; Jer 33 seventh, fourth, fourth; Josh 13 339th, 78th, 19th.

**"Possess" with names, against chance.**
- Ephraim: Josh 16:10, Judg 1:29, Obad 19 (expected 1.44).
- Benjamin: Judg 1:21, Obad 19 (expected 1.40).
- Jerusalem: Josh 15:63, Judg 1:21, Obad 20.
- Canaanite: fourteen verses (expected 0.62), nine of them negative.
- The tight lists look striking, but "possess" with a tribe's name almost always falls in these lists anyway: Manasseh, Asher, Zebulun and Naphtali do the same. So the exclusivity reflects where the Bible talks of tribes possessing, not a design.

**Sense.**
- Obad 19–20 has no negation and no "remains".

**What is left.** Judg 1 has Negeb, Shephelah, Benjamin, Ephraim, Canaanite and Jerusalem with "possess", and בֵּית יוֹסֵף ("house of Joseph", 13 verses in all; Judg 1:22, 23, 35; Obad 18). That goes beyond ordinary vocabulary, modestly.

## B8. Isa 63:1–5 and Rev 19 as a trajectory from Obad 21

**Tested.** Phrases; expected counts; all shared vocabulary of Isa 63:1–6 and Obadiah; rivals for each link; Greek of Obadiah, Isaiah, Revelation; Edom in the NT.

**Counts reproduced.** מִי־זֶה בָּא מֵאֱדוֹם ("Who is this who comes from Edom") and רַב לְהוֹשִׁיעַ ("mighty to save"): Isa 63:1, plain and skeletal. יָשַׁע ("save") with "Edom": 2 Sam 8:14, Isa 63:1, 1 Chr 18:13. Isa 63:3 לְבַדִּי ("alone").

**Corrections.**
- "Save" (198 verses) and Edom (93) would meet in 0.79 verses by chance; three is little more. In 2 Sam 8:14 and its parallel, the LORD saves David after he garrisons Edom: an unnamed David precedent as apt as anything in Obadiah.
- יָשַׁע ("save") with עֵשָׂו ("Esau"): Obad 21 only.

**Obad 21 and Isa 63.**
- Shared content vocabulary of Isa 63:1–6 and Obadiah: Edom and "save" are the only specific items; the rest (who, this, speak, none, heart, go down, with) is common.
- Scan rank of Isa 63 against Obadiah: 127th of 862, one pair.
- Isa 63's own neighbours are elsewhere: בָּצְרָה ("Bozrah") at Isa 34:6, Jer 49:13, 22, Amos 1:12, and not in Obadiah; יוֹם נָקָם ("day of vengeance") at Isa 34:8, 61:2, 63:4; Isa 63:5 nearly repeats Isa 59:16.
- And the sense opposes. Obad 21 has plural deliverers going up; Isa 63:3, 5 denies any helper: "no one with me", "none to help". A trajectory would have to be a correction.

**Obad 21's own relatives.**
- מוֹשִׁיעִים ("deliverers") as a plural: Neh 9:27 and Obad 21 only (Obad 21 spelled without yod, BHS would repoint).
- "Save" with שָׁפַט ("judge") leads to Judg 2:16, 18, then Isa 33:22, Ezek 34:22, Hos 13:10, Ps 72:4, 109:31 among eight. The obvious model for "deliverers … to judge" is the Judges pattern, unnoted by the claim.
- לַיהוָה הַמְּלוּכָה ("to the LORD the kingship"): Obad 21 and Ps 22:29 only, Hebrew and Swete alike.

**Greek.**
- Obad 21 does not have deliverers: Swete ἀνασῳζόμενοι ("those who escape"), Rahlfs ἄνδρες σεσῳσμένοι ("men who have been saved"), which BHS retroverts. It also has ἐξ ὄρους ("from the mountain") and ἐκδικῆσαι ("to avenge"). In Greek the first link does not exist.
- Isa 63:1–3 has ἱμάτια ("garments"), ληνός ("winepress"), πατητός/κατεπάτησα ("trodden"/"I trod"), θυμός ("wrath"), αἷμα ("blood"). Rev 19:13, 15 has ἱμάτιον ("garment"), αἷμα ("blood"), πατεῖ ("treads"), ληνόν ("winepress"), θυμοῦ ("wrath"). Rivals have only part: Joel 3:13 (Greek numbering; πατεῖτε "tread", ληνός "winepress") and Lam 1:15. Of the verses with ληνός ("winepress") in Swete, only Isa 63:2–3 has all five. βεβαμμένον ("dipped") is Revelation's own word. ληνός in the NT: Matt 21:33, Rev 14:19, 20, 19:15. Isa 63 to Rev 19 is firm.
- But Edom drops out: Rev 19 names Babylon, Ἰδουμαία ("Idumea") appears only at Mark 3:8 in the NT, and Ἠσαύ ("Esau") only at Rom 9:13, Heb 11:20, 12:16. Rev 11:15 Ἐγένετο ἡ βασιλεία ("the kingdom has become") is the nearest NT echo of Obad 21's kingship, not Rev 19.

## B9. Who drank at Obad 16?

**Tested.** Plural forms in the book; all 71 verses of שָׁתָה ("drink") in the Prophets, Lamentations, Psalms and Job, classed by what liquid or wrath word stands within three verses; forms of the "as … so" sentence; Greek and BHS.

**Counts and corrections.**
- כּוֹס ("cup") is absent from Obadiah; Greek likewise has no ποτήριον ("cup").
- גּוֹרָל ("lot") with "drink": Joel 4:3 only.
- "Only plural you in the book" is **not exact**. קוּמוּ ("rise!", v. 1) is a masculine plural imperative, addressed to the nations. תִּשְׁלַחְנָה (v. 13, "you shall stretch out") is formally feminine plural, though BHS corrects it. Correct form: "the only plural you in a finite indicative; every address to Edom elsewhere is singular, apart from the form in v. 13 that BHS corrects."

**Pattern.**
- Every other wrath use has a named cup, wine or wrath within three verses: Isa 51:17, 22; Jer 25:16, 26, 27, 28; 49:12; 51:7; Ezek 23:32, 34; Hab 2:16; Ps 75:9; Job 6:4; 21:20. Fourteen of fourteen.
- Absolute "drink" as wrath is well attested (Jer 25:16, 26, 27; Hab 2:16), but only with a cup nearby. Obad 16 is the only wrath use with nothing nearby, and this applies to the undisputed second half (nations drink, stagger, vanish) as much as to the first.
- So absence of כּוֹס ("cup") cannot argue against (b), since the sentence's own sense needs wrath without a cup anyway.
- Revelling use without liquid is almost always paired with אָכַל ("eat"): Isa 21:5, 65:13, Jer 22:15, Zech 7:6. Obad 16 has no "eat". Amos 4:1, "bring, that we may drink", is the closest: oppressors on הַר שֹׁמְרוֹן ("Mount Samaria"). Amos 2:8 has wine in God's house. Joel 4:3 (lots, then wine and drinking), with יַדּוּ גוֹרָל ("cast lots") found only at Joel 4:3, Obad 11 and Nah 3:10, is the best support for reading (a).
- לָעוּ ("they will swallow"/"be rash"): only Obad 16 and Job 6:3. Job 6:4 next says of God's arrows חֲמָתָם שֹׁתָה רוּחִי ("my spirit drinks their venom", the word also meaning "wrath"). That small contact points to wrath for the second half.

**The sentence.**
- "As you did, so to you" (v. 15, Lev 24:19, Judg 1:7, Jer 50:15) keeps one party in both limbs, favouring (a) if v. 16 is parallel to v. 15.
- But "as … so" with a change of party is also normal: Isa 10:11, Jer 7:14, Ezek 12:11, 24:22.
- The grammar helps (b): if "you plural" were Edom and allies, they are mostly the same people as "all the nations" and yet are addressed and then spoken of in the third person. A plural contrasting with "all the nations" fits Judah better.
- Against (b): no marker of an address to Judah, in a book that otherwise addresses only Edom and (v. 1) the nations.
- Transfer of the cup, in Jer 25:28–29, 49:12, Isa 51:17–23, Lam 4:21–22, always goes from Jerusalem to others. Jer 49:12, in the very oracle Obadiah shares, has that logic: those not sentenced drink, and so will you.

**Versions.**
- The Hebrew plural is firm: the BHS apparatus has no note on שְׁתִיתֶם ("you drank").
- Greek has ἔπιες ("you drank", singular) in both Swete and Rahlfs, making Edom the drinker. It may simply harmonise with vv. 2–15's singular; the plural is the harder reading.
- Rahlfs adds οἶνον ("wine") after "all the nations"; Swete lacks "all the nations wine" altogether. That gives Greek reading a carousal colour.
- Many manuscripts have סָבִיב ("around") for תָּמִיד ("continually"), BHS. Compare Zech 12:2.
- [S] As I recall, the Targum has "as you rejoiced" over the holy mountain and the Vulgate keeps the plural. Neither moves the verdict.
- The early versions show that the verse was already read with Edom as subject, without settling the Hebrew.

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Counts verified | Mostly; "only plural you" needs correction |
| Baseline | Wrath use without a cup is unique, but this affects both readings |
| Sense | (a) needs the verb to change sense between limbs; (b) keeps one sense but needs an unmarked change of addressee |
| Alternative explanation | Wordplay on both senses is possible |

**Verdict: Uncertain. Lean: slightly to (b), as previously, with low–moderate confidence.** The argument that holds is not the absence of "cup" but the plural contrasting with "all the nations", the single sense of the verb, and Jer 49:12's logic. Joel 4:3, Amos 2:8 and the Greek keep (a) alive. A preacher should say both are defensible and not build on either.

## New candidates

- **Joel 3:5 with Obad 14, 17, 18.** The only verse with both פְּלֵיטָה ("escape") and שָׂרִיד ("survivor"), with "on Mount Zion", and carrying כַּאֲשֶׁר אָמַר יְהוָה ("as the LORD has said"). That formula is the nearest thing to a marked citation in this field and may bear on direction between Joel and Obadiah; 2 Kgs 19:31 and Isa 37:32 are rival sources. Worth its own audit.
- **Joel 4:19 with Obad 10, 12.** חָמָס ("violence") with Edom: the only such verse, also with "sons of Judah". A stronger handoff in the Greek order, where Joel precedes Obadiah.
- **Judg 2:16, 18 and Neh 9:27 behind Obad 21.** "Deliverers … to judge" as the Judges pattern, a nearer model than Isa 63.
- **Isa 14:13–15 with Obad 3–4.** Said in the heart, stars, brought down: a closer model for Obadiah's pluses against Jeremiah than anything in Jer 49.
- **Job 39:27–28 with Obad 3–4 and Jer 49:16.** Make high, eagle, nest, rock, dwell. Ranks fifth for Obadiah (WLC) and sits in the highest windows outside Jer 49. Wisdom's eagle may be a common stock for both prophets.
- **Num 20:14 with Obad 10.** "Your brother Israel" and "your brother Jacob": unique forms of one address to Edom.
- **Zech 12:2 with Obad 16.** A reeling bowl for all the peoples *around*, set in Jerusalem, joined to the manuscripts' סָבִיב ("around") at Obad 16. It suits reading (b).
- **Amos 4:1 with Obad 16.** Oppressors drinking on a mount, without liquid or "eat": the best parallel for (a) apart from Joel 4:3.
- **Greek Mal 4:1 with Obad 11, 18.** Foreigners as stubble: a Greek-only link that the Hebrew reading misses.
