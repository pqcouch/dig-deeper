# Claims for Auditor B — field and design claims (block tests, chance baselines, rank stability)

Hebrew counts as stated are for the WLC. References follow Hebrew versification.

**B1. Jeremiah 49 and Obadiah.** Stated: Jer 49 ranks first of 862 chapters of ten verses or more for rare shared vocabulary with Obadiah, 1.90 rare pairs per verse against 0.28 for second place; all 74 pairs attach to Obad 1–5 and 8, none to vv. 10–21. Obadiah alone has the taunt מִי יוֹרִדֵנִי אָרֶץ ("Who will bring me down to earth?", v. 3) and בֵּין כּוֹכָבִים ("among the stars", v. 4). Jer 49 has no יַעֲקֹב ("Jacob") or צִיּוֹן ("Zion"). Obadiah's order runs 49:14–16, then 49:9, 10, 7. Direction is claimed to be open. Diff the shared material in two passes (consonantal, then with waw, yod and final he deleted) and say what evidence, if any, bears on direction. Consider the Greek of Jeremiah.

**B2. Amos 9:12 as a designed handoff.** Stated: יָרַשׁ ("possess") with אֱדוֹם ("Edom") in one verse: Amos 9:12 only; Amos precedes Obadiah in the Hebrew order, and Obadiah unfolds that clause. Also Amos 1:6, 9 (הִסְגִּיר, "hand over", with גָּלוּת, "exiles", and Edom), Amos 1:11 (brother), Amos 5:6 (fire and Joseph, two verses), Amos 9:2–3 (מִשָּׁם אוֹרִידֵם, "from there I will bring them down", then חפשׂ, "search"; compare Obad 4, 6). Test whether adjacent books in the Twelve share this much by chance, using other adjacent pairs and non-adjacent books. Stated by-book ranking by rare pairs per 100 verses: Joel first (8 pairs in 73 verses), Jeremiah second, Amos eleventh, Jonah sixteenth. Test the claim that Joel, not Amos, is the densest partner by rate, and whether short books distort it.

**B3. The Num 24:17–21 cluster at Obad 4, 8, 18, 19.** כּוֹכָב מִיַּעֲקֹב ("a star from Jacob"), אֱדוֹם יְרֵשָׁה ("Edom a possession"), וְהֶאֱבִיד שָׂרִיד ("will destroy the survivor"), שִׂים בַּסֶּלַע קִנֶּךָ ("set your nest in the rock", to the Kenite). שָׂרִיד with יַעֲקֹב: Num 24:19 and Obad 18 only. Scan rank modest (66th of 922 at a five-verse floor).

**B4. The three-block structure, vv. 1–9, 10–14, 15–21.** Stated: the blocks share 11 content words across their three pairings, against a mean of 30.2 in 20,000 random shuffles of verses into same-sized blocks (none as low), with content words meaning lemmas in at most 1,500 verses; of 91 contiguous three-part cuts this one is second lowest; no content word crosses the 14|15 boundary within three verses either side. Test the method too: does verse-shuffling overstate cohesion for any short poem? Compare with other short prophetic texts.

**B5. The talion mirror.** Stated: the sentence fits the crime by a few pointed links: כָּרַת ("cut off"), vv. 9, 10, 14; אָבַד ("destroy"), vv. 8, 12; שָׂרִיד ("survivor"), vv. 14, 18; פָּלִיט ("fugitive") v. 14 and its cognate פְּלֵיטָה ("those who escape") v. 17. But the whole-book mirror is not supported by count: vv. 10–14 and 15–21 share three content words (Jerusalem, survivor, Jacob) against a chance mean of 8.0. Test whether the pointed links beat chance, and whether the claim should stand as a design at all.

**B6. Edom as frame of the Twelve.** Obadiah with Mal 1:2–4 and Mal 3:19 (קַשׁ, "stubble", with זֵדִים, "arrogant ones", cognate of זָדוֹן, "arrogance", Obad 3; "arrogant" with "stubble": Mal 3:19 only). אָח with עֵשָׂו and יַעֲקֹב outside Genesis: Mal 1:2 only. Test against how Edom is distributed across the Twelve.

**B7. The land left undone, at Obad 19–20.** Claimed: the names in vv. 19–20 recall the unfinished conquest: Josh 13:2, 6 (Philistines, Sidonians; הַנִּשְׁאֶרֶת, "that remains"), Josh 16:10 and Judg 1:29 (Ephraim did not dispossess the Canaanite), Judg 1:21 (Benjamin), with Zarephath Sidon's town (1 Kgs 17:9); Obad 19–20 uses יָרַשׁ ("possess"). Needs a baseline: is this more than the ordinary vocabulary of land?

**B8. Isa 63:1–5 and Rev 19 as a trajectory from Obad 21.** Obad 21 plural מוֹשִׁעִים ("deliverers") go up to judge Mount Esau; Isa 63:1 מִי־זֶה בָּא מֵאֱדוֹם ("Who is this who comes from Edom"), רַב לְהוֹשִׁיעַ ("mighty to save"), and alone at the winepress; Rev 19:13, 15 uses that garment and winepress. יָשַׁע with אֱדוֹם in one verse: 2 Sam 8:14, Isa 63:1, 1 Chr 18:13. Claimed moderate to high as a canonical trajectory, not as anything Obadiah states. Test the Greek of Isaiah and Revelation, and rivals.

**B9. Who drank at Obad 16?** שְׁתִיתֶם ("you drank") is the only plural "you" in the book; כּוֹס ("cup") is absent. Reading (a): Edom and allies carousing; Joel 4:3 is the only verse with גּוֹרָל ("lot") and שָׁתָה ("drink"). Reading (b): Judah drank wrath, so the nations will: Jer 25:28–29; 49:12; Isa 51:17–23; Lam 4:21–22. A previous reading leaned slightly to (b). Test for a pattern: how does שָׁתָה behave with the wrath image without כּוֹס; how do other prophets word this; what does the versions' handling show? Give a reasoned lean, or say none is warranted.
