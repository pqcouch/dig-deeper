# Claim audit 3, Colossians: report of Auditor A

**Scope:** claims A1–A8 (single contacts: wording, rarity, rivals, Greek and Hebrew).
**Editions:** Colossians in the SBLGNT and the NA28 export (with its apparatus); NT counts by lemma in the SBLGNT index; the Greek OT in Swete, searched accent-free with the stem allowed anywhere in the word, and every hit read for sense; Rahlfs from the Logos exports where one exists (Genesis–Deuteronomy, Joshua, Isaiah, Psalms, Proverbs, Daniel, Chronicles); the Hebrew from the WLC lemma index. In this report Swete's Theodotion Daniel is called "Theodotion Daniel" and his other Daniel text "Old Greek Daniel". Unless I say so, Swete counts leave out the Odes, the Psalms of Solomon and 1 Enoch. Where I add them the result does not change. Swete counts are floors.
**Tags:** anything from memory is tagged [S] and carries no verdict.

## Method notes that apply to every claim

**Positive controls.** Each search pattern was run first on a verse known to contain the item, and returned it, before any nil or "only" result was accepted. The checks were: στοιχεῖον ("element") at Wis 7:17; σοφία ("wisdom") with σύνεσις ("understanding") at 1 Cor 1:19 in the NT; the hiding family at 1 Cor 2:7 (ἀποκεκρυμμένην, "hidden"); the "not for man but for the Lord" pattern at 2 Chr 19:6; ἐρεθ- ("provoke") at Deut 21:18; ἐμβατεύω ("enter") at Josh 19:49; and נַחֲלָה ("inheritance") with עֶבֶד ("servant") at Isa 54:17.

One control failed, and it was mine. A first NT search for the hiding family returned nothing at 1 Cor 2:7, because the lemma field carries accents. Once the lemmas were accent-stripped, the control returned the verse, and only then were the NT figures below accepted.

**The scanner's null baseline.** Several claims rest on rank in the six-run chapter-and-window scan. I therefore ran the same scan on 40 arbitrary four-verse NT passages, chosen at random across the whole NT. For each one I recorded the best partner chapter and its median rank across the six runs. The results:

- In **19 of 40** passages, some Swete chapter had a median rank of 1.
- In **34 of 40** the best partner had a median of 2 or better.
- In **38 of 40** it had a median of 3 or better.

The partners were often absurd. Esther 8 came out as the "best partner" of Phil 4:13–16, Titus 3:1–4 and Phlm 6–9, and 2 Maccabees 8 of Heb 10:34–37.

So a top rank with a median of 1 is what an arbitrary passage gets about half the time. **It is not evidence on its own.** This null applies to A1, A7 and A8.

**A known allusion as a check on the scanner.** Against Col 2:20–23 the scan put Isaiah 29 at a median of 2. That is the expected result, because Col 2:22 (κατὰ τὰ ἐντάλματα καὶ διδασκαλίας τῶν ἀνθρώπων, "according to the commandments and teachings of men") echoes Isa 29:13. So the scanner can find a real contact. This matters for A2.

## A1: Wisdom 7:17 behind Colossians 2:8, 20 (and 1:17)

### What was tested
- The stated Swete counts.
- στοιχει- ("element") within one and two verses of κοσμ- ("world").
- The sense of each hit.
- σύστασις ("structure") and συνίστημι ("hold together") across Swete and the NT.
- The rank of Wisdom 7 against Gal 4:3–8 and against Colossians itself.
- Rivals in Wisdom and elsewhere.

### Results and corrections
1. **The στοιχει- stem anywhere in a word, Swete:** Wis 7:17, Wis 19:18, 2 Macc 7:22 (στοιχείωσιν, "elemental make-up") and 4 Macc 12:13. That is four verses, and **the count holds exactly**. Adding the Odes, the Psalms of Solomon and 1 Enoch changes nothing.
2. **στοιχει- with κοσμ- in one verse, Swete:** Wis 7:17 only. **This holds.** Within one verse either side there is a second hit: 2 Macc 7:22–23. The mother's στοιχείωσιν ("elemental make-up", 7:22) is followed directly by ὁ τοῦ κόσμου κτίστης ("the Creator of the world", 7:23). Within two verses there is nothing more. The claim should therefore say "the only verse, and one of two places within a verse".
3. **Sense of the hits.** All four Swete uses are physical: the constituents of the cosmos (Wis 7:17; 19:18, where the elements "change places" at the Exodus) or of the human body (2 Macc 7:22; 4 Macc 12:13). None is a power that enslaves, a regime, or a set of rules. In the NT, στοιχεῖον ("element") occurs in seven verses (SBLGNT): Gal 4:3, 9; Col 2:8, 20; Heb 5:12; 2 Pet 3:10, 12. The phrase with κόσμος ("world") occurs only at Gal 4:3, Col 2:8 and Col 2:20. Galatians and Colossians use the elements as something one is "enslaved under" (Gal 4:3, ὑπὸ τὰ στοιχεῖα … δεδουλωμένοι, "enslaved under the elements") and from which one "died" (Col 2:20, ἀπεθάνετε … ἀπό, "you died … from"). Wisdom's elements are objects of knowledge that God gives. The fit in sense is therefore weak.
4. **σύστασις ("structure") and συνίστημι ("hold together").** They are real cognates. But the noun is not distinctive. It occurs in Swete at Gen 49:6 (a "conspiracy"), Wis 7:17 and 3 Macc 2:9, and as ἐπισύστασις ("insurrection") in Numbers. The verb is everywhere: well over forty Swete verses, mostly "assemble" or "commend". At Col 1:17 the NT uses συνίστημι in its cosmic sense only twice: Col 1:17 and 2 Pet 3:5 (γῆ … συνεστῶσα τῷ τοῦ θεοῦ λόγῳ, "the earth … formed by the word of God"). 2 Peter 3 also has στοιχεῖα ("elements", 3:10, 12). So the pairing of physical elements with cosmic "holding together" is a commonplace of Jewish-Hellenistic cosmological Greek. It is not a fingerprint of Wisdom 7. The closer Greek parallels to Col 1:17 are Sir 43:26 (ἐν λόγῳ αὐτοῦ σύνκειται πάντα, "by his word all things hold together") and Wis 1:7 (τὸ συνέχον τὰ πάντα, "that which holds all things together"). Both share τὰ πάντα ("all things") with Col 1:17, which Wis 7:17 does not. **The σύστασις–συνέστηκεν link is lexically true but has no discriminating power.**
5. **Scan ranks.**
   - **Against Gal 4:3–8:** Wisdom 7 is first in five of six runs and second in the sixth (median 1). The stated rank holds. But the null shows a median-1 partner for 19 of 40 arbitrary passages, so this does not beat chance. Leave one out: Wisdom 7's rare-word hits are κοσμ- ("world"), στοιχει- ("element"), φυσ- ("nature") and χρον- ("time"). Without στοιχει- it falls level with 3 Maccabees 3, which has the other three. **The rank is the single rare word counted again**, not independent evidence.
   - **Against Colossians:** for Col 2:8–10, Wisdom 7's median rank is **8**; 2 Maccabees 7 and 4 Maccabees 5 lead (both median 2). For Col 2:20–23 its median is **9**; 2 Maccabees 7 (median 1) and Isaiah 29 (median 2) lead. For Col 2:16–23 Wisdom 7 is not in the top six. So Wisdom 7 is not a leading partner for the Colossian passages in question.
6. **Rivals.**
   - **Wis 13:1–3** is the nearer Wisdom text for Paul's own argument at Gal 4:8–9, the passage the elements belong to. It has φύσει ("by nature"), θεοῦ ἀγνωσία ("ignorance of God") and οὐκ ἴσχυσαν εἰδέναι τὸν ὄντα ("they could not know him who is"). It also lists the cosmic elements (fire, wind, air, the circle of the stars, water, the lights of heaven) as πρυτάνεις κόσμου ("rulers of the world") that men "thought to be gods" (θεοὺς ἐνόμισαν). Compare Gal 4:8: οὐκ εἰδότες θεὸν ἐδουλεύσατε τοῖς φύσει μὴ οὖσι θεοῖς ("not knowing God, you served those that by nature are not gods"). This rival lacks the word στοιχεῖον ("element"). But it supplies what Wis 7:17 lacks: the elements as objects of false service.
   - **2 Macc 7:22–23** joins στοιχείωσις ("elemental make-up") and κόσμος ("world") within one verse, and ranks above Wisdom 7 against Colossians 2.

### Hays table

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Possible | Wisdom is Greek-only; Swete is the only Greek text here (there is no Rahlfs export of Wisdom). Paul's use of Wisdom 13–14 in Romans 1 is widely argued [S] |
| Volume | Weak | Two words shared (στοιχεῖον "element", κόσμος "world"), in a different syntax: genitive κόσμου ("of the world") governs σύστασιν ("structure"), not στοιχείων ("elements"). The phrase τὰ στοιχεῖα τοῦ κόσμου ("the elements of the world") is not in Swete |
| Recurrence | Weak | Paul never cites Wis 7 |
| Thematic coherence | Weak | Physical elements as objects of God-given knowledge, against elements as an enslaving regime |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | The phrase is current in Hellenistic Jewish cosmology; Philo uses "the elements of the world" for physical elements [S] |
| History of interpretation | Possible [S] | Commentators cite Wis 7:17 among the lexical parallels for the physical sense, alongside 4 Macc 12:13 and Philo, as data for the meaning of the phrase, not as a source [S] |
| Satisfaction | Weak | Reading Wis 7:17 adds nothing to Col 2:8, 20 that the Galatians parallel does not already give |

**Verdict: Needs reframing.** **Recommended rating: low** as an allusion. As lexical background for the physical sense of "the elements of the world", it can be rated **low–moderate**, but only if Wis 19:18, 2 Macc 7:22–23, 4 Macc 12:13 and 2 Pet 3:10–12 stand beside it.

The count holds, but it shows only that the words were current in cosmological Greek. Every Swete sense is physical, while Paul's is a regime one is enslaved under or dies to. The Wisdom 7 scan rank against Galatians is no better than an arbitrary passage gets, and against Colossians Wisdom 7 ranks only eighth or ninth. **Missed by the claim:** 2 Macc 7:22–23; Wis 13:1–3 as the nearer Wisdom text for Gal 4:8–9; Sir 43:26 and Wis 1:7 as closer parallels to Col 1:17.

## A2: Isaiah 29:14 behind Colossians 2:2–3, as an inversion

### What was tested
- The Swete and NT counts.
- The Rahlfs and WLC wording of Isa 29:14.
- Rivals: Proverbs 2, Theodotion Daniel 2, Isa 45:3, Sirach and Job 28.
- What Paul's own quotation at 1 Cor 1:19 does with the hiding verb.
- Whether Isaiah 29 is otherwise available in Colossians.

### Results and corrections
1. **Swete, σοφία ("wisdom") + σύνεσις ("understanding") + a hiding word (κρυπ-, κρυφ-, κρυβ-, κρυψ- anywhere) in one verse:** Isa 29:14 only. Widening to any σοφ- ("wise") and συνε- ("understand") stem gives the same single verse. **This holds.** Rahlfs agrees (κρύψω, "I will hide"). The Hebrew has hiding too: וּבִינַת נְבֹנָיו תִּסְתַּתָּר ("and the understanding of its discerning men will hide itself"). The hiding is in the Hebrew, not only in the translator.
2. **The window list (the triple within one verse either side), Swete:** Prov 2:1–3, Job 28:20–21, Sir 1:24–26, Sir 14:20–21, Sir 39:6–7, Isa 29:14 and Theodotion Dan 2:20–22. **Correction: Job 28:20–21 belongs on the list and was omitted.** There "where is wisdom found, and what is the place of understanding? … it is hidden (ἐκρύβη, 'was hidden') from the birds of heaven". It is the classic text for wisdom hidden from everyone. Sense check: in Prov 2:1 the hiding verb means "store up" (κρύψῃς παρὰ σεαυτῷ, "store up within yourself"), not concealment.
3. **NT, by lemma:**
   - σοφία ("wisdom") with σύνεσις ("understanding") in one verse: 1 Cor 1:19 and Col 1:9 only.
   - σοφία with a hiding lemma: 1 Cor 2:7 and Col 2:3 only. **The stated "only other NT verse" holds.**
   - The noun triple within one verse either side: Col 2:2–3 only. **This holds.**
   - If the adjectives σοφός ("wise") and συνετός ("intelligent") are allowed, Matt 11:25 and Luke 10:21 join, with κρύπτω and ἀποκρύπτω ("hide").
4. **Paul's route from 1 Cor 1:19 to 2:7.** Paul's quotation reads ἀθετήσω ("I will set aside"), against κρύψω ("I will hide") in both Swete and Rahlfs. His verb matches Ps 32:10 Swete (ἀθετεῖ δὲ λογισμοὺς λαῶν … ἀθετεῖ βουλὰς ἀρχόντων, "he sets aside the reasonings of peoples … sets aside the counsels of rulers"), which is the usual explanation [S]. So Paul's one explicit use of Isa 29:14 **removes** the hiding word. The hiding at 1 Cor 2:7 is God's wisdom hidden ἐν μυστηρίῳ ("in a mystery"), not human understanding hidden in judgement. **The 1 Corinthians route does not carry a hiding reading of Isa 29:14.** At most it shows that Isa 29:14 and a hidden-wisdom idea sat a chapter apart in Paul's mind.
5. **Availability inside Colossians.** It is raised by Col 2:22 (κατὰ τὰ ἐντάλματα καὶ διδασκαλίας τῶν ἀνθρώπων, "according to the commandments and teachings of men"), which echoes Isa 29:13. ἔνταλμα ("commandment") occurs in Swete only at Job 23:11–12, Isa 29:13 and Isa 55:11, and in the NT only at Matt 15:9, Mark 7:7 (both quoting Isa 29:13) and Col 2:22. So the letter itself uses the verse immediately before Isa 29:14. **The claim did not use this, and it is the strongest point in its favour.**
6. **Rivals with the shape of Col 2:2–3.** Col 2:2–3 is about treasures of wisdom that are hidden, sought and found.
   - **Isa 45:3:** θησαυροὺς σκοτεινούς, ἀποκρύφους ἀοράτους ("treasures of darkness, hidden, unseen"), in Swete and Rahlfs. θησαυρός ("treasure") with ἀπόκρυφος ("hidden") in one verse occurs in Swete **only** at Isa 45:3 and 1 Macc 1:23 (the temple's hidden treasures). Col 2:3's own collocation is θησαυροὶ … ἀπόκρυφοι ("treasures … hidden"). Isa 29:14 has no treasure word.
   - **Prov 2:1–6** has, in six verses: σοφία ("wisdom"), σύνεσις ("understanding"), θησαυρούς ("treasures", 2:4), ἐπίγνωσιν θεοῦ ("knowledge of God", 2:5), γνῶσις ("knowledge", 2:6) and a hiding verb (2:1). That is six of the key terms of Col 2:2–3 (ἐπίγνωσιν τοῦ μυστηρίου τοῦ θεοῦ, "knowledge of the mystery of God"; σοφίας καὶ γνώσεως, "of wisdom and knowledge"; συνέσεως, "of understanding"; θησαυροί, "treasures"; ἀπόκρυφοι, "hidden"). In the scan against Col 2:2–3, Proverbs 2 is second in the eight-verse all-words run (median 7).
   - **Theodotion Dan 2:20–23 (with 2:18–19, 28):** ἡ σοφία καὶ ἡ σύνεσις ("wisdom and understanding", 2:20); ἀποκαλύπτει βαθέα καὶ ἀπόκρυφα ("he reveals deep and hidden things", 2:22); ἐγνώρισάς μοι ("you made known to me", 2:23); μυστήριον ("mystery", 2:18, 19, 28). It is the only rival with μυστήριον ("mystery"), the governing noun of Col 2:2 (and 1:26–27). In the scan against Col 2:2–3, Theodotion Daniel 2 has a median rank of 3; Isaiah 29 is not in the top thirteen.
   - **Sir 1:24–25** (ἐν θησαυροῖς σοφίας, "in the treasures of wisdom"), Sir 20:30 and 41:14 (σοφία κεκρυμμένη καὶ θησαυρὸς ἀφανής, "hidden wisdom and unseen treasure") join treasure, wisdom and hiddenness. Sir 20:30 says hidden wisdom is useless, which is the opposite pole to Col 2:3.

### Hays table

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Paul quotes Isa 29:14 at 1 Cor 1:19; Col 2:22 echoes Isa 29:13 |
| Volume | Possible | Three nouns shared (wisdom, understanding, a hiding word), but the treasure noun and the mystery noun are absent from Isaiah 29; one shared form (σοφία) |
| Recurrence | Possible | 1 Cor 1:19 (with the hiding verb changed); Col 2:22 for the neighbouring verse |
| Thematic coherence | Possible | Inversion is a reading, not a datum: in Isaiah the hiding is judgement on the wise, in Colossians the treasures are stored up in Christ and disclosed |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Jesus tradition read the verse as a hiding text (Matt 11:25; Luke 10:21), but Paul's dependence on that logion is unproven [S] |
| History of interpretation | Weak–Possible [S] | Commentators on Col 2:3 more often cite Prov 2:3–4, Isa 45:3, Sirach and 1 Enoch's hidden treasures than Isa 29:14 [S] |
| Satisfaction | Possible | The inversion gives a sharp sermon point, but Prov 2 and Isa 45:3 explain the actual wording better |

**Verdict: Needs reframing.** **Recommended rating: low–moderate** for Isa 29:14 as an inverted allusion. The rarity counts hold, and the availability is better than the claim says, because Col 2:22 uses Isa 29:13. But the wording of Col 2:3, "treasures … hidden", is matched only by Isa 45:3 (and 1 Macc 1:23). The search for treasured wisdom with "knowledge of God" is the shape of Prov 2:1–6. The "mystery" disclosed, with wisdom and understanding, is the shape of Theodotion Daniel 2. The proposal is better stated like this: **Col 2:2–3 draws on the wisdom-as-hidden-treasure tradition (Prov 2; Isa 45:3; Theodotion Daniel 2), with an Isaiah 29 overtone made more plausible by 2:22.** **Missed:** Job 28:20–21 in the window list; Col 2:22 / Isa 29:13; the unique θησαυρός + ἀπόκρυφος ("treasure" + "hidden") collocation at Isa 45:3.

## A3: Psalm 50:8 (Greek numbering; Hebrew 51:8) at Colossians 1:26–27; 2:3

### What was tested
- The wording in Swete and Rahlfs.
- The Hebrew, and whether "hidden things" is in it.
- Lemmas shared with Col 1:26–27 and 2:3.
- A vocabulary-density comparison with rival passages.

### Results and corrections
1. **Wording.** Swete and Rahlfs agree: τὰ ἄδηλα καὶ τὰ κρύφια τῆς σοφίας σου ἐδήλωσάς μοι ("the unclear and hidden things of your wisdom you made plain to me").
2. **Hebrew (WLC Ps 51:8):** הֵן־אֱמֶת חָפַצְתָּ בַטֻּחוֹת וּבְסָתֻם חָכְמָה תוֹדִיעֵנִי ("behold, you delight in truth in the inward parts, and in the hidden part you make me know wisdom").
   - **The hiddenness is in the Hebrew, but as a place, not as content.** בְּסָתֻם ("in the hidden part") is a passive participle of סָתַם ("stop up, keep secret") and pairs with טֻחוֹת ("inward parts"). טֻחוֹת occurs only twice in the WLC, at Ps 51:8 and Job 38:36, both times with חָכְמָה ("wisdom"); the Job verse is about wisdom placed in the inner person.
   - סָתַם with חָכְמָה in one verse: Ps 51:8 only (WLC). The Hebrew is about God teaching wisdom **in the inner self**.
   - The Greek turns this into **God's secret wisdom disclosed**. ἄδηλα καὶ κρύφια ("unclear and hidden things") becomes the object, and σοφίας σου ("of your wisdom") adds a possessive that the Hebrew lacks.
   - So the shape the claim sees (hidden things, wisdom, disclosure) belongs to the translator, not to the Hebrew. In the Hebrew, תוֹדִיעֵנִי ("you make me know") is the hiphil of יָדַע ("know"), which Greek more often renders with γνωρίζω ("make known"). The translator chose δηλόω ("make plain") instead.
3. **Shared lemmas.** Against Col 1:26–27 and 2:3, the one shared lemma is σοφία ("wisdom", 2:3). κρύφιος ("hidden") is cognate with Colossians' ἀποκρύπτω/ἀπόκρυφος ("hide/hidden"), but it is not the same lemma. μυστήριον ("mystery"), φανερόω ("reveal"), γνωρίζω ("make known"), αἰών ("age"), γενεά ("generation"), πλοῦτος ("riches") and δόξα ("glory") are all absent. **The total is one shared lemma and one cognate.**
4. **Density comparison.** Across Swete I scored every verse, and every five-verse window, on twelve key terms of Col 1:26–27 and 2:2–3: mystery, hide, reveal, make known, wisdom, understanding, knowledge, treasure, riches, glory, age, generation.
   - Ps 50:8 scores **2 of 12**.
   - **39** five-verse windows that contain a hiding word score 4 or more.
   - At the top: **Theodotion Dan 2:20–23 scores 6** (mystery, hiding, make known, wisdom, understanding, age). Wis 6:20–23 (μυστήρια, "mysteries"; οὐκ ἀποκρύψω, "I will not hide"; σοφία, "wisdom"; γνῶσις, "knowledge"; αἰών, "age") and Wis 7:12–15 (τὸν πλοῦτον αὐτῆς οὐκ ἀποκρύπτομαι, "I do not hide her riches"; θησαυρός, "treasure") score 5. Tobit 12:6–10 (Sinaiticus) and Sir 39:6–9 also score 5.
   - Wis 6:22 is the only single verse to reach 4: τί δέ ἐστιν σοφία … καὶ οὐκ ἀποκρύψω ὑμῖν μυστήρια ("what wisdom is … and I will not hide mysteries from you").
   - The named rivals Sir 1:6 (ῥίζα σοφίας τίνι ἀπεκαλύφθη, "to whom has the root of wisdom been revealed?") and Sir 39:3 (ἀπόκρυφα παροιμιῶν, "the hidden things of proverbs") are each closer in one or two features. Neither is as close as Theodotion Daniel 2.

### Hays table

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Ps 50 LXX is a liturgical psalm; Paul quotes Ps 50:6 LXX at Rom 3:4 [S, but the text is plain in Romans] |
| Volume | Weak | One shared lemma, one cognate |
| Recurrence | Weak | Rom 3:4 uses a different verse of the psalm, for a different point |
| Thematic coherence | Weak | Penitential self-knowledge, against a cosmic mystery revealed to the nations |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | |
| History of interpretation | Weak [S] | I recall no commentator who makes this link for Colossians [S] |
| Satisfaction | Weak | |

**Verdict: Discard.** **Recommended rating: low.** The Greek psalm shares one word with Colossians. Its "hidden wisdom disclosed" shape is the translator's recasting of a Hebrew line about wisdom taught in the inner person. Dozens of Swete passages carry the hidden-wisdom-disclosed vocabulary more densely, led by Theodotion Dan 2:20–23 and Wis 6:20–23. **Missed:** Theodotion Daniel 2 as the clear lead rival, because it has μυστήριον ("mystery"), the noun Colossians builds on.

## A4: 2 Chronicles 19:6–7 (with Deuteronomy 10:17) behind Colossians 3:22–25; 4:1

### What was tested
- Every stated count, in Swete (stem anywhere, sense-checked), in Rahlfs for Chronicles and Deuteronomy, and in the WLC by lemma.
- How rare ἁπλότης καρδίας ("sincerity of heart") is.
- Rivals for the impartial judge joined to fear language.
- The NT distribution of the partiality vocabulary.

### Results and corrections
1. **A fear word + κύριος ("Lord") + an ἀδικ- ("wrong") word + a πρόσωπον ("face") idiom in one verse, Swete:** two mechanical hits, 2 Chr 19:7 and Theodotion Dan 1:10. Theodotion Dan 1:10 is a false hit: πρόσωπα ("faces") is literal (the young men's faces), and the ἀδικ- word is καταδικάσητε ("you condemn"). **With the sense check, 2 Chr 19:7 only. This holds.** Rahlfs agrees: οὐκ ἔστιν μετὰ κυρίου θεοῦ ἡμῶν ἀδικία οὐδὲ θαυμάσαι πρόσωπον οὐδὲ λαβεῖν δῶρα ("there is with the Lord our God no unrighteousness, nor respect of persons, nor taking of gifts").
2. **With an ἄνθρωπ- ("man") word added, within two verses either side, Swete.** My windows give 3 Reigns (1 Kings) 2:28–33, 2 Chr 19:3–10, Prov 29:23–27, Jdt 11:1–5 and Isa 29:19–23. **Job 11:13–14 and Jer 40:7 do not reproduce.** Job 11:15's πρόσωπον ("face") is a literal face, and no κύριος ("Lord") is in reach. Jer 40:7–9 has no πρόσωπον and no ἄνθρωπ- word in the window. The list should be corrected to the five above. Among them, 2 Chronicles 19 is the only one where partiality is a judge's sin, which holds. One near-miss deserves mention: **Prov 29:25–26**. It has φοβηθέντες … ἀνθρώπους ("fearing men"), ὁ δὲ πεποιθὼς ἐπὶ Κυρίῳ ("but he who trusts in the Lord"), πολλοὶ θεραπεύουσιν πρόσωπα ἡγουμένων ("many court the faces of rulers") and παρὰ δὲ Κυρίου γίνεται τὸ δίκαιον ἀνδρί ("but from the Lord comes justice for a man"). That is fear of men set against the Lord, face-courting, and τὸ δίκαιον ("what is just", as in Col 4:1). It is closer to the men-pleasing antithesis of Col 3:22–23 than 2 Chronicles 19 is.
3. **"Not for man but for the Lord", in Swete:** 1 Chr 29:1 (οὐκ ἀνθρώπῳ ἀλλ᾽ ἢ Κυρίῳ θεῷ, "not for man but for the Lord God") and 2 Chr 19:6 (οὐκ ἀνθρώπῳ ὑμεῖς κρίνετε ἀλλ᾽ ἤ τῷ κυρίῳ, "you judge not for man but for the Lord") only. Rahlfs agrees; it reads ἡ οἰκοδομή ("the building") at 1 Chr 29:1. **This holds.** Hos 11:9 (θεός εἰμι καὶ οὐκ ἄνθρωπος, "I am God and not man") is a different idea.
4. **ἁπλότης καρδίας ("sincerity of heart").** In Swete, ἁπλότης ("sincerity") with καρδία ("heart") occurs in **two verses only: 1 Chr 29:17 and Wis 1:1.** In the NT it occurs only at Eph 6:5 and Col 3:22. So the phrase is not common in the Greek Bible. The 1 Chr 29:17 link is real. But Wis 1:1 is an equal partner: ἐν ἁπλότητι καρδίας ζητήσατε αὐτόν ("seek him in sincerity of heart"), addressed to οἱ κρίνοντες τὴν γῆν ("you who judge the earth"), again in a context of judges. In later Jewish ethical Greek the phrase is a stock virtue [S].
5. **Deuteronomy 10:17.** Swete and Rahlfs agree: κύριος τῶν κυρίων ("Lord of lords") … οὐ θαυμάζει πρόσωπον οὐδ᾽ οὐ μὴ λάβῃ δῶρον ("does not show partiality nor take a gift"). κύριος τῶν κυρίων occurs in Swete only at Deut 10:17, Ps 135:3 and Old Greek Dan 4:34.
6. **The Hebrew.** Deut 10:17 has לֹא־יִשָּׂא פָנִים וְלֹא יִקַּח שֹׁחַד ("does not lift the face, nor take a bribe") and אֲדֹנֵי הָאֲדֹנִים ("Lord of lords"). 2 Chr 19:7 has וּמַשֹּׂא פָנִים וּמִקַּח־שֹׁחַד ("nor lifting of the face, nor taking of a bribe"), with פַּחַד־יְהוָה ("the fear of YHWH"). **The claim holds**, and Chronicles is plainly reusing Deuteronomy's phrasing. Correction to the framing: פָּנִים ("face") with שֹׁחַד ("bribe") in one verse occurs in **four** WLC verses: Deut 10:17, Deut 16:19, Prov 6:35 and 2 Chr 19:7. Prov 6:35 is a different idiom, "he will not accept any ransom".
7. **Rivals.**
   - **Lev 19:13–15** (Swete). In three verses it has οὐκ ἀδικήσεις τὸν πλησίον ("you shall not wrong your neighbour"); ὁ μισθὸς τοῦ μισθωτοῦ ("the wages of the hired man", a master's duty to a worker); φοβηθήσῃ Κύριον τὸν θεόν σου ("you shall fear the Lord your God"); οὐ λήμψῃ πρόσωπον … οὐδὲ θαυμάσεις πρόσωπον ("you shall not show partiality … nor honour the face"); and ἐν δικαιοσύνῃ κρινεῖς ("in righteousness you shall judge"). ἀδικ- with a partiality idiom in one verse also occurs at Lev 19:15. **This is as good a match as 2 Chronicles 19 for 3:25 and 4:1, and better on the master–labourer setting.**
   - **Sir 32:13–24** in Swete (numbered chapter 35 in most modern editions [S]). It has Κύριος ἀνταποδιδούς ἐστιν ("the Lord is one who repays", 32:13); Κύριος κριτής ἐστιν, καὶ οὐκ ἔστιν παρ᾽ αὐτῷ δόξα προσώπου ("the Lord is judge, and with him there is no respect of persons", 32:15); ἀδίκῳ, ἠδικημένου ("unjust", "of the wronged", 32:15–16); and ἀνταποδῷ ἀνθρώπῳ κατὰ τὰς πράξεις αὐτοῦ ("he repays a man according to his deeds", 32:24). The "οὐκ ἔστιν … partiality" statement about God matches the form of Col 3:25 (οὐκ ἔστιν προσωπολημψία, "there is no partiality"). The repayment language matches 3:24–25 (ἀνταπόδοσιν, "reward"; κομίσεται, "will receive back"). **This is the closest rival for 3:24–25 specifically.**
   - **Deut 1:17, 16:19; Job 34:19; Ps 81:2; 1 Esd 4:39:** all impartial-judge texts. 1 Esd 4:39 and Ps 81:2 join ἀδικ- ("wrong") with partiality.
   - **NT:** the partiality family (προσωπολημψία "partiality", προσωπολήμπτης "one who shows partiality", προσωπολημπτέω "show partiality", ἀπροσωπολήμπτως "impartially") occurs in Acts 10:34, Rom 2:11, Eph 6:9, Col 3:25, Jas 2:1, 2:9 and 1 Pet 1:17. It is **absent from all of Swete**, so it is a Christian coinage. 1 Pet 1:17 joins impartial judgement by works with ἐν φόβῳ ("in fear"), the same cluster as Col 3:22–25, which points to shared Christian paraenesis.

## A5: Leviticus 25:39–55 behind Colossians 3:22; 4:1

### What was tested
- The wording in Swete, Rahlfs and the WLC.
- The count of פֶּרֶךְ ("harshness").
- Any verbal contact with Col 3:22–4:1.
- Rivals.

### Results and corrections
1. **Wording.**
   - Swete and Rahlfs agree at Lev 25:43: οὐ κατατενεῖς αὐτὸν ἐν τῷ μόχθῳ, καὶ φοβηθήσῃ κύριον τὸν θεόν σου ("you shall not wear him out with toil, and you shall fear the Lord your God"). Correction to the claim: the Greek does not say "harshness". It renders בְּפָרֶךְ ("with harshness") as ἐν τῷ μόχθῳ ("with toil"), and does the same at 25:46 and 25:53.
   - Hebrew 25:43: לֹא־תִרְדֶּה בוֹ בְּפָרֶךְ וְיָרֵאתָ מֵאֱלֹהֶיךָ ("you shall not rule over him with harshness, but fear your God"). The Greek adds κύριον ("the Lord"); the Hebrew has only "your God", as the claim says.
   - Lev 25:42 and 25:55 have οἰκέται μου ("my household servants"); 25:55 adds παῖδές μου ("my servants"). The Hebrew is עֲבָדַי ("my servants").
2. **פֶּרֶךְ ("harshness") in the WLC:** six verses, Exod 1:13, 1:14; Lev 25:43, 46, 53; Ezek 34:4. **The Exodus link holds.**
3. **Verbal contact.** The claim says there is "no shared phrase". **That needs correcting.** Lev 25:43 (Swete and Rahlfs) shares "fear the Lord" with Col 3:22 (φοβούμενοι τὸν κύριον, "fearing the Lord"). Lev 25:39 has δουλεύσει ("he shall serve"; Col 3:24 δουλεύετε, "you serve"), and Lev 25:44 has δοῦλον καὶ δούλην ("male and female slave"; Col 3:22 and 4:1 δοῦλοι, "slaves"). But these are among the commonest collocations in the Greek OT: a fear verb with accusative κύριον ("the Lord") occurs in **81** Swete verses. So there are shared words, but they carry no weight.
4. **Fit of the two ideas.**
   - (a) In Leviticus the fear of God restrains the **master**. Colossians gives that fear to the **slave** (3:22) and gives the master the knowledge that he has a Master (4:1). That is a transfer, not a match.
   - (b) "They are my servants" (Lev 25:42, 55) does have a counterpart in Col 3:24 (τῷ κυρίῳ Χριστῷ δουλεύετε, "you serve the Lord Christ"): slaves belong to the Lord. This is the stronger of the two ideas. But Leviticus restricts it to Israelites and expressly allows chattel slavery of foreigners (25:44–46), which cuts against an application to a mixed Gentile household.
5. **Rivals.**
   - **Job 31:13–15** (Swete): εἰ δὲ καὶ ἐφαύλισα κρίμα θεράποντός μου ἢ θεραπαίνης ("if I despised the cause of my servant or my maidservant") … τί γὰρ ποιήσω ἐὰν ἔτασίν μου ποιῆται ὁ κύριος ("what shall I do if the Lord makes inquiry of me?") … ἐν τῇ αὐτῇ κοιλίᾳ ("in the same womb"). The Hebrew has אֵל ("God") in 31:14 and עֹשֵׂנִי עָשָׂהוּ ("he who made me made him") in 31:15. Here the master is answerable to κύριος ("the Lord") for his slave's rights, which is the exact logic of Col 4:1 (τὸ δίκαιον, "what is just", … εἰδότες ὅτι καὶ ὑμεῖς ἔχετε κύριον, "knowing that you also have a Master"). **Job 31:13–15 is closer to 4:1 than Leviticus 25 is.** The Greek has κύριος ("Lord") and κρίμα ("cause, right"); the Hebrew has the common Maker.
   - **Deut 15:12–18** (release, "remember you were a slave in Egypt") and Exodus 21 are release laws without the "Master in heaven" turn.
   - **Lev 19:13** (the hired man's wages) is the Holiness Code's master–worker verse, and it sits beside "fear the Lord" (19:14).

**Verdict: Needs reframing** (as one of several Torah and wisdom texts on masters answerable to God). **Recommended rating: low.** No distinctive phrase is shared; the shared words are commonplaces found in 81 Swete verses or more. The "fear of God" sits on the wrong party, and Leviticus' protection is ethnically limited. If any single OT text stands behind Col 4:1, Job 31:13–15 has the better claim: the master answerable to the Lord for the slave's right. **Missed:** Job 31:13–15 as the closer rival; the Greek ἐν τῷ μόχθῳ ("with toil") rather than "harshness".

## A6: Isaiah 54:17 (Hebrew) behind Colossians 3:24

### What was tested
- The twelve-verse count, with each verse read.
- The "only plural" claim.
- Swete and Rahlfs at 54:17.
- δουλ- ("serve, slave") with κληρονομ- ("inherit") in one verse across Swete.
- ἀνταπόδοσις ("reward") with κληρονομία ("inheritance").
- Availability through Galatians.

### Results and corrections
1. **נַחֲלָה ("inheritance") with עֶבֶד ("servant") in one verse, WLC:** twelve verses (Deut 12:12; Josh 13:8; 18:7; 1 Sam 26:19; 1 Kgs 8:36, 53; Isa 54:17; 63:17; Ezek 46:17; Ps 136:22; Prov 17:2; 2 Chr 6:27). **The count holds exactly.**
2. **The "only plural" claim.** Reading the twelve:
   - Isa 54:17, זֹאת נַחֲלַת עַבְדֵי יְהוָה ("this is the heritage of the servants of YHWH"), is the only verse where the construct phrase "inheritance of the servants of YHWH" appears. **Holds in that form.**
   - But 1 Kgs 8:36 and its parallel 2 Chr 6:27 speak of עֲבָדֶיךָ ("your servants", plural) and of the land given "to your people as an inheritance" (לְנַחֲלָה). That is an inheritance belonging to God's servants in the plural, though not in construct.
   - Isa 63:17 (עֲבָדֶיךָ שִׁבְטֵי נַחֲלָתֶךָ, "your servants, the tribes of your inheritance") makes the servants God's inheritance, not the reverse.
   - With the verb נָחַל ("inherit"), Ps 69:37 has זֶרַע עֲבָדָיו יִנְחָלוּהָ ("the offspring of his servants shall inherit it"); the noun search does not catch it.
   - **So the "only" holds for the construct phrase and fails for the idea.**
   - A sharper fact, not in the claim: in Isaiah 40–66 every occurrence of עֶבֶד ("servant") before 54:17 is singular (twenty occurrences, 41:8 to 53:11). **Isa 54:17 is the first plural**, followed by 56:6, 63:17, 65:8–15 and 66:14. The verse is the hinge from the Servant to the servants [S on the significance; the count is WLC].
3. **Greek at 54:17.** Swete and Rahlfs agree: ἔστιν κληρονομία τοῖς θεραπεύουσιν κύριον ("there is an inheritance for those who serve the Lord"). θεραπεύω ("serve, attend") stands where Colossians has δουλεύω ("serve as a slave"). **This holds.**
4. **δουλ- ("slave, serve") with κληρονομ- ("inherit") in one verse, Swete: 16 verses.** They include Isa 63:17 and Isa 65:9. Isa 65:9 reads κληρονομήσουσιν οἱ ἐκλεκτοί μου καὶ οἱ δοῦλοί μου ("my chosen and my servants shall inherit"). That is God's δοῦλοι ("servants", plural) as heirs, in Greek. So the argument that Col 3:24 is "closer to the Hebrew than to the Greek" of 54:17 is undercut: a Greek reader of Isaiah has the δοῦλοι-as-heirs idea four chapters on, without needing the Hebrew. Ps 68:36–37 Swete (κληρονομήσουσιν αὐτήν. καὶ τὸ σπέρμα τῶν δούλων αὐτοῦ, "they shall inherit it, and the offspring of his servants") puts the same pair within two verses.
5. **ἀνταπόδοσις ("reward") with κληρονομία ("inheritance"), Swete:** **no verse.** Within two verses, only Ps 93:2–5 and Hos 9:6–7, and both are retribution in judgement. Jer 16:18 has the verb ἀνταποδώσω ("I will repay") with κληρονομίαν ("inheritance"), again punitive. Col 3:24's τὴν ἀνταπόδοσιν τῆς κληρονομίας ("the reward of the inheritance") has no Greek OT precedent. Isa 63:4's ἡμέρα … ἀνταποδόσεως ("day of recompense") is vengeance, not reward. Better rivals for "a reward given to a servant": δοῦλος ("servant") with the noun ἀνταπόδοσις ("reward") in one verse, Swete, at **Ps 18:12** (καὶ γὰρ ὁ δοῦλός σου φυλάσσει αὐτά· ἐν τῷ φυλάσσειν αὐτὰ ἀνταπόδοσις πολλή, "your servant keeps them; in keeping them there is great reward") and **2 Sam 19:36** (the king repaying his servant Barzillai). Ps 118:17 (ἀνταπόδος τῷ δούλῳ σου, "repay your servant") is a verb, not the noun.
6. **Availability through Galatians.** Gal 4:27 quotes Isa 54:1, and Gal 4:30's "shall not inherit" quotes **Gen 21:10**, not Isaiah. This raises the availability of Isaiah 54 as a chapter. It does nothing for 54:17 in particular. Gal 4:1–7 (slave and heir; ἀπολάβωμεν, "that we might receive") is the nearer Pauline frame for "slaves receiving an inheritance".

But the Greek text renders "those who serve the Lord" with θεραπεύω ("attend"). The Greek Isaiah itself supplies δοῦλοι ("servants") as heirs at 65:9, so the appeal to the Hebrew is unnecessary. "Reward of the inheritance" has no Greek OT precedent at all. A better statement: **the Isaianic theme of the servants as heirs (Hebrew 54:17; Greek 65:9) is a possible background to the paradox of slaves who inherit**, rated low.

## A7: Deuteronomy 21:18–21 reversed at Colossians 3:20–21

### What was tested
- The ἐρεθ- ("provoke") count, stem anywhere, with each hit read for sense.
- The Hebrew behind the Greek.
- The scan rank, against the null.
- The bearing of Eph 6:4 and of the NA28 variant at Col 3:21.

### Results and corrections
1. **ἐρεθ- anywhere in a word, Swete, sense-checked.** The raw search catches place and gentilic names (Χενέρεθ "Chinnereth", Χερεθθεί "Cherethite", Ἀναχερέθ, Μασερεθμεμφωνμάιμ) and Jer 44:16's χερέθ ("vault"); all excluded. Genuine uses occur in **twelve verses**: Deut 21:18, 21:20, 28:22, 31:27; Prov 19:4, 25:23; Sir 34:29; Old Greek Dan 11:10, 11:25; 1 Macc 15:40; 2 Macc 14:27; 4 Macc 9:19 (διερεθίζοντες, "stirring up"). **ἐρεθ- with ὑπακου- ("obey") in one verse, and within two verses: Deut 21:18 and 21:20 only. This holds.**
2. **Sense, and the Hebrew.** Deut 21:18's υἱὸς ἀπειθὴς καὶ ἐρεθιστής ("a son disobedient and contentious") renders בֵּן סוֹרֵר וּמוֹרֶה ("a stubborn and rebellious son"). 21:20's ἀπειθεῖ καὶ ἐρεθίζει ("he disobeys and is contentious") renders the same pair. In Deuteronomy ἐρεθίζω ("provoke") is used **intransitively, for rebelliousness**. It renders מרה ("rebel"), just as Deut 31:27's ἐρεθισμός ("contentiousness") renders מֶרִי ("rebellion"). So the son is not "provoking" his father to anger; he is rebellious. Col 3:21's μὴ ἐρεθίζετε τὰ τέκνα ("do not exasperate the children") is **transitive**, meaning to irritate someone. The "reversal" (son provokes, so father must not provoke) depends on reading the two uses as the same act. **They are not the same act.** Correction: the claim's description of the Deuteronomy word as the son's "provoking" should be "rebelliousness".
3. **Scan.** I could not reproduce the stated "first in two of three rare-word runs, median 11", because the passage boundaries used are not given. Against Col 3:18–21 the six-run scan puts Deuteronomy 21 **first, median 1**. The null is 19 of 40 arbitrary passages with a median-1 partner, so this does not beat chance. One genuine structural feature lies behind the rank, and it is worth recording. **Deut 21:10–21 is a run of family laws**: the captive taken as wife (21:10–14); the man with two wives, ἠγαπημένη … μισουμένη ("loved … hated"), and the firstborn (21:15–17); then the rebellious son and "father and mother" (21:18–21). The sequence runs wife, husband, sons, parents, like Col 3:18–21's order of wives, husbands, children, fathers. Its ἠγαπημένη ("loved", augmented) was **missed** by the scan's word-beginning matching, so the overlap is slightly larger than the scan shows (ἀγαπάω, "love", as in Col 3:19). The sequence is loose, its content is far from the Colossian code, and household codes in Hellenistic ethics run in this order anyway [S].
4. **Eph 6:4 and the variant.** Eph 6:4 reads μὴ παροργίζετε ("do not provoke to anger"). The NA28 apparatus at Col 3:21 records παροργίζετε, marked as from Eph 6:4, in א A C D* F G L 075 0198 0278 33 81 104 365 1175 1241ˢ 1505 and Ambrosiaster. That is a variant assimilated to Ephesians. It weakens the claim on two grounds:
   - The parallel code shows the verb was not fixed in the tradition; Ephesians uses Deuteronomy's word for Israel provoking God (παροργίζω, "provoke to anger", Deut 32:21, quoted at Rom 10:19), not Deut 21's word.
   - Many scribes heard Ephesians in Col 3:21, not Deuteronomy.
   If the shared word were the point of an allusion to Deut 21, the parallel would be expected to keep it.
5. **Nearer background.** The obvious OT background to "children, obey your parents" is the fifth commandment. Eph 6:2–3 quotes it; Colossians does not.

**Verdict: Discard.** **Recommended rating: low.** The rarity count holds. But Deuteronomy's ἐρεθίζω ("provoke") is the son's rebelliousness, rendering Hebrew מרה ("rebel"), not a provoking of someone. Without that, the "reversal" disappears. The scan rank is what arbitrary passages get. The parallel code in Ephesians and a large body of witnesses replace the verb, which is not what a deliberate echo would expect. The family-law sequence of Deut 21:10–21 can be noted as a curiosity, not as evidence.

## A8: The Joshua 18–19 allotment field behind Colossians 1:12 and 2:18

### What was tested
- The ἐμβατεύω ("enter, take possession") count and senses.
- μερίς ("portion") with κλῆρος ("lot") in one verse, with strict forms (κληρονομ-, "inherit", excluded).
- The Hebrew behind both.
- Rivals for "the lot of the holy ones in the light".
- A leave-one-out test of the field.

### Results and corrections
1. **ἐμβατεύω ("enter") in Swete, stem anywhere, including the augmented ἐνεβατ-:** Josh 19:49, 19:51; 1 Macc 12:25, 13:20, 14:31, 15:40; 2 Macc 2:30. Seven verses. **This holds.** Adding the Odes, the Psalms of Solomon and 1 Enoch changes nothing. Rahlfs has the same two Joshua verses. In the NT it occurs only at Col 2:18.
2. **The Hebrew behind it.** At Josh 19:49 ἐμβατεῦσαι ("to take possession") corresponds to לִנְחֹל ("to take as inheritance"). At 19:51 the closing ἐπορεύθησαν ἐμβατεῦσαι τὴν γῆν ("they went to take possession of the land") stands where the Hebrew says "they finished dividing the land" (מֵחַלֵּק, from חָלַק, "divide"). The Greek is a free rendering there.
3. **No Swete verse with ἐμβατεύω, μερίς and κλῆρος together. This holds.** Josh 19:49 has ἐμβατεῦσαι ("to take possession") with κλῆρον ("lot"), but no μερίς ("portion").
4. **μερίς ("portion") + κλῆρος ("lot"), strict forms, one verse, Swete:** **eleven verses.**
   - Deut 10:9, 12:12, 14:26 (= Hebrew 14:27), 14:28 (= Hebrew 14:29), 18:1;
   - Josh 18:6, 19:9;
   - Wis 2:9; Esther 4:17 (a Greek addition); Isa 57:6; Jer 13:25.
   Correction to the test's own examples: Num 18:20 has μερίς ("portion") with κληρονομία ("inheritance"), not κλῆρος ("lot"), so it drops out of the strict count, though it is the source of Deuteronomy's Levitical formula. **Five of the eleven are the Deuteronomic formula for the Levites**, who have "no portion and lot" among their brothers. Only two are in the Joshua allotment chapters. The genitive μερὶς τοῦ κλήρου ("portion of the lot"), the phrase of Col 1:12, **occurs nowhere in Swete**. The NT has μερίς with κλῆρος in one verse only at Acts 8:21 (οὐκ ἔστιν σοι μερὶς οὐδὲ κλῆρος, "you have neither portion nor lot", the Deuteronomic formula on Peter's lips) and Col 1:12.
5. **Hebrew pairs.** חֵלֶק ("portion") with גּוֹרָל ("lot") occurs in four WLC verses: Josh 18:6, Isa 17:14, Isa 57:6 and Ps 16:5. חֵלֶק ("portion") with נַחֲלָה ("inheritance") occurs in eighteen, led by the Levitical formula (Num 18:20; Deut 10:9; 12:12; 14:27, 29; 18:1; Josh 18:7). The Greek μερίς … κλῆρος ("portion … lot") in Deuteronomy renders חֵלֶק וְנַחֲלָה ("portion and inheritance").
6. **Joshua or the Levites?** Neither fits cleanly.
   - The Levitical formula is negative: no portion and lot. Colossians says God has qualified Gentiles **for** a portion.
   - The Joshua chapters describe a division of land by lot. Col 1:12 is not about land; it locates the lot "of the holy ones, in the light".
   - The closest Greek texts are elsewhere. **Wis 5:5**: πῶς κατελογίσθη ἐν υἱοῖς θεοῦ, καὶ ἐν ἁγίοις ὁ κλῆρος αὐτοῦ ἐστιν; ("how was he numbered among the sons of God, and his lot is among the holy ones?"). It is **the only Swete verse where κλῆρος ("lot") stands with the holy ones in this sense**; the other κλῆρος + ἅγιος hits, 1 Chr 24:5 and Neh 11:1, are priestly or civic lot-casting. **Old Greek Dan 7:18, 22, 27** has the holy ones of the Most High receiving the kingdom, and Col 1:13 goes straight on to "the kingdom of his beloved Son". **Acts 26:18** joins σκότους εἰς φῶς ("from darkness to light"), τῆς ἐξουσίας ("from the authority"), κλῆρον ("lot") and ἐν τοῖς ἡγιασμένοις ("among the sanctified"), the same cluster as Col 1:12–13, in Paul's commission account. The Qumran phrase "the lot of the holy ones" (1QS 11:7–8) and the sons-of-light language are the standard parallels [S]. Ps 15:5 Swete (Κύριος ἡ μερὶς τῆς κληρονομίας μου, "the Lord is the portion of my inheritance") gives the genitive "portion of [the] inheritance" shape.
7. **The sense test at 2:18.** Swete itself has two senses of ἐμβατεύω. In Joshua and 1 Maccabees it means to enter or occupy territory. At **2 Macc 2:30** it means to **go into a subject in detail, investigate**: τὸ μὲν ἐμβατεύειν καὶ περίπατον ποιεῖσθαι λόγῳ ("to enter into [the matter] and go over it in discourse", said of the historian). Col 2:18's ἃ ἑόρακεν ἐμβατεύων ("entering into what he has seen") fits the 2 Maccabees sense, or the mystery-cult sense of entering a sanctuary known from inscriptions [S], at least as well as the land-occupation sense. The Joshua link at 2:18 rests on one word whose LXX range already includes the non-territorial meaning.
8. **Leave one out, for the field.** Remove ἐμβατεύω ("enter") and what is left of the Joshua 18–19 field is μερίς ("portion") and κλῆρος ("lot"). That pair is more frequent in Deuteronomy than in Joshua, and the claim's Colossian phrase is not attested in any of them. Remove μερίς and κλῆρος, and ἐμβατεύω alone has two senses in Swete and connects to 1:12 only through the field. **The field does not survive the loss of either component.**

If restated, Col 1:12 draws on the general OT portion-and-lot (inheritance) idiom as reread in the "lot of the holy ones / light" tradition (Wis 5:5; Dan 7; Acts 26:18; Qumran [S]); that rates **moderate**. ## New candidates

- **Prov 2:1–6 for Col 2:2–3.** Six of Col 2:2–3's key terms in six verses: wisdom, understanding, treasures, "knowledge of God", knowledge, and a hiding verb (in the sense "store up"). It is a better match to Colossians' treasure-and-search shape than Isa 29:14. It deserves its own claim; I would suggest moderate pending audit.
- **Isa 45:3 for Col 2:3.** θησαυροὺς … ἀποκρύφους ("treasures … hidden"), Swete and Rahlfs, one of only two Swete verses with the pair. It is the sole Greek OT precedent for Colossians' own collocation. Worth a claim; moderate pending audit.
- **Theodotion Dan 2:18–23, 28 for Col 1:26–27 and 2:2–3.** μυστήριον ("mystery"), ἀπόκρυφα ("hidden things"), σοφία καὶ σύνεσις ("wisdom and understanding"), ἐγνώρισας ("you made known"). The densest Swete window for the "mystery hidden, now made known" vocabulary. A strong candidate.
- **Wis 6:22 and Wis 7:13–14** (οὐκ ἀποκρύψω ὑμῖν μυστήρια, "I will not hide mysteries from you"; τὸν πλοῦτον αὐτῆς οὐκ ἀποκρύπτομαι, "I do not hide her riches"; θησαυρός, "treasure") for Col 1:26–27 and 2:2–3. Swete only; there is no Rahlfs export.
- **Wis 5:5** (ἐν ἁγίοις ὁ κλῆρος αὐτοῦ, "his lot is among the holy ones") and **Old Greek Dan 7:18–27** for Col 1:12–13, with **Acts 26:18** as the NT parallel to the whole cluster of light, darkness, authority, lot and the sanctified.
- **Sir 32:13–24 (Swete) and Lev 19:13–15** for Col 3:24–4:1 (reward, impartiality, wronging, the worker's wages, fear of the Lord). **Prov 29:25–26** for the men-pleasing antithesis of Col 3:22–23.
- **Job 31:13–15** for Col 4:1: the master answerable to the Lord for the cause of his slave, made by the same Maker.
- **Ps 18:12 Swete** (ὁ δοῦλός σου … ἀνταπόδοσις πολλή, "your servant … great reward") and **Isa 65:9 Swete** (οἱ δοῦλοί μου … κληρονομήσουσιν, "my servants … shall inherit") for Col 3:24.
- **Wis 13:1–3** for Gal 4:8–9, and through Galatians for the "elements" of Col 2:8, 20: elements of the cosmos taken for gods, φύσει ("by nature"), and the failure to "know him who is".
- **4 Macc 5:6–25** for Col 2:8, 16: Antiochus calls Judaism φιλοσοφία ("philosophy") and θρησκεία ("worship, religion") over abstention from foods. 4 Maccabees 5 ranks first or second against Col 2:8–10 in five of six scan runs and third in the sixth (median 2, which the null tempers), and its date makes availability uncertain [S]. Swete only.
- **Deut 21:10–21 as a family-law sequence** (wife, two wives loved and hated, sons, rebellious son, father and mother) set beside Col 3:18–21. It is weak and recorded only so that the scan rank is explained, not as a recommended claim.
