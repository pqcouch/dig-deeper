# Claim audit 1 — Daniel — Auditor B

**Scope.** Six design and field claims (B1–B6), audited blind. Primary texts: Westminster Leningrad Codex (WLC, Hebrew and Aramaic, lemma-indexed); Swete's LXX (Daniel in both Old Greek and Theodotion), searched accent-free with every hit read for sense; Rahlfs exports (Daniel OG and Theodotion, Genesis) for any wording a finding rests on. References are given in English numbering unless marked "Heb." (Heb 3:31–33 = Eng 4:1–3; Heb 4:1–34 = Eng 4:4–37; Heb 6:1 = Eng 5:31; Heb 6:2–29 = Eng 6:1–28). Where I quote a lemma search I give the corpus's own Hebrew chapter-and-verse in brackets when it differs.

**Definitions used throughout.** A *rare contact* is a pair of content words (the commonest words in the Hebrew Bible, those in more than 1,500 verses, set aside) that stands in one verse of Daniel and in one verse of the comparison text and in no more than five verses of the whole Hebrew Bible. A *strong verse contact* is a verse-to-verse link carrying two or more such pairs. The *density scan* ranks every chapter of the Hebrew Bible of ten or more verses by rare pairs shared with the target, per verse of the comparison chapter. These measure shared vocabulary, not borrowing.

**A structural caution that governs B1, B2 and B5.** The WLC gives Aramaic words their own lemma entries. A lemma search therefore cannot see a Hebrew word answered by its Aramaic cognate, and every lemma-based scan of Daniel splits along the language boundary (Hebrew 1:1–2:4a and chs 8–12; Aramaic 2:4b–7:28). Because that boundary does not coincide with the tales/visions boundary (ch. 1 is a Hebrew tale; ch. 7 an Aramaic vision), any "section" result from lemma counts is partly a language result. I therefore ran each section test twice: on the WLC Hebrew chapters by lemma, and on the whole of Daniel through the Greek (Theodotion in Swete, the Greek additions of ch. 3 removed), where both languages are rendered into one medium. Aramaic–Hebrew contacts were read, not counted, and are said to be so.

## B1 — "The tales draw on Genesis, the visions on the Prophets"

### What I tested
1. Every named contact, by lemma, for rarity.
2. My own inventory of rare contacts between each Hebrew chapter of Daniel (1; 2:1–4; 8; 9; 10; 11; 12) and six corpora: Genesis 1–36, the Joseph story (Gen 37–50), the rest of the Torah, the Former Prophets, the Latter Prophets, the Writings.
3. A chance baseline: for every other chapter of the Hebrew Bible outside Genesis and the Latter Prophets (579 chapters), the same measure of "Joseph-or-Genesis over Prophets" preference; where does each Daniel chapter fall?
4. The same inventory and baseline through the Greek, for all twelve chapters.
5. A permutation test of the split itself: of all 924 ways to divide twelve chapters six and six, how many show a Joseph-versus-Prophets contrast at least as sharp as 1–6 against 7–12?

With פָּנִים ("face") in the same verse, only Dan 1:10 and 2 Chr 26:19; within one verse of each other, Gen 40:6–7 joins them. The Greek seals the link: σκυθρωπά ("sullen, gloomy") of faces stands in Gen 40:7 and Theodotion Dan 1:10 (Rahlfs), the Old Greek having a different phrase. **Holds.**
- חַרְטֹם ("magician, dream-priest") in Hebrew: Gen 41:8, 24; Exod 7:11, 22; 8:3, 14, 15; 9:11; Dan 1:20; 2:2 (ten verses, WLC). Six of the ten are Exodus. With מְכַשֵּׁף ("sorcerer") in the same verse, only Exod 7:11 and Dan 2:2. **Holds as a contact, but the rarer contact at 2:2 is with Exodus, not Genesis** (see New candidates).
- וַתִּתְפָּעֶם רוּחוֹ ("his spirit was troubled") with חֲלוֹם ("dream") in one verse: Gen 41:8, Dan 2:1, Dan 2:3 only (WLC); פָּעַם ("be troubled") is in only five verses of the canon. חָלַם ("dream") with שָׁנָה ("year") in one verse: Gen 41:1 and Dan 2:1 only. **Holds — the strongest single Joseph contact in the book.**

- 4:8–9, 18; 5:11, 14 (Heb 4:5, 6, 15; 5:11, 14), "spirit of the holy gods in him", against Gen 41:38 "a man in whom is the spirit of God": Aramaic against Hebrew; read, the match is plain. Theodotion 4:8 πνεῦμα θεοῦ ἅγιον ἐν ἑαυτῷ ἔχει against Gen 41:38 πνεῦμα θεοῦ ἐν αὐτῷ (Swete and Rahlfs). **Holds, cross-language.**
- 5:29: see B5. **Holds, cross-language.**
- The visions' named sources: קִיץ ("awake") with עָפָר ("dust"): Isa 26:19 and Dan 12:2 only; דֵּרָאוֹן ("abhorrence"): Isa 66:24 and Dan 12:2 only; צָדַק in the causative ("make righteous") with רַבִּים ("many"): Isa 53:11 and Dan 12:3 only; כָּלָה ("full end") with חָרַץ ("decreed"): Isa 10:23, 28:22, Dan 9:27; זַעַם ("indignation") with כָּלָה ("be finished", the verb): Isa 10:25, Ezek 22:31, Dan 11:36; לָבַשׁ ("clothed") with בַּד ("linen"): Lev 6:3; 16:4, 23, 32; Ezek 9:2, 3; 10:2, 6, 7; Dan 10:5; 12:6, 7; חָזוֹן ("vision") with מוֹעֵד ("appointed time") and קֵץ ("end") in one verse: Hab 2:3 only, with Dan 8:17–19 the only other place all three fall within two verses; the ordered phrase "for the vision is yet" (כִּי עוֹד חָזוֹן): Hab 2:3 and Dan 10:14 only; צִי ("ship") with כִּתִּים ("Kittim"): Num 24:24 and Dan 11:30 only; "lives for ever" with "heaven" and "hand" (חַי הָעוֹלָם … שָׁמַיִם … יָד): Deut 32:40 and Dan 12:7 only (all WLC). **All hold.**

**Permutation of the split.** Of the 924 six-and-six divisions of the twelve chapters, 67 (7.3 %) give a Joseph-versus-Prophets contrast as sharp as or sharper than 1–6 against 7–12; the contiguous cuts after ch. 5 and after ch. 7 both score higher than the cut after ch. 6. ### Corrections
- The original list of Joseph references is right in what it names but omits the strongest lemma contact of ch. 1: נָתַן ("give") with חֶסֶד ("favour") and שַׂר ("chief, keeper") in one verse — Gen 39:21 and Dan 1:9 only (WLC). Joseph in the prison is "given favour in the sight of the keeper"; Daniel is "given favour … before the chief of the eunuchs". This is Gen 39, not 40 or 41.
- Also omitted: בְּרִיאֵי בָשָׂר ("fat of flesh") with מַרְאֶה ("appearance"): the cows of Pharaoh's dream (Gen 41:2, 4, 18) and the four youths (Dan 1:15); the pair is in five verses of the canon (Gen 41:2, 4, 18; Zech 11:16; Dan 1:15). Daniel's youths look like the good years.
- And in the visions: יְאֹר ("Nile", the Egypt-river word) with עָמַד ("stand") and שָׂפָה ("bank") in one verse: Gen 41:3, 17 and Dan 12:5 only (WLC). Daniel's river is the Tigris (10:4), yet it is called by Pharaoh's word, and the men stand "on the bank of the יְאֹר" as Pharaoh stood in his dream. The visions are not Joseph-free.

### Verdict
**Original form ("tales draw on Genesis, visions on the Prophets"): Needs reframing — low–moderate.** "Genesis" is wrong (it is the Joseph story, chiefly Gen 39–41), the tales also draw heavily on the Prophets, chs 3 and 6 have no Joseph contact, and the split survives a permutation test only at about the 7 % level.
**Restated form ("the Joseph material is concentrated in the tales"): Confirmed with nuance — moderate–high.** It is concentrated in the dream-and-advancement tales (1, 2, 4, 5), absent from the deliverance tales (3, 6), and it surfaces once more in the last vision (12:5's "bank of the Nile").

## B2 — The late Writings dominate Daniel's density scan

### What I tested
Reproduction of both scans; a leave-one-out by Daniel chapter for chs 8–12; the same scan run on every book of the Hebrew Bible, counting how many of each book's top ten partners are late-biblical narrative books (Esther, Ezra, Nehemiah, Chronicles, Daniel — together 12.4 % of eligible chapters, so about 1.2 of any ten by chance); and a rerun with proper names and gentilics removed.

### Results
- **Reproduction: exact.** Whole book, top ten: Ezra 5, 6, 4, 7, Neh 1, Ezra 9, Esth 1, Esth 7, Ezra 1, 2 Chr 36 (densities 18.7 down to 1.09). Chs 8–12, top six: Neh 1 (2.73), Ezra 9 (1.53), Esth 1 (1.05), 2 Chr 36 (0.91), Ezek 9 and Ezra 1 (tied at 0.91). Five of the top six are late books, as stated (WLC; 851 chapters scanned).
- **The whole-book top four are a language artefact.** Ezra 4–7 are Ezra's Aramaic chapters. Daniel's Aramaic chapters can share Aramaic lemmas only with Ezra's Aramaic, Jer 10:11 and a few words elsewhere. Run on chs 2–7 alone, the scan's top four are again Ezra 5, 6, 4, 7. Nothing canonical follows from this.
- **Leave-one-out, chs 8–12.** Ch. 8 alone: Esth 7, Gen 22 (the ram), Ps 40 — no late dominance. Ch. 9 alone: Neh 1 (2.27, far ahead), Ezra 9, Mal 2, Jonah 4 — the prayer. Ch. 10 alone: Ezek 9, Ezek 1, Ezra 1, Ezra 9, Ezek 10 — Ezekiel's linen-clad figure and throne vision plus the Cyrus formula. Ch. 11 alone: Ezra 1, 2 Chr 14, Esth 8, Esth 3, Esth 1 — royal and military court language. Ch. 12 alone: Lev 16, Eccl 12, Ps 16, Isa 53 — no late dominance. The chs 8–12 result is therefore a composite of ch. 9's prayer (Neh 1, Ezra 9) and chs 10–11's Persian-court vocabulary.
- **Names removed.** Whole book: late books in top ten fall from 10 to 8. Chs 8–12: from 7 to 6, and in the top six from 5 to 3 (Neh 1, Ezra 9 remain; 2 Chr 36 and Ezra 1 drop out). Part of the 8–12 affinity is simply "Cyrus king of Persia" (Dan 10:1 ~ Ezra 1:1 ~ 2 Chr 36:22).
- **Baseline across books** (late books in each book's top ten, chance ≈ 1.2): Esther 8; Nehemiah 9; Ezra 10 (but its partners are Daniel's Aramaic chapters); Ecclesiastes 4; Jonah 3; Haggai 2; Malachi 1; Zechariah 0; Song 1; Ruth 1; Lamentations 0; Job 0; Proverbs 0. Chronicles 1 Chr 0, 2 Chr 1 — Chronicles' partners are its synoptic sources in Samuel–Kings, which swamp everything. Of the classical books, only 1 Kings (10, all Chronicles) and 2 Samuel (7, all Chronicles) score as high, again from synoptic parallels.

### What the baseline can and cannot decide
The brief proposed testing a late book "not adjacent in BHS order". The difficulty is that in the BHS sequence the late narrative books are *all* adjacent: Esther, Daniel, Ezra–Nehemiah, Chronicles are the last four books of the Ketuvim. Canonical design and shared register are therefore confounded for every book in the cluster; no internal test separates them. The far-placed late books give the only lever. Jonah (in the Twelve) has Esth 4, Neh 1 and Neh 9 among its top ten — three late books at two and a half times chance, with no canonical neighbourhood to explain it; Ecclesiastes (fifth before Esther) has four (Esth 6, 2 Chr 1, Dan 1, Dan 12); Haggai has Ezra 3 and Ezra 1 because they narrate the same rebuilding. So late books attract late books at two to four times chance wherever they stand in the canon; Daniel attracts them at six to eight times, inflated by Aramaic and by names. Register (shared late-biblical idiom, Persian court vocabulary, shared liturgical forms) is a sufficient explanation; canonical binding is not excluded, but nothing here requires it. The sequence "Chronicles closes the canon" rests on the BHS/Talmudic order, not on Codex Leningradensis itself, where Chronicles opens the Writings; any design reading of 2 Chr 36 must say so.

### Verdict
**Confirmed with nuance (as an observation) — moderate; as evidence for canonical design — low.** The figures reproduce exactly, but the whole-book list should not be quoted at all without saying that its top four are Aramaic-only matches, and the 8–12 list should be described as two clusters (the ch. 9 prayer; the Persian court of chs 10–11). Register explains the pattern; canonical design is untestable within the cluster.

## B3 — Handoff to Ezra–Nehemiah and Chronicles

### What I tested
The ordered phrase and its lemma form; the Aramaic equivalent (read); the frequency of the "year one of [king]" formula across the canon; the Dan 9:4 / Neh 1:5 comparison against Deut 7:9, 21; 10:17; 1 Kgs 8:23; 2 Chr 6:14; Neh 9:32.

### Results
- **The phrase.** אַחַת לְכוֹרֶשׁ ("one [year] of Cyrus"), ordered, plain and skeletal: Dan 1:21, Ezra 1:1, 2 Chr 36:22 — **three verses, exactly as stated** (WLC). By lemma, כּוֹרֶשׁ ("Cyrus") with אֶחָד ("one") and שָׁנָה ("year") in one verse: the same three. Positive control: the lemma search for Cyrus returns all thirteen Hebrew verses including Isa 44:28 and 45:1.
- **The Aramaic** (read, not counted): בִּשְׁנַת חֲדָה לְכוֹרֶשׁ ("in year one of Cyrus") at Ezra 5:13 and 6:3; by the Aramaic lemmas for Cyrus, "one" and "year", exactly these two. So the "first year of Cyrus" stands in five verses across both languages, all in Daniel, Ezra and Chronicles.
- **But Dan 1:21 is not the decree formula.** Ezra 1:1 and 2 Chr 36:22 read וּבִשְׁנַת אַחַת ("and in year one"), introducing the decree; Dan 1:21 reads עַד־שְׁנַת אַחַת ("until year one"), the terminus of Daniel's service. And 2 Chr 36:22–23 and Ezra 1:1–3 are the same text twice; the "three books" are one verse and its doublet plus Daniel.
- **The formula is Daniel's own idiom.** The construction "year" + "one" + "to [king]" (שְׁנַת אַחַת לְ‑, Aramaic שְׁנַת חֲדָה לְ‑) stands in nine verses of the Hebrew Bible: Dan 1:21 (Cyrus); 7:1 (Belshazzar, Aramaic); 9:1 (Darius); 9:2 ("of his reign"); 11:1 (Darius); Ezra 1:1; 5:13; 6:3; 2 Chr 36:22 (WLC, every hit read). Daniel uses it five times for three kings; outside these three books it does not occur at all (the classical books use "in the first year" with the ordinal, or "in year two of X"; the ordered phrase "in year two" is in twelve verses from Kings to Esther). So the shared phrase is a regnal-dating idiom confined to Daniel, Ezra and Chronicles, and within it Cyrus is simply the king for whom Ezra and Chronicles needed it. Daniel also has "year three of Cyrus" (10:1) and "the reign of Cyrus the Persian" (6:28 [Heb 6:29]).
- **The chronology is the real handoff.** Read as narrative, Dan 1:21 dates the end of Daniel's career to the very year with which Ezra 1:1 opens. That is a thematic and chronological join and needs no lexical scaffolding.
- **Dan 9:4 // Neh 1:5.** The ordered phrase הָאֵל הַגָּדוֹל וְהַנּוֹרָא ("the great and awesome God") is Dan 9:4 and Neh 1:5 only; the ordered phrase "to those who love him and keep his commandments" is Dan 9:4 and Neh 1:5 only in plain spelling, Exod 20:6, Deut 5:10 and 7:9 joining by the skeletal pass (ketiv spellings); the set of seven words אָנָּא ("O, please"), אֵל ("God"), גָּדוֹל ("great"), נוֹרָא ("awesome"), שֹׁמֵר ("keeping"), בְּרִית ("covenant"), חֶסֶד ("steadfast love") in one verse: Dan 9:4 and Neh 1:5 only. Drop אָנָּא and Neh 9:32 joins (it adds הַגִּבּוֹר, "the mighty"). "Keeping the covenant and the steadfast love" (שֹׁמֵר הַבְּרִית וְהַחֶסֶד) alone: Deut 7:9, 1 Kgs 8:23, Dan 9:4, Neh 1:5, Neh 9:32, 2 Chr 6:14 (ordered, skeletal). "Great and awesome God" by lemma: Gen 46:3 (false positive: "great nation"), Deut 7:21, 10:17, Dan 9:4, Neh 1:5, Neh 9:32. **"Almost word for word" holds: the two verses are verbally identical apart from the divine title (אֲדֹנָי "Lord" in Daniel, יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי הַשָּׁמַיִם "the LORD God of heaven" in Nehemiah) and the article on חֶסֶד.** Both are built from Deut 7:9 plus 7:21 / 10:17.
- **Beyond the one verse.** Dan 9 shares 25 rare pairs with Neh 1 against 5 with Neh 9, 12 with Ezra 9, 8 with 1 Kgs 8, 5 with Deut 7. But the further links are liturgical stock with older sources: Neh 1:6 "let your eyes be open … to hear the prayer" with 1 Kgs 8:29 and 2 Chr 6:20 (Solomon); Dan 9:18 "incline your ear … open your eyes" with 2 Kgs 19:16 and Isa 37:17 (Hezekiah). Two prayers drawing on one confessional idiom, rather than one quoting the other, is at least as likely; the direction of dependence is not decidable from the text.

| | |
|---|---|
| Counts verified | "3 verses" exact for the Hebrew phrase; 5 with the Aramaic (Ezra 5:13; 6:3). Dan 9:4 = Neh 1:5 verified: the seven-word set is exclusive to them |

### Verdict
**Confirmed with nuance — moderate as a thematic and chronological handoff; low–moderate as a lexical design claim.** The chronology (Daniel's service ends in the year Ezra opens) and the shared confession are real. But the phrase is an idiom that only these three books use, Daniel using it five times of three kings, so its presence at Dan 1:21 is what Daniel's style predicts; and the "canon's last book" is the doublet of the next book's first verse, on the BHS order.

## B4 — The Aramaic chapters as a concentric arrangement 2 // 7, 3 // 6, 4 // 5

### What I tested
The four exclusive items; the fifteen pairings of six chapters under three measures (overlap as a share of combined vocabulary; vocabulary shared by the two chapters of a pair and by no other Aramaic chapter; frequency-weighted similarity) on three vocabularies (all lemmas; content words only, names out; rare words only, in no more than twenty verses of the canon), each on Hebrew and English chapter divisions — eighteen runs; a linear-neighbour comparison; and a chance baseline of my own: the same test on every run of six consecutive chapters within one book elsewhere in the Hebrew Bible (745 blocks), asking how often the "1 // 6, 2 // 5, 3 // 4" pairing comes out on top.

### Results — the exclusive items
- שְׁלַח מַלְאֲכֵהּ ("he sent his angel"): the Aramaic verb "send" with "angel" in one verse — Dan 3:28 and 6:22 [Heb 6:23] only (WLC). Positive control: the verb alone returns 3:2, 3:28, 5:24, 6:23. The Greek keeps the match: Theodotion ἀπέστειλεν τὸν ἄγγελον αὐτοῦ at 3:95 and 6:23 (Rahlfs Theodotion). **Holds.**
- שֵׁיזִב ("deliver"): 9 occurrences in 7 verses, all in chs 3 and 6 (3:15, 17, 28; 6:14, 16, 20, 27 [Heb 6:15, 17, 21, 28]) and nowhere else in the canon (WLC). **Holds exactly.**
- שַׁלִּיט ("ruling") with מַלְכוּ ("kingdom") and צְבָא ("will, wish") in one verse: 4:17, 25, 32 and 5:21 [Heb 4:14, 22, 29; 5:21] — exactly four. **Holds.** But 5:21 is Daniel retelling ch. 4 to Belshazzar; the shared refrain is a quotation within the story, which is a link by narration rather than by arrangement.
- דְּקַק ("crush"): 2:34, 35, 40, 44, 45; 6:24 [Heb 6:25]; 7:7, 19, 23 (WLC). It is used of the fourth kingdom and the fourth beast as stated, but it is not exclusive to 2 and 7 — the lions crush the accusers' bones in ch. 6.
- My own search found more 3 // 6 exclusives: הַצְלַח ("prospered") closing each chapter (3:30; 6:28 [Heb 6:29]; the only two in Aramaic); the royal letter opening "to all peoples, nations and tongues that dwell in all the earth: peace be multiplied to you" (שְׁלָמְכוֹן יִשְׂגֵּא, "may your peace be great") — Heb 3:31 and 6:26 only; אָתִין וְתִמְהִין ("signs and wonders") in the doxologies (Heb 3:32–33; 6:28). Fourteen content words are shared by 3 and 6 and no other Aramaic chapter (names out), the most of any pair. **Caveat:** the letter-opening and "signs and wonders" fall in Heb 3:31–33, which English Bibles print as 4:1–3; on English chapter divisions much of the 3 // 6 evidence migrates to 4 // 6.

Margins are thin (e.g. overlap share 0.630 against 0.649; on all lemmas, Hebrew division, 0.668 against the next).

A sequential reading is neither better nor worse supported than a concentric one by vocabulary; it is not excluded.

Daniel 2–7 on the same setting is second and first — a result matched or beaten by 6.7 % of arbitrary blocks. For a pairing named in advance this is suggestive (about one chance in fifteen), not decisive, and it is carried by 3 // 6 and 4 // 5.

State it as "two strong inner pairs plus a thematic frame", say which chapter division the 3 // 6 letter-formula depends on, and do not present the frame as lexically demonstrated.

## B5 — Daniel follows Genesis 40–41 in sequence across chs 1–5

### What I tested
Whether Gen 41 also appears in Dan 1 and Gen 40 in Dan 2–5; the full inventory of Joseph contacts and where they fall; a rank-order test of the whole inventory against the claim's own null (1 in 6 for three points); rival sources for the investiture.

### Results
- **The three named points.** Dan 1:10 ~ Gen 40:6–7 (dejected / sullen faces) holds (see B1). Dan 2:1–3 ~ Gen 41:8 holds strongly. Dan 5:29 ~ Gen 41:42: Aramaic against Hebrew, read. Belshazzar's men "clothed Daniel" (הַלְבִּישׁוּ, "clothed") "with purple" (אַרְגְּוָנָא) and "a chain of gold" (הַמְנִיךְ דִּי דַהֲבָא) "on his neck" (עַל־צַוְּארֵהּ); Pharaoh "clothed him" (וַיַּלְבֵּשׁ) "in garments of fine linen" and put "the chain of gold" (רְבִד הַזָּהָב) "on his neck" (עַל־צַוָּארוֹ). In Hebrew, "clothe" with "gold" and "neck" in one verse is Gen 41:42 only (WLC). The Aramaic words for "purple" and "chain" are in only Dan 5:7, 16, 29. Theodotion 5:29 (Rahlfs): "they clothed Daniel in purple and put the gold chain round his neck" (τὸν μανιάκην τὸν χρυσοῦν περιέθηκαν περὶ τὸν τράχηλον αὐτοῦ) against Gen 41:42 (Rahlfs) "he put a gold collar round his neck" (περιέθηκεν κλοιὸν χρυσοῦν περὶ τὸν τράχηλον αὐτοῦ) — a closer match in the Greek than in the Semitic. The Old Greek is looser ("put a gold chain on him"). **Holds; each constituent is verified.**
- **But the order is not "prison chapter at Dan 1, palace chapter at Dan 2".** Dan 1 draws on all three chapters: Gen 39:21 at 1:9 (favour before the chief — exclusive pair, above), Gen 40:6–7 at 1:10, Gen 41:2, 4, 18 at 1:15 (the fat-fleshed cows), Gen 41:8 at 1:20 (the magicians, shared with Exodus). And Gen 40 reappears after Dan 1 only weakly: Dan 2:1 shares "dream" and "two" with Gen 40:5 (formulaic), and in the Greek 2:7 shares "dream" and "interpretation" with Gen 40:8, 4:5 [Eng] shares "dream", "saw", "head" with Gen 40:16 — formulaic dream-reporting language.
- **Full inventory and order.** Taking every Joseph contact I would defend (thirteen: Dan 1:9 / Gen 39:21; 1:10 / 40:6; 1:15 / 41:2; 1:20 / 41:8; 2:1, 2:2 / 41:8; 2:30 / 41:16; 4:8 / 41:38; 4:18 / 41:38; 5:11 / 41:38; 5:14 / 41:15; 5:16 / 41:15; 5:29 / 41:42), the rank correlation between position in Daniel and position in Genesis is strong: 63 concordant pairs against 8 discordant (Kendall's tau 0.77); in 20,000 random reshufflings of the Genesis side, 4 matched or beat it (p ≈ 0.0002). Adding the weaker Greek-only and vision contacts (17 in all; including 12:5 / 41:3, 17 and 10:14 / 49:1) still gives tau 0.66. On the claim's own three points the probability of correct order by chance is 1 in 6 (my reshuffling: 0.16), which by itself demonstrates nothing; the fuller inventory is what makes the order striking.
- **Why the order may be narrative rather than citation.** Gen 39–41 is a single rise: favour in confinement → interpretation in prison → summons → the king's dream → interpretation → recognition of "the spirit of God" → investiture. Any story of a foreign courtier's rise will touch those moments in that order; the order belongs to the plot. A rival shows this: Esther 6:11 (Mordecai "clothed" and led in honour) and 8:15 (Mordecai in "royal apparel … a great crown of gold … purple", וְאַרְגָּמָן) give the same investiture at the same point in a different story; "clothe" with "call out before him" in one verse is Gen 41:42–43's pattern repeated at Esth 6:11 (Jer 4:30 the only other). And Dan 5:29 is followed, like Gen 41:43, by a herald's proclamation (הַכְרִזוּ, "they proclaimed"; Gen 41:43 "they cried before him").
- **Rival sources.** For the investiture: Esth 6:8–11; 8:15. For the magicians: Exod 7:11 (חַרְטֻמִּים with מְכַשְּׁפִים, "magicians" with "sorcerers", only there and Dan 2:2). The Joseph story remains the closest source overall.

### Design table
| | |
|---|---|
| Counts verified | All three constituents verified (5:29 cross-language, closest in Theodotion); claimed "prison at 1, palace at 2" refuted in detail — Dan 1 uses Gen 39, 40 and 41 |
| Baseline | Three points: 1 in 6 by chance. Thirteen defensible contacts: tau 0.77, about 2 in 10,000 by random reshuffling |
| Sense | Daniel 1–5 tracks the Joseph rise from favour to investiture |
| Alternative explanation | The order is the plot order of any courtier-rise narrative (cf. Esther 6, 8); the reshuffling null does not model that |

### Verdict
**Needs reframing — moderate.** The specific chapter-by-chapter mapping (Gen 40 → Dan 1; Gen 41 → Dan 2; 41:42 → Dan 5) is too neat: Dan 1 already runs through Gen 39–41. Restated as "Daniel 1–5 retraces the Joseph rise (Gen 39–41) in broad order, from favour before the chief (1:9) to robe and gold chain (5:29)", it is well supported by the contact inventory, but because the order is also the natural order of the plot, it shows a sustained Joseph typology rather than a sequential reading of Genesis by the author.

## B6 — Daniel 2:20–23 (the doxology) as the key of Daniel 2

### What I tested
Each claimed link by lemma; the inclusio and the antithesis; a baseline: for every four-verse window of chs 2–7 (185 windows; 46 in ch. 2), the share of its content words (names out; the five commonest words of Daniel such as "king" and "God" set aside) that recur elsewhere in the same chapter; and a second run counting only words rare within Daniel (in twelve or fewer of its verses).

### Results — the links
- שְׁנָה ("change") with עִדָּן ("time"): 2:9, 2:21, 4:13 [Heb], 7:25 (WLC). The 2:9 / 2:21 link holds ("until the time changes" / "he changes times"). But the set is not peculiar to ch. 2, and the closest echo of the hymn is 7:25: "change" with "times" (זִמְנִין) and "time" — "change" with both time-words in one verse is 2:21 and 7:25 only. The little horn "intends to change times and law" — usurping the very prerogative the hymn ascribes to God.
- קוּם in the causative ("set up"): 2:21 "sets up kings"; 2:44 "the God of heaven will set up a kingdom" — **holds**. 2:39 has the simple stem ("another kingdom shall arise"), a looser link; 2:31 is the image "standing". The verb is everywhere in chs 3 and 6 as well (28 verses in Daniel), so the 2:21 / 2:44 pair is the point, not the lemma.
- יְהַב ("give") with חָכְמָה ("wisdom") in one verse: 2:21 and 2:23 only in the canon (WLC) — the hymn's own internal link. 2:30 has "wisdom" without "give" ("not by any wisdom that is in me"); 2:37–38 have "give" without "wisdom" ("has given you the kingdom"). **Holds as a pair of half-echoes**, each answering one word of the hymn line.
- גְּלָה ("reveal"): 2:19, 22, 28, 29, 30, 47 — **holds**, and the word is confined to ch. 2 in Daniel's Aramaic. With רָז ("mystery"): 2:19, 28, 29, 30, 47 (2:22 has "deep things" instead).
- **Inclusio "all the wise men of Babylon".** The ordered phrase חַכִּימֵי בָבֶל ("wise men of Babylon"): 2:12, 18, 48 and 4:3 [Heb] (6 Eng); with the preposition, also 2:14, 24 and 5:7 (WLC). "All" with "wise men" and "Babylon" in one verse: 2:12, 2:24, 2:48, 4:3 [Heb], 5:7. The phrase is a running refrain of chs 2, 4 and 5, not a two-point frame; 2:12 → 2:48 is an inclusio only in the sense that the chapter's crisis and resolution both concern this group. **Weak as an inclusio.**
- **Antithesis "there is not … a man on earth" (2:10) / "there is a God in heaven" (2:28).** לָא אִיתַי ("there is not") in 2:10 and 2:11 ("no one else … except the gods whose dwelling is not with flesh") and again in 2:30, 3:12–18, 3:29, 4:32 [Heb], 5:11. "There is" (אִיתַי) with "God" and "heaven" in one verse: 2:28 only. Read, 2:10–11 and 2:28 are an unmistakable answer — the Chaldeans say no man on the dry land can do it and only gods who do not dwell with flesh; Daniel says there is a God in heaven who reveals. **Holds as a reading; the words are common, the antithesis is in the syntax.**

### Results — the baseline
- The hymn window (2:20–23) has 28 content words; 16 of them recur elsewhere in ch. 2 (57 %). The mean for the 46 four-verse windows of ch. 2 is 65 %; 36 of the 46 windows have a recurrence rate as high or higher. Across all 185 windows of chs 2–7, 136 are as high or higher (mean 67 %).
- Counting only words rare within Daniel: the hymn has 19 such words, of which 7 recur in ch. 2. The mean for ch. 2's windows is 10.2; 41 of 46 windows equal or exceed it.
- The reason is visible in the list: the hymn introduces much vocabulary found nowhere else in the chapter — "might", "darkness", "light", "deep things", "hidden things", "thank", "praise", "dwells", "removes [kings]", "my fathers", "understanding". Its links to the narrative are few but well placed.

### Design table
| | |
|---|---|
| Counts verified | Four links hold (one as two half-echoes; "arise" at 2:39 is a different stem). Inclusio weak: the phrase recurs at 2:14, 18, 24 and in chs 4, 5. Antithesis holds by reading |
| Baseline | Hymn's recurrence rate 57 % against a ch. 2 mean of 65 %; 36 of 46 ch. 2 windows recur as much or more; on rare words, 41 of 46 |
| Sense | The hymn names, in advance, the attributes the chapter then displays: God changes times and kings (2:21 → 2:37–45), gives wisdom (2:23 → 2:30), reveals (2:22 → 2:28–47) |
| Alternative explanation | A doxology of a deliverance naturally summarises it; lexical recurrence is ordinary or below average for the chapter |

### Verdict
**Links: Confirmed with nuance — high** for "reveal", "change times", "set up kings → set up a kingdom", and the 2:10–11 / 2:28 antithesis; moderate for the split "give / wisdom" echoes; low for the inclusio. **Design ("the key of Daniel 2"): Needs reframing — moderate.** The hymn is thematically programmatic, but it is not lexically unusual: its vocabulary recurs in the chapter less than an average stretch of ch. 2 does. Its strongest single echo lies outside the chapter, at 7:25. It is better described as the chapter's theological thesis, stated once and illustrated, than as a lexical key.

## New candidates

1. **Exodus magicians behind Dan 2:2.** חַרְטֻמִּים ("magicians") with מְכַשְּׁפִים ("sorcerers") in one verse: Exod 7:11 and Dan 2:2 only; six of the ten Hebrew verses with "magician" are in Exod 7–9 (WLC). The scene — a foreign king summons his experts, who fail before the God of Israel — is as much the plague narrative as Gen 41. Worth testing as a second, Mosaic layer in the tales (the Hebrew of ch. 1–2:4 only; read the Aramaic for the rest).
2. **The good years in the four youths (Dan 1:15).** "Fat of flesh" with "appearance" links the youths to Pharaoh's seven good cows (Gen 41:2, 4, 18). Possibly the most pointed Joseph echo in ch. 1, unnamed by the claims.
3. **"The bank of the Nile" in the last vision (Dan 12:5–7).** The Tigris of 10:4 is called by the Egypt word יְאֹר ("Nile, river"), three times, with Pharaoh's "standing on the bank" (Gen 41:3, 17). A Joseph (or Exodus) frame at the very end of the book; it cuts across the tales/visions split and deserves its own dig.
4. **Dan 7:25 as the inversion of the doxology.** The little horn "intends to change times and law"; "change" with both time-words is 2:21 and 7:25 only. A cross-chapter link that serves the 2 // 7 frame better than any lexical item offered in B4 — though it is one verse.
5. **Esther as rival for the court-investiture scene.** Esth 6:8–11 and 8:15 (robe, horse, herald; purple and gold crown) share the sequence of Gen 41:42–43 and Dan 5:29. A three-way comparison (Joseph, Mordecai, Daniel) would show whether Daniel follows Genesis or a common court-tale pattern.
6. **Persian court vocabulary in Dan 1.** פַּרְתְּמִים ("nobles"): Esth 1:3, 6:9, Dan 1:3 only; פַּת־בַּג ("king's delicacies"): Dan 1:5–16 and 11:26 only (WLC). Esther 1 and 7 head the ch. 1 density scan. Dan 1's affinity with Esther is a register datum that bears on B2.
7. **Hezekiah's prayer behind Dan 9:18.** "Incline your ear … open your eyes" (נָטָה אֹזֶן … פָּקַח עֵינַיִם): 2 Kgs 19:16, Isa 37:17, Dan 9:18 only (WLC). Daniel's confession borrows from a besieged king's prayer for deliverance from an arrogant emperor — thematically apt for chs 8–11.
