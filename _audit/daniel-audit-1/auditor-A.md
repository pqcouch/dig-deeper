# Claim audit 1 — Daniel — Auditor A report (single contacts, A1–A16)

**Auditor:** A (blind). **Sources read:** the common brief, the Auditor A claims file, and the primary texts only. **Editions:** Hebrew and Aramaic from the Westminster Leningrad Codex (WLC), counted by lemma unless stated; Greek Old Testament observed in Swete (Old Greek and Theodotion printed separately) and cited from the Rahlfs exports of Daniel and Jeremiah; Greek New Testament from the SBLGNT. Anything drawn from memory of scholarship is tagged [S] and carries no verdict by itself. English references throughout; where WLC numbering differs in Daniel 3:31–4:34 and 6, the English is given.

---

## Method note and chance baseline (applies to every claim)

Every stated count was reproduced first by lemma search across the whole WLC, and each "only" or nil result was checked against a positive control (a reference known to contain the item). Ordered-phrase claims were also checked by reading.

**A baseline for "only two verses" claims.** Because most claims in this set rest on a pair of words found together in Daniel and in exactly one other verse, I measured how often that happens by chance. Taking the Hebrew chapters of Daniel (chapters 1 and 8–12, 154 verses), I counted, for every verse, the pairs of content words (excluding particles and words found in more than 400 verses) that occur together in exactly one verse outside Daniel.

- The average Hebrew verse of Daniel has **about three** such "exclusive pair" contacts (mean 3.0, median 2); **78 per cent** of verses have at least one.
- By contrast, a word that occurs in only two verses of the whole Hebrew Bible, one of them in Hebrew Daniel, is rare: there are **only seven** such shared words in the whole of Hebrew Daniel (Dan 10:4/Gen 2:14 "Hiddekel"; Dan 10:5/Jer 10:9 "Uphaz"; Dan 10:6/Ezek 1:7 "burnished"; Dan 10:17/1 Chr 13:12 "how"; Dan 11:24/Ps 68:31 "scatter"; Dan 12:2/Isa 66:24 "abhorrence"; Dan 12:3/Ezek 8:2 "brightness").
- The partner books of the exclusive pairs, set against each book's size, are over-represented as follows: Esther 7.5 times expectation, 2 Chronicles 2.0, Isaiah 1.7, Ezekiel 1.7, Nehemiah 1.6, Jeremiah 1.2; Genesis and Psalms are under-represented (0.8 each).

**What this means.** A single "2 verses in the Hebrew Bible" pair is what an ordinary verse of Daniel yields about three times over; on its own it is weak evidence. Such a pair counts only when it is (a) a genuinely rare word, (b) part of a cluster in the same passage, (c) a replicated phrase or syntax, or (d) supported by context and reception. The Esther and Chronicles surplus is a register effect (Late Biblical Hebrew court and archive vocabulary), not allusion. The Isaiah and Ezekiel surplus is the real signal.

A chapter-density scan (rare shared vocabulary, Daniel chapter against all 851 chapters of ten or more verses, WLC) was also run for Daniel 1, 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12 and is cited where it bears.

## A1 — Isa 26:19 behind Dan 12:2

**Tested.** קִיץ ("to awake") with עָפָר ("dust") in one verse; positive control; rival awakening and sleeping texts.

**Results.**
- קִיץ ("awake") with עָפָר ("dust") in one verse: **Isa 26:19 and Dan 12:2 only** (WLC). The count **holds exactly**. Widening to a one-verse window adds nothing. Positive control: the search returns Isa 26:19, the known instance.
- In the baseline, Dan 12:2 has only one exclusive-pair contact anywhere in the Hebrew Bible, and it is this one.
- Rivals. Job 14:12 has קִיץ ("awake") with שָׁכַב ("lie down"), but no dust and a denial ("they will not awake"). Ps 22:30 has "all who go down to the dust" (עָפָר, "dust") but no awaking. שָׁכַב ("lie down") with עָפָר ("dust") occurs at Job 7:21; 20:11; 21:26, all of lying in death and none of rising. חָיָה ("live") with עָפָר ("dust") gives Isa 26:19; Ps 22:30; 119:25; Neh 3:34.
- **A rival the claim did not name: Jer 51:39 and 51:57.** The verb יָשֵׁן ("to sleep") with קִיץ ("awake") and עוֹלָם ("everlasting") is found at **Jer 51:39 and 51:57 only**: "they will sleep a perpetual sleep and not wake up", said of Babylon's princes. Dan 12:2 uses the related adjective יָשֵׁן ("sleeper") with קִיץ ("awake") and עוֹלָם ("everlasting"), and the adjective with קִיץ occurs in **Dan 12:2 only**. So Dan 12:2 reads as a reversal of Babylon's everlasting sleep. This is a secondary resonance, not a replacement for Isaiah, because Isaiah alone supplies the dust.
- Greek. Theodotion's "those who sleep in the dust of the earth will awake" (ἐξεγερθήσονται, "will be roused") matches Isa 26:19 LXX ἐγερθήσονται ("will be raised") in verb root. The Old Greek has ἀναστήσονται ("will rise"), which is Isa 26:19 LXX's first verb (Rahlfs; Swete).

**Verdict.** Confirmed. **Recommended rating: high.** The count holds, the control works, and no rival supplies awaking from dust. Add Jer 51:39, 57 as a named secondary echo: Daniel's "sleepers … shall awake" reverses Babylon's "sleep … and not wake".

## A2 — Isa 66:24 behind Dan 12:2

**Tested.** The count of דֵּרָאוֹן ("abhorrence"); whether the senses match; whether the word is just part of a shared idiom; how A1 and A2 interact.

**Results.**
- דֵּרָאוֹן ("abhorrence"): **Isa 66:24 and Dan 12:2 only** (WLC). The count **holds**. It is one of the seven words Hebrew Daniel shares with only one other verse in the Bible (see the baseline).
- Sense. In Isa 66:24 the rebels' corpses "shall be an abhorrence to all flesh": the corpses are seen, unburied and unconsumed, for ever. In Dan 12:2 some of those who awake rise "to reproaches, to everlasting abhorrence". The noun is the same and the "everlasting" outcome is the same, but the subject changes: Isaiah has corpses that are looked at, Daniel has people raised to shame. Daniel makes Isaiah's spectacle of the dead into an outcome of resurrection. This is a transformation, not a mismatch.
- Idiom pool. The other half of the line, "to reproaches" (חֲרָפוֹת, "reproaches") with עוֹלָם ("everlasting"), is a stock phrase: חֶרְפָּה ("reproach") with עוֹלָם in Jer 23:40; 49:13; Ps 78:66; Dan 12:2. Jer 23:40 reads "everlasting reproach and everlasting humiliation", and its Greek (ὀνειδισμὸν αἰώνιον, "everlasting reproach") is word for word what Theodotion gives at Dan 12:2. So the "reproach" half belongs to the idiom pool; the "abhorrence" half does not.
- Greek. Neither Greek Daniel renders דֵּרָאוֹן ("abhorrence") with Isa 66:24 LXX's ὅρασις ("spectacle"): the Old Greek has διασπορὰν καὶ αἰσχύνην ("dispersion and shame") and Theodotion αἰσχύνην ("shame") (Rahlfs). The Greek translators did not mark the link, so the history-of-interpretation criterion rests on later readers [S].
- **A1 and A2 together.** The two contacts fall in the same verse and point to the same book, 40 chapters apart, at Isaiah's resurrection text (chapter 26) and at its closing judgement scene (chapter 66). Baseline: 35 of the 154 Hebrew Daniel verses (23 per cent) have rare contacts with two places at least five chapters apart in one other book. So the pattern of two places in one book is not unique in itself. What is unusual at Dan 12:2 is the grade of the evidence: one rare word plus one exclusive pair, with nothing else competing. Leave-one-out: drop either contact and Isaiah remains the only candidate for the other. The two are **mutually reinforcing but separately sufficient**, and together they make a reading of Isaiah as one book, its first and last horizons, plausible. See also New candidate 1: Isa 26:20 continues into Dan 11:36.

| Hays criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | — |
| Volume | Strong | a word found in only two verses of the Bible |
| Recurrence | Strong | with A1, in the same verse |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | an everlasting destiny of shame |
| Historical plausibility | Strong | — |
| History of interpretation | Possible | not marked by the Greek; standard in modern commentary [S] |
| Satisfaction | Strong | — |

**Verdict.** Confirmed with nuance. **Recommended rating: high.** The rare word is decisive, but Daniel shifts it from corpses seen to people raised. The "reproach" half of the line is common idiom (Jer 23:40) and should not be counted as part of the Isaiah contact.

## A3 — Isa 52:13; 53:11 behind Dan 11:33, 35; 12:3, 10

**Tested.** The causative ("hiphil") form of צָדַק ("be righteous") with רַבִּים ("the many"); whether the lemma search distinguishes the stem; שָׂכַל ("have insight"); positive controls; the Greek.

**Results.**
- צָדַק ("be righteous") with רַבִּים ("many") in one verse: **Isa 53:11 and Dan 12:3 only** (WLC). The count **holds**. The lemma search does **not** separate stems, so I checked the forms. Both are causative: יַצְדִּיק ("he will make righteous") at Isa 53:11 and מַצְדִּיקֵי ("those who make righteous") at Dan 12:3. The causative form occurs in 12 verses in all, and only these two have "the many".
- מַשְׂכִּילִים ("those who have insight") as the group named at Dan 11:33, 35; 12:3, 10: **confirmed**. The claim leaves out Dan 1:4, where the same participle describes the exiled youths ("skilful in all wisdom"). That is a deliberate frame from the book's opening, worth adding. Isa 52:13 opens with יַשְׂכִּיל ("he will have insight / prosper"), the same verb in the same stem.
- שָׂכַל ("have insight") with צָדַק ("be righteous") in one verse: Dan 12:3 only. This **holds but proves little**, because the Isaiah pair is split across two verses (52:13 and 53:11). The fair test is the passage: the Servant song (Isa 52:13–53:12) is the only text outside Daniel where the causative of שָׂכַל, the causative of צָדַק, and רַבִּים ("many", in 52:14, 15; 53:11; 53:12 twice) come together.
- Further contacts. Isa 52:15 "what they had not heard they will understand" (בִּין, "understand") with "many"; Dan 11:33 "those who have insight will make the many understand" (בִּין with "many"). The surface form לָרַבִּים ("to the many") stands at Isa 53:11 and at Dan 9:27 and 11:33 (with three other verses). The chapter-density scan ranks Isa 53 in the top ten of 851 chapters for Daniel 12.
- Greek. Isa 52:13 LXX συνήσει ὁ παῖς μου ("my servant will understand"), and both Greek Daniels at 12:3 have οἱ συνιέντες ("those who understand", Rahlfs). Here the Greek tradition preserves the link. Theodotion's ἀπὸ τῶν δικαίων τῶν πολλῶν ("from the righteous many") loses the causative but keeps "righteous" and "many" together, as Isa 53:11 LXX (δικαιῶσαι … πολλοῖς, "to justify … the many") does.

| Recurrence | Strong | four "those who have insight" verses; "the many" repeated |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | suffering, wise teachers who make many righteous, then exaltation |

**Verdict.** Confirmed. **Recommended rating: high.** The stem check strengthens the claim rather than weakening it. Restate the "Dan 12:3 only" result as a passage-level cluster (Isa 52:13–53:12), and add Dan 1:4 and the Greek συνίημι ("understand") link.

## A4 — Isa 10:23, 25 behind Dan 9:27 and 11:36

**Tested.** The two homographs spelled כלה: the noun כָּלָה ("complete destruction") and the verb כָּלָה ("to be finished"); the counts; what Dan 11:36 actually has; how to weigh Isa 10 against Isa 28.

**Results.**
- The noun כָּלָה ("complete destruction") with חָרַץ ("decree"): **Isa 10:23, Isa 28:22, Dan 9:27** (WLC). The count **holds**. In all three it is the same fixed phrase כָּלָה וְנֶחֱרָצָה ("a complete destruction, one that is decreed").
- The verb כָּלָה ("be finished") with זַעַם ("indignation"): **Isa 10:25, Ezek 22:31, Dan 11:36**. The count **holds**, but the three are not equal. Isa 10:25 (וְכָלָה זַעַם, "and the indignation will be finished") and Dan 11:36 (עַד כָּלָה זַעַם, "until the indignation is finished") have the same simple form of the verb with indignation as subject. Ezek 22:31 is the intensive form, "I have consumed them", with indignation as a separate object. In sense, **Isa 10:25 is the only real parallel.**
- **Correction: Dan 11:36 has the verb, not the noun.** It holds the verb "be finished" + "indignation" + נֶחֱרָצָה ("decreed"). The verb with חָרַץ ("decree") in one verse is Dan 11:36 only. So 11:36 does not hold "both" phrases: it re-uses the decree word from the fixed phrase while switching to the verb of Isa 10:25. Daniel blends the two Isaiah lines.
- Weighing Isa 10 against Isa 28.
  - The fixed phrase itself does not decide between them: it stands in both.
  - For Dan 9:26–27 the context words split. חָרַץ ("decree") within three verses of שֶׁטֶף/שׁוֹטֵף ("flood, overflowing") finds Isa 10:22–23 ("destruction decreed, overflowing with righteousness") and Dan 9:26–27 ("its end with a flood … desolations decreed"). Isa 28 has "overflowing" four times (28:2, 15, 17, 18) and a "covenant" (בְּרִית, "covenant", 28:15, 18) that is annulled, which fits Dan 9:27's covenant made strong for "the many". The noun כָּלָה ("complete destruction") within four verses of "covenant" gives Isa 28:22, Dan 9:27, Neh 9:31.
  - For Dan 11:36, Isa 10 is clearly ahead. Besides "indignation is finished" (10:25), Isa 10:5 calls Assyria "the rod of my indignation" (זַעַם, "indignation"), and Dan 8:19 speaks of "the latter end of the indignation". Most tellingly, **the reflexive form of גָּדַל ("to magnify oneself") occurs in only three verses besides Ezek 38:23: Isa 10:15 ("shall the saw magnify itself against the one who wields it?"), Dan 11:36 and 11:37** (WLC). The arrogant king of Dan 11:36–37 speaks in the voice of Isaiah's boastful Assyrian.
  - In addition, עַד ("until") with זַעַם ("indignation") occurs only at **Isa 26:20 ("until the indignation passes") and Dan 11:36**, and מְעַט ("a little") with זַעַם only at Isa 10:25 and 26:20. Isaiah's two "indignation" passages are bound to each other, and Daniel uses both (see New candidates).

| Design test | Result |
|---|---|
| Counts verified | yes, with the noun/verb correction at 11:36 |
| Baseline | the fixed phrase is replicated, which is stronger than an exclusive pair; the reflexive "magnify oneself" (four verses in all) adds an independent rare contact |
| Sense | the end of indignation and the end of the Assyrian-type oppressor: coherent |
| Alternative explanation | Isa 28:22 repeats the same phrase, so Isa 28 cannot be excluded for 9:27; for 11:36 the Isa 10 cluster wins |

**Verdict.** Confirmed with nuance. **Recommended rating: high** for Isaiah's "decreed destruction / indignation" language as the source. For Dan 11:36, Isa 10 is primary, on the reflexive "magnify oneself", "indignation" and the oppressor type. For Dan 9:27, Isa 10:22–23 and 28:15–22 are co-sources that cannot be ranked from the phrase alone. Correct the statement that 11:36 "holds both".

## A5 — Num 24:24 (and 24:14) behind Dan 11:30 (and 10:14)

**Tested.** The counts; the Greek readings; whether "live source" survives.

**Results.**
- צִי ("ship") with כִּתִּים ("Kittim"): **Num 24:24 and Dan 11:30 only** (WLC). The count **holds**. צִי ("ship") is rare in itself (4 verses: Num 24:24; Isa 33:21; Ezek 30:9; Dan 11:30). Positive control: Num 24:24 is returned.
- "In the latter days" (בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים) stands in **13 verses** (Gen 49:1; Num 24:14; Deut 4:30; 31:29; Isa 2:2; Jer 23:20; 30:24; 48:47; 49:39; Ezek 38:16; Hos 3:5; Mic 4:1; Dan 10:14). The count **holds**, and taken alone the phrase is idiom.
- **But the claim undersells the second contact.** Among those 13 verses, only **Num 24:14 and Dan 10:14** also have לְעַמְּךָ ("to your people"), and both are built the same way: "what [X] will do / will befall *to your people* in the latter days" (Num 24:14 "what this people will do to your people in the latter days"; Dan 10:14 "what will happen to your people in the latter days"). This turns an idiom into a near-phrase. Both are also introductions to a revelation that ends with the Kittim (Num 24:24; Dan 11:30). The baseline confirms the pair is exclusive.
- Greek (Rahlfs). Old Greek Dan 11:30 has ἥξουσι Ῥωμαῖοι ("Romans will come"), and Theodotion has οἱ ἐκπορευόμενοι Κίτιοι ("Kitians coming forth"). Theodotion's ἐκπορευόμενοι ("coming forth") echoes Num 24:24 LXX καὶ ἐξελεύσεται ἐκ χειρὸς Κιτιαίων ("and one shall come forth from the hand of the Kitians", Swete). The Old Greek's "Romans" is an early identification of the oracle [observed]. It sees the same reference that Num 24:24 had been read to carry [S].

| Recurrence | Strong | two separate contacts in one prophetic unit (Dan 10–11 / Num 24:14–24) |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | a Western sea-power afflicting the eastern king |
| Historical plausibility | Strong | — |
| History of interpretation | Strong | the Old Greek identifies "Romans"; Theodotion's verb follows Num 24:24 LXX |

**Verdict.** Confirmed (Kittim). **Recommended rating: high.** Numbers 24 as a live source: **confirmed with nuance, moderate–high**, upgraded from provisional. The second contact is not bare idiom but a framed phrase found only in Num 24:14 and Dan 10:14, so the claim rests on two matched brackets of one oracle, not on one contact plus one idiom.

## A6 — Hab 2:3 behind Dan 8:17, 19; 10:14; 11:27, 35

**Tested.** The counts; positive controls; whether קֵץ ("end") with מוֹעֵד ("appointed time") is rare; rivals in Ezekiel 7 and 12.

**Results.**
- חָזוֹן ("vision") with קֵץ ("end") in one verse: **Hab 2:3 and Dan 8:17 only** (WLC). The count **holds**.
- The ordered phrase עוֹד חָזוֹן ("yet a vision"): **Hab 2:3 and Dan 10:14 only**. The count **holds** both with and without vowels. Unordered, "yet" with "vision" in one verse gives six verses (Ezek 7:13; 12:23, 24; Hab 2:3; Dan 9:21; 10:14), so the word order is what is decisive.
- קֵץ ("end") with מוֹעֵד ("appointed time"): **5 verses**, Deut 31:10 (calendrical, "at the end of seven years, at the appointed time"), Hab 2:3, Dan 8:19, 11:27, 11:35. Leaving out the calendrical Deuteronomy verse, Habakkuk is Daniel's only partner.
- Syntax. עוֹד ("yet") with מוֹעֵד ("appointed time") gives Num 18:22; Hos 12:10; Hab 2:3; Dan 11:27, 35. "Yet" + "end" + "appointed time" gives **Hab 2:3, Dan 11:27, 11:35 only**. The clauses line up: Hab 2:3 כִּי עוֹד חָזוֹן לַמּוֹעֵד ("for the vision is yet for the appointed time"); Dan 11:27 כִּי עוֹד קֵץ לַמּוֹעֵד ("for the end is yet for the appointed time"); Dan 11:35 כִּי עוֹד לַמּוֹעֵד ("for it is yet for the appointed time"). This is a **replicated clause**, the strongest kind of evidence in this set.
- A further contact not named: Hab 2:3 "it will not lie" (the verb כָּזַב, "to lie") and Dan 11:27 "they will speak a lie" (the noun כָּזָב, "lie"), in the same verse that carries the replicated clause.
- Rivals. Ezek 12:21–28 is real. "The days are near and the fulfilment of every vision" (12:23) is general. But **"vision" + "days" + "many": Ezek 12:27 ("the vision that he sees is for many days") and Dan 8:26 ("seal up the vision, for it is for many days") only.** Ezekiel 12 is therefore a **co-source for Dan 8:26**, not a rival to Habakkuk at 8:17. Ezekiel 7 drives the "end" vocabulary (קֵץ, "end", five times in 7:2–6), but עֵת קֵץ ("time of the end") in that word order is Daniel's own phrase (8:17; 11:35, 40; 12:4, 9 only).
- Greek. Theodotion Dan 8:17 renders "for the vision is for the time of the end" as ἔτι γὰρ εἰς καιροῦ πέρας ἡ ὅρασις ("for *yet* the vision is for the end of the time"). It adds ἔτι ("yet"), which is not in the Hebrew of 8:17 but is Hab 2:3 LXX's ἔτι ὅρασις εἰς καιρόν … εἰς πέρας ("yet a vision for the appointed time … to the end", Swete). Theodotion read Habakkuk into Daniel 8:17: early evidence of the link in reception.

| Hays criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | — |
| Volume | Strong | an ordered phrase, a replicated clause, an exclusive pair, and "lie" |
| Recurrence | Strong | five Daniel verses |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | a delayed but certain vision |
| Historical plausibility | Strong | — |
| History of interpretation | Strong | Theodotion's addition at 8:17 |
| Satisfaction | Strong | — |

**Verdict.** Confirmed. **Recommended rating: high, live.** Add the replicated clause "for it is yet for the appointed time" (Hab 2:3; Dan 11:27, 35), the "lie" contact at 11:27, and Ezek 12:27 as a co-source for Dan 8:26.

## A7 — Ezekiel 1; 9–10 behind Dan 10:5–6; 12:6–7 (and 7:9–10)

**Tested.** קָלָל ("burnished"); לָבֻשׁ ("clothed") with בַּדִּים ("linen"); the Lev 16 rival; further contacts in 10:6; the throne vision of 7:9–10 read across languages.

**Results.**
- קָלָל ("burnished"): **Ezek 1:7 and Dan 10:6 only** (WLC). The count **holds**. The whole simile matches: כְּעֵין נְחֹשֶׁת קָלָל ("like the gleam of burnished bronze"), with עַיִן ("gleam"), נְחֹשֶׁת ("bronze") and קָלָל ("burnished") together in Ezek 1:7 and Dan 10:6 only. This is one of the seven words Hebrew Daniel shares with only one other verse.
- לָבַשׁ ("clothe") with בַּד ("linen"): **12 verses** as stated. **Correction:** Ezek 9:11 ("the man clothed in linen") is missed by the lemma search because its participle is tagged under a different entry; read correctly it is **13 verses**.
- Lev 16 against Ezekiel 9–10:
  - **Form.** Leviticus uses the singular בַּד ("linen") with finite verbs ("he shall put on"). Ezekiel and Daniel use the plural בַּדִּים ("linen garments") with the passive participle ("clothed"). The plural occurs **only in Ezek 9:2, 3, 11; 10:2, 6, 7 and Dan 10:5; 12:6, 7.**
  - **Phrase.** "A man" (אִישׁ, "man") + "clothed" + "linen" occurs in Ezek 9:2, 3, (9:11); 10:2, 6; Dan 10:5; 12:6, 7 only.
  - **Linen with "loins"** (מָתְנַיִם, "loins"): Exod 28:42 and Ezek 9:2, 3, 11 and Dan 10:5.
  - The chapter-density scan **ranks Ezek 9 first and Ezek 1 second of 851 chapters** for Daniel 10, and Ezek 10 fifth.
  - For Daniel 12, Lev 16 ranks first (density 0.32), on garment and atonement vocabulary. So the priestly background is real, but it reaches Daniel through Ezekiel's scribe-figure, whose phrase Daniel reproduces.
- Further contacts in 10:6:
  - תַּרְשִׁישׁ ("beryl") in "like the gleam of" (עַיִן) beryl: Ezek 1:16; 10:9; Dan 10:6 only.
  - לַפִּיד ("torch") with אֵשׁ ("fire"): 6 verses (Gen 15:17; Judg 15:5; Ezek 1:13; Zech 12:6; Job 41:11; Dan 10:6). The count **holds**, and adding בָּרָק ("lightning") narrows it to **Ezek 1:13 and Dan 10:6 only**.
  - מַרְאֵה ("appearance") with בָּרָק ("lightning"): Ezek 1:13; Nah 2:5; Dan 10:6.
  - The voice line ("the sound of his words like the sound of a multitude", הָמוֹן, "multitude") is **weak**: voice + multitude occurs in 12 verses, and Ezek 1:24 uses a different word, הֲמֻלָּה ("tumult").
  - Setting: Dan 10:4 "by the side of the great river" and Ezek 1:1 "by the river Chebar".
- 7:9–10 (Aramaic, read against Hebrew). Daniel's "its wheels burning fire" (גַּלְגִּלּוֹהִי נוּר דָּלִק, "its wheels a burning fire"): Hebrew גַּלְגַּל ("wheel") with אֵשׁ ("fire") occurs at **Ezek 10:2 and 10:6 only**. ## A8 — Deut 32:40 behind Dan 12:7

**Tested.** The counts; how widely the oath formula and the raised hand occur.

**Results.**
- שָׁמַיִם ("heaven") + חַי ("living") + עוֹלָם ("ever"): **Deut 32:40 and Dan 12:7 only** (WLC). The count **holds**.
- Swearing (שָׁבַע, "swear") + "living" + "ever": **Dan 12:7 only**. This holds but is trivial, because the swearing verb is Daniel's own. Swearing with "life/living" occurs in 14 verses (mostly Jeremiah, Samuel and Kings), so the oath by life is common.
- Raising the hand. Deut 32:40 has נָשָׂא יָד אֶל שָׁמַיִם ("lift the hand to heaven"); Dan 12:7 has "he raised (רוּם, 'raise') his right hand and his left hand to heaven". The verb רוּם ("raise") with "hand" occurs in Gen 14:22 ("I have raised my hand to the LORD … possessor of heaven and earth"), so on the verb, Gen 14:22 is closer. נָשָׂא יָד ("lift the hand") in the land oath (Exod 6:8; Ezek 20:5–42; 47:14; Neh 9:15) has no heaven and no "living". The hand + heaven + "living for ever" combination belongs to Deut 32:40 alone.
- Within Daniel. "By the one who lives for ever" (בְּחֵי הָעוֹלָם): the ordered pair "life" + "ever" occurs only in Dan 12:2 and 12:7. That is an internal echo between the two verses. The Aramaic of 4:31 (English 4:34) has לְחַי עָלְמָא ("the one who lives for ever"), showing the phrase as Daniel's own title for God, read across languages.
- Greek. Deut 32:40 LXX reads "I will lift my hand to heaven and **swear by my right hand** (ὀμοῦμαι τὴν δεξιάν μου) and say, I live for ever" (Swete). This Greek supplies both the "right hand" and the oath, which the Hebrew of Deut 32:40 lacks and Dan 12:7 has. Theodotion 12:7: "he raised his right hand … and **swore by the one who lives for ever**" (ὤμοσεν ἐν τῷ ζῶντι τὸν αἰῶνα). Rev 10:5–6 (SBLGNT) follows Daniel 12:7 ("raised his right hand to heaven and swore by the one who lives for ever and ever").
- The direction of the oath is reversed. In Deuteronomy God swears by his own life; in Daniel an angel swears by God's.

## A9 — Genesis 40–41 behind Daniel 1–2 (and 5:29), with Exodus 7:11

**Tested.** Each count and its control; the cross-language parallels; the court-tale objection.

**Results.**
- (a) פָּעַם ("be troubled") with רוּחַ ("spirit"): **Gen 41:8; Judg 13:25; Dan 2:1; 2:3** (WLC). The count **holds**; the verb has five verses in all (plus Ps 77:5). Gen 41:8 and Dan 2:3 share the identical form וַתִּפָּעֶם רוּחוֹ / רוּחִי ("and his / my spirit was troubled"). Dan 2:1 uses another stem of the same verb. Judges 13:25 (the Spirit "stirring" Samson) has a different sense.
- (b) חַרְטֹם ("magician") with חֲלוֹם ("dream"): **Gen 41:8 and Dan 2:2 only**. The count **holds**. The Hebrew word occurs only in Gen 41 (two verses), Exod 7–9 (six) and Dan 1:20; 2:2. It is an Egyptian loanword [S], and Daniel's Hebrew can only have it from Genesis or Exodus. Daniel's Aramaic uses its own cognate (2:10, 27; 4:4, 6; 5:11), which a lemma search cannot link and which I checked by reading.
- (c) זֹעֲפִים ("dejected", of faces): the participle form occurs **only at Gen 40:6 and Dan 1:10**. The claim **holds**; the other occurrences (Prov 19:3; 2 Chr 26:19) are finite verbs or infinitives. Context strengthens it: Gen 40:7 "why are your faces bad today?" and Dan 1:10 "why should he see your faces dejected?", both about a court official and faces. However, the official's title, שַׂר ("chief") with סָרִיס ("eunuch, official"), spreads widely (Gen 37:36; 39:1; 40:2; 2 Kgs; Jer; Dan 1:7–18), and the chapter scan ranks Esther 7 and Esther 1 above Gen 40 for Daniel 1. That is the court-register effect seen in the baseline (Esther at 7.5 times expectation).
- (d) Cross-language, read:
  - "Not in me." Gen 41:16 בִּלְעָדָי ("it is not in me / apart from me"); Dan 2:30 "not by any wisdom that is in me". The sense matches; the wording differs.
  - "God has made known." Gen 41:25 uses הִגִּיד ("told"), 41:28 הֶרְאָה ("showed"), and 41:39 הוֹדִיעַ ("made known"). Dan 2:28–29, 45 uses the Aramaic causative of the same root ("made known"). Only Gen 41:39 is the cognate.
  - "Found a man." Gen 41:38 "can we find a man like this, in whom is the spirit of God?"; Dan 2:25 "I have found a man". More exact still is the unnamed parallel, Dan 4:5–6 (English 4:8–9); 5:11, 14 "in whom is the spirit of the holy gods", which reproduces Gen 41:38's "a man in whom is the spirit of God".
  - Promotion and the gold chain. Gen 41:42 "he clothed him (causative of לָבַשׁ, 'clothe') … and put the gold chain on his neck (עַל צַוָּארוֹ, 'on his neck')"; Dan 5:29 "they clothed (the Aramaic cognate causative) Daniel in purple and put the chain of gold on his neck (עַל צַוְּארֵהּ, 'on his neck')". Hebrew "neck" with "gold" occurs only at Gen 41:42 and Judg 8:26 (camels' ornaments). Esther 6:11; 8:15 has royal robing but no neck chain. This is the closest of the cross-language parallels.
- (e) חַרְטֹם ("magician") with מְכַשֵּׁף ("sorcerer"): **Exod 7:11 and Dan 2:2 only**. The count **holds**. But Exod 7:11 also has חֲכָמִים ("wise men"), as does Gen 41:8 ("all the magicians of Egypt and all its wise men"). The Exodus contact is a list of diviners, and the core term is the same Egyptian loanword. It adds the motif of failed rival experts, which Gen 41 already supplies.
- Court-tale objection. The setting (officials, courtiers, a foreign king, promotion) is generic court-tale material: Esther shows it in vocabulary, and Ahiqar in plot [S]. But the specifically oneiric chain is not generic: troubled spirit, magicians summoned for a dream, God making known, a man with the spirit of God, a gold chain on the neck. Esther supplies none of it.

## A10 — Deut 7:9 and Neh 1:5 with Dan 9:4

**Tested.** A word-by-word comparison of Dan 9:4 with Deut 7:9; 7:21; 10:17; Neh 1:5; 9:32; 1 Kgs 8:23; 2 Chr 6:14.

**Results.**

  - **Deut 7:9 shares 6.**

  - 1 Kgs 8:23 and 2 Chr 6:14 share 3 ("who keeps the covenant and the lovingkindness").

Dan 9:4 combines Deut 7:9 (covenant-keeping and love), Deut 7:21 or 10:17 ("great and awesome") and the Decalogue (Exod 20:6; Deut 5:10, "to those who love me and keep my commandments"). ## A12 — Revelation 1:1; 4:1; 22:6 framed by Theodotion's Daniel 2:28–29, 45

  - In the whole of Swete, δεῖ ("must") with γενέσθαι ("take place") in one verse occurs elsewhere only in 1 Enoch 9:11, in a different construction.

Revelation 1:1 thus combines the Old Greek verb with a phrase common to both versions. Revelation knew a Greek Daniel close to both, or a proto-Theodotionic text [S].

The pair proves little in itself; the case rests on 22:6 *also* having ἃ δεῖ γενέσθαι.

The theme (limited tenure, succession) matches, but there are no verbal contacts. ## A15 — Deut 10:17 behind Dan 2:47 (cross-language)

**Tested.** How widely the Hebrew superlative occurs; whether "God of gods" is generic.

**Results.**
- The exact surface form אֱלֹהֵי הָאֱלֹהִים ("God of gods"): **Deut 10:17 and Ps 136:2 only**. אֲדֹנֵי הָאֲדֹנִים ("Lord of lords"): **Deut 10:17 and Ps 136:3 only** (WLC, read). A lemma-only search for "God" + "God" in sequence returns 16 verses, but nearly all are false positives ("God, my God", "the God of …"). This is a warning about lemma searches on common words.
- Josh 22:22 and Ps 50:1 (אֵל אֱלֹהִים יְהוָה, "El, God, the LORD") are a string of names in apposition, not a superlative construction. They are not true parallels.
- אֵל אֵלִים ("God of gods") at Dan 11:36 is Daniel's own Hebrew form; a lemma search for "El" + "El" in sequence also returns Ps 22:2 ("my God, my God"), a false positive.
- The Aramaic of Dan 2:47 (אֱלָהּ אֱלָהִין וּמָרֵא מַלְכִין, "God of gods and Lord of kings") occurs only here. מָרֵא ("lord") occurs only at Dan 2:47; 4:16, 21; 5:23 ("Lord of heaven"). Daniel turns "Lord of lords" into "Lord of **kings**", in step with "king of kings" (2:37) and the book's theme of sovereignty over kings.
- Greek. Old Greek 2:47 has θεὸς τῶν θεῶν καὶ κύριος τῶν βασιλέων ("God of gods and Lord of kings"). The Old Greek plus at 4:34 (Rahlfs 4:37) reads θεὸς τῶν θεῶν καὶ κύριος τῶν κυρίων καὶ βασιλεὺς τῶν βασιλέων ("God of gods and Lord of lords and King of kings"). In Swete, κύριος τῶν κυρίων ("Lord of lords") occurs **only at Deut 10:17, Old Greek Dan 4:34 and 1 Enoch 9:4**. The Old Greek translator supplied Deuteronomy's full doublet where the Aramaic does not have it: a reception witness to the link.

| Hays criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | — |
| Volume | Possible | half the formula, read across languages, and adapted |
| Recurrence | Possible | 2:47; 11:36; the Old Greek of 4:34 |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | incomparability; impartial God over all powers |
| Historical plausibility | Strong | — |
| History of interpretation | Strong (the Old Greek of 4:34) | — |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Ps 136:2–3 is an equal candidate for "God of gods / Lord of lords" |

**Verdict.** Confirmed with nuance. **Recommended rating: moderate** (down from moderate–high). The superlative is a two-text formula (Deut 10:17; Ps 136:2–3), and Dan 2:47 cannot be tied to one rather than the other. Daniel deliberately adapts it ("Lord of kings"). The Old Greek's reading of 4:34 is the strongest evidence that Deuteronomy was heard.

## A16 — Arioch (Dan 2:14–25) and Gen 14:1, 9

**Tested.** The count; whether the name is attested outside the Bible; whether any feature of Genesis 14 is at work in Daniel 2.

**Results.**
- אַרְיוֹךְ ("Arioch"): Gen 14:1, 9; Dan 2:14, 15 (twice), 24, 25 (WLC; the lemma is split into two entries, one for each book, so both were searched). The Hebrew Bible count **holds**. **Correction for the Greek Bible:** Ἀριώχ ("Arioch") also appears at **Judith 1:6**, "Arioch king of the Elymaeans" (Swete). So "only" holds for the Hebrew canon but not for the Greek Bible.
- Historical attestation [S]. A name of this shape (Arriwuk/Ariukki) is reported in second-millennium texts (Mari, Nuzi). If so, the name was an old Hurrian-type name, not a coinage. This cannot carry a verdict.
- Genesis 14 features in Daniel 2. There is no war of kings, no rescue and no Melchizedek. One contextual thread exists: Gen 14:1 pairs Arioch with "Amraphel king of **Shinar**", and Dan 1:2 uses the archaic name שִׁנְעָר ("Shinar") for Babylonia. Shinar occurs in only 8 verses (Gen 10:10; 11:2; 14:1, 9; Josh 7:21; Isa 11:11; Zech 5:11; Dan 1:2), and Dan 1:2 most naturally recalls Gen 11:2 (Babel). "Arioch" + "Shinar" near each other occurs in Gen 14:1, 9 only. Daniel places the two a chapter apart.
- Arioch's role. His title is רַב טַבָּחַיָּא ("chief of the executioners / guard"). In form it is the Babylonian title of Nebuzaradan, רַב־טַבָּחִים ("chief of the guard", 2 Kgs 25; Jer 39–52), which uses רַב ("chief") with טַבָּח ("guard"). In function it is Potiphar's office, שַׂר הַטַּבָּחִים ("captain of the guard"); "chief" + "guard" with שַׂר occurs only in Gen 37:36; 39:1; 40:3, 4; 41:10, 12. Arioch introduces Daniel to the king as the chief cupbearer introduced Joseph (Gen 41:9–13 against Dan 2:25, read). His narrative echo is Genesis 40–41, not Genesis 14.

| Design test | Result |
|---|---|
| Counts verified | yes (Hebrew canon); add Judith 1:6 for the Greek Bible |
| Baseline | a personal name shared by two books; Gen 14's other names (Amraphel, Chedorlaomer) do not appear in Daniel |
| Sense | none from Gen 14 active in Daniel 2 |
| Alternative explanation | an archaic, Genesis-coloured Babylonian setting (Shinar, Arioch), and Joseph-story court roles |

**Verdict.** The name link: confirmed with nuance, **high** for the lexical fact (with the Judith correction). An intended allusion to Genesis 14: **uncertain — flag for research, low.** The better reading is archaic nomenclature that colours Babylon as Shinar/Babel, with Arioch playing the Potiphar and cupbearer role of Genesis 40–41.

## New candidates

1. **Isa 26:20 and 10:25 behind Dan 11:36 (linking A1 and A4).** עַד ("until") with זַעַם ("indignation") occurs at Isa 26:20 ("hide … until the indignation passes") and Dan 11:36 ("until the indignation is finished") only. מְעַט ("a little") with זַעַם occurs only at Isa 10:25 and 26:20. Daniel's wise "shine like the brightness of the firmament" with the glory-light of Ezekiel's visions. Suggested **moderate**.
4. **Ezek 12:27 behind Dan 8:26** ("the vision … is for many days"), an exclusive three-word combination. Suggested **moderate–high**.
5. **Jer 51:39, 57 reversed in Dan 12:2** ("sleep a perpetual sleep and not wake"). Suggested **moderate**, as a secondary resonance to Isa 26:19.
6. **Mic 4:1, 13 behind Dan 2:34–35, 44–45.** Iron and bronze crushing many peoples; the mountain of the LORD's house established in the latter days. Suggested **moderate**.
7. **Ezra 1:7; 5:14; 6:5 behind Dan 1:2 and 5:2.** The vessels "in the house of his god(s)", and the Aramaic "vessels that Nebuchadnezzar took out of the temple". Suggested **moderate**; direction undecidable.
8. **Gen 41:38 behind Dan 4:5–6 (English 4:8–9); 5:11, 14** ("a man in whom is the spirit of God / of the holy gods"), closer than the claimed Dan 2:25 "found a man". Suggested **high** as part of the Joseph complex.

10. **Ezek 26:7 behind Dan 2:37.** Nebuchadnezzar as "king of kings" in Hebrew prophecy. Suggested **low–moderate**: the title is also Persian court style (Ezra 7:12).

These give the history-of-interpretation criterion a text-based footing instead of one resting on memory alone.
