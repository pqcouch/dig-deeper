# Claim audit 5 (Colossians): Auditor B report on the field, design and chain claims

**Editions.** I counted Greek NT lemmas in the SBLGNT with its MorphGNT lemma index. I searched the Greek OT in Swete by accent-stripped stem and read every hit for sense. I checked readings against the NA28 Colossians text and apparatus, the Rahlfs Psalms and the WLC where a reading carried weight.

**Definition of "rarer".** A lemma counts as rarer at threshold N if it occurs in at most N verses of the whole SBLGNT NT. I ran each test at N = 20, 50 and 100, and at 200 where that was useful, and I report whether each result survives the change of N.

**Units.** I used the twelve units fixed in the claims file. Colossians has 95 verses in the SBLGNT. The unit 1:24–2:5 has 11 verses and 4:2–18 has 17.

## C65: The personal frame (1:24–2:5 answered by 4:2–18)

**Claim.** The close of the letter (4:2–18) shares more of 1:24–2:5's rarer vocabulary than any other 17-verse stretch does. Epaphras's prayer at 4:12 gathers four of Paul's labour-words. Romans has the same frame and Philippians, 2 Corinthians and 2 Timothy do not.

### (i) "More than any other seventeen-verse stretch"
I compared 1:24–2:5 with every 17-verse window in Colossians that does not overlap it (52 windows).

| N | Rarer lemmas shared by 1:24–2:5 and 4:2–18 | Rank of 4:2–18 among 52 windows |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | 3: Λαοδίκεια (Laodicea), τέλειος ('mature'), ἀγωνίζομαι ('strive') | 1st (tied) |
| 50 | 8: adds γνωρίζω ('make known'), διάκονος ('minister'), μυστήριον ('mystery'), σοφία ('wisdom'), φανερόω ('make manifest') | 1st (tied) |
| 100 | 9: adds πληρόω ('fulfil') | 1st (tied) |
| 200 | 15 | 1st (tied) |

The claim holds as worded at every threshold, but there are three qualifications.

- **The lead is shared.** The windows that tie with 4:2–18 (3:22–4:13 through 3:25–4:16) are tied only because they contain 4:2–13. The shared vocabulary lies in 4:2–13: prayer, the mystery, Tychicus the διάκονος ('minister'), and Epaphras. The greetings in 4:14–18 add nothing. At N = 200 the nearest window that is genuinely different is 1:7–23, with 14 shared lemmas against 15.
- **The reverse test is weaker.** I asked which 11-verse window shares most with 4:2–18 (68 windows that do not overlap it). 1:24–2:5 ranks 4th at N = 20, 3rd at N = 50, 10th at N = 100 and 2nd at N = 200. The windows that beat it are mostly 1:24–2:5 shifted by one or two verses, so the frame belongs to the region 1:25–2:7 rather than exactly to the edges of the unit. At N = 100 the opening section 1:1–11 does as well (11 lemmas).
- **One shared word is a place-name.** If proper names are removed, Λαοδίκεια (Laodicea, at 2:1 and 4:13, 15, 16) drops out. The baseline then weakens noticeably; see below.

### (ii) Chance baseline
I took every pair of windows in Colossians (an 11-verse window and a 17-verse window that do not overlap, 4,692 pairs). The table gives the proportion of pairs that share at least as many rarer lemmas as the actual pair does.

| N | All lemmas | Without proper names |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | 1,068 of 4,692 (22.8 %) | 39.1 % |
| 50 | 80 of 4,692 (1.7 %) | 5.1 % |
| 100 | 258 of 4,692 (5.5 %) | 11.1 % |

So the link stands out at N = 50, is marginal at N = 100, and is not unusual at N = 20.

**Letter-closings compared.** I took the last 17 verses of each of twelve Pauline letters and found the earlier 11-verse window that shares most rarer vocabulary with them. Colossians' closing has the strongest best partner of all twelve by the pair baseline. Its partner is 1:26–2:7 at N = 50 (0.3 % of pairs do as well) and 1:25–2:6 at N = 100 (0.6 %). The next strongest are Galatians, whose partner (5:3–13) is close to the closing, not in its opening section (0.6 % / 1.7 %), and 2 Timothy, whose partner is 1:9–2:1 (1.7 % / 9.2 %). Philippians, 1 Thessalonians, 2 Thessalonians and Titus mostly fall between 12 % and 56 %. So, to answer the brief's question: a letter-closing always shares most with *some* earlier stretch, and in seven of the twelve letters that stretch lies in the first third. A partner as strong as Colossians' is not usual.

### (iii) The four words in 4:12

| Word in 4:12 | NT verses | In Colossians | In 1:24–2:5? |
|---|---|---|---|
| ἀγωνίζομαι ('strive') | 7 | 1:29, 4:12 (cognate ἀγών 'struggle' 2:1) | Yes |
| τέλειος ('mature') | 17 | 1:28, 4:12 | Yes |
| πληροφορέω ('fully assure') | 6 | 4:12 only | Only as the cognate noun πληροφορία ('full assurance', 4 NT verses) at 2:2; it is a different lemma |
| θέλημα ('will') | 58 | 1:1, 1:9, 4:12 | **No.** It is from the opening prayer |

Two of the four are exact lemma repeats from 1:24–2:5. One is a cognate. The fourth is not from 1:24–2:5 at all. Read as a whole, 4:12 echoes the **opening prayer** of 1:9 as strongly as it echoes 1:28–2:2: ὑπὲρ ὑμῶν ('for you') + prayer + ἵνα ('that') + a πληρ- verb ('fill / fully assure') + θέλημα ('will') + πᾶς ('all'). Compare 1:9, ἵνα πληρωθῆτε τὴν ἐπίγνωσιν τοῦ θελήματος αὐτοῦ ἐν πάσῃ σοφίᾳ ('that you may be filled with the knowledge of his will in all wisdom'), and 4:12, ἵνα σταθῆτε τέλειοι καὶ πεπληροφορημένοι ἐν παντὶ θελήματι τοῦ θεοῦ ('that you may stand mature and fully assured in all the will of God'). Epaphras himself is introduced at 1:7. The pairing of τέλειος ('mature') with θέλημα ('will') is Paul's own idiom (Rom 12:2), and πληροφορέω ('fully assure') is Pauline (Rom 4:21; 14:5).

### (iv) Romans and the other letters
I ran the same pairwise measure for each proposed frame. The ranks are for the closing stretch against windows of its own length.

| Letter, proposed frame | Shared at N = 20 / 50 / 100 | Rank of closing | Pairs doing as well |
|---|---|---|---|
| Romans 1:8–17 ↔ 15:14–33 | 1 / 3 / 8 | 42nd, 49th, **1st** of 394 | 41.6 % / 27.4 % / **4.0 %** |
| Philippians 1:12–26 ↔ 4:10–23 | 0 / 3 / 6 | 56th, 26th, 41st of 65 | 100 % / 57 % / 62 % |
| 2 Corinthians 1:3–11 ↔ 13:1–13 | 0 / 1 / 1 | low | 100 % / 83 % / 92 % |
| 2 Timothy 1:3–18 ↔ 4:6–22 | 3 / 5 / 7 | 16th, **2nd**, 3rd of 49 | 41 % / 27 % / 38 % |
| (Ephesians 3:1–13 ↔ 6:18–24, added) | 0 / 4 / 5 | | 100 % / 6.2 % / 10.8 % |

The Romans frame appears lexically only at N = 100, where it shares εὐαγγέλιον/εὐαγγελίζω ('gospel / preach the gospel'), θέλημα ('will'), καρπός ('fruit'), πνευματικός ('spiritual'), προσευχή ('prayer'), ἀλλήλων ('one another') and ὀφειλέτης ('debtor'). At N = 20 and 50 it shares no more than chance would give. By lexical measure Romans is no better than 2 Timothy, which the proposal says lacks the frame. Philippians and 2 Corinthians do lack it. Any Romans frame must therefore rest on its themes (travel plans, the visit, the nations), not on vocabulary.

| Hays test | Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | It is internal to the letter |
| Volume | Possible | 8–9 rarer lemmas at N = 50–100, but they are concentrated in 4:2–13, one is a place-name, and one of the "four words" is not from the unit |
| Recurrence | Possible | The mystery, manifesting and wisdom cluster recurs (1:26–28, 2:2–3, 4:3–5) |

**Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.** **Confidence: moderate.**
**Restatement:** "4:2–13 picks up the vocabulary of Paul's labour in 1:25–2:7, above all 'mystery' and 'make manifest' (1:26–27; 4:3–4) and 'strive' and 'mature' (1:28–29; 4:12). Epaphras's prayer also echoes the opening prayer (1:9). This is the letter's most distinctive closing link (rarer at N = 50 than 98 % of window pairs), but it does not survive the strictest threshold." Drop θέλημα ('will') from the list of 1:24–2:5 words. Drop the Romans parallel as lexical support, or state it as a thematic parallel only.

**New candidates in passing:**
- μυστήριον ('mystery') + φανερόω ('make manifest') within two verses occurs in the SBLGNT only at Col 1:26 (with 1:27), Col 4:3–4 and 1 Tim 3:16. This is the sharpest single strand of the frame. Rating: moderate–high.
- διάκονος ('minister') and σύνδουλος ('fellow slave') link Epaphras and Paul (1:7, 1:23, 1:25) with Tychicus (4:7). Rating: moderate.

## C66: 3:12–17 as the summary of church life

**Claim.** 3:12–17 draws on more of the rest of the letter than any other unit does.

**Method.** For each unit I took its rarer lemmas and counted four things. **Recurring** is how many of them occur in some other unit. **Units reached** is how many other units each unit shares a rarer lemma with. **Links** is the total of shared lemmas, unit by unit. I normalised by length in two ways: links per 100 words, and the proportion of a unit's rarer lemmas that recur.

| Unit | Verses / words | N = 50: rarer / recurring / units reached / links per 100 words | N = 100: units reached / links per 100 words / proportion recurring |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1:1–14 | 14 / 236 | 35 / 11 / 7 / 8.1 | 9 / 14.0 / 0.40 |
| 1:15–20 | 6 / 112 | 17 / 7 / 3 / 6.2 | 5 / 10.7 / **0.45** |
| 1:21–23 | 3 / 67 | 18 / 7 / 5 / 14.9 | 5 / 16.4 / 0.38 |
| 1:24–2:5 | 11 / 213 | 37 / **15** / **9** / 10.8 | **9** / 13.6 / 0.41 |
| 2:6–15 | 10 / 170 | 38 / 12 / 8 / 7.6 | **9** / 15.3 / 0.39 |
| 2:16–23 | 8 / 128 | 35 / 5 / 5 / 6.2 | 7 / 9.4 / 0.20 |
| 3:1–4 | 4 / 53 | 6 / 2 / 3 / 5.7 | 4 / 7.5 / 0.33 |
| 3:5–11 | 7 / 102 | 35 / 9 / 7 / 10.8 | 8 / 14.7 / 0.28 |
| **3:12–17** | 6 / 116 | 25 / 9 / 6 / 10.3 | 6 / 13.8 / 0.43 |
| 3:18–4:1 | 9 / 116 | 21 / 0 / 0 / 0.0 | 0 / 0.0 / 0.00 |
| 4:2–6 | 5 / 61 | 13 / 6 / 7 / **18.0** | 8 / **24.6** / 0.40 |
| 4:7–18 | 12 / 206 | 38 / 11 / 6 / 7.3 | 6 / 10.2 / 0.32 |

**Where 3:12–17 ranks.**
- On units reached it is 6th equal at N = 50 and 7th equal at N = 100. At N = 200 it reaches 10 of 11, where four units reach all 11.
- On links per 100 words it is 5th at N = 50, 6th at N = 100 and 3rd at N = 200.
- On the proportion recurring it is 5th at N = 50 and 2nd at N = 100.
- It is never first on any measure at any threshold. The units that reach furthest are 1:24–2:5 and 2:6–15. The densest per word is 4:2–6, the prayer and speech paragraph.

**Baseline.** I compared 3:12–17 with every six-verse window in Colossians (90 windows). At N = 50, 25 of 90 windows have as many recurring rarer lemmas and 34 of 90 reach as many units. At N = 100 the figures are 31 of 90 and 51 of 90. At N = 200 they are 24 of 90 and 27 of 90. So 3:12–17 sits in roughly the upper third: above average, but not exceptional.

**What it does reach.** These are the N = 50 links:
- Opening prayer: εὐχαριστέω ('give thanks'), μακροθυμία ('patience'), πνευματικός ('spiritual'), σοφία ('wisdom').
- Paul's ministry: νουθετέω ('admonish', 1:28 and 3:16 only in the letter), σοφία ('wisdom').
- 2:6–15: χαρίζομαι ('forgive', 2:13 and 3:13).
- The polemic: ταπεινοφροσύνη ('humility', 2:18, 23 → 3:12, now positive), σύνδεσμος ('bond', 2:19 → 3:14; 4 NT verses).
- The putting-off: ἐνδύω ('put on').
- 4:2–6: σοφία ('wisdom').

The **confession of 1:15–20 is not reached** at N ≤ 100. Its only contacts are σῶμα ('body', 3:15; 120 NT verses) and εἰρήνη ('peace', 3:15), which is related to εἰρηνοποιέω ('make peace', 1:20) only as a cognate. The thematic part of the claim (prayer, ministry, polemic, putting-off) is borne out word by word. The confession link is thin.

**Structural observation.** The household code (3:18–4:1) shares not one rarer lemma with any other unit at N ≤ 100. It is lexically sealed off. That supports the second half of the claim: 3:12–17 is the last unit that is lexically integrated before a unit that stands apart.

| Hays test | Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Internal |
| Volume | Possible | 9–12 recurring rarer lemmas, upper third of six-verse windows |
| Recurrence | Strong | νουθετέω ('admonish'), σύνδεσμος ('bond'), ταπεινοφροσύνη ('humility'), χαρίζομαι ('forgive') each pick up a specific earlier verse |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | The polemic's words are re-used positively (humility, bond); the opening prayer's words are fulfilled |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Ephesians 4:1–3 and 5:18–20 do comparable work |
| Satisfaction | Possible | The summary role reads well, but no measure shows it as unique |

**Verdict: Needs reframing.** **Confidence: moderate** for the reframed claim, and **low** for the claim as stated.
**Restatement:** drop "more than any other unit". Say instead: "3:12–17 gathers words from the opening prayer, Paul's ministry, the polemic and the putting-off, and turns the polemic's vocabulary (ταπεινοφροσύνη 'humility', σύνδεσμος 'bond') to positive use. It is the last lexically integrated unit before the self-contained household code." Do not cite the confession (1:15–20) as one of its sources.

**New candidate in passing:** ταπεινοφροσύνη ('humility') is pejorative at 2:18 and 2:23 and commended at 3:12. It is a deliberate re-valuation inside the letter. Rating: moderate–high.

## C67b: The "you → us" switch (design side only)

I listed every first- and second-person plural pronoun and verb in Colossians (SBLGNT) and read each passage where they meet. Two kinds of first-person plural have to be set aside first:
- the **apostolic "we"** (Paul and Timothy): 1:3, 1:7–9, 1:28, 4:3, 4:8;
- **formulaic "our"** in the greeting and thanksgiving: "God our Father" (1:2) and "our Lord" (1:3).

That leaves these switches between second and first person plural inside statements about salvation:

| # | Place | Direction | Within one sentence? | Content at the switch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1:12 ὑμᾶς ('you') → 1:13 ἡμᾶς ('us') → 1:14 ἔχομεν ('we have') | you → us | Yes | rescue, transfer, redemption, **forgiveness of sins** |
| 2 | 1:14 (we) → 1:21–22 ὑμᾶς ... ἀποκατηλλάγητε / ἀποκατήλλαξεν ('you ... reconciled') | us → you | No (the confession intervenes) | reconciliation |
| 3 | 2:13 ὑμᾶς ... συνεζωοποίησεν ὑμᾶς ('he made you alive') → χαρισάμενος ἡμῖν ('having forgiven us') → 2:14 ἡμῶν / ἡμῖν ('our / against us') | you → us | Yes | **forgiveness**, the bond cancelled |
| 4 | 2:14 (us) → 2:20 ἀπεθάνετε ('you died') | us → you | No (the warnings of 2:16–19 intervene) | dying with Christ |

No other salvation statement in the letter changes person. 2:10–12, 2:20, 3:1–4 and 3:9–10 are in the second person throughout.

**Do 1:12–13 and 2:13 stand out?** Yes. They are the **only two places** where an *inclusive* first-person plural enters a salvation statement, and the only two switches *within a sentence*. In both, the inclusive "we" arrives at the point of **forgiveness**: τὴν ἄφεσιν τῶν ἁμαρτιῶν ('the forgiveness of sins', 1:14) and χαρισάμενος ἡμῖν πάντα τὰ παραπτώματα ('having forgiven us all our trespasses', 2:13). Switches 2 and 4 are only returns to the letter's normal second person after a digression.

**Cautions.**
- This kind of switch is Paul's idiom (category (a)). It is not peculiar to Colossians. Eph 2:1–5 runs ὑμᾶς ('you', 2:1) → ἡμεῖς πάντες ('we all', 2:3) → ἡμᾶς ... συνεζωοποίησεν ('he made us alive', 2:5) → ἐστε σεσῳσμένοι ('you have been saved', 2:5). Gal 4:5–6 runs ἀπολάβωμεν ('we might receive') → ἐστε ('you are') → ἡμῶν ('our').
- The design reading depends on readings that vary in the manuscripts. The NA28 apparatus records ἡμᾶς ('us') for ὑμᾶς ('you') at 1:12 in the majority of witnesses. It records ἡμᾶς and ὑμῖν variants at 2:13, and ἡμῶν ('our') for ὑμῶν ('your') at 3:4. I do not adjudicate these; the other auditor does. But if 3:4 read ἡμῶν, a third switch would appear and the "only two" observation would weaken.

**Verdict on the design side: Confirmed with nuance.** **Confidence: moderate–high**, conditional on the printed text at 1:12 and 3:4. State it as "the only two switches into an inclusive 'we' are at the forgiveness clauses", and note the Ephesians parallel.

## C68: The speech chain (λόγος, 'word')

**Counts.**
- λόγος ('word') occurs in **7 verses** of Colossians (SBLGNT lemma): 1:5, 1:25, 2:23, 3:16, 3:17, 4:3, 4:6. It occurs in 318 verses of the whole NT. The positive control is 3:16 Ὁ λόγος τοῦ Χριστοῦ ('the word of Christ'), which the search returned.
- **λόγος does not occur in 3:8–9.** The speech words there are βλασφημία ('slander'), αἰσχρολογία ('obscene talk'; an NT hapax, and a separate lemma despite the -λογ- element), "from your mouth", and ψεύδομαι ('lie'). The first link of the chain is therefore carried by other words, not by λόγος.
- **δεῖ ('must'), by form:** 4:4 and 4:6 only in Colossians. This is confirmed. **Warning:** the SBLGNT index files δεῖ under δέω, which also covers δέδεμαι ('I am bound') at 4:3. A lemma count gives three, and one of them is the imprisonment. The positive control for the form search was Eph 6:20 ὡς δεῖ με λαλῆσαι ('as I must speak'), which it returned, along with 1 Thess 4:1.

**Senses of λόγος in the chain.** It does not keep one sense:
- 3:16 'the word of Christ' and 4:3 'a door for the word' mean the gospel message;
- 3:17 'in word or deed' and 4:6 'your word' mean the believers' own speech;
- 2:23 λόγον σοφίας means 'a reputation for wisdom'.

**Baseline.**

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Share of verses with λόγος: Colossians | 7.4 % (7 of 95) |
| Same, all 13 Pauline letters | 3.9 % (79 of 2,029) |
| Comparable letters | 1 Thessalonians 7.9 %, 2 Timothy 8.4 %, 1 Timothy 7.1 %; the major letters 1.3–3.8 % |
| λόγος verses in 3:8–4:6 (24 verses) | 4 (3:16, 3:17, 4:3, 4:6) |
| 24-verse windows in the 13 letters with ≥ 4 λόγος verses | 72 of 1,730 (4.2 %) |
| Same, ≥ 3 | 150 of 1,730 (8.7 %) |
| Same, seven undisputed letters only, ≥ 4 | 12 of 1,329 (0.9 %) |
| 24-verse windows in Colossians with ≥ 4 | 9 of 72; all are this stretch, and 4 is the letter's maximum |

So the stretch from 3:16 to 4:6 is denser in λόγος than about 96 % of Pauline 24-verse stretches. But Colossians as a whole runs at about twice the Pauline rate, so part of the excess belongs to the letter, not to the chain.

**A stronger link than λόγος.** ἐν χάριτι ('in grace / with graciousness') occurs at 3:16 and again at 4:6. It is found in only seven NT verses. The SBLGNT prints ἐν χάριτι at 3:16; the NA28 prints ἐν [τῇ] χάριτι. This ties the singing of 3:16 to the speech of 4:6 more specifically than λόγος does. The other speech words in the stretch are λαλέω ('speak', 4:3, 4:4), ἀποκρίνομαι ('answer', 4:6), the three song words of 3:16, and ἅλας ('salt', 4:6; 4 NT verses, the other three in the Gospels).

**Paul's idiom, not a design peculiar to Colossians.** Ephesians has the same sequence:
- 4:29 πᾶς λόγος σαπρός ('no rotten word') ... ἵνα δῷ χάριν ('that it may give grace');
- 5:4 αἰσχρότης ('obscenity'), μωρολογία ('foolish talk'), and εὐχαριστία ('thanksgiving') instead;
- 5:6 κενοῖς λόγοις ('empty words');
- 5:19 psalms, hymns and spiritual songs;
- 6:19–20 λόγος ἐν ἀνοίξει τοῦ στόματός μου ('a word in the opening of my mouth') ... ὡς δεῖ με λαλῆσαι ('as I must speak'). This last is verbatim with Col 4:4.

The chain is therefore a paraenetic pattern shared by Colossians and Ephesians (category (a)). The phrase πῶς δεῖ ('how one must') is also common Pauline usage (1 Thess 4:1; 2 Thess 3:7; 1 Tim 3:15).

| Hays test | Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Internal |
| Volume | Possible | 4 λόγος verses in 24, plus ἐν χάριτι ('in grace') twice, plus δεῖ ('must') twice |
| Recurrence | Strong | Speech words at 3:8–9, 3:16–17, 4:3–4, 4:6 |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | Foul speech put off, then the word of Christ, then the apostle's speech, then the believers' speech |
| Historical plausibility | Strong | The same sequence in Ephesians shows it is a known pattern |
| Satisfaction | Possible | It is a good reading, but λόγος changes sense and is missing from the first link |

**Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.** **Confidence: high** on the counts (with the δέω warning); **moderate** as design.
**Restatement:** "The speech thread runs 3:8–9 → 3:16–17 → 4:3–4 → 4:6. It is carried by a cluster of speech words, not by λόγος alone; λόγος is absent from 3:8–9. ἐν χάριτι ('in grace', 3:16 and 4:6) is its most specific link. The same sequence appears in Ephesians 4:29–6:20, so this is Paul's paraenetic pattern rather than a design unique to Colossians." Any count of δεῖ must be made by form, not by the index lemma.

**New candidate in passing:** δέδεμαι ('I am bound', 4:3) sits between the two δεῖ ('must') clauses. Paul's bonds and the necessity of his speaking are juxtaposed, as at Eph 6:20 ('an ambassador in a chain ... as I must speak'). Rating: low–moderate.

| 3:17 → 3:23: ὃ ἐὰν ποιῆτε ('whatever you do') | Relative + ἐάν + ποιῆτε: NT only Col 3:17 (ὅ τι ἐάν) and 3:23 (ὃ ἐάν). John 13:17 and 15:14 have conditional ἐὰν ποιῆτε ('if you do'), which is a false friend | Confirmed. The SBLGNT and NA28 agree on ὅ τι at 3:17. Eph 6:8 has ὃ ἂν ποιήσῃ ('whatever he does') at the same point in its household code |
| 3:22–4:1 → 4:9: the slave addressed, then named | **Colossians never calls Onesimus a slave.** 4:9 has "the faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you". The identification depends on Philemon (Philemon 10–16). Inside Colossians δοῦλος ('slave') is applied at 4:12 to Epaphras ("a slave of Christ"), and σύνδουλος ('fellow slave') at 1:7 and 4:7 | Needs reframing. This is a weld only for readers who know Philemon. The weld internal to the letter is δοῦλος ('slave') → Epaphras the δοῦλος Χριστοῦ ('slave of Christ') |

### (ii) The chance baseline for "seam density"
**Measures.** For every possible cut point in Colossians I counted the rarer lemmas shared across the cut in two ways:
- **Local:** the 3 verses before the cut against the 3 after.
- **Unit:** the whole preceding unit against the first 3 verses of the next. At random cut points the "preceding unit" is the 8 verses before the cut.

I compared the 11 real unit boundaries with all cut points, and drew 20,000 random sets of 11 cut points. I did the same for Philippians and Ephesians at these stated paragraph breaks:
- Philippians: 1:12, 1:27, 2:1, 2:12, 2:19, 3:1, 3:12, 4:1, 4:10, 4:21.
- Ephesians: 1:15, 2:1, 2:11, 3:1, 3:14, 4:1, 4:17, 5:1, 5:21, 6:10, 6:21.

| Letter | N | Mean shared across real boundaries (local / unit) | Mean shared across all cut points (local / unit) | Random 11-point sets doing as well (local / unit) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Colossians | 20 | 0.09 / 0.18 | 0.16 / 0.22 | 84 % / 70 % |
| Colossians | 50 | 0.27 / 0.36 | 0.47 / 0.72 | 89 % / 95 % |
| Colossians | 100 | 0.45 / 0.73 | 0.66 / 1.11 | 82 % / 91 % |
| Colossians | 200 | 0.91 / 1.45 | 1.18 / 2.12 | 85 % / 98 % |
| Philippians | 50 | 0.20 / 0.70 | 0.36 / 0.64 | 90 % / 47 % |
| Philippians | 100 | 0.50 / 1.50 | 0.66 / 1.19 | 81 % / 22 % |

| Ephesians | 100 | 0.36 / 1.36 | 0.69 / 1.41 | 93 % / 59 % |

**A third measure.** I also compared whole unit with whole unit, using windows the length of an average unit (8, 9 and 13 verses). The real boundaries again fell below the mean: 77–88 % of random sets do as well in Colossians, 71–79 % in Philippians and 67–84 % in Ephesians.

**Reading.** Across the actual unit joints in Colossians there is *less* rarer-vocabulary pick-up than across an average cut point. This holds at every threshold and on every measure. That is expected, since editors put paragraph breaks where the vocabulary changes, but it means the lexical data give no support to a claim of exceptional welding. Philippians and Ephesians, measured the same way, are at or slightly above their own averages. If anything, Colossians' joints are *less* lexically welded than theirs.

**Connectives.** The connective seams are ordinary Pauline practice. Five Colossian units open with οὖν ('therefore': 2:6, 2:16, 3:1, 3:5, 3:12), and others with a relative, καί ('and') or νῦν ('now'). Ephesians opens three units with οὖν, plus διὰ τοῦτο ('for this reason'), διό ('therefore'), τούτου χάριν ('for this reason') twice, and καὶ ὑμᾶς ('and you', 2:1).

**What does hold** is that several welds reach *across* a unit rather than across the joint: 1:23 → 1:25, 2:8 → 2:20, 2:14 → 2:20, 3:17 → 3:23, 2:15 → 3:9. These are distance echoes of rare words. That is a real feature, but it is not "seam density".

| Hays test (the whole-letter claim) | Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Internal |
| Volume | Weak | Pick-up at the real joints is below the chance mean |
| Recurrence | Possible | Individual distance echoes are real (ἐγενόμην ... διάκονος 'I became ... minister', στοιχεῖα 'elements', ὃ ἐὰν ποιῆτε 'whatever you do') |
| Thematic coherence | Possible | Several welds are clear (2:20 → 3:1) |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Linking by catchword is a known technique, but the same connective idiom occurs in Ephesians |
| Satisfaction | Weak | The "whole letter built this way" claim adds nothing that ordinary Pauline transitions do not already explain |

**Verdict:**
- For the individual welds: **Confirmed with nuance.** Correct 2:5 → 2:6–7 and the Onesimus weld, and note that ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς ('on earth') is not rare.
- For whole-letter design: **Discard**, or at most **Uncertain**. The baseline the claim lacked has now been run, and the result is negative.

**Confidence:** high for 1:23 → 1:25, 2:20 → 3:1, 3:17 → 3:23 and ἀποκαταλλάσσω ('reconcile'); moderate for the others; **low** for the whole-letter claim.
**Restatement:** "Colossians repeats rare phrases at a distance (1:23/1:25; 2:8/2:20; 3:17/3:23). Its unit joints are no more lexically welded than random cut points, and no more than those of Philippians or Ephesians."

**New candidate in passing:** ἀπεκδύομαι ('strip off') occurs in the NT only at Col 2:15 and 3:9, with the cognate noun ἀπέκδυσις ('stripping off') at 2:11, an NT hapax. Christ's stripping of the powers is mirrored in the believer's stripping off of the old self. This is a distance echo, not a seam. Rating: moderate–high.

## C72: The dwelling chain (temple thread)

### (i) ἐνοικέω ('dwell in') in the NT (SBLGNT, 5 verses)

| Verse | What dwells | Temple context? |
|---|---|---|
| Rom 8:11 | the Spirit, in you | Not explicit. Indwelling, with οἰκέω ('dwell') in the same verse; 1 Cor 3:16 makes the link to the temple |
| 2 Cor 6:16 | God, "I will dwell in them" | **Yes**: ναὸς θεοῦ ('temple of God') twice |
| 2 Tim 1:5 | faith, in Lois, Eunice and Timothy | No |
| 2 Tim 1:14 | the Holy Spirit, in us | No |
| Col 3:16 | the word of Christ, in you | No (see iv) |

The phrase in 2 Cor 6:16 is **not LXX wording**. Swete's Lev 26:11–12 reads θήσω τὴν διαθήκην μου ἐν ὑμῖν ... ἐνπεριπατήσω ἐν ὑμῖν ('I will set my covenant among you ... I will walk among you'). Ezek 37:27 reads ἡ κατασκήνωσίς μου ἐν αὐτοῖς ('my dwelling will be among them'). So ἐνοικήσω ('I will dwell') is Paul's own choice of verb for divine indwelling. A note on the search: Swete spells ἐνπεριπατήσω with -νπ-, so a search on the -μπ- spelling misses Lev 26:12.

οἰκέω ('dwell') at 1 Cor 3:16 is indeed in a temple text: ναὸς θεοῦ ἐστε ... τὸ πνεῦμα τοῦ θεοῦ οἰκεῖ ἐν ὑμῖν ('you are God's temple ... the Spirit of God dwells in you').

### (ii) ἐνοικέω in Swete
This verb occurs in **33 verses**, not counting the Odes, which duplicate Isaiah. I set aside these false friends:
- ἐνοικίζω ('settle someone', Sir 11:34);
- ἐνοικειόω (Esther 8:1);
- ἐνοίκιον (2 Macc 8:33);
- ἔνοικος ('inhabitant', Jer 31:9; 51:2).

The positive control was Lev 26:32, οἱ ἐνοικοῦντες ἐν αὐτῇ ('those who live in it'). **In every one of the 33 verses the subject is human inhabitants**, mostly "the inhabitants of the land / Jerusalem" in Isaiah and Jeremiah. **ἐνοικέω is never used of God dwelling in Swete.**

### (iii) κατοικέω ('dwell') and κατοικητήριον ('dwelling-place') in Swete
**κατοικέω, the verb:** 569 verses outside the Odes. I set aside these false friends: the noun κάτοικος ('inhabitant'), κατοικία ('dwelling'), κατοικίζω ('cause to settle'), whose aorist κατῴκισεν mimics the verb, and κατοίκησις ('dwelling'). Read for sense, the verb has **God as subject in 16 verses (about 2.8 %)**:

| Setting | Verses |
|---|---|
| The temple or house (house built "for me to dwell in"; "will God indeed dwell with men?") | 2 Kgdms 7:5; 1 Chr 17:4; 3 Kgdms 8:27; 3 Kgdms 8:53 (the LXX plus, twice); 2 Chr 6:18 |
| Zion or Jerusalem | Ps 9:12; Ps 67:17; Ps 131:14 ('here I will dwell'); Ps 134:21; Isa 8:18 |
| Heaven or the height | Ps 2:4; Ps 112:5; Ps 122:1; Isa 33:5; Isa 57:15; Ps. Sol. 18:11 |

There is also one *false* divine claim, Ezek 28:2: the prince of Tyre says κατοικίαν θεοῦ κατῴκηκα ('I have dwelt in the dwelling of God').

**κατοικητήριον, the noun:** 19 verses. God's dwelling in 11 of them: Exod 15:17; 3 Kgdms 8:39, 43, 49; 2 Chr 6:30, 33, 39; 2 Chr 30:27; Ps 32:14; Ps 75:3; 3 Macc 2:15. The others are human cities, lions' dens, and the haunt of jackals. Two corrections to the evidence as proposed:
- 2 Chr 6:21 has κατοίκησις ('dwelling'), not κατοικητήριον.
- κατοικητήριον itself never occurs in Colossians. Its Pauline-circle use is Eph 2:22, where the church is the dwelling-place of God.

**Ps 67:17 (LXX).** The only verse in Swete where εὐδοκέω ('be pleased') and κατοικέω ('dwell') coincide with God as subject is **Ps 67:17**: τὸ ὄρος ὃ εὐδόκησεν ὁ θεὸς κατοικεῖν ἐν αὐτῷ ('the mountain in which God was pleased to dwell'). The positive control for the pairing search was Isa 62:4, which returned εὐδοκέω ('be pleased') near a dwelling word, not of God. The Rahlfs export reads identically. The Hebrew (Ps 68:17, WLC) has חָמַד אֱלֹהִים לְשִׁבְתּוֹ ('God desired it for his dwelling'). That verbal pairing is a striking match for Col 1:19, ἐν αὐτῷ εὐδόκησεν πᾶν τὸ πλήρωμα κατοικῆσαι ('in him all the fullness was pleased to dwell'). Availability in Paul's circle is good, because Ps 67:19 is quoted at Eph 4:8. The chapter scan ranks Ps 67 4th against Col 1:19–20 in all six settings. It is not first, because the chapter totals are diluted by γῆ ('earth') and οὐρανός ('heaven'). The verse-level pairing is what distinguishes it.

**NT κατοικέω** (40 verses) with God as subject is a temple-polemic use: Matt 23:21 (the one who dwells in the temple); Acts 7:48 and Acts 17:24 (not in temples made with hands). With Christ or the fullness as subject: Col 1:19, Col 2:9, Eph 3:17 ("that Christ may dwell in your hearts").

### (iv) Does 3:16 carry temple vocabulary of its own?
No. Colossians has no ναός ('temple'), ἱερόν ('temple'), οἶκος ('house') in a cultic sense (it occurs only at 4:15, "the church in her house"), οἰκοδομή ('building'), θυσία ('sacrifice') or σκηνή ('tent'). 3:12–17 has words of worship: ψαλμός ('psalm'), ὕμνος ('hymn'), ᾠδή ('song'), ᾄδω ('sing'), εὐχαριστέω ('give thanks'). It also has ἅγιοι ('holy ones', 3:12) and ἐν ἑνὶ σώματι ('in one body', 3:15). None of these is temple-specific. The building word ἐποικοδομέω ('build upon') is at 2:7, not in 3:12–17.

### Do the three verses form one thread?
- **1:19 and 2:9 certainly belong together.** They repeat the same formula: ἐν αὐτῷ ... πᾶν τὸ πλήρωμα ... κατοικ- ('in him ... all the fullness ... dwell'). 2:9 is a deliberate resumption that adds θεότης ('deity') and σωματικῶς ('bodily'), both NT hapaxes. The temple background for κατοικέω is real but a minority use: about 3 % of the verb's uses in Swete. It is the εὐδόκησεν ('was pleased') of 1:19 that points specifically to Ps 67:17 and so to Zion.
- **3:16 uses a different verb with a different subject.** The verb's LXX use is never divine. Its Pauline use is an indwelling idiom (the Spirit at Rom 8:11 and 2 Tim 1:14; God at 2 Cor 6:16; faith at 2 Tim 1:5), not a temple word as such. The verse carries no temple vocabulary of its own.
- **The link of 3:16 to 1:19 and 2:9 is conceptual**, a movement from "dwelling in him" to "dwelling in you". The nearest true parallel is Eph 3:17, where Christ dwells in your hearts, with κατοικέω ('dwell'). That supports an indwelling thread in Paul's thought. It does not support a temple thread carried by Colossians' own words.

| Hays test | Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Ps 67 LXX is used in Paul's circle (Eph 4:8); the dwelling language of the Solomon temple prayer is widespread |
| Volume | Possible (1:19) / Weak (3:16) | εὐδοκέω + κατοικέω with God as subject: Ps 67:17 alone in Swete. ἐνοικέω never divine in Swete |
| Recurrence | Possible | 1:19 → 2:9 is exact. 3:16 changes verb |
| Thematic coherence | Possible | Fullness dwelling in Christ, then Christ's word dwelling in the church. Eph 2:22 makes the church God's dwelling, but Colossians does not say so |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Pauline temple-indwelling texts exist (1 Cor 3:16; 2 Cor 6:16), but they use ναός ('temple'), which Colossians lacks |
| Satisfaction | Possible (1:19) / Weak (3:16) | The Zion echo illuminates 1:19. The temple reading adds little at 3:16 |

**Verdict: Needs reframing.**
- 1:19 with Ps 67:17 (εὐδόκησεν ... κατοικεῖν, 'was pleased ... to dwell'): **moderate–high**, as a specific echo of Zion as God's chosen dwelling.
- 1:19 and 2:9 as a pair: **high** as a repetition within the letter; the temple overtone is **moderate**.
- 3:16 as part of a temple thread: **low**. Its connection to 1:19 and 2:9 is an indwelling idea in category (a), not temple vocabulary.

**Restatement:** "1:19 echoes Ps 67:17 LXX (God 'was pleased to dwell' on Zion), and 2:9 resumes 1:19. 3:16 extends the *indwelling* idea to the church in Paul's own idiom (cf. Eph 3:17; Rom 8:11), but carries no temple vocabulary. Call it an indwelling thread, not a temple thread."

**New candidate in passing:** Wis 1:4: οὐδὲ κατοικήσει ἐν σώματι καταχρέῳ ἁμαρτίας ('nor will [wisdom] dwell in a body in debt to sin'). Here wisdom dwells in a σῶμα ('body'), close to the lexical set of 2:9 (fullness dwelling σωματικῶς, 'bodily') and of 2:3 (treasures of wisdom), and the χειρόγραφον ('record of debt', 2:14) theme is also nearby. Rating: low–moderate; it needs research.

## What I could not test
- **The ending of Romans.** The corpus's SBLGNT layer lacks Romans 16:25–27; it ends at a bracketed 16:24. So I could not include the μυστήριον + φανερόω ('mystery' + 'make manifest') doxology in the Romans frame test, and that might strengthen the Romans comparison.
- **Phrase and syntax welds.** Welds made by syntax (the relative chain, twin conditionals) or by phrase rather than rare lemma are not captured by a lemma-based seam measure. I checked them one by one, not against a baseline.
- **Rahlfs for Kings, Chronicles and Leviticus.** The Rahlfs text of Kingdoms, Chronicles and Leviticus is not in the exports. The 3 Kingdoms 8 and 2 Chronicles 6 readings, and Swete's διαθήκην ('covenant') at Lev 26:11 (where the Hebrew has "my dwelling"), are cited from Swete only.
- **The pronouns.** I did not adjudicate the person variants at Col 1:12, 2:13 and 3:4.
- **Exhaustiveness of the God-as-subject count.** The list for κατοικέω ('dwell') in Swete was made by reading filtered candidates (singular forms near divine, heavenly or Zion words, and first-person forms). A divine subject expressed only by context several verses away could have been missed, so 16 is a floor.
