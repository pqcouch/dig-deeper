Colossians is divided (for these claims) into twelve units: 1:1-14 · 1:15-20 · 1:21-23 · 1:24-2:5 · 2:6-15 · 2:16-23 ·
3:1-4 · 3:5-11 · 3:12-17 · 3:18-4:1 · 4:2-6 · 4:7-18. Use the SBLGNT lemma index. "Rarer" means you must define it
(e.g. lemmas occurring in at most N verses of the NT) and state it; test whether the result survives changing N.

## C65 — The personal frame: 1:24-2:5 and 4:2-18
Proposal: Paul's account of his own labour (1:24-2:5) is answered at the close (4:2-18): 4:2-18 shares more of
1:24-2:5's rarer vocabulary than any other seventeen-verse stretch of the letter, and 4:12 gathers four of Paul's
labour-words — ἀγωνίζομαι ('strive', 1:29), τέλειος ('mature', 1:28), πληροφορέω/-ία ('fully assure', 2:2), θέλημα
('will', 1:9) — into Epaphras's prayer. Romans is said to have the same frame (15:14-33 answering 1:8-17); Philippians,
2 Corinthians and 2 Timothy are said not to. Proposed: moderate; synthetic. Test the "more than any other stretch",
the four words, the Romans comparison and a chance baseline (how often does a letter's closing stretch share most
rare vocabulary with some earlier stretch?).

## C66 — 3:12-17 as the summary of church life
Proposal: 3:12-17 draws on more of the rest of the letter than any other unit (its internal echoes reach the opening
prayer, the confession of 1:15-20, Paul's ministry, the polemic and the putting-off); it is the letter's last word to the
whole church before the household code. Proposed: moderate; synthetic. Test: measure, for each unit, how many of its
rarer lemmas recur elsewhere in the letter and in how many other units; normalise for unit length.

## C67b — (design side only) the "you → us" switch
Do not test the pronouns themselves (another auditor does). Only: across Colossians, list every switch between 2nd and
1st person plural in statements about salvation, and say whether 1:12-13 and 2:13 stand out among them.

## C68 — The speech chain (λόγος)
Proposal: a chain on speech runs 3:8-9 (foul talk, lies) → 3:16 (the word of Christ) → 4:3 (a door for the word) → 4:6
(your word, seasoned with salt), with λόγος ('word') carrying it; δεῖ ('must') at 4:4 and 4:6 only in the letter.
Proposed: high on the words, moderate as design. Test the counts (λόγος in Colossians, δεῖ by form), and whether the
chain is more than the ordinary frequency of λόγος in a Pauline letter (baseline).

## C71 — The welded seams (whole-letter design)
Proposal: at each joint between the twelve units, the next unit picks up a rare word or phrase from the one before:
1:14→1:15 (relative chain, no full stop); 1:20→1:21-22 (καὶ ὑμᾶς; ἀποκαταλλάσσω 'reconcile' — NT only Eph 2:16, Col
1:20, 22); 1:23→1:25 ("of which I became a minister" repeated); 2:5→2:6-7 (walk, Lord, as you learned/were taught, the
faith); 2:8↔2:20, 22 (στοιχεῖα τοῦ κόσμου; δόγμα→δογματίζομαι); 2:20→3:1 (twin conditionals εἰ …); 3:2→3:5 (ἐπὶ τῆς
γῆς 'on earth'); 3:10→3:12 (ἐνδύω 'put on'); 3:17→3:23 (ὃ ἐὰν ποιῆτε 'whatever you do', NT only these two); 3:22-4:1→4:9
(the slave addressed, then named). Proposed: strong on each weld, MODERATE as a claim that the whole letter was built
this way, because no chance baseline has been run on "seam density". Run that baseline: compare rare-word pick-up
across the actual unit boundaries with pick-up across many random cut points in Colossians (same window sizes), and
with unit boundaries in another letter (e.g. Philippians or Ephesians, using sensible paragraph breaks you state).

## C72 — The dwelling chain (temple thread)
Proposal: the dwelling verbs carry a temple thread through the letter: κατοικέω ('dwell') at 1:19 ("all the fullness
was pleased to dwell") and 2:9 ("all the fullness of deity dwells bodily"), and ἐνοικέω ('dwell in') at 3:16 ("let the
word of Christ dwell in you richly"). Evidence offered: (a) in the Greek OT κατοικέω and κατοικητήριον are used of God's
dwelling in heaven and in the temple (1 Kgs 8:39, 43, 49; 2 Chr 6:18, 21, 30, 33, 39; Ps 67:17 LXX, "the mountain where
God was pleased (εὐδόκησεν) to dwell"); (b) ἐνοικέω's nearest Pauline use is 2 Cor 6:16, inside a temple text ("we are
the temple of the living God … I will dwell in them"), and οἰκέω at 1 Cor 3:16 is likewise a temple text. Proposed:
near the gate, not yet a "macro" thread; 2:9 adds little beyond 1:19. Test: (i) all NT uses of ἐνοικέω and their
contexts; (ii) LXX uses of ἐνοικέω (is it ever used of God dwelling?); (iii) how often κατοικέω in Swete has God as
subject, and in temple contexts, versus overall frequency; (iv) whether 3:16 has any temple vocabulary of its own.
Give a verdict on whether the three Colossian verses form one thread, and at what confidence.
