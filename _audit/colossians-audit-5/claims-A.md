## C61 — Psalm 106 LXX (107 Hebrew) behind Col 1:13-14
Proposal: Ps 106 LXX:2-20 — the redeemed (λυτρόω, 106:2), rescued (ῥύομαι, 106:6, 20), "sitting in darkness … brought
out of darkness" (106:10, 14) — stands behind "rescued us from the authority of darkness … redemption" (1:13-14).
The rescued are themselves in the dark, the shape of Col 1:13. Proposed rating: low–moderate. Named rival: Isa 42:7, 16
(LXX). Test the verbal volume, the rival, and whether either adds anything that ordinary Greek-Bible redemption idiom
does not.

## C62 — Deuteronomy 9:26-29 behind Col 1:12-14
Proposal: Deut 9:26-29 LXX has the people as God's μερίς ("portion", 9:26) and κλῆρος ("lot", 9:29), redeemed
(λυτρόω, 9:26) out of Egypt — all three ideas of Col 1:12-14 within four verses (the Greek translator renders Hebrew
נַחֲלָה twice by different words). Also echoed in Esther 4:17g-h (Rahlfs; check Swete's numbering). Proposed: moderate.
Named rivals: Deut 32:9; Josh 19:9; the many verses pairing μερίς and κλῆρος/κληρονομία. Test whether the
μερίς + κλῆρος + λυτρόω combination within a few verses is rare in Swete, and check the Hebrew.

## C63 — Luke 1:68-79 with Col 1:13-14
Proposal: Luke 1:68-79 and Col 1:13-14 are the only NT places where ῥύομαι ("rescue") and a λυτρ- word ("redeem /
redemption") stand within twelve verses, with forgiveness of sins and darkness/light nearby. Proposed: moderate, as
shared idiom (not literary dependence). Verify the "only", and test the cluster against chance.

## C69 — The Lukan affinities of Colossians
Proposal: Colossians shares conversion and mission idiom with Luke–Acts: Acts 26:18 at Col 1:12-14 ("six of the rarer
lemmas of 1:12-14 in one verse, against three for any other NT verse"); Luke 1:68-79 at 1:13-14 (C63); Luke 8:15 at
1:6-11 (the only NT verse with καρποφορέω 'bear fruit' and ὑπομονή 'endurance'). Proposed: moderate, as shared idiom
bearing on the letter's setting (Luke is named at Col 4:14); nothing about authorship. Test: (i) re-count the Acts 26:18
figure; (ii) is Colossians' contact with Luke–Acts unusual compared with the contact of OTHER Pauline passages of the
same size with Luke–Acts (a baseline)? (iii) does Paul's own idiom elsewhere supply the same words?

## C70 — The exodus redemption pattern behind 1:12-14
Proposal: Col 1:12-14 uses exodus redemption language as a pattern, with no single source: Exod 6:6-8 (ῥύσομαι,
λυτρώσομαι), Exod 14:30 (ἐρρύσατο), Deut 9:26-29 (C62), Ps 106 LXX (C61). Proposed: moderate as pattern. Test whether
the ῥύομαι + λυτρόω (+ inheritance) pattern is in fact concentrated in exodus contexts in Swete, or spread so widely
that "exodus" is not specific.

## C67 — The "you" → "us" shift at forgiveness (1:12-14 and 2:13)
Proposal: at both places where Colossians speaks of forgiveness, the pronoun moves from 2nd to 1st plural:
ὑμᾶς (1:12) → ἡμᾶς, ἔχομεν (1:13-14); ὑμᾶς … συνεζωοποίησεν ὑμᾶς (2:13a) → χαρισάμενος ἡμῖν (2:13b). At both places
some manuscripts level the pronoun (see the NA28 apparatus at 1:12 and 2:13). Proposed: high on the pronouns,
moderate–high as deliberate design. Test: (i) the apparatus at both places; (ii) a baseline — how often, in Paul's
letters, does a 2nd-plural address switch to 1st plural at a statement of salvation/forgiveness (e.g. Eph 2:1-5;
Gal 4:5-6; 1 Thess 5:9-10)? If the switch is Paul's habit, it is idiom, not design specific to Colossians.
