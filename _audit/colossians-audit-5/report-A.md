# Claim audit 5 — Colossians — Auditor A report

**Scope.** This report tests six claims about single contacts and wording at Colossians 1:12–14 and 2:13: C61, C62, C63, C69, C70 and C67. I worked only from the primary-text corpus:
- the SBLGNT with its lemma index, for the New Testament;
- Swete's LXX, for the Greek Old Testament, searched by accent-free stem, with every hit read for sense;
- the Westminster Leningrad Codex, for the Hebrew;
- the Logos exports (NA28 Colossians with apparatus, Rahlfs Psalms and Deuteronomy, NASB95 Colossians), to cite readings.

Every count names its edition. Where a claim says "within N verses", I counted consecutive verses in the same chapter unless I say otherwise. The Odes appendix in Swete reprints the Benedictus (Luke 1:68–79) as Ode 13, so it turns up in several Swete searches. I report it where it appears but leave it out of the Old Testament counts.

**A warning about the automatic contact scan.** The ranked scan of Swete chapters against Col 1:12–14 is a poor guide for this passage, for three reasons:
- it cannot see the aorist ἐρρύσατο ("he rescued") or the verb μεθίστημι ("transfer");
- it does not link ἀπολύτρωσις ("redemption") in Colossians with the verb λυτρόω ("redeem") in the LXX;
- it miscounts a lookalike word as ἄφεσις ("forgiveness") at Isa 59:11 and 59:14, where the word is actually ἀφέστηκεν ("has stood far off").

As a result it underrates exactly the chapters these claims propose. Across its six runs, the proposed chapters rank low and unstably: Ps 106 LXX 269–600; Deut 9 137–618; Isa 42 58–307; Exod 6 316–542; Exod 14 258–539. Only three chapters hold the top places in all six runs: Wisdom 18 (median rank 2), Daniel 4 (median 3) and Isaiah 59 (median 3), and Isaiah 59 is partly inflated by the miscount above. So every verdict below rests on direct searches, not on scan ranks.

## C61 — Psalm 106 LXX (107 Hebrew) behind Col 1:13–14

**Claim.** Ps 106:2–20 LXX stands behind "rescued us from the authority of darkness … redemption" (Col 1:13–14). Its people are redeemed and rescued, and they are themselves in the dark.

**What the psalm says.** It has the vocabulary, but in a different arrangement:
- λυτρόω ("redeem") comes at 106:2: οἱ λελυτρωμένοι … οὓς ἐλυτρώσατο ἐκ χειρὸς ἐχθροῦ ("the redeemed … whom he redeemed from the hand of the enemy").
- ῥύομαι ("rescue") comes at 106:6 and 106:20. Its objects are ἐκ τῶν ἀναγκῶν ("from their distresses") and ἐκ τῶν διαφθορῶν ("from their corruptions"), never darkness.
- The darkness lines are 106:10, καθημένους ἐν σκότει καὶ σκιᾷ θανάτου ("sitting in darkness and the shadow of death"), and 106:14, ἐξήγαγεν αὐτοὺς ἐκ σκότους ("he brought them out of darkness").

So the psalm never says "rescued from darkness". It uses ἐξάγω ("bring out") for leaving the darkness, and σῴζω ("save") at 106:13. Most of the rest of Col 1:12–14 is missing from it: ἐξουσία ("authority"), φῶς ("light"), ἄφεσις ("forgiveness"), μερίς ("portion"), κλῆρος ("lot"), βασιλεία ("kingdom") and μεθίστημι ("transfer").

The Hebrew agrees with the Greek:
- 107:2 uses the "redeem" root (gā'al).
- At 107:6 and 107:20, the "deliver" root (nāṣal) and the "let escape" root (mālaṭ) are the words rendered by ῥύομαι ("rescue").
- 107:10 has יֹשְׁבֵי חֹשֶׁךְ ("sitters in darkness") and 107:14 has יוֹצִיאֵם מֵחֹשֶׁךְ ("he brings them out from darkness").

Rahlfs agrees with Swete in every word that matters here. The only difference is that Rahlfs reads καὶ σκιᾶς θανάτου ("and the shadow of death") at 106:14, without a second ἐκ ("from").

**Counts (Swete).**

| Search | Result |
|---|---|
| ῥύομαι ("rescue") + the λυτρ- family ("redeem") within 12 verses | 18 Old Testament clusters (plus the Benedictus in the Odes) |
| … adding the σκότος family ("darkness") | Ps 68 LXX and Ps 106 LXX only (plus the Benedictus) |
| Ps 68:15–24 | the darkness falls on the enemies' eyes (σκοτισθήτωσαν, "let them be darkened", 68:24), not on the rescued |
| ἐκ/ἀπὸ σκότους ("out of darkness") with a verb of deliverance | Ps 106:14 only (the hits at Job 12:22, 15:22 and 37:15 are not about deliverance) |
| "sitting" + "darkness" + "shadow of death" together | Ps 106:10 only; its exact Greek wording comes back in Luke 1:79 |

**Controls.** The darkness search returns Ps 106:14, and the rescue search returns Exod 14:30 (ἐρρύσατο, "he rescued").

So Ps 106 is indeed the only Old Testament place in Swete where rescue, redemption and the redeemed being in darkness come together. That supports the claimed "shape". Two findings weaken it as a source for Colossians.

**1. The rival has the same shape.**
- Isa 42:7 LXX has καθημένους ἐν σκότει ("sitting in darkness"). Its Hebrew is the same phrase as Ps 107:10, יֹשְׁבֵי חֹשֶׁךְ ("sitters in darkness").
- Isa 42:16 adds what Ps 106 lacks: ποιήσω αὐτοῖς τὸ σκότος εἰς φῶς ("I will make the darkness into light for them"). That is the light/darkness contrast of Col 1:12–13.
- But Isaiah 42 has neither ῥύομαι ("rescue") nor λυτρόω ("redeem"). The nearest are λυτρόω at 43:1, 43:14 and 44:22–24, and ῥύομαι at 44:6.

Each text supplies half of the Colossian picture, and neither supplies all of it.

**2. The psalm's distinctive phrases go to Luke, not to Colossians.**
- Luke 1:79, τοῖς ἐν σκότει καὶ σκιᾷ θανάτου καθημένοις ("those sitting in darkness and the shadow of death"), is Ps 106:10 exactly.
- Luke 1:74, ἐκ χειρὸς ἐχθρῶν ῥυσθέντας ("rescued from the hand of enemies"), matches ἐκ χειρὸς ἐχθροῦ ("from the hand of the enemy") at Ps 106:2 and 105:10.
- Luke 1:79's εἰς ὁδόν ("into a way") matches Ps 106:7.

Colossians has none of the psalm's signature phrases: no "sitting", no "shadow of death", no "hand of the enemy". What it shares with Ps 106 is only what it also shares with the Benedictus.

**Does it add anything beyond ordinary redemption idiom?** Very little that can be shown. Ps 106 is a source of the "redeemed from darkness" idiom, and the Benedictus plainly uses it. Colossians can be accounted for by that idiom without the psalm.

| Criterion | Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Part of the common Greek psalter |
| Volume | Weak | Three shared lemmas (rescue, redeem, darkness) in different syntax; no shared phrase |
| Recurrence | Weak | No other Ps 106 contact in Colossians; no Ps 106 phrase ("shadow of death", "hand of the enemy") anywhere in Paul |
| Thematic coherence | Possible | The redeemed in darkness fits Col 1:13, but Isa 42:7, 16 has the same shape |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Plausible as idiom passed on (compare the Benedictus), not as a text Paul consciously draws on |
| Satisfaction | Weak | Does not explain ἐξουσία ("authority"), μεθίστημι ("transfer") or the light |

**Verdict:** Needs reframing. **Confidence:** low.

**Suggested change:** present Ps 106 LXX (with Ps 105:10) as a source of the early Jewish–Christian "redeemed from darkness" idiom, seen most clearly in Luke 1:74–79, and say that Col 1:13–14 shares that idiom. Do not present the psalm as a text behind Colossians. Treat Isa 42:7, 16 as an equal partner, not a weaker rival.

## C62 — Deuteronomy 9:26–29 behind Col 1:12–14

**Claim.** Deut 9:26–29 LXX calls the people God's μερίς ("portion", 9:26) and κλῆρος ("lot", 9:29), redeemed (λυτρόω, "redeem", 9:26) out of Egypt. That puts all three ideas of Col 1:12–14 within four verses, and Esther 4:17 echoes it.

**The text is as claimed.**
- Deut 9:26 (Swete) reads τὴν μερίδα σου ἣν ἐλυτρώσω ("your portion which you redeemed"). Rahlfs adds ἐν τῇ ἰσχύι σου τῇ μεγάλῃ ("by your great strength") after ἐλυτρώσω ("you redeemed").
- Deut 9:29 reads λαός σου καὶ κλῆρός σου ("your people and your lot").
- In the Hebrew (WLC), both 9:26 and 9:29 have נַחֲלָה ("inheritance"): וְנַחֲלָתְךָ and וְנַחֲלָתֶךָ. So one Hebrew word is rendered once by μερίς ("portion") and once by κλῆρος ("lot"), as the claim says.
- The verb at 9:26 is פָּדִיתָ ("you ransomed", the pādāh root), not the gā'al root.

**Counts (Swete).** μερίς ("portion") and κλῆρος ("lot") were counted as exact noun forms only; the λυτρ- family ("redeem/ransom") in all its forms.

| Search | Result |
|---|---|
| μερίς ("portion") + κλῆρος ("lot") in the same verse | 11 verses |
| μερίς + κλῆρος within four verses | 20 clusters |
| μερίς + κλῆρος + λυτρ- ("redeem") within four verses | Deut 9:26–29 and Esther 4:17 (Addition C) only |
| … within six verses | adds Num 18:15–21, a different sense: λύτρα is the ransom price of the firstborn, and the portion/lot is the Levites' lack of land |
| μερίς + any κληρ- word ("lot/inheritance") + λυτρ- within four verses | the same three places only |

**Controls.** The portion search returns Deut 9:26, and the lot search returns Deut 9:29.

The combination really is rare. In the sense of a redeemed people who are God's portion and lot, it occurs in only two places, and the second is plainly copied from the first. Swete prints Addition C inside Esther 4:17 as bracketed sub-verses:
- sub-verse 8: ἀπολέσαι τὴν ἐξ ἀρχῆς κληρονομίαν σου ("to destroy your inheritance from of old");
- sub-verse 9: μὴ ὑπερίδῃς τὴν μερίδα σου ἣν σεαυτῷ ἐλυτρώσω ἐκ γῆς Αἰγύπτου ("do not overlook your portion, which you redeemed for yourself out of the land of Egypt");
- sub-verse 10: ἱλάσθητι τῷ κλήρῳ σου ("be gracious to your lot").

The same Addition also has ῥῦσαι ("rescue") at sub-verse 25 and at 4:8, and ἐξουσία ("authority") at sub-verse 2. That makes Esther C the densest single Greek Old Testament match to Col 1:12–14: portion, lot, redeem, rescue and authority, though without darkness or light.

**Esther numbering.** Swete does not use Rahlfs's lettered verses: in Swete the portion/redeem line is 4:17 sub-verse 9 and the lot line is sub-verse 10. There is no Rahlfs Esther in the corpus, so I could not check the claimed "4:17g–h". If Rahlfs's letters run a = 1 and skip j, these lines would be 4:17i and 4:17k, not g–h. The main session should check this before citing.

**Two qualifications.**

1. **The direction is reversed.** In Deut 9, as in Deut 32:9 (μερὶς Κυρίου λαὸς αὐτοῦ, "the LORD's portion is his people"), Israel is God's portion. In Col 1:12 believers receive a portion: εἰς τὴν μερίδα τοῦ κλήρου τῶν ἁγίων ("for the portion of the lot of the saints"). That receiving sense belongs to the Levitical and tribal formula μερὶς καὶ κλῆρος ("portion and lot"): Deut 10:9, 12:12, 14:27–29, 18:1; Josh 19:9. In the New Testament it appears at Acts 8:21: οὐκ ἔστιν σοι μερὶς οὐδὲ κλῆρος ("you have neither portion nor lot"). Acts 8:21 and Col 1:12 are the only two New Testament verses with both words (SBLGNT; the control search returns Acts 8:21). So the wording of Col 1:12 is better explained by this "share received" formula than by Deut 9.

2. **Paul's own idiom supplies the redemption half.** Col 1:14, ἐν ᾧ ἔχομεν τὴν ἀπολύτρωσιν … τὴν ἄφεσιν ("in whom we have the redemption … the forgiveness"), matches Eph 1:7 word for word as far as it goes.

| Criterion | Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Deuteronomy LXX is central; Esther C shows the passage being reused in prayer |
| Volume | Possible | Three ideas in four verses, but Colossians has the noun ἀπολύτρωσις ("redemption"), not the verb, and a genitive pairing rather than two parallel titles |
| Recurrence | Weak | No other contact with Deut 9 found in Colossians |
| Thematic coherence | Possible | Exodus redemption plus inheritance fits, but the portion is God's in Deuteronomy and the believers' in Colossians |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Esther C shows the combination was alive in Greek-speaking Jewish prayer |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Explains the three ideas together; the "share received" formula and Paul's own idiom explain the wording better |

**Verdict:** Confirmed with nuance. The cluster's rarity is real, but the claim should not suggest a direct source. **Confidence:** low–moderate (lowered from the proposed moderate).

**Suggested change:** call Deut 9:26–29, as reused in Esther C, "the one Greek Old Testament prayer-cluster of portion + lot + redeemed". State that the direction is reversed, and name the μερὶς καὶ κλῆρος ("portion and lot") formula (Deut 10:9; Acts 8:21) as the nearer explanation of Col 1:12's wording. Give the Esther reference in Swete's numbering, or verify the Rahlfs one.

## C63 — Luke 1:68–79 with Col 1:13–14

**Claim.** Luke 1:68–79 and Col 1:13–14 are the only New Testament places where ῥύομαι ("rescue") and a λυτρ- word ("redeem/redemption") stand within twelve verses, with forgiveness of sins and darkness or light nearby. The claim offers this as shared idiom.

**Counts (SBLGNT, by lemma).**
- ῥύομαι ("rescue"): 15 verses.
- The λυτρ- family: 20 verses, made up of λύτρον ("ransom") 2, λύτρωσις ("redemption") 3, ἀπολύτρωσις ("redemption") 10, λυτρόομαι ("redeem") 3, λυτρωτής ("redeemer") 1 and ἀντίλυτρον ("ransom") 1.
- ἄφεσις ("forgiveness"): 16 verses.
- The σκότος family ("darkness"): 44 verses.

| Search | Result |
|---|---|
| ῥύομαι ("rescue") + λυτρ- ("redeem") within 12 verses | Luke 1:68/74 and Col 1:13/14 only; still only these two at 20 verses |
| … adding ἄφεσις ("forgiveness") | the same two (Luke 1:77; Col 1:14) |
| … adding σκότος ("darkness") | the same two (Luke 1:79; Col 1:13) |
| nearest miss | Rom 7:24 (ῥύσεται, "will rescue") to Rom 8:23 (ἀπολύτρωσις, "redemption"), 24 verses apart |

**Controls.** The rescue search returns Luke 1:74 and Col 1:13; the redeem search returns Luke 1:68 and Col 1:14. **The "only" is confirmed.**

**Chance baseline.** I scattered each word family at random across the New Testament's verses, keeping its real number of verses, 2,000 times, and counted how often each cluster appeared by accident.

| Cluster (within 12 verses) | Observed | Average by chance | Share of random runs doing as well |
|---|---|---|---|
| rescue + redeem | 2 | 0.97 | 26% |
| rescue + redeem + forgiveness | 2 | 0.05 | 0.1% |
| rescue + redeem + darkness | 2 | 0.13 | 0.75% |
| all four | 2 | 0.004 | under 0.05% |

The pair on its own is nothing unusual: two co-locations is about what chance gives. The four-term cluster is far beyond chance. Because random scattering ignores the fact that salvation words naturally cluster, these figures overstate the significance. But the four-term cluster is still rare in the real text, since only these two examples exist.

**Character of the contact.** The two passages share words and themes, not phrases.
- Luke has λύτρωσις ("redemption"), ἐκ χειρὸς ἐχθρῶν ῥυσθέντας ("rescued from the hand of enemies"), ἐν ἀφέσει ἁμαρτιῶν ("in forgiveness of sins") and ἐν σκότει ("in darkness").
- Colossians has ἐρρύσατο ἡμᾶς ἐκ τῆς ἐξουσίας τοῦ σκότους ("he rescued us from the authority of darkness") and τὴν ἄφεσιν τῶν ἁμαρτιῶν ("the forgiveness of sins").

Luke's wording comes from Ps 105–106 LXX (see C61); Colossians' wording does not. That points to shared idiom with a common Old Testament background, not to literary dependence.

| Criterion | Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Possible | Shared idiom needs no written text; direct use of Luke cannot be shown |
| Volume | Possible | Four shared lemmas (rescue, redeem, forgiveness of sins, darkness), no shared phrase |
| Recurrence | Possible | Col 1:12–14 also matches Acts 26:18 and Luke 22:53 (see C69) |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | Both give thanks for a past act of rescue and redemption into a new realm |
| Historical plausibility | Strong | As shared liturgical and teaching idiom |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Places Col 1:13–14 in an idiom field without explaining any single Colossian phrase |

**Verdict:** Confirmed, as shared idiom. **Confidence:** moderate.

**Suggested change:** add that "rescue" + "redeem" alone is within chance, and that the cluster becomes rare only when forgiveness and darkness are added. Keep the "not literary dependence" wording.

## C69 — The Lukan affinities of Colossians

**Claim.** Colossians shares conversion and mission idiom with Luke–Acts in three places: Acts 26:18 at 1:12–14 ("six of the rarer lemmas … against three for any other verse"), Luke 1:68–79 at 1:13–14, and Luke 8:15 at 1:6–11.

### (i) Re-count of Acts 26:18

Acts 26:18 shares six lemmas with Col 1:12–14: σκότος ("darkness"), φῶς ("light"), ἐξουσία ("authority"), ἄφεσις ("forgiveness"), ἁμαρτία ("sin") and κλῆρος ("lot"). It also has ἁγιάζω ("sanctify"), which is related to ἅγιος ("holy/saint") but is a different lemma.

Who comes second depends on how "rarer" is defined (SBLGNT, by the number of New Testament verses that contain the lemma):

| "Rarer" means a lemma found in … | Lemmas of 1:12–14 that qualify | Acts 26:18 shares | Best other verse(s) share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 verses or fewer | 8 | 3 | 2 (Eph 1:7; Acts 8:21) |
| 100 or fewer | 11 | 5 | 2 |
| 150 or fewer | 13 | 6 | 2 |
| 400 or fewer | 17 | 6 | 3 (Matt 8:12, 9:6, 28:19; Mark 2:10, 8:38 and others) |

"Six against three" holds when "rarer" is drawn widely (17 lemmas); any narrower definition widens the gap. **Confirmed.**

Two further exclusive contacts strengthen the Lukan side:
- ἐξουσία ("authority") and σκότος ("darkness") stand in the same verse only at Luke 22:53, Acts 26:18, Eph 6:12 and Col 1:13. The phrase ἡ ἐξουσία τοῦ σκότους ("the authority of darkness") occurs only at Luke 22:53 and Col 1:13. In Eph 6:12 the two words are apart: τὰς ἐξουσίας … τοὺς κοσμοκράτορας τοῦ σκότους ("the authorities … the world-rulers of this darkness").
- μερίς ("portion") and κλῆρος ("lot") stand in the same verse only at Acts 8:21 and Col 1:12.

Where the words cluster is also telling. Ten of the 16 New Testament verses with ἄφεσις ("forgiveness") are in Luke–Acts; in Paul it appears only at Eph 1:7 and Col 1:14. Three of the 5 verses with μεθίστημι ("transfer") are in Luke–Acts.

### (ii) Baseline against other Pauline passages

**Local measure.** I took every three-verse window within a chapter of the thirteen Pauline letters (1,855 windows). For each, I found the single Luke–Acts verse that shares the most of the window's rarer lemmas (those found in 150 New Testament verses or fewer).

| Best match with one Luke–Acts verse | Pauline windows |
|---|---|
| 1 lemma | 171 |
| 2 | 1,361 |
| 3 | 300 |
| 4 | 18 |
| 6 | 2 |
| 7 | 3 |

Only five windows reach six or more. Four of them overlap at 1 Cor 11:22–25, which matches the Last Supper words of Luke 22:19–20, a known shared tradition. The fifth is Col 1:12–14 with Acts 26:18. So only two distinct passages in the whole Pauline corpus (about 0.3% of windows) match a Luke–Acts verse this closely, and the other one is known shared tradition. On this measure the local contact is a real outlier.

**Letter-wide measure.** For each letter, I took every lemma that occurs elsewhere in the New Testament in 1 to 50 verses, and asked what share of those other occurrences fall in Luke–Acts. (Luke–Acts makes up 27.1% of New Testament verses.)

| Letter | Average Lukan share | Lemmas found elsewhere only in Luke–Acts |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Cor | 0.254 | 25 |
| 2 Tim | 0.249 | 13 |
| Gal | 0.247 | 13 |
| 1 Tim | 0.230 | 16 |
| Rom | 0.219 | 26 |
| 2 Thess | 0.219 | 4 |
| 2 Cor | 0.218 | 16 |
| Phlm | 0.215 | 2 |
| 1 Thess | 0.213 | 4 |
| Phil | 0.210 | 3 |
| Eph | 0.203 (0.219 leaving Colossians out) | 9 (11) |
| Titus | 0.197 | 6 |
| **Col** | **0.166 (0.186 leaving Ephesians out)** | **1 (3)** |

Across the whole letter, Colossians has the least Lukan vocabulary of the thirteen Pauline letters, even when its overlap with Ephesians is set aside. With Ephesians left out, its three lemmas found elsewhere only in Luke–Acts are δόγμα ("decree"), Ἰοῦστος ("Justus") and σύνδεσμος ("bond"). So "the Lukan affinities of Colossians" is true of 1:12–14 and false of the letter as a whole.

### (iii) Paul's own idiom

Paul's letters supply most of the words of 1:12–14 without Luke:
- ἀπολύτρωσις … ἄφεσις ("redemption … forgiveness"): Eph 1:7, word for word in the clause ἐν ᾧ ἔχομεν ("in whom we have");
- κληρονομία … ἐν τοῖς ἁγίοις ("inheritance … among the saints"): Eph 1:18;
- the contrast of darkness and light: Eph 5:8, 1 Thess 5:5, Rom 13:12;
- ῥύομαι ("rescue") as a present saving act: 1 Thess 1:10;
- ἱκανόω ("make sufficient"): only 2 Cor 3:6 besides Col 1:12.

Two things Paul does not supply elsewhere: the phrase ἡ ἐξουσία τοῦ σκότους ("the authority of darkness") and the pairing μερὶς … κλῆρος ("portion … lot"). Those are found only in Luke–Acts.

The direction of influence cannot be settled from the text. Acts 26:18 is placed on Christ's lips as Paul's commission, so the contact may be Luke reporting Paul's own conversion vocabulary as much as Paul echoing Luke.

### The Luke 8:15 leg

Luke 8:15 is indeed the only New Testament verse with both καρποφορέω ("bear fruit") and ὑπομονή ("endurance"). But this leg is weak:
- In Colossians the two words are in different verses: καρποφορέω at 1:6 and 1:10, ὑπομονή at 1:11.
- ὑπομονή ("endurance") is common in Paul: 16 of its 31 New Testament verses are in the Pauline letters.
- The Colossian pairing καρποφορούμενον καὶ αὐξανόμενον ("bearing fruit and growing", 1:6, 1:10) is closer to Mark 4:8, καρπὸν … αὐξανόμενα ("fruit … growing").
- καρποφορέω ("bear fruit") together with ἀκούω ("hear") and λόγος ("word") appears in all three Synoptic versions of the sower's explanation (Matt 13:23, Mark 4:20, Luke 8:15).

So this is shared sower-parable tradition, not a specifically Lukan contact.

| Criterion | Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Possible | Idiom shared in mission circles; literary access cannot be tested |
| Volume | Strong (1:12–14); Weak (1:6–11) | A six-lemma match plus two phrases found only in Luke–Acts, against a sower match common to all three Synoptics |
| Recurrence | Possible | Acts 26:18, Luke 22:53, Acts 8:21 and Luke 1:68–79 all converge on 1:12–14, but nowhere else in the letter |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | Conversion as transfer from darkness and Satan's authority into light, forgiveness and a lot among the sanctified |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Luke is named at Col 4:14; Acts 26:18 is presented as Paul's own commission |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Explains 1:12–14 well and the rest of the letter not at all (lowest letter-wide score of 13) |

**Verdict:** Confirmed with nuance: strong locally, wrong as a description of the letter. **Confidence:** moderate–high for the contact of 1:12–14 with Acts 26:18 and Luke 22:53; low for "Lukan affinities" as a description of the letter.

**Suggested change:** retitle the claim "the Lukan conversion idiom of Col 1:12–14". Add Luke 22:53 (ἡ ἐξουσία τοῦ σκότους, "the authority of darkness") and Acts 8:21 (μερὶς … κλῆρος, "portion … lot"). Drop Luke 8:15, or downgrade it to shared sower tradition. State the letter-wide result: Colossians' vocabulary is the least Lukan of the thirteen letters.

## C70 — The exodus redemption pattern behind 1:12–14

**Claim.** Col 1:12–14 uses exodus redemption language as a pattern, with no single source: Exod 6:6–8, Exod 14:30, Deut 9:26–29 and Ps 106 LXX.

**Concentration test (Swete).** I defined an "exodus context" mechanically: a verse within three verses (in the same chapter) of a word for Egypt, Pharaoh or the Red Sea. Across the whole of Swete, 9.7% of verses fall in such a context.

| Word family | Verses | Share in an exodus context | Compared with the 9.7% baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| ῥύομαι ("rescue") | 184 | 9.2% (17) | no concentration |
| λυτρ- ("redeem/ransom") | 131 | 19.8% (26) | about twice the baseline |
| μερίς ("portion") | 135 | 5.9% | below baseline |
| κλῆρος ("lot") | 122 | 9.0% | at baseline |

So ῥύομαι ("rescue") is not an exodus word in Swete, while λυτρόω ("redeem") leans towards exodus.

**The pair and the triad.** ῥύομαι ("rescue") + λυτρ- ("redeem") within 12 verses gives 18 Old Testament clusters (control: Exod 6:6 is found).
- **5 of the 18 are clearly exodus-related:** Exod 6:6; Esther C (through Deut 9); Isa 51:10–11 (the sea, and "the redeemed" passing over); Isa 52:3–9 (Egypt at 52:4); Isa 63:4–16 (Moses and the sea, 63:11–13).
- **The other 13 are not:** psalms of personal or communal distress (Ps 7, 24, 30, 33, 58, 68, 71, 106 and 143 LXX), Proverbs 6 and 13 (λύτρα, "ransom price"), Hos 13:14 and Dan 3:88.

Adding the inheritance element narrows the field sharply. ῥύομαι ("rescue") + λυτρ- ("redeem") + κλῆρος ("lot") within three, four or six verses occurs only at **Exod 6:6–8 and Esther C**. Within 12 verses, Ps 30 LXX is added, where κλῆροι ("lots", 30:16) means "my times".

**Exod 6:6–8** is the one passage that sets out the whole movement in order:
- ἐξάξω ὑμᾶς ἀπὸ τῆς δυναστείας ("I will bring you out from the domination", 6:6);
- ῥύσομαι ὑμᾶς ἐκ τῆς δουλίας ("I will rescue you from the slavery");
- λυτρώσομαι ("I will redeem");
- λήμψομαι ἐμαυτῷ ὑμᾶς λαόν ("I will take you as a people for myself", 6:7);
- εἰς τὴν γῆν … δώσω ὑμῖν αὐτὴν ἐν κλήρῳ ("into the land … I will give it to you as a lot", 6:8).

Set beside Col 1:13, ἐρρύσατο … ἐκ τῆς ἐξουσίας … καὶ μετέστησεν εἰς ("he rescued … from the authority … and transferred into"), this is the closest match in structure anywhere in the Greek Old Testament: rescue from a power, movement into a new realm, and an allotted share. In the Hebrew of Exod 6:6–8, ῥύομαι ("rescue") renders the "deliver" root (nāṣal), λυτρόω ("redeem") renders the "redeem" root (gā'al), and κλῆρος ("lot", 6:8) renders מוֹרָשָׁה ("possession").

**How the four named texts fare.**
- **Exod 6:6–8:** strong, as shown above.
- **Exod 14:30:** only ἐρρύσατο ("he rescued"), rendering the Hebrew "save" root (yāšaʿ). There is no redemption or inheritance word nearby. It shows only that ἐρρύσατο ("he rescued") can be used of the exodus.
- **Deut 9:26–29:** redeem plus portion and lot in an explicitly exodus setting (see C62), but without ῥύομαι ("rescue").
- **Ps 106 LXX:** not an exodus context at all. There is no word for Egypt, Pharaoh or the sea, and the redeemed are "gathered from the lands" (106:3). It belongs with the return-from-exile and general thanksgiving texts, and should be dropped from an exodus list.

**New support found in passing: Wisdom 18.** This is Col 1:12–14's most stable partner across all six scan runs (median rank 2). It retells the exodus plague of darkness:
- τοῖς δὲ ὁσίοις σου μέγιστον ἦν φῶς ("but for your holy ones there was very great light", 18:1);
- εὐχαριστοῦσιν ("they give thanks", 18:2);
- the oppressors were ἄξιοι … στερηθῆναι φωτὸς καὶ φυλακισθῆναι σκότει ("deserving … to be deprived of light and imprisoned in darkness", 18:4);
- the ἁγίους ("holy ones") are to share (μεταλήμψεσθαι, "to partake") alike (18:9).

Compare Exod 10:23, πᾶσι δὲ τοῖς υἱοῖς Ἰσραὴλ φῶς ἦν ("but all the sons of Israel had light"). This supplies the light/darkness and "holy ones" half of Col 1:12–13 within an exodus frame, which is exactly what the four named texts lack.

| Criterion | Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | All the texts are central or widely read |
| Volume | Possible | Exod 6:6–8 gives rescue + redeem + lot, but no phrase shared with Colossians |
| Recurrence | Possible | Several exodus texts each supply a part; Ps 106 and Exod 14:30 add little |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | Rescue from a power, transfer into a realm, an allotted share: the exodus sequence |
| Historical plausibility | Strong | The exodus as the pattern of redemption is common in Jewish and early Christian thought |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Explains the sequence of 1:12–13; the light/darkness element needs the darkness-plague tradition (Exod 10:23; Wisdom 18) or Isa 42 |

**Verdict:** Confirmed with nuance. **Confidence:** moderate as a pattern. The pattern is specific only when it is defined by the triad of rescue + redeem + allotted lot, which is concentrated in Exod 6:6–8 (with Deut 9 and Esther C for the portion element). The rescue + redeem pair on its own is mostly not exodus (13 of 18 clusters).

**Suggested change:** name Exod 6:6–8 as the anchor of the pattern. Drop Ps 106 from the exodus list, or move it to the "redemption idiom" of C61 and C63. Say plainly that ῥύομαι ("rescue") by itself is not an exodus marker. Consider adding the darkness-plague tradition (Exod 10:23; Wisdom 17–18) as the source of the light/darkness element.

## C67 — The "you" → "us" shift at forgiveness (1:12–14 and 2:13)

**Claim.** At both places where Colossians speaks of forgiveness, the pronoun moves from 2nd plural to 1st plural, and some manuscripts level the pronoun at both. The claim is rated high on the pronouns and moderate–high as deliberate design.

### (i) The text and the apparatus (NA28)

**1:12–14.** NA28 and SBLGNT print ὑμᾶς ("you", 1:12), then ἡμᾶς ("us", 1:13) and ἔχομεν ("we have", 1:14).
- The apparatus at 1:12 lists ἡμᾶς ("us") in A C D F G K L P Ψ 075 33 81 630 1241ˢ 1505 2464 𝔪 lat sy bo.
- The text ὑμᾶς ("you") is supported by א B 104 365 629 1175 1739 1881 vgᵐˢˢ syʰᵐᵍ sa; Ambst.

So the majority tradition levels the pronoun to "us". **The NASB95 follows the levelled reading ("qualified us")**, which means a reader of the study text alone will not see the shift. At 1:14, B and the Coptic read ἔσχομεν ("we had"); that is a change of tense, not of person.

**2:13.** NA28 prints ὑμᾶς … συνεζωοποίησεν ὑμᾶς ("you … he made you alive"), then χαρισάμενος ἡμῖν ("having forgiven us"). The first person continues in 2:14 with καθ' ἡμῶν ("against us") and ἡμῖν ("to us"). The scribes levelled in both directions:
- the second ὑμᾶς ("you") becomes ἡμᾶς ("us") in 𝔓⁴⁶ B 33 323 m vgᵐˢ; Or¹⁷³⁹ᵐᵍ Ambr;
- it is omitted in א² D F G P Ψ 075 0208 0278 104 365 1175 1241ˢ 1505 2464 𝔪 lat; Tert;
- the text ὑμᾶς ("you") is supported by א* A C K L 6 81 326 630 1739 1881 vgᵐˢˢ;
- ἡμῖν ("us") becomes ὑμῖν ("you") in א² K* L P 6 323 326 f vg saᵐˢˢ; Tert.

**A third pronoun variant, not in the claim.** At 3:4, ἡ ζωὴ ὑμῶν ("your life"), the reading ἡμῶν ("our") is found in B D¹ H K L 0278 630 1175 1241ˢ 1505 1739 2464 𝔪 sy sa. So at a third salvation statement NA28 keeps the 2nd plural, and the scribes again move towards the 1st.

**The pronoun facts are confirmed, but "both places" is inaccurate.** Colossians has three statements of forgiveness (SBLGNT, by lemma: ἄφεσις "forgiveness" and χαρίζομαι "forgive"): 1:14, 2:13 and **3:13**, καθὼς καὶ ὁ κύριος ἐχαρίσατο ὑμῖν ("just as the Lord forgave you"). At 3:13 there is no shift; the 2nd plural is needed for the comparison οὕτως καὶ ὑμεῖς ("so also you"). The accurate statement is that the shift comes at both indicative, doctrinal statements of forgiveness, but not at the exhortation in 3:13.

### (ii) Baseline in Paul

**Method.** Across the thirteen Pauline letters (SBLGNT), I marked every 1st-plural and 2nd-plural pronoun and finite verb. A "switch" is a verse whose 1st plural comes after a 2nd plural, either earlier in the same verse or in the two verses before it, within the same chapter. I compared verses with salvation vocabulary against all verses. The salvation words counted were those for forgiveness, redemption, ransom, rescue, save and salvation, making alive (together), reconciling, buying out, forgiving, justifying and propitiation.

| Set of verses | Verses with a 2nd plural in context | Of these, switching to 1st plural |
|---|---|---|
| All Pauline verses | 1,106 | 250 (22.6%) |
| Verses with salvation vocabulary | 47 | 12 (25.5%) |

| Switch within a single verse | Verses | Switching |
|---|---|---|
| All Pauline verses | 2,029 | 118 (5.8%) |
| Verses with salvation vocabulary | 114 | 5 (4.4%) |

**There is no rise at salvation statements.** Paul moves between "you" and "us" at much the same rate everywhere. Several of the salvation verses that do switch are close parallels to Colossians:
- 1 Cor 1:30: ὑμεῖς ἐστε … ἡμῖν … ἀπολύτρωσις ("you are … for us … redemption");
- 1 Thess 1:9–10: ἐπεστρέψατε ("you turned") → τὸν ῥυόμενον ἡμᾶς ("the one who rescues us"), with the same verb as Col 1:13;
- Gal 1:3–4: ὑμῖν ("to you") → ὑπὲρ τῶν ἁμαρτιῶν ἡμῶν … ἐξέληται ἡμᾶς ("for our sins … that he might deliver us");
- Eph 1:13–14: ὑμεῖς ("you") → κληρονομίας ἡμῶν εἰς ἀπολύτρωσιν ("our inheritance, for redemption");
- Rom 13:11 and Gal 4:6.

The closest of all is **Eph 2:1–5**, which has the same wording as Col 2:13:
- 2:1, ὑμᾶς ὄντας νεκροὺς τοῖς παραπτώμασιν ("you being dead in trespasses");
- 2:3, ἡμεῖς πάντες ("we all");
- 2:5, ὄντας ἡμᾶς νεκροὺς … συνεζωοποίησεν ("us being dead … he made alive together");
- then back to "you" with χάριτί ἐστε σεσῳσμένοι ("by grace you have been saved", 2:5).

**Limits of the method.** It cannot tell an inclusive "we" (writer and readers) from an apostolic "we" (Paul and his co-workers), so the hits at 2 Cor 1:10, 1 Thess 2:16 and 2 Thess 3:2 are probably apostolic. It also misses some real switches: Eph 2:5 is not counted, because the switch has already happened at 2:3. The rates are therefore rough, but good enough to show that the move is ordinary Pauline practice.

### A finding inside Colossians that partly supports "design"

I sorted every 1st-plural verse in Colossians (SBLGNT). Apart from the greeting formula at 1:2 ("God our Father"), every one is an apostolic "we", meaning Paul and his co-workers: 1:3, 1:7–9, 1:28, 4:3 and 4:8. The only exceptions are **1:13–14 and 2:13–14**. In other words, the only places where the letter's "we" clearly includes the readers are its two indicative statements of forgiveness. That is a real feature of Colossians, even though the move itself is Paul's habit.

| Criterion | Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Not applicable | An internal feature, not a source |
| Volume | Strong | The shift is in the critical text at both places, and the variants show that scribes noticed it |
| Recurrence | Possible | Twice in Colossians, at both doctrinal forgiveness statements; absent at 3:13 |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | A confessional "us" at the statement of the saving act |
| Historical plausibility | Strong | As Pauline habit: 1 Cor 1:30; 1 Thess 1:9–10; Gal 1:3–4; Eph 1:13–14; Eph 2:1–5 |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Explains the variants; "deliberate design specific to Colossians" goes beyond the evidence |

**Verdict:** Needs reframing. **Confidence:** high on the pronouns and the apparatus; low–moderate on design specific to Colossians (lowered from moderate–high).

**Suggested change:** say "at both indicative statements of forgiveness (1:14; 2:13), not at the exhortation of 3:13". Describe the shift as Paul's habit when stating the saving act, citing 1 Thess 1:9–10 and Eph 2:1–5. Note that in Colossians these are the only places where the "we" includes the readers. Warn that the NASB95 follows the levelled "us" at 1:12.

## New candidates met in passing (record only)

- **Esther Addition C (Swete 4:17, sub-verses 2, 8–10 and 25):** brings together ἐξουσία ("authority"), κληρονομία ("inheritance"), μερίς ("portion") with ἐλυτρώσω ("you redeemed"), κλῆρος ("lot") and ῥῦσαι ("rescue") in one prayer. It is the densest Greek Old Testament match to Col 1:12–14, though it lacks darkness and light. Suggested rating: low–moderate, as evidence of the prayer idiom rather than as a source.
- **Wisdom 17–18 with Exod 10:23:** light for the "holy ones", darkness as imprisonment for the oppressors, giving thanks and sharing. It is the scan's most stable partner (median rank 2 across all six runs). Suggested rating: low–moderate; worth a dig for Col 1:12–13.
- **Luke 22:53:** ἡ ἐξουσία τοῦ σκότους ("the authority of darkness") is the only other New Testament instance of the Col 1:13 phrase. Suggested rating: moderate, as shared idiom; it belongs inside C69.
- **Acts 8:21:** μερὶς οὐδὲ κλῆρος ("neither portion nor lot") is the only other New Testament verse with both words of Col 1:12. Suggested rating: moderate, as idiom, with the Deut 10:9 formula behind both.
- **Ps 24 LXX (25 Hebrew):** ῥῦσαί με ("rescue me", 24:20), λύτρωσαι ("redeem", 24:22), κληρονομήσει γῆν ("will inherit the land", 24:13) and ἄφες πάσας τὰς ἁμαρτίας μου ("forgive all my sins", 24:18). Rescue, redemption, inheritance and forgiveness come together in one psalm, though without darkness. Suggested rating: low.
- **Daniel 7:18, 22, 27 (LXX and Theodotion):** the kingdom and authority are given to the "holy ones". Daniel 4 also ranks high in the scan (median 3), through ἀπολύτρωσις ("redemption"), ἐξουσία ("authority") and βασιλεία ("kingdom"). Suggested rating: low–moderate, as background to "the kingdom … of the saints" in 1:12–13.

## What I could not test

- The Rahlfs lettering of Esther 4:17 ("g–h"), because there is no Rahlfs Esther in the corpus. Nor could I check Rahlfs for Exodus, Isaiah 42, Wisdom or Daniel: only Psalms and Deuteronomy have been exported.
- Inclusive versus apostolic "we" across Paul. My switch baseline cannot separate them, so its rates are approximate.
- Whether the Luke–Acts contacts run from Paul to Luke, from Luke to Paul, or from a shared third source. The text cannot decide this.
- The NA28 apparatus outside Colossians. The Pauline baseline uses the SBLGNT text without variants.
- Complete coverage in Swete. Stem searching can miss compound verbs with an internal augment and unusual spellings, so the Swete counts are minimums.
