# Claim audit 2 — Colossians — Auditor B (field and design claims)

**Scope.** Six claims, B1–B6. I read only the common brief, my claims file and the primary-text corpus. Every count names its edition: SBLGNT with its lemma index for the New Testament, Swete for the Greek Old Testament (a floor, accent-stripped, read for sense), WLC where noted. No Rahlfs text of Wisdom, Sirach, Tobit or the Maccabees is available, so every finding in those books rests on Swete alone. Anything from my own knowledge of scholarship is tagged [S] and carries no verdict on its own.

**How the ranks were produced.** The house ranking scan compares every content word of a Colossians passage (nouns, verbs and adjectives, by SBLGNT lemma, cognates merged by stem) with each of Swete's 1,259 chapters and ranks the chapters in six runs: whole chapters, eight-verse windows and sixteen-verse windows, each once with all shared words and once with only the rarer words. A chapter's summary figure is the median of its six ranks (the scan reports the fourth-lowest of the six). The scan takes a passage within one chapter only. To test cross-chapter spans (1:24–2:5, 1:26–2:3), leave-one-out variants and null passages, I rebuilt the same ranking in my own working. On 1:24–29 it gives identical ranks to the house scan in all six runs (Wisdom 7: 2, 5, 3, 6, 4, 10), so the two can be compared directly.

**What "null" means here.** A null passage is an arbitrary stretch of a Pauline letter of similar length, chosen at random. I ran the same six-run scan on it and recorded how often a given chapter, or any chapter of a given book, reaches the rank the claim relies on. A rank counts as evidence only if it is rare in the null.

## B1 — Wisdom 6:22–7:17 as a field partner of Colossians 1:26–2:3

### What I tested
I reproduced the stated verse-level exclusivities, the ranks for 1:24–29 and 2:1–5 (run separately, as the tool requires), and for 1:24–2:5 and 1:26–2:3 (on the rebuilt ranking). I checked every word the scan credited in Wisdom 6 and 7 for sense, ran leave-one-out without μυστήριον ("mystery"), without σοφία ("wisdom"), without both, and without each of the other strong words. I ran two null sets: 28 Pauline passages chosen because they talk about wisdom or knowledge, and 120 random six-verse and 120 random nine-verse Pauline passages. I compared Proverbs 2, Daniel 2 in both Greek texts (Old Greek and Theodotion), Sirach 24, and verse-level rivals.

### Results — the stated counts
- **Wis 6:22 is the only Swete verse joining μυστήρια ("mysteries"), a hiding word and σοφία ("wisdom").** This holds exactly. I searched the hiding root anywhere in the word, so that ἀποκρύψω ("I will hide"), κέκρυμμαι ("I have been hidden") and κρύφιος ("secret") were all caught. The verse also has γνῶσις ("knowledge"), ἀπ᾽ ἀρχῆς ("from the beginning") and εἰς τὸ ἐμφανές ("into the open"), as stated. Positive control: the same search finds the hiding word with μυστήριον at Wis 14:23, Tob 12:7 and 12:11 (both texts) and Dan 2:47. **But the hiding is negated.** The verse reads οὐκ ἀποκρύψω ὑμῖν μυστήρια ("I will not hide mysteries from you"): an author promising to be open. Colossians 1:26 says the opposite, that the mystery τὸ ἀποκεκρυμμένον ("which had been hidden") ἀπὸ τῶν αἰώνων ("from the ages") is νῦν δὲ ἐφανερώθη ("but now revealed"). The words are shared; the shape of hidden-then-revealed is not.
- **Wis 7:13–14** reads τὸν πλοῦτον αὐτῆς οὐκ ἀποκρύπτομαι ("I do not hide her wealth") and ἀνεκλιπὴς γὰρ θησαυρός ἐστιν ἀνθρώποις ("for she is an unfailing treasure for men"). Confirmed. Wis 7:13 is the only Swete verse joining πλοῦτος ("wealth") with a hiding word. Again the hiding is negated.
- **ἐνέργεια ("working") at Wis 7:17 and 7:26.** Confirmed. The word stem occurs in eleven Swete verses in all, four of them in Wisdom (7:17, 7:26, 13:4, 18:22) and five in 2–3 Maccabees. At 7:17 it is ἐνέργειαν στοιχείων ("the working of the elements"). At 7:26 it is the working of God, of which wisdom is the mirror, in the same verse as εἰκών ("image").
- **Ranks.** Against 1:24–29, Wisdom 7 has a median of 5 (2, 5, 3, 6, 4, 10). This holds. Against 1:24–2:5, its median is 10 (10, 13, 4, 18, 4, 10). This holds. Against 2:1–5 alone, Wisdom 7 has a median of 42. The claim's own span, 1:26–2:3, gives Wisdom 7 rank 1 in five of the six runs and 3 in the sixth (median 1). **Wisdom 6 is weak as a chapter:** median 31 against 1:24–29, 24 against 1:26–2:3, 85 against 1:24–2:5 and 136 against 2:1–5.

### Sense check of every credited word
- **Wisdom 6.** The genuine matches are μυστήρια ("mysteries", 6:22); σοφία ("wisdom", several verses); κοπιάσει ("will toil", 6:14: whoever rises early for wisdom "will not grow weary", a near-sense to the "labour" of Col 1:29); τελειότης ("perfection", 6:15, against τέλειον, "mature", in 1:28); and λόγοι ("words"). Generic: αἰών ("age"), ἔθνη ("nations"), θεός ("God"). One false friend: γενέσεως ("origin") is credited to γενεά ("generation", 1:26). The scan does **not** see the hiding word in 6:22: ἀποκρύψω escapes its stem, so Wisdom 6 is slightly under-credited.
- **Wisdom 7.** Genuine: ἀποκρύπτομαι ("I hide", negated, 7:13); πλοῦτος ("wealth", 7:8, 11, 13); θησαυρός ("treasure", 7:14); ἐνέργεια ("working", 7:17, 26); σοφία; γνῶσις ("knowledge", 7:17); δύναμις ("power"); δόξα ("glory", 7:25); πνεῦμα ἅγιον ("holy spirit", 7:22). Off-sense: λογισθήσεται ("will be reckoned", 7:9) credited to λόγος ("word"); γένεσις ("origin") credited to γενεά; σάρξ ("flesh", 7:2: the embryo), which is not the "my flesh" of Col 1:24. Not seen by the scan: κρυπτά ("hidden things", 7:21).

### Leave-one-out
Each line gives the median rank with the full count, then with one word (or two) removed.
- **Wisdom 7 against 1:24–29: 5.** Without μυστήριον: 5. Without σοφία: 7. Without both: 7. Without πλοῦτος: 10. **Without the hiding word (ἀποκρύπτω): 29.**
- **Wisdom 7 against 1:26–2:3: 1.** Without μυστήριον, without σοφία, or without both: still 1. Without πλοῦτος or θησαυρός: 3. **Without the hiding word: 14.**
- **Wisdom 6 against 1:26–2:3: 24.** Without μυστήριον: 62. Without both μυστήριον and σοφία: 88.

So Wisdom 7's standing does not depend on "mystery" or "wisdom" at all. It depends most on the hiding word, which in Wisdom is negated. Wisdom 6 depends on μυστήριον.

### Null baselines
- **"A Wisdom chapter in the top ten" is a near-default result.** In the 28 Pauline passages chosen for wisdom or knowledge vocabulary, a Wisdom chapter reached a median of ten or better in 12 (43 per cent). In 120 random six-verse Pauline passages it did so in 52 (43 per cent), whatever their subject. Wisdom has nineteen chapters of philosophical vocabulary, and one of them usually scores.
- **Wisdom 7 in particular is rare.** In 120 random six-verse passages it reached median 5 or better in 5 (4 per cent). Its quartiles were roughly 33, 219 and 452 for passages with wisdom vocabulary, and 112, 267 and 655 for the rest. In 120 random nine-verse passages it reached median 1 once (Rom 1:18–26), 5 or better three times, and 14 or better seven times (6 per cent).

So Wisdom 7's rank 1 against 1:26–2:3 is in the extreme tail. It is still in the top 6 per cent of the null with the hiding word removed.

### Rivals
- **Theodotion's Daniel 2: median 2 against 1:26–2:3** (2, 2, 3, 2, 2, 9). It equals Wisdom 7, and the null reaches it once in 120. It carries the revelatory shape that Wisdom lacks: ἀποκαλύπτει βαθέα καὶ ἀπόκρυφα ("he reveals deep and hidden things", 2:22); ἀποκαλύπτων μυστήρια, καὶ ἐγνώρισεν ("revealing mysteries, and he made known", 2:28, against γνωρίσαι, "to make known", in Col 1:27); and σοφία throughout. Without μυστήριον it falls to 13. The Old Greek Daniel 2 is weaker (median 34).
- **Proverbs 2: median 7 against 1:26–2:3, 6 against 1:24–2:5 and 6 against 2:1–5.** No null passage of 120 reached 7. Proverbs 2:3–6 joins σοφία ("wisdom"), σύνεσις ("understanding"), θησαυροί ("treasures"), ἐπίγνωσις θεοῦ ("knowledge of God") and γνῶσις ("knowledge"). That is nearly the whole of Col 2:2–3 (συνέσεως, "understanding"; ἐπίγνωσιν τοῦ μυστηρίου, "knowledge of the mystery"; θησαυροὶ τῆς σοφίας καὶ γνώσεως, "treasures of wisdom and knowledge"). Without θησαυρός it falls to 21.
- **Sirach 24** ranks high only on whole chapters (7 and 17) and falls to between 90 and 125 in every window run, against 1:24–29. It is a setting artefact, not a partner.
- **Verse-level rivals for 2:3 that the scan cannot see**, because they are single verses:
  - Isa 45:3, θησαυροὺς σκοτεινοὺς ἀποκρύφους ("dark, hidden treasures"), is the only non-literal Swete verse joining θησαυρός ("treasure") with ἀπόκρυφος ("hidden"). The other is 1 Macc 1:23, the temple's hidden treasures.
  - Sir 1:25, ἐν θησαυροῖς σοφίας ("in the treasures of wisdom"), is the exact genitive phrase.
  - Sir 20:30 and 41:14, σοφία κεκρυμμένη καὶ θησαυρὸς ἀφανής ("hidden wisdom and unseen treasure"), join wisdom, hiding and treasure.
  - Isa 33:6 has θησαυροί ("treasures") with σοφία καὶ ἐπιστήμη ("wisdom and knowledge"). Isaiah 33 also has a median of 10 against 1:24–2:5 (no null passage reached 14).
  - Ps 50:8 (Swete numbering; Ps 51:6 in English) reads τὰ κρύφια τῆς σοφίας σου ἐδήλωσάς μοι ("you made known to me the hidden things of your wisdom"). It joins hidden wisdom with disclosure.

  In Swete, only five verses join θησαυρός with σοφ- ("wisdom"): Prov 21:20, Sir 1:25, Sir 20:30, Sir 41:14 and Isa 33:6. Wisdom 7:14's treasure is two verses from its σοφία.

### Verdict
**Needs reframing. Recommended rating: moderate for Wisdom 7 as one of three field partners; low–moderate for Wis 6:22 specifically.** The field result is real but belongs to Wisdom 7, not 6:22. Against 1:26–2:3 it ranks first, and the null reaches that once in 120. But Theodotion's Daniel 2 (median 2) and Proverbs 2 (median 7) beat the null just as clearly. Daniel 2 supplies the hidden-then-revealed shape, and Proverbs 2 the "treasures of wisdom and knowledge" vocabulary. Wis 6:22 and 7:13 deny that anything is hidden, which reverses Colossians' own point. **What the claim missed:** the three-way field (Wisdom 7, Theodotion Daniel 2, Proverbs 2); the verse-level rivals for 2:3 (Isa 45:3, Sir 1:25, Sir 20:30 = 41:14, Isa 33:6); and Wis 7:17 (see New candidates).

## B2 — The adjudication frame 2:16, 18 → 3:15, 16

### What I tested
I read the parse field of every verb in Colossians (SBLGNT index) and listed every imperative. I made the same list of third-person imperatives for every Pauline letter, plus Hebrews, James and 1 Peter for scale. I checked the NT spread of βραβεύω ("rule, act as umpire"), καταβραβεύω ("disqualify, rule against") and ἐνοικέω ("dwell in"), and the Swete spread of the βραβ- root. I also searched for the "let no one … you" formula outside the imperative mood.

### Results
- **Count holds exactly.** Colossians has thirty imperatives. Four are third person: κρινέτω ("let [no one] judge", 2:16), καταβραβευέτω ("let [no one] disqualify", 2:18), βραβευέτω ("let [the peace of Christ] rule", 3:15) and ἐνοικείτω ("let [the word of Christ] dwell", 3:16). All the rest are second person.
- **καταβραβεύω ("disqualify") and βραβεύω ("rule") each occur once in the NT**, at 2:18 and 3:15 (SBLGNT). Confirmed. In Swete the βραβ- root occurs only twice: ἐβράβευσεν ("awarded the prize", Wis 10:12, of wisdom deciding Jacob's contest) and συνεβράβευσαν ("acted as arbiters with them", 1 Esd 9:14). Both are augmented forms, found only by searching the root anywhere in the word; that search also serves as the positive control. καταβραβεύω has no Swete occurrence.
- **ἐνοικέω ("dwell in") is not an adjudication verb.** Its NT uses are Rom 8:11, 2 Cor 6:16, Col 3:16, 2 Tim 1:5 and 1:14, all about indwelling. Inside Colossians it belongs with κατοικέω ("dwell"), used of the fullness at 1:19 and 2:9. So 3:16 continues the indwelling chain, not the umpire chain. The adjudication frame proper is three verbs, not four.
- **The negative side is part of a longer series.** The "no one … you" warning occurs four times in quick succession, in three different grammatical forms:
  - 2:4, ἵνα μηδεὶς ὑμᾶς παραλογίζηται ("so that no one deludes you"), a subjunctive;
  - 2:8, βλέπετε μή τις ὑμᾶς ἔσται ὁ συλαγωγῶν ("see that no one takes you captive"), a future indicative;
  - 2:16, μὴ οὖν τις ὑμᾶς κρινέτω ("let no one judge you"), an imperative;
  - 2:18, μηδεὶς ὑμᾶς καταβραβευέτω ("let no one disqualify you"), an imperative.

  Only the last two happen to be imperatives. In the whole NT the formula ("no one / anyone" with "you" and a verb of deceiving or judging) occurs otherwise only at Matt 24:4, Mark 13:5, Eph 5:6 and 2 Thess 2:3. Colossians holds four of about eight. So the "two negative imperatives" are really the end of a four-part warning series that is defined by meaning, not by mood.

Colossians' four is unremarkable for its length.

**Letters that mix negated and positive third-person imperatives** (I checked each instance by hand) are Romans, 1 Corinthians, Galatians, Ephesians, 1 Timothy and Titus, as well as Colossians.

**The closest analogue is Rom 14:3–5.** It has a negative pair in one verse, μὴ ἐξουθενείτω … μὴ κρινέτω ("let him not despise … let him not judge"), followed by a positive imperative, ἕκαστος … πληροφορείσθω ("let each be fully convinced", 14:5), and later another (15:2). It concerns the same food-and-days dispute and uses the same verb, κρίνω ("judge").

**Ephesians 4:26–5:6** alternates negative and positive third-person imperatives five times. It ends with Μηδεὶς ὑμᾶς ἀπατάτω ("let no one deceive you", 5:6), the very formula of Col 2:18.

**An abstract subject ruling in the person is also paralleled:** μὴ βασιλευέτω ἡ ἁμαρτία ("let not sin reign", Rom 6:12) is the negative counterpart of "let the peace of Christ rule" (3:15). So neither the negative-then-positive arrangement nor the shift from a human subject to an abstract one is unusual in Paul.

**The distinctive item is lexical:** the compound καταβραβεύω ("rule against") at 2:18 answered by the simple verb βραβεύω ("rule") at 3:15. Paul does play compound against simple verb in adjudication language elsewhere: διακρίνω ("discern"), κρίνω ("judge") and κατακρίνω ("condemn") at 1 Cor 11:29–32 (SBLGNT). The play is therefore in character, which supports intention without needing a four-part design.

### Does any second-person imperative dilute the frame?
Βλέπετε ("see to it", 2:8) introduces a human captor, but its adjudication sense is weak. μομφή ("complaint", 3:13) is a mildly forensic word in a participial clause about forgiving. δογματίζεσθε ("submit to decrees", 2:20) is an indicative, not an imperative. None competes with κρίνω or βραβεύω, so the frame is not diluted. But it is not exclusive either: the meaning-based series 2:4–2:18 is wider than the imperatives.

### Verdict
**Needs reframing. Recommended rating: moderate for the four-part frame; moderate–high for the καταβραβεύω → βραβεύω antithesis on its own.** The count is exact, and the compound-to-simple answer from 2:18 to 3:15 is a real and characteristically Pauline reversal. But "the only four third-person imperatives" is a grammatical coincidence: the negative series is a four-clause "no one … you" warning in three moods, and 3:16's "dwell" is an indwelling verb, not an adjudicating one. Preach it as "the umpire who disqualifies you is replaced by Christ's peace as umpire", and drop the symmetrical frame of four.

## B3 — 3:12–16 as the reversal of 2:18–23

### What I tested
I took the content words (nouns, verbs and adjectives, with the commonest excluded) of 2:16–23. For each threshold of rarity in the letter (words in at most 4, 6, 8 or 10 verses of Colossians), I counted how many of those words each stretch of the letter outside 2:16–23 shares. I used stretches of 4, 6, 8 and 10 verses. I then judged each claimed reversal for sense, and ran a control: do other pairs of passages show as many reversals of value among their shared words?

### Reproduction of the stated baseline (SBLGNT)
- **Words in at most eight verses.** 2:16–23 has 47 such words.
  - 3:12–17 shares five: λόγος ("word"), σοφία ("wisdom"), σύνδεσμος ("ligament, bond"), σῶμα ("body") and ταπεινοφροσύνη ("humility, self-abasement"). This holds exactly.
  - 2:6–13 shares five: κεφαλή ("head"), κόσμος ("world"), στοιχεῖον ("element"), σῶμα and ἄνθρωπος ("human being"). This holds.
  - **1:26–2:4 shares six on my count**, not five: θέλω ("wish"), μηδείς ("no one"), σοφία, συμβιβάζω ("knit together"), ἄνθρωπος and ὁράω ("see"). It is five if μηδείς is treated as a pronoun. A small correction.
  - Across the 77 six-verse stretches outside 2:16–23, the median is 2, as stated (mean 2.3, maximum 6). **14 of the 77 reach five or more**, so 3:12–17 sits at about the top fifth, not at the top.
- **Words in at most four verses.** 3:12–17 shares two, σύνδεσμος and ταπεινοφροσύνη. This holds. 2:6–13 shares three (κεφαλή, κόσμος, στοιχεῖον), and 1:26–2:4 shares four (three without μηδείς). Six-verse stretches have a median of 1, and 20 of 77 reach two or more.
- **Other settings.** At most six verses: 3:12–17 shares three (σοφία, σύνδεσμος, ταπεινοφροσύνη) against a median of 1 for six-verse stretches, and 19 of 77 do as well. Four-verse stretches at the eight-verse threshold: median 1, 3 of 81 reach five. Eight-verse stretches: median 3, 21 of 73 reach five. Ten-verse stretches: median 3–4.

At every setting, 3:12–17 is above the median but not exceptional. The claim's own admission that "the claim rests on reversed sense, not on count" is correct.

### Item-by-item sense
1. **ταπεινοφροσύνη ("humility / self-abasement"): a genuine reversal.** At 2:18 and 2:23 it is part of the rival practice ("delighting in self-abasement"); at 3:12 it is a virtue to put on. The NT has seven uses (Acts 20:19; Eph 4:2; Phil 2:3; Col 2:18, 2:23, 3:12; 1 Pet 5:5), and every one outside Col 2 is positive. The noun is absent from Swete (positive control: its cognates ταπεινοφρονέω, "be humble", at Ps 130:2, and ταπεινόφρων, "humble", at Prov 29:23, are found). So the pejorative use is unique to Col 2, and 3:12 reclaims it. This is the strongest item.
2. **λόγος + σοφία ("word" + "wisdom"): a genuine reversal.** These are the only two Colossians verses with both, confirmed. 2:23 has λόγον μὲν ἔχοντα σοφίας ("having a reputation, an appearance, of wisdom"); 3:16 has ὁ λόγος τοῦ Χριστοῦ … ἐν πάσῃ σοφίᾳ ("the word of Christ … in all wisdom"). Apparent wisdom gives way to real wisdom.
3. **σύνδεσμος ("ligament / bond"): not a reversal.** At 2:19 the ligaments belong to the body that grows from the head, a positive image; the fault lies with the one "not holding the head", not with the ligaments. At 3:14 love is σύνδεσμος τῆς τελειότητος ("the bond of perfection"), also positive. This is continuity of value, not reversal.
4. **σῶμα ("body"): a partial reversal.** 2:17 (the reality, belonging to Christ) and 2:19 (the whole body, the church) are positive. Only 2:23, ἀφειδίᾳ σώματος ("harsh treatment of the body"), is negative. 3:15, ἐν ἑνὶ σώματι ("in one body"), continues 2:19 rather than reversing it.
5. **"Teachings" → "teaching": weak, and better explained by 1:28.** 2:22 has διδασκαλίας ("teachings"), a noun; 3:16 has διδάσκοντες ("teaching"), a verb. But διδάσκω ("teach") also occurs at 1:28 and 2:7. And 3:16 ties to 1:28 far more tightly than to 2:22. Only these two verses join νουθετέω ("admonish") with διδάσκω, and both add ἐν πάσῃ σοφίᾳ ("in all wisdom"). 3:16 hands Paul's own ministry (1:28) to the congregation. That is its primary echo.
6. **The umpire verbs.** A genuine antithesis (see B2), but it lies in 3:15, not in the 3:12–14 virtue list.

### Control: do other pairs show as many reversals?
- **1:26–2:4 against 2:16–23**, reversals of value among the shared words:
  - θέλω ("wish"): God ἠθέλησεν ("willed", 1:27) against the rival θέλων ("delighting, self-willed", 2:18). A reversal.
  - σοφία ("wisdom"): 1:28 and 2:3 against 2:23's "appearance of wisdom". A reversal.
  - ἄνθρωπος ("human being"): "every human being" made mature (1:28) against "teachings of human beings" (2:22). A reversal.
  - συμβιβάζω ("knit together", 2:2 and 2:19) and μηδείς ("no one", 2:4 and 2:18): same value, so continuity.

  That makes about three reversals, as many as 3:12–17's clear ones (ταπεινοφροσύνη, λόγος with σοφία, part of σῶμα).
- **2:6–13 against 2:16–23**: κόσμος ("world") and στοιχεῖον ("element"), 2:8 and 2:20, are negative in both. κεφαλή ("head", 2:10 and 2:19) is positive in both. ἄνθρωπος ("human", 2:8 and 2:22) is negative in both. σῶμα (2:11, the "body of flesh" stripped off) is negative against positive at 2:17 and 2:19. That gives about one reversal: 2:6–13 restates 2:16–23 rather than reversing it.

So 3:12–17 is not unique in how many value-reversals it shows: 1:26–2:4 matches it. What is unique is the reclaiming of ταπεινοφροσύνη, and that rests on one word.

### Verdict
**Needs reframing. Recommended rating: moderate for the narrowed claim (3:12 reclaims ταπεινοφροσύνη and 3:16 gives the real λόγος σοφίας, "word of wisdom"); low–moderate for "3:12–16 as a designed reversal of 2:18–23" taken as a whole.** The count baseline reproduces and, as the claim concedes, does not carry the case. Of the six listed items, two are real reversals of value, σύνδεσμος is not one at all, and the teaching link belongs to 1:28. A control passage (1:26–2:4) shows as many reversals. **Missed:** 3:16's tie to 1:28. It is the only other verse joining νουθετέω and διδάσκω, and it also has ἐν πάσῃ σοφίᾳ ("in all wisdom").

## B4 — The triple σῶμα (2:17, 19, 23) as deliberate play

### What I tested
I verified the verse counts. I built a baseline: in Paul's letters, how often does a common noun occur in three or more verses within an eight-verse span, and then a six-verse span? In those clusters I judged whether the senses shift. I checked σκιά ("shadow") with σῶμα ("body") in Swete and the NT, and confirmed the passage's last word.

### Results — counts
- σῶμα occurs in eight Colossians verses: 1:18, 1:22, 1:24, 2:11, 2:17, 2:19, 2:23 and 3:15. Three of them fall in 2:16–23. σωματικῶς ("bodily") occurs at 2:9. All confirmed (SBLGNT).
- 2:23 ends τῆς σαρκός ("of the flesh"), confirmed. σάρξ also occurs at 2:18 (τοῦ νοὸς τῆς σαρκὸς αὐτοῦ, "his fleshly mind"), so 2:18–23 is bracketed by "flesh".
- **σκιά with σῶμα.** Not in any Swete verse, confirmed. Positive control: σκια- returns 72 Swete verses, so the search works. Searching the root anywhere finds only Job 36:28, which has ἐσκίασεν ("overshadowed", a verb) and σώματος ("body") in a Swete plus with a different sense. In the NT, σκιά occurs in seven verses and joins σῶμα only at Col 2:17.

**Within a six-verse span**, σῶμα has 7 clusters. **Col 2:17–23 drops out at that setting**: its three uses are spread over seven verses. So the Colossian cluster is looser than most of Paul's.

**Do the senses shift in these σῶμα clusters?** These are my judgements; the texts can be checked.
- About **three senses**: 1 Cor 6:13–20 (the physical body; "your bodies are members of Christ"; "one body" with a prostitute) and 1 Cor 15:35–44 (bodies of seed, flesh and heavenly beings, the natural against the spiritual body: a deliberate sequence).
- **Two senses**: Rom 12:1–5 (bodies offered, then "one body in Christ"); 1 Cor 11:24–29 ("my body", then "discerning the body", which is debated [S]); 1 Cor 12 (the human body as an analogy for the church); Eph 5:23–30 (the church as body, wives as one's own bodies); and, inside this letter, Col 1:18–24 (the church as body at 1:18 and 1:24, around Christ's physical "body of flesh" at 1:22).
- **One sense**: Rom 8:10–13 and 2 Cor 5:6–10.

On this count about seven of ten σῶμα clusters shift sense, and two or three run through three senses. The same habit shows with νόμος ("law") at Rom 7:21–8:2 (God's law, the law of sin, the law of the mind, the law of the Spirit) [S], and σάρξ ("flesh") at Rom 9:3–8.

### Sense of 2:17
ἅ ἐστιν σκιὰ τῶν μελλόντων, τὸ δὲ σῶμα τοῦ Χριστοῦ ("which are a shadow of the things to come, but the body/substance belongs to Christ"). The shadow-and-body contrast is a stock Hellenistic idiom for appearance against reality. Philo uses it (σκιὰς … σωμάτων, "shadows of bodies") and so does Josephus (σκιάν against σῶμα, of a kingdom) [S]. So "substance" is ordinary Greek and needs no play. The ecclesial overtone ("the body of Christ") is invited by 1:18, 1:24 and 2:19 in the same letter, but it cannot be proven from the text.

| Check | Result |

### Verdict
**Confirmed with nuance. Recommended rating: moderate (down from moderate–high).** The three senses are there in sequence, and the absence of any σκιά–σῶμα pairing in Swete is correct. But shifting the sense of σῶμα within a few verses is what Paul ordinarily does: Colossians itself does it at 1:18–24, and 1 Corinthians 6 and 15 do it at greater density. And 2:17's sense is a stock idiom. The play is real as Pauline style; "deliberate design" is more than the evidence shows.

## B5 — Isaiah 49 (the Servant) as the frame of Paul's suffering in 1:24–29

### What I tested
I checked the cited Pauline uses of Isaiah verbatim. I compared every stem shared between Col 1:24–29 and Isaiah 49 (and 49–53), with a sense check. I took Isaiah 49's six-run rank, then ranked all 66 Isaiah chapters to see how many share as much. I compared the "labour in vain" idiom across Paul's letters and Swete.

### Results
- **Verbatim citations confirmed (SBLGNT against Swete).** Rom 15:21 = Isa 52:15b (οἷς οὐκ ἀνηγγέλη περὶ αὐτοῦ ὄψονται, "those to whom it was not told about him will see"). 2 Cor 6:2 = Isa 49:8a (καιρῷ δεκτῷ ἐπήκουσά σου, "at an acceptable time I heard you"). Gal 1:15 shares ἐκ κοιλίας μητρός μου ("from my mother's womb") with Isa 49:1. Not named in the claim, Acts 13:47 quotes Isa 49:6 (εἰς φῶς ἐθνῶν, "as a light to the nations") as Paul and Barnabas's own commission.
- **Shared stems, 1:24–29 with Isaiah 49.** The scan credits seven: ἅγιος ("holy"), δόξα ("glory"), ἔθνη ("nations"), θεός ("God"), θλιβ- ("affliction"), κοπιάω ("labour") and σάρξ ("flesh"). In sense:
  - κοπιάω (49:4, κενῶς ἐκοπίασα, "I laboured in vain") is substantive.
  - ἔθνη (49:1, 6, 7, 8, 22) against ἐν τοῖς ἔθνεσιν ("among the Gentiles", 1:27) is thematically live.
  - δοξασθήσομαι ("I shall be glorified", 49:3, 5) is loose.
  - θλίψαντες ("oppressors", 49:26) is a cognate of θλῖψις ("affliction", 1:24), but the sense is different.
  - σάρκας ("flesh", 49:26, oppressors eating their own flesh) is a false friend.

  The scan also misses ἔκρυψέν με ("he hid me", 49:2, twice), against the hidden mystery of 1:26: same root, but the referent is the Servant, not a mystery.

  **Correction:** "only κοπιάω is shared" is too strong as a statement of shared vocabulary. It is right as a statement of distinctive vocabulary, with ἔθνη as a thematic second.
- **Rank.** Isaiah 49's six runs against 1:24–29 are 127, 113, 241, 144, 362 and 179 (median 179 of 1,259). Among Isaiah's 66 chapters it is **11th**. Isaiah 30 (41) and 33 (46) rank highest. **23 Isaiah chapters share at least seven stems.** Isaiah 50–53 are weaker still: 52 and 53 rank about 1,070, and Isaiah 53 shares only three generic stems. Nothing in 1:24–29's παθήματα ("sufferings") or θλίψεις ("afflictions") touches Isaiah 53 lexically.
- **Paul's "labour in vain" idiom.** Gal 4:11 has εἰκῇ κεκοπίακα ("I have laboured in vain", with a different adverb). Phil 2:16 has οὐδὲ εἰς κενὸν ἐκοπίασα ("nor laboured in vain"). 1 Thess 3:5 has εἰς κενὸν … ὁ κόπος ("our labour … in vain"). Gal 2:2 has εἰς κενὸν τρέχω ("run in vain").

  In Swete, κοπιάω with κεν- ("empty") occurs at Isa 49:4 (κενῶς, an adverb, plus εἰς μάταιον, "for nothing"), Isa 65:23 (οὐ κοπιάσουσιν εἰς κενόν, "they shall not labour in vain"), Jer 28:58 (Swete numbering), and Job 2:9 and 39:16. **Paul's set phrase εἰς κενόν is verbally closer to Isa 65:23 than to Isa 49:4.**

  Col 1:29 itself has no "in vain" at all: κοπιῶ ἀγωνιζόμενος ("I labour, striving"). The Isa 49:4 link therefore needs the missing half of its own phrase.

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Paul quotes Isa 49 and 52 verbatim elsewhere |

### Verdict
**Confirmed (as background only). Recommended rating: low–moderate.** The cited Pauline uses are verbatim, so Isaiah 49 is part of Paul's self-understanding. But inside Col 1:24–29 it has one distinctive contact (κοπιάω, "labour"), without its "in vain", plus the generic ἔθνη ("nations"). Isaiah 49 is only 11th among Isaiah's chapters, and 23 Isaiah chapters share as much vocabulary. Treat it as the horizon of Paul's apostolic self-understanding, not as a frame demonstrable from this text.

### Sense check

Without στοιχείωσις, 2 Maccabees 7 falls from 2 to 8.
- **2 Maccabees 6.** γεύσασθαι ("to taste", 6:20), ἑορτάς ("feasts", 6:6, 7), σαββατίζειν ("keep sabbath", 6:6) and σῶμα are genuine. Its median of 36 is the honest measure of the food-and-calendar text.

- **Isaiah 1:13–14** joins νουμηνίαι ("new moons"), σάββατα ("sabbaths") and ἑορταί ("feasts") with πλησμονή ("surfeit", 1:14), the rare word of Col 2:23 (πρὸς πλησμονὴν τῆς σαρκός, "for the indulgence of the flesh"). Without πλησμονή it falls from 4 to 16; without σάββατα, to 11.
- **Isaiah 29:13** has ἐντάλματα ἀνθρώπων καὶ διδασκαλίας ("commandments and teachings of men"), the wording of Col 2:22 (and of Mark 7:7) [S]. Without ἐντάλματα it falls from 22 to 93.

### The 4 Maccabees 5:6 count
- θρησκ- ("worship") anywhere in a Swete word: six verses (Wis 11:15, 14:16, 14:18, 14:27; 4 Macc 5:6, 5:13).
- φιλοσοφ- ("philosophy"): twelve verses (Dan 1:20 in the Old Greek, and eleven in 4 Maccabees).
- Both in one verse: **4 Macc 5:6 only.** Confirmed. 4 Macc 5:11–13 has both within three verses as well.

**Sense matters here.** At 5:6 it is Antiochus who says to Eleazar that "you do not seem to me to philosophise, using the religion of the Jews", denying that Jewish religion is philosophy. At 5:22 Eleazar replies, χλευάζεις … ἡμῶν τὴν φιλοσοφίαν ("you mock our philosophy"), and at 5:25–26 he grounds abstention from pork in the law of the Creator.

So 4 Maccabees does call law-observant food abstention "our philosophy" and "religion", which supports the claim's conceptual point. But the valuation is positive, and in Colossians it is negative.

### The calendar triad
νουμηνία ("new moon"), σάββατα ("sabbaths") and ἑορτή ("feast") occur together in seven Swete verses: 2 Chr 2:4, 2 Chr 31:3, 1 Esd 5:51, Neh 10:33, Hos 2:11, Ezek 45:17 and 1 Macc 10:34. Isaiah 1:13–14 has all three across two verses.

The only Maccabean instance is a royal grant of feast days (1 Macc 10:34), not a test of fidelity. The Col 2:16 triad is an Old Testament cultic formula. 2 Macc 6:6 has only two of its three items.
