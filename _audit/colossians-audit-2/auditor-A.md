# Claim audit 2 — Colossians — Auditor A

**Scope.** Seven single-contact claims (A1–A7) on Colossians 1:19, 2:3 and 2:21–23. Every claim was tested against the primary-text corpus only: SBLGNT with its lemma index for the New Testament; Swete's LXX (searched accent-free, by stem anywhere in the word, every hit read for sense); the WLC with its lemma index for the Hebrew; and the Rahlfs, BHS and NA28 exports wherever a finding turns on wording. Where Swete is the only Greek text (Sirach, the Maccabees, Tobit, Wisdom), that is said. Anything drawn from my own knowledge of the scholarship is tagged [S] and carries no verdict on its own.

**Editions named in this report.** "Swete" = Swete's LXX; "Rahlfs" = the Rahlfs export; "SBLGNT" = the SBLGNT lemma index; "NA28" = the NA28 export; "WLC" = the Westminster Leningrad Codex lemma index. Psalms are cited in LXX numbering when Greek is in view, with the Hebrew number in brackets where it matters.

**Positive controls.** Every nil or "only" result below was preceded by a search that returned the known instance: θιγγάνω ("touch") returned Exod 19:12; ἔνταλμα ("commandment") with διδασκαλία ("teaching") returned Isa 29:13; πλήρωμα ("fullness") with a dwelling stem returned Ps 23:1; εὐδοκέω ("be pleased") with κατοικέω ("dwell") returned Ps 67:17; אוֹצָר ("treasure") with חָכְמָה ("wisdom") and דַּעַת ("knowledge") returned Isa 33:6. Augmented and reduplicated forms were caught by searching the stem anywhere in the word (for example ἥψατο, "he touched", under ἅπτω, "touch"; ηὐδόκησας, "you were pleased", under εὐδοκέω).

## A1 — Isaiah 29:13 → 29:14 as the sequence behind Colossians 2:22 → 2:23

### What was tested
The accepted context (Isa 29:13 quoted at Col 2:22) was re-checked first, then the claim proper: that Paul follows Isaiah's own move from 29:13 (human-taught worship) to 29:14 (God's judgement on human wisdom) when Col 2:23 turns to the regulations' "word of wisdom". I tested every stated count, the three Pauline uses of Isaiah 29, the shared vocabulary between Col 2:23 and Isa 29:14, the τιμ- ("honour") link, internal alternatives inside Colossians, and a chance baseline across Paul's quotations.

### Results

**Context reproduced exactly.** In Swete, ἔνταλμα ("commandment") occurs in four verses (Job 23:11; 23:12; Isa 29:13; 55:11), and only Isa 29:13 joins it to διδασκαλία ("teaching"). Swete and Rahlfs both read ἐντάλματα ἀνθρώπων καὶ διδασκαλίας ("commandments of men and teachings"). Col 2:22 keeps that order, τὰ ἐντάλματα καὶ διδασκαλίας τῶν ἀνθρώπων ("the commandments and teachings of men"), against Mark 7:7 and Matt 15:9, διδασκαλίας ἐντάλματα ἀνθρώπων ("as teachings, commandments of men") (SBLGNT). The WLC of 29:13 has מִצְוַת אֲנָשִׁים מְלֻמָּדָה ("a commandment of men, learned by rote") and no separate noun for "teachings". One small divergence between editions: Swete's 29:13 has ἐν τῷ στόματι αὐτοῦ ("with its mouth"), which Rahlfs lacks. It does not touch the claim.

**The three Pauline uses: verified.**
- Rom 11:8 (SBLGNT) has πνεῦμα κατανύξεως ("a spirit of stupor"), the wording of Isa 29:10 (Swete and Rahlfs), within a composite quotation that is otherwise Deuteronomy 29, introduced by καθὼς γέγραπται ("as it is written").
- Rom 9:20 has μὴ ἐρεῖ τὸ πλάσμα τῷ πλάσαντι ("will the thing moulded say to the one who moulded it"), six words identical to Isa 29:16 (Rahlfs; Swete adds αὐτό, "it"). The next words, τί με ἐποίησας ("why did you make me"), are nearer 29:16 (οὐ … με ἐποίησας, "you did not make me") than 45:9 (τί ποιεῖς, "what are you making"). No formula introduces it.
- 1 Cor 1:19 has γέγραπται γάρ ("for it is written") and then Isa 29:14b word for word, **except that Paul writes ἀθετήσω ("I will set aside") where Isaiah has κρύψω ("I will hide")** (Swete and Rahlfs both read κρύψω). That point bears on the claim, and the claim did not mention it.

**The "triple" counts: partly corrected.** σοφία ("wisdom"), σύνεσις ("understanding") and a hiding word (any form of κρυπτ-, κρυβ-, κρυψ-, κρυφ-) in one verse: Isa 29:14 only (Swete). **This holds.** Within one verse either side, my search returns eight places, not six:
- Theodotion Dan 2:20–22/23. Holds.
- Isa 29:13–15. This is the control.
- **Job 28:20–21: missing from the claim.** "Wisdom, whence was it found? and where is the place of understanding (συνέσεως)? … it was hidden (ἐκρύβη) from the birds of heaven."
- **Prov 2:1–3, not 2:2–4 as stated.** The hiding word is κρύψῃς ("you hide", that is, treasure up) in 2:1; 2:4 has θησαυρούς ("treasures") but no hiding word.
- Sir 1:24–25. The hiding word, κρύψει ("he will hide"), is in 1:24, and σύνεσιν ("understanding") is also in 1:24.
- Sir 14:20–21 (ἀποκρύφοις, "hidden things", in 14:21).
- Sir 39:6–7 (ἀποκρύφοις, "hidden things", in 39:7).

The triple is therefore a modest wisdom-literature idiom, eight places strong. It is not a signature of Isaiah 29.

**Shared vocabulary between Col 2:23 and Isa 29:14.** None beyond σοφία ("wisdom"). Isa 29:14's content words are προσθήσω ("I will again"), μεταθεῖναι / μεταθήσω ("to remove / I will remove"), λαόν ("people"), ἀπολῶ ("I will destroy"), σοφίαν / σοφῶν ("wisdom / of the wise"), σύνεσιν / συνετῶν ("understanding / of the prudent") and κρύψω ("I will hide"). Col 2:23 has λόγον ("word, reputation"), ἔχοντα ("having"), σοφίας ("of wisdom"), ἐθελοθρησκίᾳ ("self-made worship"), ταπεινοφροσύνῃ ("humility"), ἀφειδίᾳ ("severity"), σώματος ("of the body"), τιμῇ ("value, honour"), πλησμονήν ("indulgence") and σαρκός ("of the flesh"). The nearest semantic touch is in Col 2:22, not 2:23: εἰς φθοράν ("for perishing") is said of the regulated things, where Isaiah's ἀπολῶ ("I will destroy") is said of wisdom. The two have different objects.

**How common σοφία is.** σοφία ("wisdom") stands in 6 of the 95 verses of Colossians (1:9, 1:28, 2:3, 2:23, 3:16, 4:5; SBLGNT), about one verse in sixteen. A "wisdom" word in the verse after any given Colossian verse is therefore unremarkable in itself.

**The τιμ- link.** Isa 29:13 has τιμῶσίν με ("they honour me", that is, with their lips); the Hebrew is כִּבְּדוּנִי ("they have honoured me"). Col 2:23 has οὐκ ἐν τιμῇ τινι ("not of any value"), which on the usual construal [S] is "worth". The lexeme is related, but the sense differs: worshippers honour God in Isaiah, while in Colossians the regulations have no worth. A minority of interpreters render τιμή ("honour") in 2:23 as "honour" [S], which would bring the senses closer. On the dominant reading this is a false friend.

**A sturdier link to 29:13 than to 29:14.** Col 2:23's ἐθελοθρησκίᾳ ("self-made worship") answers Isaiah's μάτην δὲ σέβονταί με ("in vain they worship me") in 29:13 itself. The worship theme of 2:23 therefore continues the quoted verse rather than turning to the next one.

**An internal alternative.** Col 2:8 already pairs φιλοσοφία ("philosophy") with κατὰ τὴν παράδοσιν τῶν ἀνθρώπων ("according to the tradition of men"). Col 2:22–23 repeats the pattern: κατὰ … τῶν ἀνθρώπων ("according to … of men"), followed by σοφίας ("of wisdom"). The move from "of men" to "wisdom" is therefore predicted by the letter's own 2:8 framing (an inclusio across 2:8–23) without any appeal to Isaiah.

**Chance baseline.** I tested 46 Pauline OT quotations (Romans, 1–2 Corinthians and Galatians, plus Col 2:22), leaving out cases where the following Pauline verse is itself a quotation. For each I asked whether the verse after the quotation shares a content word (stems of four letters or more, function words excluded) with the LXX verse after the quoted verse (Swete for the LXX, SBLGNT for the NT).
- Six of the 46 share something. Two of the six continue the quotation itself (Rom 4:8, Rom 9:28), and two are pronoun noise.
- **Only two are substantive: Rom 9:8 (σπέρμα, "seed", a word already present in the quoted verse) and Col 2:23 (σοφία, "wisdom").** That is 2 in 46, or 4.3%.
- Against the same 46 following verses, a randomly chosen Swete verse shares at least one content word 3.6% of the time (mean of 200 draws).
- **The Col 2:22–23 "sequence" is therefore at the chance rate.** One shared common word after a quotation is what arbitrary pairings produce.

**Where Isa 29:14 does land in Colossians (see New candidates).** In the whole SBLGNT, σοφία ("wisdom"), σύνεσις ("understanding") and a hiding word fall within a verse of one another at one place only: Col 2:2–3 (συνέσεως, "understanding"; σοφίας, "wisdom"; ἀπόκρυφοι, "hidden"). σοφία ("wisdom") stands in the same verse as a hiding word in only two NT verses: 1 Cor 2:7 (ἐν μυστηρίῳ, τὴν ἀποκεκρυμμένην, "in a mystery, the hidden [wisdom]") and Col 2:3. 1 Cor 2:7 sits inside the argument that Paul opens with the Isa 29:14 quotation at 1 Cor 1:19, by way of 1:24 and 1:30 (Christ as θεοῦ σοφίαν, "God's wisdom"). Isa 29:14 is the only Swete verse holding that triple. If Isa 29:14 echoes anywhere in Colossians, the textual evidence points to 2:2–3, as an inversion (God hides the understanding of the wise; in Christ all treasures of wisdom are hidden). It does not point to 2:23.

### Hays table (Isa 29:14 → Col 2:23)

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Isa 29:13 is quoted in the very previous verse; 29:14 is quoted at 1 Cor 1:19 |
| Volume | Weak | One shared word, σοφία ("wisdom"), which is frequent in Colossians (6 of 95 verses) |
| Recurrence | Strong | Isaiah 29 is used at Rom 9:20; 11:8; 1 Cor 1:19 |
| Thematic coherence | Possible–Weak | Col 2:23 concedes the regulations a "reputation" for wisdom; it does not announce God's destruction of it |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Paul elsewhere turns 29:14 against human wisdom (1 Cor 1:19–20) |
| History of interpretation | Possible [S] | Some commentators mention 29:14 here in passing; it is not a standard identification |
| Satisfaction | Weak | The 2:8 inclusio explains the move to "wisdom" more economically |

### Verdict
**Needs reframing. Recommended rating: low–moderate for the 2:22→2:23 sequence.** The single shared word sits at the chance baseline, the τιμ- link is a false friend on the usual reading, and Colossians' own 2:8 framing predicts the turn to "wisdom". The worthwhile part of the claim is that Isaiah 29 is in Paul's mind at 2:22. The better landing site for 29:14 is 2:2–3, recorded below as a new candidate.

## A2 — Exodus 19:12 behind Colossians 2:21

### What was tested
θιγγάνω ("touch") in Swete, searched by stem anywhere in the word; ἅπτω ("touch, handle") with θιγγάνω; the NT counts by lemma; the Hebrew; rival pairings of touch-verbs in prohibitions; and whether a Sinai background suits the food context of 2:20–22.

### Results

**Counts reproduced.**
- θιγγάνω ("touch") in Swete: **one verse, Exod 19:12** (θιγεῖν, "to touch"). The only other match for the stem is καθίξετε, a form of καθίζω ("sit"), which is a false hit.
- Rahlfs agrees word for word, apart from punctuation.
- The same verse has ἁψάμενος ("whoever touches"), and 19:13 adds οὐχ ἅψεται αὐτοῦ χείρ ("no hand shall touch it").
- In the SBLGNT, θιγγάνω ("touch") occurs in three verses: Col 2:21; Heb 11:28; Heb 12:20. **Col 2:21 is the only NT verse with both θιγγάνω and ἅπτω**, and also the only NT verse with ἅπτω and γεύομαι ("taste") together.
- The WLC of Exod 19:12 has one verb, נָגַע ("touch"), twice: וּנְגֹעַ בְּקָצֵהוּ ("and touch its edge") and כָּל־הַנֹּגֵעַ ("everyone who touches").

**A wording correction.** The claim glosses the Greek as "touch (θιγεῖν) its border". The Greek reads θιγεῖν τι αὐτοῦ ("to touch any part of it"). "Its border/edge" is the Hebrew בְּקָצֵהוּ ("its edge"). Swete and Rahlfs both lack "border".

**The Sinai prohibition in the NT without LXX prompting.** Heb 12:20 cites the Sinai prohibition as κἂν θηρίον θίγῃ τοῦ ὄρους ("if even a beast touches the mountain"), although the LXX verse it draws on (Exod 19:13) uses ἅπτω ("touch"), not θιγγάνω. Heb 11:28 uses θίγῃ ("touch") of the destroyer at Passover, where Exodus 12 has no such verb. A NT writer could therefore reach for θιγγάνω ("touch") in Exodus settings on his own initiative. The word is ordinary literary Koine and Attic, frequent in Philo and Josephus [S]. Its single LXX occurrence measures the translators' vocabulary, not the word's rarity in Paul's Greek.

**Rival pairings: a Levitical eat-and-touch prohibition.** Verses in Swete joining an eating word and ἅπτω ("touch") in a prohibition:
- Gen 3:3: οὐ φάγεσθε ἀπ᾽ αὐτοῦ οὐδὲ μὴ ἅψησθε αὐτοῦ ("you shall not eat of it, nor touch it").
- Lev 11:8: ἀπὸ τῶν κρεῶν αὐτῶν οὐ φάγεσθε, καὶ τῶν θνησιμαίων αὐτῶν οὐχ ἅψεσθε ("you shall not eat of their flesh, and their carcasses you shall not touch").
- Deut 14:8: the same as Lev 11:8 (Rahlfs agrees on all three).
- Lev 7:9; 7:11; 11:39: purity rulings on touching and eating.
- Hag 2:12: holy meat touching food.

Taste-with-touch pairings (γεύομαι, "taste", with ἅπτω, "touch") are very rare in Swete: Num 11:8 (ἥψουν, "they boiled", which is ἕψω, "boil", a false hit) and 4 Macc 6:15 (ἡψημένων, "boiled", also ἕψω). **No Swete verse joins "taste" and "touch" in a prohibition.** The three-verb chain of Col 2:21 has no LXX model. Its nearest verbal-thematic kin are the Levitical food laws, not Sinai.

**Paul's own touch-prohibition.** 2 Cor 6:17 quotes Isa 52:11, ἀκαθάρτου μὴ ἅπτεσθε ("touch nothing unclean") (Swete Isa 52:11 has ἅψησθε, "touch"). This is a purity use of ἅπτω ("touch") in Paul's own hands.

**Context fit.** Col 2:16 names food and drink. 2:21 names tasting. 2:22 says these things "all perish with use" (εἰς φθορὰν τῇ ἀποχρήσει, "for perishing in the using"), which is language about consumables. Sinai's prohibition concerns approach to a holy mountain on pain of death and has nothing to do with food. The context points to food and purity regulation.

### Hays table (Exod 19:12 → Col 2:21)

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Sinai narrative |
| Volume | Weak–Possible | Two touch-verbs shared, but θιγγάνω ("touch") is ordinary Greek; no shared syntax or object |
| Recurrence | Weak | Paul never alludes to Exod 19:12 elsewhere |
| Thematic coherence | Weak | Food context (taste, perishing in use) does not fit Sinai |
| Historical plausibility | Weak | Paul is not casting the Colossian regulations as Sinai law here |
| History of interpretation | Weak [S] | Not a standard identification |
| Satisfaction | Weak | Lev 11:8 / Deut 14:8 explain the eat-and-touch pattern better |

### Verdict
**Discard as an allusion. Recommended rating: low.** The count is correct (θιγγάνω, "touch", is unique to Exod 19:12 in Swete), but the uniqueness measures LXX vocabulary, not Paul's. The verse's sense and the food context both point away from Sinai. If any background is recorded, it should be the Levitical "do not eat … do not touch" formula (Lev 11:8; Deut 14:8), noted as idiom rather than allusion.

## A3 — Isaiah 33:6 at Colossians 2:3

### What was tested
The Hebrew triad by lemma; the Greek of 33:6 in Swete and Rahlfs; the sense of the Hebrew; other verses or short windows with θησαυρός ("treasure"), σοφία ("wisdom") and γνῶσις ("knowledge") or a near-synonym; and whether a Hebrew-only triad can bear on a Greek letter.

### Results

**Counts reproduced.** אוֹצָר ("treasure"), חָכְמָה ("wisdom") and דַּעַת ("knowledge") in one verse: **Isa 33:6 only** (WLC). The treasure-and-wisdom pair alone occurs in two verses (Isa 33:6; Ezek 28:4). Within two verses either side, the triad also occurs at:
- Jer 10:12–14 and Jer 51:15–17: storehouses of wind, which is a different sense;
- Dan 1:2–4: the treasure-house of a god, then wise and knowing youths, which is incidental.

**The sense of the Hebrew is looser than the triad suggests.** Isa 33:6 reads חֹסֶן יְשׁוּעֹת חָכְמַת וָדָעַת ("a wealth of salvation, wisdom and knowledge"), and then יִרְאַת יְהוָה הִיא אוֹצָרוֹ ("the fear of YHWH is his treasure"). The treasure is the fear of YHWH. The verse does not speak of treasures that consist of wisdom and knowledge.

**The Greek of 33:6.**
- Swete: ἐν θησαυροῖς ἡ σωτηρία ἡμῶν, ἥκει σοφία καὶ ἐπιστήμη καὶ εὐσέβεια … οὗτοί εἰσιν θησαυροὶ δικαιοσύνης ("in treasures is our salvation; wisdom and understanding and piety come … these are treasures of righteousness").
- Rahlfs reads ἐκεῖ ("there") where Swete has ἥκει ("come").
- In both editions the treasures belong to salvation and righteousness, not to wisdom, and the knowledge-word is ἐπιστήμη ("understanding, skill"), not γνῶσις ("knowledge").

**Greek co-occurrence.**
- θησαυρός ("treasure"), σοφία ("wisdom") and γνῶσις / ἐπίγνωσις / ἐπιστήμη ("knowledge / understanding") in one verse: Isa 33:6 and Sir 1:25 only (Swete). With γνῶσις or ἐπίγνωσις proper: no verse.
- Within one verse either side: Prov 2:3–8, Isa 33:4–8, Sir 1:23–27, Wis 7:14–16, Theodotion Dan 1:2–4, and Jer 10:12–14 / Jer 28:15–17 (storehouses, a different sense).
- With γνῶσις ("knowledge") itself, within two verses either side: Prov 2:1–10, Theodotion Dan 1, Jer 10, Jer 28 and Wis 7. **Isa 33 drops out.**

**The decisive rival is Proverbs 2.** The Greek of Prov 2:1–6 (Swete; Rahlfs identical in substance) contains:
- κρύψῃς ("you treasure up / hide"), 2:1;
- σοφίας / σύνεσιν ("wisdom / understanding"), 2:2–3;
- θησαυρούς ("treasures"), 2:4;
- ἐπίγνωσιν θεοῦ ("knowledge of God"), 2:5;
- σοφίαν … γνῶσις καὶ σύνεσις ("wisdom … knowledge and understanding"), 2:6.

This is the vocabulary of Col 2:2–3 almost entire: συνέσεως ("understanding"), ἐπίγνωσιν τοῦ μυστηρίου τοῦ θεοῦ ("knowledge of the mystery of God"), θησαυροί ("treasures"), σοφίας ("wisdom"), γνώσεως ("knowledge") and ἀπόκρυφοι ("hidden"). The Hebrew of Prov 2:4–6 has מַטְמוֹנִים ("hidden treasures"), חָכְמָה ("wisdom") and דַּעַת ("knowledge") within two verses, the only such window in the WLC. Proverbs 2 is closer than Isa 33:6 in both languages.

**Chance.** In Swete, θησαυρός ("treasure") occurs in 104 verses and σοφία ("wisdom") in 264 of 30,533. By chance about 0.9 verses would hold both; four do (Isa 33:6; Sir 1:25; 20:30; 41:14). There is a real "treasure of wisdom" idiom, roughly four times the chance rate. Isa 33:6 is one member of it, and in Greek not the closest.

**The scan.** Ranking every Swete chapter and window against Col 2:2–3 across the six settings, Proverbs 2 has a median rank of 7 and Isaiah 33 a median rank of 19. Isaiah 61, Theodotion's Daniel 2 and 9, Proverbs 8 and 13, and Isaiah 45 all rank above Isaiah 33.

### Hays table (Isa 33:6 → Col 2:3)

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Isaiah |
| Volume | Weak | Greek shares θησαυρός ("treasure") and σοφία ("wisdom") only; the triad is Hebrew-only |
| Recurrence | Weak | No other Pauline use of Isa 33:6 |
| Thematic coherence | Possible | Treasure, wisdom and salvation in Zion |
| Historical plausibility | Weak | The Hebrew triad would require Paul to read through a Greek that loses it |
| History of interpretation | Possible [S] | Cited occasionally alongside Prov 2:3–6 and Isa 45:3 |
| Satisfaction | Weak | Prov 2:1–6 explains the Greek vocabulary far better |

### Verdict
**Needs reframing. Recommended rating: low as a standalone contact.** The Hebrew uniqueness is real but rests on a looser sense than the claim implies, and it disappears in the Greek that Colossians uses. Isa 33:6 belongs, at most, as a minor member of a "treasury of wisdom" field headed by Prov 2:1–6.

## A4 — Sirach 20:30; 41:14; 1:25 at Colossians 2:3

### What was tested
Counts in Swete (the only Greek of Sirach available here); the phrase "treasures of wisdom"; the sense of each verse; and possible verse-numbering issues.

### Results

**Counts reproduced.**
- θησαυρός ("treasure"), σοφία ("wisdom") and a hiding word in one verse: **Sir 20:30 and 41:14 only** (Swete). Both read σοφία κεκρυμμένη καὶ θησαυρὸς ἀφανής, τίς ὠφέλεια ἐν ἀμφοτέροις; ("hidden wisdom and an unseen treasure: what profit is in either?"); 41:14 spells it ὠφελία ("profit").
- Treasure with a hiding word, without wisdom: Isa 45:3 (θησαυροὺς … ἀποκρύφους, "hidden treasures") and 1 Macc 1:23 (τοὺς θησαυροὺς τοὺς ἀποκρύφους, "the hidden treasures", of temple plunder).
- **Sir 1:25 holds the only "treasure(s) + wisdom" phrase in Swete**: ἐν θησαυροῖς σοφίας παραβολὴ ἐπιστήμης ("in the treasures of wisdom is a parable of understanding"). No other Swete verse has a θησαυρ- word followed within three words by a wisdom or knowledge noun.

**Numbering.** Swete's numbering for these three verses matches the references as stated. There is no Rahlfs or Göttingen Sirach in the corpus, so I could not test for an off-by-one difference. From memory, Rahlfs numbers these verses the same way [S]. The pair 20:30–31 = 41:14–15 (Swete) is a doublet: the verse that follows each (20:31; 41:15) adds "better a man who hides his folly than a man who hides his wisdom".

**Sense.** Sir 20:30 = 41:14 censures *withheld* wisdom: hidden wisdom profits no one. Col 2:3 says that the treasures are hidden in Christ, and the surrounding text treats that hiddenness as stored-up and now disclosed:
- 1:26 says the mystery was "hidden … but now made manifest";
- 2:2 speaks of "the full assurance of understanding, into the knowledge of the mystery".

Sirach's maxim would undercut Colossians only if "hidden" meant inaccessible. Paul could have answered the maxim (wisdom hidden in Christ is not useless, because it is made known), but nothing in the text signals that. Sirach's hiding words are also different lexemes: κεκρυμμένη ("hidden", a perfect participle of κρύπτω, "hide") and ἀφανής ("unseen"). Colossians has ἀπόκρυφοι ("hidden").

Sir 1:24–25 is the better-fitting Sirach text: ἕως καιροῦ κρύψει τοὺς λόγους αὐτοῦ ("until the time he will hide his words") stands beside "treasures of wisdom". That gives a hidden-until-the-time pattern next to the phrase that matches Col 2:3. Its subject, however, is the patient man, not God.

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Possible | Sirach was widely read in Greek-speaking Judaism [S]; Swete-only text here |
| Volume | Possible (1:25) / Weak (20:30; 41:14) | 1:25 has the exact noun pair; 20:30 and 41:14 share treasure and wisdom with a different hiding lexeme |
| Recurrence | Weak | No secure Pauline citation of Sirach |
| Thematic coherence | Possible (1:25) / Weak–contrary (20:30; 41:14) | The maxim censures hidden wisdom |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | |
| History of interpretation | Weak–Possible [S] | |

### Verdict
**Confirmed with nuance (as idiom evidence only). Recommended rating: low–moderate for Sir 1:25 inside a "treasury of wisdom" idiom; low for Sir 20:30 / 41:14.** The counts hold, and Sir 1:25 is the only Swete instance of the "treasures of wisdom" phrase. The proposed fold into a wider idiom is sound, but the fold should be led by Prov 2:1–6 and Isa 45:3 (see New candidates), not by Sirach. Sir 20:30 / 41:14 should be recorded as tension, not support.

## A5 — Isaiah 45:3, 15 in Hebrew: a סתר ("hide") frame around 45:14

### What was tested
The Hebrew words by lemma; the LXX renderings in Swete and Rahlfs; whether the frame exists in the Hebrew; and whether a Hebrew-only frame can bear on Colossians.

### Results

**Lemmas verified (WLC).**
- Isa 45:3: אוֹצְרוֹת ("treasures"), חֹשֶׁךְ ("darkness"), מַטְמֻנֵי ("hidden riches of") and מִסְתָּרִים ("secret places", from the root סתר, "hide"), followed by לְמַעַן תֵּדַע ("so that you may know").
- Isa 45:15: אֵל מִסְתַּתֵּר ("a God who hides himself", hithpael of סתר).
- Isa 45:14 ends אַךְ בָּךְ אֵל ("surely God is in you"). The "you" is feminine singular (Zion), addressed by nations who bow down to her.

**The "frame" is not a frame.** The root סתר ("hide") occurs a third time in the chapter, **after** 45:14: 45:19, לֹא בַסֵּתֶר דִּבַּרְתִּי ("I did not speak in secret"). 45:3 also stands eleven verses before 45:14. The pattern is a hiddenness motif running through the chapter, ending in God's denial that he spoke in secret. It is not an envelope around 45:14.

**The Greek does not lose the motif as claimed.**
- Swete and Rahlfs both render 45:15 σὺ γὰρ εἶ θεός, καὶ οὐκ ᾔδειμεν ("for you are God, and we did not know"). The "self-hiding" is lost there. Rahlfs adds ὁ θεὸς τοῦ Ισραηλ σωτήρ ("the God of Israel, Saviour"); Swete lacks σωτήρ ("Saviour").
- At 45:3 the Greek keeps the hiddenness: θησαυροὺς σκοτεινούς, ἀποκρύφους ἀοράτους ("dark treasures, hidden, unseen") (Rahlfs; Swete punctuates θησαυροὺς σκοτινοὺς ἀποκρύφους, ἀοράτους, "dark hidden treasures, unseen").
- At 45:19 the Greek keeps it too: οὐκ ἐν κρυφῇ λελάληκα ("I have not spoken in secret").

The claim therefore has the languages back to front: the Greek keeps two of the three hiddenness markers, and the one it drops (45:15) is the only one that would frame 45:14 from the far side.

**Bearing on Colossians.**
- Col 1:26–27 has τὸ μυστήριον τὸ ἀποκεκρυμμένον ("the mystery that has been hidden") and Χριστὸς ἐν ὑμῖν ("Christ in you", plural) "among the Gentiles" (ἐν τοῖς ἔθνεσιν).
- Isa 45:14 LXX has ἐν σοὶ ὁ θεός ἐστιν ("God is in you"), spoken by Gentile nations to Zion.

There is some thematic coherence here: Gentiles, and "God / Christ in you". But the hiddenness that Colossians uses at 2:3 most closely matches the Greek of 45:3: θησαυροί ("treasures") with ἀπόκρυφος ("hidden"). In all of Swete that pairing occurs only at Isa 45:3 and 1 Macc 1:23. Chance would give about 0.1 such verses (θησαυρός, "treasure", in 104 verses; ἀπόκρυφος, "hidden", in 29). Isa 45:3 also has ἵνα γνῷς ("so that you may know"; cf. γνώσεως, "of knowledge", Col 2:3) and ἀοράτους ("unseen"; cf. ἀόρατα, "invisible things", Col 1:16). A Greek contact exists, but at 2:3, not as a frame at 1:26–27.

Recommended rating: low (record only), and replace with the Greek contact Isa 45:3 → Col 2:3 (see New candidates).** The Hebrew lemmas are correct, but the envelope does not exist: hiddenness recurs at 45:19, and the Greek keeps the motif at 45:3 and 45:19. The real Greek contact in this chapter is θησαυροὺς … ἀποκρύφους ("hidden treasures") at 45:3, against Col 2:3.

## A6 — Psalm 23:1 LXX (and kin) at Colossians 1:19

### What was tested
πλήρωμα ("fullness") with a κατοικ- ("dwell") stem, searched anywhere in the word including augmented forms, with every hit read for sense; comparison with the accepted Ps 67:17 contact; and the bearing of 1 Cor 10:26.

### Results

**Counts reproduced exactly.** πλήρωμα ("fullness") occurs in 20 verses of Swete. **Five** of them also have a κατοικ- ("dwell") word: Ps 23:1; Ps 97:7; Jer 8:16; Jer 29:2; Ezek 12:19. Widening to one verse either side adds none. Rahlfs agrees for Ps 23:1.

**Every hit is the same formula.** "The earth / sea / land and its fullness (πλήρωμα), the world and those who dwell (οἱ κατοικοῦντες) in it." In all five, πλήρωμα ("fullness") means the contents of the earth or sea, and the κατοικ- word ("dwell") is a participle for human inhabitants. **πλήρωμα ("fullness") is never the subject of dwelling, and no hit concerns divine dwelling.** The co-occurrence is formulaic. With κατοικ- ("dwell") in 670 Swete verses (2.2%), chance predicts about 0.4 co-occurrences in 20 verses, and five arise only because of the formula.

**Against Ps 67:17.** Ps 67:17 (Swete and Rahlfs), τὸ ὄρος ὃ εὐδόκησεν ὁ θεὸς κατοικεῖν ἐν αὐτῷ ("the mountain in which God was pleased to dwell"), matches Col 1:19 in construction: aorist εὐδόκησεν ("was pleased") + infinitive of κατοικέω ("to dwell") + ἐν αὐτῷ ("in him/it"). It is the only Swete verse joining εὐδοκέω ("be pleased") and κατοικέω ("dwell"). None of the πλήρωμα hits comes near it.

**1 Cor 10:26.** Paul quotes Ps 23:1 to settle a food question ("eat whatever is sold in the market … for the earth is the Lord's and its fullness"). That shows the verse was available to him, but the use is about food scruples. It bears on Col 2:16–23, where food regulations are in view, not on 1:19. Ps 23:2 (ἐθεμελίωσεν, "he founded") and 23:3 ("who shall ascend the mountain of the Lord?") give the psalm a creation and temple setting, a slight thematic affinity with Ps 67:17's mountain. It is not a verbal contact with 1:19.

**A closer "fullness" text.** Ps 88:12 (Ps 89:12 Hebrew) has σοί εἰσιν οἱ οὐρανοί, καὶ σή ἐστιν ἡ γῆ· τὴν οἰκουμένην καὶ τὸ πλήρωμα αὐτῆς σὺ ἐθεμελίωσας ("yours are the heavens, and yours is the earth; the world and its fullness you founded") (Swete and Rahlfs). It ranks first against Col 1:19–20 in the window scan under several settings (see New candidates).

## A7 — Zion-dwelling companions to Psalm 68 at Colossians 1:19

### What was tested
The wording of Hag 1:8 (Swete, Rahlfs), 3 Macc 2:15–16 (Swete only), and Ps 131:13–14 (Swete, Rahlfs; Hebrew Ps 132:13–14). I also ran a wider search for εὐδοκ- ("be pleased") or a choosing / desiring verb beside a dwelling word for God's presence. In the Hebrew I searched רצה ("be pleased"), חפץ ("delight"), בחר ("choose"), אוה ("desire") and חמד ("desire") beside ישב ("dwell"), שכן ("dwell"), מוֹשָׁב ("dwelling"), מִשְׁכָּן ("tabernacle") and מָעוֹן ("habitation").

### Results

**The accepted context holds.** εὐδοκέω ("be pleased") with κατοικέω ("dwell") in one verse: Ps 67:17 only (Swete). Rahlfs reads the same words, punctuated as a question. In the WLC, Ps 68:17 reads הָהָר חָמַד אֱלֹהִים לְשִׁבְתּוֹ אַף־יְהוָה יִשְׁכֹּן לָנֶצַח ("the mountain God desired for his dwelling; indeed YHWH will dwell for ever"). The verb is חָמַד ("desire"), not רצה ("be pleased"). **Ps 68:17 is the only WLC verse joining a desire verb to both ישב ("dwell") and שכן ("dwell"),** and the only verse with חָמַד ("desire") + שכן ("dwell") at all.

**The named companions, checked.**
- **Hag 1:8.** Swete: ἀνάβητε εἰς τὸ ὄρος … καὶ οἰκοδομήσατε τὸν οἶκον, καὶ εὐδοκήσω ἐν αὐτῷ καὶ ἐνδοξασθήσομαι ("go up to the mountain … and build the house, and I will be pleased with it and be glorified"). Rahlfs reads ἐπὶ τὸ ὄρος ("onto the mountain"). The tense is future, there is no dwelling verb, and ἐν αὐτῷ ("in it") goes with εὐδοκήσω ("I will be pleased"). The temple-and-pleasure theme holds; a dwelling construction does not.
- **3 Macc 2:15–16** (Swete only). 2:15: τὸ μὲν γὰρ κατοικητήριόν σου οὐρανὸς τοῦ οὐρανοῦ ἀνέφικτος ἀνθρώποις ἐστίν ("for your dwelling place, the heaven of heaven, is unreachable by men"). 2:16: ἀλλὰ ἐπεὶ εὐδόκησας τὴν δόξαν σου ἐν τῷ λαῷ σου Ἰσραήλ, ἡγίασας τὸν τόπον τοῦτον ("but since you were pleased [to set] your glory among your people Israel, you sanctified this place"). **Correction:** the κατοικητήριον ("dwelling place") is heaven, set *against* the earthly sanctuary, not a word for the temple. 2:16 has εὐδοκέω ("be pleased") with an accusative and ἐν ("among"), and no infinitive. The theme, heaven's God choosing to locate his glory on earth, is genuinely close to Col 1:19–20.
- **Ps 131:13–14** (Swete and Rahlfs). ἐξελέξατο Κύριος τὴν Σιών, ᾑρετίσατο αὐτὴν εἰς κατοικίαν ἑαυτῷ … ὧδε κατοικήσω, ὅτι ᾑρετισάμην αὐτήν ("the Lord chose Zion, he preferred her as a dwelling for himself … here I will dwell, for I have preferred her"). The verbs are ἐκλέγομαι ("choose") and αἱρετίζω ("prefer"), not εὐδοκέω ("be pleased"), with κατοικία ("dwelling") and κατοικήσω ("I will dwell"). In the WLC, Ps 132:13–14 has בחר ("choose") + אוה ("desire") + מוֹשָׁב ("dwelling") + ישב ("dwell"). It is the only verse joining בחר ("choose") with מוֹשָׁב ("dwelling").

**Members the claim missed (Swete).** εὐδοκ- ("be pleased") with a dwelling or house word in one verse returned nine verses. Read for sense, four concern God's pleasure in a house, Zion or temple:
- **2 Macc 14:35** (Swete only): Σύ, Κύριε, τῶν ὅλων ἀπροσδεὴς ὑπάρχων, ηὐδόκησας ναὸν τῆς σῆς σκηνώσεως ἐν ἡμῖν γενέσθαι ("You, Lord, though in need of nothing, were pleased that a temple of your dwelling should be among us"). It has an aorist εὐδοκέω ("be pleased") + infinitive + a dwelling noun + ἐν ("among / in"). **After Ps 67:17 this is the nearest construction to Col 1:19 in Swete, and the claim did not name it.** Its ἐν ἡμῖν ("among us") also brushes Col 1:27, Χριστὸς ἐν ὑμῖν ("Christ in you").
- **Isa 62:4**: εὐδόκησεν Κύριος ἐν σοί ("the Lord was pleased with you"), of Zion. It has the aorist and ἐν σοί ("in you"), but no dwelling verb; the dwelling stem is a false hit on οἰκουμένη ("inhabited").
- **Ps 50:20**: ἐν τῇ εὐδοκίᾳ σου τὴν Σιών … οἰκοδομηθήτω τὰ τείχη ("in your good pleasure [do good to] Zion … let the walls be built").
- **1 Chr 29:3**: David's εὐδοκῆσαι ("delight") in the house of God, which is human pleasure.

The remaining five hits are noise (Ps 101:15 οἰκτειρήσουσιν, "they will pity"; Esther; Tobit; and others).

**The Hebrew tradition the claim missed.**
- רצה ("be pleased") and חפץ ("delight") never stand with ישב or שכן ("dwell") in one verse (WLC), and within a verse either side they give only incidental hits.
- The large Hebrew tradition is Deuteronomy's chosen-place formula: בחר ("choose") + שכן ("make dwell"), "the place which YHWH will choose to make his name dwell there". It occurs at Deut 12:11; 14:23; 16:2; 16:6; 16:11; 26:2; and Neh 1:9 (WLC).
- **The LXX renders that dwelling as ἐπικληθῆναι τὸ ὄνομα αὐτοῦ ἐκεῖ ("for his name to be called there")** (Swete, Deut 12:11; 14:23; 16:2; 26:2), so the Greek reader loses the indwelling.

**Recurrence for Psalm 68 in the Pauline corpus.** Eph 4:8 quotes Ps 67:19, ἀναβὰς εἰς ὕψος ᾐχμαλώτευσεν αἰχμαλωσίαν ("ascending on high he led captivity captive"). That verse is two verses after 67:17, and 67:19 itself ends καὶ γὰρ ἀπειθοῦντες τοῦ κατασκηνῶσαι ("even the disobedient, that [God] might dwell"). Eph 4:10 then glosses the psalm with ἵνα πληρώσῃ τὰ πάντα ("that he might fill all things"), and Eph 1:23 has τὸ πλήρωμα ("the fullness") (SBLGNT). So a closely related letter reads Psalm 68 together with *fullness* language. Ephesians' authorship is debated [S], but either way it is the nearest evidence of how Psalm 68 was read in this circle.

**Strengthen or dilute?** The wider tradition strengthens the *temple* reading of 1:19: "God was pleased to have his dwelling / glory / temple among (ἐν, "in") his people" is a recognisable idiom (Ps 67:17; 2 Macc 14:35; 3 Macc 2:16; Ps 131:13–14; Isa 62:4; Hag 1:8). At the same time it weakens the claim that 1:19 alludes to Ps 67:17 *specifically*, because the idiom could reach Paul without that verse. What keeps Ps 67:17 in front is that it alone has the exact construction (εὐδόκησεν, "was pleased", + κατοικεῖν, "to dwell", + ἐν αὐτῷ, "in it"), together with the Ephesians recurrence. 2 Macc 14:35 is its nearest rival.

### Hays table (Zion-dwelling tradition → Col 1:19)

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Psalms, Haggai, Maccabees all current [S] |
| Volume | Possible | Varies by member: Ps 67:17 strong; 2 Macc 14:35 possible; Hag 1:8, 3 Macc 2:16 and Ps 131:13–14 weak verbally |
| Recurrence | Possible | Ps 68 recurs at Eph 4:8–10 with fullness language |

### Verdict
**Confirmed with nuance. Recommended rating: moderate as a tradition**, folded into the Ps 68 claim with three changes:
1. Add 2 Macc 14:35, the nearest construction after Ps 67:17.
2. Correct 3 Macc 2:15–16: κατοικητήριον ("dwelling place") is heaven, set against the earthly sanctuary.
3. Note that Hag 1:8 and Ps 131:13–14 are thematic members, not verbal ones.

The tradition supports the temple reading. Ps 67:17 keeps its place by construction, not by theme, and Eph 4:8–10 is its best recurrence evidence.

## New candidates

**1. Isa 29:14 → Col 2:2–3 (inversion), by way of 1 Cor 1:19–2:7.** *Recommended for testing; provisional rating moderate.*
- Isa 29:14 is the only Swete verse with σοφία ("wisdom"), σύνεσις ("understanding") and a hiding word together.
- Col 2:2–3 is the only NT window with the same three (συνέσεως, "understanding"; σοφίας, "wisdom"; ἀπόκρυφοι, "hidden").
- Paul's own reading of 29:14 runs from 1 Cor 1:19 through Christ as "God's wisdom" (1:24, 30) to "God's wisdom in a mystery, the hidden one" (2:7). 2:7 is the only other NT verse where σοφία ("wisdom") and a hiding word share a verse.
- Col 1:26–2:3 restates that sequence: mystery hidden, now revealed, treasures of wisdom hidden in Christ.
- The Jesus tradition also reads 29:14 as a hiding text: Matt 11:25 / Luke 10:21, ἔκρυψας / ἀπέκρυψας ταῦτα ἀπὸ σοφῶν καὶ συνετῶν ("you hid these things from the wise and prudent") (SBLGNT).
- Caveat: Paul's 1 Cor 1:19 swaps κρύψω ("I will hide") for ἀθετήσω ("I will set aside"), so the hiding verb is not his quoted wording.

This would replace A1's 2:22→2:23 sequence as the place to look for Isa 29:14.

**2. Isa 45:3 → Col 2:3.** *Provisional rating low–moderate to moderate.*
- θησαυρούς … ἀποκρύφους ("hidden treasures") is one of only two Swete verses with θησαυρός ("treasure") + ἀπόκρυφος ("hidden"); the other is 1 Macc 1:23, which is about temple plunder.
- The verse adds ἵνα γνῷς ("so that you may know") and ἀοράτους ("unseen"; cf. Col 1:16 ἀόρατα, "invisible things").
- Isaiah 45 is in Paul's repertoire (Rom 14:11; Phil 2:10–11 [S]; probably Rom 9:20).
- Null check: in the window scan, Isaiah 45 is the top-ranked Swete chapter against Col 2:3 alone at the 8- and 16-verse settings, but its score (11.4) sits at the median top score of 40 random single Pauline verses (11.6; 90th percentile 14.1). Its case therefore rests on the rare collocation, not on the scan.
- 1 Macc 1:23 shows that "hidden treasures" was a stock Greek phrase, which caps the rating.

**3. Prov 2:1–6 → Col 2:2–3.** *Provisional rating moderate.*
- The Greek supplies almost the whole word-field of Col 2:2–3: hide / treasure up, wisdom, understanding, treasures, ἐπίγνωσις ("knowledge"), γνῶσις ("knowledge"). The Hebrew window Prov 2:4–6 is unique in the WLC for "hidden treasure" + wisdom + knowledge.
- Proverbs 2 has a median rank of 7 across the six settings against Col 2:2–3, and is second at the 8-verse setting.
- Prov 2:1–6 should head the proposed "treasury of wisdom" fold, ahead of Isa 33:6 and Sirach.

**4. 2 Macc 14:35 at Col 1:19.** *Provisional rating low–moderate (Swete only).* An aorist εὐδοκέω ("be pleased") + infinitive + "temple of your dwelling" + "among us". It is the nearest construction to Col 1:19 after Ps 67:17, and should join the Zion-dwelling tradition (A7).

**5. Psalm 88 LXX (Ps 89 Hebrew) behind Col 1:15–20.** *Flag for research; provisional rating low–moderate.*
- Against Col 1:15–20, Psalm 88 is the top Swete chapter in all six scan settings.
- The words credited are πρωτότοκον ("firstborn", 88:28; cf. Col 1:15, 18); οὐρανοί / γῆ ("heavens / earth", 88:12); πλήρωμα ("fullness", 88:12); ἐθεμελίωσας / ἔκτισας ("you founded / created", 88:12–13); θρόνος ("throne"); εὐδοκία ("good pleasure", 88:18); and ἐκκλησία ("assembly", 88:6) (Swete; Rahlfs agrees at 88:12, 13, 18, 28).
- Null: a single chapter tops all six settings for 4 of 30 random six-verse Pauline passages (13%). Ps 88's chapter score (21.6) is below the null median (22.9), although the null passages carried more stems. So the scan alone is not decisive.
- The case rests on content: a royal "firstborn" with creation of heaven and earth and their "fullness". The Hebrew בְּכוֹר ("firstborn") stands at Ps 89:28.

**6. Levitical food-purity idiom and Ps 23:1 at Col 2:16–23.** *Record.*
- Lev 11:8 / Deut 14:8 ("do not eat … do not touch") are the nearest LXX models for 2:21's pattern.
- Paul's own use of Ps 23:1 at 1 Cor 10:26, settling a food scruple, is a relevant cross-reference for the food regulations of 2:16–23. It supplies a theme, not a verbal contact.

**7. Sir 1:24–25, hidden until the time.** *Record, low.* ἕως καιροῦ κρύψει ("until the time he will hide") stands beside "treasures of wisdom", a pattern like Col 1:26 ("hidden from the ages … now made manifest"). The subject is the patient man, not God, and the text is Swete only.
