# Claims file — Auditor A (single contacts: wording, rarity, rivals, Greek and Hebrew)

Each claim is stated as the proposing reports state it, counts included. Test each.

## A1 — Isaiah 29:13 → 29:14 as the sequence behind Colossians 2:22 → 2:23 [queue C24]
- Col 2:22 κατὰ τὰ ἐντάλματα καὶ διδασκαλίας τῶν ἀνθρώπων; 2:23 ἅτινά ἐστιν λόγον μὲν ἔχοντα σοφίας ἐν ἐθελοθρησκίᾳ καὶ ταπεινοφροσύνῃ [καὶ] ἀφειδίᾳ σώματος, οὐκ ἐν τιμῇ τινι πρὸς πλησμονὴν τῆς σαρκός.
- Stated: ἔνταλμα ("commandment") with διδασκαλία ("teaching") stands in one verse of Swete, Isa 29:13; Col 2:22 keeps the LXX order (ἐντάλματα … καὶ διδασκαλίας), against Mark 7:7 / Matt 15:9 (διδασκαλίας ἐντάλματα ἀνθρώπων). The Hebrew of 29:13 has no noun "teachings" (מִצְוַת אֲנָשִׁים מְלֻמָּדָה) — the Colossian wording is Greek-only. **This quotation is accepted; it is context, not the claim.**
- **The claim:** Isa 29:14 follows at once — "I will destroy the wisdom of the wise (τὴν σοφίαν τῶν σοφῶν), and the understanding of the prudent I will hide (κρύψω)" — and Col 2:23 turns directly from "commandments and teachings of men" to the regulations' mere "word (appearance) of wisdom". So Paul follows Isaiah's own sequence (human-taught worship → God's judgement on human wisdom). Paul uses Isaiah 29 at least three times elsewhere: Rom 11:8 (Isa 29:10), Rom 9:20 (Isa 29:16, cf. 45:9), 1 Cor 1:19 (Isa 29:14, with γέγραπται). 29:13 has τιμῶσίν ("honour"), and Col 2:23 has τιμῇ ("value, honour").
- Stated: σοφία + σύνεσις + a hiding word in one verse: Isa 29:14 only (Swete); but within one verse either side the same triple also occurs at Prov 2:2–4, Sir 1:25, Sir 14:20, Sir 39:6 and Theodotion Dan 2:20–23.
- Rated **moderate**; not load-bearing.
- Test: verify every count and the three Pauline uses (wording against Swete and Rahlfs). Does Col 2:23 share any word with 29:14 beyond σοφία? Is "wisdom" after "commandments of men" a sequence Paul could reach without Isaiah (e.g. generic polemic; 1 Cor 1–2)? Is the τιμ- link real (sense) or a false friend? Baseline: how often does a quoted verse's *next* verse share a key word with the verse following the quotation in Paul's letters?

## A2 — Exodus 19:12 behind Colossians 2:21 [C27]
- Col 2:21 μὴ ἅψῃ μηδὲ γεύσῃ μηδὲ θίγῃς ("do not handle, do not taste, do not touch").
- Stated: θιγγάνω ("touch") occurs in three NT verses (Col 2:21; Heb 11:28; 12:20) and **once in Swete's LXX, at Exod 19:12**, beside ἅπτω — "do not go up the mountain or touch (θιγεῖν) its border; whoever touches (ἁψάμενος) the mountain shall surely be put to death" (Rahlfs agrees). Col 2:21 is the only NT verse with both verbs. Heb 12:20 cites the Sinai prohibition with θίγῃ. The Hebrew has one verb, נָגַע ("touch"), twice. The purity idiom "do not eat … do not touch" also at Lev 11:8; Deut 14:8; Gen 3:3.
- Rated **low–uncertain**; queued "so that it is tested rather than preached".
- Test: verify the counts (θιγγάνω in Swete by stem anywhere in the word; ἅπτω with θιγγάνω); rivals for the *pairing* of touch-verbs in prohibitions; does Col 2:21's food context (taste; 2:22 "perish in the using") fit Sinai at all? Verdict.

## A3 — Isaiah 33:6 at Colossians 2:3 [C17]
- Col 2:3 ἐν ᾧ εἰσιν πάντες οἱ θησαυροὶ τῆς σοφίας καὶ γνώσεως ἀπόκρυφοι.
- Stated: אוֹצָר ("treasure") with חָכְמָה ("wisdom") and דַּעַת ("knowledge") stands in one Hebrew verse only, Isa 33:6 (WLC). Swete's 33:6 keeps θησαυροί with σοφία and ἐπιστήμη ("understanding"), not γνῶσις. Rated **low–moderate** (Hebrew-only triad; the Greek keeps treasure and wisdom).
- Test: counts (lemma); the Greek of 33:6 in Swete and Rahlfs; is the Hebrew triad relevant to a Greek letter? Other verses with θησαυρός + σοφία + γνῶσις (or ἐπιστήμη) within a verse or two? Verdict.

## A4 — Sirach 20:30; 41:14; 1:25 at Colossians 2:3 [C21]
- Stated: Sir 20:30 and 41:14 ("hidden wisdom and an unseen treasure: what use is either?") are the only Swete verses with wisdom, a hiding word and treasure together; Sir 1:25 has ἐν θησαυροῖς σοφίας ("in the treasures of wisdom"), the exact noun pair of Col 2:3. Rated **low–moderate**; proposed to fold into a wider "treasury of wisdom" idiom (with Isa 45:3, Prov 2:2–6, Isa 33:6).
- Note: there is no Rahlfs export for Sirach; Swete only. Sirach's Greek verse numbering differs between editions `[S]` — say if a stated reference is off by one.
- Test: counts; sense of each (Sir 20:30 = 41:14 is a criticism of *withheld* wisdom — does that fit "hidden in Christ" or cut against it?); verdict.

## A5 — Isaiah 45:3, 15 in Hebrew: a סתר ("hide") frame around 45:14 [C20]
- Stated: Isa 45:3 has אוֹצְרוֹת חֹשֶׁךְ וּמַטְמֻנֵי מִסְתָּרִים ("treasures of darkness and hidden riches of secret places"); 45:15 has אֵל מִסְתַּתֵּר ("a God who hides Himself"), which the LXX does not keep (σὺ γὰρ εἶ θεός, καὶ οὐκ ᾔδειμεν, "You are God, and we did not know"); between them 45:14, ἐν σοὶ ὁ θεός ("God is in you"). Claim: the Hebrew frames "God in you" with hiddenness, as Col 1:26–27 frames "Christ in you" with a hidden mystery. Rated **low** (record only).
- Test: verify the Hebrew words by lemma and the LXX renderings (Swete, Rahlfs); is a Hebrew-only frame relevant to Colossians' Greek? Verdict.

## A6 — Psalm 23:1 LXX (and kin) at Colossians 1:19 [C22]
- Col 1:19 ὅτι ἐν αὐτῷ εὐδόκησεν πᾶν τὸ πλήρωμα κατοικῆσαι.
- Stated: πλήρωμα ("fullness") with κατοικ- ("dwell") in one verse: Ps 23:1 (τοῦ κυρίου ἡ γῆ καὶ τὸ πλήρωμα αὐτῆς … καὶ πάντες οἱ κατοικοῦντες ἐν αὐτῇ, "the earth and its fullness … and all who dwell in it"), Ps 97:7, Jer 8:16, Jer 29:2, Ezek 12:19 (Swete). Both words are used in other senses (the earth's contents; the inhabitants). Ps 23:2 has "He founded it". Rated **low**.
- Test: counts (stem anywhere, sense-check every hit); is any of them closer to Col 1:19 than the phrase-level contact Ps 67:17 (εὐδόκησεν … κατοικεῖν ἐν αὐτῷ, the accepted rival)? 1 Cor 10:26 quotes Ps 23:1 — does that matter? Verdict.

## A7 — Zion-dwelling companions to Psalm 68 at Colossians 1:19 [C18]
- Accepted context: Ps 67:17 LXX τὸ ὄρος ὃ εὐδόκησεν ὁ θεὸς κατοικεῖν ἐν αὐτῷ — the aorist εὐδόκησεν governing an infinitive of κατοικέω with ἐν αὐτῷ, a construction found nowhere else in Swete; Col 1:19 has the same.
- **The claim:** the temple function of 1:19 belongs to a wider Zion-dwelling tradition, whose other members are Hag 1:8 ("build the house, and I will be pleased (εὐδοκήσω) with it"), 3 Macc 2:15–16 (εὐδόκησας of God's glory, beside κατοικητήριον, "dwelling place"), and Ps 131:13–14 (the Lord chose Zion for his dwelling; Hebrew Ps 132:13–14). Rated **moderate as a tradition**; proposed to fold into the Ps 68 claim.
- Test: verify the wording of each (Swete; Rahlfs for Haggai and Psalms; 3 Maccabees Swete only); search for other verses joining εὐδοκ- or a choosing/desiring verb with a dwelling word for God's presence (κατοικ-, κατασκην-, οἰκ-, σκην-); in Hebrew, רצה / חפץ / בחר / אוה with ישב / שכן for God's dwelling. Does the tradition strengthen the temple reading of 1:19, or dilute the Ps 68 contact? Verdict.
