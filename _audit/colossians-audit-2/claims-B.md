# Claims file — Auditor B (field and design claims: block tests, chance baselines, rank stability)

Each claim is stated as the proposing reports state it, counts included. Test each.

## B1 — Wisdom 6:22–7:17 as a field partner of Colossians 1:26–2:3 (and 1:29) [queue C16]
- Col 1:26 τὸ μυστήριον τὸ ἀποκεκρυμμένον … νῦν δὲ ἐφανερώθη; 1:27 γνωρίσαι τί τὸ πλοῦτος τῆς δόξης τοῦ μυστηρίου; 1:28 ἐν πάσῃ σοφίᾳ; 1:29 κατὰ τὴν ἐνέργειαν αὐτοῦ; 2:2–3 τοῦ μυστηρίου τοῦ θεοῦ … θησαυροὶ τῆς σοφίας καὶ γνώσεως ἀπόκρυφοι.
- Stated: Wis 6:22 is the only Swete verse joining μυστήρια ("mysteries"), a hiding word (οὐκ ἀποκρύψω, "I will not hide") and σοφία ("wisdom"); it also has γνῶσις ("knowledge"), ἀπ᾽ ἀρχῆς ("from the beginning") and εἰς τὸ ἐμφανές ("into the open"). Wis 7:13–14: "I do not hide her wealth (πλοῦτον) … for she is an unfailing treasure (θησαυρός) for men". ἐνέργεια ("working") at Wis 7:17, 26. The scan ranks Wisdom 6–7 at a median of about 5 against 1:24–29 alone and about 10 against 1:24–2:5.
- Claim: Wisdom 6–7 is a co-candidate field (with Proverbs 2) for 1:26–2:3. Rated **moderate**. No Rahlfs export for Wisdom (Swete only).
- Test: reproduce the counts and ranks (six runs; the tool takes one chapter, so run 1:24–29 and 2:1–5 separately and say so); sense-check every matched word in Wisdom 6 and 7; leave-one-out (without μυστήριον; without σοφία); null passages — how often does a Wisdom chapter rank in the top ten against an arbitrary Pauline passage of similar length that talks about wisdom or knowledge? Compare Proverbs 2, Daniel 2 and Sirach 24 as rivals. Verdict.

## B2 — The adjudication frame 2:16, 18 → 3:15, 16 [C19]
- 2:16 Μὴ οὖν τις ὑμᾶς κρινέτω ("let no one judge you"); 2:18 μηδεὶς ὑμᾶς καταβραβευέτω ("let no one disqualify you"); 3:15 ἡ εἰρήνη τοῦ Χριστοῦ βραβευέτω ("let the peace of Christ rule/umpire"); 3:16 ὁ λόγος τοῦ Χριστοῦ ἐνοικείτω ("let the word of Christ dwell").
- Stated: these are the **only four third-person imperatives in Colossians** (SBLGNT). Two negative, with human adjudicators; two positive, with subjects "of Christ". καταβραβεύω and βραβεύω each occur once in the NT. Claim: a designed frame — the human judges replaced by Christ's peace and word. Rated **moderate–high**.
- Test: verify the count of third-person imperatives in Colossians (parse field). Baseline: in the other Pauline letters, how often do third-person imperatives cluster into a negative pair and a positive pair in one letter? Is "word of Christ dwell" an adjudication term at all (sense)? Do any other Colossians imperatives (second person) carry adjudication vocabulary that would dilute the frame? Verdict.

## B3 — 3:12–16 as the reversal of 2:18–23 [C25]
- Stated shared words, with reversed value: ταπεινοφροσύνη ("self-abasement" 2:18, 23 → "humility" 3:12); σύνδεσμος ("ligament" 2:19 → love "the bond of perfection" 3:14); σῶμα ("body" 2:17, 19, 23 → "called in one body" 3:15); λόγος + σοφία ("word" + "wisdom") in one verse only at 2:23 and 3:16 in the letter; "teachings" (2:22) → "teaching" (3:16); the umpire verbs (B2).
- Stated baseline: counting 2:16–23's content words that occur in at most eight verses of the letter, **3:12–17 shares five** (λόγος, σοφία, σύνδεσμος, σῶμα, ταπεινοφροσύνη), **tied with 2:6–13 and with 1:26–2:4**, against a median of two across six-verse stretches outside the passage. With words in at most four verses, 3:12–17 shares two and other stretches three. So the claim rests on reversed **sense**, not on count. Rated **moderate** as design.
- Test: reproduce the baseline exactly (both thresholds); try other stretch lengths; judge the "reversal" item by item — is each a reversal of value, or just a shared word used in another context? A control: does any other pair of Colossian passages show as many value-reversals among shared words? Verdict.

## B4 — The triple σῶμα (2:17, 19, 23) as deliberate play [C26]
- Stated: Colossians uses σῶμα in eight verses (1:18, 1:22, 1:24, 2:11, 2:17, 2:19, 2:23, 3:15), three in 2:16–23, with σωματικῶς ("bodily") at 2:9. Three senses in sequence: 2:17 τὸ δὲ σῶμα τοῦ Χριστοῦ — the reality that casts the shadow (and a Pauline ear would hear "the body of Christ"); 2:19 πᾶν τὸ σῶμα — the church; 2:23 ἀφειδίᾳ σώματος — the physical body treated harshly, set against σάρξ ("flesh", the passage's last word). σκιά ("shadow") with σῶμα ("body") stands together nowhere in Swete. Claim: the three senses are a deliberate play. Rated **moderate–high**.
- Test: verify counts; baseline — how often does Paul use one noun three times in eight verses in three different senses (pick comparable nouns: σάρξ, νόμος, πνεῦμα, ἄνθρωπος, σῶμα in other letters)? Is the 2:17 sense "substance" ordinary Greek idiom (shadow/body) such that no play is needed? Verdict.

## B5 — Isaiah 49 (the Servant) as the frame of Paul's suffering in Colossians 1:24–29 [C23]
- Stated (from a commentary, Dunn NIGTC): Paul understood his mission in the light of the Servant of Isaiah 49 — Rom 15:21 = Isa 52:15 and 2 Cor 6:2 = Isa 49:8 verbatim; Gal 1:15 shares ἐκ κοιλίας μητρός μου ("from my mother's womb") with Isa 49:1. **Inside Col 1:24–29 only κοπιάω ("labour") is shared** (1:29; Isa 49:4 κενῶς ἐκοπίασα, "I have laboured in vain"). Rated **low–moderate**, background only.
- Test: verify the shared vocabulary between Col 1:24–29 and Isaiah 49 (and 49–53) by stem; the scan rank of Isaiah 49 against 1:24–29 (six runs); null — how many Isaiah chapters share as much with 1:24–29? Paul's other κοπιάω-in-vain uses (Gal 4:11; Phil 2:16; 1 Thess 3:5) and whether they make Isa 49:4 live in his idiom. Verdict.

## B6 — The Maccabean food-and-calendar field as background to Colossians 2:8, 16–23 [C28]
- Stated: the scan for 2:16–23 ranks (field) Wis 14, 2 Macc 7, Isa 1, 4 Macc 5, Lam 4, Isa 29. In the Maccabean books refusing to taste forbidden food and keeping sabbaths and feasts are the marks of fidelity: 2 Macc 6:6 (one could "neither keep the Sabbath nor observe the feasts"); 2 Macc 6:18–20; 4 Macc 5:2, 6 (Eleazar refuses pork); 2 Macc 7:1 (forced to "touch", ἐφάπτεσθαι, swine's flesh). **4 Macc 5:6 is the only Swete verse with θρησκεία ("religion, worship", Col 2:18) and a "philosophy" word (φιλοσοφ-, Col 2:8)**. Claim: a first-century Jewish reader could hear food abstention and calendar-keeping called "philosophy" and "religion" in Colossians' own terms; background, not allusion. Rated **moderate (background)**. Swete only for the Maccabees.
- Test: reproduce the scan ranks for 2:16–23 (six runs) and sense-check 2 Macc 6–7 and 4 Macc 5; verify the 4 Macc 5:6 count (θρησκεία anywhere in Swete; φιλοσοφ- anywhere; both together); null — do the Maccabean books rank this high against *any* NT passage about food or law (e.g. Rom 14:1–6; 1 Cor 8:1–8; Gal 4:8–11; Mark 7:1–15) — i.e. is this a Colossae-specific background or the default field of any food dispute? Verdict.
