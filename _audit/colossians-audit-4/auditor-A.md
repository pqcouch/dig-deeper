# Claim audit 4 — Colossians — Auditor A

**Scope.** Six single-contact claims (A1–A6) about the Old Testament and Jewish Greek background of Colossians 1:12–14; 3:3–4; 3:12; 3:22–4:1. **Method.** I worked only from the primary texts: Swete's LXX (the observation layer for the whole Greek Old Testament, searched without accents, with every hit read for sense and verbs searched with the stem anywhere in the word so that augmented and reduplicated forms are caught); the Rahlfs LXX export (the citation of record for Job, Deuteronomy, Exodus, Psalms, Esther and Daniel, wherever a finding rests on wording); the WLC Hebrew with its lemma index; the SBLGNT with its lemma index (all New Testament counts are by lemma); and the NA28 text and apparatus of Colossians. Where a finding rests on Wisdom, Sirach, Tobit or Maccabees, Swete is the only Greek text available and I say so. Window ranks come from the corpus's six-run scan of each Colossian passage against all 1,259 Swete chapters (whole chapters, 8-verse and 16-verse windows, each scored on all shared words and on rarer words only). Knowledge from memory is marked [S] and carries no verdict.

**Colossian text.** NA28 and SBLGNT agree at every word tested here. Variants that touch the claims: 1:12 ὑμᾶς ('you', NA28 text) against ἡμᾶς ('us', A C D and the majority); 3:22 τὸν κύριον ('the Lord', NA28 text) against τὸν θεόν ('God', 𝔓⁴⁶ א² D² and the majority); 3:24 ἀπολήμψεσθε ('you will receive') against λήμψεσθε; 4:1 οὐρανῷ ('heaven', singular, NA28 text with א* A B C) against οὐρανοῖς (plural, א² D F G and the majority — very probably assimilation to Eph 6:9). None of these moves a verdict.

## A1 — Job 31:13–15 behind Colossians 4:1

### What I tested
The Greek of Job 31:13–15 in Swete and Rahlfs; the Hebrew in the WLC; shared lemmas with Col 4:1; the rarity of ἰσότης ('equality, fairness'); rival texts in which a master is answerable to God for a slave; and the rank of Job 31 against Col 4:1 in the six-run scan.

### Results
**The Greek of Job 31:13–15** (Rahlfs; Swete identical in substance, ποιῆται for ποιήσηται at 31:14):
- 31:13 εἰ δὲ καὶ ἐφαύλισα κρίμα θεράποντός μου ἢ θεραπαίνης κρινομένων αὐτῶν πρός με ('if I made light of the cause of my servant or maidservant when they were contending with me');
- 31:14 τί γὰρ ποιήσω, ἐὰν ἔτασίν μου ποιήσηται ὁ κύριος; ἐὰν δὲ καὶ ἐπισκοπήν, τίνα ἀπόκρισιν ποιήσομαι; ('what shall I do if the Lord makes examination of me? and if a visitation, what answer shall I make?');
- 31:15 πότερον οὐχ ὡς καὶ ἐγὼ ἐγενόμην ἐν γαστρί, καὶ ἐκεῖνοι γεγόνασιν; γεγόναμεν δὲ ἐν τῇ αὐτῇ κοιλίᾳ ('did not they come to be just as I came to be in the womb? we came to be in the same belly').

**"One Maker" is not in the Greek.** The Hebrew reads הֲלֹא־בַבֶּטֶן עֹשֵׂנִי עָשָׂהוּ וַיְכֻנֶנּוּ בָּרֶחֶם אֶחָד ('did not he who made me in the womb make him, and did not One fashion us in the womb?'), with עָשָׂה ('make') twice and אֶחָד ('one'). The Greek has turned the shared Creator into a shared *birth*: γίνομαι ('come to be') three times and no verb of making, no subject God, and "the same belly" where the Hebrew has "one" (whether אֶחָד qualifies God or the womb). A reader of the Greek finds an argument from common humanity, not from one Maker. The Greek does, however, give 31:14 a subject ὁ κύριος ('the Lord') where the Hebrew has אֵל ('God') — the single word it shares with Col 4:1.

**Shared vocabulary with Col 4:1.** Col 4:1 has κύριος ('lord/master', twice), δίκαιος ('just'), ἰσότης ('fairness'), δοῦλος ('slave'), παρέχω ('grant'), οἶδα ('know'), ἔχω ('have'), οὐρανός ('heaven'). Job 31:13–15 Greek shares only κύριος. The slave words differ (θεράπων 'attendant', θεράπαινα 'maidservant' against δοῦλος); the justice word differs (κρίμα 'cause, judgement' against τὸ δίκαιον 'what is just'); there is no fairness word and no heaven.

**ἰσότης ('fairness') in Swete:** two verses only, Job 36:29 and Zech 4:7, neither of them ethical (the "evenness" of God's tent; a stone "of equality"). Adding the Psalms of Solomon gives one ethical use, Pss. Sol. 17:46 (the messianic king "will lead them all in equality"). In the New Testament: 2 Cor 8:13, 14 and Col 4:1. Ethical ἰσότης, and the pairing τὸ δίκαιον καὶ τὸ ἴσον ('what is just and equal'), belongs to Hellenistic moral vocabulary [S: Aristotle and Philo use the pair], not to the Greek Bible. Positive control: the search returns Col 4:1 and 2 Cor 8:13–14.

**Window rank.** Against Col 4:1, Job 31 ranks 76th to 117th of 1,259 chapters across the six runs (median 84), on δίκαιος, κύριος and οἶδα — and the δίκαιος and οἶδα hits in Job 31 are other senses. Lev 25 ranks 180–328. The top-ranked chapters (Job 36, Zech 4) owe their place entirely to non-ethical ἰσότης: the scan has no signal for this verse.

**How common is the "master answerable to God" idea in Swete?** Not rare. Read for sense, it appears at least in: Lev 25:42–43 (διότι οἰκέται μού εἰσιν οὗτοι … οὐ κατατενεῖς αὐτὸν ἐν τῷ μόχθῳ, καὶ φοβηθήσῃ Κύριον τὸν θεόν σου — 'for they are my servants … you shall not oppress him with toil, and you shall fear the Lord your God'); Lev 25:55; Deut 15:15 and 24:18 (remember you were a slave and the Lord redeemed you); Jer 41:8–22 in Swete's numbering (MT 34, the Lord's judgement on masters who re-enslaved their released slaves); Job 31:13–15; and, more generally, Prov 22:2 (πλούσιος καὶ πτωχὸς συνήντησαν ἀλλήλοις, ἀμφοτέρους δὲ ὁ κύριος ἐποίησεν — 'rich and poor meet; the Lord made them both') and Prov 29:13 (ἐπισκοπὴν ἀμφοτέρων ποιεῖται ὁ κύριος — 'the Lord makes a visitation of both', sharing ἐπισκοπή 'visitation' with Job 31:14). Mal 2:10 (οὐχὶ θεὸς εἷς ἔκτισεν ὑμᾶς; 'did not one God create you?') states the "one Maker" in Greek, which Job 31:15 does not. Sirach's slave sayings (Swete 30:33–40 = Rahlfs 33:25–33; the claim's "33:31" is Swete 30:39) and Sir 7:20–21 are prudential, with no appeal to God's oversight (Sirach is Swete only).

**The nearest text of all is Eph 6:9**, which shares οἱ κύριοι ('masters'), εἰδότες ὅτι ('knowing that'), καὶ … ὑμῶν ὁ κύριός ἐστιν ἐν οὐρανοῖς ('your Lord too is in heaven'), and the impartiality clause (προσωπολημψία, 'partiality', which Colossians places at 3:25). That clause has its own OT root in Deut 10:17 and 2 Chr 19:7 (God οὐ θαυμάζει πρόσωπον, 'does not regard faces'), a root Job 31 lacks.

**A Swete/Rahlfs divergence worth noting for any Job 31 work:** at 31:23 Rahlfs reads φόβος γὰρ κυρίου συνέσχεν με ('fear of the Lord gripped me'), Swete φόβος γὰρ συνέσχεν με without κυρίου.

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Job in Greek was available |

### Verdict
**Needs reframing — recommended rating: low.** Job 31:13–15 is a real *thematic* parallel, and in the Hebrew the strongest Old Testament statement that master and slave share one Maker. But the proposed "one Maker" link depends on the Hebrew, which the Greek has dropped, and the Greek shares a single word with Col 4:1. It is not closer than Lev 25:42–43, which in Greek has κύριος with the master's duty to "fear the Lord" (a motif Col 3:22 applies to slaves). File it as "illustrative parallel (Hebrew)", not as a source.

## A2 — 1 Kingdoms 12:24 (12:14) and Psalm 18:8–14 LXX behind Colossians 3:22–24

### What I tested
Fear + serve + heart in Swete, with both Greek verbs for "serve" (δουλεύω and λατρεύω); every Swete verse with ἀνταπόδοσις ('recompense') and its sense; the claimed "only" for ἀνταπόδοσις + δοῦλος ('servant') + fear within four verses, with a positive control and a baseline; and the New Testament use of the word family.

### Results
**The formula.** Verses joining a fear word with δουλεύω ('serve as a slave') and καρδία ('heart'): 1 Rgns 12:24 (πλὴν φοβεῖσθε τὸν κύριον καὶ δουλεύσατε αὐτῷ ἐν ἀληθείᾳ καὶ ἐν ὅλῃ καρδίᾳ ὑμῶν, 'only fear the Lord and serve him in truth with all your heart') and 1 Rgns 12:20, where the fear word is "do not fear" but the clause δουλεύσατε τῷ κυρίῳ ἐν ὅλῃ καρδίᾳ ὑμῶν ('serve the Lord with all your heart') is present (Judith 11:1 is a false hit). 1 Rgns 12:14 has fear + δουλεύω without heart. Other God-directed fear + δουλεύω verses: Ps 2:11 (δουλεύσατε τῷ κυρίῳ ἐν φόβῳ, 'serve the Lord in fear') and 4 Rgns 17:41 (fearing the Lord while serving idols). With λατρεύω ('serve in worship'): Deut 6:13; 10:12; 10:20; Josh 24:14; 4 Rgns 17:33, 35. Fear + λατρεύω + heart in one verse: Deut 10:12 only — and Deut 10:12 also has ψυχή ('soul'): ἐξ ὅλης τῆς καρδίας σου καὶ ἐξ ὅλης τῆς ψυχῆς σου ('with all your heart and all your soul'), matching Col 3:22–23's καρδία and ἐκ ψυχῆς ('from the soul, heartily'). So the formula is **Deuteronomic covenant idiom**, which 1 Kingdoms 12 inherits; 1 Rgns 12 has the advantage of δουλεύω (the verb of Col 3:24), Deut 10:12 the advantage of ψυχή. Neither has Colossians' own heart phrase, ἐν ἁπλότητι καρδίας ('in singleness of heart'), whose Swete parallels are 1 Chr 29:17 and Wis 1:1 (with the bare noun at 2 Rgns 15:11; 1 Macc 2:37, 60; 3 Macc 3:21; Sus 60).

**ἀνταπόδοσις in Swete:** 14 verses for the noun (Judg 9:16; 16:28; 2 Rgns 19:36; Ps 18:12; 68:23; 90:8; 93:2; Hos 9:7; Isa 34:8; 59:18; 61:2; 63:4; 66:6; 1 En 22:11); the stem also picks up the imperative ἀνταπόδος at Ps 118:17. **Sense:** only two are plainly a *reward* — 2 Rgns 19:36 (Barzillai: "why should the king repay me with this recompense?", spoken by "your servant", ὁ δοῦλός σου) and Ps 18:12 (ἐν τῷ φυλάσσειν αὐτὰ ἀνταπόδοσις πολλή, 'in keeping them there is great reward'); Judg 9:16 is neutral requital; the other eleven are retribution ("the day of recompense", "repay recompense to the proud"). Ps 18:12 Hebrew (WLC Ps 19:12) is עֵקֶב רָב ('great consequence/reward').

**The claimed "only" holds.** ἀνταπόδοσις with a δοῦλος-family word and a φόβος/φοβέομαι-family word ('fear') within four verses: Ps 18:10–14 only (φόβος Κυρίου 18:10; ὁ δοῦλός σου and ἀνταπόδοσις 18:12; τοῦ δούλου σου 18:14). The result is unchanged when the whole ἀνταποδ- family (verbs included) is searched, and the Psalms of Solomon add nothing. Positive control: the search returns Ps 18.

**But the "only" is not evidence.** In Swete as a whole, 648 of 30,201 verses (2.1 %) have a δοῦλος-family word and a fear word within four verses either side. Among the 15 ἀνταποδοσ- verses the expected number is about 0.3, so finding one is what chance gives roughly one time in four. The rarity is the rarity of ἀνταπόδοσις, not of the combination.

**Window ranks against Col 3:22–24:** Ps 18 ranks 19th to 100th (median about 58); 1 Rgns 12 ranks 15th to 99th (median about 34); Deut 10 ranks 34th to 153rd. The chapter at the top is **2 Rgns 19** (median rank 5), a narrative of a king rewarding loyal servants (see New candidates).

**New Testament.** ἀνταπόδοσις: Col 3:24 only (lemma search; positive control returns Col 3:24). But the family is Paul's ordinary vocabulary: ἀνταποδίδωμι ('repay') in Rom 11:35; 12:19; 1 Thess 3:9; 2 Thess 1:6 (with Luke 14:14; Heb 10:30); ἀνταπόδομα ('repayment') in Rom 11:9 and Luke 14:12. Strikingly, Rom 11:9 quotes Ps 68:23 LXX, which in both Swete and Rahlfs reads εἰς ἀνταπόδοσιν, but Paul writes εἰς ἀνταπόδομα — so Paul does not hold to the -σις form even when citing a psalm that has it. Col 3:24–25 then balances reward (3:24) with retribution (ὁ ἀδικῶν κομίσεται ὃ ἠδίκησεν, 'the wrongdoer will be paid back for the wrong', 3:25), which is exactly the word's two-sided LXX range; that range is better explained by the word than by one psalm.

### Verdict
**(i) Confirmed with nuance — moderate, as a formula.** The fear-and-serve-with-the-whole-heart formula is real and stands behind 3:22–24, but it is Deuteronomic (Deut 10:12, with heart *and* soul) as much as Samuel's; 1 Rgns 12:24 should not be named as the single source. **(ii) Needs reframing — low** (lower than proposed). The "only Swete passage" statement is reproducible, but a 2.1 % baseline makes it a chance result; ἀνταπόδοσις is explained by Paul's own word family and by its two-sided LXX range, which Col 3:24–25 uses.

## A3 — "The lot of the holy ones in light" (Colossians 1:12)

### What I tested
κλῆρος ('lot, share') with ἁγι- words in one Swete verse; the stock status of μερίς ('portion') with κλῆρος; the Acts 26:18 overlap, reproduced by lemma and set against a chance baseline; Daniel 7 in both Greek texts; the referent of οἱ ἅγιοι ('the holy ones') in Wis 5:5, Daniel 7 and Colossians; and a WLC check on the Hebrew pair "lot" + "holy".

### Results
**(a) κλῆρος with ἅγιος in one verse (Swete):** three verses with κλῆρος proper — 1 Chr 24:5 (priests divided "by lots", "rulers of the holy things"), Neh 11:1 (lots cast to settle "the holy city") and Wis 5:5 (πῶς κατελογίσθη ἐν υἱοῖς θεοῦ, καὶ ἐν ἁγίοις ὁ κλῆρος αὐτοῦ ἐστιν; 'how was he counted among the sons of God, and his lot is among the holy ones?'). Widening the search to any κληρ- word except κληρονομ- adds three false hits (σκληρύνω 'harden'; 2 Macc 6:30; 1 En 1:9). **The claim holds: Wis 5:5 is the only Swete verse that puts someone's κλῆρος among "the holy ones".** In Wis 5:5 the holy ones stand in parallel with υἱοὶ θεοῦ ('sons of God'); three of the other four Swete uses of that phrase (Deut 32:43; Ps 28:1; 88:7) are heavenly beings (the fourth, Hos 1:10, is Israel), so the most natural reading of Wis 5:5 is the righteous man's vindication into the company of angels [S: most commentators on Wisdom read it so].

**A rival the claim missed — Ps 15:3–6 LXX (Ps 16 MT).** 15:3 τοῖς ἁγίοις τοῖς ἐν τῇ γῇ αὐτοῦ ('to the holy ones who are in his land'); 15:5 Κύριος ἡ μερὶς τῆς κληρονομίας μου ('the Lord is the portion of my inheritance'). This is **the only Swete verse in which μερίς governs a genitive κληρ- noun** — the very construction of Col 1:12, εἰς τὴν μερίδα τοῦ κλήρου ('for the portion of the lot'). In the Hebrew (WLC Ps 16:3, 5), "holy ones" (קְדוֹשִׁים), "portion" (חֵלֶק) and "lot" (גּוֹרָל) stand within three verses — the only place in the Hebrew Bible where גּוֹרָל ('lot') and קָדוֹשׁ ('holy') fall within three verses of each other at all (no verse has both). Here the holy ones are "in the land", that is, human. Ps 15 LXX has κληρονομία ('inheritance') where Colossians has κλῆρος, so the contact is structural rather than verbal.

**μερίς + κλῆρος as a stock pair.** The claim's figure of 20 verses does not reproduce on any definition I can find. Strictly (both nouns, verb μερίζω excluded): **11 Swete verses** — Deut 10:9; 12:12; 14:26; 14:28; 18:1; Josh 18:6; 19:9; Wis 2:9; Esth 4:17 (Addition C); Isa 57:6; Jer 13:25 — of which eight coordinate the two as a fixed doublet (μερὶς καὶ κλῆρος, mostly the Levites' "no portion or lot"). Counting κληρονομία as well gives **29 verses**. In the New Testament the doublet appears once outside Colossians: Acts 8:21 (οὐκ ἔστιν σοι μερὶς οὐδὲ κλῆρος, 'you have no portion or lot'). So the pairing is idiomatic Septuagintal (Deuteronomic) Greek, and the *genitive* form of Col 1:12 is the rarer thing (Ps 15:5 only).

**(b) Daniel 7.** Old Greek 7:18 καὶ παραλήψονται τὴν βασιλείαν ἅγιοι Ὑψίστου ('the holy ones of the Most High shall receive the kingdom'); 7:22 τὸ βασίλειον κατέσχον οἱ ἅγιοι; 7:27 ἔδωκε λαῷ ἁγίῳ ὑψίστῳ ('he gave it to a holy people, to the Most High'). Theodotion matches closely and at 7:26 has τὴν ἀρχὴν μεταστήσουσιν ('they shall remove his dominion') — the verb μεθίστημι of Col 1:13 ('transferred'). Shared with Col 1:12–13: ἅγιοι, βασιλεία ('kingdom'), ἐξουσία ('authority', OG 7:14, 27); Theodotion adds μεθίστημι. There is no κλῆρος or μερίς. Daniel 7's six-run rank against Col 1:12–14 is unremarkable (OG 171–266; Theodotion 107–247 of 1,259). Notably, the Old Greek itself identifies the holy ones as a *people* (λαῷ ἁγίῳ, 7:27).

**(c) Acts 26:18.** Lemma overlap with Col 1:12–14 (content words only): κλῆρος, σκότος ('darkness'), φῶς ('light'), ἐξουσία, ἄφεσις ('forgiveness'), ἁμαρτία ('sin') — **six, as stated**; seven if ἡγιασμένοις ('sanctified', from ἁγιάζω) is credited to ἅγιος. **The claim's "next NT rival has four" does not reproduce: the next best single verse has three** (a dozen verses tie, e.g. 1 Thess 5:5; 1 Pet 2:9; Acts 2:38), and on rarer lemmas only (New Testament frequency of 200 or less) the next best has two. **Baseline:** for 298 random three-verse passages from Paul's letters, the best single-verse match anywhere else in the New Testament on rarer lemmas reached six or more in 4 cases (1.3 %); the typical best was three. Acts 26:18 therefore beats the null comfortably. Its sense matches too: a commission to turn people "from darkness to light and from the authority of Satan to God", to receive "forgiveness of sins and a lot among the sanctified". The Lukan Benedictus is the other New Testament text in this orbit: across the whole New Testament, ῥύομαι ('rescue') and a λυτρ- word ('redeem') fall within twelve verses of each other only in Luke 1:68–80 and Col 1:13–14, and both passages add σκότος (Luke 1:79).

**(d) Saints or angels.** Plural ἅγιος occurs six times in Colossians: 1:2, 1:4, 1:12, 1:22, 1:26, 3:12. Substantival: 1:2 (the addressees), 1:4 (love for "all the saints"), 1:12 (disputed), 1:26 (the mystery "now manifested to his saints", explained in 1:27 as those to whom God chose to make it known). Adjectival of believers: 1:22 ("holy and blameless"), 3:12 ("holy and beloved"). **The claim holds: all five other plurals refer to believers.** Across Paul's other letters plural ἅγιοι is overwhelmingly believers, with the debated exceptions 1 Thess 3:13 and 2 Thess 1:10 [S], and Eph 1:18 (τῆς κληρονομίας αὐτοῦ ἐν τοῖς ἁγίοις, 'his inheritance among the holy ones'), the closest Pauline-tradition parallel to Col 1:12 and itself debated [S]. The Qumran phrase "the lot of the holy ones" (1QS 11:7–8, where it stands beside "the sons of heaven") [S] and Wis 5:5 favour angels; Ps 15:3 LXX, OG Dan 7:27 and Acts 26:18 favour humans.

### Hays table
| Sub-claim | Avail. | Volume | Recur. | Them. coh. | Hist. plaus. | Hist. interp. [S] | Satisf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Wis 5:5 | Strong | Possible (κλῆρος + ἅγιοι; no μερίς, no light) | Weak | Possible | Possible | Possible (often cited with Qumran) | Possible |
| (b) Dan 7 OG/Th | Strong | Weak (ἅγιοι, βασιλεία, ἐξουσία; no lot) | Possible (kingdom given to the holy ones) | Possible | Strong | Possible | Possible |

### Verdict
**(a) Wis 5:5 — Confirmed with nuance, low–moderate.** The "only Swete verse" is exact, but Wis 5:5's holy ones are probably angels, and Ps 15:3–5 LXX equals it as a background, with the better construction. **(b) Daniel 7 — Uncertain, low–moderate:** background for "kingdom" and "authority" in 1:13 rather than for the "lot" of 1:12. **(c) Acts 26:18 — Confirmed, moderate–high,** as a shared conversion idiom (Pauline and Lukan); the corrected baseline makes it stronger than stated. **(d) Saints, not angels — Confirmed with nuance, moderate–high:** the letter's own usage is decisive enough to prefer believers, but "in the light" and the Qumran and Wisdom parallels keep "a share with the holy ones (angels included)" a live minority reading. The stated count for the stock pair should be corrected from 20 to 11 (or 29 with κληρονομία).

## A4 — Exodus 6:6–8 behind Colossians 1:12–14

### What I tested
ῥύομαι ('rescue') with λυτρόω ('redeem') in one Swete verse, searched with the stem anywhere in the word (so that ἐρρύσατο, ἐλυτρώσατο, ἐλυτρώσω are caught) and every hit read; κλῆρος ('lot') at Exod 6:8 in Swete, Rahlfs and the Hebrew; rivals; ἀπολύτρωσις against λυτρόω; the scan's view of Exodus 6; and whether "darkness" and "transferred" fit an exodus reading.

### Results
**Count corrected: four verses, not three.** ῥύομαι with λυτρόω in one verse (Swete): Exod 6:6 (ῥύσομαι ὑμᾶς ἐκ τῆς δουλείας, καὶ λυτρώσομαι ὑμᾶς, 'I will rescue you from slavery and redeem you'); Ps 68:19 (λύτρωσαι … ῥῦσαί με); Hos 13:14 (ἐκ χειρὸς ᾅδου ῥύσομαι καὶ ἐκ θανάτου λυτρώσομαι, 'from the hand of Hades I will rescue, from death I will redeem'); and **Old Greek Dan 3:88** (ἐρρύσατο ἡμᾶς ἐκ μέσου καιομένης φλογός, καὶ ἐκ τοῦ πυρὸς ἐλυτρώσατο ἡμᾶς, 'he rescued us from the burning flame and redeemed us from the fire'), confirmed in the Rahlfs Old Greek; Theodotion's 3:88 has ἐρρύσατο twice and no λυτρόω. The lumped Swete "verse" Esth 4:17 (the whole of Addition C) also contains both words, but in different lettered verses (Rahlfs 4:17g ἐλυτρώσω; ῥῦσαι later), so it is not a one-verse co-occurrence. Positive controls: the search returns Exod 6:6 and Col 1:13's ἐρρύσατο in the New Testament. Under independence the two stems (214 and 128 Swete verses) would share about one verse; four is a modest collocation, the ordinary Greek rendering of Hebrew parallelism of "deliver"/"redeem".

**"Only Exodus 6 adds κλῆρος" holds**, in the form: one of the four verses has κλῆρος within two verses — Exod 6:6–8 (6:8 δώσω ὑμῖν αὐτὴν ἐν κλήρῳ, 'I will give it to you as a lot'; identical in Rahlfs, though Rahlfs reads εἰσάξω 'bring in' where Swete has ἐξάξω at the start of 6:8). The Hebrew of 6:8 has מוֹרָשָׁה ('possession'), not גּוֹרָל or נַחֲלָה: κλῆρος is the translator's choice. In 6:6 the Hebrew verbs are הִצַּלְתִּי ('I will deliver') and גָאַלְתִּי ('I will redeem').

**The scan cannot see it — confirmed.** Against Col 1:12–14, Exod 6 ranks 316th to 542nd of 1,259 chapters in all six runs, because ἀπολύτρωσις does not begin with λυτρ- and the augmented ῥύομαι forms are missed; and the scan's one "μερ-" hit in Exodus 6 is the proper name Merari, a false match.

**ἀπολύτρωσις against λυτρόω.** The noun of Col 1:14 is almost absent from the Greek Old Testament: one Swete verse, Old Greek Dan 4:30 (an expansion: "the time of my redemption came", of Nebuchadnezzar). The stem search also finds Exod 21:8, the verb ἀπολυτρόω (a slave-girl's redemption). In the New Testament ἀπολύτρωσις is a Pauline word (Rom 3:24; 8:23; 1 Cor 1:30; Eph 1:7, 14; 4:30; Col 1:14, plus Luke 21:28; Heb 9:15; 11:35). So the volume between Col 1:14 and Exod 6:6 is cognate, not identical.

**Rivals (read for sense):**
- **The Exodus ῥύομαι chain itself.** Exod 5:23 (οὐκ ἐρρύσω τὸν λαόν σου, 'you have not rescued your people') → 6:6 (promise) → 12:27 (Passover: τοὺς δὲ οἴκους ἡμῶν ἐρρύσατο, 'he rescued our houses') → 14:30 (καὶ ἐρρύσατο Κύριος τὸν Ἰσραήλ … ἐκ χειρὸς τῶν Αἰγυπτίων, 'and the Lord rescued Israel from the hand of the Egyptians'). Exod 14:30 has the exact aorist of Col 1:13, ἐρρύσατο. The claim is better framed as the Exodus rescue narrative, with 6:6–8 as its programmatic promise.
- **Deut 9:26–29** (new, and strong): μὴ ἐξολεθρεύσῃς τὸν λαόν σου καὶ τὴν μερίδα σου ἣν ἐλυτρώσω ('do not destroy your people and your portion, whom you redeemed', 9:26) … οὗτοι λαός σου καὶ κλῆρός σου ('these are your people and your lot', 9:29), confirmed in Rahlfs. The Greek has rendered one Hebrew word, נַחֲלָה ('inheritance'), as μερίς at 9:26 and κλῆρος at 9:29, so the pairing is the translator's. **It is the only Swete passage in which μερίς, κλῆρος and λυτρόω fall within four verses with the sense of the exodus** (Num 18:15–25, the other window, is about redemption-money for firstborn and the Levites' portion). Rahlfs Esth 4:17g–h echoes it: μὴ ὑπερίδῃς τὴν μερίδα σου, ἣν σεαυτῷ ἐλυτρώσω ἐκ γῆς Αἰγύπτου … ἱλάσθητι τῷ κλήρῳ σου ('do not overlook your portion, which you redeemed for yourself from Egypt … be merciful to your lot'). Col 1:12–14 has all three ideas (μερίς, κλῆρος, ἀπολύτρωσις) — but inverts them: in Deuteronomy Israel *is* God's portion and lot; in Colossians believers are given a portion *of* the lot.
- **Ps 106 LXX (107 MT).** οἱ λελυτρωμένοι ὑπὸ Κυρίου ('those redeemed by the Lord', 106:2); ἐρρύσατο αὐτούς (106:6, 20); καθημένους ἐν σκότει ('sitting in darkness', 106:10); ἐξήγαγεν αὐτοὺς ἐκ σκότους ('he brought them out of darkness', 106:14). This and Ps 68 LXX are **the only Swete passages where ῥύομαι, a λυτρ- word and σκότος fall within eight verses** (and Ps 68's darkness is only the enemies' darkened eyes, 68:24) (at sixteen verses add OG Dan 3, the Odes copy, and Ps 68:15). Ps 106 is the best Swete match for "rescued … out of darkness" combined with redemption — though it is not itself an exodus psalm in the narrow sense.
- Deut 7:8 and 13:5 (ἐλυτρώσατο ἐξ οἴκου δουλείας, 'redeemed from the house of slavery'); Exod 15:13 (τὸν λαόν σου τοῦτον ὃν ἐλυτρώσω … εἰς κατάλυμα ἅγιόν σου, 'this people whom you redeemed … into your holy dwelling'); Ps 105:10; 135:24 — all λυτρόω of the exodus without ῥύομαι.

**"Darkness" and "transferred".** σκότος is not exodus vocabulary in the narrow sense except for the ninth plague (Exod 10:21–23; the Wisdom 17–18 treatment is Auditor B's claim and I do not judge it). μεθίστημι ('transfer, remove') is never used of the exodus in Swete; its nearest theological uses are 4 Rgns 17:23 (μετέστησεν Κύριος τὸν Ἰσραήλ ἀπὸ προσώπου αὐτοῦ, 'the Lord removed Israel from his presence' — exile, the reverse direction), OG Dan 2:21 (μεθιστῶν βασιλεῖς, 'removing kings') and Theodotion Dan 7:26 (removing the beast's dominion before the kingdom passes to the holy ones). "Transferred into the kingdom" therefore sits better with Daniel 7 than with Exodus; "rescued from the authority of darkness" sits best with Ps 106 LXX and with the Isaianic darkness-to-light texts.

Name the background as "the exodus redemption (Exod 6:6–8; 14:30; Deut 9:26–29)", not Exod 6 alone.

## A5 — Deuteronomy 29:29 (Swete; 29:28 in Hebrew and Rahlfs) behind Colossians 3:3–4

### What I tested
The verse number in each edition; κρυ- words ('hide') with φανερ- words ('manifest') in one Swete verse, with the stem anywhere in the word, and a positive control for reduplicated and augmented forms; rivals using other "reveal" verbs; the New Testament pairs by lemma; and which pair is closest in sense to Col 3:3–4.

### Results
**Numbering.** Swete numbers the verse **Deut 29:29** (as the English versions do); Rahlfs and the WLC number it **29:28**. The editions also differ in person: Rahlfs τὰ κρυπτὰ κυρίῳ τῷ θεῷ ἡμῶν, τὰ δὲ φανερὰ ἡμῖν ('the hidden things belong to the Lord our God, the manifest things to us'); Swete ὑμῶν/ὑμῖν ('your … to you'). The Hebrew is הַנִּסְתָּרֹת … וְהַנִּגְלֹת ('the hidden things … the revealed things'), with the famous extraordinary points over "to us and to our children".

**The claimed "only" fails.** κρυ- with φανερ- in one Swete verse: **three verses** — Deut 29:29; **2 Macc 12:41** (εὐλογήσαντες τὰ τοῦ δικαιοκρίτου κυρίου τοῦ τὰ κεκρυμμένα φανερὰ ποιοῦντος, 'blessing the ways of the Lord, the righteous judge, who makes the hidden things manifest'); and 2 Macc 1:33, where φανερόν ('it became known') and ἔκρυψαν ('they had hidden' the fire) are not a pair. 2 Macc 12:41 is found only because its perfect κεκρυμμένα ('hidden') is caught with the stem anywhere — exactly the reduplicated form the claim's floor caveat warned of. The corpus scan likewise credits 2 Macc 12 with φανερ- only and misses κεκρυμμένα. Positive controls: the stem search returns Gen 3:8 and 3:10 (ἐκρύβησαν, ἐκρύβην — augmented aorists), Ps 18:13 (κρυφίων) and Isa 45:3 (ἀποκρύφους). (2 Maccabees is Swete only; there is no Rahlfs export for it.) So there are **two genuine "hidden/manifest" pairs in Swete**, and the second is closer in sense to Paul: God *making* hidden things manifest, as judge.

**φανερόω ('make manifest, appear') is almost absent from Swete**: two verses (Jer 40:6 Swete numbering; 2 Macc 3:28). The whole φανερ- stem has only 22 Swete verses. The rarity of the pair is the rarity of the word family in Greek-Jewish scripture, not a sign of a link; the verb Col 3:4 uses twice is Christian vocabulary.

**Rivals with other "reveal" words (Swete):** Wis 7:21 (ὅσα τέ ἐστιν κρυπτὰ καὶ ἐμφανῆ ἔγνων, 'all things hidden and manifest I learned'); Sir 1:30 and 4:18 (ἀποκαλύψει … τὰ κρυπτά, 'he will reveal the hidden things' — Sirach is Swete only); Theodotion Dan 2:22 (αὐτὸς ἀποκαλύπτει βαθέα καὶ ἀπόκρυφα, 'he reveals deep and hidden things'); Isa 22:9; Tob 12:7, 11 (μυστήριον βασιλέως καλὸν κρύψαι, τὰ δὲ ἔργα τοῦ θεοῦ ἀνακαλύπτειν, 'it is good to hide a king's mystery, but to reveal the works of God' — Tobit is Swete only). Window ranks against Col 3:3–4: Deut 29 ranks 1st to 4th in all six runs (median 2); 2 Macc 12 ranks 21st to 133rd because the scan misses its κρυ- word. Col 3:3–4 has only seven content stems, so any chapter carrying a rare pair rises to the top: the rank shows the rarity of the pair, not a link.

**New Testament, by lemma (κρύπτω and its compounds, κρυπτός, ἀπόκρυφος, κρυφαῖος with φανερός, φανερόω, φανερῶς, φανέρωσις):** in one verse — Mark 4:22; Luke 8:17; John 7:4; 7:10; 1 Cor 4:5; 14:25; 2 Cor 4:2; **Col 1:26**; within two verses add Rom 2:28–29, Eph 5:11–13 and **Col 3:3–4** itself. Colossians has three κρυ- words: ἀποκρύπτω 1:26, ἀπόκρυφος 2:3, κρύπτω 3:3.

**Which pair is Col 3:3–4 closest to?**
- Deut 29:28/29 is a *division of knowledge*: hidden things are God's business; revealed things (the law) are Israel's, to do. Nothing hidden is later revealed.
- Mark 4:22 / Luke 8:17 is a *temporal* "hidden now, manifest later", but about the word or the kingdom, not believers' life.
- **1 Cor 4:5** is temporal and eschatological — "until the Lord comes, who will bring to light the hidden things of darkness and make manifest (φανερώσει) the counsels of hearts" — and has φανερόω, as Col 3:4 does.
- **Col 1:26**, in the same letter: τὸ μυστήριον τὸ ἀποκεκρυμμένον … νῦν δὲ ἐφανερώθη ('the mystery hidden … now made manifest'), Paul's own hidden-then-revealed pattern [S: the "revelation schema" identified by N. A. Dahl and others].
- 1 John 3:2 [S: not searched as a κρυ- pair, since it lacks one] is the closest *content* parallel — when he appears (ἐὰν φανερωθῇ) we shall be like him — on believers' future identity revealed at the appearing.

Col 3:3–4 is closest to Paul's own schema (1:26; 1 Cor 4:5), then to the Jesus saying, and least to Deuteronomy, whose pair it would have to *invert* to use.

More decisively, Paul's own pattern in the same letter (Col 1:26) and in 1 Cor 4:5 explains 3:3–4 without Deuteronomy. Keep Deut 29:28 (Rahlfs and Hebrew numbering; 29:29 in Swete and EVV) at most as a contrastive note.

## A6 — Deuteronomy 7:6–8 (with 4:37; 10:15) behind "chosen of God, holy and beloved" (Colossians 3:12)

### What I tested
Election words (ἐκλεκτός, ἐκλέγομαι including the augmented ἐξελέξατο, προαιρέομαι 'choose beforehand') with ἁγι- ('holy') and ἀγαπ- ('love') words within three verses in Swete, every hit read for sense; election with love in one verse; the participle ἠγαπημένος ('beloved'); the idolatry link in Deuteronomy 7; and the New Testament triad by lemma, including Paul's own election vocabulary.

### Results
**Positive control for augmented forms:** the search returns ἐξελέξατο at Deut 7:7, 4:37, 10:15 and Sir 45:4, and προείλατο at Deut 7:6, 7:7 and 10:15.

**Election + holy + love in one Swete verse:** none. **Election + love in one verse:** Deut 4:37; 10:15; Ps 46:5; 77:68; Wis 3:9; Sir 47:22; Isa 41:8; 44:2 — eight verses, so the claim's two Deuteronomy verses are not alone.

**Election + holy + love within three verses:** seven places (Swete): Deut 7:6–9; Ps 77:67–71; Sir 45:1–4; Tobit (Sinaiticus) 13:10–14; Isa 56:4–7; 3 Macc 2:9–12. Read for sense:
- **Deut 7:6–8 is the only one in which a *people* is called holy, chosen and loved by God**: λαὸς ἅγιος ('a holy people', 7:6), προείλατο … ἐξελέξατο ('he chose … he elected', 7:6–7), παρὰ τὸ ἀγαπᾶν Κύριον ὑμᾶς ('because the Lord loves you', 7:8). So the claim's "densest" is right in sense.
- Sir 45:1–4 (Swete only) applies all three to **Moses**: ἠγαπημένον ὑπὸ θεοῦ ('beloved by God', 45:1), δόξῃ ἁγίων ('the glory of the holy ones', 45:2), ἐν πίστει καὶ πραΰτητι αὐτοῦ ἡγίασεν, ἐξελέξατο αὐτόν ('in faithfulness and meekness he sanctified him; he chose him', 45:4). It has Colossians' exact participle ἠγαπημένος and, unusually, πραΰτης ('meekness'), which is in the virtue list of Col 3:12.
- Ps 77:68–69 (Judah and Zion chosen and loved; ἁγίασμα 'sanctuary'), 3 Macc 2:9–10 (the city chosen, the place sanctified, the house of Israel loved), Tobit 13 (Jerusalem) and Isa 56 (people who choose what God wills) have the three stems with other objects or subjects.

**ἠγαπημένος as a title for Israel.** The Greek renders Hebrew יְשֻׁרוּן (Jeshurun) as ὁ ἠγαπημένος ('the beloved') at every one of its four occurrences — Deut 32:15; 33:5; 33:26; Isa 44:2 (WLC lemma search: four verses; Swete agrees at each). Isa 44:1–2 joins it to election: Ἰσραὴλ ὃν ἐξελεξάμην … ὁ ἠγαπημένος Ἰσραὴλ ὃν ἐξελεξάμην ('Israel whom I chose … the beloved Israel whom I chose'). **For the participle of Col 3:12, Isa 44:1–2 is closer than Deut 7:8**, which has the infinitive ἀγαπᾶν. Isa 41:8 (ὃν ἐξελεξάμην … ὃν ἠγάπησα, 'whom I chose … whom I loved') is the same pattern. Both lack "holy" within three verses.

**Deuteronomy has the verbs, not the adjective ἐκλεκτός — confirmed.** The adjective appears with a holy people elsewhere: Isa 65:9, 15 (οἱ ἐκλεκτοί μου, 'my chosen ones', inheriting τὸ ὄρος τὸ ἅγιόν μου, 'my holy mountain'), and in the New Testament 1 Pet 2:9 (γένος ἐκλεκτόν … ἔθνος ἅγιον, 'a chosen race … a holy nation', drawn from Exod 19:6 and Isa 43:20 [S]).

**The idolatry link.** Deut 7:5 commands the burning of τὰ γλυπτὰ τῶν θεῶν αὐτῶν ('the carved images of their gods'); and 7:25 — not cited by the claim — adds οὐκ ἐπιθυμήσεις ἀργύριον καὶ χρυσίον ἀπ' αὐτῶν ('you shall not covet the silver and gold on them'), which joins covetousness to idolatry in the same chapter. That makes the parallel with Col 3:5 (τὴν πλεονεξίαν, ἥτις ἐστὶν εἰδωλολατρία, 'covetousness, which is idolatry') somewhat better than claimed, though the Greek words differ (ἐπιθυμέω, 'covet', against πλεονεξία, 'greed').

**The New Testament triad, by lemma.** ἐκλεκτός + ἅγιος + ἀγαπάω in one verse: Col 3:12 only — **as stated, but only on that strict definition.** Widen election to Paul's other election words and the triad appears twice more: **Eph 1:4** (ἐξελέξατο ἡμᾶς … εἶναι ἡμᾶς ἁγίους … ἐν ἀγάπῃ, 'he chose us … that we should be holy … in love') and **2 Thess 2:13** (ἀδελφοὶ ἠγαπημένοι ὑπὸ κυρίου, ὅτι εἵλατο ὑμᾶς ὁ θεὸς … ἐν ἁγιασμῷ πνεύματος, 'brothers beloved by the Lord, because God chose you … in sanctification of the Spirit'). 2 Thess 2:13 has the same participle ἠγαπημένοι and the verb αἱρέομαι ('choose'), the simple form of Deuteronomy's προαιρέομαι (Deut 7:6–7; 10:15; and Deut 26:18 εἵλατο). Election with love in one verse: Acts 15:25; **Rom 11:28** (κατὰ δὲ τὴν ἐκλογὴν ἀγαπητοὶ διὰ τοὺς πατέρας, 'as regards election, beloved for the sake of the fathers'); Col 3:12; **1 Thess 1:4** (ἠγαπημένοι ὑπὸ θεοῦ, τὴν ἐκλογὴν ὑμῶν); Jas 2:5; 2 John 1. Rom 11:28 is itself the Deuteronomic logic of Deut 4:37 and 10:15 — he loved the fathers and chose their seed — so Paul demonstrably knows this Deuteronomic pattern and uses it in his own words. The window scan cannot test this claim: it misses augmented ἐξελέξατο, and Deut 7 ranks 99th to 210th, Isa 44 447th to 1,002nd.

But the participle comes from the Isaiah–Deuteronomy "beloved" title (Isa 44:1–2; Deut 32–33), Sir 45:1–4 has the triad with ἠγαπημένος and πραΰτης for Moses, and Paul's own triad (2 Thess 2:13; Eph 1:4; 1 Thess 1:4; Rom 11:28) is enough to explain the wording. The claim's NT "only" holds solely on the strict lemma ἐκλεκτός.

- **Luke 1:68–79 for Col 1:12–14.** With Col 1:13–14, the only NT place where ῥύομαι and a λυτρ- word stand within twelve verses, with forgiveness (1:77) and darkness and light (1:79). A Lukan companion to Acts 26:18; suggested rating: moderate (shared idiom).
- **Sir 45:1–4 for Col 3:12.** Moses beloved (ἠγαπημένον), sanctified and chosen, "in faithfulness and meekness" (πραΰτης is in Col 3:12's list). Swete only. Suggested rating: low (an individual, not the people), but worth a line beside Deut 7.
- **Isa 44:1–2 (and the Jeshurun rendering) for ἠγαπημένοι in Col 3:12.** The LXX title "the beloved" for Israel, joined to "whom I chose". Suggested rating: low–moderate; arguably the source of the participle rather than Deut 7:8.
- **Exod 14:30 (with 5:23; 12:27) for Col 1:13** — the exact aorist ἐρρύσατο; moderate, as part of A4. **Deut 7:25** (covetousness bound to idols) strengthens A6's thematic case; **2 Macc 12:41** (Swete only) corrects A5's "only"; low.
- **Prov 22:2 LXX and Mal 2:10 for Col 4:1.** Both state in Greek the "one Maker of master and slave / of all" idea that the Greek of Job 31:15 lacks. Suggested rating: low (thematic).
- **2 Rgns 19 for Col 3:22–24.** Ranks first in three of six scan runs: servant, "my lord the king", heart, and the king's ἀνταπόδοσις to a loyal servant (19:36). A human-master analogue, not a theological source; suggested rating: low, noted because it out-ranks both proposed texts.
