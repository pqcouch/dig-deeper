# Claim audit 4 — Colossians — Auditor B

**Claims audited:** B1–B6 (field, design and cluster claims). **Editions:** Colossians from the SBLGNT (lemma index) and the NA28 export; NT comparisons from the SBLGNT lemma index; Greek OT from Swete (observation layer), with the Rahlfs export checked wherever a finding rests on wording (Psalms, Daniel OG and Theodotion, Torah); Hebrew from the WLC. Wisdom of Solomon and the Psalms of Solomon exist here only in Swete; findings that depend on them say so. Anything from memory is tagged [S] and carries no verdict.

---

## 0. The instrument, its floors, and the general null

Most of these claims rest on the corpus's NT-to-Swete contact scan, which ranks every Swete chapter against an NT passage in six runs (whole chapters, 8-verse windows and 16-verse windows, each once with all shared content words and once with the rarer words only) and reports the median rank across the six. Before using it I tested it, because three of the six claims turn on a single word.

**Stem floors found.** The scan reduces each NT lemma to a stem and matches it against word-beginnings in Swete. In this audit it silently missed:

- **μεθίστημι** ('transfer', Col 1:13): no match anywhere in Swete, although the verb is there (Dan 2:21 in both Greek texts; 1 Kgdms 6:12; 1 Macc 8:13; 3 Macc 6:12, 24; and, by augmented or compound forms, Isa 54:10, 3 Kgdms 15:13, 4 Kgdms 17:23 and others).
- **ῥύομαι** ('rescue', Col 1:13): the scan catches only participles beginning ῥυομ-. It misses every aorist and future (ἐρρύσατο, ἐρύσατο, ῥῦσαι, ῥύσεται, ῥυσάμενος …), roughly 180 verses — so, for example, Ps 106 LXX:6, 20 ἐρρύσατο ('he rescued') are invisible to it.
- **φῶς** ('light'): the scan matches the nominative/accusative only, not φωτός/φωτί (so Wis 18:4 φωτός is missed).
- **δέω** ('bind', Col 4:3 δέδεμαι) and **ἀρτύω** ('season', Col 4:6): no match. For ἀρτύω this is a true nil — a search for every word beginning ἀρτυ- or ἠρτυ- in Swete returns nothing.

I re-ran the scans with these stems repaired (adding the missed forms of μεθίστημι, ῥύομαι and φῶς) and report both the tool's ranks (which reproduce the claims) and the repaired ranks. The scan also cannot tell a cognate from a false friend; I list every sense correction.

**General null for "a stable top partner".** I drew random Pauline passages (Romans, 1–2 Corinthians, Galatians, Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, 1 Thessalonians; passages kept within one chapter), 150 of each length, and recorded the best median rank any Swete chapter achieved:

| Passage length | Some chapter at median 1 | Some chapter at median ≤ 2 | Some chapter at median ≤ 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 verses | 54 % | 83 % | 93 % |
| 3 verses | 46 % | 77 % | 89 % |
| 5 verses | 42 % | 67 % | 90 % |

So "chapter X is the most stable partner, median 1 or 2" is what three Pauline passages in four produce, whatever they say. A rank is evidence only if it is (a) specific to the target — the same chapter does not top unrelated passages with the same vocabulary — and (b) robust to removing its strongest word and to sense correction. Every field claim below is tested on both.

## B1 — Wisdom 17:2–18:9 as a field for Colossians 1:12–13

### What was tested
The scan for Col 1:12–14 and 1:12–13, at the tool's settings and with stems repaired; sense check of every Swete word credited to Wisdom 18; leave-one-out; a specificity null (Wisdom 18's rank for fifteen other NT light/darkness passages); rival sources in Swete and the NT; the "holy ones" wording; and who gives thanks in Wis 18:2.

### Results
**Reproduction.** The stated median rank holds exactly. Col 1:12–14 against Swete: Wisdom 18 ranks 1, 2, 3, 3, 1, 2 across the six runs, median 2, the most stable partner; then OG Daniel 4 (median 3) and Isaiah 59 (median 3). For 1:12–13 alone Wisdom 18 is median 1 (runs 1, 1, 1, 2, 1, 1).

**Sense check (Swete, Wisdom 18).** The words credited to Wisdom 18 are: ἁγίους ('holy ones', 18:9); βασιλεῖ ('king', 18:11) and βασιλείων ('royal', of thrones, 18:15); εὐχαριστοῦσιν ('they give thanks', 18:2); ἱκανοί/ἱκανή ('enough', 'able', 18:12, 25); σκότει ('darkness', 18:4); υἱόν/υἱούς ('son', 'sons', 18:4, 13); φῶς ('light', 18:1, 4). Two are false friends for Colossians: βασιλεύς/βασίλειος ('king', 'royal') is not βασιλεία ('kingdom'), and ἱκανός ('sufficient') is not the sense of ἱκανόω ('qualify') at Col 1:12. υἱός is real but its referent differs (Israel as God's sons; in Col 1:13 the Son of his love). What Wisdom 18 genuinely shares is **φῶς, σκότος, ἅγιος (once), εὐχαριστέω and υἱός**. It shares none of Colossians' action words: no ῥύομαι ('rescue'), μεθίστημι ('transfer'), ἐξουσία ('authority'), βασιλεία ('kingdom'), κλῆρος ('lot') or μερίς ('share'), ἀπολύτρωσις ('redemption') or ἄφεσις ('forgiveness').

**Who gives thanks in Wis 18:2?** The claim says "the righteous give thanks". The syntax of 18:1–2 (Swete) does not allow it. Those who "hear the voice but do not see the form" (18:1) are the Egyptians in the dark; they "called them blessed" (ἐμακάριζον), and in 18:2 "that [the holy ones], though previously wronged, were not harming them, they give thanks (εὐχαριστοῦσιν) and begged pardon (ἐδέοντο) for having been at variance". The verb that asks forgiveness for past hostility can only belong to the Egyptians, and εὐχαριστοῦσιν is coordinated with it. The thanksgiving in Wisdom 18 is the oppressors' gratitude for not being avenged — not the saints' thanks for their inheritance. (Standard translations read it so [S].)

**"Holy ones".** At 18:1 the word is ὅσιοι ('devout ones', τοῖς ὁσίοις σου), and again ὅσιοι παῖδες at 18:9; ἅγιοι ('holy ones') occurs once, at 18:9 (τοὺς ἁγίους, 'the holy ones' who will share alike in blessings and dangers). Colossians has ἅγιοι. Swete only; no Rahlfs export exists for Wisdom.

**Leave-one-out (tool settings, 1:12–14).** Dropping φῶς: Wisdom 18 falls to median 5. Dropping σκότος: median 4. Dropping ἅγιος: still median 2. Dropping ἱκανός (the false friend): median 6. **Dropping εὐχαριστέω: median 16.** For 1:12–13: dropping φῶς, median 3; σκότος, 3; ἅγιος, 2; **εὐχαριστέω, 11**.

**Repaired stems and sense correction.** With μεθίστημι, ῥύομαι and φῶς repaired, Wisdom 18 is median 3 for 1:12–14 (Isaiah 59 moves to median 1) and still median 1 for 1:12–13. Removing the two false friends (ἱκανός, βασιλεύς/βασίλειος): median 9 for 1:12–14 and 7 for 1:12–13. Removing as well εὐχαριστέω, whose subject is the Egyptians: **median 118 (1:12–14) and 109 (1:12–13)**. The rank rests on one verb used of the wrong people and two false friends.

**Specificity.** Wisdom 18 does not top other light/darkness passages. Across thirteen of them (2 Cor 4:4–6; 6:14; Eph 5:8–14; 1 Thess 5:4–8; Rom 13:12–13; 1 Pet 2:9–10; John 1:4–5; 3:19–21; Matt 4:15–16; 1 John 1:5–7; Acts 26:17–18; Luke 1:78–79; 22:53) its median rank runs from 7 to 261, best for 1 Thess 5:4–8 and 2 Cor 6:14 (both 7). Positive controls worked: Matt 4:15–16 puts Isaiah 9 at median 1 in all six runs, and 1 Pet 2:9–10 puts Isaiah 42–43 at the top. So Wisdom 18's rank for Col 1:12–13 is specific — but specific because of εὐχαριστέω, not because of light and darkness.

**The roles are inverted.** In Wisdom 17–18 the oppressors are the "prisoners of darkness" (δέσμιοι σκότους, 17:2; "bound with one chain of darkness", 17:18), and the holy ones were never in the dark ("for your holy ones there was very great light", 18:1). In Colossians "we" were under the authority of darkness and were rescued out of it. Wisdom's picture is of two peoples side by side, one dark, one lit; Colossians' is a transfer of one people from one realm to the other.

**Rivals.**
- *Psalm 106 LXX* (Swete): οἱ λελυτρωμένοι … οὓς ἐλυτρώσατο ('the redeemed … whom he redeemed', 106:2); ἐρρύσατο ('he rescued', 106:6, 20); καθημένους ἐν σκότει ('sitting in darkness', 106:10); ἐξήγαγεν αὐτοὺς ἐκ σκότους ('he led them out of darkness', 106:14). Rescue, redemption and leading out of darkness, with the rescued themselves in the dark — the shape of Col 1:13–14. The scan ranks it far down (median 217–551) because it misses ἐρρύσατο and has no stem for λυτρόω.
- *Isaiah 42:6–7, 16* (Swete): "a light to the nations … to lead out from bonds those bound and from the prison-house those sitting in darkness"; "I will make the darkness into light". *Isaiah 9:1–7*: the people in darkness see great light; a son is given; "his kingdom" (βασιλείαν αὐτοῦ, 9:7). Isaiah 9 shares light, darkness, son and kingdom (βασιλεία in the right sense), but ranks at median 72–202 because those are common words.
- *Allotment language.* μερίς ('share') with κλῆρος ('lot', the noun proper) in one verse (Swete): Deut 10:9; 12:12; 14:26, 28; 18:1; Wis 2:9; Isa 57:6; Jer 13:25 — the Levites' "no share or lot". "The share of the lot" (τὴν μερίδα τοῦ κλήρου) is Torah inheritance idiom.
- *Wisdom 5:5* (Swete): "how was he counted among the sons of God, and his lot (κλῆρος) is among the holy ones (ἐν ἁγίοις)?" — the only verse in Swete with κλῆρος and ἅγιοι in that sense, and verbally closer to "the lot of the holy ones" than anything in Wisdom 18. It scans at median 458–484.
- *Daniel 7*: the holy ones receive the kingdom and authority (Theodotion Dan 7 scans at median 17–30).
- *NT.* ἐξουσία ('authority') with σκότος ('darkness') in one verse (SBLGNT): Luke 22:53 ("this is your hour and the authority of darkness"), Acts 26:18, Eph 6:12, Col 1:13. Acts 26:18 is the nearest verse anywhere: "from darkness to light and from the authority of Satan to God, that they may receive forgiveness of sins and a lot (κλῆρον) among those sanctified" — darkness, light, authority, forgiveness and lot, with κλῆρος beside ἁγιάζω ('sanctify'), the only other NT verse that has it. ἐξουσία with σκότος in one verse in Swete: none.

### Corrections to the claim
- Median 2 for 1:12–14 holds at the tool's settings; with stems repaired it is median 3, and Isaiah 59 is the most stable partner (though Isaiah 59's own contacts include two false friends, ἀφέστηκεν 'has stood off' for ἄφεσις and μετέστησαν 'they turned aside' for μεθίστημι).
- "The righteous give thanks (18:2)" is wrong: the Egyptians give thanks.
- "For your holy ones" at 18:1 is ὅσιοι, not ἅγιοι; ἅγιοι occurs once, at 18:9.

### Hays table

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | Wisdom is a pre-Pauline Greek Jewish text [S] |
| Volume | Weak | Shared: φῶς, σκότος, one ἅγιος, εὐχαριστέω (wrong subject); none of the action words; no phrase |
| Recurrence | Weak | No Wisdom 17–18 contact elsewhere in Colossians tested here |
| Thematic coherence | Possible | Exodus darkness/light is apt; but the roles are inverted (the holy ones were never in darkness) |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Paul's contact with Wisdom is debated [S] |
| History of interpretation | Weak [S] | Commentators usually cite Qumran "lot of the holy ones in light" and Acts 26:18, not Wisdom 18 [S] |
| Satisfaction | Weak | Adds nothing that Acts 26:18, Psalm 106 LXX and Isaiah 42 do not supply better |

### Verdict
**Needs reframing. Recommended rating: low (as a field); Wisdom 18 should not be cited as the stable partner.** The rank is reproducible but depends on εὐχαριστέω, which in 18:2 belongs to the Egyptians, and on two false friends; once those are removed Wisdom 18 falls to median 109–118. If anything survives it is the general Hellenistic-Jewish habit of retelling the exodus as darkness and light, for which Psalm 106 LXX and Isaiah 9 and 42 are closer in shape than Wisdom.

**Missed:** the inversion of roles; Wis 5:5; Psalm 106 LXX; Acts 26:18 as the nearest parallel in wording.

## B2 — The sower behind Colossians 1:5–11

### What was tested
Lemma counts in Mark 4:1–20, Luke 8:4–15 and Matt 13:1–23 against Col 1:5–11 (SBLGNT); the exclusivity of each pairing; positive controls; two baselines (how the sower passages rank among all Gospel and NT windows for overlap with Col 1:5–11, and how Col 1:5–11 ranks among all Pauline paragraphs for overlap with the sower); Paul's other uses; the Genesis rival in Swete; and further rivals.

### Results
**Counts (SBLGNT, lemma).** καρποφορέω ('bear fruit') occurs in 8 NT verses: Matt 13:23; Mark 4:20, 28; Luke 8:15; Rom 7:4, 5; Col 1:6, 10. Outside Colossians, 4 of the 6 are in the seed parables; Paul's other uses (Rom 7:4–5, fruit borne "to God" or "to death") have no parable in view. αὐξάνω ('grow'): 21 verses.

| Lemma (Col 1:5–11) | Mark 4:1–20 | Luke 8:4–15 | Matt 13:1–23 |
|---|---|---|---|
| καρποφορέω | 4:20 | 8:15 | 13:23 |
| αὐξάνω | 4:8 (with καρπός 'fruit') | — | — |
| ὑπομονή ('endurance') | — | 8:15 | — |
| χαρά ('joy') | 4:16 | 8:13 | 13:20 |
| λόγος ('word') | 4:14–20 (7 verses) | 8:11–15 (4) | 13:19–23 (5) |
| ἀκούω ('hear') | 7 verses | 6 | 11 |

- "Luke 8:15 is the only NT verse with καρποφορέω + ὑπομονή": **holds** (positive control: the search returns Luke 8:15 itself). Colossians does not have them in one verse either — καρποφοροῦντες is at 1:10 and ὑπομονήν at 1:11.
- Luke 8:15 also has ἀγαθός ('good'): "in a good and noble heart … bear fruit with endurance"; Col 1:10–11 "in every good work bearing fruit … for all endurance". ἀγαθός + καρποφορέω + ὑπομονή falls in one verse in Luke and across two adjacent verses in Colossians — the strongest single point for the claim.
- καρποφορέω with αὐξάνω in one verse: Col 1:6 and 1:10 only in the NT. Mark 4:8 has καρπός ('fruit') with αὐξάνω (αὐξανόμενα — SBLGNT marks a variant here), and καρποφορέω comes twelve verses later at 4:20.
- "With joy" (μετὰ χαρᾶς), Luke 8:13 and Mark 4:16: **holds, but it is the wrong soil.** In both Gospels it is the rocky-ground hearers who receive the word "with joy" and then fall away. In Colossians joy goes with endurance and patience (1:11), which is the opposite of what the parable says of that soil.

**Baseline A — do the sower passages stand out for Col 1:5–11?** I scored every NT window (12, 16 and 20 verses; Colossians and Ephesians excluded) by rarity-weighted shared content lemmas with Col 1:5–11. Among Gospel windows, Luke 8 ranks 1st, 2nd and 2nd at the three sizes (John 15, John 7 and John 3 are its neighbours); Matt 13 ranks 18th, 13th and 8th; **Mark 4 ranks 39th, 34th and 35th** of 88–89 chapters. Across the whole NT (outside Colossians and Ephesians), Luke 8 ranks 22nd–29th and Mark 4 102nd–151st. So Luke's version is a real neighbour; Mark's is not distinguished by overall vocabulary.

**Baseline B — does Col 1:5–11 stand out among Pauline paragraphs?** Scoring every Pauline 7-verse paragraph (1,343) against the three sower passages, Col 1:5–11 ranks 39th of windows and 12th of distinct passages. 1 Cor 2:3–9 (mystery, hearing, depth), 1 Thess 2:11–13 (receiving the word) and 2 Cor 8:1–2 score higher. With rare words only (lemmas in 60 NT verses or fewer), Col 1:5–11 is not in the top ten: 2 Cor 8:2 (depth, affliction, abundance, riches, joy, against Matthew 13) leads. **What singles Colossians out is not the overall field but one verb pair.** By the six lemmas the claim names, Col 1:5–11 is the only Pauline 7-verse paragraph with all six (four more windows overlapping it have five); 1,268 of 1,343 have none or one.

**Genesis (Swete).** αὐξάνω with πληθύνω ('multiply') in one verse: Gen 1:28; 8:17; 9:1, 7; 17:20; 28:3; 35:11; 48:4; Exod 1:7; Lev 26:9; Jer 3:16; 23:3 (plus 1 Enoch 5:9; 89:49). καρποφορέω occurs in Swete only at Hab 3:17 (with its doublet in the Odes) and Wis 10:7 (the participle), plus the adjective καρποφόρος three times. καρποφορέω with αὐξάνω within two verses in Swete: none; καρπός with αὐξάνω in one verse: none (within one verse: Gen 1:28–29 only). Colossians has no πληθύνω. The Genesis echo therefore rests on αὐξάνω alone — on the scan it ranks at median 62 for Col 1:5–6 and 274 for 1:5–11.

**A closer rival: the "word grows" formula.** αὐξάνω with λόγος ('word') in one verse (SBLGNT): Acts 6:7, 12:24, 19:20 only — "the word of God grew and multiplied" (ηὔξανεν καὶ ἐπληθύνετο, 12:24; compare 6:7), "according to might the word grew and prevailed" (κατὰ κράτος, 19:20; compare Col 1:11 κατὰ τὸ κράτος). This early-Christian formula joins the Genesis pair to the word. Paul has his own sowing-and-growing imagery: 1 Cor 3:6–7 ("God gave the growth") and 2 Cor 9:10 (seed, sower, πληθύνω, αὐξήσει — from Isa 55:10).

### Corrections to the claim
None to the counts. Two to the sense: "with joy" in the parable belongs to the rocky ground; and Mark 4 is not a close vocabulary neighbour — Luke 8 is.

### Hays table

| Criterion | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Possible | Sayings tradition plausibly known; no written Gospel need be assumed [S] |
| Volume | Possible | καρποφορέω (rare) + αὐξάνω; ἀγαθός + καρποφορέω + ὑπομονή with Luke 8:15; no phrase |
| Recurrence | Weak–Possible | The verb pair recurs at 1:6 and 1:10; the Mark 4 contact proposed at 4:3–5 is not supported (B6) |
| Thematic coherence | Possible | The word heard, received, fruitful — apt; the joy contact inverted |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Fruit-bearing of the word looks like an early-Christian idiom (Acts' "word grows") |
| History of interpretation | Possible [S] | Several commentators note the sower behind 1:6 [S] |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Explains καρποφορέω for the gospel; the Acts formula explains αὐξάνω equally well |

### Verdict
**Needs reframing. Recommended rating: low–moderate** for a specific allusion to the sower; **moderate** for "early-Christian fruit-and-growth idiom for the word, whose likeliest root is the sower tradition (Luke's form closest)". The verb pair is distinctive and four of the six NT uses of καρποφορέω outside Colossians are in seed parables. But by overall vocabulary Col 1:5–11 does not stand out against the sower, Mark's version is not a close neighbour, and the joy contact attaches to the wrong soil. It is a better rival to the Genesis echo than Genesis is, because Genesis lacks καρποφορέω entirely.

**Missed:** ἀγαθός in Luke 8:15; the Acts word-growth formula; Matt 13:38 ("the field is the world", κόσμος), beside Col 1:6 "in all the world".

## B3 — Exodus 34:6 behind the virtues of Colossians 3:12–13

### What was tested
Swete searches for any οἰκτ- word with any μακροθυμ- word, in both spellings and with augment; a positive control; co-occurrence of the Colossian nouns (any two within two verses); a frequency baseline for the virtue words; the NT pattern for "as the Lord … so you"; Rahlfs checks.

### Results
**Reproduction.** οἰκτ- with μακροθυμ- in one verse (Swete, stems searched anywhere in the word, spellings οἰκτιρ- and οἰκτειρ-, and augmented ᾠκτ-): exactly 8 — Exod 34:6; Neh 9:17; Ps 85:15; 102:8; 144:8; Joel 2:13; Jonah 4:2; Theodotion Dan 4:24. **Holds exactly**; Exod 34:6 is returned (positive control); no augmented form adds anything. Theodotion Dan 4:24 is indeed "another use": "atone for your wrongs by compassion (οἰκτιρμοῖς) to the poor; perhaps God will be patient (μακρόθυμος)". There human compassion and divine patience stand side by side — a closer analogue of Colossians' transfer of attributes than the claim allowed. Within two verses the set grows to 11 (adding Neh 9:19, Ps 144:9, Sir 5:6).

**Psalm 144 LXX.** Confirmed and stronger than stated. Ps 144:7 χρηστότητος ('goodness', the noun), 144:8 οἰκτίρμων … μακρόθυμος ('compassionate … patient'), 144:9 χρηστός … οἱ οἰκτιρμοί ('kind … his compassions', the noun). Rahlfs agrees, except that at 144:9 Rahlfs reads "kind to all" (τοῖς σύμπασιν) where Swete has "to those who wait" (τοῖς ὑπομένουσιν). Ps 110:4 and 111:4–5 are as stated (111:5 "kind (χρηστός) is the man who is compassionate (οἰκτίρμων) and lends": the attributes given to the righteous man).

**Colossians' nouns in Swete.** Searching the nouns only — οἰκτιρμός ('compassion'), χρηστότης ('kindness'), μακροθυμία ('patience'), πραΰτης ('gentleness'), ταπεινοφροσύνη ('humility'), σπλάγχνα ('heart', literally 'bowels') — **no two occur in one verse anywhere in Swete**. Within two verses there is one pair only: χρηστότης (Ps 144:7) with οἰκτιρμοί (Ps 144:9). μακροθυμία as a noun occurs only five times in Swete (Prov 25:15; Sir 5:11; Isa 57:15; Jer 15:15; 1 Macc 8:4), never near the others; ταπεινοφροσύνη does not occur (the adjective ταπεινόφρονας once, Prov 29:23); πραΰτης ten times, six of them in Sirach. Allowing any form of each word, the richest grouping in Swete is Ps 144:7–9, with three of the five (kindness, compassion, patience), all of God.

**Frequency baseline (Swete).** χρηστότης: 25 verses, 15 in Psalms and 7 in the Psalms of Solomon. οἰκτίρμων: 21, 11 in Psalms. μακροθυμ- (any form): 33, mostly Proverbs (6) and Sirach (7), mostly human patience. The adjectives of Exod 34:6 are almost wholly divine predicates; the nouns Colossians uses are mostly not.

**NT pattern (SBLGNT).** Windows of three verses holding three or more of the Colossian virtue families: Rom 2:2–4 (χρηστότης, ἀνοχή 'forbearance', μακροθυμία — of God); Gal 5:21–23 (χρηστότης, μακροθυμία, πραΰτης); Eph 4:1–2 (ταπεινοφροσύνη, πραΰτης, μακροθυμία, ἀνεχόμενοι 'bearing with'); Eph 4:30–32 (χρηστοί, εὔσπλαγχνοι, χαριζόμενοι … καθὼς καὶ ὁ θεὸς … ἐχαρίσατο ὑμῖν); Phil 2:1–3 (σπλάγχνα καὶ οἰκτιρμοί, ταπεινοφροσύνη). Rom 2:4 gives God χρηστότης, ἀνοχή and μακροθυμία in that order; Col 3:12–13 gives believers χρηστότης … μακροθυμία, ἀνεχόμενοι. Phil 2:1 joins σπλάγχνα with οἰκτιρμοί, as Col 3:12 does. 2 Cor 6:6 and Gal 5:22 pair μακροθυμία with χρηστότης.

**"As the Lord forgave you, so also you."** Three closer analogues than Exodus 34:
- Eph 4:32: "forgiving one another as God in Christ forgave you", followed by 5:1 "be imitators of God".
- Luke 6:35–36: "he is kind (χρηστός) to the ungrateful … be compassionate (οἰκτίρμονες) as your Father is compassionate" — kindness and compassion of God turned into a command to imitate him, which is exactly the move the claim credits to Exodus 34.
- Matt 18:26–35: "be patient (μακροθύμησον) with me" (18:26, 29), "having compassion" (σπλαγχνισθείς, 18:27), the debt forgiven, "should you not have had mercy … as I had mercy on you?" (18:33). Patience, compassion and the pattern "as I to you, so you to another", all about forgiving.

### Corrections to the claim
None to the eight. Ps 144 should be read 144:7–9 (adding the noun χρηστότης at 144:7), and Rahlfs differs from Swete at 144:9.

### Design-and-allusion table

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Counts verified | 8 verses, exactly (Swete); Rahlfs agrees for the Psalms cited |
| Baseline | Colossians' nouns never co-occur in Swete except χρηστότης/οἰκτιρμοί in Ps 144:7–9; in the NT the same nouns cluster repeatedly in Paul |
| Sense | In Exod 34:6 and its echoes the words are God's self-description; in Colossians they are clothing for believers, grounded in the Lord's forgiveness |
| Alternative explanation | Pauline virtue vocabulary (Rom 2:4; Gal 5:22; Eph 4:2, 32; Phil 2:1) plus the Jesus-tradition imitation pattern (Luke 6:35–36; Matt 18:23–35) |
| Availability / History of interpretation | Exod 34:6 universally available; commentators note that several of these are divine attributes elsewhere (Rom 2:4) [S] |
| Volume | Weak: Colossians uses nouns the formula lacks, lacks ἐλεήμων ('merciful') and πολυέλεος ('abounding in mercy'), the formula's own words |

### Verdict
**Needs reframing. Recommended rating: low–moderate** for a field claim framed as "divine-attribute vocabulary, ultimately from the Exod 34:6 tradition but reaching Colossians through Paul's own usage (Rom 2:4) and the Greek Psalter (Ps 144:7–9)"; **low** for an allusion to Exod 34:6 itself. The co-occurrence the claim counts is real, but it is the adjectives of the formula, which Colossians does not use. The imitation pattern "as the Lord … so you" is closer in Eph 4:32, Luke 6:36 and Matt 18:33 than in anything in the Greek OT.

**Missed:** Ps 144:7 χρηστότης; Theodotion Dan 4:24 as a human-and-divine pairing; Luke 6:35–36 and Matt 18:26–35.

## B4 — The Greek Psalter's titles behind "psalms, hymns, spiritual songs" (Colossians 3:16)

### What was tested
Reproduction of every count with all forms of ψαλμός ('psalm'), ὕμνος/ὑμνέω ('hymn', 'sing hymns') and ᾠδή ('song'); how often each noun occurs in Psalm titles, in the Psalm bodies and elsewhere in Swete; co-occurrence outside the Psalter; how the Hebrew titles map onto the Greek (WLC lemma index); the NT distribution.

### Results
**Reproduction (Swete).** ψαλμ- with ᾠδ- in one Psalms verse: Ps 4, 29, 47, 64, 65, 67, 74, 75, 82, 86, 87, 91, 107 — **13, exactly, all titles.** "ἐν ὕμνοις" ('with hymns') heads Ps 6, 53, 54, 60, 66 and 75: **holds**; searching every case of ὕμνος and every form of ὑμνέω adds no further title — ὕμνος at Ps 64:2 ("praise befits you", Σοὶ πρέπει ὕμνος) is the first line of the psalm, not the title. Ps 75:1 has all three nouns: **holds**, and it is the only verse in Swete with all three. Within two verses Ps 64:1–2 also has all three (title ψαλμὸς … ᾠδή, then ὕμνος in 64:2). Psalms of Solomon: ψαλμ- with ᾠδ- at 15:1, 15:5 and 17:1; ὕμνος at 10:1 (and 3:1–2, 14:1, 16:1). The stated "floor" was therefore not hiding anything.

**Where each noun lives (Swete, verses).**

| Noun | Total | Psalms verse 1 (titles) | Psalms body | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ψαλμός | 93 | 68 (73 %) | 4 | 21 (9 in the Psalms of Solomon) |
| ᾠδή | 113 | 35 (31 %) | 18 | 60 (Exod 15, Deut 31–32, Judges, Samuel, Kings, Chronicles 12, Nehemiah, Wisdom, Sirach, Amos, Habakkuk, Maccabees, Odes) |
| ὕμνος | 32 | 6 (19 %) | 5 | 21 (Maccabees 7, Judith, Chronicles, Nehemiah, Isaiah, Sirach, Psalms of Solomon) |
| ὑμνέω (verb) | 147 | 0 | 6 | 141 (Daniel 3 and the Odes dominate) |

Only ψαλμός is predominantly a Psalter-title word. ᾠδή and ὕμνος are ordinary Greek-Jewish song words. Comparison: αἴνεσις ('praise') 114 verses, half in Psalms.

**Outside the Psalter titles,** two of the three nouns within one verse of each other (Swete): 2 Chr 7:6 ("instruments of the songs (ᾠδῶν) of the Lord … with the hymns (ἐν ὕμνοις) of David"), Amos 5:23 ("the noise of your songs (ᾠδῶν) … the psalm (ψαλμόν) of your instruments"), 1 Macc 13:51 ("with hymns and with songs", ἐν ὕμνοις καὶ ἐν ᾠδαῖς), and the Psalms of Solomon (3:1–2; 15:1, 5; 17:1), whose titles imitate the Psalter. The full triad: Ps 75:1 (and Ps 64:1–2) only.

**Hebrew mapping (WLC).** מִזְמוֹר ('psalm') with שִׁיר ('song') in one verse: 14 verses, all title lines except Ps 98:1. These correspond to the Greek ψαλμός + ᾠδή titles (MT 30, 48, 65, 66, 68, 75, 76, 83, 87, 88, 92, 108 = LXX 29, 47, 64, 65, 67, 74, 75, 82, 86, 87, 91, 107). But:
- Greek **ὕμνοι in the titles renders בִּנְגִינוֹת ('with stringed instruments')**: נְגִינָה ('stringed music') stands in the titles of MT Ps 4, 6, 54, 55, 61, 67, 76 (and in the bodies of 69:13 and 77:7). LXX 6, 53, 54, 60, 66, 75 = MT 6, 54, 55, 61, 67, 76 render it ἐν ὕμνοις; LXX Ps 4 renders the same Hebrew ἐν ψαλμοῖς. So "hymn" in the titles is a translator's rendering of a word for instrumental accompaniment, not a Hebrew genre label.
- In LXX Ps 4 the ᾠδή of the title renders מִזְמוֹר, not שִׁיר; and LXX 66 (MT 67) drops שִׁיר.
- The Hebrew genre word for praise in a title, תְּהִלָּה (MT Ps 145:1), is rendered αἴνεσις ('praise'), not ὕμνος.

So the Greek titles do not use the three nouns as a fixed classification. They render overlapping Hebrew terms inconsistently.

**NT (SBLGNT).** ψαλμός: Luke 20:42; 24:44; Acts 1:20; 13:33 (all the book of Psalms); 1 Cor 14:26 ("each one has a psalm" — a contribution in worship); Eph 5:19; Col 3:16. ὕμνος: Eph 5:19 and Col 3:16 only. ᾠδή: Eph 5:19; Col 3:16; Rev 5:9 and 14:3 ("a new song"); Rev 15:3 ("the song of Moses … and the song of the Lamb"). All three nouns together: Eph 5:19 and Col 3:16 only — **holds**. ὑμνέω: Matt 26:30 and Mark 14:26 (after the Passover meal), Acts 16:25, Heb 2:12 (quoting Ps 21:23 LXX). The NA28 apparatus at Col 3:16 records manuscripts that add καί ('and') between the nouns; this does not affect the question.

**Baseline.** Would a Greek-speaking Jew naming three kinds of song be bound to land on Psalter words? For ᾠδή and ὕμνος, largely yes: they are the commonest nouns for song and hymn across Swete (Chronicles, Maccabees, Judith, Sirach), and Josephus and Philo use ᾠδαί and ὕμνοι freely of compositions to God [S]. For ψαλμός, no: three-quarters of its uses are Psalter titles. The triad itself is attested only in Ps 75:1 (and 64:1–2), so the observation has some force. But it is carried by ψαλμός, with the other two nouns supplied by the ordinary song vocabulary.

### Design table

| Item | (a) Observation: the nouns are the Psalter's own labels | (b) Inference: "the Psalms only" |
|---|---|---|
| Counts verified | Yes, exactly (13; 6; Ps 75:1) | — |
| Baseline | ψαλμός 73 % Psalter titles; ᾠδή 31 %; ὕμνος 19 % (title ὕμνοι renders "with strings") | The two nouns that are general song words cannot restrict the reference |
| Sense | In the Greek titles the nouns overlap and are applied inconsistently | "Spiritual" (πνευματικαῖς) qualifies at least the last noun; 1 Cor 14:26 ("each has a psalm") and Rev 5:9; 14:3 (a *new* ᾠδή) show NT use of these words for new compositions |
| Alternative explanation | Ordinary Jewish song vocabulary, with ψαλμός naturally suggesting the Psalter | Three overlapping terms for sung praise (rhetorical fullness), as in 1 Macc 13:51 and 2 Chr 7:6 |
| History of interpretation [S] | The title argument is made chiefly by advocates of exclusive psalmody [S] | Most commentators read the terms as overlapping and not restricted to the canonical Psalter [S] |

### Verdict
**(a) Confirmed with nuance — moderate.** The counts hold exactly, and Ps 75:1 alone combines all three nouns in Swete. But only ψαλμός is a Psalter-title word in its own right, and the title ὕμνοι renders "with stringed instruments". **(b) Discard — low.** The Greek titles use the words inconsistently; ᾠδή and ὕμνος are ordinary song words throughout Greek-Jewish literature; and the NT uses ψαλμός and ᾠδή for new compositions as well as for the book of Psalms. The texts do not support a "Psalms only" reading of 3:16.

**Missed:** Ps 64:1–2; the בִּנְגִינוֹת mapping; the inconsistency at Ps 4.

## B5 — Daniel 2 as a field for Colossians 4:2–5

### What was tested
ἐξαγοράζω ('buy up', 'redeem') with the stem searched anywhere in the word and with augment; a positive control; Rahlfs and the Aramaic; the scan's ranks in six runs for 4:2–6, 4:2–5 and 4:3–5; leave-one-out; sense check; a null of twenty NT "mystery" or "wisdom" passages; idiom.

### Results
**The verb (Swete).** Searching ἐξαγορ- and ἐξηγορ- anywhere in the word returns 17 verses. Fifteen are ἐξαγορεύω ('confess', Lev 5:5, Neh 9:2 and so on) or ἐξηγορία ('confession', Job 22:22; 33:26; Pss Sol 9:12); these are false friends, and they serve as the positive control for the augmented search. The remaining two are **Dan 2:8 OG and Dan 2:8 Theodotion — the only occurrences of ἐξαγοράζω in Swete**, both "you are buying time" (καιρὸν ὑμεῖς ἐξαγοράζετε). The Rahlfs export reads the same in both Greek texts. In the WLC the Aramaic is עִדָּנָא אַנְתּוּן זָבְנִין ('you are buying time'); זְבַן ('buy') occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible. In the NT (SBLGNT) the middle with καιρός is Eph 5:16 and Col 4:5 only (the active ἐξαγοράζω at Gal 3:13; 4:5 means 'redeem'). **The verb contact holds exactly.**

There is a further point the claim did not make: both Pauline uses stand beside wisdom and "walking" — Eph 5:15–16 "walk … not as unwise but as wise (σοφοί), buying up the time", and Col 4:5 "walk in wisdom (σοφίᾳ) … buying up the time" — and Daniel's phrase is addressed to the σοφοί, the king's wise men (Dan 2:12–14, both texts). ἐξαγοράζω with σοφ- within a few verses: Dan 2:8 (both), Eph 5:15–16, Col 4:5.

**Ranks (tool settings).** Col 4:2–6: OG Daniel 2 ranks 1, 2, 1, 6, 1, 1, **median 1**; Theodotion Daniel 2 ranks 3, 3, 3, 7, 2, 2, **median 3** — **holds exactly**. Between them, at median 2, stands 2 Maccabees 1. For 4:3–5 the medians are 1 (OG) and 2 (Theodotion). For 4:2–5 (dropping 4:6), 2 Maccabees 1 is median 1 and Daniel 2 median 3.

**Sense check.** Words credited to OG Daniel 2: ἐξαγοράζετε, καιρός ('time'), μυστήριον ('mystery'), σοφία/σοφοί ('wisdom', 'wise men'), οἶδα ('know'), λόγος, ἐλάλησαν ('spoke'), ἕκαστα, θεός, and χάριν — which is the preposition 'for the sake of', a false friend for χάρις ('grace', Col 4:6). The claimed Daniel 2 features hold, but in different words. The prayer "concerning this mystery" (2:18) is δέησις ('petition') in the OG, and in Theodotion there is no prayer word at all ("they sought compassions"). The thanks of 2:23 is ἐξομολογοῦμαι καὶ αἰνῶ ('I confess and praise'), not εὐχαριστία. The revealing is ἐκφαίνω/ἀνακαλύπτω (OG) or ἀποκαλύπτω/γνωρίζω (Theodotion), not φανερόω; and Daniel's speaking is δηλόω/ἀναγγέλλω, not λαλέω. None of προσευχή, προσκαρτερέω, γρηγορέω, εὐχαριστία, θύρα or φανερόω is in Daniel 2.

**Leave-one-out (4:2–6).** Dropping ἐξαγοράζω: OG Daniel 2 falls to median 14 and Theodotion to 25, and 2 Maccabees 1 takes median 1 in all six runs. Dropping μυστήριον: OG median 4, Theodotion 5. Dropping σοφία: OG 2, Theodotion 3. Dropping χάρις (the false friend): OG 2. Dropping ἐξαγοράζω and μυστήριον together: **OG median 159, Theodotion 160.** The field is one verb plus "mystery".

**2 Maccabees 1 as a chance control.** It reaches median 2 (median 1 for 4:2–5) on προσευχή, εὐχαριστέω, θύρα, φανερόω and "χριστ-". The θύρα is the secret door in the temple ceiling through which Antiochus' men are stoned (1:16), and "χριστ-" is "the anointed priests" (1:10). A chapter can sit at the top of the scan on words that have nothing to do with the NT passage.

**Null: does Daniel 2 top any "mystery" passage?** I took twenty NT passages with μυστήριον or σοφία, including Rom 16:25–27 from the NA28 (lemmas assigned by hand, since the SBLGNT index lacks it). One or other Greek Daniel 2 is in the top three for **9 of the 20**: 1 Cor 2:6–10 (Theodotion median 1), Eph 1:8–10 (Theodotion 1), Eph 3:3–10 (Theodotion 2), Mark 4:10–12 (OG 3), Luke 8:9–10 (Theodotion 3), Col 2:2–3 (Theodotion 3), Rom 16:25–27 (Theodotion 3), 1 Cor 13:2 (OG 1) and 2 Thess 2:6–8 (Theodotion 1). It ranks lower for Matt 13:10–17 (67), Rom 11:25–26 (53), 1 Cor 15:51–52 (45), Eph 6:18–20 (18), and Eph 5:15–17, the other ἐξαγοράζω passage (OG 6). The general null (section 0) puts some chapter at median 1 for 42 % of arbitrary 5-verse Pauline passages.

**Recurrence across Colossians.** By the same scan, Daniel 2 is top three for Col 2:2–3 (Theodotion, median 3) and 4:2–6 (both texts), but not for 1:25–28 (OG 48, Theodotion 14). Two "stations" in three is what the null predicts for a letter that uses μυστήριον four times. Ephesians, which also uses it often, shows the same behaviour (Theodotion median 1 at 1:8–10 and 2 at 3:3–10).

**Idiom [S].** I cannot verify from the corpus whether "buy up the time" (ἐξαγοράζεσθαι τὸν καιρόν) is ordinary Greek. To my recollection the lexica give Dan 2:8 as the only exact parallel and compare commercial expressions for "seizing the opportunity", and the classical examples sometimes cited use other verbs [S, uncertain]. The Greek of Daniel is in any case a literal rendering of the Aramaic זְבַן, which favours its being a translation idiom that Paul's phrase shares, not a Greek commonplace. **Flag for research.**

### Verdict
**(a) Confirmed with nuance — recommended low–moderate (as allusion), moderate as "the only Greek-Bible precedent for the phrase".** ἐξαγοράζω occurs in Swete only at Dan 2:8, in both Greek texts, with καιρός, in a scene addressed to "wise men". Whether Paul echoes Daniel or uses a current idiom cannot be settled from these texts. **(b) Needs reframing — low.** The ranks reproduce, but Daniel 2's position rests on ἐξαγοράζω and μυστήριον (median 159–160 without both). Daniel 2 tops nearly half of all NT "mystery" passages, so its recurrence across Colossians adds nothing beyond the presence of the word μυστήριον. The rest of the claimed shape is carried by different Greek words.

**Missed:** the σοφοί addressees and the walking-in-wisdom frame of both Pauline uses; 2 Maccabees 1 as a demonstration of chance; Eph 6:18–20 as the nearest parallel for 4:2–4 (see B6).

## B6 — Three Jesus-tradition pairings in Colossians 4:2–6

### What was tested
The three counts by lemma (SBLGNT), with positive controls; the senses; rival early-church idiom; and a null — for 400 random 5-verse Pauline paragraphs, how many pairs of rarer lemmas occur in the paragraph and elsewhere in the NT only in the Gospels.

### Results
**Reproduction (SBLGNT, lemma).**
1. γρηγορέω ('keep awake') with προσεύχομαι ('pray') in one verse: Matt 26:41 and Mark 14:38. With the noun προσευχή ('prayer'): Col 4:2 only. With δέησις or δέομαι ('petition'): none. **Holds** (Gethsemane returned as control).
2. μυστήριον with ἔξω ('outside') in one verse: Mark 4:11 only; within two verses Col 4:3–5 as well. **Holds.**
3. ἅλας ('salt') with ἀρτύω ('season') in one verse: Mark 9:50, Luke 14:34, Col 4:6. **Holds.** ἀρτύω occurs in the NT only in these three verses; in Swete it does not occur at all.

**Senses.**
1. Gethsemane: keep awake and pray "that you may not enter into temptation", against sleep. Col 4:2: persist in prayer, keeping alert in it "with thanksgiving". The two share the summons to vigilance in prayer; temptation and sleep are absent from Colossians.
2. Mark 4:11: the mystery is *given to insiders*, and to "those outside" everything comes in parables — the mystery withheld. Col 4:3–5: Paul prays to *speak* the mystery and make it plain, and the church walks wisely *towards* outsiders. The relation is reversed, and in Colossians the two words are two verses apart.
3. Mark 9:50 and Luke 14:34: salt that loses its saltiness — "with what will it be seasoned?"; Mark 9:50 adds "have salt in yourselves and be at peace". Col 4:6: speech "seasoned with salt", that is, gracious and apt; salt as wit in conversation is a Hellenistic idiom [S]. "Seasoned with salt" is also the natural collocation for ἀρτύω.

**Rivals (SBLGNT).**
- προσκαρτερέω ('persist') with προσευχή in one verse: Acts 1:14; 2:42; 6:4; Rom 12:12 ("persisting in prayer", τῇ προσευχῇ προσκαρτεροῦντες — the same dative phrase as Col 4:2); Col 4:2.
- Eph 6:18–20: prayer and petition, "keeping alert" (ἀγρυπνοῦντες) "in all perseverance" (προσκαρτερήσει), "that utterance may be given me in the opening of my mouth, to make known the mystery … for which I am an ambassador in a chain, that I may speak as I ought". This is the nearest text to Col 4:2–4 in the NT, verse for verse, and it has no Synoptic colour.
- εὐχαριστία with προσευχή: Phil 4:6, 1 Tim 2:1, Col 4:2. 1 Thess 5:6, 17–18: "let us keep awake (γρηγορῶμεν) … pray without ceasing, give thanks in everything" — vigilance, prayer and thanksgiving in one Pauline paragraph. 1 Pet 4:7: "be sober for prayers".
- ἔξω with περιπατέω ('walk'): Col 4:5 and 1 Thess 4:12 ("that you may walk properly towards those outside") only. οἱ ἔξω as Paul's term for outsiders: 1 Cor 5:12–13; 1 Thess 4:12. The Pauline parallel to "walk in wisdom towards outsiders" is 1 Thess 4:12, not Mark 4:11.
- "A door for the word": θύρα ('door') with ἀνοίγω ('open') is Paul's missionary idiom (1 Cor 16:9; 2 Cor 2:12).

**The method generates pairings freely.** By the claim's own procedure Col 4:2–6 yields more "Jesus-tradition pairings":
- θύρα with προσεύχομαι in one verse, elsewhere only at Matt 6:6 ("shut your door and pray" — the opposite of an opened door);
- θύρα with λόγος and λαλέω, elsewhere only at Mark 2:2 ("not even room at the door, and he was speaking the word to them") and Rev 3:8 — a fourth "Markan" contact.

**Null.** For each of 400 random 5-verse paragraphs in the Pauline letters (Romans to Philemon), I counted pairs of rarer content lemmas (each in 150 NT verses or fewer; cognates crudely merged by stem) that fall in one verse of the paragraph and whose other NT occurrences in one verse are 1–3 in number and all in the four Gospels. Col 4:2–6 scores 3 (γρηγορέω + prayer; ἅλας + ἀρτύω; θύρα + prayer). The random paragraphs: mean 1.6, median 1, 90th percentile 4; **25 % score 3 or more.** Counting cross-verse pairs as well (the μυστήριον + ἔξω type: elsewhere in the NT within two verses, only in the Gospels, in at most three places), Col 4:2–6 scores 14 in all, and **12 % of random paragraphs score as high**. Col 4:2–6 is above the median but unremarkable: about one Pauline paragraph in four matches it on the verse-level pairs the claim names. The procedure cannot tell sayings from narrative, so it overstates the null slightly in one direction; it also cannot see sense, which the three senses above show matters.

### Design table

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Counts verified | All three exactly (SBLGNT) |
| Baseline | 25 % of random Pauline 5-verse paragraphs have ≥ 3 such verse-level pairs; 12 % match Col 4:2–6 on all pairs counted |
| Sense | (1) partial; (2) reversed and non-adjacent; (3) different (spoiled salt / gracious speech) |

| History of interpretation [S] | Mark 9:50 and Gethsemane are sometimes noted at Col 4:2, 6 [S]; a "Markan thread" is not, to my knowledge, a standard proposal [S] |

### Verdict
**Discard the cluster and the "Markan thread" — low.** Each pairing reproduces, but three such pairings in five verses is what a quarter of Pauline paragraphs show. The same method finds further "Jesus pairings" here (Matt 6:6; Mark 2:2) that nobody would defend, and the nearest parallel to the whole paragraph is Eph 6:18–20, which has no Synoptic colour. Individually: **γρηγορέω + prayer, low–moderate**, as an early-Christian "watch and pray" idiom plausibly rooted in Jesus' words but used by Paul elsewhere (1 Thess 5:6, 17); **ἅλας + ἀρτύω, low**, a natural collocation whose sense differs; **μυστήριον + οἱ ἔξω, low**, reversed in sense, with 1 Thess 4:12 the closer source of "towards outsiders".

## New candidates

- **Psalm 106 LXX:2–20 for Col 1:13–14** (Swete): λυτρόω ('redeem', 106:2), ῥύομαι ('rescue', 106:6, 20), "sitting in darkness" (106:10), "he led them out of darkness" (106:14). The rescued are themselves in the dark — the structure of Col 1:13 and the reverse of Wisdom 18. It is invisible to the scan because of the ῥύομαι stem fault. Worth a proper dig, alongside Isa 42:7, 16 (and Acts 26:18, which uses that text [S]). Provisional: low–moderate.
- **Wisdom 5:5 for "the lot of the holy ones" (Col 1:12)** (Swete only): "his lot (κλῆρος) is among the holy ones (ἐν ἁγίοις)" — the only verse in Swete with that collocation, closer than anything in Wisdom 18. Provisional: low–moderate, with Qumran's "lot of the holy ones" [S] as the known comparison.
- **Acts 26:18 as the nearest NT verbal parallel to Col 1:12–14** (SBLGNT): darkness/light, authority, forgiveness of sins, lot among the sanctified. This points to a shared conversion vocabulary, not an OT field.
- **Luke 6:35–36 and Matt 18:26–35 for Col 3:12–13**: God's kindness and compassion made a pattern to imitate; patience, compassion and forgiveness with "as I to you". Provisional: low–moderate (Jesus-tradition imitation pattern).
- **Eph 6:18–20 and Rom 12:12 for Col 4:2–4**: the closest parallels in wording for persistence and vigilance in prayer and for speaking the mystery; better evidence for Pauline paraenetic stock than for any Gospel source.
- **The σοφοί frame of ἐξαγοράζω**: Dan 2:8's addressees are the wise men, and both Pauline uses sit in a "walk wisely" clause. This sharpens B5(a), and an idiom search in non-biblical Greek is needed to settle it.
- **Habakkuk 3:17–19 for Col 1:10–11** (Swete): the fig tree that "will not bear fruit" (καρποφορήσει), yet "I will rejoice … the Lord God is my strength (δύναμις)". It tops the scan for Col 1:5–11 (median 1). Recorded for completeness only: given the null, almost certainly chance (low).
- **Tool note for the main session:** the NT-to-Swete scan's stems for μεθίστημι, ῥύομαι (aorist and future), φῶς (oblique cases), δέω and ἀρτύω fail silently. Any earlier rank that depended on those words, or was depressed by them, should be re-run.
