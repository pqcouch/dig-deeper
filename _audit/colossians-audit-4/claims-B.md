# Claims file — Auditor B (fields, designs, clusters: baselines and nulls)

Each claim is stated as the proposing reports state it, counts included. Test each; most need a chance baseline. Colossians text: NA28 export and SBLGNT.

## B1 — Wisdom 17:2–18:9 (the plague of darkness retold) as a field for Colossians 1:12–13 [C49]
- Col 1:12–13 … τοῦ κλήρου τῶν ἁγίων ἐν τῷ φωτί· ὃς ἐρρύσατο ἡμᾶς ἐκ τῆς ἐξουσίας τοῦ σκότους καὶ μετέστησεν εἰς τὴν βασιλείαν τοῦ υἱοῦ τῆς ἀγάπης αὐτοῦ.
- Stated: Wisdom 17:2–18:9 retells the plague of darkness: the lawless as "prisoners of darkness" (δέσμιοι σκότους, 17:2), "for your holy ones (τοῖς ὁσίοις σου) there was very great light" (18:1), the righteous give thanks (18:2), "your sons" (18:4), the night of the exodus (18:6–9). **Wisdom 18 is the most stable partner of 1:12–14 in the NT scan (median rank 2).** Suggested **moderate (field)**.
- Test: reproduce the scan ranks under all six runs; leave-one-out (drop φῶς, σκότος, ἅγιος/ὅσιος in turn); whether "holy ones" in Wis 18 is ἅγιοι or ὅσιοι (sense check); the null: how often does some Swete chapter reach median ≤2 for arbitrary Pauline passages (audit 3 found 63–70 %), and how often is Wisdom 18 itself at the top for other light/darkness NT passages (2 Cor 4:4–6; Eph 5:8–14; 1 Thess 5:4–8; Acts 26:18; 1 Pet 2:9)? Rivals (Isa 9:1; 42:7; 60:1–3; Ps 106 LXX:10–14 "darkness … bonds … he brought them out"). Verdict.

## B2 — The sower (Mark 4:8, 20; Luke 8:13–15) behind Colossians 1:5–11 [C50]
- Col 1:6 καθὼς καὶ ἐν παντὶ τῷ κόσμῳ ἐστὶν καρποφορούμενον καὶ αὐξανόμενον; 1:10 ἐν παντὶ ἔργῳ ἀγαθῷ καρποφοροῦντες καὶ αὐξανόμενοι; 1:5 τῷ λόγῳ τῆς ἀληθείας; 1:6 ἠκούσατε; 1:11 εἰς πᾶσαν ὑπομονὴν καὶ μακροθυμίαν μετὰ χαρᾶς.
- Stated: Luke 8:15 is **the only NT verse with καρποφορέω + ὑπομονή**; Luke 8:13 has "with joy" (μετὰ χαρᾶς); Mark 4:8, 20 have the word heard, bearing fruit and growing (αὐξάνω); this widens the overview's proposed rival to the Genesis 1 "be fruitful and multiply" echo. Suggested **moderate–uncertain (synthetic)**. Note: a second Mark 4 contact is proposed at Col 4:3–5 (μυστήριον + οἱ ἔξω, Mark 4:11) — see B6.
- Test: the counts (καρποφορέω, αὐξάνω, ὑπομονή, χαρά, λόγος, ἀκούω) in Mark 4:1–20, Luke 8:4–15, Matt 13:1–23 against Col 1:5–11; a baseline: how many other NT passages share as many of these lemmas with any Synoptic parable of comparable length; whether Paul elsewhere uses καρποφορέω (Rom 7:4–5) without the parable; the Genesis rival (αὐξάνω + πληθύνω, no καρποφορέω in Genesis). Verdict.

## B3 — The Exodus 34:6 formula behind the virtues of Colossians 3:12–13 [C54]
- Col 3:12 σπλάγχνα οἰκτιρμοῦ, χρηστότητα, ταπεινοφροσύνην, πραΰτητα, μακροθυμίαν; 3:13 ἀνεχόμενοι … χαριζόμενοι … καθὼς καὶ ὁ κύριος ἐχαρίσατο ὑμῖν, οὕτως καὶ ὑμεῖς.
- Stated: an οἰκτ- word with a μακροθυμ- word in one verse in **8 Swete verses** (Exod 34:6; Neh 9:17; Ps 85:15; 102:8; 144:8; Joel 2:13; Jonah 4:2; and Theodotion Dan 4:24, another use). Swete spells both οἰκτίρμων and οἰκτείρμων — search both. Ps 144:8–9 has three of the five virtues (compassionate, patient, kind — χρηστός at 144:9), all of God. Ps 110:4 LXX "merciful and compassionate is the Lord"; Ps 111:4–5 gives the same adjectives to the righteous man ("good is the man who is compassionate"). Colossians uses nouns and lacks ἐλεήμων. NT side: Rom 2:4 (χρηστότης, ἀνοχή, μακροθυμία of God). Suggested **moderate (field); low–moderate (allusion)**.
- Test: reproduce counts with both spellings; positive control; whether Colossians' nouns (οἰκτιρμός, χρηστότης, μακροθυμία) co-occur in Swete anywhere (any two within two verses); baseline: are these simply the common Hellenistic-Jewish virtue words (Wis 15:1; 2 Cor 6:6; Gal 5:22)? Does the "as the Lord … so you" pattern need the Exodus formula, or is Eph 4:32–5:1 / Luke 6:36 ("be merciful as your Father", οἰκτίρμονες) the closer? Verdict.

## B4 — The Greek Psalter's superscription titles behind "psalms, hymns, spiritual songs" (Colossians 3:16) [C56]
- Col 3:16 … ψαλμοῖς ὕμνοις ᾠδαῖς πνευματικαῖς ἐν [τῇ] χάριτι ᾄδοντες …
- Stated: ψαλμ- with ᾠδ- in **13 Psalm superscriptions** in Swete (Ps 4, 29, 47, 64, 65, 67, 74, 75, 82, 86, 87, 91, 107), plus Pss Sol 15:1, 17:1 (and 15:5 in a text); ἐν ὕμνοις heads Pss 6, 53, 54, 60, 66, 75 (and Pss Sol 10:1); Ps 75:1 LXX has all three. (Floor: ὕμνος searched in one form, ὕμνοις.) The observation (the three nouns are the Psalter's own labels) is offered at moderate; the inference that 3:16 therefore means "the Psalms only" is **uncertain** and contested by 1 Cor 14:26 and Rev 5:9 (new ᾠδή). The three nouns together in the NT: Eph 5:19 and Col 3:16 only.
- Test: reproduce the counts with all forms of ὕμνος/ὑμνέω and ᾠδή; count how often each noun appears in Psalm titles vs elsewhere in Swete (are they also general words for song — Isa, Sir, 1–3 Macc, Odes?); whether the Hebrew titles (מִזְמוֹר, שִׁיר, תְּהִלָּה / בִּנְגִינוֹת) map onto them; baseline: would any Greek Jewish writer naming three song types be bound to hit Psalter words? Verdict on (a) the observation and (b) the "Psalms only" inference.

## B5 — Daniel 2 (Old Greek and Theodotion) as a field for Colossians 4:2–5 [C57]
- Col 4:2–5 Τῇ προσευχῇ προσκαρτερεῖτε … ἐν εὐχαριστίᾳ … ἵνα ὁ θεὸς ἀνοίξῃ ἡμῖν θύραν τοῦ λόγου λαλῆσαι τὸ μυστήριον τοῦ Χριστοῦ … ἵνα φανερώσω αὐτό … Ἐν σοφίᾳ περιπατεῖτε πρὸς τοὺς ἔξω τὸν καιρὸν ἐξαγοραζόμενοι.
- Stated: ἐξαγοράζω occurs in Swete **only at Dan 2:8 (both Greek texts)**, with καιρόν ("you are buying time"); in the NT only Eph 5:16 and Col 4:5, both with καιρός. Dan 2 ranks **1st (OG) and 3rd (Theodotion)** in the NT scan for 4:2–6: prayer "concerning this mystery" (2:18), God gives wisdom and changes times (2:20–21), thanks (2:23), a captive speaks the mystery to the king (2:27–30). This extends an earlier-audited link (Daniel 2 as the *shape* behind Col 1:26–27; 2:2–3 — rated moderate as shape, low–moderate as wording) to a third station; the sense of the verb is reversed (the wise men stall; the church seizes). Suggested **moderate (field, by recurrence); low–moderate (wording)**.
- Test: reproduce the ἐξαγοραζ- count (stem anywhere; augment ἐξηγορ-) and the ranks under all six runs; leave-one-out (drop ἐξαγορ-, μυστηρι-, σοφ- in turn); the null for Daniel 2 at the top of arbitrary NT passages (how often does Dan 2 rank top three for passages with "mystery" or "wisdom" — 1 Cor 2; Rom 16:25–27; Eph 3; Matt 13:11); whether ἐξαγοράζεσθαι καιρόν is ordinary Greek idiom (Polybius, Plutarch — from knowledge, tagged [S]) that makes Daniel's use idiom rather than source. Verdict on (a) the verb contact and (b) Daniel 2 as a letter-level field.

## B6 — Three Jesus-tradition pairings in Colossians 4:2–6 [C58]
- Stated: in Col 4:2–6 three pairings occur elsewhere in the NT only or mainly in sayings of Jesus (SBLGNT): (1) γρηγορέω + prayer in one verse — only Matt 26:41, Mark 14:38, Col 4:2; (2) μυστήριον + οἱ ἔξω in one verse — only Mark 4:11 (Colossians has them across 4:3–5); (3) ἅλας + ἀρτύω — only Mark 9:50, Luke 14:34, Col 4:6. Three in five verses, two Markan; with the sower proposed at 1:5–11 (B2), a possible Markan thread. Suggested **uncertain (cluster); low–moderate each**.
- Test: reproduce the three counts by lemma (γρηγορέω with προσεύχομαι or προσευχή; μυστήριον with ἔξω; ἅλας with ἀρτύω); a baseline — for random five-verse Pauline paragraphs, how many lemma pairs occur in the paragraph and elsewhere in the NT only in the Gospels' sayings of Jesus? Is three unusual? Do the senses match (Gethsemane sleep / mystery withheld from outsiders / salt as distinctiveness)? Alternative: shared early-church paraenetic idiom (1 Thess 5:6, 17; 1 Pet 4:7; Eph 6:18). Verdict.
