# Point & Purpose: Exodus 19:9–20:21

**Sermon 2 of the Sinai pair** — "The LORD Comes Down"
**Translation:** ESV (Anglicised)
**Date:** 5 August 2026
**Exegesis:** `dig-deeper-exodus-19-9to20-21.md` (⭐ HIGH-weight solo dig)
**Overview:** `book-overview-exodus.md` v.1.0.0

---

## Scope Decision

**Single sermon**, and the **second of the matched pair** — with one significant caveat.

The unit runs the announced descent (19:9), the consecration (19:10–15), the theophany (19:16–25), the Ten Words (20:1–17) and the people's response (20:18–21) as a single arc, ignoring the 19/20 chapter break. That is the right preaching unit because **the double frame is the argument**: the outer purpose-frame (19:9 → 20:19) states and fulfils the mediation-aim, and the inner theophany-frame (19:16 → 20:18) brackets the Words with terror. Split it at chapter 20 and both frames break.

**The caveat.** The Decalogue sits at the centre of this sermon and will be preached at **structural and functional depth** — what the Ten Words *are* and *do* in this scene — not commandment by commandment. If the series wants the commandments expounded, that is a separate sermon (or several) on 20:1–21, and the dig for it has not been run. **Say which you are doing from the pulpit**, or hearers will expect ten points and get one.

*This is a single sermon, not a series, because the arc is one event with one stated purpose that is achieved in one movement. Preaching it as a series would break the frames that carry its meaning.*

---

## Fallen Condition Focus

**We cannot bear the unmediated presence and voice of a holy God — we need someone to stand between us and him.**

Anchored in the text: the people hear God speak, and their response is not obedience or worship but retreat — "You speak to us, and we will listen; but do not let God speak to us, lest we die" (20:19). They stand far off (20:18, 21). This is not a failure of nerve that better people would avoid; the narrative endorses it, and Deuteronomy records that God *commended* it (5:28).

*Shared with the first hearers:* a redeemed, consecrated, washed people, three days prepared, still could not stand it. *Shared with us:* the instinct to want God near but not too near, and the discovery that everything we want from him has to come through someone else.

---

## Teaching Point

> **When the holy God comes down to speak, his own redeemed people cannot survive the sound of his voice — so he gives his words inside the terror and provides the mediator they beg for, turning dread into the fear that keeps them from sin.**

**Subject:** What happens when God speaks to his people directly?
**Complement:** They cannot bear it — and God's answer is not silence but a mediator, and words given through him.

*Derived from convergent Headline Findings 1, 2, 4 and 5 of the dig: the purpose-frame of 19:9 discharged at 20:19; the theophany-frame that puts the Decalogue* inside *the terror; the people's endorsed request for a mediator; and Moses' resolution of the two fears at 20:20.*

---

## Purpose Statement

**To replace a casual familiarity with God with a reverence that keeps hearers from sin — and to move them from standing far off to resting on the Mediator who draws near where they cannot.** So that the outsider meets a God who is not safe and not silent; the complacent believer is re-awed; the frightened hear Moses' "do not fear… that the fear of God may be before you"; and the whole congregation understands why they have never had, and never needed, unmediated access.

*Derived from the dig's So What: "the fear of God is the beginning of not sinning (20:20) — reverence, not cringing; sinful people need, and now have, a mediator."*

---

## Candidate Big Question

> **"If God started speaking — actually speaking, out loud, to this room — could you stand it? And who would you send for?"**

*Marked candidate.* A tighter alternative for a congregation that will resist the dramatic framing: **"Why has God never spoken to you directly — and is that a loss or a mercy?"**

## Proposed Structure

Four movements, tracing the double frame. **Note that movement 3 is the Decalogue treated whole**, not itemised.

| | Verses | Movement | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 19:9–15 | **He announces why he is coming** | 19:9 states the purpose the whole event serves: "that the people may hear when I speak with you, and may also believe you forever." Then the consecration — washing, boundaries, three days. Surfaces the FCF: enormous preparation is required simply to *survive* an approach. **Do not skip to the thunder.** |
| **2** | 19:16–25 | **He comes down, and it is unbearable** | Thunder, lightning, thick cloud, trumpet, smoke, a quaking mountain, a voice answering in thunder. The boundary re-warned twice. Establishes the weight the Words will carry — and the danger. |
| **3** | 20:1–17 | **He speaks — and grace is the first word** | The Ten Words *inside* the terror. Two things to make: the preamble (v. 2, "who brought you out… of the house of slavery") puts redemption before a single command; and the two-table shape (God-ward vv. 3–11, neighbour-ward vv. 12–17) shows what a rescued life looks like. Treated at structural depth. |
| **4** | 20:18–21 | **They cannot bear it — and he gives them a man** | The people retreat; they ask for a mediator; the request is right and God commends it. Moses' two fears (v. 20): "do not fear" and "that the fear of God may be before you." Moses draws near to the thick darkness while they stand far off. Lands the PS — and opens to Christ. |

**The arc:** the purpose announced (19:9) → the terror that makes it necessary → the words given inside it → the purpose achieved (20:19), with the mediator drawing near.

**A note on proportion.** Movements 1, 2 and 4 carry the argument; movement 3 carries the content. Resist letting the Decalogue eat the sermon — the frames are what this unit is *for*.

## Keep Honest

Five correctives, drawn from the dig's Preaching Pitfalls.

1. **Do not open at 19:16.** Starting at the thunderclap and treating 19:9–15 as run-up loses the verse that states the whole event's purpose — "that the people may believe you forever" — which 20:19 then fulfils. Drop 19:9 and the mediation-arc has no stated aim, and the sermon becomes a weather report.

2. **Do not preach the Decalogue as a freestanding ethical code.** Ten principles for a good life, lifted clean out of the fire, loses both the authority and the terror. These words are the voice of a holy God that his own redeemed people cannot bear. The frame is not scenery; it is the argument.

3. **Do not preach law without grace.** "Here are God's rules; keep them to stay in favour" is refuted by the Decalogue's own opening clause. The indicative of rescue sits *inside* the grammar of the commandments (20:2) — as it does in the covenant proposal of the previous sermon (19:4–5).

4. **Do not scold the people for asking Moses to mediate.** "If only they'd had more faith" is exactly wrong: God commends the request (Deut 5:28) and builds the prophet-like-Moses promise on it (Deut 18:15–19). **Preach the rightness of needing a mediator** — it is the sermon's road to Christ.

5. **Do not flatten the two fears of 20:20.** Neither "do not fear" as "God isn't really fearsome", nor terror treated as the goal. Moses names two: the dread that flees, and the reverence that keeps from sin. The sermon's work is the transposition from the first into the second.

## Christ-centred check

- **Is the application "be like X" or "trust the Christ whom X points to"?** Neither Moses nor the people is held up for imitation. The people's *instinct* is endorsed, but as a diagnosis, not a virtue.
- **Is Christ the subject or merely an example?** The mediator-institution is *born* in this passage — the request commended at Deut 5:28, becoming the prophet-like-Moses promise (Deut 18:15–19), fulfilled in "one mediator between God and men" (1 Tim 2:5) and "the mediator of a new covenant" (Heb 12:24). And Hebrews 12:18–24 sets *this very scene* against Zion: "you have not come to what may be touched, a blazing fire… but you have come to Mount Zion… and to Jesus, the mediator of a new covenant."
- **Is the good news grounded in Christ's saving work?** Yes, and Hebrews 12:18–24 is the text to land it on. **Recommendation:** read it aloud at movement 4. It is the NT's own sermon on this passage.

**Gate: passed.**

## Hand-off

The candidate Big Question and the four-movement structure are ready for the one-sentence-sermon stage and the manuscript. Four notes:

- **Hebrews 12:18–24 is the destination.** It quotes this scene and answers it. Do not improvise a Christ-connection when the NT has supplied one.
- **The pair's logic.** Sermon one ends on a vow ("all that the LORD has spoken we will do", 19:8). Sermon two shows the same people, days later, unable to bear the voice of the God they promised to obey (20:19). **Name that reversal explicitly** — it is the strongest single move available across the two sermons, and it is text-derived (the dig logs it as `Internal: [answers §19:8 — vow → recoil]`).
- **The Decalogue decision must be announced.** If the congregation expects ten points, say at the outset that the commandments are being preached whole, in their setting, and that they may get their own treatment later.
- **A dedicated 20:1–21 dig has not been run.** If you decide to preach the commandments individually, that exegesis is outstanding.
