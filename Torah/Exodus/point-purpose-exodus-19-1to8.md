# Point & Purpose: Exodus 19:1–8

**Sermon 1 of the Sinai pair** — "Proposal and Vow"
**Translation:** ESV (Anglicised)
**Date:** 5 August 2026
**Exegesis:** `dig-deeper-exodus-19-1to8.md` (⭐ HIGH-weight solo dig)
**Overview:** `book-overview-exodus.md` v.1.0.0

---

## Scope Decision

**Single sermon**, and deliberately the **first of a matched pair**.

The overview's Option B re-cut divides the Sinai theophany at the seam the text marks: **19:1–8** (proposal and consent) and **19:9–20:21** (the LORD comes down). This unit hangs together on its own — arrival, offer, answer — and is short enough to preach whole without flattening anything.

But it should not be preached *alone*. Its climax is a vow (v. 8) whose meaning is only visible in the sequel: the same people recoil when God actually speaks (20:19) and forge a calf within forty days (32:1–6). The exegesis is explicit that this unit "is best preached with, or hard against, its sequel." **Preach it as sermon one of two, and tell the congregation that is what you are doing** — the second sermon is where the vow is tested.

---

## Fallen Condition Focus

**We need to belong to God on terms we could never set or earn — and we are perpetually tempted to treat that belonging as an achievement rather than a gift.**

Anchored in the text: the covenant is offered on the basis of what God has *already* done ("you have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I bore you on eagles' wings and brought you to myself", v. 4) before the first "if" (v. 5) arrives. The temptation to invert that order is not a modern one; it is what the passage's grammar is arranged to prevent.

*Shared with the first hearers:* they were being offered a status they had not earned, by a God who had already rescued them, and they answered with a promise they would break. *Shared with us:* every instinct to secure our standing by performance runs the same way.

---

## Teaching Point

> **The God who has already rescued his people offers them himself — belonging, vocation and identity as his treasured possession and a kingdom of priests — as sheer gift, so that their obedience is the shape of the rescued life and never its price.**

**Subject:** On what basis does God bind a people to himself?
**Complement:** On the basis of a rescue he has already accomplished, offering himself as the goal of it — "I brought you to myself."

*Derived from convergent Headline Findings 1, 2 and 4 of the dig: the grace-before-law grammar of vv. 4–5 (the "now therefore" of v. 5 resting on v. 4); the mediatorial-missional identity of v. 6 against a universal horizon; and v. 4's naming of God himself as the rescue's true goal.*

---

## Purpose Statement

**To move hearers from a relationship with God they are quietly trying to secure, into resting on one he has already secured** — so that the person who thinks their standing depends on their obedience hears the order reversed; the complacent believer is re-astonished at being called God's treasured possession; the outsider hears a God who rescues *in order to have people, not services*; and the whole congregation is re-grounded in grace before a single demand is made of them.

*Derived from the dig's So What: "the covenant is grace-grounded (v. 4) and world-facing… belonging to God is the goal of rescue," with its four-audience breakdown.*

---

## Candidate Big Question

> **"What is the rescue actually for — and are you still trying to pay for a place you have already been given?"**

*Marked candidate.* A shorter alternative if the sermon wants the tenderness rather than the challenge: **"What did God go to all that trouble to get?"** — answered by v. 4's "and brought you to **myself**."

---

## Proposed Structure

Three movements, tracing the passage's own proposal–response shape.

| | Verses | Movement | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 19:1–2 | **The destination reached** | The dated itinerary lands Israel "before the mountain" — the place God named at the burning bush as the sign (3:12). Surfaces the FCF gently: this is what the whole rescue was heading for, and it is not a place but a Person. |
| **2** | 19:3–6 | **The offer, and the order it comes in** | The heart of the sermon. Eagles' wings and "brought you to myself" (v. 4) **before** "now therefore, if…" (v. 5). Establishes the TP: identity given, then vocation — treasured possession, kingdom of priests, holy nation, set against "all the earth is mine". |
| **3** | 19:7–8 | **The answer — and what it will cost to keep** | The unanimous "All that the LORD has spoken we will do." Preach it as *genuine* consent, not folly — and then let it stand unresolved, pointing forward. Lands the PS: rest on the giver, and come back next week to see what happens to the promise. |

**The arc:** the destination is God himself (felt need) → the offer is grace before demand (truth) → the vow is real and will not hold (response — and the hinge into sermon two).

## Keep Honest

Four correctives, drawn from the dig's Preaching Pitfalls.

1. **Do not read v. 5's "if" as salvation by works.** "God got you out of Egypt; now earn your keep" inverts the passage. The "if" conditions *vocation and status* for an already-rescued people, not the rescue itself — and v. 4 forecloses the works-reading before the conditional arrives. Preach the *wə-ʿattâ*, the "now therefore": it is the hinge on which the whole sermon turns.

2. **Do not end on a triumphant "we will do."** "Be like Israel — say yes to God wholeheartedly" is the natural close and it is moralism. The vow's weight only appears in the sequel: these people recoil at God's voice (20:19) and make the calf (32). Preach the genuine yes *and* signal the coming exposure — which is what makes the second sermon necessary rather than optional.

3. **Do not scorn the vow either.** The opposite error. God does not treat their consent as folly, and neither should the sermon. It is real, wholehearted and right; the problem is not that they said it but that a stiff-necked people cannot keep it (a diagnosis the book itself supplies at 32:9; 33:3, 5; 34:9).

4. **Do not apply "kingdom of priests" to the church while skipping Christ.** "You're a royal priesthood — live up to it" lifts 19:6 over the Mediator's head. 1 Peter 2:9 quotes it *of people in Christ*. Route the priestly vocation through him.

---

## Christ-centred check

- **Is the application "be like X" or "trust the Christ whom X points to"?** The TP makes God the actor throughout — he bore, he brought, he offers. The human response (v. 8) is deliberately left *unresolved* rather than held up for imitation.
- **Is Christ the subject or merely an example?** The passage's Christ-connection is the vow Israel cannot keep, answered by the covenant-keeper who obeyed perfectly and bore the covenant curse (Gal 3:13) — and the priestly vocation received in him (1 Pet 2:9; Rev 1:6; 5:10).
- **Is the good news grounded in Christ's saving work?** Yes — and the sermon should say so explicitly at movement 3, where the human "yes" is shown to need someone to make it good.

**Gate: passed.**

## Hand-off

The candidate Big Question and the three-movement structure are ready for the one-sentence-sermon stage and the manuscript. Two notes for whoever writes it:

- The sermon's emotional centre is **v. 4**, not v. 6. "Eagles' wings" and "brought you to myself" carry the tenderness the dig flagged (Headline 4); the vocation of v. 6 is glorious but derivative.
- **Sermon two picks up at 19:9** and runs to 20:21. Its stated purpose-frame (19:9 → 20:19) is what this sermon's unresolved vow hands over to.
