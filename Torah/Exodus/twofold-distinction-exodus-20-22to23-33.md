## The Framework and the Tests

The paper's conclusion, as restated in the attached note:

1. **Kind 1 — commandments expressing the created moral order**, binding on humanity as such, attested before and outside Sinai (Gen 9; Lev 18:24–28; Rom 1–2).
2. **Kind 2 — commandments constituting Israel as a separated and priestly people** (food, purity, cult, calendar, the covenant signs), marked by the language of separation (Lev 20:24–26) and of perpetuity within Israel's generations.
3. **The judicial law** is not a third kind. It is the polity's **application** and **sanction** of both.

**The tests used for each commandment.** These are set out so that every classification can be checked.

| Mark | Test | Example of the test passing |
|---|---|---|
| **K1-a** | Attested **before Sinai** as a norm | Gen 9:5–6 (bloodshed; a beast that kills a man) |
| **K1-b** | **Outsiders held to it** — nations judged, or a non-Israelite setting assumes it | Lev 18:24–28; Deut 18:9–12; Amos 1:6, 9; Job (set in Uz); Ezek 16:49 (Sodom) |
| **K1-c** | **Grounded in creation**, not in Israel's history | Gen 9:6 (*"in the image of God"*); Job 31:15 |
| **K2-a** | **Content** is cult, calendar, food, purity or covenant sign | 20:24–26 (altar); 23:14–17 (feasts) |
| **K2-b** | **Separation or holiness** stated as the ground | 22:31 (*"holy men to Me"*) |
| **K2-c** | **Perpetuity in Israel's generations** | Exod 31:13, 16 (the Sabbath) |
| **K2-d** | The Torah elsewhere **draws the line at the foreigner** — permits to outsiders what it forbids to Israel | Deut 14:21; Deut 23:20 [Heb 23:21] |

**For the judicial law:**

- **A (application)** means the code specifies how a norm works in Israel's circumstances: the ox, the pit, debt-servitude, procedure, intent, measure.
- **S (sanction)** means a penalty or remedy enforced by the community: death, restitution, ransom, compensation, release.
- **D (divine sanction)** is added because the code needs it. God himself is the enforcer, not the court. By the paper's own definition, that is **not** judicial law.

## A Finding Before the Table: The Code Lacks Kind-2 Vocabulary

The paper identifies kind 2 partly by its vocabulary. **None of that vocabulary appears in 20:22–23:33** (WLC lemma index):

- **בָּדַל ('to separate')** — absent.
- **חֻקָּה / חֹק ('statute'), and so *ḥuqqat ʿôlām* ('a perpetual statute')** — absent. The paper's list of 32 Torah occurrences contains none from the code.
- **דּוֹר ('generation')**, so *"throughout your generations"* — absent.
- **עוֹלָם ('for ever')** appears **once**, at 21:6. There the bond-slave serves לְעֹלָם ('permanently'): a lifetime, as the paper's own note on Jenni observes.

So **in the Book of the Covenant, kind 2 can be identified only by its content (K2-a), by its one statement of holiness (22:31, K2-b), and by parallels elsewhere in the Torah (K2-c, K2-d).** The vocabulary marks the paper relies on belong to Exodus 25–31, Leviticus and Numbers. That does not count against the scheme: the markers are sufficient, not necessary. But it means the classification of the code's kind-2 laws rests on content, and should be said to.

The code is headed *"these are the ordinances"* (הַמִּשְׁפָּטִים, 21:1). The paper notes the rabbinic distinction between *mišpāṭîm* ('ordinances', laws reason would require) and *ḥuqqîm* ('decrees', laws without evident rationale). **The code's own heading uses the first word, and, as the table shows, its contents fall overwhelmingly under kind 1.** `[I]` *Moderate–high.*

## What the Table Shows

**The count.** Of 58 commandments distinguished above:

- **39 are kind 1** (three of them with a kind-2 element or an uncertain one: 21:14; 21:28–32; and 21:13 as an application).
- **12 are kind 2** (23:33 with a kind-1 ground).
- **6 are both**: 21:2–6; 22:20; 22:25; 23:10–11; 23:12; 23:24.
- **1 is neither cleanly**: 23:21–22.

The count depends on how the laws are divided, so take the proportion rather than the numbers: **roughly two-thirds kind 1, one-fifth kind 2.** `[T]` for each classification's evidence; `[I]` for the counting.

**1. Every sanction in the code enforces kind 1.** The death penalties are at 21:12, 14, 15, 16, 17, 29 and 22:18, 19, 20, and every restitution, ransom and compensation in 21:18–22:17 attaches to a kind-1 norm. **The single exception is 22:20**, where the norm is kind 1 and the act and its sanction are cultic. **No purely kind-2 commandment in the code carries a human sanction**: the altar, the firstborn, the holy food and the feasts are commanded and left unpenalised. `[T]` *High.*

**So in the Book of the Covenant the judicial law is, in practice, the polity's application and sanction of kind 1.** The paper's claim that the judicial law applies and sanctions *both* kinds is borne out elsewhere: the Sabbath carries death (31:14–15; Num 15:32–36), and blood and fat carry being *"cut off"* (Lev 7:25–27). **But this code instances only one half of it.** `[T]` for the code; `[S: paper]` for the wider claim.

**2. The death penalties follow the land's judgement of the nations.** Two of the code's capital laws, bestiality (22:19) and sorcery (22:18), are named elsewhere as the reason the Canaanites lost the land (Lev 18:23–25; Deut 18:12). **Israel's courts are to sanction in Israel what God sanctioned in the nations, by the same created-order standard.** This is the paper's K1-b criterion working from inside the code. `[T]` *High.*

**3. Kind 2 encloses kind 1.** The kind-2 laws are not scattered. They stand at the **beginning** (the altar, 20:24–26) and are gathered in the **second half and at the end**: 22:29–31; 23:10–19; 23:32–33. The created-order law of persons and property sits in the middle. **This is the same shape the dig found in the frames**: worship at the ends, the life of the neighbour inside. In the twofold terms, Israel's priestly constitution brackets the created moral order and claims it for the covenant. `[I]` *Moderate — synthetic. Claim audit 2 rated the dig's "nested frames" design low–moderate, but confirmed worship at both ends and the two verbal turns at moderate, which is all this point needs.*

**4. "Application" is the code's dominant judicial mode.** The cases are not a statute book. They are **paradigms of application**: the ox already known to gore, the burglar by night or by day, the deposit that disappears. Many specify **procedure**: before God or the judges, the oath *"by the LORD"*, the torn carcass brought as evidence. `[T]` *High.*

**5. The New Testament carries the code's kind-1 laws into the church, and handles their sanctions by analogy.** Jesus quotes the parent law (Matt 15:4). He takes the talion out of personal retaliation (Matt 5:38–42) while keeping the principle of proportion in judgement. Paul binds his own speech by 22:28 (Acts 23:5). **The apostles never quote the code's kind-2 laws as binding.** This is the paper's "general equity" at work on this code. `[T]` for the citations; `[I]` for the pattern. *Moderate–high — the sample is small.*

## Where the Scheme Strains — Critical Assessment

The point of the exercise is to test the scheme, not only to apply it. Seven gaps or pressure points appear when it is run across this code.

**1. Kind 2's markers do not fire here.** As noted above, the code has none of the vocabulary of separation or perpetuity. Kind 2 is identified by **content** and by **parallels elsewhere** (Deut 14:21; 23:20). The definition should say the markers are characteristic, not required. Otherwise the code's altar and feasts escape the scheme altogether.

**2. "Stated rationale" can mislead.** The paper separates the kinds by **addressee and stated rationale** (§3). In this code the stated rationale for the most universal laws is **Israel's own history**: *"for you were sojourners in the land of Egypt"* (22:21; 23:9). On rationale alone, the sojourner law would be kind 2. On the norm, and on Job 31:32 and Ezek 16:49, it is plainly kind 1. **The motive is Israel's; the norm is creation's.** The scheme needs to classify the **norm**, not the motive.

**3. Several laws are two kinds at once — and the line runs through a single law.** The clearest case is interest (22:25). Mercy to the poor is kind 1; interest-free lending to *"My people"*, with interest permitted to the foreigner (Deut 23:20), is kind 2. The slave law (21:2–6), the sabbatical year (23:10–11), the Sabbath (23:12) and the idolatry laws (22:20; 23:24) divide the same way. **The paper already divides a single law into rule and sanction for the judicial category. The code suggests the two kinds should be applied the same way: to the *norm*, the *ground*, the *form* and the *sanction* of a law, separately, rather than to the law as a whole.** This refines the scheme rather than overturning it.

**4. The pre-Sinai evidence is uneven.** It is strong for:

- bloodshed and the goring ox (Gen 9:5–6);
- kidnapping (Gen 40:15; Amos 1:6, 9);
- custody and theft (Gen 31:39);
- the bride-price (Gen 34:12);
- bestiality and sorcery (Lev 18:24; Deut 18:12);
- the duties toward slaves, widows, sojourners and pledges (Job 22; 31).

It is **weak or indirect** for honour of parents (Gen 9:22–25 is narrative), for the courts and bribes (Proverbs is Israel's own wisdom), and for the enemy's animal (Prov 25:21). For those laws the kind-1 classification rests chiefly on the New Testament (Rom 1:30; 12:20; 1 Tim 1:9–10). **That is a legitimate warrant, but it is a different test (K1-b by apostolic reading, not K1-a).** Say which test is carrying the weight.

**5. Job's warrant is its setting, not its date.** Job's place outside Israel is in the text (*"in the land of Uz"*, Job 1:1) `[T]`. Its date and its relation to Sinai are reconstruction `[S]`. Using Job as evidence of norms "outside Sinai" depends on the book's setting, which is enough for K1-b. It should not be stated as evidence of norms "before Sinai".

**6. Divine-sanction laws are neither judicial nor unsanctioned.** At 22:22–24, 22:27, 23:7 and 23:21 God is the enforcer: *"I will kill you with the sword"*; *"I will hear"*; *"I will not justify"*. **No court could enforce these.** On the paper's definition they are not judicial law, yet they are not bare exhortation either. The scheme would be sharper with a named place for **covenant law under divine sanction**. These laws are also the ones closest to the code's emotional centre (the dig's Tone and Feel).

**7. The Sabbath remains the hinge, and the code adds one datum.** The paper concludes that the Fourth Word belongs to both kinds and that the evidence is divided. The code supports that verdict. Its Sabbath law (23:12) is **calendar** (kind 2) with a stated **humane purpose** (kind 1). **And unlike Exodus 20 and 31, it gives neither the creation ground nor the sign.** Here the Sabbath is grounded only in the rest of the dependent (לְמַעַן, 'so that'). `[T]` That leans slightly toward kind 1 in this code. It does not settle the question.

**Overall verdict.** The twofold scheme **fits the Book of the Covenant well at the level of norms**: every commandment can be placed, and the placement is checkable. **It fits less well at the level of whole laws**, where six laws are both. **And it exposes one asymmetry**: the code's judicial law sanctions kind 1 only. The scheme survives the test, with two refinements:

- classify the norm, the ground, the form and the sanction of a law separately;
- name divine-sanction covenant law as its own case.

## For the Point-Purpose Stage

Three points from this analysis bear on the sermon. None is a teaching point.

- **The code's centre of gravity is the created moral order, applied in Israel's polity and enclosed by Israel's worship.** A sermon on the whole code can say so without the tripartite labels.
- **The motive clauses carry Israel's history; the norms carry creation's.** That is why the code can be preached to a Gentile congregation: the norms bind them already, and the motive is theirs in Christ, who released them as God released Israel.
- **Where the congregation will feel the strain** is the kind-1 laws with Israel's sanctions, especially the death penalties. The table shows exactly which laws those are, and that the sanction, not the norm, is what has changed.
