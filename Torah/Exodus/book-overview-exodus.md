## Presenting Situation

**Narrator / author.** The book narrates in the third person, with large blocks of first-person divine speech (the law, the tabernacle instructions). The text states that Moses is commanded to *write* — the Amalek memorial (17:14), the words of the covenant (24:4), and the renewed covenant words (34:27) — and that he read the Book of the Covenant aloud (24:7). `[T]` That the book as a whole is Mosaic, and its date and compositional history, are reconstruction, not text-observation: hold them as received and hang nothing on them. `[S]`

**Audience.** Narrative-world: the enslaved-then-redeemed generation, addressed directly at points (12:24–27; 13:8, 14). `[T]` Compositional: wilderness Israel with the tabernacle standing in the camp, hearing how that tent — and the God who fills it — came to be among them. `[I]` Canonical: later Israel, and the church, reading the paradigm of redemption. `[I]` Default to the canonical audience; keep the wilderness camp in the ear, because Exodus explains the reader's own institutions (Passover, Sabbath, tabernacle, covenant).

**Occasion.** The founding of Israel as the LORD's redeemed, covenant, God-indwelt people — from a family of seventy under a death-sentence to a nation with God dwelling in its midst. `[T]` for the movement the book narrates; `[I]` for "occasion" as a compositional judgement.

**Pressure.** How can the God of the fathers keep his sworn word (Gen 15:13–16) when his people are enslaved and targeted for death — and, once redeemed, how can a holy God *dwell* among a stiff-necked people without consuming them? The book's own refrain frames the aim: "that you may know that I am the LORD" (6:7; 7:5, 17; 8:10, 22; 9:14, 16, 29; 10:2; 11:7; 14:4, 18; 29:46). `[T]` The plagues dig established that this refrain is the interpretive key of the whole contest, not a decorative tag: the cycle is theophany-by-judgment, and Egypt, Israel and the reader are all the intended knowers. `[S: dig 7:1–11:10]`

Note where the refrain *lands* the last time it is used: **29:46**, at the tabernacle. Its final object is not a plague or a sea but a residence.

**And note where the question is *asked*.** The refrain is an answer, and the 4:18–6:30 dig locates the question that provokes it: Pharaoh's **"Who is the LORD, that I should obey his voice…? I do not know the LORD" (5:2)**. Everything from 7:5 onwards answers that sentence, and Pharaoh is himself made a knower of it (7:5; 14:4, 18). Read alongside 3:13–14 (Moses asks the same thing from the other side, and gets the Name) and 6:2–8 (the Name given again, to a people who cannot hear it), the book's whole first half is a sustained reply to a question two men ask for opposite reasons. `[T]` `[S: dig 4:18–6:30, Logos-checked]` *High confidence.* **New in v1.0.3.**

**The people's own pressure, stated by the narrator.** The same dig supplies the book's most exact pastoral datum: Israel believes and worships at 4:31, and by 6:9 **"they did not listen to Moses, because of their broken spirit and harsh slavery."** The inversion is deliberate — the unit opens with faith and closes with faith crushed — and Scripture assigns the cause to the duress, not to sin. This is the human floor from which the seven "I will"s of 6:6–8 are spoken. `[T]` `[S: dig 4:18–6:30]` *High confidence.* **New in v1.0.3.**

**Purpose.** To reveal the LORD as the covenant-keeping Redeemer who defeats the powers, binds his people to himself at Sinai, and comes down to dwell — moving the reader from a God who *hears and remembers* (2:24) to a God whose *glory fills the tent* (40:34). The digs sharpen the goal twice over: the rescue's stated end is not liberation but God himself — "I bore you on eagles' wings and **brought you to myself**" (19:4) `[S: dig 19:1–8]` — and the book states its own purpose outright at **29:46**: "they shall know that I am the LORD their God, who brought them out of the land of Egypt **that I might dwell among them**." Grammatically the dwelling is a purpose clause hanging off the exodus, not a sequel to it. That is Exodus's own thesis sentence about its own plot, and every other statement of purpose in this overview should be read as a gloss on it. `[T]` / `[S: dig 25:1–31:18]`

## Structural Arc Map

| Verses | Movement | Function |
|--------|----------|----------|
| 1:1–15:21 | **From Egypt** — bondage to deliverance | The assault on the seed (1), the deliverer (2), the call (3–4), the contest of the plagues (7–11), Passover (12), the Sea and its Song (14–15): God redeems his people by blood and water |
| 15:22–18:27 | **Through the wilderness** — the journey to the mountain | Testing at the water, manna, the rock, and Amalek (15–17); Jethro's confession and the delegation of judges (18): the redeemed are formed and begin to grumble |
| 19:1–40:38 | **At the mountain** — covenant and dwelling | Covenant proposal and consent (19:1–8), the descent and the Ten Words (19:9–20:21), the Book of the Covenant sealed in blood (20:22–24:18), the tabernacle instructions (25–31), the calf and the revelation of glory (32–34), the dwelling built and filled (35–40) |

**Device.** A **linear, spatial build** — *from Egypt → through the wilderness → at the mountain* — mapping the progressive proximity of God: deliverance → journey → dwelling. The proportions are the point: nearly half the book stays at one mountain, because the weight of Exodus is not the escape from Egypt but *how a holy God can dwell with a sinful people*, the question chs. 32–34 nearly answer in the negative and chs. 35–40 answer in grace. Two large command→fulfilment doublings (instructions 25–31 ↔ construction 35–40) and an ascent→descent pattern (Moses up into glory, ch. 24 ↔ down into the calf, ch. 32) shape the Sinai block. `[T]` / `[S: Schnittjer confirms the three-movement spatial shape]`

**The descent-spine (sweep-confirmed).** The spatial arc is carried by a single verb. "I have **come down** (*yārad*) to deliver" (3:8) is planted at the bush; the LORD **comes down** on Sinai (19:11, 18, 20); the glory finally settles in the tent (40:34). The bush is the first move of a descent that ends in the tabernacle — so the book's structure and its keynote are the same thing. `[S: dig 3:1–4:17, Headline 2]` *High confidence.*

**Refinement at the Sinai seam.** The digs re-cut the head of the Sinai block: 19:1–8 (proposal and consent) and 19:9–20:21 (the LORD comes down), dividing where the text divides (19:9 opens the descent it announces) and ignoring the 19/20 chapter break. The theophany-frame at 19:16 and 20:18 — the same thunder, lightning, trumpet and smoking mountain — brackets the Ten Words, so the Decalogue is spoken *inside* the terror, not after it. `[S: digs 19:1–8; 19:9–20:21]` *High confidence.* The Preaching Units table below is revised accordingly.

**Why the tabernacle block sits where it does.** The instructions do not merely follow the covenant; they answer a problem chapter 24 has just created. At 24:16 "the glory of the LORD **dwelt** (*šākan*) on Mount Sinai" — the glory has taken up residence on a mountain that Israel must leave. Chapters 25–31 make the dwelling portable: poles through rings, carried on shoulders, going where the camp goes. The same verb moves from the mountain (24:16) to the people (25:8) to the finished tent (40:34–35). **The tabernacle is Sinai made portable** — a reading the commentators share (Morales, NSBT 37; Köstenberger & Goswell). `[T]` for the verb-chain; `[S: dig 25:1–31:18, Logos-checked]` *High confidence.*

**And the calf is set inside it.** Chapters 25–31 are spoken during the forty days of 24:18, and ch. 32 opens "when the people saw that Moses **delayed**." The apostasy is placed as a jarring break between God's instructions (25–31) and their communication to Israel (35:1–19), and Israel's making of the calf parodies the making of the tabernacle. A **Sabbath inclusio** encloses it: the Sabbath speech that ends the instructions (31:12–17) and the Sabbath command that opens their delivery (35:1–3). So the Sinai block's real shape is **instructed (25–31) → broken (32) → interceded and renewed (33–34) → built (35–40)**, with the sign of covenant fellowship framing the breach. `[T]` for the placement; `[S: dig 25:1–31:18, Logos-checked]` *High confidence.*

**The Sinai block's driving question: who goes with whom.** The presence-spine is not a steady ascent. Chapters 32–34 nearly lose it: "make us gods who shall **go before us**" (32:1) is answered by "my angel shall **go before you**" (32:34) and "I will **not go up** among you" (33:3) — a granted request that is in fact a sentence — and it is reversed only by petition: "if your presence will not go with me, do not bring us up" (33:15), "my presence will go with you" (33:14), "let the Lord **go in the midst of us**" (34:9). The same prepositional phrase carries the promise (25:8; 29:45–46), the threat (33:3) and the plea (34:9). `[T]` `[S: dig 32:1–34:35]` *High confidence.* **New in v0.2.2 — and it is the Sinai block's real crisis.**

**And the book ends in motion, one verse short of its answer.** The construction account renders obedience in a form the calf had made necessary: "as the LORD commanded Moses" **seven times** in the making of the garments (39:1, 5, 7, 21, 26, 29, 31) and **seven times** in the erection (40:19, 21, 23, 25, 27, 29, 32) — each seven followed by a blessing, Moses' at 39:43 and God's own at 40:34. Then the glory fills the tent **and shuts the mediator out** (40:35), and the last three verses shift to habitual time: the cloud, the fire, "throughout all their journeys" (40:36–38). **Exodus does not conclude; it becomes a routine, and hands the problem of 40:35 straight to Leviticus 1:1, where God calls.** `[T]` `[S: dig 35:1–40:38, Logos-checked]` *High confidence.* **New in v0.3.0.**

**A three-zone gradient, transposed.** Sinai had graded access — summit (Moses alone), boundary (priests and elders, 19:22; 24:1, 9), base (the people behind a limit, 19:12). The tent has the same gradient — Most Holy Place, Holy Place, court — so the holiness that was **vertical** becomes **horizontal**, and the axis of approach rotates from *up* to *in*. `[T]` for both structures; `[I]` for the transposition. `[S: dig 25:1–31:18]` *Moderate–high.*

## Microscript

*The God of the fathers keeps his sworn word: hearing his enslaved people, he comes down to redeem them by the blood of a lamb and through the sea, binds them to himself in covenant at Sinai, and — the true summit — comes down to dwell in their midst, so that the glory that filled creation now fills a tent among a stiff-necked people who deserve to be consumed and instead are graced.* Keynotes: **"come down" / "dwell"** (*yārad* / *šākan*); **"stiff-necked"** (*qĕšēh-ʿōrep*, 32:9; 33:3, 5; 34:9 — the book's diagnosis of its own people); the refrain **"that you may know that I am the LORD."** Structural pulse: deliverance → covenant → dwelling; and grace **before** law, always. `[T]`

**The microscript's last clause is now text-warranted.** "A stiff-necked people who deserve to be consumed and instead are graced" is not a gloss: it is the passage's own vocabulary, and its pivot is 34:9, where Moses turns the diagnosis into the *reason* for the presence — "let the Lord go in the midst of us, **for** it is a stiff-necked people" — using the same causal *kî* that grounded God's withdrawal at 33:3. `[S: dig 32:1–34:35]` *(Note: the LXX lacks 32:9, so the count is fourfold in Hebrew, threefold in Greek.)*

Test against the movements: from-Egypt (redemption) → wilderness (formation) → mountain (covenant and dwelling). Each serves the presence-spine; the calf (32–34) is the crisis that makes the final dwelling an act of sheer grace.

**Sweep confirmation.** Two digs independently placed *dwelling* at the end of the arc from inside the first half of the book. The Song of the Sea already looks past the rescue to "your holy abode… the sanctuary, O Lord, which your hands have established" (15:13, 17) — freedom is *for* God, not merely *from* Egypt. `[S: dig 13:17–15:21, Headline 2]` And the covenant proposal names the destination as God himself: "brought you to myself" (19:4). `[S: dig 19:1–8, Headline 4]` The microscript stands; it is now demonstrated from the text rather than projected onto it.

## Christological Trajectory

- **The Name and the Angel (3:2–15).** The narrator identifies the appearer as "the angel of the LORD" and then simply as "the LORD"/"God" — a strong **Christophany candidate**, to be held at moderate–high confidence and not asserted as settled. The Name itself runs a direct line: the "I AM" of the bush is claimed by Jesus (John 8:58), and Jesus cites 3:6 (Mark 12:26). Crucially, the Name is glossed by its own context — "I will be with you" (3:12) and "I AM" (3:14) are the same Hebrew verb — so the Name asserts self-existence *as covenant presence*. The book's whole later question is already compressed into it. `[S: dig 3:1–4:17]` *Confidence: high (the Name); moderate–high (Christophany).*
- **Passover / substitution.** The firstborn-son claim (4:22–23) sets a fuse that burns to the Passover, where blood substitutes for the firstborn (12), completed at the Sea (14) and sealed in "the blood of the covenant" (24:8). Two sharpenings from the dig: **the danger passing through the land is God himself** — "I will pass through… I will strike" (12:12) — so Passover is rescue from the judgment *of God*, not merely escape from Egypt; and **the distinction on the night is applied blood, not ethnicity** — Israel's firstborn stood under the identical threat. "Christ our Passover lamb has been sacrificed" (1 Cor 5:7); no bone broken (John 19:36); the firstborn Son who was *not* spared (Rom 8:32). `[S: dig 12:1–13:16]` *Confidence: high.*
- **The seven "I will"s, and the covenant formula (6:6–8).** Bracketed by "I am the LORD" at 6:2 and 6:8, God speaks seven first-person promises — bring out, deliver, **redeem** (*gāʾal*), take you to be my people, be your God, bring you in, give it to you. Two things run forward. (a) ***Gāʾal* makes its first Exodus appearance here** — the kinsman-redeemer verb, the one that presumes a relative with both the right and the will to pay; it is the vocabulary behind "you were ransomed… not with perishable things such as silver or gold, but with the precious blood of Christ" (1 Pet 1:18–19), and behind Isaiah's Redeemer (Isa 43:1; 44:6). (b) **The covenant formula** — "I will take you to be my people, and I will be your God" — is stated here in full for the first time, and runs to Jer 31:33, 2 Cor 6:16, and its terminus at Rev 21:3, "he will dwell with them, and they will be his people." **Exodus states its own destination at 6:7, thirty-four chapters before 40:34.** `[T]` `[S: dig 4:18–6:30, Logos-checked]` *Confidence: high.* **New in v1.0.3.**
- **The redeemed people already non-ethnic.** A mixed multitude goes up (12:38) and the circumcised sojourner keeps the feast under "one law" (12:48–49) — the redeemed congregation is bounded by covenant sign, yet open to the outsider who comes under it, from the very first night. A trajectory to the gospel's welcome of the nations. `[S: dig 12:1–13:16]` *Confidence: moderate–high.*
- **Redemption / new exodus.** The whole rescue becomes the template: Jesus' death is an *exodos* (Luke 9:31); Isaiah 40–55 reads Exodus forward; Israel-brought-from-Egypt is applied to Christ (Matt 2:15 ← Hos 11:1). The Sea is read as baptism (1 Cor 10:1–2), and its Song confesses "he has become my *yĕšûʿâ*" (15:2) — the noun behind the name Jesus. The Divine Warrior who "will reign for ever" (15:18) is the Christ who "disarmed the rulers and authorities" (Col 2:15) and whose song is sung again by the Lamb's people (Rev 15:3). `[S: dig 13:17–15:21]` *Confidence: high.*
- **The mediator who points beyond himself — and whose institution is born at Sinai.** Moses is commissioned at the bush by a God who answers "who am I?" with "I will be with you" (3:11–12); he shuttles between God and people before any theophany (19:3, 7, 8); and when God speaks, the people beg for him — "you speak to us… but do not let God speak to us, lest we die" (20:19). The narrative endorses the request: "the people stood far off, while Moses drew near to the thick darkness where God was" (20:21). Deuteronomy has God commend it (5:28) and turn it into the prophet-like-Moses promise (18:15–19). The mediator-gap is made physical here, and filled at 1 Tim 2:5; Heb 3; 8–9; 12:24. After the calf Moses offers to be blotted out (32:32) — an offer that exposes its own limit, and the Christ-shaped vacancy Rom 9:3 gestures at. `[S: digs 3:1–4:17; 19:1–8; 19:9–20:21]` *Confidence: high.*
- **The vow that cannot be kept.** "All that the LORD has spoken we will do" (19:8) is genuine consent, not folly — but the next unit already undoes it (20:19), and ch. 32 shatters it. The human "yes" is made good only by the covenant-keeper who obeyed and bore the curse (Gal 3:13). `[S: digs 19:1–8; 19:9–20:21]` *Confidence: moderate–high.*
- **The hardened heart → the new heart.** The heart no plague can soften (7–11) is answered by the Spirit who gives a heart of flesh (Ezek 36:26; 2 Cor 3:3) — Christ softens what judgment only hardens. `[S: dig 7:1–11:10]` *Confidence: high.*
- **Presence / tabernacle.** God comes down to dwell (25:8; 40:34) → the Word "tabernacled among us" (John 1:14) → "the dwelling of God is with man" (Rev 21:3). The Song of the Sea already names this destination (15:13, 17). The glory that fills the tent is the goal the whole book climbs toward. *Confidence: high.* The 25–31 dig sharpens the typology: **the text signals its own provisionality** — it calls itself a copy of a *tabnît* shown on the mountain (25:9, 40) — which is why Hebrews 8:5 can quote it without importing anything foreign. `[S: dig 25:1–31:18]` **Remaining gap:** chs. 32–40 still undug.
- **"The blood of the covenant" (24:8).** Moses divides the blood — half thrown against the altar, half thrown on the people — and names it: "Behold the blood of the covenant that the LORD has made with you in accordance with all these words." One blood, two parties, applied to a documented covenant. **Jesus takes the phrase onto his own lips over a cup** ("this is my blood of the covenant", Matt 26:28; cf. Mark 14:24; "the new covenant in my blood", Luke 22:20), and **Hebrews 9:20 quotes the verse** to argue that "without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness" (9:22). *Tests: category, NT precedent, escalation and authorial pattern all pass.* `[S: dig 20:22–24:18]` *Confidence: high — as strong as OT→NT connections get, and the reason 20:22–24:18 is the series hinge.* **New in v1.0.0.**
- **The covenant meal (24:9–11).** Seventy-four men "beheld God, and ate and drank", and the narrator adds that "he did not lay his hand on" them — telling the reader what did *not* happen, which is how we know it might have. Eating with God is the shape covenant fellowship takes, and the shape the Supper takes after it; parodied at 32:6. `[S: dig 20:22–24:18]` *Confidence: moderate–high (trajectory, not typology).* **New in v1.0.0.**
- **The sanctuary stands on ransom money.** The hundred talents of census silver — the half-shekel paid "as a ransom for his life" (30:12–16) — "were for casting the bases of the sanctuary and the bases of the veil; a hundred bases for the hundred talents, a talent a base" (38:25–27). Every upright of God's house is footed on atonement, and the arithmetic checks. Commentators give it exactly this weight: the sanctuary's structural stability rests on the ransom paid for communal life before God (T. D. Alexander, Apollos OTC, 2:603). → 1 Pet 1:18–19, which names silver and gold precisely to say the real ransom was not made of them. `[S: dig 35:1–40:38, Logos-checked]` *Confidence: high on the text; moderate–high on the 1 Peter resonance.* **New in v0.3.0.**
- **Arrival and exclusion, in one scene.** 40:34 is the book's destination — and 40:35 is its unfinished business: "Moses was not able to enter." If the mediator cannot go in, no one can, and that is where Exodus stops. Hebrews names this as the point — "the way into the holy places was not yet opened" (9:8) — and answers it: "we have confidence to enter… by the new and living way that he opened for us through the curtain, that is, through his flesh" (10:19–20). **Preach 40:34 without 40:35 and the gospel has nothing to open.** `[S: dig 35:1–40:38, Logos-checked]` *Confidence: high.* **New in v0.3.0.**
- **The mercy seat and the empty throne.** The *kappōret* is where blood is applied *and* where God speaks from (25:22) — law inside the box, atonement on the lid, voice above it. The LXX calls it the *hilastērion*, and Paul calls Christ a *hilastērion* whom God put forward by his blood (Rom 3:25; cf. Heb 9:5). Note also what is *not* there: the throne-furniture of an ANE sanctuary is kept and the image that normally sat on it is abolished — Israel "saw no form" (Deut 4:12, 15). The most important thing in God's throne room is an absence, and the Christian claim is that it was finally filled by a person. `[S: dig 25:1–31:18, Logos-checked]` *Confidence: high on the lexical chain; moderate–high on pressing the empty-throne point.* **New in v0.2.1.**
- **The high priest who carries names in and guilt out.** Aaron bears the tribes' names on his shoulders and over his heart "for regular remembrance before the LORD" (28:12, 29) and bears their guilt on his forehead "that they may be accepted" (28:38) — and must first be atoned for himself, seven days over (29:1, 35–37). Likeness and insufficiency in one rite: Heb 7:25, 27. `[S: dig 25:1–31:18]` *Confidence: high.* **New in v0.2.1.**
- **The character of God at 34:6–7.** "Merciful and gracious… forgiving iniquity… who will by no means clear the guilty" — a tension the OT states but cannot resolve, answered at the cross where guilt is *borne, not cleared* (Rom 3:25–26). The formula is anticipated in the Decalogue's own jealous-God/*ḥesed* clause (20:5–6), and it becomes **the most re-used text within the OT** (Num 14:18 — Moses quoting it back at the next rebellion; Neh 9:17; Ps 86:15; 103:8; 145:8; Joel 2:13; Jonah 4:2; Nah 1:3; Mic 7:18). Note the two clauses stand side by side with **no adversative in the Hebrew** — the tension is not softened, and a sermon that dissolves it has thrown away the passage's best gift. `[S: dig 32:1–34:35]` *Confidence: high.*
- **Moses' refused substitution.** "If you will forgive their sin— but if not, blot me out of your book" (32:32); "Whoever has sinned against me, I will blot out" (32:33). The offer is right and the offerer is not qualified — and Paul makes the identical unfulfillable wish (Rom 9:3) before naming the one who *was* made a curse (Gal 3:13). **The passage's most important Christological datum is a refusal.** `[S: dig 32:1–34:35]` *Confidence: high.* **New in v0.2.2.**
- **John 1:14–18 as a compressed retelling of Exodus 33–34.** "Tabernacled among us… we have seen his **glory**… full of **grace and truth**" (1:14 ← *ḥesed weʾĕmet*, 34:6); "the **law** was given through Moses" (1:17 ← 34:1, 28); "**no one has ever seen God**" (1:18 ← 33:20); "the only Son… has made him known" — where Moses was given a back and a rock-cleft. The four markers arrive in the Exodus narrative's own order, and **the request of 33:18 is answered in John 1:14.** `[S: dig 32:1–34:35, Logos-checked]` *Confidence: high.* **New in v0.2.2.**
- **34:29–35 → 2 Corinthians 3:7–18**, and note *why*: the LXX renders *qāran* (34:29) as **δεδόξασται, "had been glorified"** — so Paul's whole *doxa* argument is reading the word the Greek translator supplied. `[S: dig 32:1–34:35, Logos-checked]` *Confidence: high.* **New in v0.2.2.**

**Moralism check.** Resist "be brave like the midwives," "be a bold leader like Moses," "overcome your excuses like Moses," "conquer your Red Sea," "make and keep your vow like Israel," "be willing to sacrifice like the givers of ch. 35." Grace precedes every demand — and the digs found the rule to be **textual, not merely narrative**: the covenant proposal grounds its first "if" in what God has already done ("*now therefore*," 19:4–5), and the Ten Words open "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out… of the house of slavery" (20:2) before a single command. **And it operates inside the case law too, which is the strongest form of the claim:** "You shall not wrong a sojourner… **for you were sojourners in the land of Egypt**" (22:21); "**You know the heart of a sojourner**" (23:9); and, in a statute about a poor man's pledged coat, "if he cries to me, I will hear, **for I am gracious**" (22:27) — the first occurrence in Exodus of the adjective that stands second in the self-declaration of 34:6. `[S: dig 20:22–24:18]` **The indicative is not only in the preamble; it is in the statutes.** At the Sea Israel's part is to "stand firm… and be silent" (14:13–14); at the door, there is nothing in the lamb to imitate, only something to receive. God is the Redeemer-hero; Moses points to the greater Mediator. Preach the indicative of rescue under every imperative. `[T]` / `[S: digs 13:17–15:21; 19:1–8; 19:9–20:21]`

## Preaching Traps

1. **Preaching the plagues as a spectacle (or a one-to-one map onto Egyptian gods).** *Bites at:* 7:1–11:10. *Why wrong:* 12:12 warrants the theme of judgment on Egypt's gods, but the specific one-to-one mappings are largely later reconstruction; the point is "that you may know that I am the LORD." *Corrective:* preach the self-revelation of God in judgment, and preach the *shape* — three triads, rising intensity, the Goshen distinction, the magicians' collapse — not nine sermons on nine disasters. `[S: dig 7:1–11:10 confirms]`
2. **Moralising Moses into a leadership seminar.** *Bites at:* **2:11–15 hardest of all**, then 3–4; 18; 32. *Why wrong:* the book keeps exposing the mediator's limits so we look past him — the call is answered not by upgrading Moses but by the sufficiency of God, and the fifth objection is marked by the narrator as sin (4:14). **At 2:11–15 the narrator supplies two condemning details** (he checked no one was watching; he hid the body) and reports his fear of exposure rather than remorse — so "be like Moses, standing up for the oppressed" makes a model of the chapter's one failure. *Corrective:* preach the greater Mediator the book reaches for — and at ch. 2, preach the **rejection**, which is what Acts 7:35 does. **Two further passage-level forms named by the 2:1–25 dig:** the Midian years as "God's waiting room" (the text says nothing about Moses' formation there), and reading the chapter as Moses' biography when its last three verses change the subject to God. `[S: digs 3:1–4:17; 2:1–25]`
3. **Treating the tabernacle chapters (25–31; 35–40) as tedious detail to skip.** *Bites at:* the whole Sinai block. *Why wrong:* the length **is** the emphasis — Exodus gives more space to this tent than to the plagues, the Sea or the Ten Words — and the creation-shaped, glory-filled climax is the book's *destination*. *Corrective:* preach the tent as the answer to Eden's lost presence and the anticipation of Immanuel; preach the seven-speech shape and the dwelling purpose rather than the fittings. **Tested and confirmed by the 25–31 dig**, which adds a passage-level twin: *preaching the furniture* — assigning a symbolic meaning to each item when the text glosses only a few (the pattern, the testimony, the meeting-place, the remembrance-stones, the guilt-bearing plate). `[S: dig 25:1–31:18]`
4. **Divorcing law from grace (Sinai as merit).** *Bites at:* 19:5 ("if you will indeed obey…") and the whole 19–24 block, and hardest at the case law of 21–23. *Why wrong:* the "if" conditions vocation and status for an *already-rescued* people, not the rescue itself; v. 4 forecloses the works-reading before the "if" arrives; 20:2 puts the indicative inside the Decalogue's own grammar; **and the case law carries its own motive clauses — "for you were sojourners in the land of Egypt" (22:21; 23:9) and "for I am gracious" (22:27).** *Corrective:* the imperative rides on the indicative — always, and the statutes say so themselves. **Tested in the block most exposed to it and confirmed.** `[S: digs 19:1–8; 19:9–20:21; 20:22–24:18]`
5. **Sentimentalising the Red Sea ("name your Red Sea").** *Bites at:* 13:17–15:21. *Why wrong:* Israel is silent and helpless; the LORD fights (14:13–14). The Sea also *inverts* the Nile-as-grave of ch. 1, and the Song is a janus looking back to the rescue and forward to the sanctuary. *Corrective:* preach accomplished salvation and the reversal; let the matched patterns (1↔14; 24↔32; 25–31↔35–40) shape a series. `[S: dig 13:17–15:21 confirms]`
6. **Reading the calf (32) as merely Israel's lapse.** *Bites at:* 32–34. *Why wrong:* it exposes that the deepest problem is the redeemed people themselves, which no exodus cures — the pressure that makes 34:6–7 and the later new-heart promises necessary. *Corrective:* preach **the stiff neck** and the God who stays. **Tested and confirmed by the 32–34 dig**, with three passage-level forms named there (the Levites' sword as a model of zeal; "be an intercessor like Moses"; making the calf a sin the congregation has never committed). `[S: dig 32:1–34:35]`

   **Warrant correction, v0.2.2:** this trap previously read "preach the stiff-necked **heart**". **Exodus 32–34 never uses the word "heart"** — the diagnosis throughout is *qĕšēh-ʿōrep*, a stiff **neck**, an animal that will not turn to the rein. The *heart* language is **Deuteronomy's**, and Deuteronomy supplies it while retelling this very episode: "stiff-necked" at Deut 9:6, 13, and then, in the immediate aftermath, "**Circumcise therefore the foreskin of your heart, and be no more stiff-necked**" (Deut 10:16), leading to 29:4 and 30:6. Exodus's own next use of "heart" runs the other way — the **willing hearts** of 35:5, 21, 22, 26, 29. Preach the trajectory in that order (Exodus: neck → Deuteronomy: heart → the prophets: new heart → Christ), and the claim is unassailable. **Untested — no dig yet.**
7. **Sentimentalising the nearness.** *Bites at:* 25:1–31:18, and anywhere the presence-spine is preached. *Why wrong:* "God just wants to be close to you" — with the veil, the blood, the graded access and the three "lest he die" clauses (28:35, 43; 30:20–21) quietly omitted — makes the entire architecture unintelligible. If nearness were safe, none of those seven chapters would be necessary. *Corrective:* hold both; the cost is the measure of the desire. `[S: dig 25:1–31:18]` **New in v0.2.1.**
8. **The Bezalel vocation sermon, detached from its object.** *Bites at:* 31:1–11. *Why wrong:* the Spirit-filling is the sixth of seven speeches and is for *this* building; lifted out, it becomes a general encouragement about creativity that the text is not giving. (It is, though, the first place in the canon where anyone is said to be **filled** with the Spirit of God — Gen 41:38 describes Spirit-gifting in other words.) *Corrective:* God specifies, then God equips. The vocational encouragement follows; it does not lead. `[S: dig 25:1–31:18, Logos-checked]` **New in v0.2.1.**
9. **"Be brave like the midwives."** *Bites at:* 1:15–21. *Why wrong:* it makes two minor characters the chapter's engine, when the engine is the creation blessing (1:7, 12, 20) and the oath sworn to Abram (Gen 15:13). The midwives are genuinely commended and rewarded — but the chapter would work without them, and the narrator's verdict grounds the reward in **the fear of God**, not in their courage or their answer to Pharaoh. *Corrective:* preach the promise that outruns the policy; let the midwives be what the text makes them — the one place where a human being does the right thing in a chapter where God is almost silent. `[S: dig 1:1–22, Logos-checked]` **New in v1.0.1.**
10. **Scolding or defending the midwives' answer (1:19).** *Bites at:* the same scene. *Why wrong:* whether v. 19 is a lie is a real question, but it is not the chapter's question, and a sermon that becomes a debate about deception has lost the plot. **The text states the ground of the blessing and it is not the answer** — "because the midwives feared God, he gave them families" (1:21). *Corrective:* name the debate in two sentences, say where the text puts the weight, move on. `[S: dig 1:1–22, Logos-checked]` **New in v1.0.1.**
11. **Scorning the people's vow (19:8) or their recoil (20:19).** *Bites at:* 19:1–20:21. *Why wrong:* the vow is genuine consent, and God explicitly *commends* the request for a mediator (Deut 5:28) — the request is right, and it seeds the prophet-like-Moses institution. Sermons that mock the people miss that their fear is the correct response to a holy God, and that Moses answers it with a paradox, not a rebuke: "do not fear… that the fear of God may be before you" (20:20). *Corrective:* preach the two fears — terror transposed into reverence — and the mediator God provides. `[S: digs 19:1–8; 19:9–20:21]` **New in v0.2.0.**

12. **Blaming Israel for not listening (6:9).** *Bites at:* 4:18–6:30, and anywhere the congregation's discouragement is in view. *Why wrong:* the instinct is to preach "if only they'd had more faith they'd have believed the promises" — but **the narrator gives the reason and it is not sin**: "they did not listen to Moses, **because of their broken spirit and harsh slavery**" (6:9). Scripture here describes faith failing under duress and assigns the cause to the duress. *Corrective:* say what the text says. Someone in the room has stopped being able to hear good news, and this verse is for them, not against them. **Two companion forms named by the same dig:** preaching 5:22–23 as a leadership sermon on ministry discouragement (the complaint is real and unrebuked, but it is the set-up, not the point — the answer to "why" is "who"); and skipping 4:24–26 in the reading, which the congregation will notice. `[S: dig 4:18–6:30, Logos-checked]` **New in v1.0.3.**

**Pastoral / interpretive landmines (flag).** The hardening of Pharaoh (divine and human agency; the verb-marked shift at 9:12; Rom 9); the aspect of *ʾehyeh ʾăšer ʾehyeh* (3:14, "I AM" / "I WILL BE"); 4:11 ("who makes him mute… or blind?") and its weight for hearers with disabilities; the identity of "the destroyer" (12:23); the 430 years (12:40, MT vs LXX/Samaritan, Gal 3:17); "the LORD is a man of war" (15:3) and the drowning of the Egyptian army; **21:22–25, where the LXX has a materially different law**. Give each careful solo work before the pulpit. `[S: sweep + dig open questions]`

**Two landmines now worked (v1.0.3), and how to preach them.**

- **"Bridegroom of blood" (4:24–26).** *Worked, not solved.* **The LORD's target is Moses, not the son** — the syntax and the sense both run that way. Three readings are live: a **covenant-obligation** reading (Moses, about to demand that Pharaoh release God's firstborn son, has neglected the covenant sign on his own); a **wordplay** reading linking the bridegroom's blood to covenant blood; and a **sardonic redefinition** in Zipporah's mouth. The ambiguity may be deliberate — the text resists neat resolution and leaves the reader with a woman performing a priestly act to save her husband's life. *Preach it in three honest minutes:* name the likeliest reading, name the target, name what the text withholds, and let it stand. **Do not manufacture certainty.** Note the placement — it falls immediately after "I will kill your firstborn son" (4:23), which is the strongest clue the text gives. *`[S: dig 4:18–6:30]` — **this portion of the Logos answer returned uncited**; treat as a summary of standard options.*
- **The sense of 6:3.** *Worked.* Four live options, with sources. (1) **Character versus name** — Rashi: *ʾĒl Šadday* is the promise-giving name, YHWH the fulfilment name; Rambam: providential versus miracle-working power. (2) **Rhetorical question** — "Did I not make myself known to them?", with W. J. Martin as a named proponent; the Hebrew permits it. (3) **Experiential knowledge**, and T. D. Alexander's is the sharpest formulation: the verb "make oneself known" refers **not to announcing a previously unknown name but to God's acting in a way consistent with what the name means** (Apollos OTC 2:114–15). (4) **Source-critical** — noted as existing, not adopted; Alexander objects that it struggles to explain why a final redactor would overlook so plain a contradiction. *Preach it in one careful paragraph, on Alexander's formulation.* **Flag for the pulpit:** the NIV's "fully" in "did not make myself fully known" is a **translator's supply**, and a hearer following in that version will not know it. `[S: dig 4:18–6:30, Logos-checked]` **Still open:** how the LXX and the Targums construe the clause — not pursued, and it bears directly on option 2.

## Finalise Adjudications (v0.1.0 → v0.2.0)

Every tension the digs raised, decided in the open.

1. **Unit structure at Sinai — accepted.** Both 19-unit digs argue for the re-cut. Adopted in the Preaching Units table and the arc map. The superseded 19:1–25 and 19:16–20:21 digs are archived, not deleted.
2. **Warrant re-tagging in the Presenting Situation — corrected.** v0.1.0 tagged Mosaic narration `[T]`/`[S]` as a single claim. Split: the third-person narration and the write-commands (17:14; 24:4; 34:27) are `[T]`; Mosaic authorship of the book, its date and its compositional history are `[S]`. Compositional and canonical audiences downgraded to `[I]`.
3. **Grace-before-law is textual, not just narrative — accepted and promoted.** Carried into the Christological trajectory's moralism check and Trap 4, with 19:4–5 and 20:2 as the two hooks.
4. **The mediator-institution's origin — accepted.** The trajectory previously ran from the commissioning (3) to the calf (32); it now names 20:19–21 as the point where the institution is *born* and endorsed (Deut 5:28 → 18:15–19).
5. **De-creation strengthened, frame still `[I]`.** The *tannîn*/*nāḥāš* distinction (7:9) and the *rûaḥ*-over-the-waters crossing (14:21) are added as text-level hooks. The individual reversals are high-confidence; the overarching de-creation *frame* remains moderate — do not preach it as certain.
6. **Christophany at the bush — added at held confidence.** Moderate–high, not settled. The Name→John 8:58 line is high.
7. **New trap added (7).** Sermons that scorn the people's vow or their recoil now have a named corrective.
8. **No dig contradicted the overview.** All six reported "no material tension." That is reassuring but not self-validating: the three untested ⭐ HIGH units are precisely the ones on which the overview's climax (dwelling, glory, 34:6–7) rests.
