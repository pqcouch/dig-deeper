## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims that emerged across multiple tools:

1. **The unit is bracketed by a stated purpose and its fulfilment: God comes down "that the people may… believe you [Moses] forever" (19:9), and the people answer "You speak to us… but do not let God speak to us" (20:19). The whole event exists to authenticate the mediator.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Positional Necessity, Structure, Quotation/Allusion (Move 4). This outer purpose-frame (19:9 → 20:19) is the unit's controlling arc — the reason the re-cut opens here rather than at 19:16.

2. **Inside that purpose-frame sits the theophany-frame: 19:16 and 20:18 repeat the same phenomena (thunder, lightning, trumpet, smoking mountain), bracketing the Ten Words — so the Decalogue is spoken *within* the terror, not after it.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Repetition (resumptive repetition), Narrator's Comment. The chapter break at 19/20 hides a single scene. The frame is the argument: God gives his law and, in the same event, exposes the people's need for a mediator.

3. **Grace precedes law at the seam itself: the Ten Words open "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out… of the house of slavery" (20:2) before any command — the same prologue-before-stipulation shape as the proposal (19:4–5).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Linking Words, Structure, Quotation/Allusion. Redemption grounds every imperative; grace-before-law is built into the grammar of the Decalogue.

4. **The people rightly ask for a mediator (20:19); the narrative endorses it — "the people stood far off, while Moses drew near to the thick darkness where God was" (20:21).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Narrator's Comment, Christological Reading. Deut 5:28 has God commend the request, and it seeds the prophet-like-Moses institution (Deut 18:15–19) and the one Mediator (1 Tim 2:5; Heb 12:24). The mediator-gap is made physical.

5. **Moses resolves the paradox of two fears: "Do not fear (_al-tîrā'û_)… that the fear (_yir'â_) of God may be before you, that you may not sin" (20:20).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary (the *yārē'* wordplay), Tone, So What?. The terror is pedagogical — dread meant to be transposed into a reverence that keeps from sin.

## Phase 0.6 — Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement (19:1–8).** Israel has arrived, received the eagles'-wings covenant proposal (19:4–6), and sworn "All that the LORD has spoken we will do" (19:8). A covenant has been *offered and accepted*; it now awaits ratification. `[T]`

**Necessity answer.** Exodus 19:9–20:21 exists *here* to *inaugurate by God's own voice* the covenant just sworn, and to establish the mediation the rest of Sinai will run on. The unit's own opening states its purpose: God comes "that the people may hear… and may also believe you forever" (19:9) — the theophany is to authenticate Moses as mediator. The consecration (19:10–15) prepares a people to survive the descent; the theophany (19:16–25) enacts it; the Ten Words (20:1–17) are spoken from within it; and the people's terror and plea (20:18–21) *achieve the stated purpose* (19:9) — they now want exactly the mediator God intended. Everything downstream flows from this: the rest of the covenant law comes *through Moses* (20:22–24:18), because the people have just asked for that; the tabernacle (25–40) is the institutional form of the mediated presence they now know they need. `[T]`

**Implication.** The re-cut opens the unit at 19:9 (not 19:16) because **19:9 is the unit's thesis**: it names the purpose the whole arc fulfils. The consecration (19:10–15) faces *forward* into the theophany, not back into the proposal. Read the Decalogue inside this arc and it is the voice of the redeeming God, so holy that his own rescued people beg him to stop — and the mediator God announced in 19:9 is vindicated in 20:19.

**Revisit after Tool 11.** Move 4 confirms the frame: 19:9's purpose-clause is discharged at 20:19, and the vow of 19:8 (previous unit) is answered — reversed — at 20:19. The double frame (19:9 → 20:19 outer; 19:16 → 20:18 inner) is the unit's architecture.

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Passage-level:** to inaugurate the sworn covenant by God's own thunderous descent, to deliver the covenant's foundational words directly (20:1–17), and — through the people's terror and plea (20:18–21) — to establish that a holy God's unmediated address drives sinful people to need, and rightly to request, a mediator. The unit secures the *authority* of the law (spoken by God) and the *necessity of mediation* (which 19:9 announced and 20:19 achieves), grounding the mediated law-code and the tabernacle that follow. The Decalogue's preamble (20:2) keeps it all covenant, not bare law: the words are given to an already-redeemed people. `[T]`

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check *(see Phase 0.6 above)*

**Immediately before (19:1–8):** the proposal and the vow — the covenant offered and freely accepted, awaiting ratification. (Note the resumptive clause at 19:9b, "When Moses told the words of the people to the LORD," which loops back to the vow of v. 8 and stitches this unit to its predecessor.) `[T]`

**Immediately after (20:22–24:18):** "The LORD said to Moses, 'Thus you shall say to the people…'" — the Book of the Covenant, delivered *through Moses* exactly as the people requested (20:19), then ratified in blood (24:8). The mediated mode of 20:22ff is the direct consequence of this unit's climax.

**The two seams to read across.** (i) The chapter break at 19/20 splits one theophany: 20:18 recapitulates 19:16 (thunder, lightning, trumpet, smoking mountain) by *resumptive repetition* (Wiederaufnahme), signalling that 20:1–17 is inset within an ongoing scene. (ii) The front seam at 19:8/9 is where the re-cut divides, because 19:9 opens the descent it announces. `[T]`

### 3. Structure

A five-part arc under a double frame:

| Panel | Verses | Content |
|-------|--------|---------|
| **Announcement (outer frame opens)** | 19:9 | God will come in the cloud "that the people may… believe you forever" — the stated purpose |
| **Preparation** | 19:10–15 | Consecrate, wash garments, three days, boundaries set, abstinence — a people made ready to survive the descent |
| **Theophany (inner frame opens)** | 19:16–25 | Thunder, fire, trumpet, quaking; the LORD descends; the boundary re-warned; Moses down |
| **The Ten Words (centre)** | 20:1–17 | Preamble of grace (v. 2) → duties God-ward (vv. 3–11) → duties neighbour-ward (vv. 12–17) |
| **Response (both frames close)** | 20:18–21 | The people fear, stand far off, ask for a mediator; Moses to the darkness — 19:9's purpose achieved |

**The double frame is the key.** The *outer* purpose-frame (19:9 → 20:19) states and fulfils the mediation-aim; the *inner* theophany-frame (19:16 → 20:18) brackets the Words with terror. Within the centre-panel, the two-table shape (God-ward, vv. 3–11; neighbour-ward, vv. 12–17) and the acceleration from expansive early commands to terse later ones. `[T]`

### 4. Linking Words

- **19:9 "that (_baʿăbûr_) the people may hear… and may also believe you forever"** — the purpose clause that governs the whole unit; the descent is *for* the authentication of the mediator. `[T]`
- **19:11–13 "For (_kî_) on the third day… "; the "lest"-warnings** — the boundary logic: set limits "lest" they break through and perish (also 19:21, 22, 24) — the barrier protects the people from a holiness that would consume them. `[T]`
- **20:2 — the prologue-as-ground.** "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out…" carries no "therefore," but grounds "You shall have no other gods" (v. 3): *because I redeemed you, therefore…* — the move of 19:4 → 19:5 repeated. `[T]`
- **The motive *kî* clauses in the Decalogue** — v. 5 (jealous God), v. 7 (not held guiltless), v. 11 (creation) — each command rooted in God's character or acts. `[T]`
- **20:20 nested purpose clauses** — "God has come to test you, *that* the fear of God may be before you, *that* you may not sin" — interpreting the whole theophany. `[T]`

### 5. Parallels

Prose, so sustained parallelism is largely **N/A**. The sharp antithesis of **20:5–6** (judgment "to the third and fourth generation… of those who hate me" vs *ḥesed* "to thousands of those who love me") sets mercy vastly above judgment. The two-table arrangement is a structural parallelism of the covenant's whole obligation (God-ward // neighbour-ward). `[T]`

### 6. Narrator's Comment

- **19:16 / 20:18** — the narrator sets and resets the theophany-scene (the same four phenomena), bracketing the Words and reporting the people's trembling and retreat. `[T]`
- **19:18** "…because the LORD had descended on it in fire" — the causal clause interprets the smoke and quaking as God's descent, not a natural event. `[T]`
- **20:1** "And God spoke all these words" — the Decalogue attributed *directly to God*, unmediated (contrast everything from 20:22). `[T]`
- **20:21** "…while Moses drew near to the thick darkness where God was" — the narratorial statement of the mediator-gap. `[T]`

### 7. Vocabulary

**Proper-noun inventory:** Sinai, Moses, Aaron (19:24), *Egypt / the house of slavery* (20:2). Triage: *"house of slavery" (bêt ʿăbādîm)* — the place of servitude (root *ʿābad*) from which they were freed, set against the true *service/worship* of the LORD the commands describe. `[T/I]`

**Key words:**

- **_qādaš_ (19:10, 14, 22, 23), "consecrate / be holy"** — graded holiness enacted in the preparation: the people, then even the priests, must be consecrated to survive proximity. `[T]`
- **_pāraṣ_ (19:22, 24), "break out"** — the LORD "breaking out" lethally against transgressed holiness (the same verb as the strike on Uzzah, 2 Sam 6:8). `[I]` *Moderate–high; cross-report to Difficult Verses.*
- **_yārad_ (19:11, 18, 20), "come down / descend"** — the book's spine-word; God's descent, thrice named. *High.* `[T]`
- **_ʿărāpel_ (20:21), "thick darkness"** — God's dwelling at the moment of revelation (Deut 4:11; 5:22; 1 Kgs 8:12; Ps 97:2): revelation and hiddenness together. *High.* `[T]`
- **_yārē' / yir'â_ (20:18, 20), "fear"** — the wordplay: "**do not fear** (*al-tîrā'û*)… that the **fear** (*yir'â*) of God may be before you" — dread that flees vs. reverence that restrains sin. *High.* `[T]`
- **_rāṣaḥ_ (20:13), "murder"** — unlawful killing, not the general "kill" (bears on translation, Tool 8). `[T]`

### 8. Translations

- **20:13 "You shall not murder."** ESV/NASB correct (*rāṣaḥ*); **KJV "kill"** is imprecise and has fuelled confusion; correct it if a KJV is in the room. `[T]` *High.*
- **19:5 / 20:3** — as in the companion unit: *sĕgullâ* ("treasured possession," KJV "peculiar"), and *ʿal-pānāy* ("before me / in my presence," 20:3). `[T]`
- **19:13 "shot":** ESV/NASB "shot" (*yārâ*); KJV "thrust through." Minor.

**Ancient versions / order check (triggered).** Two items where the ancient tradition matters: (i) **19:6** *mamleket kōhănîm* → LXX *basileion hierateuma* → 1 Pet 2:9 (the reception bridge; the phrase falls in the companion 19:1–8 unit but its LXX form governs the church's self-understanding of this whole Sinai material). (ii) **the order of 20:13–15**: MT murder/adultery/theft; some **LXX** witnesses (Vaticanus), the Nash Papyrus, and several **NT citations** (Luke 18:20; Rom 13:9; James 2:11) place adultery before murder — a versional/textual point (cross-report to Textual Variants); nothing doctrinal turns on it. *High that the orders diverge; the LXX manuscript detail is moderate — verify in NA28/NETS if load-bearing.* `[T/S]`

**Pulpit edition note:** Pulpit text matches report default (ESV Anglicised); no finding depends on a spelling/idiom difference.

### 9. Tone and Feel

A **preparation-terror-voice-terror** dynamic. The consecration (19:10–15) builds a solemn, graded readiness — wash, wait, keep back. Then the frame (19:16–19; 20:18) is overwhelming dread — thunder, fire, the trumpet "louder and louder," the mountain "trembled greatly." At the centre (20:1–17) is the austere, measured *voice of God* out of the fire — the calm at the storm's heart, more awesome than the storm. Then the register collapses into the people's fear: "they stood far off" and beg the voice to stop (20:19). The soundtrack: the trumpet crescendo, the clear divine voice, the frightened murmur backing away. The arc drives to 20:20's resolution — this terror is *for* you, to make you reverent. `[T]`

### 10. Repetition

- **The outer purpose-frame** — "believe you forever" (19:9) ↔ "you speak to us, and we will listen" (20:19).
- **The inner theophany-frame** — 19:16 ↔ 20:18 (thunder, lightning, trumpet, smoking mountain).
- **The boundary / "break out" refrain** — "set limits" (19:12, 23), "break through / break out" (*pāraṣ*, 19:22, 24), "lest… perish / lest he break out" (19:21, 22, 24).
- **"consecrate / holy" (_qādaš_)** — 19:10, 14, 22, 23.
- **"third day"** — 19:11, 15, 16; the countdown to descent.
- **"You shall not" (_lō'_ + imperfect)** — the Decalogue drumbeat.
- **"stood far off"** — 20:18, 21, bracketing Moses' "drew near" (20:21).

### 11. Quotation / Allusion

**External / conceptual (Genesis — live source).** **20:11** grounds the Sabbath in the creation week (← Gen 1:1–2:3; the divine rest of Gen 2:2–3). *Move 1:* creation ends in God's blessing and hallowing of the seventh day. *Move 2:* Genesis is Exodus's tracked live source. *Move 3:* Deut 5:15 grounds the same command in the *exodus* — the canon holds creation and redemption together. *What it adds:* Sabbath is participation in the Creator's own rhythm, given to the redeemed. `[T]`

**Move 4 — internal echo (required).**

- **Fulfils 19:9 (the unit's own opening).** "That the people may… believe you forever" (19:9) is discharged at 20:19 ("you speak to us, and we will listen"). `Internal: [19:9 purpose → 20:19 fulfilment]` `[T]` *High.*
- **Answers — reverses — 19:8 (previous unit).** The vow "All that the LORD has spoken we will do" (19:8) meets its undoing at 20:19: the people who swore obedience cannot bear the voice of the God they promised to obey. `Internal: [answers §19:8 — vow → recoil]` `[T]` *High.*
- **Planted for later.** The mediator-request (20:19) → the mediated Book of the Covenant (20:22–24:18); → Deut 5:23–31 (God approves it) and the prophet-like-Moses promise (Deut 18:15–19); → the "one mediator" and "mediator of a new covenant" (1 Tim 2:5; Heb 12:24). The jealous-God / *ḥesed* formula (20:5–6) → the character-formula of 34:6–7. `Internal: [planted → mediated law, prophet like Moses, 34:6–7]` `[T]` *High.*

**Reception (forward):** Heb 12:18–24 sets *this* mountain of fire, darkness, trumpet, and unbearable voice against Mount Zion; Deut 5 retells and interprets the scene; the Decalogue is cited across the NT (Matt 19:18–19; Rom 13:9; James 2:11). The "thick darkness" (20:21) feeds the motif of the God who dwells in unapproachable hiddenness (1 Kgs 8:12).

### 12. Genre

Covenant law delivered *within theophany narrative*. The Ten Words are **apodictic** ("you shall / shall not") — categorical covenant boundaries, distinct from the **casuistic** ("if a man…") case-law beginning at 21:1. Reading rules: apodictic law states the non-negotiable shape of covenant life; and the narrative frame (announcement → preparation → theophany → response) makes these words *covenant*, the terms on which a redeemed people belongs to God. `[T]`

### 13. Copycat

Narrative frame: **the people's request for a mediator (20:19) is presented positively** — Deut 5:28 has God commend it ("They are right in all that they have spoken"). It is right recognition of their condition, not cowardice to be scolded. The **consecration (19:10–15)** is instructive about reverent approach to God, not a ritual to reproduce. Moses "drawing near to the thick darkness" (20:21) is *descriptive* of his unique office, pointing to the greater Mediator — not "be bold like Moses." `[T/I]`

### 14. Bible Timeline

The old-covenant law-giving, ~50 days after the exodus. Where the reader stands: the law is holy yet cannot justify (Rom 7:12; Gal 3:11); we have come "to Mount Zion… and to Jesus, the mediator of a new covenant" (Heb 12:22–24), not to the mountain of terror; the mediator the people rightly longed for has come (1 Tim 2:5); and the law once thundered from outside is written on the heart by the Spirit (Jer 31:33; 2 Cor 3:3). Filter every command through the Mediator and the new-covenant fulfilment. `[T/I]`

### 15. Who Am I?

We are **the people who "stood far off"** — who cannot bear the unmediated voice of a holy God and rightly need a go-between. We are not Moses (the mediator is Christ). The properly Christian response is neither raw dread nor casual approach but the *reverent fear that keeps from sin* (20:20) — now possible because the Mediator has drawn near to the darkness *for* us and brought us to the better mountain. `[T/I]`

### 16. So What?

**Worldview:** the law is spoken by God himself (20:1), grounded in redemption (20:2) and creation (20:11); the fear of God is the beginning of *not sinning* (20:20) — reverence, not cringing; sinful people need, and now have, a mediator. **Behaviour:** obedience flowing from rescue; reverent, un-casual worship; honouring God and neighbour along the two tables. **Motivation:** "who brought you out of… the house of slavery" — gratitude, not fear of reprisal. **Four audiences:** the outsider meets a holy God not to be approached lightly; the complacent believer is re-awed; the fearful hear "do not fear… God has come to test you"; the whole covenant people is re-grounded in grace. **Prayer:** for the fear of God that keeps from sin, and thanks for the Mediator who draws near where we stand far off. `[T]`

### Textual Variants

The one variant worth flagging is the **order of the sixth–eighth commandments (20:13–15)**: MT murder/adultery/theft; some LXX witnesses (Vaticanus), the Nash Papyrus, Philo, and several NT citations (Luke 18:20; Rom 13:9; James 2:11) attest adultery before murder. Nothing doctrinal depends on it. The differing Sabbath *motive* (Exod 20:11 creation vs Deut 5:15 exodus) is a canonical doublet, not a textual variant (Tool 11). Otherwise the unit's Hebrew text is stable. *Moderate confidence on the versional detail.* `[T/S]`

### Historical and Cultural Background

The unit continues the **suzerain-treaty** frame (self-identifying overlord, 20:2; stipulations). Two features would strike the audience as extraordinary against their ANE world: **aniconic worship** — the image-ban (20:4–6) is unique among image-centred cultures, because Israel's God speaks and cannot be seen (Deut 4:12, 15); and the **Sabbath** — a weekly rest for the whole household *including slaves, sojourners, and livestock* (20:10), without ANE parallel. The **preparation** rites (three-day consecration, washed garments, abstinence, boundary-setting, 19:10–15) are recognisable purity-preparations for a divine encounter; **graded sacred space** (who may approach, how near) is the category the tabernacle will formalise. The **"thick darkness" as divine dwelling** (20:21) belongs to storm-theophany vocabulary, transformed into covenant self-disclosure. `[S/T]` *Moderate–high.*

### Original Audience Reception

Wilderness Israel — tabernacle standing among them — hears its own constitution spoken by God's voice and learns that their ancestors *could not bear it* and asked for a go-between. The mediated law and the sanctuary are the gracious answer to that terror. The confident vow (19:8) and the recoil (20:19) frame their self-understanding: a people who mean well and cannot, unmediated, stand before God.

**Candidate Fallen Condition Focus:** *we cannot bear the unmediated presence and voice of a holy God — we need one to stand between us and him.* Universally shared; answered in the Mediator who draws near where we stand far off. (Feeds `/point-purpose`.)

### Biblical-Theological Themes

1. **Mediation / the prophet like Moses.** The office is born here: the people ask (20:19), God approves (Deut 5:28) and promises a prophet like Moses (Deut 18:15–19), fulfilled in Christ (Acts 3:22–23; 1 Tim 2:5; Heb 12:24).
2. **Grace before law.** The Decalogue's preamble (20:2) — redemption before command.
3. **The fear of the LORD.** 20:20 defines it: reverence that restrains sin (Prov 1:7; 9:10).
4. **God in thick darkness.** Revelation-in-hiddenness (20:21; 1 Kgs 8:12).
5. **Holiness and graded access.** The consecration and boundaries (19:10–24) inaugurate what Leviticus systematises and the veil embodies — resolved at the torn curtain (Heb 10:19–20).
6. **Sabbath and creation.** The maker's rest given to the redeemed (20:11; Heb 4).

### Schnittjer Pass

**Skeleton:** Exodus · the *At-the-Mountain* movement · the covenant's foundational words set at the centre of a doubly-framed theophany (purpose announced, 19:9; terror, 19:16–20:18; purpose fulfilled, 20:19); serves the leading idea (the problem of God's presence) by showing the holy God's own voice unbearable and mediation required.

**Findings (strongest first):**

1. **Structure & shape / resumptive repetition (step 3)** — the double frame: outer purpose (19:9 → 20:19), inner theophany (19:16 → 20:18) around the Words. `[T]` *High.*
2. **Narrative space (step 2)** — "the people stood far off, while Moses drew near to the thick darkness" (20:21): the geometry of mediation; the up/down of the mountain (19:14, 20, 25). `[T]` *High.*
3. **Leading word / repetition (step 4)** — *qādaš* and the *pāraṣ*/boundary refrain in the preparation; the *fear* cluster and *lō'*-drumbeat at the climax. `[T]`
4. **First vs second reading (step 5)** — the vow "we will do" (19:8) read against the recoil (20:19) and the calf (32). `[T/I]`
5. **Intertextuality / polyacoustic (step 6)** — Deut 5 as canonical retelling; the mediator-request building toward the prophet like Moses and the one Mediator, heard polyacoustically. `[T/I]`

**Nil returns:** no chiasm beyond the enclosing frames; no janus.

### Christological Reading

**Type of connection:** **trajectory + contrast**, with the **mediator/prophet typology** at the centre.

**How the passage points to Christ.** (i) *The mediator rightly requested* (20:19), *approved* by God (Deut 5:28), generating the prophet-like-Moses promise (Deut 18:15–19), landed on Christ (Acts 3:22–23; 1 Tim 2:5; Heb 12:24). (ii) *Sinai vs Zion* (Heb 12:18–24) — *this* scene of fire, darkness, trumpet, and unbearable voice is the NT's own foil for the access believers have in Christ. (iii) *The law fulfilled inwardly* — written on the heart in the new covenant (Jer 31:33; 2 Cor 3), kept perfectly by the Mediator for his people. (iv) *The fear that keeps from sin* (20:20) is the reverence the gospel produces, not abolishes.

**Tests:** category ✓ (covenant mediation, prophetic office); NT-precedent ✓ *explicit* (Acts 3:22–23; Heb 12); escalation ✓ (the greater Mediator; Zion > Sinai); authorial-pattern ✓ (the text raises the mediator-need; Deut develops it). *High confidence.*

**Moralism check.** The trap: "keep the Ten and be accepted" — which the frame refutes: a redeemed people cannot even *hear* the law unmediated. The corrective: the law is given to the *already-rescued* (20:2), exposes our need for the Mediator, and it is he — not our keeping — who secures us. The hero is the God who speaks and the Mediator who draws near; the people and Moses point beyond themselves. `[T]`

### Difficult / Contested Verses

- **"Lest the LORD break out against them" (19:22, 24).** The lethal danger of holiness is real (cf. Nadab and Abihu, Lev 10; Uzzah, 2 Sam 6:6–8, the same verb *pāraṣ*); the pressure that makes mediation and the torn veil glorious.
- **The severity of 19:12–13 (the transgressor "shall not live").** The barrier is *mercy* — it keeps a people who cannot survive God's presence from destroying themselves.
- **"Do not go near a woman" (19:15).** Ritual purity for an extraordinary encounter (cf. 1 Sam 21:4–5), not a denigration of marriage; handle pastorally.
- **20:5 "visiting iniquity… to the third and fourth generation."** Corporate/generational solidarity, *qualified* by "of those who hate me" and outweighed by *ḥesed* "to thousands" (v. 6); read with Ezek 18. Preach the disproportion of mercy.
- **20:13 "murder" vs "kill."** Correct the KJV imprecision; *rāṣaḥ* is unlawful killing.
- **20:4–6 images.** The ban addresses *worship* images of God; handle Christian-liberty questions about art with care.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: preaching the Decalogue as a freestanding ethical code

- **What it looks like:** ten principles for a good life, lifted clean out of the fire and the fear.
- **Why it's wrong:** the frame (19:16–25; 20:18–21) makes these words the *voice of a holy God* his own redeemed people cannot bear; strip it and you lose both authority and terror.
- **The corrective:** preach the Words inside the theophany — law given by God, received by a people who beg for a go-between.

### Pitfall: law without grace (Sinai as merit)

- **What it looks like:** "Here are God's rules; keep them to stay in favour."
- **Why it's wrong:** the Decalogue opens "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out… of the house of slavery" (20:2) — redemption grounds every command.
- **The corrective:** the indicative of rescue under every imperative.

### Pitfall: scolding the people for asking Moses to mediate (20:19)

- **What it looks like:** "If only they'd had more faith, they'd have drawn near themselves."
- **Why it's wrong:** God *commends* the request (Deut 5:28) and builds the prophet-mediator promise on it (Deut 18:15–19).
- **The corrective:** preach the rightness of needing a mediator — and the Mediator who came.

### Pitfall: flattening the two fears of 20:20

- **What it looks like:** "Do not fear" as "God isn't really fearsome," or the terror taken as the goal.
- **Why it's wrong:** Moses names *two* fears — the dread that flees and the reverence "that you may not sin."
- **The corrective:** dread transposed into worship; the fear of the LORD as the beginning of holiness.

### Pitfall: opening at 19:16 and losing 19:9

- **What it looks like:** starting the sermon at the thunderclap, treating 19:9–15 as mere run-up.
- **Why it's wrong:** 19:9 states the whole event's purpose ("that the people may… believe you forever"), which 20:19 fulfils; drop it and the mediation-arc loses its stated aim.
- **The corrective:** let 19:9 set the agenda the scene delivers.
