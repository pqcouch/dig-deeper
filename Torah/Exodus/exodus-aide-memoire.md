# Exodus — Aide-Mémoire

*For holding the whole in mind while working the detail. From `book-overview-exodus` v1.4.0 and the fifteen digs. Counts are Hebrew.*

**In one sentence.** The God of the fathers keeps his sworn word: he comes down to redeem a people by blood and through the sea, binds them to himself at Sinai, and — the true summit — comes down to **dwell** among them, so that his glory fills a tent pitched among a stiff-necked people who deserve to be consumed and are graced instead. **Pulse: deliverance → covenant → dwelling; and grace before law, always.**

**Proportions are the argument.** Half the book stays at one mountain: the weight is not escape from Egypt but *how a holy God can dwell with a sinful people* — which 32–34 nearly answer in the negative.

**Its own thesis sentences.**
**29:46** *"…that I might dwell among them"* — a purpose clause hanging off the exodus, not a sequel. The book's thesis about its own plot.  
**6:7** *"I will take you to be my people"* — the covenant formula in full, thirty-four chapters early; it runs to Jer 31:33 and Rev 21:3.  
**19:4** *"I bore you on eagles' wings and brought you to myself"* — the destination is a Person, not a place.  

## Three movements

**1:1–15:21 · From Egypt** — the seed assaulted, the deliverer, the call, the plagues, Passover, the Sea and its Song.  
**15:22–18:27 · Through the wilderness** — water, manna, rock, Amalek, Jethro; the redeemed formed, and grumbling: *"Is the LORD among us or not?"* (17:7).  
**19:1–40:38 · At the mountain** — covenant proposed and consented to, the Ten Words, the Book sealed in blood, the tent instructed, the calf, the tent built and filled.  

**The Sinai block's real shape:** instructed (25–31) → **broken (32)** → interceded and renewed (33–34) → built (35–40), with a **Sabbath inclusio** (31:12–17; 35:1–3) framing the breach.

## The fifteen units

**Compressing 25–31?** Keep the **Sabbath speech** (31:12–17): seventh, climactic, the only one giving a reason outside itself. A **20:1–21** dig is still outstanding.

## Six threads

**Presence — two verb-chains, not one.** *Yārad*, "come down": 3:8 → 19:11, 18, 20 — **and it stops at Sinai; there is no *yārad* in ch. 40.** *Šākan*, "dwell": 24:16 → 25:8 → 29:45 → 40:35. *(At 40:34 the glory* fills *the tent; the* šākan *at 40:35 has the cloud as its subject.)* The glory settles on a mountain Israel must leave — **the tabernacle is Sinai made portable.**  
**Grace before law.** 19:4 before the "if" of 19:5; 20:2 before the first command; the case law's own motives — *"you were sojourners in Egypt"* (22:21; 23:9), *"I am gracious"* (22:27). **And law arrives before Sinai:** a statute at Marah (15:25), the manna test *"whether they will walk in my tôrâ"* (16:4), the Sabbath given, kept and broken (16:23–30), *"How long will you refuse…?"* (16:28). **Sinai *covenants* law; it does not introduce it — to people already unable to keep it.**  
**The mediator.** Moses shuttles before any theophany (19:3, 7, 8); the people beg for him (20:19) and **God commends the request** (Deut 5:28), which becomes the prophet-like-Moses promise (Deut 18:15–19). The office fragments from 18:18 on: no man can hold it (Heb 7:23–25).  
**The vow that cannot be kept.** Sworn three times (19:8; 24:3, 7), the last after the Book was read aloud; undone within days (20:19); shattered while the mediator is still on the mountain (32:1–6). Genuine consent, not folly — made good only by the covenant-keeper who bore the curse (Gal 3:13).  
**Knowing the LORD.** The refrain *"that you may know that I am the LORD"* runs from the plagues to the tent. The diagnosis is *qĕšēh-ʿōrep*, **stiff-necked** — an animal that will not turn to the rein — four times (32:9; 33:3, 5; 34:9), and **at the fourth Moses turns it from the ground of exclusion into the reason for the presence**.  
**Who goes with whom — the Sinai block's real crisis.** *"Make us gods who shall go before us"* (32:1) → *"my angel shall go before you"* (32:34) → *"I will not go up among you"* (33:3), a granted request that is a sentence — reversed only by petition (33:15; 33:14).

## Where Christ is

**From Egypt.** The Name claimed (John 8:58) and cited (Mark 12:26); the lamb, no bone broken (1 Cor 5:7; John 19:36); **the firstborn Son who was *not* spared** (Rom 8:32); his death an *exodos* (Luke 9:31); the Sea as baptism (1 Cor 10:1–2); *yĕšûʿâ* (15:2), the noun behind the name Jesus.  
**Wilderness.** Israel was tested by hunger and demanded bread; **Christ, hungry after forty days, refused — quoting the lesson Israel failed to learn** (Deut 8:3 → Matt 4:4). Made by citation, not construction. The manna as bread of life (John 6:31–58); the rock heard across five texts, not flattened to one.  
**Sinai.** The Mediator rightly begged for (1 Tim 2:5); Sinai set against Zion (Heb 12:18–24). **And the strongest link in the book: LXX Exod 24:8 *ἰδοὺ τὸ αἷμα τῆς διαθήκης* → Matt 26:28 *τὸ αἷμά **μου** τῆς διαθήκης*. The exact Greek, plus one pronoun — *my*. He puts himself where the sacrificial blood stood. Preach the pronoun.**  
**The dwelling.** The tent signals its own provisionality — a copy of a *tabnît* shown on the mountain (25:9, 40) — so Heb 8:5 imports nothing foreign. The Word "tabernacled among us" (John 1:14); Rev 21:3. The Song of the Sea already named this destination (15:13, 17).

## The standing traps

**Plagues as spectacle**, or mapped onto Egyptian gods — preach the shape and the self-revelation · **Moses as a leadership seminar**, worst at 2:11–15 and 18:13–27; the book exposes the mediator's limits so we look past him · **skipping the tabernacle**, when the length *is* the emphasis (its twin: preaching the furniture) · **law divorced from grace** — the imperative rides on the indicative, and the statutes say so · **"name your Red Sea"**, when Israel is silent and the LORD fights · **the calf as merely Israel's lapse** — preach the stiff neck and the God who stays; note that *ḥeṭeʾ gĕdōlâ* is marital language, so the calf is covenant adultery · **scorning the vow (19:8) or the recoil (20:19)** — both right; preach the two fears of 20:20 · **the Exodus 18 delegation seminar**, in a chapter where God does not speak, the narrator passes no verdict, and Jethro's plan is conditional, its ratification never reported · **blaming Israel at 6:9**, when the narrator gives the reason and it is not sin — *"their broken spirit and harsh slavery"* · **"be brave like the midwives"**, when the stated ground of the reward is **the fear of God**.

## Handle with care

**4:11** *"Who makes him mute, or deaf…?"* — an argument from greater to lesser about sovereignty over capacity, **not a verdict that disability is punishment**. Say John 9:1–3 out loud, watch the register, and **speak to affected families beforehand**.  
**15:3** *"The LORD is a man of war"* — the confrontation is **Pharaoh against the LORD**, not Egypt against Israel; Israel is to *"be still and see"*. The stated motive is that the Egyptians would know him — revelation, not dominance.  
**15:26** *"I am the LORD, your healer"* — **conditional, corporate, national**, and compared explicitly to *the plagues on Egypt*. Preach the name; decline the transaction; pastorally, not polemically. Someone has been told their illness persists because their faith failed.  

*Two questions remain open, both seams in 19:8–9: the division at v. 8 or v. 9 (asked of the library, unanswered — the re-cut rests on the text alone), and the resumptive 19:9b.*
