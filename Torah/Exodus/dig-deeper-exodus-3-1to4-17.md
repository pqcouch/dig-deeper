## The Passage

**3:1** Now Moses was keeping the flock of his father-in-law, Jethro, the priest of Midian, and he led his flock to the west side of the wilderness and came to Horeb, the mountain of God. **2** And the angel of the LORD appeared to him in a flame of fire out of the midst of a bush. He looked, and behold, the bush was burning, yet it was not consumed. **3** And Moses said, "I will turn aside to see this great sight, why the bush is not burned." **4** When the LORD saw that he turned aside to see, God called to him out of the bush, "Moses, Moses!" And he said, "Here I am." **5** Then he said, "Do not come near; take your sandals off your feet, for the place on which you are standing is holy ground." **6** And he said, "I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob." And Moses hid his face, for he was afraid to look at God.

**7** Then the LORD said, "I have surely seen the affliction of my people who are in Egypt and have heard their cry because of their taskmasters. I know their sufferings, **8** and I have come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians and to bring them up out of that land to a good and broad land, a land flowing with milk and honey, to the place of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites. **9** And now, behold, the cry of the people of Israel has come to me, and I have also seen the oppression with which the Egyptians oppress them. **10** Come, I will send you to Pharaoh that you may bring my people, the children of Israel, out of Egypt."

**11** But Moses said to God, "Who am I that I should go to Pharaoh and bring the children of Israel out of Egypt?" **12** He said, "But I will be with you, and this shall be the sign for you, that I have sent you: when you have brought the people out of Egypt, you shall serve God on this mountain."

**13** Then Moses said to God, "If I come to the people of Israel and say to them, 'The God of your fathers has sent me to you,' and they ask me, 'What is his name?' what shall I say to them?" **14** God said to Moses, "I AM WHO I AM." And he said, "Say this to the people of Israel: 'I AM has sent me to you.'" **15** God also said to Moses, "Say this to the people of Israel: 'The LORD, the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you.' This is my name forever, and thus I am to be remembered throughout all generations. **16** Go and gather the elders of Israel together and say to them, 'The LORD, the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, of Isaac, and of Jacob, has appeared to me, saying, "I have observed you and what has been done to you in Egypt, **17** and I promise that I will bring you up out of the affliction of Egypt to the land of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites, a land flowing with milk and honey."' **18** And they will listen to your voice, and you and the elders of Israel shall go to the king of Egypt and say to him, 'The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, has met with us; and now, please let us go a three days' journey into the wilderness, that we may sacrifice to the LORD our God.' **19** But I know that the king of Egypt will not let you go unless compelled by a mighty hand. **20** So I will stretch out my hand and strike Egypt with all the wonders that I will do in it; after that he will let you go. **21** And I will give this people favor in the sight of the Egyptians; and when you go, you shall not go empty, **22** but each woman shall ask of her neighbor, and any woman who lives in her house, for silver and gold jewelry, and for clothing. You shall put them on your sons and on your daughters. So you shall plunder the Egyptians."

**4:1** Then Moses answered, "But behold, they will not believe me or listen to my voice, for they will say, 'The LORD did not appear to you.'" **2** The LORD said to him, "What is that in your hand?" He said, "A staff." **3** And he said, "Throw it on the ground." So he threw it on the ground, and it became a serpent, and Moses ran from it. **4** But the LORD said to Moses, "Put out your hand and catch it by the tail"—so he put out his hand and caught it, and it became a staff in his hand— **5** "that they may believe that the LORD, the God of their fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has appeared to you." **6** Again, the LORD said to him, "Put your hand inside your cloak." And he put his hand inside his cloak, and when he took it out, behold, his hand was leprous like snow. **7** Then God said, "Put your hand back inside your cloak." So he put his hand back inside his cloak, and when he took it out, behold, it was restored like the rest of his flesh. **8** "If they will not believe you," God said, "or listen to the first sign, they may believe the latter sign. **9** If they will not believe even these two signs or listen to your voice, you shall take some water from the Nile and pour it on the dry ground, and the water that you shall take from the Nile will become blood on the dry ground."

**10** But Moses said to the LORD, "Oh, my Lord, I am not eloquent, either in the past or since you have spoken to your servant, but I am slow of speech and of tongue." **11** Then the LORD said to him, "Who has made man's mouth? Who makes him mute, or deaf, or seeing, or blind? Is it not I, the LORD? **12** Now therefore go, and I will be with your mouth and teach you what you shall speak." **13** But he said, "Oh, my Lord, please send someone else." **14** Then the anger of the LORD was kindled against Moses and he said, "Is there not Aaron, your brother, the Levite? I know that he can speak well. Behold, he is coming out to meet you, and when he sees you, he will be glad in his heart. **15** You shall speak to him and put the words in his mouth, and I will be with your mouth and with his mouth and will teach you both what to do. **16** He shall speak for you to the people, and he shall be your mouth, and you shall be as God to him. **17** And take in your hand this staff, with which you shall do the signs."

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims that emerged across multiple converging tools:

1. **The divine name is defined by the clause that surrounds it: "I AM WHO I AM" (3:14) is glossed by "I will be with you" (3:12) — the same Hebrew verb — so the Name asserts self-existence *as* covenant presence, not bare metaphysics.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Original Languages, Linking Words, Repetition, Positional Necessity. Both "I will be with you" (3:12) and "I AM"/"the LORD" (3:14–15) are forms of *hāyâ*, "to be"; God answers *who am I?* (Moses' first objection) with *I will be with you*, and *what is your name?* (the second) with *I AM*. The book's whole later question — how can this God dwell with his people? — is already compressed into the Name. *High confidence.*

2. **"I have come down to deliver" (3:8, *yārad*) is the programmatic spine of the entire book, planted here.** `[T]` for the verb; `[I]` for the book-spanning arc. Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Schnittjer Pass (narrative space), Biblical Theology, Internal echo. The God who *comes down* to rescue will *come down* on Sinai (19:11, 18, 20) and finally *dwell* in the tent his glory fills (40:34). The bush is not a mystical episode but the first move of a descent that ends in the tabernacle. *High confidence.*

3. **The sign given is not proof-in-advance but promise-of-return: "you shall serve God on this mountain" (3:12) makes Sinai the guaranteed destination from the outset.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Linking Words, Positional Necessity, Internal echo. The call is anchored *backward* in the covenant with the fathers (3:6, 15–16) and *forward* in worship at this very mountain (resolved at 19:1–2). Faith is asked to trust a sign it will only see fulfilled after obedience. *High confidence.*

4. **The commission is the covenant's answer to the groan of 2:23–25: four divine verbs of hearing become four divine verbs of acting.** `[T]` Surfaced by: Positional Necessity, Repetition, Linking Words. In 2:24–25 God *heard… remembered… saw… knew*; in 3:7–8 he has *seen… heard… known… come down*. The theophany is covenant-driven relief for a specific cry, not a general appearance — "I have surely seen" (infinitive absolute, emphatic) answers "God saw." *High confidence.*

5. **Moses' five objections are met not by upgrading Moses but by the sufficiency of God — presence, name, power, the Maker of mouths, and finally Aaron; and the narrator marks the fifth as sin (4:14), distinguishing honest fear from refusal.** `[T]` for the pattern and the anger-notice; `[I]` for reading Aaron as concession rather than ideal. Surfaced by: Structure, Narrator's Comment, So What?. Each answer is a facet of God, not a résumé upgrade; the giving of Aaron follows kindled divine anger — accommodation to unbelief, and Aaron will later lead the calf apostasy (32). *High/moderate confidence.*

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book-level.** Exodus reveals the LORD as the covenant-keeping Redeemer who defeats the powers, binds Israel to himself at Sinai, and comes down to dwell — moving the reader from a God who *hears and remembers* (2:24) to a God whose *glory fills the tent* (40:34). The refrain "that you may know that I am the LORD" (6:7; 7:5; 14:4, 18; 29:46) frames the aim.

**Passage-level.** This unit *launches the deliverance and names its God*. It does two things the book cannot proceed without: (a) it reveals the covenant Name and binds it to presence, and (b) it commissions the human mediator through whom the rescue will run. Everything after — plagues, Passover, Sea, Sinai, tabernacle — is the outworking of the self-revelation given at the bush. The passage's own concern is not Moses' psychology but *God's identity and sufficiency*: the objections exist to draw out five facets of who God is. `[T]`

**Book-overview note:** Confirms the overview's purpose statement and the "come down / dwell" keynote; the passage plants the *yārad* spine the overview names.

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement.** Chapter 1 narrates the assault on the seed (Pharaoh's genocide, the Nile as intended grave, 1:22); chapter 2 gives the deliverer drawn from the water, his flight to Midian, and — decisively — 2:23–25: the king dies, the people groan, and "God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob. God saw the people of Israel—and God knew." The covenant clock set at Genesis 15:13–16 (400 years) has effectively run out.

**Necessity answer.** The call must come *now* because the God who has just been said to *hear, remember, see, and know* must act — and he acts by commissioning a mediator. 3:1–4:17 exists here as the divine response to the groan of 2:24–25: the four verbs of God's attention (2:24–25) are answered by the four verbs of God's intervention ("I have surely seen… heard… I know… I have come down," 3:7–8). Position is the argument: the bush is not a mystical interlude in Moses' life but the covenant turning to action. `[T]`

**Implication.** Read the theophany as *covenant relief*, not private spirituality. The heat of the passage is God's, not Moses'; "I have come down to deliver" (3:8) is the enactment of "God remembered his covenant" (2:24). This also governs why the Name is revealed *here*: a people about to be rescued need to know *who* is rescuing them.

**Revisit after Tool 11 (book-usage).** Genesis is the tracked live source; its re-citation here (3:6, 15) is not decorative but the legal ground of the whole action — the rescue is *sworn word being kept*, which is precisely why the Name is anchored to "the God of your fathers."

**Immediate surroundings.** *Before:* 2:23–25 (the groan and the remembered covenant). *After:* 4:18–31 — Moses returns to Jethro, the strange "bridegroom of blood" episode (4:24–26), and the elders' initial belief and worship (4:31, which resolves the anxiety of 4:1). The unit is bounded at the front by the shift of scene to Horeb (3:1) and at the back by the close of the commissioning dialogue (4:17), with 4:18 turning to departure. The chapter break at 3/4 is artificial — the five objections run continuously from 3:11 to 4:17.

### 3. Structure

| Verses | Section | Function |
|--------|---------|----------|
| 3:1–6 | **The theophany** | Scene at Horeb; the bush; holy ground; self-identification as the God of the fathers; Moses hides his face |
| 3:7–10 | **The commission** | God declares he has seen/heard/known/come down; sends Moses to Pharaoh |
| 3:11–12 | **Objection 1 — "Who am I?"** | Answered by presence + the sign of return to worship |
| 3:13–22 | **Objection 2 — "What is his name?"** | Answered by the Name (I AM / the LORD) + a detailed forecast of the mission |
| 4:1–9 | **Objection 3 — "They will not believe"** | Answered by three authenticating signs (staff, leprous hand, Nile-to-blood) |
| 4:10–12 | **Objection 4 — "I am not eloquent"** | Answered by the Maker of mouths + "I will be with your mouth" |
| 4:13–17 | **Objection 5 — "Send someone else"** | Answered by kindled anger + the giving of Aaron |

**Device.** A **theophany-and-commission** followed by a **five-fold objection–answer sequence**. The centre of gravity is the escalation *of Moses' resistance* against the constancy *of God's self-disclosure*: each objection is met by another facet of God (presence → name → power → the Maker of speech → a helper). The first two objections are answered by *who God is* (presence, name); the middle by *what God can do* (signs); the last two circle back to Moses' inadequacy and are answered first by God's creatorship and finally, when the resistance turns to refusal, by accommodation. The rising resistance and the shift from "who am I / what is your name" (honest) to "send someone else" (refusal, 4:13) is the structural hinge the narrator flags at 4:14. `[T]`

### 4. Linking Words

- **3:9–10 "And now… Come" (`wĕʿattâ`)** →therefore→ the cry has come up, *therefore* God sends Moses. The commission is grounded in the covenant response of 3:7–8; the logic is: I have seen and come down → *so* I send you. `[T]`
- **3:5 "for (`kî`) the place… is holy ground"** ←for← the command to remove sandals is grounded in the character of the place. The reason precedes the reverence. `[T]`
- **3:12 "But (`kî`) I will be with you"** — the emphatic *kî* both answers "Who am I?" (the answer is not about Moses at all) and grounds the sign that follows ("*that* I have sent you… you shall serve God on this mountain"). The connector carries the whole reversal: the question about Moses' identity is answered by God's presence. `[T]`
- **4:12 "Now therefore (`wĕʿattâ`) go, and I will be with your mouth"** →therefore→ because God made the mouth (4:11), *therefore* Moses is to go; the creatorship of 4:11 is the ground of the command in 4:12. `[T]`
- **Contrastive "But" at each objection** (3:11; 3:13; 4:1; 4:10; 4:13) marks the pivots — the narrative advances by Moses' successive *buts* answered by God's declarations. The final "But he said, 'please send someone else'" (4:13) is the *but* that breaks the pattern into refusal. `[T]`

### 5. Parallels

Narrative, not poetry, so formal parallelism is limited — **but two prose parallelisms carry weight:**

- **The verb-series parallel between 2:24–25 and 3:7–8** (see Positional Necessity). *heard / remembered / saw / knew* → *seen / heard / know / come down*. The near-matching of the two tetrads is the strongest structural signal that the bush answers the groan. `[T]`
- **The three-fold patriarchal formula** "the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob" (3:6, 15; cf. 3:16; 4:5) — a fixed liturgical tricolon whose repetition (rather than parallelism proper) anchors the new revelation to the old covenant.

**Otherwise N/A** — this is theophany narrative and divine speech, without sustained poetic parallelism.

### 6. Narrator's Comment

The narrator is sparing but decisive:

- **3:1 "Horeb, the mountain of God"** — a *proleptic* narrator label: it is not yet the mountain of God in the story's own time (the covenant-giving is chapters away), so the narrator writes from the later vantage, signalling to the reader where this is heading. `[T]`
- **3:2 "the angel of the LORD appeared to him… out of the midst of a bush"** — the narrator identifies the appearer as "the angel of the LORD," and then 3:4 has "the LORD saw… God called" — the narrator lets *angel of the LORD*, *the LORD*, and *God* stand in apposition, refusing to separate them (cross-report: Christological Reading). `[T]`
- **4:14 "Then the anger of the LORD was kindled against Moses"** — the single most important narrator's comment in the unit. The narrator, whose verdict is authoritative, marks the fifth objection as *sin*, distinguishing it from the honest fear of the earlier four (which drew no anger). Without this aside a reader might treat "send someone else" as more humility; the narrator forbids that reading. `[T]`

### 7. Vocabulary

**Proper-noun inventory** (run before triage): *Moses; Jethro; Midian; Horeb; the mountain of God; the angel of the LORD; Abraham, Isaac, Jacob; Egypt; the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites, Jebusites; Pharaoh; the LORD (YHWH); the Hebrews; the Nile; Aaron the Levite.* Triage: most are scene-setting or standard, but three carry canonical freight — *Horeb/the mountain of God* (planted for ch. 19), *the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob* (Genesis re-cited, cross-report Tool 11), and *Aaron the Levite* (the tribe-designation quietly anticipates the priesthood and, ominously, the calf).

The payload terms:

- **ʾehyeh / YHWH (3:12, 14–15).** "I will be with you" (*ʾehyeh ʿimmāk*, 3:12), "I AM WHO I AM" (*ʾehyeh ʾăšer ʾehyeh*, 3:14), and "the LORD" (*YHWH*, 3:15) are all built on the verb *hāyâ*, "to be." The Name is not defined in the abstract; 3:12 supplies the gloss — the God who *is* is the God who *will be with* his people. *High confidence for the root connection; the aspect of the imperfect ("I AM" vs "I WILL BE") is moderate — see Original Languages.*
- **qōdeš, "holy ground" (3:5).** The book's *first* occurrence of holiness — and it attaches to *ground*, before any tabernacle exists. The command to remove sandals and the "do not come near" institute graded access, the logic the tabernacle will later formalise. *High confidence.*
- **yārad, "I have come down" (3:8).** The programmatic verb (Headline 2): God descends to deliver. *High confidence.*
- **The four verbs of 3:7–8** — *rāʾâ* (see), *šāmaʿ* (hear), *yādaʿ* (know), and the descent verb — deliberately echo 2:24–25. *High confidence.*
- **"A land flowing with milk and honey" (3:8, 17).** The first canonical use of this refrain-phrase for the promised land; connotes settled agricultural abundance over against wilderness scarcity — a promise the wilderness audience feels acutely. *High confidence.*
- **naḥash, "serpent" (4:3).** The staff becomes a *nāḥāš* — the Eden word; the sign that Moses flees from is a controlled serpent, a small foretaste of God's mastery over the very power Egypt's iconography claimed (the cobra on Pharaoh's crown). *Moderate confidence on the Eden/Egyptian resonance; the word itself is certain.*

**Names.** *Moses* — the narrator has already given the folk-etymology "I drew him out of the water" (2:10, from *māšâ*); *Horeb* ≈ "waste/desolation," fitting for the back of the wilderness. `[T]` for the words; `[I]` for freight.

### 8. Translations

- **3:14 "I AM WHO I AM."** ESV, NASB, and KJV agree on "I AM THAT/WHO I AM." The divergence is not among the moderns but between them and the interpretive options in the Hebrew imperfect (see Original Languages) — some render "I WILL BE WHAT I WILL BE" (a defensible reading kept in footnotes). The rendering carries exegetical weight, so the ancient-versions check applies.

  **Translation-tradition split (LXX):** the Septuagint renders 3:14 *egō eimi ho ōn* — "I am the One who is / the Being." This shifts the sense toward *ontological self-existence* and is the reading that stands behind John's absolute "I AM" (*egō eimi*, John 8:58) and Revelation's "who is and who was and who is to come." The Hebrew holds *both* self-existence and covenant faithfulness together; the LXX foregrounds the being-language. Preaching implication: the "philosophy of being" reading is not a modern imposition — it is ancient and canonically fruitful — but it must be *held together* with the covenant-presence gloss of 3:12, not allowed to replace it. *High confidence on the LXX wording (a very frequently cited verse); cross-report to Tool 11 and Christological Reading.*
- **4:10 "slow of speech and of tongue."** ESV/NASB "slow of speech and slow of tongue"; KJV "slow of speech, and of a slow tongue"; the Hebrew is *kĕbad-peh ûkĕbad lāšôn*, literally "heavy of mouth and heavy of tongue" — the *kābēd* ("heavy") root that runs through the plagues (Pharaoh's "heavy" heart) and resonates with *kābôd* ("glory"). The smooth English "slow" obscures the wordplay. Flag for the preacher. *Moderate confidence on the resonance.*
- **4:13 "please send someone else."** ESV/NASB render the sense plainly; KJV is more literal and opaque: "send, I pray thee, by the hand of him whom thou wilt send" (*šĕlaḥ-nāʾ bĕyad tišlāḥ*). The Hebrew is a deliberately vague brush-off — "send by whatever hand you will send" = "send anyone but me." The plainer modern rendering captures the refusal the narrator then calls sin (4:14). *High confidence.*

**Pulpit edition note:** Pulpit text matches report default (ESV Anglicised). No divergence load-bearing here.

### 9. Tone and Feel

Two registers in tension, with a decisive shift. The opening is **awe and holy dread**: the wonder of the unconsumed bush, the double vocative "Moses, Moses," the command to unshod and not approach, and Moses hiding his face "afraid to look at God" (3:6). The soundtrack is hushed, trembling — the reverent silence of holy ground. Against this, from 3:11 onward, runs the register of **human reluctance** — the five objections, mounting from honest self-doubt to evasion. The tonal climax is the **heat of divine anger** at 4:14 ("the anger of the LORD was kindled") — the only place the reverent hush breaks into displeasure. The passage moves from *fear-of-God* (right) to *fear-that-refuses* (wrong), and God's anger marks the boundary between them. A preacher must honour both: the wonder must not be rushed, and the anger must not be softened. `[T]`

### 10. Repetition

- **"The God of Abraham, (the God of) Isaac, and (the God of) Jacob"** — 3:6, 15, 16; 4:5. Four times; the drumbeat anchoring the new revelation to the patriarchal covenant. `[T]`
- **The ʾehyeh / "I will be" motif** — "I will be with you" (3:12), "I AM" (3:14 ×2), "I will be with your mouth" (4:12), "with your mouth and with his mouth" (4:15). The Name and the presence-promise are the same verb recurring. `[T]`
- **"Send" (šālaḥ)** — the leitmotif of commissioning: "I will send you" (3:10), "that I have sent you" (3:12), "has sent me" (3:13, 14, 15), climaxing in Moses' "send someone else" (4:13) — the same verb Moses tries to turn back on God. `[T]`
- **"Believe" (ʾāman)** — 4:1, 5, 8 (×2), 9 — the keyword of the third objection and its signs; the elders' belief in 4:31 will resolve it. `[T]`
- **"Listen to my/your voice" (šāmaʿ bĕqôl)** — 3:18; 4:1, 8, 9 — the twin of "believe." `[T]`
- **"Hand" (yād)** — a striking motif: God delivers "out of the *hand* of the Egyptians" (3:8); Pharaoh will not yield except by a "mighty *hand*" (3:19); God's outstretched *hand* strikes Egypt (3:20); "what is in your *hand*?" / the staff seized and restored in Moses' *hand* (4:2, 4); the leprous *hand* healed (4:6–7); "take this staff in your *hand*" (4:17). Power contested and conferred, all figured through the hand. `[T]`

### 11. Quotation/Allusion

**Independent candidate-finding first.** The passage's distinctive vocabulary — the patriarchal formula, "come down," "holy ground," the serpent, "milk and honey" — was searched against the canon before testing the overview's named candidate (Genesis). The clear external citation is Genesis; the richest yield is internal (Move 4).

**Genesis / the Abrahamic covenant → 3:6, 15–17 (and 4:5)** *(high confidence — direct, repeated citation)*

- *Source context (Move 1):* "I am the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob" reaches back to the covenant sworn and re-sworn in Genesis 12; 15; 17; 26; 28 — land, seed, and blessing to the nations. The specific promise activated is the *land* oath: "a good and broad land… the place of the Canaanites…" (3:8, 17) echoes the boundary-and-peoples lists of Genesis 15:18–21. The God who speaks from the bush is the God who bound himself by oath to the fathers.
- *Book usage (Move 2):* Genesis is the confirmed live source (overview map: 1:1–5; 2:24; and forward at 6:3–8; 32:13). Here it is invoked to *authorise the mediator and ground the rescue in sworn word*. The citation is not decorative: it is the legal basis of the action — cf. the Positional Necessity Check, where 2:24's "God remembered his covenant" is the pivot the bush enacts. This is Genesis re-cited at the *deliverance-inauguration* stage, following its own internal arc (promise → the 400-year clock of Gen 15:13–16 now expired). *Phrase-level check:* the six-nation list (3:8, 17) is a near-verbatim self-echo of Genesis 15's peoples — Move 2 at phrase level, not just theme.
- *OT-to-OT (Move 3):* N/A within this passage — Genesis is the only external source cited.
- *What it adds:* the rescue is covenant-keeping, not improvisation; and the Name revealed (3:15) is explicitly *the God of the fathers'* name — continuity, not a new deity.

**Internal echoes (Move 4 — required):**

- *Answers §2:23–25:* the four verbs of divine attention are answered by four verbs of divine action (see Headline 4). `Internal: [answers §2:24–25]` `[T]` *high*.
- *Planted → Sinai:* "you shall serve God on this mountain" (3:12) is resolved at 19:1–2 when Israel arrives at the same mountain to worship — the sign is a promise the reader watches come true. `Internal: [planted — Sinai worship, resolved 19:1]` `[T]` *high*.
- *Planted → the Name proclaimed:* the name given here is the name expounded in the great self-revelation of 34:6–7. `Internal: [planted — name, developed 34:6]` `[T]` *high*.
- *Planted → plagues and plunder:* "I will stretch out my hand and strike Egypt with all the wonders" (3:20) is enacted in chs. 7–12; "you shall not go empty… you shall plunder the Egyptians" (3:21–22) is fulfilled at 12:35–36. `Internal: [planted — wonders → chs. 7–12; plunder → 12:35–36]` `[T]` *high*.
- *Planted → the staff-signs:* the staff-to-serpent and the wonders (4:2–5, 17) recur when Aaron's staff swallows the magicians' serpents (7:8–13) and through the plague-cycle. `Internal: [planted — staff, ch. 7]` `[T]` *high*.
- *Contrast, reversed later:* the *nāḥāš* (serpent) Moses flees (4:3) contrasts with Pharaoh's serpent-iconography soon overpowered (7:12). `Internal: [planted — serpent motif]` `[I]` *moderate*.

**The Name as the master-allusion of the canon.** *ʾehyeh*/YHWH becomes the reference-point echoed at Hosea 1:9 ("Lo-Ammi… I am not *ʾehyeh* to you," reversing the bush), Isaiah 41–48's repeated "I am he," Jesus' seven "I AM" sayings and absolute "before Abraham was, I AM" (John 8:58), and Mark 12:26 where Jesus cites 3:6 ("I am the God of Abraham…") to prove the resurrection. *High confidence — cross-report to Christological Reading.*

**The prophetic call-type-scene.** The commission-with-objection follows a recurring canonical pattern (Gideon, Judg 6; Isaiah, Isa 6; Jeremiah, Jer 1) — an *extended echo effect* that points beyond any one prophet. Moses' is the *founding* instance the later calls echo. *Moderate confidence (pattern, not citation); cross-report Schnittjer Pass.*

### 12. Genre

Historical narrative with **theophany and extended divine speech**. Reading rules: the burning-not-consumed bush is presented as a real event, not a symbol — resist the allegorising move ("the bush is the church, ever-burning yet never consumed"), which the text does not license. The signs of 4:1–9 are likewise presented as genuine wonders. The embedded divine speeches (3:7–10, 14–22; 4:11–12, 14–17) function as authoritative revelation within the narrative frame. Brief — genre is not contested here. `[T]`

### 13. Copycat

Narrative, so label the actors:

- **Moses' commissioning** — *unique event.* A one-off salvation-historical office (Israel's founding mediator); not a template for "burning-bush experiences" or a leadership paradigm to imitate.
- **The five objections** — the first four are *descriptive* (honest fear, patiently answered); the fifth ("send someone else," 4:13) is a *negative example*, explicitly marked as sin by the narrator's anger-notice (4:14).
- **The signs** — *unique*, authenticating a unique commission; not a warrant for expecting sign-gifts on demand.
- **God's self-revelation and covenant faithfulness** — the constant that is *not* an example to copy but the God to trust.

The characteristic error is to convert this into "overcome your excuses and step up like Moses" — which the narrative resists at every turn (see Who Am I? and Christological Reading). `[T]/[I]`

### 14. Bible Timeline

**Where the passage sits:** the hinge between the patriarchal promises (Genesis) and the exodus — the covenant clock of Genesis 15:13–16 has run out; the founding rescue is about to begin. **Where the reader sits:** this side of Christ's *exodos* (Luke 9:31) and Pentecost. **Reading through the middle:** the "Russian-doll" pattern applies directly — Israel's slavery pictures our slavery to sin, the coming Passover pictures the cross, the wilderness our pilgrimage, the land our rest (1 Cor 10:1–13; Heb 3–4). Crucially, the divine Name revealed here *governs all later revelation*: read through the cross, the "I AM" is claimed by Jesus (John 8:58), so the God who commissions Moses is the God who takes flesh. Do not stop the passage at the Iron-Age horizon. `[T]/[I]`

### 15. Who Am I?

The classic **"Moses-is-me" trap** lives here (the tool's own worked example is Exodus 3). Moses is being commissioned as Israel's *mediator* — a role the NT assigns to Christ (1 Tim 2:5; Heb 3:1–6), not to the hearer. The right identification for the congregation is *Israel-to-be-delivered*: the enslaved people whose cry God has heard and whom he comes down to rescue. God is the hero of the passage; Moses is the reluctant instrument who points beyond himself. Where we *do* resemble Moses, it is in the inadequacy the passage exposes (Who am I? / I cannot speak) — answered not by self-improvement but by "I will be with you." The one legitimate imitation is *worship*: the fear-of-God posture of 3:5–6 (removing the sandals, hiding the face) is the reader's proper response to the same holy God. `[T]`

### 16. So What?

**Stage 1 — the author's intended response.** To *know and trust the LORD* — the self-existent, covenant-keeping God who has come down to save — and, consequent on that, to obey his call on the strength of his presence rather than one's own sufficiency. The passage forms a people who read their rescue as God's sworn faithfulness and who reverence his holiness.

**Stage 2.**
- *Worldview:* God's self-sufficient, covenant presence ("I AM… I will be with you") is the answer to every inadequacy; the ground of obedience is *who God is*, not who I am.
- *Behaviour:* go where sent on the strength of "I will be with you," not self-assessment (stop excusing; start obeying) — while learning the reverence that removes its sandals before a holy God.
- *Motivation:* the Name and the presence, not the résumé; and the covenant faithfulness that keeps sworn word across centuries.

**Four audiences.** *For me:* where am I answering God's call with "send someone else," dressing refusal as humility? *For a Christian friend:* the one paralysed by inadequacy needs "I will be with you," not "you can do it." *For the church:* we are Israel-to-be-delivered, a people whose confidence rests on God's presence among us (now by the Spirit). *For the unbeliever:* the God who says "I AM" has come down in Christ to deliver from a deeper slavery.

**Prayer.** Adoration of the holy, self-existent, covenant God (3:5–6, 14–15); confession of the reluctance that hides behind false humility (4:13–14); petition for grace to go on the strength of his presence (3:12; 4:12); thanksgiving that the God who came down to deliver has come down finally in Christ. `[T]/[I]`

**Grammatical/syntactical features.**
- **Infinitive absolute in 3:7:** *rāʾōh rāʾîtî*, "seeing I have seen" = "I have *surely* seen." The doubled verb is emphatic — God's attention to the affliction is intense, not casual. *(high)*
- **The imperfect of 3:14** is the crux: Hebrew *ʾehyeh* is not tensed like English. It can carry present ("I AM") or future/durative ("I WILL BE") force; the context (the future-oriented "I will be with you," 3:12) leans the sense toward *active, faithful presence about to be demonstrated*, while the LXX's *ho ōn* leans toward timeless being. Both are defensible; the text likely intends the fullness. Route the pulpit decision to a lexicon/commentary if a sermon leans hard on one. *(moderate — flag)*
- **The *kî* of 3:12** is emphatic/asseverative ("Indeed I will be with you"), not merely causal.

**Wordplay / sound-features.**
- *ʾehyeh* (3:12, 14) ↔ *YHWH* (3:15): the Name and the presence-promise are audibly the same root — the wordplay *is* the theology. *(high)*
- *kābēd* "heavy" (4:10) anticipates the plague-cycle's *kābēd* (Pharaoh's heavy heart) and resonates with *kābôd* "glory" — the man "heavy of mouth" serves the God of glory. *(moderate — hard-rule check: canonical precedent for the kābēd/kābôd play is established later in the book, so moderate, not high)*
- *nāḥāš* "serpent" (4:3) — the Eden word; note without over-reading (no narrative corroboration of an Eden-typology here beyond the shared word). *(moderate)*

**Recommended verification.** The aspect of *ʾehyeh* (BDB/HALOT, and a commentary that works the Hebrew — e.g. on whether to render "I WILL BE"); the *kābēd* resonance across the book.

### Historical and Cultural Background

**Setting.** Time: the eve of the exodus (traditionally 15th or 13th c. BC — date debated, hang nothing on it). Location: the wilderness of Midian and Horeb/Sinai, the far side of the grazing lands; a shepherd's world. Political: Israel enslaved under a new Egyptian regime; the previous Pharaoh who sought Moses' life has died (2:23).

**What the original audience knew.**
- **Shepherding in Midian** placed Moses — the man raised in Pharaoh's court — at the social and geographic margin; the deliverer is fetched from the back of the wilderness, not the palace.
- **Removing sandals** before a sacred space is an attested Near-Eastern reverence practice (bare feet at holy places); the audience would recognise the gesture instantly as the marking of sacred ground.
- **"A land flowing with milk and honey"** denotes settled pastoral-agricultural abundance — a vivid contrast to wilderness subsistence, and a phrase the wandering audience would hear with longing.
- **The "three days' journey… to sacrifice"** (3:18) fits a recognisable request for a festival pilgrimage; it is not deception but the opening move in a negotiation Pharaoh will reject.
- **"Plunder the Egyptians"** (3:22) evokes the custom of departing slaves/defeated peoples receiving goods; the audience hears reparation for generations of unpaid labour, and the equipping of Israel for the tabernacle to come (the gold will build the tent — and, disastrously, the calf).
- **The staff** is the ordinary shepherd's implement; that God commissions through *what is already in Moses' hand* (4:2) would land with a people who knew such staffs.

**What this changes.** The deliverer's marginality and the ordinariness of the staff underline that the power is God's, not Moses'; and the graded-holiness of 3:5 is the same logic the audience's own tabernacle embodies — the bush-scene explains their institutions. `[T]/[S]`

### Original Audience Reception

**Canonical audience.** *Section:* Torah. For the Torah the compositional and canonical audiences **coincide** — wilderness Israel with the tabernacle standing in the camp. *What the reader knows:* Genesis entire (creation, fall, the patriarchal covenant, the descent into Egypt) — so "the God of your fathers" and the land-promise are already loaded; and the reader holds the *cult* simultaneously, so "holy ground" and graded access are the native reading frame, not later theology.

**The first hearers.** A redeemed-but-grumbling wilderness generation, camped *at this very mountain* (or having just left it), for whom 3:12's sign — "you shall serve God on this mountain" — is not a distant prophecy but *their own experience*: they are standing on the proof. The sermon writes itself for that audience: the God who promised return-to-worship kept his word, and you are the evidence.

**Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances.**
- *Shocking:* the God of the fathers speaks *now*, after centuries of apparent silence; and he knows their sufferings by name (3:7).
- *Surprising:* the deliverer is a fugitive shepherd, not a warrior or prince; and God accommodates his refusal rather than crushing it.
- *Comforting:* "I have surely seen… I have come down" — the covenant God is neither absent nor forgetful; the Name he gives is a name to be *remembered throughout all generations* (3:15).
- *Disturbing:* the kindling of God's anger at Moses (4:14) — reverence has limits it must not cross into refusal.

**What we bring that they didn't.** Individualism (we hear "God's call" as a private career question rather than the corporate rescue of a people); a therapeutic reading of "burning-bush moments"; and a moralising instinct to make Moses the model. The first hearers heard, first of all, *who their God is*.

### Biblical-Theological Themes

**1. Divine presence / "coming down" → tabernacle → incarnation.** *(textual signal: yārad 3:8; qōdeš 3:5; purpose-connection: strong; trajectory payoff: high)* The passage plants the presence-spine: God *comes down* to a patch of holy ground in the wilderness (3:5, 8). The trajectory runs Eden (God walks with man) → the bush → Sinai's descent (19) → the tabernacle filled with glory (40:34) → "the Word became flesh and *tabernacled* among us" (John 1:14) → "the dwelling of God is with man" (Rev 21:3). *Confidence: high.*

**2. The Name / self-revelation of God.** *(textual signal: 3:14–15; payoff: high)* The covenant name is *coined-as-revealed* here and becomes the canon's master-vocabulary for God's identity, expounded at 34:6–7, echoed by Isaiah's "I am he," reversed as judgment at Hosea 1:9, and claimed by Jesus (John 8:58; Mark 12:26). *Confidence: high.*

**3. Mediator / prophet-deliverer.** *(textual signal: the commission; payoff: high)* Moses is the founding mediator (the call-type-scene's first instance), the "prophet like Moses" of Deuteronomy 18:15, whose office the NT fills in Christ (Heb 3; Acts 3:22). *Confidence: high.*

**Confluence.** These three converge **climactically**, not merely in parallel: the *presence* that comes down, the *Name* that is "I will be with you," and the *mediator* through whom the coming-down reaches the people are one reality — God draws near to dwell, and does so through a mediator he names by his own presence. That convergence is the preaching gold: the bush is where presence, name, and mediation meet, and all three land in the incarnation.

### Schnittjer Pass

**Skeleton.** Exodus · the *From-Egypt* movement (1:1–15:21) · leading idea served: *the progressive proximity of God* — this is the commissioning that launches the deliverance and *names the God whose nearness the whole book will trace.*

**Findings.**
1. **Leading word (step 4/6):** *hāyâ/ʾehyeh* binds the presence-promise (3:12) to the Name (3:14–15) — the unit's dominant sound and its theology. `[T]` *high*.
2. **Narrative space (step 2):** *yārad*, "come down" (3:8), is a leading *spatial* word for the whole book — deliverance is a descent that culminates in indwelling. `[T]` *high*.
3. **Type-scene / extended echo (step 3/6):** the prophetic call-with-objection is a repeated ordering (Gideon, Isaiah, Jeremiah) that points beyond Moses to the pattern of the God who commissions the reluctant. `[I]` *moderate*.
4. **Narrative selectivity & time (step 1):** the narrator *brakes* hard on the five-fold objection dialogue — five rounds of resistance given more space than the deliverance-declaration itself — signalling that the unit's weight is on *God's self-disclosure under human resistance*, not on the mission logistics. `[T]` *high*.
5. **First vs second reading (step 5):** on first reading, "you shall serve God on this mountain" (3:12) is an unverifiable promise; on the backward re-read (from ch. 19) it is seen as guaranteed foreshadowing — the sign was a promise all along. `[T]` *high*.

**Echoes/signals.** Leading word: *ʾehyeh/hāyâ*. Structural device: the objection-answer sequence (braking); the proleptic "mountain of God." Intertextual link: Genesis patriarchal covenant (3:6, 15); the call-type-scene.

**Nil returns.** No chiasm; no janus in this unit (the janus function belongs to the Song of the Sea, ch. 15). No symbolic-number play.

### Christological Reading

**Type(s) of connection: Christophany candidate + the Name (direct trajectory) + mediator typology + contrast.**

- **The angel of the LORD (3:2, 4)** — the narrator identifies the appearer as "the angel of the LORD" and then as "the LORD"/"God" without distinction (Tool 6). This is a strong candidate **Christophany** — the angel who *is* God, a pre-incarnate appearing of the Son. *Tests: theological-category (divine self-revelation) — pass; NT-precedent — moderate (the NT reads the wilderness rock as Christ, 1 Cor 10:4, and treats OT theophany Christologically); escalation — pass; authorial-pattern — the apposition is in the text. Confidence: moderate–high; do not overclaim as certain.*
- **The Name (3:14) → Jesus' "I AM."** *Tests: category (divine self-revelation) — pass; NT-precedent — explicit and strong (John 8:58 claims the bush-name; Mark 12:26 has Jesus cite 3:6). Confidence: high.* The "I AM" who was with Moses is the "I AM" who took flesh.
- **Moses as mediator-type (contrast + trajectory).** Moses is the commissioned deliverer who *points beyond himself* — reluctant, "slow of tongue," needing Aaron; the Deuteronomy 18:15 "prophet like me" and the Hebrews 3 "Moses was faithful… as a servant… but Christ as a son" set the trajectory. The *contrast* is as instructive as the likeness: the mediator who cannot speak for himself anticipates the Mediator who is himself the Word. *Confidence: high for the trajectory.*

**Moralism check.**
- *The "be like X" temptation:* "overcome your excuses and step up like Moses" / "have your own burning-bush encounter."
- *The gospel grounding:* the passage's call gets its foundation not in Moses' courage (he has none to spare) but in "I will be with you" — God's presence, which for us is the finished work of Christ and the indwelling Spirit.
- *Christ as hero, not just example:* the God who says "I AM," who comes down to deliver, and who provides the mediator — is the subject of the passage. Preach the I AM who took flesh, not the Moses who found his confidence.

*Confidence: high (Name, mediator trajectory); moderate–high (Christophany).*

### Difficult / Contested Verses

- **3:14 — the sense of *ʾehyeh ʾăšer ʾehyeh*.** *Category: doctrinal/interpretive.* Difficulty: "I AM" (being) vs "I WILL BE" (faithful presence). *Rhetorical function:* it answers "what is his name?" as *assurance* to a people about to be rescued — which tilts against a purely abstract-being reading and toward being-expressed-as-presence, without excluding the ontological note the LXX and John draw out. Handling: preach both, held together (Headline 1); don't reduce to philosophy or to mere "he'll be there for you."
- **3:22 — "you shall plunder the Egyptians."** *Category: ethical.* Difficulty: does God sanction theft? *Honest exegesis:* the framing is reparation for generations of unpaid slave labour and the willing gift of favour-granted neighbours (3:21; 12:36), not stealth robbery — and the gold equips the tabernacle (and, tragically, the calf). Handle with a sentence; don't let it derail.
- **4:11 — "Who makes him mute, or deaf, or seeing, or blind? Is it not I, the LORD?"** *Category: pastoral/apologetic (theodicy and disability).* Difficulty: God claims sovereignty over disability; hearers with disabilities or affected families may be wounded by a careless reading. *Rhetorical function:* the verse is *not* a theodicy statement offered for its own sake but an argument answering Moses' excuse — the Maker of mouths can equip the mouth he made. *Suggested handling:* name the pastoral weight; frame the claim within its rhetorical purpose (God's sufficiency to use whom he calls), and resist both a glib "God causes all disability" and an embarrassed silence.
- **4:14 — "the anger of the LORD was kindled against Moses."** *Category: doctrinal/pastoral.* Difficulty: divine anger at a reluctant servant. Handling: the narrator's verdict distinguishes honest fear (patiently answered ×4) from refusal (4:13); God's accommodation (Aaron) is grace that nonetheless *costs* — Aaron will lead the calf. Don't soften the anger; don't make it capricious.
- **4:21 (adjacent) — the hardening previewed.** *Category: doctrinal.* Flag only: the hardening theme is announced just beyond this unit; route it to the plagues dig (unit 5). `[S: overview landmine list]`

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: "What are your burning-bush moments?"
- **What it looks like:** turning the theophany into a template for dramatic private guidance experiences.
- **Why it's wrong:** confuses narrative with metaphor (Genre) and assumes Moses-is-me when Moses is being uniquely commissioned as Israel's mediator (Who Am I?).
- **The corrective:** the bush reveals *God's* name and presence; ask what it means that *this* God calls and keeps covenant.

### Pitfall: reducing "I AM" to abstract philosophy of being
- **What it looks like:** a metaphysics lecture on divine aseity, detached from the rescue.
- **Why it's wrong:** 3:12 ties the Name to covenant presence ("I will be with you"); the LXX's being-language is true but must not swallow the presence-gloss.
- **The corrective:** preach self-existence *and* faithful nearness together — the God who simply *is* is the God who *will be with* his people.

### Pitfall: moralising Moses into a leadership seminar
- **What it looks like:** "Overcome your excuses and step up to your calling like Moses."
- **Why it's wrong:** the unit keeps exposing the mediator's inadequacy (slow of tongue, needing Aaron) precisely so we look past him; the fifth "excuse" draws God's anger, not applause.
- **The corrective:** preach the greater Mediator the passage reaches for, and the "I will be with you" that answers inadequacy — not a self-help arc.

### Pitfall: treating Aaron's appointment as the happy solution
- **What it looks like:** "God graciously gives Moses a partner — a lovely picture of teamwork."
- **Why it's wrong:** 4:14 marks it as a response to sinful refusal after kindled anger; Aaron will later lead the golden-calf apostasy.
- **The corrective:** God accommodates unbelief, but it costs — grace here is not endorsement.

### Pitfall: mishandling 4:11 ("who makes the deaf, mute, blind?")
- **What it looks like:** either a blunt "God causes all disability" or an embarrassed skip.
- **Why it's wrong:** the verse is an argument for God's *sufficiency to equip whom he calls*, not a free-standing theodicy; careless handling wounds hearers.
- **The corrective:** frame it within its rhetorical purpose, name the pastoral weight, and let it serve the point that the Maker of mouths will be *with* Moses' mouth.

### Pitfall: skipping 2:23–25 and starting at the bush
- **What it looks like:** opening cold at 3:1 as a stand-alone spiritual-encounter story.
- **Why it's wrong:** the bush is the covenant's answer to the groan just narrated; without 2:23–25 the theophany floats free of its reason.
- **The corrective:** establish the groan and the remembered covenant first, so the four verbs of 3:7–8 land as God keeping his word.

## Open Questions / Uncertainties

**Three of the four were resolved at the Research Assistant pass (August 2026); one remains.**

1. ~~**The aspect of *ʾehyeh* (3:14)**~~ **⭐ RESOLVED — and the ambiguity turns out to be the finding.** **Grammar favours the future:** *ʾehyeh* is imperfect, which makes the statement soteriological — a promise of continuing activity — rather than an ontological claim. **Tradition favours the present:** the LXX and Vulgate read it that way, and the resulting "I AM" is the ontological reading the church has largely preached. **Context tilts back toward "I AM"**, since 3:14 echoes 3:12's *"I will be with you"* — which is exactly this report's own finding. **The verdict to preach:** *"the Lord's response is intentionally vague rather than a complete definition, refusing to confine the name within fixed boundaries while giving it a future orientation that opens possibilities for further revelation"* — **the tension may be intentional rather than resolvable** (Blackburn, NSBT 28, 35–37; Bechtold, *LBD*; Johnstone; *Eerdmans Dictionary*). *High on the state of the question.* `[revised: research-assistant pass]`
2. **The angel-of-the-LORD Christophany (3:2, 4)** — **still open, and deliberately not pursued at this pass.** A strong candidate but not certain; **the confidence should stay moderate–high and must not be asserted as settled.** *Verify against a commentary that treats OT Christophany, particularly the angel/LORD apposition.* `[revised: research-assistant pass — not pursued]`
3. ~~**The *kābēd* / *kābôd* resonance (4:10)**~~ **⭐ RESOLVED — recognised, and not read back.** The wordplay is acknowledged in Exodus scholarship and *"operates across the plague narrative rather than being read back exclusively from 14:4, 17"*. It rests on the shared notion of **weight** — giving due weight to a person or deity. **So Moses' heavy mouth, Pharaoh's heavy heart, Israel's heavy labour and the LORD's glory are one lexical field, available from chapter 4 onward.** *Upgraded from moderate to moderate–high.* `[revised: research-assistant pass]`
4. ~~**Extent of the "plunder" ethic (3:22)**~~ **⭐ RESOLVED — the robust reading is right.** The **piel of *nāṣal* means "plunder"**, functioning as a military metaphor for *"taking something away by force, but never by fraud"*. **The softer "ask and receive" will not carry it.** Commentators resolve the ethics three ways rather than softening the verb: *šāʾal* ("ask") describes the **manner** of acquisition while *nāṣal* describes its **nature**; the wealth fulfils the sworn promise of **Genesis 15:14** that Israel would come out "with great possessions"; and the text presents the Egyptians as **favourably disposed, not coerced**. **The spoils function as back-wages — reparation for enslavement, not theft** (Kaiser, *Recovering the Unity of the Bible*, 93–94; NET notes). *High.* `[revised: research-assistant pass]`
