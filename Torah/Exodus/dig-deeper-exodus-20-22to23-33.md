## The Passage

**20:22** וַיֹּ֤אמֶר יְהוָה֙ אֶל־מֹשֶׁ֔ה כֹּ֥ה תֹאמַ֖ר אֶל־בְּנֵ֣י יִשְׂרָאֵ֑ל אַתֶּ֣ם רְאִיתֶ֔ם כִּ֚י מִן־הַשָּׁמַ֔יִם דִּבַּ֖רְתִּי עִמָּכֶֽם׃

*NASB95:* Then the LORD said to Moses, “Thus you shall say to the sons of Israel, ‘You yourselves have seen that I have spoken to you from heaven.

**20:23** לֹ֥א תַעֲשׂ֖וּן אִתִּ֑י אֱלֹ֤הֵי כֶ֨סֶף֙ וֵאלֹהֵ֣י זָהָ֔ב לֹ֥א תַעֲשׂ֖וּ לָכֶֽם׃

*NASB95:* ‘You shall not make other gods besides Me; gods of silver or gods of gold, you shall not make for yourselves.

**20:24** מִזְבַּ֣ח אֲדָמָה֮ תַּעֲשֶׂה־לִּי֒ וְזָבַחְתָּ֣ עָלָ֗יו אֶת־עֹלֹתֶ֨יךָ֙ וְאֶת־שְׁלָמֶ֔יךָ אֶת־צֹֽאנְךָ֖ וְאֶת־בְּקָרֶ֑ךָ בְּכָל־הַמָּקוֹם֙ אֲשֶׁ֣ר אַזְכִּ֣יר אֶת־שְׁמִ֔י אָב֥וֹא אֵלֶ֖יךָ וּבֵרַכְתִּֽיךָ׃

*NASB95:* ‘You shall make an altar of earth for Me, and you shall sacrifice on it your burnt offerings and your peace offerings, your sheep and your oxen; in every place where I cause My name to be remembered, I will come to you and bless you.

**20:25** וְאִם־מִזְבַּ֤ח אֲבָנִים֙ תַּֽעֲשֶׂה־לִּ֔י לֹֽא־תִבְנֶ֥ה אֶתְהֶ֖ן גָּזִ֑ית כִּ֧י חַרְבְּךָ֛ הֵנַ֥פְתָּ עָלֶ֖יהָ וַתְּחַֽלְלֶֽהָ׃

*NASB95:* ‘If you make an altar of stone for Me, you shall not build it of cut stones, for if you wield your tool on it, you will profane it.

**20:26** וְלֹֽא־תַעֲלֶ֥ה בְמַעֲלֹ֖ת עַֽל־מִזְבְּחִ֑י אֲשֶׁ֛ר לֹֽא־תִגָּלֶ֥ה עֶרְוָתְךָ֖ עָלָֽיו׃ פ

*NASB95:* ‘And you shall not go up by steps to My altar, so that your nakedness will not be exposed on it.’

**21:1** וְאֵ֨לֶּה֙ הַמִּשְׁפָּטִ֔ים אֲשֶׁ֥ר תָּשִׂ֖ים לִפְנֵיהֶֽם׃

*NASB95:* “Now these are the ordinances which you are to set before them:

**21:2** כִּ֤י תִקְנֶה֙ עֶ֣בֶד עִבְרִ֔י שֵׁ֥שׁ שָׁנִ֖ים יַעֲבֹ֑ד וּבַ֨שְּׁבִעִ֔ת יֵצֵ֥א לַֽחָפְשִׁ֖י חִנָּֽם׃

*NASB95:* “If you buy a Hebrew slave, he shall serve for six years; but on the seventh he shall go out as a free man without payment.

**21:3** אִם־בְּגַפּ֥וֹ יָבֹ֖א בְּגַפּ֣וֹ יֵצֵ֑א אִם־בַּ֤עַל אִשָּׁה֙ ה֔וּא וְיָצְאָ֥ה אִשְׁתּ֖וֹ עִמּֽוֹ׃

*NASB95:* “If he comes alone, he shall go out alone; if he is the husband of a wife, then his wife shall go out with him.

**21:4** אִם־אֲדֹנָיו֙ יִתֶּן־ל֣וֹ אִשָּׁ֔ה וְיָלְדָה־ל֥וֹ בָנִ֖ים א֣וֹ בָנ֑וֹת הָאִשָּׁ֣ה וִילָדֶ֗יהָ תִּהְיֶה֙ לַֽאדֹנֶ֔יהָ וְה֖וּא יֵצֵ֥א בְגַפּֽוֹ׃

*NASB95:* “If his master gives him a wife, and she bears him sons or daughters, the wife and her children shall belong to her master, and he shall go out alone.

**21:5** וְאִם־אָמֹ֤ר יֹאמַר֙ הָעֶ֔בֶד אָהַ֨בְתִּי֙ אֶת־אֲדֹנִ֔י אֶת־אִשְׁתִּ֖י וְאֶת־בָּנָ֑י לֹ֥א אֵצֵ֖א חָפְשִֽׁי׃

*NASB95:* “But if the slave plainly says, ‘I love my master, my wife and my children; I will not go out as a free man,’

**21:6** וְהִגִּישׁ֤וֹ אֲדֹנָיו֙ אֶל־הָ֣אֱלֹהִ֔ים וְהִגִּישׁוֹ֙ אֶל־הַדֶּ֔לֶת א֖וֹ אֶל־הַמְּזוּזָ֑ה וְרָצַ֨ע אֲדֹנָ֤יו אֶת־אָזְנוֹ֙ בַּמַּרְצֵ֔עַ וַעֲבָד֖וֹ לְעֹלָֽם׃ ס

*NASB95:* then his master shall bring him to God, then he shall bring him to the door or the doorpost. And his master shall pierce his ear with an awl; and he shall serve him permanently.

**21:7** וְכִֽי־יִמְכֹּ֥ר אִ֛ישׁ אֶת־בִּתּ֖וֹ לְאָמָ֑ה לֹ֥א תֵצֵ֖א כְּצֵ֥את הָעֲבָדִֽים׃

*NASB95:* “If a man sells his daughter as a female slave, she is not to go free as the male slaves do.

**21:8** אִם־רָעָ֞ה בְּעֵינֵ֧י אֲדֹנֶ֛יהָ אֲשֶׁר־לא יְעָדָ֖הּ וְהֶפְדָּ֑הּ לְעַ֥ם נָכְרִ֛י לֹא־יִמְשֹׁ֥ל לְמָכְרָ֖הּ בְּבִגְדוֹ־בָֽהּ׃

*NASB95:* “If she is displeasing in the eyes of her master who designated her for himself, then he shall let her be redeemed. He does not have authority to sell her to a foreign people because of his unfairness to her.

**21:9** וְאִם־לִבְנ֖וֹ יִֽיעָדֶ֑נָּה כְּמִשְׁפַּ֥ט הַבָּנ֖וֹת יַעֲשֶׂה־לָּֽהּ׃

*NASB95:* “If he designates her for his son, he shall deal with her according to the custom of daughters.

**21:10** אִם־אַחֶ֖רֶת יִֽקַּֽח־ל֑וֹ שְׁאֵרָ֛הּ כְּסוּתָ֥הּ וְעֹנָתָ֖הּ לֹ֥א יִגְרָֽע׃

*NASB95:* “If he takes to himself another woman, he may not reduce her food, her clothing, or her conjugal rights.

**21:11** וְאִם־שְׁלָ֨שׁ־אֵ֔לֶּה לֹ֥א יַעֲשֶׂ֖ה לָ֑הּ וְיָצְאָ֥ה חִנָּ֖ם אֵ֥ין כָּֽסֶף׃ ס

*NASB95:* “If he will not do these three things for her, then she shall go out for nothing, without payment of money.

**21:12** מַכֵּ֥ה אִ֛ישׁ וָמֵ֖ת מ֥וֹת יוּמָֽת׃

*NASB95:* “He who strikes a man so that he dies shall surely be put to death.

**21:13** וַאֲשֶׁר֙ לֹ֣א צָדָ֔ה וְהָאֱלֹהִ֖ים אִנָּ֣ה לְיָד֑וֹ וְשַׂמְתִּ֤י לְךָ֙ מָק֔וֹם אֲשֶׁ֥ר יָנ֖וּס שָֽׁמָּה׃ ס

*NASB95:* “But if he did not lie in wait for him, but God let him fall into his hand, then I will appoint you a place to which he may flee.

**21:14** וְכִֽי־יָזִ֥ד אִ֛ישׁ עַל־רֵעֵ֖הוּ לְהָרְג֣וֹ בְעָרְמָ֑ה מֵעִ֣ם מִזְבְּחִ֔י תִּקָּחֶ֖נּוּ לָמֽוּת׃ ס

*NASB95:* “If, however, a man acts presumptuously toward his neighbor, so as to kill him craftily, you are to take him even from My altar, that he may die.

**21:15** וּמַכֵּ֥ה אָבִ֛יו וְאִמּ֖וֹ מ֥וֹת יוּמָֽת׃

*NASB95:* “He who strikes his father or his mother shall surely be put to death.

**21:16** וְגֹנֵ֨ב אִ֧ישׁ וּמְכָר֛וֹ וְנִמְצָ֥א בְיָד֖וֹ מ֥וֹת יוּמָֽת׃ ס

*NASB95:* “He who kidnaps a man, whether he sells him or he is found in his possession, shall surely be put to death.

**21:17** וּמְקַלֵּ֥ל אָבִ֛יו וְאִמּ֖וֹ מ֥וֹת יוּמָֽת׃ ס

*NASB95:* “He who curses his father or his mother shall surely be put to death.

**21:18** וְכִֽי־יְרִיבֻ֣ן אֲנָשִׁ֔ים וְהִכָּה־אִישׁ֙ אֶת־רֵעֵ֔הוּ בְּאֶ֖בֶן א֣וֹ בְאֶגְרֹ֑ף וְלֹ֥א יָמ֖וּת וְנָפַ֥ל לְמִשְׁכָּֽב׃

*NASB95:* “If men have a quarrel and one strikes the other with a stone or with his fist, and he does not die but remains in bed,

**21:19** אִם־יָק֞וּם וְהִתְהַלֵּ֥ךְ בַּח֛וּץ עַל־מִשְׁעַנְתּ֖וֹ וְנִקָּ֣ה הַמַּכֶּ֑ה רַ֥ק שִׁבְתּ֛וֹ יִתֵּ֖ן וְרַפֹּ֥א יְרַפֵּֽא׃ ס

*NASB95:* if he gets up and walks around outside on his staff, then he who struck him shall go unpunished; he shall only pay for his loss of time, and shall take care of him until he is completely healed.

**21:20** וְכִֽי־יַכֶּה֩ אִ֨ישׁ אֶת־עַבְדּ֜וֹ א֤וֹ אֶת־אֲמָתוֹ֙ בַּשֵּׁ֔בֶט וּמֵ֖ת תַּ֣חַת יָד֑וֹ נָקֹ֖ם יִנָּקֵֽם׃

*NASB95:* “If a man strikes his male or female slave with a rod and he dies at his hand, he shall be punished.

**21:21** אַ֥ךְ אִם־י֛וֹם א֥וֹ יוֹמַ֖יִם יַעֲמֹ֑ד לֹ֣א יֻקַּ֔ם כִּ֥י כַסְפּ֖וֹ הֽוּא׃ ס

*NASB95:* “If, however, he survives a day or two, no vengeance shall be taken; for he is his property.

**21:22** וְכִֽי־יִנָּצ֣וּ אֲנָשִׁ֗ים וְנָ֨גְפ֜וּ אִשָּׁ֤ה הָרָה֙ וְיָצְא֣וּ יְלָדֶ֔יהָ וְלֹ֥א יִהְיֶ֖ה אָס֑וֹן עָנ֣וֹשׁ יֵעָנֵ֗שׁ כַּֽאֲשֶׁ֨ר יָשִׁ֤ית עָלָיו֙ בַּ֣עַל הָֽאִשָּׁ֔ה וְנָתַ֖ן בִּפְלִלִֽים׃

*NASB95:* “If men struggle with each other and strike a woman with child so that she gives birth prematurely, yet there is no injury, he shall surely be fined as the woman’s husband may demand of him, and he shall pay as the judges decide.

**21:23** וְאִם־אָס֖וֹן יִהְיֶ֑ה וְנָתַתָּ֥ה נֶ֖פֶשׁ תַּ֥חַת נָֽפֶשׁ׃

*NASB95:* “But if there is any further injury, then you shall appoint as a penalty life for life,

**21:24** עַ֚יִן תַּ֣חַת עַ֔יִן שֵׁ֖ן תַּ֣חַת שֵׁ֑ן יָ֚ד תַּ֣חַת יָ֔ד רֶ֖גֶל תַּ֥חַת רָֽגֶל׃

*NASB95:* eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot,

**21:25** כְּוִיָּה֙ תַּ֣חַת כְּוִיָּ֔ה פֶּ֖צַע תַּ֣חַת פָּ֑צַע חַבּוּרָ֕ה תַּ֖חַת חַבּוּרָֽה׃ ס

*NASB95:* burn for burn, wound for wound, bruise for bruise.

**21:26** וְכִֽי־יַכֶּ֨ה אִ֜ישׁ אֶת־עֵ֥ין עַבְדּ֛וֹ אֽוֹ־אֶת־עֵ֥ין אֲמָת֖וֹ וְשִֽׁחֲתָ֑הּ לַֽחָפְשִׁ֥י יְשַׁלְּחֶ֖נּוּ תַּ֥חַת עֵינֽוֹ׃ ס

*NASB95:* “If a man strikes the eye of his male or female slave, and destroys it, he shall let him go free on account of his eye.

**21:27** וְאִם־שֵׁ֥ן עַבְדּ֛וֹ אֽוֹ־שֵׁ֥ן אֲמָת֖וֹ יַפִּ֑יל לַֽחָפְשִׁ֥י יְשַׁלְּחֶ֖נּוּ תַּ֥חַת שִׁנּֽוֹ׃ פ

*NASB95:* “And if he knocks out a tooth of his male or female slave, he shall let him go free on account of his tooth.

**21:28** וְכִֽי־יִגַּ֨ח שׁ֥וֹר אֶת־אִ֛ישׁ א֥וֹ אֶת־אִשָּׁ֖ה וָמֵ֑ת סָק֨וֹל יִסָּקֵ֜ל הַשּׁ֗וֹר וְלֹ֤א יֵאָכֵל֙ אֶת־בְּשָׂר֔וֹ וּבַ֥עַל הַשּׁ֖וֹר נָקִֽי׃

*NASB95:* “If an ox gores a man or a woman to death, the ox shall surely be stoned and its flesh shall not be eaten; but the owner of the ox shall go unpunished.

**21:29** וְאִ֡ם שׁוֹר֩ נַגָּ֨ח ה֜וּא מִתְּמֹ֣ל שִׁלְשֹׁ֗ם וְהוּעַ֤ד בִּבְעָלָיו֙ וְלֹ֣א יִשְׁמְרֶ֔נּוּ וְהֵמִ֥ית אִ֖ישׁ א֣וֹ אִשָּׁ֑ה הַשּׁוֹר֙ יִסָּקֵ֔ל וְגַם־בְּעָלָ֖יו יוּמָֽת׃

*NASB95:* “If, however, an ox was previously in the habit of goring and its owner has been warned, yet he does not confine it and it kills a man or a woman, the ox shall be stoned and its owner also shall be put to death.

**21:30** אִם־כֹּ֖פֶר יוּשַׁ֣ת עָלָ֑יו וְנָתַן֙ פִּדְיֹ֣ן נַפְשׁ֔וֹ כְּכֹ֥ל אֲשֶׁר־יוּשַׁ֖ת עָלָֽיו׃

*NASB95:* “If a ransom is demanded of him, then he shall give for the redemption of his life whatever is demanded of him.

**21:31** אוֹ־בֵ֥ן יִגָּ֖ח אוֹ־בַ֣ת יִגָּ֑ח כַּמִּשְׁפָּ֥ט הַזֶּ֖ה יֵעָ֥שֶׂה לּֽוֹ׃

*NASB95:* “Whether it gores a son or a daughter, it shall be done to him according to the same rule.

**21:32** אִם־עֶ֛בֶד יִגַּ֥ח הַשּׁ֖וֹר א֣וֹ אָמָ֑ה כֶּ֣סֶף ׀ שְׁלֹשִׁ֣ים שְׁקָלִ֗ים יִתֵּן֙ לַֽאדֹנָ֔יו וְהַשּׁ֖וֹר יִסָּקֵֽל׃ ס

*NASB95:* “If the ox gores a male or female slave, the owner shall give his or her master thirty shekels of silver, and the ox shall be stoned.

**21:33** וְכִֽי־יִפְתַּ֨ח אִ֜ישׁ בּ֗וֹר א֠וֹ כִּֽי־יִכְרֶ֥ה אִ֛ישׁ בֹּ֖ר וְלֹ֣א יְכַסֶּ֑נּוּ וְנָֽפַל־שָׁ֥מָּה שּׁ֖וֹר א֥וֹ חֲמֽוֹר׃

*NASB95:* “If a man opens a pit, or digs a pit and does not cover it over, and an ox or a donkey falls into it,

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims, each reached from the Hebrew and each surfaced by more than one tool:

1. **The code is framed by the first commandment and by the Name — whose name is remembered, and whom Israel serves.** It opens, *"You shall not make [gods] with Me… In every place where I cause My name to be remembered (אַזְכִּיר אֶת־שְׁמִי, 'I cause my name to be remembered') I will come to you and bless you"* (20:23–24). It closes its legal part, *"the name of other gods you shall not cause to be remembered"* (וְשֵׁם אֱלֹהִים אֲחֵרִים לֹא תַזְכִּירוּ, 'and the name of other gods you shall not mention', 23:13), and its epilogue, *"you shall not… serve them… you shall serve the LORD your God, and He will bless"* (23:24–25), ending on *"if you serve their gods, it will surely be a snare to you"* (23:33). **The causative of זָכַר ('to remember') with שֵׁם ('name') stands in only these two verses of Exodus, 20:24 and 23:13 (WLC).** `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Repetition, Vocabulary, Translations, Author's Purpose. *High confidence on the wording.* **Revised at claim audit 2:** as a *design of nested frames* the claim is **low–moderate** (above chance, 7th–19th percentile, but not unusual). What stands at **moderate** is narrower: the code opens and closes with worship, and two verbal turns mark the ends — God's name remembered (20:24) against other gods' names (23:13), and the slave who *serves* (21:2, 6) against the people who must *serve* the LORD (23:24–25, 33). `[S: audit 2]` **Both English versions break the link** — "remembered" at 20:24, "mention" at 23:13 — so the congregation cannot hear it unless the preacher says it.

2. **The exodus is turned into an institution: the code's first law is a release, and the first section ends where it began.** *"If you buy a Hebrew slave, he shall serve for six years; but on the seventh he shall go out as a free man"* (21:2). **חָפְשִׁי ('free') stands in Exodus only at 21:2, 5, 26, 27 (WLC)** — so the first section opens with a slave going out free and closes, at Leningrad's *petuchah* after 21:27, with a slave sent out free for an eye or a tooth. The verb at 21:26–27 is שִׁלַּח ('let go, send away'), the verb of *"Let My people go"*; עִבְרִי ('Hebrew') appears **for the last time in the book** at 21:2; and the six/seventh rhythm of 21:2 returns at 23:10–12 to close the legal corpus. `[T]` Surfaced by: Structure, Repetition, Vocabulary, Positional Necessity, Move 4. *High confidence.* **A nation of released slaves is told, first of all, to release.**

3. **The verbs of Egypt are redeployed against Israel.** The piel of עָנָה ('afflict') stands in Exodus **only at 1:11, 1:12, 10:3 and 22:22–23** — Pharaoh's taskmasters, then Moses to Pharaoh, then *"You shall not afflict any widow or orphan"*. לָחַץ ('oppress') stands **only at 3:9, 22:21 and 23:9** — the oppression God saw in Egypt, then the sojourner Israel must not oppress. And the cry God heard at 3:7 is the cry he will hear at 22:23 and 22:27 — *against Israel* (22:24). The motive clause *"for you were sojourners in the land of Egypt"* stands word for word at 22:21 and 23:9, bracketing the block on the vulnerable and on justice. `[T]` Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Repetition, Move 4, Tone, Original Audience. *High confidence.* **God takes the side of the afflicted inside Israel exactly as he took it against Egypt.**

4. **The code plants both halves of 34:6–7.** *"I will hear him, for I am gracious"* (22:27): **חַנּוּן ('gracious') stands in Exodus only at 22:27 and 34:6.** *"He will not pardon your transgression"* (23:21): **נָשָׂא ('to bear, forgive') with פֶּשַׁע ('transgression') stands in Exodus only at 23:21 and 34:7.** And between them, *"I will not justify the wicked"* (לֹא־אַצְדִּיק רָשָׁע, 23:7). The God who is gracious to the poor man in his cold night is the God who will not clear the guilty. `[T]` for the pairs; `[I]` for reading them as the two halves of the character formula, planted. Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Move 4, Christological Reading, Difficult Verses. *High on the data; moderate–high on the reading.* **The tension the Old Testament cannot resolve is already inside the case law.**

5. **The code's epilogue is the Gospel of Mark's first quotation.** *"Behold, I am going to send an angel before you to guard you along the way"* (23:20) reads in the Greek of record **Καὶ ἰδοὺ ἐγὼ ἀποστέλλω τὸν ἄγγελόν μου πρὸ προσώπου σου** ('and behold, I send my messenger before your face', Rahlfs). **Matthew 11:10 reproduces that clause word for word, ἐγώ included**, and Mark 1:2 and Luke 7:27 nearly so, completing it with Malachi's *"who will prepare your way"*. Malachi had already reworked this verse: מַלְאָךְ ('messenger') + שָׁלַח ('send') + דֶּרֶךְ ('way') + פָּנִים ('face') in one verse **only at Gen 24:40, Exod 23:20 and Mal 3:1 (WLC)**. `[T]` Surfaced by: Quotation/Allusion (Move 1–3), Translations (ancient versions), Christological Reading. *High confidence on the Gospel wording.* **Revised at claim audit 2:** Malachi 3:1 **fuses** Exod 23:20 (with 33:2) and Isa 40:3 — the second clause, *"he will clear the way"*, is Isaiah's idiom (the piel of פָּנָה, 'clear', with דֶּרֶךְ, 'way', only Isa 40:3; 57:14; 62:10; Mal 3:1); the "only three verses" claim is overstated (Gen 24:40 is accidental; Exod 33:2–3 is a near-twin of 23:20); הָאָדוֹן ('the Lord') is closer to Isaiah's usage. **Moderate–high in that form; moderate for Exodus alone.** `[S: audit 2]` **In the Gospels "you" is Jesus: he stands where Israel stood at the edge of the land.**

## Phase 0.55 — Canonical Position

**Section:** Torah, second book. **Reading implication:** the code is read by a people who already have Genesis — the oath to Abraham, the land promise, the patriarchs' altars — and who are being told *within the founding story* what life as the redeemed is to look like. **Presupposes (checked):** the bush speech, *"I have surely seen the affliction (עֳנִי) of My people… I have heard their cry (צַעֲקָתָם)… the oppression (הַלַּחַץ) with which the Egyptians oppress (לֹחֲצִים) them"* (3:7, 9) — the code re-uses the cry, the hearing and the oppressing (22:21–23; 23:9); the Song's *"terror (אֵימָה) and dread fall upon them"* (15:16) — re-issued at 23:27; *"I am the LORD, your healer… none of the diseases (הַמַּחֲלָה)"* (15:26) — re-issued at 23:25; the people's vow *"All that the LORD has spoken we will do"* (19:8), given before any terms were heard. **Handoff:** to 24:3–8 (the terms read and sworn to), and canonically to Deuteronomy, which re-issues much of this code (15:12–18 the slave law; 22:1–4 the stray animal; 16 the festivals). **Neighbours:** the Decalogue before it; the tabernacle instructions after the ratification.

**Canonical and compositional order.** Nothing in the passage requires the question to be raised. The relation between this code, the Decalogue and Deuteronomy's restatements is a compositional question the text does not settle; it is recorded, not resolved.

## Phase 0.6 — Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement.** Four things have been set up that this passage now provides.

1. **A mediator has been requested and granted.** *"Speak to us yourself and we will listen; but let not God speak to us"* (20:19); *"Moses approached the thick cloud where God was"* (20:21). The next words are *"Thus you shall say to the sons of Israel"* (20:22). **This is the first mediated speech after the people asked for one.** `[T]`
2. **A covenant has been proposed, and agreed to, without terms.** *"If you will indeed obey My voice and keep My covenant…"* (19:5); *"All that the LORD has spoken we will do"* (19:8). The Decalogue has given the covenant's foundations, but it does not regulate a lawsuit, a loan or a goring ox. **This passage supplies the terms the vow of 19:8 had already accepted, and 24:3, 7 has the people accept them again — this time having heard them.** `[T]`
3. **Judges have been appointed with nothing yet to judge by.** In chapter 18 Moses sits from morning to evening because *"when they have a dispute (דָּבָר), it comes to me, and I judge between a man and his neighbour (רֵעֵהוּ), and make known the statutes of God and His laws"* (18:16), and he is told to delegate the small cases. **The code opens *"these are the ordinances (הַמִּשְׁפָּטִים) which you are to set before them"* (21:1), and its property section turns on רֵעַ ('neighbour', ten verses of the block) and on *"the case of both parties"* (דְּבַר־שְׁנֵיהֶם, 22:9).** The judges of 18:21–26 now have their case law. `[T]` for the vocabulary; `[I]` for the link. *Moderate–high.*
4. **The question "Is the LORD among us?" (17:7) has been asked.** The code's first promise answers it in kind — *"I will come to you and bless you"* (20:24) — before the tabernacle gives it an address (25:8). `[T]` / `[S: overview, 20:24 → 25:8]`

**Necessity answer.** The code stands **between the voice and the blood**: after the people have heard God speak from heaven and asked not to hear him again (20:18–19), and before the blood is thrown on them in token of these very words (24:8, *"in accordance with all these words"*). It must stand here because the covenant cannot be ratified until its terms have been set before the people, and it cannot be set before them except by the mediator they have just asked for. It also stands **after the exodus and in its vocabulary**: the book's first half has been about a slave people let go (שִׁלַּח), going out (יָצָא), serving (עָבַד) another master, crying and being heard. The first thing the code legislates is a slave going out in the seventh year (21:2), and the last thing it warns against is serving other gods (23:33). **The redeemed are told what it will mean, in the courts and fields and households of the land, to be people whom God brought out.**

**Implication for this passage.** The code is not a detour from the story into legislation. It is the story's answer to "what now?" — and its first and last words (no gods with me; my name remembered; serve the LORD) are the Decalogue's first word made concrete.

**Revisit after Tool 11.** Move 2 and Move 4 sharpen the answer. The code re-voices the bush (3:7–9) and the Song (15:16, 26) — the two places where God first promised to see, hear, bring in and heal — and turns them into Israel's own obligations (22:21–27) and conditional promises (23:20–31). **The promises of chapters 3 and 15 are restated as covenant terms.** `[T]` for the chains below.

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book level.** The overview's purpose stands: the LORD redeems, binds his people to himself, and comes to dwell — *"that I might dwell among them"* (29:46). `[S: overview]`

**Passage level.** To set before the redeemed people the terms of the covenant they have agreed to keep, so that the whole of their common life — worship, slaves, violence, property, sex, the vulnerable, the courts, the calendar, the conquest — is ordered under the God who brought them out of Egypt and will bring them in. Three features of the text show where its weight lies:

- **It is God's own speech, and he speaks in the first person even inside the case law.** *"I will appoint you a place"* (21:13); *"I will surely hear his cry, and My anger will be kindled, and I will kill you"* (22:23–24); *"I will hear him, for I am gracious"* (22:27); *"I will not justify the wicked"* (23:7). A Mesopotamian code speaks in the king's voice; this one speaks in God's, and God names himself as the party who hears the injured. `[T]` for the first-person forms; `[S: overview research]` for the ANE comparison.
- **The frame is worship.** The code opens with an altar (20:24–26) and closes with festivals and the first fruits brought *"into the house of the LORD your God"* (23:14–19), then with service of the LORD rather than other gods (23:24–25, 33). `[T]`
- **It carries its own motives.** Egypt (22:21; 23:9, 15), God's hearing (22:23, 27), God's character (22:27; 23:7), God's promise (23:20–31). `[T]`

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

See Phase 0.6 above.

**Immediately before.** 20:18–21: the people see the thunder and the smoking mountain, stand far off, and ask Moses to speak to them. 20:21 ends with a *petuchah* in Leningrad, and 20:22 opens a new speech of the LORD to Moses.

**Immediately after.** 24:1–2: *"Then He said to Moses, 'Come up to the LORD'"*. The addressee changes from the people (through Moses) to Moses and the leaders, and the subject changes from what Israel is to do to who is to ascend. 23:33 ends with a *petuchah*. **The extent 20:22–23:33 is therefore the extent of one speech-content, bounded by paragraph markers at both ends in Leningrad.** `[T]` for the change of addressee; `[unchecked — apparatus spread]` for the paragraph markers as boundaries.

**The name "Book of the Covenant".** 24:7 says Moses *"took the book of the covenant (סֵפֶר הַבְּרִית) and read it in the hearing of the people"*. **What the text itself says the book contained is less precise than the label.** 24:3 says Moses told the people *"all the words of the LORD and all the ordinances (הַמִּשְׁפָּטִים)"*, and 24:4 that he *"wrote down all the words of the LORD"*. "The ordinances" picks up 21:1 exactly, so the code is certainly *in* the book. Whether the book also held the Ten Words (*"God spoke all these words"*, 20:1) — so that "the words of the LORD" are 20:1–17 and "the ordinances" are 21–23 — the text does not say. **That the "Book of the Covenant" is coextensive with 20:22–23:33 is the scholarly convention**, convenient and probably close, not something Exodus states. `[T]` for 24:3–7; `[S]` for the conventional extent. *This matters for one sentence of the sermon, not its shape — but say "the covenant terms read out in 24:7" rather than "what Exodus calls the Book of the Covenant", and the claim is unassailable.*

**Wider context.** The code sits in the third movement of the book, "at the mountain" (19–40), in the stretch between the covenant proposal (19:1–8) and the tabernacle instructions (25–31). Its content returns twice in the book: the festival laws of 23:14–19 are re-issued almost verbatim in the covenant renewal after the calf (34:18–26), and the angel promise of 23:20–23 is turned into a sentence at 32:34; 33:2–3. `[T]`

### 3. Structure

**The paragraph markers first.** Leningrad divides the 105 verses with **five *petuchot*** — after 20:26, 21:27, 22:13 [Heb 22:12], 22:24 [Heb 22:23] and 23:33 — and **twenty-nine *setumot*** inside them. `[T]` as a datum about the manuscript; `[unchecked — apparatus spread]` — paragraphing varies between manuscripts, and nothing below rests on the *petuchot* alone. Read as sections, the five are:

| Leningrad paragraph | Verses | Content | How it ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20:22–26 | The altar prologue | *"…that your nakedness will not be exposed on it"* |
| 2 | 21:1–27 | Heading; slave release; capital offences; injuries | **A slave let go free** for an eye or a tooth (21:26–27) |
| 3 | 21:28–22:13 | The goring ox; property: pit, theft, grazing, fire, deposits | An animal torn by beasts: *"he shall not make restitution"* |
| 4 | 22:14–24 | Loans; seduction; sorcery; bestiality; other gods; **sojourner, widow, orphan** | **"Your wives shall become widows and your children fatherless"** |
| 5 | 22:25–23:33 | The poor; pledges; God and rulers; first fruits; courts; the enemy's animal; sabbatical year and day; festivals; the angel and the land | **"…it will surely be a snare to you"** |

The *petuchot* do not cut the code by topic so much as **close each paragraph on a sentence of weight**: release, then the limit of liability, then God's threat, then the snare. *Moderate confidence on that observation.* `[I]`

**By content, the code has three parts and a frame.**

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 20:22–26 | **Prologue: God spoke from heaven; no gods with me; the altar** | 2nd person, God's "I"; *"I will come to you and bless you"* |
| 21:1 | Heading | *"These are the ordinances"* |
| 21:2–22:17 | **Casuistic law** ("if a man…") | Slaves (21:2–11); capital offences (21:12–17); injury (21:18–32); property (21:33–22:15); seduction (22:16–17) |
| 22:18–23:19 | **Apodictic and motivated law** ("you shall not…") | Death penalties (22:18–20); the vulnerable (22:21–27); God's claims (22:28–31); justice (23:1–9); sacred time (23:10–19) |
| 23:20–33 | **Epilogue: the angel, the name, the land** | God's "I" again; promise and warning |

The change of grammar at 22:18 is visible on the page: the "if/when" (כִּי / אִם) clauses with third-person subjects give way, almost at once, to second-person prohibitions, and the divine "I" multiplies (22:23–27; 23:7, 13, 15). `[T]` *High.*

**Devices — nested frames, not a chiasm.** The code is enclosed by several frames, each verified in the Hebrew:

- **The Name and the gods (outer).** 20:23–24 (no gods *"with Me"*; *"where I cause My name to be remembered"*) ↔ 23:13 (*"the name of other gods you shall not cause to be remembered"*) and 23:24–25, 32–33 (other gods; serve the LORD; snare). The causative of זָכַר with שֵׁם: **20:24 and 23:13 only, in Exodus (WLC).** `[T]` *High on words.*
- **Blessing and place (outer).** 20:24 *"I will come to you and **bless** you"* ↔ 23:25 *"He will **bless** your bread and your water"*; 20:24 *"in every **place**"* ↔ 23:20 *"to the **place** which I have prepared"* — with 21:13 *"I will appoint you a **place** to which he may flee"* in between. `[T]`
- **Serving (outer).** עָבַד ('to serve'): 21:2, 6 (the slave serves his master) ↔ 23:24, 25, 33 (serve their gods / serve the LORD / serve their gods). **The verb appears five times in the code, at its two ends and nowhere between (WLC).** `[T]` *High on the distribution.*
- **Release and the seventh (the legal corpus).** 21:2 (six years, the seventh) ↔ 23:10–12 (six years, the seventh year; six days, the seventh day). **שֵׁשׁ ('six') and שְׁבִיעִי ('seventh') stand in the code only at 21:2 and 23:10–12 (WLC).** `[T]` *High.*
- **Release (the first section).** חָפְשִׁי ('free'): 21:2, 5 ↔ 21:26, 27, **its only occurrences in Exodus**, with Leningrad's *petuchah* after 21:27. `[T]` *High on words.*
- **The sojourner and Egypt (the motivated law).** 22:21 ↔ 23:9, the identical clause כִּי־גֵרִים הֱיִיתֶם בְּאֶרֶץ מִצְרָיִם ('for you were sojourners in the land of Egypt'). `[T]` *High.*

**What the frames say together.** The outermost frame is about **God** — his presence, name, blessing and exclusive claim. The next is about **release** — the seventh year and the slave going out free. The innermost motivated block is about **memory of Egypt**. So the shape is: *the God who is present and alone → the release he commands → the vulnerable he protects.* `[I]` *Moderate–high as a reading — synthetic, built from several verified frames; see Open Questions for the audit it needs before it is preached as the design of the whole.* **And do not press a centre:** there is no single centre-verse, and the Schnittjer caution applies — enclosure does not make the middle the point.

### 4. Linking Words

The code's logic lives in a handful of particles, and their pattern is the clearest structural signal after the frames.

- **כִּי / אִם ('when, if')** — the casuistic engine of 21:2–22:17. A main case is opened with כִּי (*"When you buy a Hebrew slave"*, 21:2; *"When men quarrel"*, 21:18; *"When an ox gores"*, 21:28) and sub-cases with אִם (*"If he comes alone… If he is the husband of a wife…"*, 21:3). `[T]` The particles mark the code's divisions more reliably than the English headings do.
- **אוֹ ('or') — and its weight.** *"He who strikes his father or his mother"* (21:15) is written with וְ ('and') in the Hebrew (אָבִיו וְאִמּוֹ), while the code uses אוֹ freely elsewhere (21:4, 6, 18, 20, 26, 28…). The house's earlier finding stands: the conjunction is disjunctive in sense, and the Gospels quote 21:17 with ἤ ('or'). `[S: overview / Gentry assessment, 11 Sep 2026]` *High.*
- **The motive כִּי ('for, because')** — the code's theology, attached to commands:
  - *"for if you wield your tool on it, you will profane it"* (20:25)
  - *"for he is his money"* (כִּי כַסְפּוֹ הוּא, 21:21) — the most uncomfortable motive in the code; see Difficult Verses
  - *"for you were sojourners in the land of Egypt"* (22:21; 23:9)
  - *"for if he does cry out to Me, I will surely hear"* (22:23)
  - *"for that is his only covering… for I am gracious"* (22:27)
  - *"for I will not justify the wicked"* (23:7)
  - *"for a bribe blinds the clear-sighted"* (23:8)
  - *"for in it you came out of Egypt"* (23:15)
  - *"for he will not pardon your transgression, for My name is in him"* (23:21)
  - *"for I will deliver the inhabitants of the land into your hand"* (23:31)
  - *"for if you serve their gods, it will surely be a snare to you"* (23:33)

  **→ The motives are God's history with Israel (Egypt), God's character (gracious; will not justify the wicked), and God's future with Israel (the land).** The casuistic core (21:2–22:17) carries almost none; the motive clauses cluster in the second half. `[T]` *High.*
- **לְמַעַן ('in order that')** — once only, at 23:12: *"so that your ox and your donkey may rest, and the son of your female slave, as well as your stranger, may refresh themselves."* The Sabbath's stated purpose here is **the rest of the dependent**, not the worship of the householder. `[T]` *High.*
- **פֶּן ('lest')** — twice, at the end: *"lest the land become desolate"* (23:29); *"lest they make you sin against Me"* (23:33). The epilogue's two dangers are wilderness reclaiming the land and the land's gods reclaiming Israel. `[T]`

### 5. Parallels

**Mostly N/A** — the code is prose case law, not poetry. Three prose parallelisms carry meaning:

- **The talion series, 21:23–25:** *"life for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot, burn for burn, wound for wound, bruise for bruise"* — eight pairs with תַּחַת ('in place of'). **The next two verses use the same preposition to free a slave:** *"he shall let him go free **on account of** (תַּחַת) his eye… his tooth"* (21:26–27). Eye and tooth, the first two body-parts of the talion, are exactly the two cases chosen for the slave. **The same formula that measures retribution between free men is converted, for the slave, into liberty.** `[T]` *High on the wording; moderate–high on the design.*
- **Antithetical pairs in the court law, 23:1–3, 6–8:** *"You shall not follow the masses in doing evil… nor shall you be partial to a poor man in his dispute"* (23:2–3) ↔ *"You shall not pervert the justice due to your needy… in his dispute"* (23:6). **בְּרִיבוֹ ('in his dispute') closes both 23:3 and 23:6** — favour neither the poor (23:3) nor deny the poor (23:6). Justice is the point, not sympathy. `[T]` *High.*
- **The enemy's animal, 23:4–5,** in two parallel cases (stray / fallen), each with an infinitive absolute (הָשֵׁב תְּשִׁיבֶנּוּ, 'you shall surely return it'; עָזֹב תַּעֲזֹב, 'you shall surely help'). `[T]`

### 6. Narrator's Comment

**N/A** — there is no narration inside 20:22–23:33. The narrator's only words are the speech-introduction at 20:22 (*"Then the LORD said to Moses"*). The nearest thing to a narrator's comment is God's own commentary on his law, in the motive clauses (Tool 4). The narrator's verdict on the code comes after it, in the people's response and the blood (24:3–8), which lies outside this extent.

### 7. Vocabulary

**Proper-noun inventory.** *Persons/peoples:* Moses (20:22); Israel (20:22; 23:17 in "Lord GOD"); the Egyptians by implication — **Egypt** (22:21; 23:9, 15); the Amorites, Hittites, Perizzites, Canaanites, Hivites, Jebusites (23:23; three of them again at 23:28). *Places:* the **Red Sea** (יַם־סוּף), the **Sea of the Philistines**, the **wilderness**, the **River** (23:31). *Times:* the month **Abib** (23:15). *Numbers:* six/seven (21:2; 23:10–12); three (21:11; 23:14, 17); thirty shekels (21:32); five and four (22:1); seven days/eighth day (22:30). **Triage:** Egypt carries the code's motive and is treated under Repetition and Tool 11. The six peoples are the standard list (Gen 15:19–21 and many places); the Greek adds the Girgashites to make seven (Tool 8). The borders of 23:31 restate the Abrahamic land (Gen 15:18, *"from the river of Egypt as far as the great river"*). Thirty shekels: Tool 11. *No other name carries freight here.*

**הַמִּשְׁפָּטִים ('the ordinances', 21:1)** `[T]` — the code's heading noun. מִשְׁפָּט stands four times in the code (21:1, 9, 31; 23:6), then names the whole at 24:3: *"all the words of the LORD and all the ordinances"*. In 23:6 it is the right a poor man has in court: *"the justice due to your needy"* (מִשְׁפַּט אֶבְיֹנְךָ). **The word runs from "the case law" to "a poor man's right"** — a law code that names its own subject matter with the word it uses for the poor man's due. *High confidence.*

**חָפְשִׁי ('free, released', 21:2, 5, 26, 27)** `[T]` — the only four occurrences in Exodus (WLC). In the canon the word runs to Deut 15:12–18 (the restated slave law), Jer 34:9–16 (the Jerusalem release revoked) and Isa 58:6 (*"let the oppressed go free"*). See Tool 11. *High.*

**עִבְרִי ('Hebrew', 21:2)** `[T]` — fourteen verses of Exodus have it, **all in 1:15–10:3 except this one**: the Hebrew midwives, Moses as *"one of the Hebrews' children"*, *"the LORD, the God of the Hebrews"* in Moses' demands to Pharaoh (3:18; 5:3; 7:16; 9:1, 13; 10:3). **21:2 is the word's last appearance in the book**, and it is the outsiders' word for Israel under Egypt. A law about a *Hebrew* slave carries the name Israel bore in the house of slavery. *High on the distribution; moderate–high on the reading.*

**עָנָה, piel ('afflict', 22:22–23 [Heb 22:21–22])** `[T]` — in Exodus only 1:11, 1:12, 10:3 and here (WLC). At 1:11 it is the taskmasters' purpose, *"to afflict them with hard labour"*; at 10:3 God asks Pharaoh, *"How long will you refuse to humble yourself?"* (the same verb in another stem-sense). **The next time anyone afflicts in Exodus, it is Israel, and the victims are widows and orphans.** *High.*

**לָחַץ ('oppress', 22:21; 23:9)** `[T]` — in Exodus only 3:9, 22:21 and 23:9, with the noun לַחַץ ('oppression') only at 3:9 (WLC). 3:9: *"I have seen the oppression with which the Egyptians are oppressing them."* *High.*

**גֵּר ('sojourner, stranger', 22:21; 23:9 ×3; 23:12)** `[T]` — and in Exodus also at 2:22 and 18:3, the naming of Gershom: *"I have been a sojourner in a foreign land"*. **Moses himself was a גֵּר.** The overview already carries this echo `[S: overview, Echo Table]`. *Moderate–high.*

**חַנּוּן ('gracious', 22:27 [Heb 22:26])** `[T]` — in Exodus only here and 34:6 (WLC). In the canon the adjective is almost always predicated of God; this is its **first occurrence in the Bible's order**, inside a statute about a poor man's coat. *High on the Exodus pair; the canonical "first" rests on Genesis having no instance, which the Exodus count makes plain.* `[S: overview]` found the Exodus pair first; **the agreement is not independent.**

**נָשָׂא + פֶּשַׁע ('bear/forgive' + 'transgression', 23:21)** `[T]` — together in Exodus only at 23:21 and 34:7 (WLC). The angel *"will not bear (לֹא יִשָּׂא) your transgression"*; the LORD at 34:7 is *"bearing (נֹשֵׂא) iniquity, transgression and sin"*. *High on the pair.* **New in this dig.**

**צדק ('righteous / justify') at 23:7–8** `[T]` — three times in two verses: *"do not kill the innocent or the **righteous** (צַדִּיק), for I will not **justify** (אַצְדִּיק) the wicked"*; *"a bribe… subverts the cause of the **just** (צַדִּיקִים)"*. The repeated root is the argument: a judge who calls the wicked righteous perverts what God himself refuses to do. *High.*

**שִׁלַּח ('let go, send away', piel)** `[T]` — the exodus verb (*"Let My people go"*, 5:1 and throughout 3–15). In the code it stands at 21:26, 27 (the injured slave sent out free), 22:5 [Heb 22:4] (a beast let loose to graze) and 23:27 (God *"will send"* his terror). *High on presence; moderate on reading 21:26–27 as Pharaoh's verb redeemed — but both English versions keep "let… go", and a congregation will hear it.*

**שַׁלֵּם ('make restitution', piel)** `[T]` — **every occurrence in Exodus falls in 21:34–22:15** (fourteen verses, WLC). The property section is the book's only vocabulary of restitution. *High.*

**מוֹקֵשׁ ('snare', 23:33)** `[T]` — in Exodus only 10:7, 23:33 and 34:12 (WLC). At 10:7 Pharaoh's servants say of Moses, *"How long will this man be a snare to us? Let the men go, that they may serve the LORD their God."* **מוֹקֵשׁ with עָבַד ('serve') stands in one verse only at Exod 10:7, 23:33, Deut 7:16 and Ps 106:36 (WLC).** In Egypt, refusing to let Israel serve the LORD was the snare; in the land, serving other gods will be. *High on words; moderate–high on the reversal as intended.* **New in this dig.**

**וַיִּנָּפֵשׁ / וְיִנָּפֵשׁ ('be refreshed', 23:12)** `[T]` — the niphal of נָפַשׁ, in Exodus only 23:12 and 31:17 (WLC). At 31:17 its subject is God: *"in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, but on the seventh day He ceased from labour, **and was refreshed**."* At 23:12 its subjects are **the son of your female slave and the sojourner**. The rest God took is the rest the slave-woman's son is to be given. *High on words; moderate–high on the reading.* **New in this dig.**

**אֵימָה ('terror', 23:27) and מַחֲלָה ('sickness', 23:25)** `[T]` — each in Exodus only here and in the Song/Marah block: אֵימָה at 15:16 (*"terror and dread fall upon them"*), מַחֲלָה at 15:26 (*"none of the diseases"*). Both promises from chapter 15 are re-issued in the epilogue — now **conditional on obedience** (23:22, 25). *High on words.* **New in this dig.**

**קֹדֶשׁ ('holiness', 22:31 [Heb 22:30])** `[T]` — *"You shall be holy men to Me"* (אַנְשֵׁי־קֹדֶשׁ). The root recalls 19:6, *"a holy nation"* (גּוֹי קָדוֹשׁ, a different word of the same root). The holy nation's holiness is made concrete as what it does not eat. *Moderate–high.*

### 8. Translations

**The NASB95 (study) against the ESV (pulpit), where it matters:**

- **20:23 — where the verse divides.** In Leningrad the main pause (*atnach*) falls on אִתִּי ('with Me'): *"You shall not make [anything] with Me | gods of silver and gods of gold you shall not make for yourselves."* **The NASB95 follows the accent** (*"You shall not make other gods besides Me; gods of silver or gods of gold…"*, supplying "other gods"). **The ESV divides across it**: *"You shall not make gods of silver to be with me, nor shall you make for yourselves gods of gold."* Both are defensible; the ESV pairs silver with "with me" and makes two parallel prohibitions, the accent makes one general prohibition followed by its specification. `[T]` for the accent in Leningrad; `[unchecked — apparatus spread]` for accentuation as a tradition. **What the congregation hears:** two prohibitions about metal idols, not a ban on anything "with Me".
- **20:24 / 23:13 — the frame the English breaks.** Both versions render the causative זָכַר as "cause… to be remembered" at 20:24 and as "mention" at 23:13. Nothing is wrong with either rendering; but **no English hearer will connect them.** *A pulpit note, not a translation verdict.*
- **21:20–21.** נָקֹם יִנָּקֵם — ESV *"he shall be avenged"* (closer: the verb is נָקַם, 'avenge'); NASB95 *"he shall be punished"*. כִּי כַסְפּוֹ הוּא — ESV *"for the slave is his money"* (closer: כֶּסֶף is 'silver, money'); NASB95 *"for he is his property"*. **The ESV is the more literal at both points here, and the harder to hear.**
- **21:22–23.** ESV *"so that her children come out"* (literal: וְיָצְאוּ יְלָדֶיהָ); NASB95 *"so that she gives birth prematurely"* (interpretive). ESV *"if there is harm"*; NASB95 *"if there is any **further** injury"* — "further" is supplied, and it decides the very question the verse leaves open (whether the "harm" is additional to the premature birth). **The ESV leaves the crux open; the NASB95 has closed it.** See Difficult Verses.
- **22:8–9 [Heb 22:7–8] — God or judges?** הָאֱלֹהִים: ESV *"come near to **God**… before **God**… the one whom **God** condemns"*; NASB95 *"before the **judges**"* three times. At 22:9 the verb is plural — יַרְשִׁיעֻן ('they condemn') with אֱלֹהִים as subject. A plural verb with אֱלֹהִים is the NASB95's best support; but the same construction refers to God elsewhere (Gen 20:13; 35:7), and the ESV reading has 21:6 (*"to God"* in both versions) and the oath *"before the LORD"* at 22:11 on its side. The BHS apparatus records the Samaritan with a singular verb at 22:9 — a scribe treating the subject as God. `[T]` for the Hebrew; `[S: BHS apparatus, read from the export]` for the Samaritan. *Moderate.* **What the congregation hears:** the case brought before God. Say that the word can bear both, and that the code means God's judgement mediated through Israel's judges.
- **22:22–23 — the Egypt verb.** NASB95 *"You shall not **afflict** any widow or orphan. If you **afflict** him at all…"*; ESV *"You shall not **mistreat**… If you do **mistreat** them…"*. At 1:11 both versions have "afflict". **The NASB95 keeps the link to 1:11–12; the ESV breaks it.** *A pulpit note with weight: Headline 3 rests on this verb.*
- **23:6.** NASB95 *"your needy **brother**"* — "brother" supplied; the Hebrew is אֶבְיֹנְךָ ('your needy one'). ESV *"your poor"* — closer.
- **23:7.** *"I will not **acquit** the wicked"* in both. The Hebrew is the hiphil of צדק, 'justify, declare righteous'. **Both versions level the repeated root** (Tool 7). And the Greek changes the speaker — see below.
- **23:9.** *"You know the **heart**"* (ESV) / *"the **feelings**"* (NASB95) — the Hebrew is נֶפֶשׁ ('life, soul, self'). Neither is wrong; KJV *"the heart"*. You know a sojourner from the inside.
- **23:20 — angel or messenger?** Both have *"an angel"*. ESV Matt 11:10 has *"Behold, I send my **messenger** before your **face**"*. **An English hearer will not connect the two.** מַלְאָךְ and ἄγγελος mean both.
- **23:21.** Both *"he will not **pardon** your transgression"*; at 34:7 both have *"**forgiving** iniquity, transgression and sin"*. The Hebrew verb is the same (נָשָׂא). *Pulpit note.*
- **23:23.** ESV subordinates the verse (*"When my angel goes before you… and I blot them out, you shall not bow down"*); the NASB95 keeps it independent (*"For My angel will go…"*), following כִּי. Minor.

**Ancient versions check.** The moderns agree on most renderings that carry weight; the Rahlfs Greek diverges at several. Each is classified under the three-way triage.

### 9. Tone and Feel

**The dominant register is legal and level** — the "if a man… then" of the casuistic core, unhurried, specific to the point of the ox's owner having been warned the day before yesterday (21:29). The detail is the tone: this God cares where the ox was kept.

**Then the temperature changes at 22:21.** The cases become persons who cannot defend themselves, the grammar becomes second person, and **God speaks with heat**: *"if he does cry out to Me, I will surely hear his cry; and My anger will be kindled, and I will kill you with the sword, and your wives shall become widows and your children fatherless"* (22:23–24). Then, at once, tenderness: *"for that is his only covering; it is his cloak for his body. What else shall he sleep in?"* (22:27). **A question, inside a statute, from God.** `[T]` *High.* The emotional centre of the code is not the altar or the talion; it is a cold man without his coat.

**The epilogue's register is promise and warning together** — *"My terror… hornets… little by little… a snare"* — a God who will fight for Israel and will not overlook what Israel serves.

**Soundtrack.** A magistrate's reading of the statutes, interrupted twice by the voice of the Judge himself: once in anger, once in compassion. **Do not preach the code in one even tone.** The text does not.

### 11. Quotation/Allusion

**Book-level source inventory.** Genesis is the only earlier Scripture available. The overview's live sources: Genesis (re-cited); the Abrahamic covenant; creation. **Earlier in Exodus, the internal sources the code re-voices** are the bush (3:7–9), the Song and Marah (15:16, 26), the covenant proposal (19:4–6) and the Sabbath (16; 20:8–11).

**Independent candidate-finding.** From the passage's own distinctive wording, searched by lemma across the Hebrew Bible: *messenger + send + way + face* (23:20) → Gen 24:40; Mal 3:1. *Snare + serve* (23:33) → Exod 10:7; Deut 7:16; Ps 106:36. *Snare + gods* → Deut 7:16; Josh 23:13; Judg 2:3. *Free* (21:2) → Deut 15:12–18; Jer 34:9–16; Isa 58:6. *Thirty + silver* (21:32) → Zech 11:12–13 (with the Numbers dedication lists, irrelevant). *Justify + wicked* in the Greek (23:7) → Isa 5:23; Prov 17:15 (Hebrew); Sir 42:2; Rom 4:5. *You have seen* (20:22) → Exod 19:4; Deut 29:1 [Heb 29:1]; Jer 44:2; Josh 23:3.

**Gen 24:7, 40 → Exod 23:20** *(moderate — the formula's first instance)* `[T]` for both texts

*Source context.* Abraham sends his servant to find a wife for Isaac and swears him not to take Isaac back to Mesopotamia: *"The LORD… who swore to me, 'To your descendants I will give this land,' He will send His angel before you"* (24:7); *"He will send His angel with you to make your journey successful"* (24:40). The angel goes before a man bound for the promised line, under the land oath.

*Book usage.* Genesis is Exodus's live source. The angel-before-you idea also appears at 14:19 (the angel of God who went before the camp) and 32:34; 33:2.

*OT-to-OT.* Genesis 24 is about bringing a bride to the land of promise; Exodus 23:20 about bringing a people into it. Both stand under the same oath.

*What it adds.* The promise of 23:20 is not new: it is the Abrahamic oath's own provision, now for a nation. *Moderate.*

---

**Exod 23:20 → Mal 3:1 → Matt 11:10; Mark 1:2; Luke 7:27** *(high — Gospel citation verified in the Greek; moderate–high for Malachi's dependence)* `[T]`

*Source context (Move 1).* 23:20 opens the epilogue: God sends his messenger ahead *"to guard you along the way and to bring you into the place which I have prepared"*. Then: *"Be on your guard before him and obey his voice; do not be rebellious toward him, for he will not pardon your transgression, since My name is in him"* (23:21). The messenger carries the Name — the Name the code began with (20:24) — and therefore carries God's authority to judge. The promise is conditional (23:22, *"if you truly obey his voice"*). Its function is the code's closing assurance: **the God who gave these terms will go ahead of the people who keep them.**

*Book usage (Move 2).* The angel appeared at 3:2 (the bush) and 14:19 (the pillar). After the calf, the same provision becomes a sentence: *"My angel shall go before you… but I will not go up in your midst"* (32:34; 33:2–3) `[S: overview, Echo Table]`. **The promise of 23:20 is the thing Moses refuses at 33:15** — because by then it means the angel *instead of* the presence.

*OT-to-OT (Move 3).* **Malachi rewrites this verse.** *"Behold, I am going to send My messenger, and he will clear the way before Me. And the Lord (הָאָדוֹן), whom you seek, will suddenly come to His temple; and the messenger of the covenant (מַלְאַךְ הַבְּרִית)"* (Mal 3:1). Messenger + send + way + face stand in one verse only at Gen 24:40, Exod 23:20 and Mal 3:1 (WLC); הָאָדוֹן with the article ('the Lord') stands at Exod 23:17, three verses earlier, and at Mal 3:1. Malachi turns *"before **you**"* into *"before **Me**"*: the messenger now prepares for God's own coming. **And the "messenger of the covenant" belongs to a book whose whole complaint is covenant-breaking (Mal 2:4–10).** `[T]` for the shared words; `[I]` for dependence. *Moderate–high — the only three-verse set is suggestive but not conclusive; unaudited.*

*The Gospels (category 3).* The Greek of record for 23:20 reads **Καὶ ἰδοὺ ἐγὼ ἀποστέλλω τὸν ἄγγελόν μου πρὸ προσώπου σου, ἵνα φυλάξῃ σε ἐν τῇ ὁδῷ** (Rahlfs). Malachi's Greek reads **Ἰδοὺ ἐξαποστέλλω τὸν ἄγγελόν μου, καὶ ἐπιβλέψεται ὁδὸν πρὸ προσώπου μου** (Swete). Jesus, of John the Baptist: **Ἰδοὺ ἐγὼ ἀποστέλλω τὸν ἄγγελόν μου πρὸ προσώπου σου, ὃς κατασκευάσει τὴν ὁδόν σου ἔμπροσθέν σου** (Matt 11:10, SBLGNT). **The first half is Exodus word for word — ἐγώ, the simple ἀποστέλλω, and πρὸ προσώπου *σου*, 'before *your* face' — and none of those three is in Malachi's Greek.** Only the second half (*"who will prepare your way"*) is Malachi's. Mark 1:2 and Luke 7:27 have the same conflation without ἐγώ (SBLGNT; Mark attributes the whole to Isaiah, adding Isa 40:3). `[T]` — read directly from the three Greek texts. *High.*

*What it adds.* In the Gospels **the messenger is John, the Lord who comes is Jesus — and the "you" before whose face the messenger goes is also Jesus**, because Malachi's "before Me" has been put back into Exodus's "before you". Jesus stands where Israel stood at 23:20: the one brought through the wilderness into the place God has prepared. The Gospels do **not** identify Jesus with the angel of 23:20 — they identify John with him. **This is a sharper and safer Christological use of 23:20 than the angel-as-Christ reading, and it is the New Testament's own.** The overview's caution (do not preach the angel as a straightforward Christophany) stands; this citation was missing from both the August dig and the overview's forward-reception list.

**Exod 21:24 → Matt 5:38** *(high — direct quotation)* `[T]`

*Source context.* The talion (21:23–25) governs injury in a quarrel and is followed at once by its conversion into liberty for the slave (21:26–27). It is a **limit**: no more than an eye for an eye, set before judges (21:22, בִּפְלִלִים, 'as the judges decide'). The formula recurs at Lev 24:20 and Deut 19:21, in both cases inside a court.

*Book usage.* First and only Exodus use.

*OT-to-OT.* Deuteronomy uses it against a false witness (Deut 19:16–21) — the same court setting.

*What it adds.* Matt 5:38 quotes it in the Greek of all three Torah texts (ὀφθαλμὸν ἀντὶ ὀφθαλμοῦ καὶ ὀδόντα ἀντὶ ὀδόντος). Jesus addresses its use **as a personal ethic** ("do not resist the one who is evil"), not its use by judges. **Exodus 21 itself already bends the formula toward mercy** — the slave goes free for the eye. *High.*

**Exod 21:17 → Matt 15:4; Mark 7:10** *(high — direct quotation)* `[T]`

The Gospels quote *"He who curses father or mother, let him surely die"* (Ὁ κακολογῶν πατέρα ἢ μητέρα θανάτῳ τελευτάτω, SBLGNT). The Greek of Exodus at this point (Rahlfs 21:16 — the Greek has moved the verse) reads τελευτήσει θανάτῳ; θανάτῳ τελευτάτω is the form of its next verse. The Gospels' wording is close to, not identical with, the Greek of record. Jesus uses the law against a tradition that let a son dedicate to God what he owed his parents. `[T]` *High on the quotation; the wording detail moderate.*

**Exod 22:28 → Acts 23:5** *(high — direct quotation, category 3)* `[T]`

*"You shall not speak evil of a ruler of your people"* — Ἄρχοντα τοῦ λαοῦ σου οὐκ ἐρεῖς κακῶς (Acts 23:5) is **the Greek of 22:28b [Heb 22:27b] word for word**. Paul, having called the high priest a *"whitewashed wall"*, applies the code to himself. **The apostle treats the Book of the Covenant as binding on his own speech.** *High.*

**Exod 23:7 (Greek) → Rom 4:5** *(moderate — verbal, contested as an allusion)* `[T]` for the wording; `[I]` for the allusion

*Source context.* In the Hebrew, God says of himself, *"I will not justify the wicked"* (לֹא־אַצְדִּיק רָשָׁע), as the ground for a judge's integrity. In the Greek of record, the judge is commanded: *"you shall not justify the ungodly for the sake of bribes"* (οὐ δικαιώσεις τὸν ἀσεβῆ ἕνεκεν δώρων).

*The phrase across the Greek canon.* δικαιόω with ἀσεβής as its object: Exod 23:7; Isa 5:23 (*"who justify the ungodly for a bribe"*, condemned); Sir 42:2 (Swete); and Paul, *"God, who justifies the ungodly"* (τὸν δικαιοῦντα τὸν ἀσεβῆ, Rom 4:5). The Hebrew equivalent stands at Prov 17:15 and Isa 5:23 as an abomination.

*What it adds.* **Paul names God by the act the Torah forbids.** Whether Paul has 23:7 in particular in mind cannot be shown — Isa 5:23 is as close, and the phrase may be a commonplace. But the juxtaposition is the gospel's own problem, stated by Paul himself at Rom 3:26 (*"that He might be just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus"*). The God of 23:7 does not justify the wicked *for a bribe* — and Romans says he justifies them on the ground of a propitiation. *Moderate as allusion; high as the theological crux the code plants.* See Christological Reading.

**Exod 21:32 → Zech 11:12–13 → Matt 26:15; 27:9** *(moderate — mediated)* `[S: Matthew queue N1, Logos 3 Oct]`

Thirty shekels of silver is the price paid to a master for a slave gored to death. Zechariah's shepherd is valued at thirty pieces of silver (Zech 11:12–13); Matthew's wording is Zechariah's, and Exodus stands behind Zechariah. The house's Matthew work rated this **moderate** and found three commentators for it. *Moderate.* **Preach it, if at all, through Zechariah.**

**Moves 3 and 4 for the release law: Exod 21:2 → Deut 15:12–18 → Jer 34:8–22 → Isa 58:6 → Luke 4:18** *(high for each link's wording; moderate for the line as a whole)* `[T]`

- **Deut 15:12–18** re-issues the slave law with *"you shall remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt, and the LORD your God redeemed you"* (15:15) and חָפְשִׁי at 15:12, 13, 18.
- **Jer 34:8–22.** Zedekiah's Jerusalem makes a covenant to release its Hebrew slaves, then takes them back. God's indictment quotes the law: *"I made a covenant with your forefathers in the day that I brought them out of the land of Egypt, from the house of bondage, saying, 'At the end of seven years… he shall serve you six years, and you shall send him out free (חָפְשִׁי)'"* (Jer 34:13–14). **The slave law — the first law of this code — is the specific covenant term on whose breach Jeremiah hangs the city's fall.** `[T]` *High.*
- **Isa 58:6:** *"to let the oppressed go free (וְשַׁלַּח… חָפְשִׁים)"* — both of the code's release words, שִׁלַּח and חָפְשִׁי, in one clause.
- **Luke 4:18** quotes Isa 58:6 in its Greek — ἀποστεῖλαι τεθραυσμένους ἐν ἀφέσει, *"to set free those who are oppressed"* (SBLGNT = LXX Isa 58:6, ἀπόστελλε τεθραυσμένους ἐν ἀφέσει) — in Jesus' first sermon.

*What it adds.* **The release that opens the Book of the Covenant runs, by its own vocabulary, through the prophet who indicts its breach and the prophet who reframes it as true fasting, into the Nazareth sermon.** Each link is verified wording; the line as a line is synthetic. *Moderate as a line, high at each step.*

**Exod 23:20–33 → Judg 2:1–3** *(high — the canonical outworking)* `[T]`

The angel of the LORD comes up from Gilgal and says, *"I brought you up out of Egypt and led you into the land which I have sworn to your fathers… and you shall make no covenant with the inhabitants of this land… But you have not obeyed Me… Therefore I also said, 'I will not drive them out before you; but they will become as thorns in your sides and their gods will be a snare (מוֹקֵשׁ) to you'"* (Judg 2:1–3). Snare + gods in one verse: Exod 23:33; Deut 7:16; Josh 23:13; Judg 2:3 (WLC; with Exod 10:7 for snare + serve). **The epilogue's angel, its "no covenant", its "drive out", and its "snare" all return together at the turn into Judges** — the warning of 23:33 come true. *High.*

### 12. Genre

**Ancient Near Eastern law — covenant stipulations inside narrative.** Three forms are mixed on the page: *casuistic* ("if a man…"; 21:2–22:17), *apodictic* ("you shall not…"; 20:23; 22:18–23:19) and *paraenetic* address with promises (20:22–26; 23:20–33). `[T]` for the forms. **Reading rules:** (1) the cases are **examples**, not an exhaustive code — no judge could decide every dispute in Israel from 21–22, and the code does not claim to; it teaches by paradigm; (2) the law is **covenant law** — given by the God who brought them out, to be sworn to, sealed in blood, and kept in his presence; (3) it is **pre-Christ** — Tool 14 governs how a Christian hears it. The comparison with Hammurabi and the Hittite laws is real and the overview's qualified verdict stands `[S: overview research]`: the covenant framing and the absence of class-graded penalties for free persons hold; "no capital property crimes" needs qualification.

### 13. Copycat

**Law is prescriptive by genre — but prescriptive *to whom*, and in what form, is the question.** Classify by kind, not by modern category:

| Kind | Example | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct command re-affirmed in the NT** | No other gods (20:23; 23:13, 24); do not revile a ruler (22:28 → Acts 23:5); honour parents (21:15, 17 → Matt 15:4) | Binding, with the NT's own handling |
| **Command whose principle the NT re-applies** | Protect the sojourner, widow, orphan (22:21–24 → Jas 1:27); pay the labourer; no partiality in judgement (23:3, 6 → Jas 2:1–9); help an enemy (23:4–5 → Matt 5:44; Rom 12:20) | The principle binds; the case-form is Israel's |
| **Civil penalty in Israel's courts** | Death for murder, kidnap, cursing parents, bestiality (21:12–17; 22:19); restitution scales (22:1–15) | Israel's judicial law; teaches justice and proportion; not a template for a modern church's discipline or a state's code without argument |
| **Cultic law fulfilled in Christ** | The altar (20:24–26); firstborn and first fruits (22:29–30); festivals (23:14–19) | Read through Christ and the cross |
| **Promise conditional on Israel's obedience in the land** | Health, fertility, long life, borders (23:25–31) | Not a promise to an individual Christian; see Pitfalls |

**The code does not sort itself.** It interleaves altar, slaves, oxen, sorcery, widows, courts and calendar without a seam, and the motive clauses run across all of them. Any division into moral, civil and ceremonial is a theological judgement brought to the text — perhaps a sound one, but **not one the text makes**. `[T]` for the interleaving; the judgement is the preacher's. *(The folder holds Patrick's own work on tripartite law; this dig takes no position on it.)*

### 14. Bible Timeline

**Where the passage is.** At Sinai, three months after Egypt (19:1), between the Decalogue and the ratification. Israel has been redeemed but has not yet sworn to the terms, and has not yet broken them.

**Canonical position.** Torah. The code is read by the Torah's own audience — the camp with the tabernacle in it — and reread by the prophets (Jeremiah 34; Isaiah 58; Malachi 3) as the standard Israel was judged by.

**Where I am.** After the covenant blood has been shed again — *"This is My blood of the covenant"* (Matt 26:28, taking Exod 24:8's own words) — and after the ascension and Pentecost. **The covenant of which this code is the terms has been called "obsolete" in its form** (Heb 8:13) and its righteous requirement **fulfilled in those who walk by the Spirit** (Rom 8:4). The code still teaches what God values and how he judges; it no longer constitutes the Christian's covenant, and it was never the way into it.

**What has happened between.** The conquest the epilogue promised happened *"little by little"* and stalled (Judg 1–2); the release law was broken (Jer 34); the slave's freedom became the Servant's ministry (Isa 58:6; 61:1 → Luke 4:18); the messenger who goes before came, and the Lord came to his temple (Mal 3:1 → Matt 11:10).

### 16. So What?

**Author's intended response.** Israel is to hear the terms, swear to them with understanding (24:3, 7), and live them in the land as people who remember Egypt — worshipping the LORD alone, releasing, protecting, judging justly, resting and letting others rest, and refusing the gods of the land.

**Worldview.** (1) **God is present in the ordinary.** He legislates for oxen, pits, loans, coats and bribes, because the covenant people's common life is where his name is remembered. (2) **God takes sides with the weak.** He heard Israel's cry against Egypt; he will hear the widow's cry against Israel. (3) **God is gracious and will not justify the wicked** — and both are said inside the code. (4) **Whom you serve is the whole question.** The code begins and ends with it.

**Behaviour — stop / start, with the code's own motives.**

- *Stop* treating the vulnerable as leverage: interest from the poor, a coat kept overnight, a bribe, a vote with the majority (22:25–27; 23:1–8). **Motive:** *"you were sojourners"*; *"I will hear… for I am gracious."*
- *Start* doing good to your enemy where it costs you (23:4–5). **Motive:** the God who rescued his enemies' victims, and in Christ his enemies.
- *Start* giving rest — not only taking it (23:12). **Motive:** God's own seventh-day refreshment (31:17).
- *Stop* mentioning other gods' names as though they had a claim (23:13). **Motive:** the God who causes his name to be remembered, and comes and blesses (20:24).

**Four audiences.**

- *For me:* where am I the creditor, the judge, the employer, the one with the coat in my hand?
- *For a Christian friend:* the one who thinks the Old Testament law is a list of harsh rules needs 22:27 — God asking, *"What else shall he sleep in?"*
- *For the church community:* the "you" is plural. A church that sings about its release and keeps its widows waiting has not heard 22:21–24.
- *For an unbeliever:* the God who will not justify the wicked (23:7) is the God the gospel says justifies the ungodly (Rom 4:5). That is either a contradiction or a cross.

**Prayer.** Lord God, you heard our cry and brought us out. Make us a people who hear the cry of others; who remember your name and no other; who rest and give rest. And since you will not justify the wicked, we thank you for the one who was made sin for us, so that in him we might become your righteousness.

### Textual Variants

**21:8 — ketiv / qere.** The written text has לא ('not'); the Masoretes read לוֹ ('for himself'). Ketiv: *"who has **not** designated her"*; qere: *"who has designated her **for himself**"*. The Greek, Targum and Vulgate follow the qere (BHS). **Both English versions follow the qere.** `[unchecked — apparatus spread]` — the ketiv/qere is a reading-tradition feature. *Moderate.* **Preaching implication:** none unless asked.

**22:5 [Heb 22:4] — ketiv and a long plus.** The written בעירה is read as בְּעִירוֹ ('his beast'). The Samaritan and, in substance, the Greek add a further clause about a field grazed entirely (BHS). The English follows the Hebrew. *No preaching implication.*

**22:27 [Heb 22:26] — ketiv.** כסותה ('her covering') written; כְּסוּתוֹ ('his covering') read. *No preaching implication.*

**21:15–17 — the order of the capital laws.** The Greek of record runs strike-parent, curse-parent, kidnap, and limits the kidnap law to *"one of the sons of Israel"* (cf. Deut 24:7). **Most likely original:** the Hebrew order — the Greek groups like with like, which is the easier reading. *Moderate.* **Preaching implication:** none; but it shows an early reader felt the Hebrew's interleaving needed tidying — which is itself a datum about the code's shape.

**21:22–23 — the formed / unformed child.** A materially different law in the Greek. Worked in the overview (three positions on אָסוֹן; do not settle from the pulpit). `[S: overview]`

**23:7 — who speaks.** Hebrew: God, *"I will not justify the wicked"*. Greek: the judge, *"you shall not justify the ungodly for the sake of bribes"*. BHS records the Greek's second person and its plus. **Most likely original:** the Hebrew — the Greek's plus reads like an explanation drawn from 23:8. *Moderate–high.* **Preaching implication:** preach the Hebrew; mention the Greek only if you take up Rom 4:5.

**23:22 — the long plus.** The Greek repeats 19:5–6 almost in full: *"…and keep My covenant, you shall be to Me a people for possession from all the nations, for all the earth is Mine; and you shall be to Me a royal priesthood and a holy nation. These words you shall speak to the sons of Israel: If you truly obey…"* (Rahlfs; Swete identical in substance). **The BHS apparatus as exported records only the Samaritan's and the Greek's plural forms at this verse and does not register the plus** — so whether the Samaritan shares it was not checked here. **Most likely original:** the shorter Hebrew; the plus is a harmonising expansion. *Moderate.* **Preaching implication:** none directly — but **the Greek translator saw what Move 4 sees**: the code ends where the covenant proposal began (19:4–6 ↔ 20:22; 23:22). And the Greek of 19:6 / 23:22 is the text 1 Pet 2:9 quotes (βασίλειον ἱεράτευμα, ἔθνος ἅγιον).

**23:3 — "poor" or "great"?** BHS suggests דָּל ('poor') may be a slip for גָּדֹל ('great'), comparing Lev 19:15. No witness is cited; it is a conjecture. **Both English versions keep "poor".** *Keep the text: "you shall not be partial to a poor man" is the harder and more striking reading, and 23:6 balances it.*

### Historical and Cultural Background

**Setting.** The text sets the speech at Sinai in the third month after the exodus (19:1). Its laws presuppose settled agricultural life — fields, vineyards, olive groves, threshing-floors, houses that can be broken into, and courts — that Israel does not yet have. `[T]` That mismatch is part of the speech's own design (*"when you come into the land"*), not evidence against it. Compositional history is `[S]` and not hung on here.

**What the original audience knew.**

- **Debt-slavery.** In the ancient Near East a man could sell himself or his children to pay a debt; codes regulated the term. The Hebrew slave law limits servitude to six years. `[S]` *High as background.*
- **The daughter sold as a concubine-bride (21:7–11)** was a recognised arrangement; the law protects her — food, clothing and conjugal rights, or she goes free. `[S]` *High.*
- **The ox that gores** is a stock case in Mesopotamian law (Eshnunna, Hammurabi), with the same distinction between a first-time goring and a known gorer. `[S: overview research]` *High.* The Israelite twist is that the ox is stoned and its flesh not eaten — the animal that kills a human being is treated as guilty of blood (cf. Gen 9:5).
- **The pledged cloak.** The outer garment doubled as a blanket; to keep it overnight was to leave a poor man cold. An ostracon from Yavneh-Yam (seventh century BC) records a labourer's appeal for a confiscated garment. `[S]` *Moderate–high; flag for verification before citing.*
- **Three pilgrimage feasts** tied to barley harvest (Abib/Unleavened Bread), wheat harvest (Harvest/Weeks) and ingathering (autumn). `[S]` *High.*
- **"Do not boil a kid in its mother's milk"** has been linked to a Ugaritic ritual text; **that reading of the Ugaritic text is now widely doubted**. Do not preach it as a known Canaanite rite. `[S]` *Uncertain — verify before use.*
- **The tabernacle in the camp.** For the Torah's audience, the altar law (20:24–26) and the *"house of the LORD"* (23:19) are read with a sanctuary in view. See Original Audience.

**What this changes.** The code's protections are not generic charity; they are specific interventions in known mechanisms of ruin — debt, pledge, sale of children, bribery of courts. **The God of the code knows how the poor are crushed, mechanism by mechanism.**

### Original Audience Reception

**Canonical audience.** Torah: the reader of the founding story, with the tabernacle in the camp. **What they know:** Genesis; the oath to Abraham; Egypt, from the inside. **Canonical register:** Genesis and the rest of the Torah are in the ear.

**The first hearers.** Ex-slaves, three months out. Every one of them knew what it was to be afflicted, oppressed, and to cry out. **The code is spoken to people who remember the whip.**

**Where the passage fits their world.**

- The first case — *"when you buy a Hebrew slave"* — would land with a jolt. They have just stopped being slaves; the first law imagines one of them owning another. **And it limits that ownership to six years, before it says anything about oxen or theft.**
- *"For you know the life (נֶפֶשׁ) of a sojourner"* (23:9): they did not need to be told what that meant.
- The festivals are tied to the harvest of a land they have never sown. **The code is spoken in the future tense of a promise.**

**Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances.**

- **Shocking:** *"I will kill you with the sword, and your wives shall become widows"* (22:24) — God threatening Israel with what Pharaoh did.
- **Surprising:** the enemy's ox (23:4–5); the slave freed for a tooth (21:27); God asking where the poor man will sleep (22:27).
- **Comforting:** God hears the cry — as he heard theirs.
- **Disturbing:** a slave beaten to death is avenged, but one who lingers a day or two is not, *"for he is his money"* (21:20–21).

**What we bring that they did not.** A sharp line between "religious" and "civil" law; individual rights rather than covenant obligation; a horror of slavery that is right but anachronistic in its categories (ANE debt-servitude is not the Atlantic slave trade, though 21:16 forbids the kidnapping that trade required).

### Biblical-Theological Themes

**1. Covenant — the terms of the Sinai covenant.**
- *Where it sits:* the stipulations of the Mosaic covenant, between proposal (19:5) and ratification (24:8).
- *Before:* the Abrahamic oath (Gen 15; 24:7 — the angel and the land); *"I will take you for My people"* (6:7).
- *After:* the covenant re-issued after the calf (34:10–28, with 23:14–19 repeated); broken in the land (Judg 2:1–3; Jer 34:13–18); the new covenant promised (Jer 31:31–34, *"not like the covenant which I made with their fathers in the day I took them by the hand to bring them out of the land of Egypt"*); the blood of the covenant on Jesus' lips (Matt 26:28).
- *Contribution:* the code shows what covenant faithfulness looks like on the ground; its history shows why a new covenant was needed.
- *Confidence:* high.

**2. Release / exodus as institution.**
- *Where it sits:* the exodus made into law — six years and the seventh (21:2), the seventh year and day (23:10–12).
- *Before:* *"Let My people go"*; the house of slavery (20:2).
- *After:* sabbatical release and jubilee (Deut 15; Lev 25); its breach (Jer 34); its prophetic reframing (Isa 58:6; 61:1–2); its fulfilment announced at Nazareth (Luke 4:18–19).
- *Contribution:* the code's first law is a gospel-shaped law: liberty.
- *Confidence:* high at each step; moderate as a single line.

**3. God's presence — the Name in a place.**
- *Where it sits:* *"In every place where I cause My name to be remembered I will come to you"* (20:24); *"My name is in him"* (23:21).
- *Before:* the Name given at the bush (3:14–15); *"Is the LORD among us?"* (17:7).
- *After:* the tabernacle (25:8); the place the LORD chooses for his name (Deut 12:5); the temple; the Word who tabernacled (John 1:14); *"where two or three are gathered in My name"* (Matt 18:20).
- *Contribution:* the code is framed by presence, not merely by law.
- *Confidence:* high.

**Confluence.** *(c) Climactic convergence.* The three meet in one reality: **the God who is present (theme 3) releases (theme 2) and binds the released to himself by covenant (theme 1).** In Christ the three converge again — the presence in flesh, the release announced at Nazareth, and the covenant in his blood.

### Schnittjer Pass

**Skeleton:** Exodus · at the mountain (19–40) · progressive proximity of God — the covenant terms that make cohabitation with a holy God possible, between the voice from heaven and the blood.

**Findings.**
- *(Step 3, framing)* Nested frames, not a chiasm: Name/gods (20:23–24 ↔ 23:13, 24–33); serving (21:2, 6 ↔ 23:24–25, 33); the seventh (21:2 ↔ 23:10–12); release (21:2, 5 ↔ 21:26–27); Egypt (22:21 ↔ 23:9). `[T]` *High on each; moderate–high as a design.*
- *(Step 4, leading word)* No single *Leitwort*; a **cluster** — the exodus verbs (afflict, oppress, cry, hear, let go, go out, serve) redeployed. `[T]` *High.*
- *(Step 1, selectivity and time)* The narrator brakes completely: 105 verses of speech with no narrative. **The braking is the weight** — the story stops so that the terms can be heard in full. `[T]`
- *(Step 2, narrative space)* "Up" and "in": God speaks *"from heaven"* (20:22) and the altar must have no *"steps"* (20:26); the epilogue moves the people *"in"* to the place prepared (23:20, 23). `[T]` *Moderate.*
- *(Step 5, first vs second reading)* On first reading 20:23 is a general prohibition; on re-reading after chapter 32 it is the exact charge. The angel is a promise on first reading and a sentence on the second. `[T]` *High.*
- *(Step 6, intertextuality)* Progressive imaging *backwards*: the code is narrated in the sound of the bush and the Song; *forwards*, Malachi and the Gospels hear 23:20 polyacoustically — messenger, way, face, Lord, covenant — not as a flat "angel = Jesus". `[T]`/`[I]`

**Echoes/signals:** leading-word cluster (exodus verbs); framing device (nested); intertextual links Gen 24:7; Mal 3:1; Judg 2:1–3.

**Nil returns:** type-scene — none; chiasm — none found (and the centre would not be "the point" if there were).

**Step 7 (irreducibility).** Do not reduce the code to principles. Its force is in its particularity: *this* ox, *this* coat, *this* night.

### Christological Reading

**Type of connection.** Trajectory (release; presence; covenant); contrast (the covenant broken, the covenant kept); NT citation (23:20 → Matt 11:10 and parallels; 21:24 → Matt 5:38; 21:17 → Matt 15:4; 22:28 → Acts 23:5); and one crux the code plants (23:7 against Rom 4:5).

**How the passage points to Christ.**

- **The covenant whose terms these are is sealed in blood (24:8), and Jesus takes that sentence onto his own lips with one pronoun added** (*"My blood of the covenant"*). `[S: overview, verified in v1.3.0]` **The code is what that blood ratified.** Preach the code, and the Supper is the place it lands. *High.*
- **The messenger and the way (23:20 → Mal 3:1 → Matt 11:10; Mark 1:2).** The Gospels put Jesus in Israel's place at the edge of the promised land, with a messenger sent ahead. **Typology tests:** category (exodus/wilderness — pass); NT precedent (explicit citation — pass); escalation (the place prepared becomes the kingdom; Malachi's Lord comes to his temple — pass); authorial pattern (the angel-before-you motif recurs in Exodus — pass). *High as NT-authorised reading; do not extend it to "the angel is Christ".*
- **Release (21:2 → Luke 4:18).** *Trajectory.* The code's first word for the Hebrew slave becomes Isaiah's and then Jesus' first sermon. *Moderate as a line; high at each verified step.*
- **The crux of 23:7 and 34:7.** *Contrast / trajectory.* God *"will not justify the wicked"* (23:7) and *"will by no means leave the guilty unpunished"* (34:7), yet is *"gracious"* (22:27; 34:6). The code states both; Rom 3:25–26 answers how both can stand: *"that He might be just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus."* *High as the theological shape; moderate on Rom 4:5 as an allusion to 23:7.*
- **The slave freed on account of his eye (21:26–27).** *Illustration, not type.* The talion's formula turned into liberty is Christ-shaped — but no NT text uses it. Label it.

**Trajectory.** *What the passage longs for:* a people who keep the terms; a release that is not revoked (Jer 34); a presence that goes with them without consuming them (33:3). *What Christ fulfils:* the covenant-keeper whose blood ratifies a new covenant; the Spirit who fulfils *"the righteous requirement of the law"* in us (Rom 8:4). *What remains:* the land of 23:20–31 as the inheritance still to come (Heb 11:16; 4:9).

**Moralism check.**
- *The "be like X" temptation:* "Be generous like the good Israelite householder"; "be a just judge".
- *The gospel grounding:* every motive clause grounds the command in what God has done — *"you were sojourners in Egypt"* — and the Christian's equivalent is *"you were slaves of sin, and you were set free"* (Rom 6:17–18).
- *Christ as hero:* the one who kept covenant, shed the covenant blood, and announced release.

**Confidence.** High on the 24:8 hinge and the Matt 11:10 citation; moderate–high on the 23:7 / 34:6–7 crux as planted; moderate on Rom 4:5 and on the release line as a whole.

### Difficult / Contested Verses

**21:2–11, 20–21, 26–27, 32 — slavery.**
- **Category:** ethical; apologetic.
- **The difficulty:** God's law regulates rather than abolishes slavery, and 21:21 says *"for he is his money"*.
- **Common mishandlings:** equating ANE debt-servitude with chattel slavery (and so either condemning the text wholesale or defending the Atlantic trade); skipping the slave laws in the reading.
- **Rhetorical function in context:** the slave laws **open** the code and **close** its first section with release; 21:16 makes kidnapping a man to sell him a capital crime (the very act the Atlantic trade depended on); 21:26–27 frees a slave for a tooth. The direction of the text is toward release, inside a world it does not abolish at a stroke.
- **Main options:** (a) accommodation — God regulates a fallen institution (cf. Matt 19:8 on divorce); (b) trajectory — the law moves toward abolition (Deut 15; Jer 34; Philemon; 1 Tim 1:10 lists slave-traders with murderers); (c) both.
- **Honest exegesis:** 21:20–21 genuinely values the slave as property, and the text does not soften that; it also avenges a slave's death, which most ANE codes did not. *Moderate–high.*
- **Handling:** address it directly and briefly; read 21:16 aloud; do not apologise for God or sanitise the text.

**21:22–25 — the pregnant woman.** Contested; worked in the overview (three positions on אָסוֹן; the Greek's different law). The ESV leaves the crux open; the NASB95 closes it with a supplied "further". **Handling:** do not make this the sermon; do not build a doctrine on one reading; acknowledge in a sentence if the passage is read in full. `[S: overview]`

**21:23–25 — "eye for eye".** Weaponised as a licence for revenge. In context it is a judicial ceiling, administered by judges (21:22), and the code immediately converts it into liberty for a slave (21:26–27). Jesus forbids its use as personal retaliation (Matt 5:38–39). **Handling:** explain in two sentences.

**22:18 — "You shall not allow a sorceress to live."** Weaponised in the European witch trials; the Greek has the masculine plural. **Handling:** name the history honestly; the verse is Israel's judicial law against covenant treachery, not a mandate for the church (Copycat).

**22:19–20 — death for bestiality; "devoted to destruction" for sacrifice to other gods.** Ethical. **Handling:** Israel's judicial law; teach what it shows about the seriousness of covenant betrayal, not what it prescribes for a modern state.

**22:24 — "I will kill you with the sword."** God threatens his own people with Egypt's fate. **Handling:** let it stand; it is the measure of how much he cares for the widow.

**23:23–33 — the conquest.** The "driving out" of peoples, *"I will completely destroy them"* (וְהִכְחַדְתִּיו). Ethical; apologetic. Note what the text actually says: God does the work (hornets, terror, little by little); the warning is chiefly against **their gods** (23:24, 32–33). **Handling:** acknowledge; place it in the canon's arc (Gen 15:16, *"the iniquity of the Amorite is not yet complete"*).

**23:25–26 — health, fertility and long life.** Pastoral. **This is the overview's Trap 14 at its second site** — conditional, corporate, national promises to Israel in the land, re-issuing 15:26. Someone in the room has been told their illness or childlessness is a faith failure. **Handling:** say so gently; preach the God who gives life, not a transaction (Heb 11:35–39).

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: Calling 20:22–23:33 "what Exodus calls the Book of the Covenant"

- **What it looks like:** "Exodus 24 tells us these chapters are called the Book of the Covenant."
- **Why it's wrong:** 24:7 names a book Moses read; 24:3–4 says it held *"all the words of the LORD and all the ordinances"*. The ordinances are certainly 21–23; whether the book also held the Ten Words the text does not say. The exact extent is a scholarly convention.
- **The corrective:** "the covenant terms Moses read aloud at 24:7" — and the sermon loses nothing.

### Pitfall: Sorting the code into moral, civil and ceremonial before reading it

- **What it looks like:** "We keep 23:4–5, but the oxen and the festivals are for Israel" — said in the first five minutes.
- **Why it's wrong:** the code interleaves its kinds deliberately and grounds them all in the same motives; sorting first means the congregation never hears the shape.
- **The corrective:** preach the frames and the motives first; deal with application by asking what each law shows about God, then how the NT handles it (Copycat table).

### Pitfall: Preaching the case law without its motive clauses

- **What it looks like:** a list of duties — be fair, be generous, rest.
- **Why it's wrong:** the code itself grounds its commands in Egypt, in God's hearing, in his grace. This is the overview's Trap 4, and this passage is where it bites hardest.
- **The corrective:** let *"for you were sojourners in the land of Egypt"* govern the sermon.

### Pitfall: Skipping or apologising for the slave laws

- **What it looks like:** omitting 21:1–11 in the reading, or explaining them away.
- **Why it's wrong:** they are the code's **first** law, and they are a law of release. Omitting them hides the frame.
- **The corrective:** read them; say what they do (limit, protect, release) and what they do not (abolish); read 21:16.

### Pitfall: Preaching the angel of 23:20 as Christ — or ignoring the Gospels' use of it

- **What it looks like:** "The angel with God's name in him is the pre-incarnate Christ" — or silence.
- **Why it's wrong:** the same provision becomes a penalty at 32:34; 33:2–3, and the Gospels identify the *messenger* with John and put Jesus in Israel's place.
- **The corrective:** preach Matt 11:10 / Mark 1:2: the one who goes ahead prepares the way of the one who is *brought in*.

### Pitfall: Promising health and fertility from 23:25–26

- **What it looks like:** "God says: obey, and there will be no miscarriage or barrenness."
- **Why it's wrong:** a corporate, conditional, national promise to Israel in the land, re-issuing 15:26; the overview's Trap 14 at its second site.
- **The corrective:** preach the God who gives life; decline the transaction; speak pastorally.

### Pitfall: "Eye for eye" as a licence for revenge — or as primitive cruelty

- **What it looks like:** either "the Old Testament says get even" or "the Old Testament God is vindictive".
- **Why it's wrong:** it is a ceiling in a court, followed at once by liberty for a slave.
- **The corrective:** 21:22–27 read together.
