Claim Audit: Exodus

Document audited: book-overview-exodus.md v.1.1.0, together with the twelve macro-arguments in exodus-dig-deeper-macro-synthesis.md v1.0 Passages behind the claims: all fifteen preaching units (1:1–40:38) Date: 6 August 2026 Purpose: Test the overview’s load-bearing book-level claims before the series is preached. The overview was built from fifteen dig-deeper runs; each run reconciled against it from inside its own passage, but no run has ever tested the claims as a set — and a run that “confirms” a claim is often the run that produced it. This audit exists to break that circle.

Method note — the discipline this audit adopted. Because most of the overview’s assertions originate in my own runs, confirmation by the originating run was not accepted as evidence. For every load-bearing claim the text was re-checked against a public-domain text (KJV) in this session rather than taken from a run’s report. That is slower and it changed three verdicts.

Phase 0.6 — Positional Necessity, applied at book level

Run before scoping the claims, per the mode’s own discipline: scoping first would answer the positional question inside a frame the scope had already fixed.

Question 1 — what does the preceding canonical movement require that Exodus provides? Genesis ends with a family of seventy in Egypt, a coffin (50:26), and three unfulfilled promises — seed, land, blessing to the nations — plus a dated prediction of affliction and rescue (15:13–16). Exodus is required to deliver the seed-promise’s first instalment and the rescue on schedule; it does so at 1:7 and 12:40–41.

Question 2 — why does Exodus stop where it stops? It does not stop at the Sea, or at Sinai, or at a fulfilled land-promise. It stops at a filled tent with the mediator outside it (40:34–35) and a people still travelling (40:36–38). That ending is the strongest single control on the overview’s framing.

What the positional check surfaces about the overview itself. The overview’s spine — presence, descending to a dwelling — is vindicated by where the book ends. But the check also exposes a framing risk the audit should test: the overview consistently describes the book as arriving. The book’s own last word is unfinished — a way not yet opened, a routine not a resolution. Claims 2, 3 and 9 below are tested against that.

Claims Audited

Claim 1: The two questions (5:2 / 17:7), answered one per half, with 29:46 answering both

Source: Presenting Situation (added v1.0.3, expanded v1.0.4); macro-argument 1. Current confidence: high. Claim: Pharaoh’s “Who is the LORD?” (5:2) is answered by the plagues; Israel’s “Is the LORD among us or not?” (17:7) is answered by the tabernacle; and 29:46 answers both in one sentence.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability n/a Internal-echo claim, not an external allusion
Volume Strong Both questions are verbatim in the text; the answering refrains are verbatim and repeated
Recurrence Strong “That you may know that I am the LORD” recurs ×10+; “dwell in their midst” at 25:8; 29:45, 46
Thematic coherence Strong Each answer sits in the half the claim assigns it to
Historical plausibility n/a
History of interpretation Possible The tabernacle-as-answer is standard; the pairing of the two questions I have not found asserted in the literature
Satisfaction Strong Explains why the book gives half its length to a tent

Verdict: Confirmed.

Reasoning. 29:46 was re-verified in this session and it does hold both halves: “they shall know that I am the LORD their God, that brought them forth out of the land of Egypt, that I may dwell among them.” Knowing and dwelling in one verse, in that order. Both questions are quoted text, both refrains are repeated, and the division of labour between the halves is real.

One qualification, and it should be stated in the overview. The pairing is my synthesis, not a received reading — I have not found it asserted in the commentaries, and the checking stages never tested it directly. It is a strong inference from verified data, not an established position, and the overview should say so rather than presenting it as settled.

Book-overview action required: Add one clause marking the pairing as an inference. Downgrade the pairing (not the components) from stated-as-fact to high-confidence inference.

Claim 2: The descent-spine — “one descent in three stages”, yārad → šākan

Source: Structural Arc Map, “The descent-spine (sweep-confirmed)”; echo table; macro-argument 2. Current confidence: high. Claim: “I have come down (yārad) to deliver” (3:8) → the LORD comes down on Sinai (19:11, 18, 20) → the glory fills the tent (40:34); and šākan “moves from the mountain (24:16) to the people (25:8) to the finished tent (40:34).”

Criterion Score Reasoning
Volume Possible Yārad is verified at 3:8; 19:11, 18, 20 — but does not occur at 40:34
Recurrence Strong Both verbs recur; each chain is real on its own
Thematic coherence Strong The theological movement is genuinely there
History of interpretation Strong The presence-spine is widely held
Satisfaction Strong It does explain the book’s shape

Verdict: ⭐ Needs reframing.

Reasoning — and this is the audit’s most useful catch. The “descent-spine” is two chains welded, not one verb running through. Verified in this session:

  • Yārad (“come down”) runs 3:8 → 19:11, 18, 20 — “the LORD will come down… upon mount Sinai” (v. 11), “the LORD descended upon it in fire” (v. 18), “the LORD came down upon mount Sinai” (v. 20). All confirmed. But there is no yārad in chapter 40. The chain stops at Sinai.
  • Šākan (“dwell”) runs 24:16 → 25:8 → 29:45 → 40:35. Also confirmed — 24:16 reads “the glory of the LORD abode upon mount Sinai”, with the glory as subject.
  • But at the end the subject shifts. 40:34–35: “the glory of the LORD filled (mālēʾ) the tabernacle”, and “the cloud abode (šākan) thereon.” The glory fills; the cloud dwells.

So the overview’s sentence “the same verb moves from the mountain (24:16) to the people (25:8) to the finished tent (40:34)” contains a verse-reference error — šākan is not at 40:34 but at 40:35, and there its subject is the cloud, not the glory.

None of this damages the theology. God comes down and God dwells; the book’s shape is what the overview says it is. What is wrong is the presentation of it as a single lexical chain, which is the kind of claim a hearer with a concordance can falsify.

Book-overview action required: Reframe as two interlocking chains, correct 40:34 → 40:35, and note the subject-shift (glory fills / cloud dwells). Apply in the Arc Map, the echo table, and macro-argument 2.

Claim 3: The creation frame closed — “the same saw–finished–blessed sequence as Genesis”

Source: Structural Arc Map and macro-argument 4; the 35:1–40:38 run. Current confidence: high. Claim: Exodus 39:43 and 40:33 close the book with the same saw / finished / blessed sequence as Genesis 1:31–2:3.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability Strong Genesis is the book’s confirmed live source
Volume Strong All three lexemes present in both
Recurrence Strong Exodus draws on Genesis constantly
Thematic coherence Strong The tabernacle-as-cosmos reading is well founded
History of interpretation Strong Widely held
Satisfaction Strong Explains the shape of 39–40

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning. All three elements verified in both places. But the order differs, and the overview claims a sequence. Genesis: finished (2:1–2) → blessed and sanctified (2:3), with saw earlier at 1:31. Exodus: saw and blessed together at 39:43 (“Moses did look upon all the work… and Moses blessed them”) → finished at 40:33 (“So Moses finished the work”). The elements correspond; the sequence is inverted at the end.

Book-overview action required: Change “the same saw–finished–blessed sequence” to “the same three elements”, noting the order differs. Minor, but it is the sort of claim that gets checked.

Claim 5: “The blood of the covenant” (24:8) → Matt 26:28 / Heb 9:20

Source: Christological trajectory (added v1.0.0). Current confidence: high.

Verdict: Confirmed. NT-authorised at two independent points, one of them a direct quotation of the verse. The strongest OT→NT link in the book, and the reason 20:22–24:18 was re-weighted to ⭐HIGH. No action required.

Claim 6: The Amalek oath as an outstanding word — 17:14, 16 → Deut 25:17–19 → 1 Sam 15 → Esther 3:1

Claim 7: “The tabernacle is Sinai made portable and laid on its side”

Source: Structural Arc Map, “Why the tabernacle block sits where it does”. Current confidence: high. Claim: the mountain’s vertical holiness-gradient (summit / midway / base, 19:12, 21–24) becomes the tent’s horizontal three-zone architecture.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Volume Possible The verb-chain (24:16 → 25:8) is textual; the three-zone mapping is not stated by the text
Recurrence Strong The šākan thread is real
Thematic coherence Strong Graded access is genuinely present in both
History of interpretation Strong Morales, NSBT 37; Köstenberger & Goswell — a mainstream reading
Satisfaction Strong Explains why 25–31 follows 24

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance.

Reasoning. Two things are being claimed and only one is textual. The positional argument is textual and strong: the glory takes up residence on a mountain Israel must leave (24:16), and 25–31 makes the dwelling portable — the šākan verb does the work. The “laid on its side” three-zone mapping is a commentators’ construct ([S]), attractive and mainstream but not stated by Exodus.

A correction to this audit’s own first draft. I initially recorded that “the overview presents both at the same confidence”. That was unfair to the document. A separate paragraph three items later — “A three-zone gradient, transposed” — already carries the nuance correctly: [T] for both structures, [I] for the transposition, moderate–high. So the overview had the right verdict; what it lacked was consistency, since the arc-map paragraph asserted “laid on its side” without the qualification the later paragraph supplies. The fix is to align the two, not to introduce a caution that was already there.

Book-overview action required: Align the arc-map paragraph with the existing three-zone paragraph. Keep the positional argument at high; attribute the mapping.

Claim 8: The plagues as targeted de-creation of Genesis 1

Source: Intertextual map, “Creation & blessing” row; macro-argument 4. Current confidence: high (individual reversals) / moderate (overarching frame) — already split in the overview.

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance — and the overview already states the nuance correctly.

Reasoning. The individual reversals are text-derivable (Nile to blood, swarming creatures, darkness, death; and the tannîn of 7:9–12 against the bush’s nāḥāš). The claim that the nine plagues constitute a systematic reversal of the creation days is an inference, and the 7:1–11:10 run flagged it [I] and moderate–high rather than asserting it. The overview inherited the flag honestly. No action required — this is an example of the sweep’s warrant-tagging working.

Claim 9: The seven divine speeches of 25–31 map Genesis 1

Source: Arc Map and macro-argument 4. Current confidence: high on the pattern; moderate–high on the creation mapping — already split.

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. The seven-fold division is countable and confirmed; the Genesis 1 mapping is inference. One residual caution the overview carries but should keep visible: the seven speeches are marked by a divine-address formula that is not uniform — six use wayyǝdabbēr, one (30:34) uses wayyōʾmer, and the ESV levels the distinction. The count is sound; a hearer reading the ESV cannot see what it rests on. No action required beyond keeping that note attached.

Claim 11: Law precedes Sinai

Source: Arc Map (added v1.0.4); macro-argument 5. Current confidence: high.

Verdict: Confirmed. Verified in this session across two independent units: 15:25 (“a statute and a rule”), 16:4 (tôrâ), 16:23–30 (Sabbath given, kept, violated), 16:28 (“my commandments and my laws”), and — from a different chapter and a different run — 18:16, 20, where Moses is already teaching “the statutes of God and his laws”. Two runs reached it independently, which is the strongest form of internal corroboration available in this sweep. No action required.

Claim 12: Genesis 2:18 behind “not good… alone” (18:17–18)

Source: 18:1–27 run; listed as a candidate thread in the macro-synthesis, not promoted. Current confidence: moderate.

Criterion Score Reasoning
Availability Strong Genesis is live
Volume Possible Two lexemes (lōʾ-ṭôb, lĕbad), not a phrase; ʿēzer sits in a proper name
Recurrence Strong
Thematic coherence Strong Both texts name a deficiency and supply help

Reasoning. Correctly held at moderate and correctly kept out of the macro-arguments. The audit’s contribution is to confirm that the restraint was right: on Hays criteria this scores Strong on four, Possible on one and Weak on history of interpretation, which is exactly the profile of a connection that may be real and may be the reader’s construction. Claim 13: Elim’s twelve springs and seventy palms echo 1:5

Source: 15:22–17:16 run; not carried into the overview. Current confidence: uncertain.

Verdict: Discard — or rather, correctly never adopted.

Reasoning. The text does not gloss the numbers; Exodus glosses its other significant numbers and names. Twelve and seventy are common. The run reported it and declined to build on it, and the overview never took it up — the right outcome. Recorded here so a later reader does not rediscover it and promote it.

Claim 14: The ransom silver → 1 Peter 1:18–19

Source: Christological trajectory (added v0.3.0). Current confidence: high on the text; moderate–high on the 1 Peter resonance — already split.

Verdict: Confirmed with nuance. The Exodus arithmetic is textual and remarkable (38:25–27: a hundred talents, a hundred bases, a talent a base, footing the sanctuary on atonement money). The 1 Peter link is a resonance, not a citation — Peter names silver and gold to deny their sufficiency, which is thematically apt but is not a quotation of Exodus. The overview’s existing split is correct. No action required.

The headline: the overview survives the audit. Nothing load-bearing was overturned, no Christological claim collapsed, and the two NT-authorised chains (24:8; the mediator trajectory) are untouched. The one reframing is a matter of precision, not substance.

What the audit found that the runs could not. All three corrections came from re-checking the text rather than trusting the reports — the yārad/šākan weld, the 40:34/40:35 reference error, and the inverted saw–finished–blessed order. None of the fifteen runs would have caught these, because each was working one passage and the claims span the book. That is the specific value of this mode.

One methodological observation worth keeping. The claims that needed correction were all synthetic — assembled across passages — while the claims resting on a single verified passage held without exception. The sweep’s passage-level work is more reliable than its book-level work, which is what one would hope, and an argument for auditing again if further book-level claims are added.

Addendum — the Research Assistant pass (13 August 2026)

All fifty outstanding questions across the Exodus corpus were put to the Logos Research Assistant (exodus-questions-for-logos.odt). 43 returned usable answers; 7 remain open; 2 were rejected as unreliable. This addendum records only what changed a claim, a confidence rating, or a caution. Every change has been propagated to the document it came from.

Claims corrected

Claim Was Now
The hardening “verb-marked shift at 9:12” (plagues dig Headline 2; overview landmines) a clean shift from Pharaoh-hardens to God-hardens The verbs interleave. Kābēd, ḥāzaq and qāšâ each occur with both subjects; Pharaoh has already hardened himself three times before God acts at 9:12. The shift is real but not clean — preach the process, not a switch (Childs, OTL, 171)
“One of only three chapters in Exodus with no divine speech” (18:1–27) my own unverified count Withdrawn. Chapter 15 also lacks divine speech, as do the construction chapters. The observation that God does not speak in ch. 18 stands and is all the report needs
The four cups of the seder ← 6:6–7 flagged unverified Not ancient. The cups were added to the seder over time; the tidy correspondence is a popular-source claim. The caution is strengthened, not relaxed
The Hammurabi contrast (three differences) all three asserted Two hold securely (covenant framing; class-stratified penalties). The third — property crimes non-capital — “requires important qualification” and should not be asserted flatly

Claims upgraded

Claim Was Now
Ps 95:8 LXX → Heb 3:8 (the corpus’s most load-bearing unverified item) moderate, unsourced High — and it gained a payoff we had missed: the Greek shifts the allusion from Exodus 17 to Numbers 14, Kadesh-barnea, which is the generation Hebrews 3–4 actually warns against
Elim’s twelve springs and seventy palms uncertain — reported, not built on Moderate–high. Commentators read them as deliberately recalling Jacob’s seventy and the twelve tribes; the Mekilta agrees
1 Kings 8:11 ← Exodus 40:35 moderate High. Schnittjer’s “retrospective typology”; Morales; Keil & Delitzsch. The pattern can be preached as intentional
Ḥeṭeʾ gĕdōlâ, “a great sin” unpursued High. Five OT occurrences (one of adultery, four of idolatry) — and the same idiom in Egyptian marriage contracts and at Ugarit, of adultery. The calf is covenant adultery
Kābēd / kābôd wordplay moderate, possibly read back Moderate–high. Recognised in scholarship and operating across the whole plague narrative
Pāraṣ → 2 Samuel 6 moderate, possibly stock vocabulary Deliberate. Perez-Uzzah preserves the verb in nominal form
Gôy at 19:6 flagged for lexical confirmation Confirmed and sourced — an intentional allusion to Genesis 12:2 (Blackburn, NSBT 28)
The ANE background [S], unverified, “check before asserting” Citable. Rekhmire’s tomb (c. 1450 BC, ten men per overseer); the Rameses II leather scroll (2,000 bricks per gang); the Qenqenento straw complaint; Tutankhamun’s nested shrines as the closest known tabernacle parallel

Landmines discharged

Five of the seven on the overview’s standing list: the destroyer (12:23 — the ambiguity is deliberate and the text’s own); the 430 years (12:40 — both readings live, Paul reckoning with the LXX/Samaritan span); the aspect of 3:14 (grammar favours the future, context “I AM”, the ambiguity may be intentional); the ʾāsôn referent (21:22–23 — three positions named, Alexander’s “serious injury” the likeliest); and 4:24–26 (the LXX and Targums read the attacker as an angel of death, not Yahweh, and the circumcision as sacrificial). Only 4:11 and 15:3 remain unworked.

⭐ Two answers rejected — and why this matters

Not every cited answer is a reliable one. Two replies were rejected after checking against the text:

  • On šabbāt (Q35): the reply claimed the noun first occurs at Exodus 12:16. 12:16 reads “a holy convocation” (miqrāʾ-qōdeš), not šabbāt — verified here. The reply also ran backwards, describing 20:8–10 and 31:14–15 as preceding 16:23 in the book.
  • On the first altar (Q36): the reply placed 17:15 after the altar law of 20:24–25, which is the wrong way round.

Neither was incorporated, and both questions remain open. This is the third time in this project that a sourced answer has needed checking against the text before use — the same discipline that caught the descent-spine error in this audit and the “not perish” chain in Jonah. Cited is not the same as correct.

Still open after the pass

Seven questions returned no usable answer: the LXX wording of 24:8 (Band 1 — still the only unread Greek behind the strongest OT→NT link in the book); the ancient versions of 6:3; the LXX of 1:7, 38:8 and 40:35; qānâ at 15:16; and ʿal-pānāy at 20:3. Plus the two rejected above. All are versional or lexical, and all would be settled by direct consultation of a Greek text or a lexicon rather than a research query.

Second addendum — user research in the primary texts (13 August 2026)

Of the twenty-four questions left after the Research Assistant pass, seventeen were answered or advanced — this time by working the Greek texts, HALOT, a tagged Hebrew text and the commentaries directly, rather than by query. That is why this round succeeded where three research passes had not: eleven of the twenty-four were versional or lexical, and those wanted a book opened, not a search run.

Corrections to the corpus

Claim Was Now
The seven speech-formulae (25:1–31:18) six wayyǝdabbēr, one wayyōʾmer, inferred from the KJV Five and two — wayyǝdabbēr at 25:1; 30:11, 17, 22; 31:1, wayyōʾmer at 30:34 and 31:12 (tagged text). And the method was unsound: the KJV reads “spake” at 31:12, levelling one of the two. A translation cannot establish a Hebrew verb-count. The corrected data is better — the climactic seventh speech is one of the two marked differently
The rhetorical-question reading of 6:3 strengthening on Hebrew grounds The LXX is a flat denial (οὐκ ἐδήλωσα αὐτοῖς), and every major English version follows it. Martin’s proposal is a modern grammatical argument against an ancient interpretive consensus — name it, don’t adopt it
Exodus 15:2 // Ps 118:14 // Isa 12:2 grouped as one link Split. Exodus and the Psalm share ἐγένετό μοι εἰς σωτηρίαν verbatim; Isaiah has only σωτήρ μου — a thematic echo, not a verbal one
Qānâ at 15:16 open “Purchased”. Alexander: “there is no need to follow the translation ‘created’”; the warrant is the redemption of donkey and son at 13:13, 15, and the parallel with “this people you redeemed” at v. 13
Jethro’s condition at 18:23 an unmet condition, supporting the divine-silence finding Contested. Ross and Oswalt read a flat assertion — “God commands you to do it” — citing Moses’ immediate compliance. The finding is not withdrawn but no longer stands alone

Gains

⭐ The best single exegetical result in the project. LXX Exodus 24:8: ἰδοὺ τὸ αἷμα τῆς διαθήκης. Matthew 26:28 and Mark 14:24: τὸ αἷμά μου τῆς διαθήκης. The Gospels reproduce the Septuagint phrase word for word and add one genitive pronoun. He does not allude to Exodus 24:8; he takes its exact words and puts himself where the sacrificial blood stood. (They also drop the ἰδού — Exodus points at a basin; the Supper needs no pointing.) Asked and unanswered three times; settled in one line of Greek.

Also gained: the Genesis 2:18 echo at 18:17 is in print (Ross & Oswalt) and is promoted to moderate–high; 16:23 is the first šabbāt in Exodus and apparently in the Torah, strengthening the law-before-Sinai argument; 17:15 is the first altar in Exodus; LXX 1:7 preserves the Genesis 1:28 link (not a translation loss); LXX 38:8 has women who fasted where the Hebrew has women who served; LXX 40:35 has the cloud ἐπεσκίαζεν — the verb of the Transfiguration and Luke 1:35; and HALOT gives six live senses for ʿal-pānāy, with the ancient versions rendering it differently between Exodus 20:3 and Deuteronomy 5:7.

And both remaining landmines are discharged — 4:11 and 15:3 — emptying a list this corpus has carried since the overview’s first version.

Still open

Seven, and all are in the “smaller items” band: the angel-of-the-LORD Christophany (3:2, 4); the rear boundary of the 19:1–8 unit; the Hebrews 12:21 attribution; the resumptive 19:9b; whether 20:22–24:18 divides at 23:33; the Reuel/Jethro double-naming; and the Hur identification with the frontlets question. Nothing load-bearing remains unresolved.

The methodological finding, now firm

Three research passes could not supply six Greek texts and a HALOT entry; one session with the books supplied all of them. Set beside the two rejected answers of the previous round, the rule for this project is settled: research tools are for the state of a scholarly question; primary texts are for the data. Do not ask a search engine what a verse says.
