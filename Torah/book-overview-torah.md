## Presenting Situation

**Narrator / author.** The Torah presents itself as Mosaic: Moses writes and speaks (Exod 24:4; 34:27; Num 33:2; Deut 31:9, 24), and Deuteronomy is largely Moses' own first-person preaching. The four narrative books are told by a mostly unnamed, omniscient, retrospective narrator who writes *after* the events — explaining obsolete customs, using "to this day" formulae, and narrating Moses' own death and burial (Deut 34). The honest position for preaching: substantial Mosaic authorship with later compositional shaping; the founding voice is Moses, the final form looks back. `[T]` (self-claims, retrospective markers) / `[S]` (composition/date debates — flag lightly, hang nothing on them).

**Audience.** Three horizons, which the volumes themselves distinguish. The *narrative-world* audience is the generation being formed — Egypt, Sinai, wilderness, Moab. The *compositional* audience is wilderness/plains-of-Moab Israel: redeemed from Egypt, the tabernacle standing in the camp, poised between Egypt behind and a land of giants ahead — frightened, prone to grumble, prone to return. The *canonical* audience is later Israel — monarchy, exile, and beyond — reading its own origin story to learn who its God is, who it is, and why it is where it is. Default to the canonical audience for interpretation; keep the wilderness audience in the ear, because the Torah is **pastoral literature before it is archive** — it strengthens faith under threat. `[T]` / `[I]`

**Occasion.** The birth and formation of a covenant nation out of a family of seventy — its rescue, its constitution at Sinai, its testing in the wilderness, and its re-commissioning on the edge of the land. `[T]`

**Pressure.** One sustained tension in two registers. *Will God keep his sworn word* — seed, land, blessing to the nations, and his own presence among them (Gen 12:1–3; 15) — against Egypt's tyranny, the wilderness, and the nations of Canaan? And *will this people survive its own heart* — the stiff-necked, uncircumcised heart that no exodus, no law, and no wilderness can mend? The Torah answers the first with an unqualified yes and leaves the second deliberately open. `[T]`

**Purpose.** To found Israel's identity and hope on the covenant faithfulness of the LORD — Creator, Redeemer, and holy Dweller-in-the-midst — and to press on the redeemed a life of wholehearted covenant love; and, in ending outside the land with the mediator dead and the heart uncured, to lean the whole story forward to a resolution it cannot itself supply. `[I]`

## Structural Arc Map

The five volumes are the five movements. Each is a whole book with its own overview; here they are read as one covenant-historical arc.

| Volume | Movement | Function in the whole |
|--------|----------|-----------------------|
| **Genesis** | Origins & promise | Creation and its unravelling (1–11); the covenant call and oath to Abraham (12; 15; 17); the seed preserved through the patriarchs to Judah's line; ends in a **coffin in Egypt** — promise held, unconsummated (50:26) |
| **Exodus** | Redemption & presence | God keeps the Abrahamic oath: redeems by blood (Passover) and water (the Sea), binds the people by covenant at Sinai, and — the true summit — *comes down to dwell*; the glory fills the tent (40:34) |
| **Leviticus** | Holiness & atonement | The answer to Exodus's crisis (a holy glory now resident in a sinful camp): **atonement** makes approach possible (1–16), **holiness** makes the people fit to host the presence (17–27) |
| **Numbers** | Wilderness & the heart | The redeemed people's own unbelieving heart proves the real enemy; a whole generation is buried in the wilderness, yet God's faithfulness carries the next to the edge of the land |
| **Deuteronomy** | Covenant renewal | On the plains of Moab, Moses re-preaches the whole covenant to the second generation — the Shema, the heart's incurable disease and its promised cure — and the Torah ends on **Nebo, looking in** (34) |

**Binding device.** A **linear covenant-historical build** carried by a **spatial-theological spine of presence**: Eden-presence lost (Gen 3:24) → Egypt → Sinai and the tabernacle where God comes down to dwell (Exod) → the holiness that dwelling demands (Lev) → the wilderness journey (Num) → Moab, looking into the land (Deut 34). Genesis 1–11 states the cosmic problem (a good creation disordered, humanity exiled from God's presence, the nations scattered); Genesis 12 through Deuteronomy runs the covenant answer. The whole is framed by **promise given** (Gen 12; 15) and **promise unconsummated** (Deut 34) — an inclusio of longing. Proportion is meaning: the vast Sinai-to-tabernacle block (Exod 19 – Lev 27) and the wilderness (Num) sit at the centre because the book's weight is not the escape from Egypt but *how a holy God can dwell with a stiff-necked people without consuming them*. `[T]` / `[S]` (Schnittjer confirms the spatial spine and proportional weighting).

## Microscript

*The LORD, Creator of all, having lost humanity from his presence and watched the nations scatter, swears to one man — Abraham — a seed, a land, a blessing for the nations, and his own dwelling in their midst. Against Egypt's tyranny and Israel's own stiff-necked heart he keeps that oath: he redeems a people by blood, comes down to dwell, teaches them how a sinful nation may live with the Holy One, and carries them through the wilderness — burying a faithless generation yet bringing the next — to the threshold of the land, where Moses re-preaches the covenant and distils it to whole-hearted love for the one LORD. Yet the Torah ends outside the land, its mediator dead on the mountain and the human heart still uncircumcised — a founding story deliberately unfinished, leaning forward to the Prophet like Moses, the greater Joshua who brings God's people to rest, and the God-given new heart.*

**Keynotes:** seed · land · blessing-to-the-nations · **presence/dwelling** · covenant · holiness · **love/heart**.
**Structural pulse (twin):** *promise → threat → keeping* (God is faithful) and *presence lost → presence given → presence longed-for* (the goal is God-with-us). `[T]`

Test against the five movements: origins-and-promise (Gen) → redemption-and-presence (Exod) → holiness-and-atonement (Lev) → wilderness-and-the-heart (Num) → covenant-renewal-leaning-forward (Deut). Each volume serves the spine; none is an appendix, least of all Leviticus (the atonement grammar) or Numbers (the diagnosis of the heart).

## Intertextual Map

The Torah's intertextual profile is the **inverse** of a late book like Ruth or Malachi. Standing at the head of the canon, the Torah has almost **no external OT sources to quote** — it is the *fountainhead the rest of Scripture quarries*, not a book carried by citation. Its dense intertextuality is therefore of two kinds: **(a) internal cross-volume welds** — later volumes running on Genesis and on each other; and **(b) forward reception** — the way the Prophets, Writings, and especially the New Testament mine the Torah. For the downstream volume overviews and their dig-deeper runs, the load-bearing point is the set of **confirmed live cross-volume sources** below: when a passage in Exodus–Deuteronomy echoes Genesis or the Abrahamic oath, Move 2 should give the echo an elevated prior.

| Source / thread | Where used across the Torah | What the use does | Live? | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Genesis (as a book)** | Re-cited at Exod 1:1–5 (← Gen 46); presupposed in Lev, Num, Deut | Genesis is the confirmed **live source** for all four following volumes; every later Genesis resonance carries a raised prior | **live** (all four books) | high |
| **The Abrahamic covenant** (seed, land, blessing, presence — Gen 12:1–3; 15; 17) | Exod 2:24; 3:6,15–17; 6:3–8; 32:13 · Num 23–24 (Balaam blesses in Abrahamic terms) · Deut's "sworn to the fathers" refrain | The stated ground of the whole plot — redemption, land, and blessing are covenant-*keeping*, not improvisation | **live** (all four) | high |
| **Creation & blessing vocabulary** (Gen 1:28 "fruitful, multiply"; the seven-day frame) | Exod 1:7 (multiplied under threat); the seven-speech, "finished and blessed" tabernacle (Exod 25–40); Sabbath (Exod 16; 20:11; 31); Lev clean/unclean taxonomy (separation/division) | New-creation-of-a-people; the tabernacle as a re-made creation-space for God's rest; holiness as ordered creation | **live** (Exod, Lev) | high / moderate |
| **The Exodus event & Sinai covenant** | Presupposed throughout Lev (the resident glory of Exod 40) and Num; retold and re-applied wholesale in Deut (5 ← Exod 20; the law re-given) | Grounds the ethics of every later volume in prior redemption ("I am the LORD who brought you out") | **live** (Lev, Num, Deut) | high |
| **Exod 34:6–7 character formula** ("merciful and gracious… who will by no means clear the guilty") | Coined Exod 34; redeployed by Moses at Num 14:18; the grace-note under all of Deut | The OT's master statement of God's character; states a tension (forgives yet does not clear) the Torah cannot resolve | **live** (Exod, Num) | high |
| **Gen 3:15 / the seed line under threat** | Judah's line (Gen 38; 49:8–12) · the assault on the infants (Exod 1) · the star-and-sceptre (Num 24:17) · the prophet like Moses (Deut 18:15) | The messianic-royal trajectory the Torah plants and hands on | **live** (thematic, cross-volume) | moderate–high |

**Consequence for the sweep.** Because the Torah is internally driven and externally source-less, the volume-level digs should lean heavily on **Move 4 (internal echo)** and on **Move 2 tracking of Genesis and the Abrahamic oath** as the confirmed live intertexts, rather than hunting external OT citations that are not there. `[T]` / `[S]` (reception links).

## Christological Trajectory

The Torah does not merely predict Christ; it **manufactures the categories** the New Testament then fills. Five load-bearing lines run across the volumes:

1. **A substitution grammar climbing toward the cross.** The ram *taḥat* ("instead of") Isaac (Gen 22:13) → Judah *taḥat* Benjamin (Gen 44:33) → the Passover lamb's blood for the firstborn (Exod 12) → the Levitical blood-principle, "the life… is in the blood… it makes atonement" (Lev 17:11), the two goats (Lev 16) → the bronze serpent lifted up (Num 21 → John 3:14). Isaiah then casts the Servant as an *ʾāšām*, a guilt-offering (Isa 53 ← Lev 5–7), and the NT detonates the whole chain: "Christ our Passover" (1 Cor 5:7), the once-for-all sacrifice (Heb 9–10), "he did not spare his own Son" (Rom 8:32 ← Gen 22:16 LXX). *Confidence: high (NT-authorised).*
2. **Presence — the through-line to Immanuel.** Eden's lost presence (Gen 3:24) → "the LORD was with" the patriarchs → God comes down to *dwell* (Exod 25:8; 40:34) → the holiness that guards the dwelling (Lev) → John 1:14, the Word who "tabernacled among us," and Rev 21:3, "the dwelling of God is with man." *Confidence: high.*
3. **The indispensable-yet-failing mediator.** Moses at the bush, at Sinai, and in his blot-me-out intercession after the calf (Exod 32:32); Aaron's incense between the dead and the living (Num 16:48); yet Moses himself is barred from the land (Num 20:12; Deut 34) and Aaron is mortal and must atone for himself (Lev 9; 16). Every mediator points past himself to the flawless one — "a Son… faithful over God's house" where Moses was a servant (Heb 3:1–6; 7). *Confidence: high.*
4. **The true Israel and the Prophet like Moses.** Where the first Israel failed in the wilderness, Jesus answers every temptation from Deuteronomy (Deut 8:3; 6:16; 6:13 → Matt 4), the true Israel and true Son; and Deut 18:15 / 34:10's unfilled longing for "a prophet like Moses" is answered in him (Acts 3:22; Mark 9:7, "listen to him"). *Confidence: high.*
5. **The curse borne and the new heart given.** Deuteronomy's blessing/curse sanctions (27–28) and "cursed is everyone hanged on a tree" (21:23) are resolved at the cross, where Christ becomes a curse "so that the blessing of Abraham might come to the nations" (Gal 3:13–14) — closing the loop back to Genesis 12:3. And the uncircumcised heart the Torah diagnoses but cannot cure (Deut 10:16; 29:4) is met by the promised divine circumcision (Deut 30:6) fulfilled in the Spirit and the new covenant (Jer 31; Ezek 36; Rom 2:29). *Confidence: high.*

**The Torah's ache.** Beneath all five: a longing for a true human who keeps the creation mandate, a true seed who blesses the nations, a true mediator, a true king from Judah, and a heart that loves God — and every figure comes close and fails. The book of origins ends in a **coffin** (Gen 50:26); the whole Torah ends on **Nebo, looking in** (Deut 34). The unfinishedness is designed. `[T]` / `[S]` (NT links).

**Moralism check (Torah-wide).** The besetting temptation is exemplarism in five keys — *be faithful like Abraham, brave like the midwives, a bold leader like Moses, zealous like Phinehas; just "choose life" and try harder.* The corrective is built into every volume: **grace precedes law**, always (redemption *before* Sinai; the Decalogue opens "I brought you out"; atonement chs. 1–16 *before* the Holiness Code; election "not because of your righteousness"). God is the hero who keeps his oath; the characters are recipients of grace and mirrors of need, not techniques to copy; the law exposes the heart-disease the gospel heals. Preach the figures *pointing to Christ* and the people *receiving grace as we do* — then any call to obedience flows **from** rescue, never toward it. `[T]`

## Preaching Traps

1. **Reading the Torah as a rulebook rather than a covenant story.** *Sounds like:* Leviticus/Deuteronomy as ancient legislation to be defended or excused; the narrative as mere backdrop to the laws. *Why wrong:* the law is embedded in and grounded on a story of prior redemption — grace-then-obedience, never the reverse. *Bites at:* Leviticus, the Deuteronomy law-code (12–26). *Corrective:* preach every command riding on the indicative of rescue.
2. **Moralising the characters ("be like / don't be like").** *Sounds like:* Abraham as a faith-technique, Joseph as a purity-and-forgiveness arc, Moses as a leadership seminar. *Why wrong:* the figures are instruments of providence and mirrors of failure; the pattern is God-initiates → human-responds → God-responds. *Bites at:* everywhere, especially the patriarchs and Moses. *Corrective:* ask of every text, "what does this reveal about *this God*, and how does faith respond?"
3. **Hurrying Genesis 1–11, and letting "days-and-dates" apologetics swallow Genesis 1.** *Sounds like:* three quick weeks before the "real" series at Abraham; a whole first sermon on young-earth vs old-earth. *Why wrong:* 12–Deuteronomy is unintelligible without the categories 1–11 establishes (image, blessing/curse, seed, scattered nations, lost presence); Genesis 1's own signals are about *what the world is for*, not the mechanism. *Bites at:* Genesis 1–11. *Corrective:* make the congregation feel the weight of the disordered world before the promise lands.
4. **Treating Leviticus and Numbers as sub-Christian or skippable.** *Sounds like:* apologising for the ritual and the wilderness, or preaching them only as negative example. *Why wrong:* Leviticus is the **grammar of the cross** (Hebrews' quarry), and Numbers is the canon's most sustained **warning against unbelief** (1 Cor 10; Heb 3–4) and a seedbed of Christ-ward types (serpent, rock, red heifer, star, shepherd, the greater Joshua). *Bites at:* Leviticus 1–16; Numbers 11–25. *Corrective:* preach them *forward* — their repetitions and restrictions are designed to make Christ's finished work glorious.
5. **Resolving the Torah's deliberately open ending.** *Sounds like:* a triumphant "and they all lived happily in the land." *Why wrong:* the Torah ends with the seed in a coffin (Gen 50:26) and the mediator dead outside the land (Deut 34); the land is unentered, the heart uncured. *Bites at:* the close of Genesis and of Deuteronomy. *Corrective:* preach the unfinishedness as a leaning-forward — into Joshua, and ultimately into the not-yet of our own waiting.
6. **Self-inserting where Christ is the type.** *Sounds like:* "I am Moses/Israel," making the sermon about our journey. *Why wrong:* the Torah's mediators, its true-Israel figure, and its coming Prophet are Christ-shaped vacancies the NT fills. *Bites at:* the exodus, the wilderness, Moses' intercession. *Corrective:* let Christ occupy the type; we stand with the rescued people.

**Pastoral landmines (flag HIGH; reserve for solo study).** The *ḥerem* / conquest-ethics texts (Num 31; Deut 7; 13; 20:16–18) and the contested sexuality and civil laws (Lev 18; 20; Deut 21–25) are among Scripture's hardest. Do not hot-take them: present the bounded, non-normative, theocratic character and the ANE context honestly, name the pastoral stakes, and give each a careful full dig before the pulpit. `[T]` / `[S: sweeps]`

**For the series as a whole:** the God who lost humanity from his presence swears to Abraham a seed, land, blessing, and dwelling (6), and keeps the oath against every failure (7); he redeems before he commands (1), comes down to dwell (4), and gives substitutionary blood so a sinful people may live with him (5), through a mediator whose failure points past him (3) — yet the deepest problem is the people's own heart (2), and the God who forgives will not simply acquit (8); so the Torah ends unfinished (10), its whole vocabulary the quarry the rest of Scripture works (9). Preach it as one book magnifying Christ from five angles at once: the seed, the presence, the substitute, the Mediator, and the new heart.
