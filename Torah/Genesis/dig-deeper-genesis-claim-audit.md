# Claim Audit: Genesis

**Documents audited:** `dig-deeper-genesis-whole-book.md` (the sixteen-unit sweep, 14 July 2026) and the four passage digs — Genesis 22:1–19 (12 July), Genesis 23 (11 July), Genesis 24 (12 July), Genesis 25:1–11 (23 July)
**Tested against:** `book-overview-genesis.md` v1.0.0 (7 September 2026, text-first) and the `_texts/` primary-text corpus
**Substrate:** WLC Hebrew (lemma-indexed), Swete LXX, SBLGNT for observation; `_texts/logos-exports/` (BHS, Rahlfs-based LXX, NASB95 Genesis) for citation
**Date:** 7 September 2026
**Mode:** Claim Audit (dig-deeper Phase 0.5b), standalone file

---

## The headline, stated plainly

**Every lexical chain in the sweep was unverified when it was written, and the sweep says so.** Its own Text-First Declaration reads: *"Wording-dependent findings (welds, puns, taḥat/ʿārēv/haker-nāʾ/pāqōd, Jabbok pun, 1:28↔47:27) checked against the Hebrew **as recalled**."* The sweep predates the `_texts/` corpus by seven weeks; there was no mechanical check available to it. This audit is the first time those chains have been run against a text.

That is the uncomfortable part. Here is the other part, and it matters as much: **the recall was mostly right.** Of 43 checkable claims across the five documents, 24 are confirmed outright, 8 confirmed with a nuance worth stating, 6 need reframing, 4 are wrong, and 1 open question is now closed. A 74% clean rate on unverified recall is a good deal better than the Jonah and Exodus audits would have predicted — but the four failures are all of one shape, and it is the shape this project has now named three times.

**Every one of the four failures is a number.** "Son occurs eleven times"; "*ʿōlāh* seven-fold"; "the longest speech in Genesis"; "*nûaḥ*… cf. 5:29". Not one failure is a claim about what a passage *means*. The September amendments' sixth rule turns out to be the operative one: *a stated count is the most auditable claim a report can make* — and a superlative is a count wearing a rhetorical coat.

---

## Method

Every claim below was tested by lemma against `_texts/hebrew-wlc/_index/`, never by pointed surface form or English gloss, with each claimed reference confirmed to contain the word. Counts name their edition. Greek claims were checked in `greek-lxx-swete/` and, where a reading is load-bearing, against the Rahlfs-based Logos export; NT claims against `greek-nt-sblgnt/`.

Claims are grouped rather than given one Hays table apiece: the great majority are **chain and count claims**, for which the Hays criteria are the wrong instrument — a chain either contains the word at every claimed reference or it does not. The three genuine **allusion** claims that warrant Hays treatment are worked in full in Part C.

### A2. Confirmed with a nuance worth stating (7)

**15 · The *ʿārēv* thread, 38:17–18 ↔ 43:9 / 44:32 (§13, §15).** Confirmed as a **root** link, which is what the sweep claims — but the two ends are different lexemes: *ʿērābôn* (6162, the noun "pledge") at 38:17, 18, **and 20**; *ʿārav* (6148, the verb "go surety") at 43:9 and 44:32. Say "the same root, ʿ-r-b, in two words" rather than letting "*ʿārēv*" stand as though one word ran through. The third occurrence at 38:20 strengthens the Tamar end.

**16 · *taḥat* — the substitution grammar, 22:13 → 44:33 (§9, §15).** Confirmed, and **the sweep missed the third term, which is the best of the three.** *taḥat* (8478) occurs 30× in Genesis, almost all as the ordinary preposition "under". Three carry the substitutionary sense, and the third is **50:19** — Joseph's הֲתַחַת אֱלֹהִים אָנִי, "am I *in the place of* God?" The ram stands *taḥat* the son (22:13); Judah offers to stand *taḥat* the boy (44:33); Joseph refuses to stand *taḥat* God (50:19). The sweep tracks *taḥat* as substitution and separately tracks 3:5 ↔ 50:19 as the god-complex bookend, and never notices that **50:19 is the single word where both threads meet**. This is the audit's best find and it belongs in the sweep and the overview.

**17 · "The LORD was with Joseph" (§14).** Confirmed at 39:2, 21, 23 — and the sweep undercounts. **39:3** also carries it ("his master saw that the LORD was with him"). The refrain is **4× in chapter 39**, and 39:3 is the one that says the Egyptian could see it.

**18 · "God sent me" ×3 at 45:5–8 (§15).** Confirmed: *šālaḥ* (7971) at 45:5, 7, 8. The nuance is that the third is contrastive — 45:8 is "it was not **you** who sent me here, but God". Three occurrences, but the third negates human sending rather than repeating divine sending, which is the point and is worth saying.

**19 · *ṣālēaḥ* (§14).** Confirmed at 39:2, 3, 23 — and it is not confined to Joseph. 6743 also stands at **24:21, 40, 42, 56**, four times of the servant's journey. The word links Genesis 24's providence to Genesis 39's, which is a thread the sweep's §10 and §14 could have shared.

**20 · The El-name triptych, 14:18–20 / 16:13 / 17:1 (§8).** All three confirmed on the page. But they are a **selection, not the set**: Genesis also has *El Olam* (21:33), *El Elohe-Israel* (33:20), and *El Bethel* (35:7). The triptych is a real cluster within one unit; present it as that, not as Genesis's complete El-naming.

**21 · The presence programme (§14, Cross-Passage 5).** Confirmed and **substantially extended.** Beyond the sweep's 26:24 / 28:15 / 39:2, 21 / 46:4, the formula also stands at **21:20, 21:22, 26:3, 26:28, 28:20, 31:3, 31:5, 35:3, 39:3, 39:23, 48:21**. Sixteen places, not five — and 21:22 is an outsider's observation, 28:20 is Jacob's conditional. A stronger programme than claimed.

### A3. Needs reframing (4)

**22 · "1:28 ↔ 47:27 (near-verbatim)" (Book-Level Threads, §16).** **Overstated.** 1:28 carries 1288 + 6509 + 7235 + 4390 + 776; 47:27 carries only 6509 + 7235 + 3966. That is a **two-root echo**, not near-verbatim. The near-verbatim partner of 1:28 is **9:1**. The theological point — the creation mandate fulfilled in Egypt rather than in the land — survives intact and is worth keeping; only the word "near-verbatim" fails. *Restate as:* "1:28's *pārāh* + *rābāh* pair recurs at 47:27, the mandate reported fulfilled in the wrong land."

**23 · "Table of 70 nations (10) ↔ 70 descendants (46:8–27)", tagged high `[T]` (Book-Level Threads, §6, §16).** **Warrant error, and an ancient-versions check that never ran.** Genesis 10 nowhere says seventy; the figure is arrived at by counting proper nouns and requires editorial decisions the text does not make (whether to count cities, gentilic plurals, Nimrod, the three sons). The WLC index yields 86 distinct proper-noun lemmas in the chapter before any of those decisions are taken. Genesis 46:27, by contrast, **states** שִׁבְעִים. So one half is `[S]`/`[I]` and the other `[T]` — and the LXX reads **seventy-five** at 46:27 (ἑβδομήκοντα πέντε, with Joseph's sons "nine" rather than two), which is what **Acts 7:14** follows. *Restate as:* a stated seventy at 46:27 against a conventional seventy at ch. 10, with the LXX/Acts variant named — a Three-Way Triage **category 2** item the sweep did not classify.

**24 · "3:5 'like God' ↔ 50:19 'Am I in the place of God?'" listed among the *verbal* welds, high `[T]` (Book-Level Threads, §16).** **Conceptual, not verbal.** 3:5 reads כֵּאלֹהִים (*kə-* + 430); 50:19 reads הֲתַחַת אֱלֹהִים. The shared word is only אֱלֹהִים. The thematic resolution is real and worth preaching; it is not a diction weld, and it should not sit in a list headed "verbal welds" at `[T]`. *And see #16:* the verbal weld at 50:19 runs to 22:13 and 44:33, not to 3:5.

**25 · The goat-kid motif, 27:9, 16 → 37:31 → 38:17 (§11, §13).** **The named failure mode, exactly.** *gədî* (1423) stands at 27:9, 16 and 38:17, 20, 23 — **not at 37:31**, which reads שְׂעִיר עִזִּים (8163 + 5795), a male goat. The chain was extended by inference into a verse that does not contain the word. It is repairable rather than fatal: **ʿizzîm (5795) is present at all three** — 27:9, 16; 37:31; 38:17, 20. *Restate the chain on ʿizzîm*, and the motif stands with its middle term intact.

### A4. Wrong (2)

**26 · "*nûaḥ* ('rest') puns on *Nōaḥ*, cf. 5:29, 'he shall bring us relief'" — *High*, `[T]` (§5).** **Wrong as stated.** 5:29's verb is יְנַחֲמֵנוּ, from **nāḥam** (5162, "comfort"), not *nûaḥ*. The mismatch between Noah's name and the etymology 5:29 gives it is a well-known crux; the sweep resolved it silently in the wrong direction and stamped it `[T]` at high confidence. *And the real finding was underneath:* **nûaḥ (5117) occurs exactly once in Genesis — 8:4, where the ark *rested* on Ararat.** The one place Genesis uses the verb the name sounds like is of Noah's own vessel coming to rest. *Replace the claim with that.*

**27 · "Judah's speech (44:18–34), the longest speech in Genesis" (§15 twice, Schnittjer pass, Cross-Passage 2).** **Wrong.** Counted in accent units from the WLC: 44:18–34 runs to **186**; the servant's speech at 24:34–49 runs to **198**; Jacob's blessing at 49:1–27 runs to **249**. The narrative-braking argument the sweep builds on it does not need the superlative — the speech is still the longest by any of the twelve brothers, and it is still the moral peak. *Restate without the superlative;* the same error was independently caught and corrected in the new book overview.

### A5. Open question now closed (1)

**28 · "12:3 *wĕnivrĕkû*: niphal passive or hithpael reflexive?" (§7, Open Questions).** **Resolved from the morphology, and it is not one stem but two.** Checked word by word in `_texts/hebrew-wlc/_index/`: **niphal** at 12:3 (וְנִבְרְכוּ, HC/VNp3cp), 18:18 (HC/VNq3cp) and 28:14 (HC/VNp3cp); **hithpael** at 22:18 (וְהִתְבָּרֲכוּ, HC/Vtq3cp) and 26:4 (HC/Vtq3cp). The refrain alternates across its five occurrences. Galatians 3:8 quotes it passively (ἐνευλογηθήσονται). *The stem is no longer an open question; what the alternation means still is* — and that is a better question than the one the sweep left standing.

## Part B — The four passage digs

### B1. Overall assessment

**These are the better documents.** All four are genuinely text-first, warrant-tagged, honestly confidence-flagged, and they meet the solo depth floors. Genesis 24 in particular is a model run: it consulted Schnittjer *only* at the checking stage, says so, and can name the one point at which its own text-work **extends** the commentary rather than reproducing it (the 22:17 → 24:60 verbal echo). That is what an earned confirmation looks like, and it is rare.

Three structural facts constrain all four, none of them the digs' fault:

1. **All four were run with no book-overview in conversation** (each says so). They therefore never had a Phase 0.5 active layer, and their Book-Overview Tensions sections are empty by necessity. Genesis 25:1–11 helpfully lists what it *would* have tested — that list can now be run.
2. **All four use the ESV as both study text and pulpit text**, with "wording transfers directly to /sermon-creator" in the header. Under the current standing rule the study text is NASB95 and no pulpit text is assumed where no engagement is declared. This costs a real check: at **23:6** the ESV's "a prince of God" and the NASB95's "a mighty prince" differ in *sense*, and the Genesis 23 dig noticed the divergence but had no NASB95 in the frame to run it as a pulpit check.
3. **All four predate the corpus.** Genesis 22 is candid about it — its passage text carries `[unverified — check wording]` at vv. 12–13, and Genesis 23's header says the ESV wording is "from careful recall". Genesis 24 alone verified its passage text against an external witness.

### B3. What the digs should carry forward

- **Genesis 22** is otherwise sound; correct the two counts and it stands. Its Moriah handling is exemplary — it flags the 2 Chr 3:1 identification as the Chronicler's retrospective, `[S]`, *moderate*, rather than preaching it as Genesis's own claim.
- **Genesis 23** should absorb #35: the *ʾăḥuzzâ* thread runs from 17:8 through 23 to 47:11, 48:4, 49:30 and 50:13, which makes its Headline Finding 5 (the grave as the family's anchor) demonstrable at word level rather than thematic.
- **Genesis 24** needs nothing but a header rebase; #40 should be promoted into the overview's echo table, where it is currently absent.
- **Genesis 25:1–11** should carry the *qedem* correction and the 2:8 counter-instance, and can now run the three tests its own Book-Overview Tensions section listed as pending.

## Part C — Allusion claims, worked in full

Three claims across the five documents are genuine intertextual assertions rather than chains, and warrant the Hays criteria.

### C1 · Gen 22:16 LXX "you did not spare" → Romans 8:32

**Source:** sweep §9, Cross-Passage 3; asserted *high*, `[S: NT]`.
**Claim:** Paul's οὐκ ἐφείσατο at Rom 8:32 takes its verb from the LXX of Gen 22:16.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | The Greek Genesis was Paul's Bible |
| Volume | Strong | οὐκ ἐφείσω τοῦ υἱοῦ σου τοῦ ἀγαπητοῦ (Gen 22:12, 16, identical in Swete and the Rahlfs-based export) against Paul's τοῦ ἰδίου υἱοῦ οὐκ ἐφείσατο — verb and object both |
| Recurrence | Strong | Paul argues from Abraham at length in Rom 4 and Gal 3 |
| Thematic coherence | Strong | Rom 8:32's whole argument is *a fortiori* from a father who did not withhold a son |
| Historical plausibility | Strong | The Aqedah was a fixed point of Second-Temple reflection |
| History of interpretation | Strong | Widely held |
| Satisfaction | Strong | Positing it explains Paul's otherwise odd verb choice |

**Verdict: Confirmed.** **Action:** none — but attach the correction below, which the sweep does not carry.

**A correction the sweep and the overview both need.** The LXX renders *yāḥîd* at 22:2, 12 and 16 as **ἀγαπητός** ("beloved") at all three — verified in Swete and in the Rahlfs-based export. It does **not** read μονογενής. *Monogenēs* for Isaac comes from **Hebrews 11:17**, not from the Greek Genesis. Any sermon that says "the LXX calls Isaac *monogenēs*, the word John 3:16 uses of Christ" is wrong on its premise while landing on a true conclusion. Say it the accurate way: the LXX gives "beloved son", Hebrews supplies "only-begotten".

### C2 · Machpelah / Moriah → the temple mount (2 Chr 3:1)

**Source:** Genesis 22 dig, Tool 7(f) and Tool 11.
**Claim:** Gen 22's Moriah is the site of Solomon's temple.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | 2 Chr 3:1 makes the identification explicitly |
| Volume | Weak | One shared proper noun; Genesis says only "the land of Moriah" |
| Recurrence | Weak | מֹרִיָּה occurs twice in the whole Hebrew Bible — Gen 22:2 and 2 Chr 3:1 |
| Thematic coherence | Possible | Provision-at-a-mountain and sacrifice-at-a-mountain cohere |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | The identification is late but not implausible |
| History of interpretation | Strong | Ancient and near-universal in Jewish and Christian reading |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Illuminating, but the illumination is the Chronicler's, not Genesis's |

**Verdict: Confirmed with nuance — and the dig already got this exactly right.** It tagged the link `[S — later canon, not established within Genesis itself]` at *moderate*, and refused to let it become Genesis's own claim. That is the correct handling and should be the pattern. **Action:** none.

### C3 · Gen 3:15's LXX αὐτός as a messianic reading

**Source:** the new book overview (Christological Trajectory 1); **absent from the sweep**, which treats 3:15 without reference to the Greek.
**Claim:** The LXX's masculine αὐτός against neuter σπέρμα personalises the seed.

| Criterion | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Strong | n/a — this is a translation datum, not an allusion |
| Volume | Strong | The grammatical mismatch is unambiguous and present in both Swete and Rahlfs |
| Recurrence | Weak | A single verse |
| Thematic coherence | Possible | Coheres with the seed-line Genesis then tracks |
| Historical plausibility | Possible | Contested whether it reflects messianic intent or a Hebraism |
| History of interpretation | Strong | Long read messianically; long disputed |
| Satisfaction | Possible | Explains something; proves less than it is usually made to prove |

**Verdict: Confirmed as a textual fact; uncertain as a messianic intention.** The divergence is high-confidence; the translator's motive is not. **Action:** the sweep's §3 should carry the datum, flagged this way, since §3 currently handles 3:15 from the Hebrew alone and the sweep never ran a Tool 8 ancient-versions check on the book's most-preached verse.
