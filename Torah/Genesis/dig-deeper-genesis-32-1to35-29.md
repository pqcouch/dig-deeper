## The Passage

The Hebrew is the WLC as it stands in `_texts/hebrew-wlc/01-Torah/01-Genesis.txt`; the English beneath each verse is the NASB95 from `_texts/logos-exports/`. Three features of the printed Hebrew are the manuscript's, not the corpus's: the dots over וַיִּשָּׁקֵהוּ ("and he kissed him", 33:4) are *puncta extraordinaria*; the פ in the middle of 35:22 is a *petuchah* inside a verse; and 35:22 carries a double accentuation in BHS (Textual Variants). The section headings are the run's own division (Tool 3).

**32:27** [Eng 32:26] וַיֹּ֣אמֶר שַׁלְּחֵ֔נִי כִּ֥י עָלָ֖ה הַשָּׁ֑חַר וַיֹּ֨אמֶר֙ לֹ֣א אֲשַֽׁלֵּחֲךָ֔ כִּ֖י אִם־בֵּרַכְתָּֽנִי׃

> Then he said, “Let me go, for the dawn is breaking.” But he said, “I will not let you go unless you bless me.”

**32:28** [Eng 32:27] וַיֹּ֥אמֶר אֵלָ֖יו מַה־שְּׁמֶ֑ךָ וַיֹּ֖אמֶר יַעֲקֹֽב׃

> So he said to him, “What is your name?” And he said, “Jacob.”

**32:29** [Eng 32:28] וַיֹּ֗אמֶר לֹ֤א יַעֲקֹב֙ יֵאָמֵ֥ר עוֹד֙ שִׁמְךָ֔ כִּ֖י אִם־יִשְׂרָאֵ֑ל כִּֽי־שָׂרִ֧יתָ עִם־אֱלֹהִ֛ים וְעִם־אֲנָשִׁ֖ים וַתּוּכָֽל׃

> He said, “Your name shall no longer be Jacob, but Israel; for you have striven with God and with men and have prevailed.”

**32:30** [Eng 32:29] וַיִּשְׁאַ֣ל יַעֲקֹ֗ב וַיֹּ֨אמֶר֙ הַגִּֽידָה־נָּ֣א שְׁמֶ֔ךָ וַיֹּ֕אמֶר לָ֥מָּה זֶּ֖ה תִּשְׁאַ֣ל לִשְׁמִ֑י וַיְבָ֥רֶךְ אֹת֖וֹ שָֽׁם׃

> Then Jacob asked him and said, “Please tell me your name.” But he said, “Why is it that you ask my name?” And he blessed him there.

**32:31** [Eng 32:30] וַיִּקְרָ֧א יַעֲקֹ֛ב שֵׁ֥ם הַמָּק֖וֹם פְּנִיאֵ֑ל כִּֽי־רָאִ֤יתִי אֱלֹהִים֙ פָּנִ֣ים אֶל־פָּנִ֔ים וַתִּנָּצֵ֖ל נַפְשִֽׁי׃

> So Jacob named the place Peniel, for he said, “I have seen God face to face, yet my life has been preserved.”

**32:32** [Eng 32:31] וַיִּֽזְרַֽח־ל֣וֹ הַשֶּׁ֔מֶשׁ כַּאֲשֶׁ֥ר עָבַ֖ר אֶת־פְּנוּאֵ֑ל וְה֥וּא צֹלֵ֖עַ עַל־יְרֵכֽוֹ׃

> Now the sun rose upon him just as he crossed over Penuel, and he was limping on his thigh.

**32:33** [Eng 32:32] עַל־כֵּ֡ן לֹֽא־יֹאכְל֨וּ בְנֵֽי־יִשְׂרָאֵ֜ל אֶת־גִּ֣יד הַנָּשֶׁ֗ה אֲשֶׁר֙ עַל־כַּ֣ף הַיָּרֵ֔ךְ עַ֖ד הַיּ֣וֹם הַזֶּ֑ה כִּ֤י נָגַע֙ בְּכַף־יֶ֣רֶךְ יַעֲקֹ֔ב בְּגִ֖יד הַנָּשֶֽׁה׃

> Therefore, to this day the sons of Israel do not eat the sinew of the hip which is on the socket of the thigh, because he touched the socket of Jacob’s thigh in the sinew of the hip.

### 33:1–17 · The meeting

**33:1** וַיִּשָּׂ֨א יַעֲקֹ֜ב עֵינָ֗יו וַיַּרְא֙ וְהִנֵּ֣ה עֵשָׂ֣ו בָּ֔א וְעִמּ֕וֹ אַרְבַּ֥ע מֵא֖וֹת אִ֑ישׁ וַיַּ֣חַץ אֶת־הַיְלָדִ֗ים עַל־לֵאָה֙ וְעַל־רָחֵ֔ל וְעַ֖ל שְׁתֵּ֥י הַשְּׁפָחֽוֹת׃

> Then Jacob lifted his eyes and looked, and behold, Esau was coming, and four hundred men with him. So he divided the children among Leah and Rachel and the two maids.

**33:2** וַיָּ֧שֶׂם אֶת־הַשְּׁפָח֛וֹת וְאֶת־יַלְדֵיהֶ֖ן רִֽאשֹׁנָ֑ה וְאֶת־לֵאָ֤ה וִֽילָדֶ֨יהָ֙ אַחֲרֹנִ֔ים וְאֶת־רָחֵ֥ל וְאֶת־יוֹסֵ֖ף אַחֲרֹנִֽים׃

> He put the maids and their children in front, and Leah and her children next, and Rachel and Joseph last.

**33:3** וְה֖וּא עָבַ֣ר לִפְנֵיהֶ֑ם וַיִּשְׁתַּ֤חוּ אַ֨רְצָה֙ שֶׁ֣בַע פְּעָמִ֔ים עַד־גִּשְׁתּ֖וֹ עַד־אָחִֽיו׃

> But he himself passed on ahead of them and bowed down to the ground seven times, until he came near to his brother.

**33:4** וַיָּ֨רָץ עֵשָׂ֤ו לִקְרָאתוֹ֙ וַֽיְחַבְּקֵ֔הוּ וַיִּפֹּ֥ל עַל־צַוָּארָ֖ו וַׄיִּׄשָּׁׄקֵ֑ׄהׄוּׄ וַיִּבְכּֽוּ׃

> Then Esau ran to meet him and embraced him, and fell on his neck and kissed him, and they wept.

**33:5** וַיִּשָּׂ֣א אֶת־עֵינָ֗יו וַיַּ֤רְא אֶת־הַנָּשִׁים֙ וְאֶת־הַיְלָדִ֔ים וַיֹּ֖אמֶר מִי־אֵ֣לֶּה לָּ֑ךְ וַיֹּאמַ֕ר הַיְלָדִ֕ים אֲשֶׁר־חָנַ֥ן אֱלֹהִ֖ים אֶת־עַבְדֶּֽךָ׃

> He lifted his eyes and saw the women and the children, and said, “Who are these with you?” So he said, “The children whom God has graciously given your servant.”

**33:6** וַתִּגַּ֧שְׁןָ הַשְּׁפָח֛וֹת הֵ֥נָּה וְיַלְדֵיהֶ֖ן וַתִּֽשְׁתַּחֲוֶֽיןָ׃

> Then the maids came near with their children, and they bowed down.

**33:7** וַתִּגַּ֧שׁ גַּם־לֵאָ֛ה וִילָדֶ֖יהָ וַיִּֽשְׁתַּחֲו֑וּ וְאַחַ֗ר נִגַּ֥שׁ יוֹסֵ֛ף וְרָחֵ֖ל וַיִּֽשְׁתַּחֲוֽוּ׃

> Leah likewise came near with her children, and they bowed down; and afterward Joseph came near with Rachel, and they bowed down.

**33:8** וַיֹּ֕אמֶר מִ֥י לְךָ֛ כָּל־הַמַּחֲנֶ֥ה הַזֶּ֖ה אֲשֶׁ֣ר פָּגָ֑שְׁתִּי וַיֹּ֕אמֶר לִמְצֹא־חֵ֖ן בְּעֵינֵ֥י אֲדֹנִֽי׃

> And he said, “What do you mean by all this company which I have met?” And he said, “To find favor in the sight of my lord.”

**33:9** וַיֹּ֥אמֶר עֵשָׂ֖ו יֶשׁ־לִ֣י רָ֑ב אָחִ֕י יְהִ֥י לְךָ֖ אֲשֶׁר־לָֽךְ׃

> But Esau said, “I have plenty, my brother; let what you have be your own.”

**33:10** וַיֹּ֣אמֶר יַעֲקֹ֗ב אַל־נָא֙ אִם־נָ֨א מָצָ֤אתִי חֵן֙ בְּעֵינֶ֔יךָ וְלָקַחְתָּ֥ מִנְחָתִ֖י מִיָּדִ֑י כִּ֣י עַל־כֵּ֞ן רָאִ֣יתִי פָנֶ֗יךָ כִּרְאֹ֛ת פְּנֵ֥י אֱלֹהִ֖ים וַתִּרְצֵֽנִי׃

> Jacob said, “No, please, if now I have found favor in your sight, then take my present from my hand, for I see your face as one sees the face of God, and you have received me favorably.

**33:11** קַח־נָ֤א אֶת־בִּרְכָתִי֙ אֲשֶׁ֣ר הֻבָ֣את לָ֔ךְ כִּֽי־חַנַּ֥נִי אֱלֹהִ֖ים וְכִ֣י יֶשׁ־לִי־כֹ֑ל וַיִּפְצַר־בּ֖וֹ וַיִּקָּֽח׃

> “Please take my gift which has been brought to you, because God has dealt graciously with me and because I have plenty.” Thus he urged him and he took it.

**33:12** וַיֹּ֖אמֶר נִסְעָ֣ה וְנֵלֵ֑כָה וְאֵלְכָ֖ה לְנֶגְדֶּֽךָ׃

> Then Esau said, “Let us take our journey and go, and I will go before you.”

**33:13** וַיֹּ֣אמֶר אֵלָ֗יו אֲדֹנִ֤י יֹדֵ֨עַ֙ כִּֽי־הַיְלָדִ֣ים רַכִּ֔ים וְהַצֹּ֥אן וְהַבָּקָ֖ר עָל֣וֹת עָלָ֑י וּדְפָקוּם֙ י֣וֹם אֶחָ֔ד וָמֵ֖תוּ כָּל־הַצֹּֽאן׃

> But he said to him, “My lord knows that the children are frail and that the flocks and herds which are nursing are a care to me. And if they are driven hard one day, all the flocks will die.

**33:14** יַעֲבָר־נָ֥א אֲדֹנִ֖י לִפְנֵ֣י עַבְדּ֑וֹ וַאֲנִ֞י אֶֽתְנָהֲלָ֣ה לְאִטִּ֗י לְרֶ֨גֶל הַמְּלָאכָ֤ה אֲשֶׁר־לְפָנַי֙ וּלְרֶ֣גֶל הַיְלָדִ֔ים עַ֛ד אֲשֶׁר־אָבֹ֥א אֶל־אֲדֹנִ֖י שֵׂעִֽירָה׃

> “Please let my lord pass on before his servant, and I will proceed at my leisure, according to the pace of the cattle that are before me and according to the pace of the children, until I come to my lord at Seir.”

**33:15** וַיֹּ֣אמֶר עֵשָׂ֔ו אַצִּֽיגָה־נָּ֣א עִמְּךָ֔ מִן־הָעָ֖ם אֲשֶׁ֣ר אִתִּ֑י וַיֹּ֨אמֶר֙ לָ֣מָּה זֶּ֔ה אֶמְצָא־חֵ֖ן בְּעֵינֵ֥י אֲדֹנִֽי׃

> Esau said, “Please let me leave with you some of the people who are with me.” But he said, “What need is there? Let me find favor in the sight of my lord.”

**33:16** וַיָּשָׁב֩ בַּיּ֨וֹם הַה֥וּא עֵשָׂ֛ו לְדַרְכּ֖וֹ שֵׂעִֽירָה׃

> So Esau returned that day on his way to Seir.

**33:17** וְיַעֲקֹב֙ נָסַ֣ע סֻכֹּ֔תָה וַיִּ֥בֶן ל֖וֹ בָּ֑יִת וּלְמִקְנֵ֨הוּ֙ עָשָׂ֣ה סֻכֹּ֔ת עַל־כֵּ֛ן קָרָ֥א שֵׁם־הַמָּק֖וֹם סֻכּֽוֹת׃ ס

> Jacob journeyed to Succoth, and built for himself a house and made booths for his livestock; therefore the place is named Succoth.

### 33:18–20 · Shechem

**33:18** וַיָּבֹא֩ יַעֲקֹ֨ב שָׁלֵ֜ם עִ֣יר שְׁכֶ֗ם אֲשֶׁר֙ בְּאֶ֣רֶץ כְּנַ֔עַן בְּבֹא֖וֹ מִפַּדַּ֣ן אֲרָ֑ם וַיִּ֖חַן אֶת־פְּנֵ֥י הָעִֽיר׃

> Now Jacob came safely to the city of Shechem, which is in the land of Canaan, when he came from Paddan-aram, and camped before the city.

**33:19** וַיִּ֜קֶן אֶת־חֶלְקַ֣ת הַשָּׂדֶ֗ה אֲשֶׁ֤ר נָֽטָה־שָׁם֙ אָהֳל֔וֹ מִיַּ֥ד בְּנֵֽי־חֲמ֖וֹר אֲבִ֣י שְׁכֶ֑ם בְּמֵאָ֖ה קְשִׂיטָֽה׃

> He bought the piece of land where he had pitched his tent from the hand of the sons of Hamor, Shechem’s father, for one hundred pieces of money.

**33:20** וַיַּצֶּב־שָׁ֖ם מִזְבֵּ֑חַ וַיִּ֨קְרָא־ל֔וֹ אֵ֖ל אֱלֹהֵ֥י יִשְׂרָאֵֽל׃ ס

> Then he erected there an altar and called it El-Elohe-Israel.

### 34:1–31 · Dinah

**34:1** וַתֵּצֵ֤א דִינָה֙ בַּת־לֵאָ֔ה אֲשֶׁ֥ר יָלְדָ֖ה לְיַעֲקֹ֑ב לִרְא֖וֹת בִּבְנ֥וֹת הָאָֽרֶץ׃

> Now Dinah the daughter of Leah, whom she had borne to Jacob, went out to visit the daughters of the land.

**34:2** וַיַּ֨רְא אֹתָ֜הּ שְׁכֶ֧ם בֶּן־חֲמ֛וֹר הַֽחִוִּ֖י נְשִׂ֣יא הָאָ֑רֶץ וַיִּקַּ֥ח אֹתָ֛הּ וַיִּשְׁכַּ֥ב אֹתָ֖הּ וַיְעַנֶּֽהָ׃

> When Shechem the son of Hamor the Hivite, the prince of the land, saw her, he took her and lay with her by force.

**34:3** וַתִּדְבַּ֣ק נַפְשׁ֔וֹ בְּדִינָ֖ה בַּֽת־יַעֲקֹ֑ב וַיֶּֽאֱהַב֙ אֶת־הַֽנַּעֲרָ֔ וַיְדַבֵּ֖ר עַל־לֵ֥ב הַֽנַּעֲרָֽ׃

> He was deeply attracted to Dinah the daughter of Jacob, and he loved the girl and spoke tenderly to her.

**34:4** וַיֹּ֣אמֶר שְׁכֶ֔ם אֶל־חֲמ֥וֹר אָבִ֖יו לֵאמֹ֑ר קַֽח־לִ֛י אֶת־הַיַּלְדָּ֥ה הַזֹּ֖את לְאִשָּֽׁה׃

> So Shechem spoke to his father Hamor, saying, “Get me this young girl for a wife.”

**34:5** וְיַעֲקֹ֣ב שָׁמַ֗ע כִּ֤י טִמֵּא֙ אֶת־דִּינָ֣ה בִתּ֔וֹ וּבָנָ֛יו הָי֥וּ אֶת־מִקְנֵ֖הוּ בַּשָּׂדֶ֑ה וְהֶחֱרִ֥שׁ יַעֲקֹ֖ב עַד־בֹּאָֽם׃

> Now Jacob heard that he had defiled Dinah his daughter; but his sons were with his livestock in the field, so Jacob kept silent until they came in.

**34:6** וַיֵּצֵ֛א חֲמ֥וֹר אֲבִֽי־שְׁכֶ֖ם אֶֽל־יַעֲקֹ֑ב לְדַבֵּ֖ר אִתּֽוֹ׃

> Then Hamor the father of Shechem went out to Jacob to speak with him.

**34:7** וּבְנֵ֨י יַעֲקֹ֜ב בָּ֤אוּ מִן־הַשָּׂדֶה֙ כְּשָׁמְעָ֔ם וַיִּֽתְעַצְּבוּ֙ הָֽאֲנָשִׁ֔ים וַיִּ֥חַר לָהֶ֖ם מְאֹ֑ד כִּֽי־נְבָלָ֞ה עָשָׂ֣ה בְיִשְׂרָאֵ֗ל לִשְׁכַּב֙ אֶת־בַּֽת־יַעֲקֹ֔ב וְכֵ֖ן לֹ֥א יֵעָשֶֽׂה׃

> Now the sons of Jacob came in from the field when they heard it; and the men were grieved, and they were very angry because he had done a disgraceful thing in Israel by lying with Jacob’s daughter, for such a thing ought not to be done.

**34:8** וַיְדַבֵּ֥ר חֲמ֖וֹר אִתָּ֣ם לֵאמֹ֑ר שְׁכֶ֣ם בְּנִ֗י חָֽשְׁקָ֤ה נַפְשׁוֹ֙ בְּבִתְּכֶ֔ם תְּנ֨וּ נָ֥א אֹתָ֛הּ ל֖וֹ לְאִשָּֽׁה׃

> But Hamor spoke with them, saying, “The soul of my son Shechem longs for your daughter; please give her to him in marriage.

**34:9** וְהִֽתְחַתְּנ֖וּ אֹתָ֑נוּ בְּנֹֽתֵיכֶם֙ תִּתְּנוּ־לָ֔נוּ וְאֶת־בְּנֹתֵ֖ינוּ תִּקְח֥וּ לָכֶֽם׃

> “Intermarry with us; give your daughters to us and take our daughters for yourselves.

**34:10** וְאִתָּ֖נוּ תֵּשֵׁ֑בוּ וְהָאָ֨רֶץ֙ תִּהְיֶ֣ה לִפְנֵיכֶ֔ם שְׁבוּ֙ וּסְחָר֔וּהָ וְהֵֽאָחֲז֖וּ בָּֽהּ׃

> “Thus you shall live with us, and the land shall be open before you; live and trade in it and acquire property in it.”

**34:11** וַיֹּ֤אמֶר שְׁכֶם֙ אֶל־אָבִ֣יה וְאֶל־אַחֶ֔יהָ אֶמְצָא־חֵ֖ן בְּעֵינֵיכֶ֑ם וַאֲשֶׁ֥ר תֹּאמְר֛וּ אֵלַ֖י אֶתֵּֽן׃

> Shechem also said to her father and to her brothers, “If I find favor in your sight, then I will give whatever you say to me.

**34:12** הַרְבּ֨וּ עָלַ֤י מְאֹד֙ מֹ֣הַר וּמַתָּ֔ן וְאֶ֨תְּנָ֔ה כַּאֲשֶׁ֥ר תֹּאמְר֖וּ אֵלָ֑י וּתְנוּ־לִ֥י אֶת־הַֽנַּעֲרָ֖ לְאִשָּֽׁה׃

> “Ask me ever so much bridal payment and gift, and I will give according as you say to me; but give me the girl in marriage.”

**34:13** וַיַּעֲנ֨וּ בְנֵֽי־יַעֲקֹ֜ב אֶת־שְׁכֶ֨ם וְאֶת־חֲמ֥וֹר אָבִ֛יו בְּמִרְמָ֖ה וַיְדַבֵּ֑רוּ אֲשֶׁ֣ר טִמֵּ֔א אֵ֖ת דִּינָ֥ה אֲחֹתָֽם׃

> But Jacob’s sons answered Shechem and his father Hamor with deceit, because he had defiled Dinah their sister.

**34:14** וַיֹּאמְר֣וּ אֲלֵיהֶ֗ם לֹ֤א נוּכַל֙ לַעֲשׂוֹת֙ הַדָּבָ֣ר הַזֶּ֔ה לָתֵת֙ אֶת־אֲחֹתֵ֔נוּ לְאִ֖ישׁ אֲשֶׁר־ל֣וֹ עָרְלָ֑ה כִּֽי־חֶרְפָּ֥ה הִ֖וא לָֽנוּ׃

> They said to them, “We cannot do this thing, to give our sister to one who is uncircumcised, for that would be a disgrace to us.

**34:15** אַךְ־בְּזֹ֖את נֵא֣וֹת לָכֶ֑ם אִ֚ם תִּהְי֣וּ כָמֹ֔נוּ לְהִמֹּ֥ל לָכֶ֖ם כָּל־זָכָֽר׃

> “Only on this condition will we consent to you: if you will become like us, in that every male of you be circumcised,

**34:16** וְנָתַ֤נּוּ אֶת־בְּנֹתֵ֨ינוּ֙ לָכֶ֔ם וְאֶת־בְּנֹתֵיכֶ֖ם נִֽקַּֽח־לָ֑נוּ וְיָשַׁ֣בְנוּ אִתְּכֶ֔ם וְהָיִ֖ינוּ לְעַ֥ם אֶחָֽד׃

> then we will give our daughters to you, and we will take your daughters for ourselves, and we will live with you and become one people.

**34:17** וְאִם־לֹ֧א תִשְׁמְע֛וּ אֵלֵ֖ינוּ לְהִמּ֑וֹל וְלָקַ֥חְנוּ אֶת־בִּתֵּ֖נוּ וְהָלָֽכְנוּ׃

> “But if you will not listen to us to be circumcised, then we will take our daughter and go.”

**34:18** וַיִּֽיטְב֥וּ דִבְרֵיהֶ֖ם בְּעֵינֵ֣י חֲמ֑וֹר וּבְעֵינֵ֖י שְׁכֶ֥ם בֶּן־חֲמֽוֹר׃

> Now their words seemed reasonable to Hamor and Shechem, Hamor’s son.

**34:19** וְלֹֽא־אֵחַ֤ר הַנַּ֨עַר֙ לַעֲשׂ֣וֹת הַדָּבָ֔ר כִּ֥י חָפֵ֖ץ בְּבַֽת־יַעֲקֹ֑ב וְה֣וּא נִכְבָּ֔ד מִכֹּ֖ל בֵּ֥ית אָבִֽיו׃

> The young man did not delay to do the thing, because he was delighted with Jacob’s daughter. Now he was more respected than all the household of his father.

**34:20** וַיָּבֹ֥א חֲמ֛וֹר וּשְׁכֶ֥ם בְּנ֖וֹ אֶל־שַׁ֣עַר עִירָ֑ם וַֽיְדַבְּר֛וּ אֶל־אַנְשֵׁ֥י עִירָ֖ם לֵאמֹֽר׃

> So Hamor and his son Shechem came to the gate of their city and spoke to the men of their city, saying,

**34:21** הָאֲנָשִׁ֨ים הָאֵ֜לֶּה שְֽׁלֵמִ֧ים הֵ֣ם אִתָּ֗נוּ וְיֵשְׁב֤וּ בָאָ֨רֶץ֙ וְיִסְחֲר֣וּ אֹתָ֔הּ וְהָאָ֛רֶץ הִנֵּ֥ה רַֽחֲבַת־יָדַ֖יִם לִפְנֵיהֶ֑ם אֶת־בְּנֹתָם֙ נִקַּֽח־לָ֣נוּ לְנָשִׁ֔ים וְאֶת־בְּנֹתֵ֖ינוּ נִתֵּ֥ן לָהֶֽם׃

> “These men are friendly with us; therefore let them live in the land and trade in it, for behold, the land is large enough for them. Let us take their daughters in marriage, and give our daughters to them.

**34:22** אַךְ־בְּ֠זֹאת יֵאֹ֨תוּ לָ֤נוּ הָאֲנָשִׁים֙ לָשֶׁ֣בֶת אִתָּ֔נוּ לִהְי֖וֹת לְעַ֣ם אֶחָ֑ד בְּהִמּ֥וֹל לָ֨נוּ֙ כָּל־זָכָ֔ר כַּאֲשֶׁ֖ר הֵ֥ם נִמֹּלִֽים׃

> “Only on this condition will the men consent to us to live with us, to become one people: that every male among us be circumcised as they are circumcised.

**34:23** מִקְנֵהֶ֤ם וְקִנְיָנָם֙ וְכָל־בְּהֶמְתָּ֔ם הֲל֥וֹא לָ֖נוּ הֵ֑ם אַ֚ךְ נֵא֣וֹתָה לָהֶ֔ם וְיֵשְׁב֖וּ אִתָּֽנוּ׃

> “Will not their livestock and their property and all their animals be ours? Only let us consent to them, and they will live with us.”

**34:24** וַיִּשְׁמְע֤וּ אֶל־חֲמוֹר֙ וְאֶל־שְׁכֶ֣ם בְּנ֔וֹ כָּל־יֹצְאֵ֖י שַׁ֣עַר עִיר֑וֹ וַיִּמֹּ֨לוּ֙ כָּל־זָכָ֔ר כָּל־יֹצְאֵ֖י שַׁ֥עַר עִירֽוֹ׃

> All who went out of the gate of his city listened to Hamor and to his son Shechem, and every male was circumcised, all who went out of the gate of his city.

**34:25** וַיְהִי֩ בַיּ֨וֹם הַשְּׁלִישִׁ֜י בִּֽהְיוֹתָ֣ם כֹּֽאֲבִ֗ים וַיִּקְח֣וּ שְׁנֵֽי־בְנֵי־יַ֠עֲקֹב שִׁמְע֨וֹן וְלֵוִ֜י אֲחֵ֤י דִינָה֙ אִ֣ישׁ חַרְבּ֔וֹ וַיָּבֹ֥אוּ עַל־הָעִ֖יר בֶּ֑טַח וַיַּֽהַרְג֖וּ כָּל־זָכָֽר׃

> Now it came about on the third day, when they were in pain, that two of Jacob’s sons, Simeon and Levi, Dinah’s brothers, each took his sword and came upon the city unawares, and killed every male.

**34:26** וְאֶת־חֲמוֹר֙ וְאֶת־שְׁכֶ֣ם בְּנ֔וֹ הָרְג֖וּ לְפִי־חָ֑רֶב וַיִּקְח֧וּ אֶת־דִּינָ֛ה מִבֵּ֥ית שְׁכֶ֖ם וַיֵּצֵֽאוּ׃

> They killed Hamor and his son Shechem with the edge of the sword, and took Dinah from Shechem’s house, and went forth.

**34:27** בְּנֵ֣י יַעֲקֹ֗ב בָּ֚אוּ עַל־הַ֣חֲלָלִ֔ים וַיָּבֹ֖זּוּ הָעִ֑יר אֲשֶׁ֥ר טִמְּא֖וּ אֲחוֹתָֽם׃

> Jacob’s sons came upon the slain and looted the city, because they had defiled their sister.

**34:28** אֶת־צֹאנָ֥ם וְאֶת־בְּקָרָ֖ם וְאֶת־חֲמֹרֵיהֶּ֑ם וְאֵ֧ת אֲשֶׁר־בָּעִ֛יר וְאֶת־אֲשֶׁ֥ר בַּשָּׂדֶ֖ה לָקָֽחוּ׃

> They took their flocks and their herds and their donkeys, and that which was in the city and that which was in the field;

**34:29** וְאֶת־כָּל־חֵילָ֤ם וְאֶת־כָּל־טַפָּם֙ וְאֶת־נְשֵׁיהֶ֔ם שָׁב֖וּ וַיָּבֹ֑זּוּ וְאֵ֖ת כָּל־אֲשֶׁ֥ר בַּבָּֽיִת׃

> and they captured and looted all their wealth and all their little ones and their wives, even all that was in the houses.

**34:30** וַיֹּ֨אמֶר יַעֲקֹ֜ב אֶל־שִׁמְע֣וֹן וְאֶל־לֵוִי֮ עֲכַרְתֶּ֣ם אֹתִי֒ לְהַבְאִישֵׁ֨נִי֙ בְּיֹשֵׁ֣ב הָאָ֔רֶץ בַּֽכְּנַעֲנִ֖י וּבַפְּרִזִּ֑י וַאֲנִי֙ מְתֵ֣י מִסְפָּ֔ר וְנֶאֶסְפ֤וּ עָלַי֙ וְהִכּ֔וּנִי וְנִשְׁמַדְתִּ֖י אֲנִ֥י וּבֵיתִֽי׃

> Then Jacob said to Simeon and Levi, “You have brought trouble on me by making me odious among the inhabitants of the land, among the Canaanites and the Perizzites; and my men being few in number, they will gather together against me and attack me and I will be destroyed, I and my household.”

**34:31** וַיֹּאמְר֑וּ הַכְזוֹנָ֕ה יַעֲשֶׂ֖ה אֶת־אֲחוֹתֵֽנוּ׃ פ

> But they said, “Should he treat our sister as a harlot?”

### 35:1–15 · Bethel again

**35:1** וַיֹּ֤אמֶר אֱלֹהִים֙ אֶֽל־יַעֲקֹ֔ב ק֛וּם עֲלֵ֥ה בֵֽית־אֵ֖ל וְשֶׁב־שָׁ֑ם וַעֲשֵׂה־שָׁ֣ם מִזְבֵּ֔חַ לָאֵל֙ הַנִּרְאֶ֣ה אֵלֶ֔יךָ בְּבָרְחֲךָ֔ מִפְּנֵ֖י עֵשָׂ֥ו אָחִֽיךָ׃

> Then God said to Jacob, “Arise, go up to Bethel and live there, and make an altar there to God, who appeared to you when you fled from your brother Esau.”

**35:2** וַיֹּ֤אמֶר יַעֲקֹב֙ אֶל־בֵּית֔וֹ וְאֶ֖ל כָּל־אֲשֶׁ֣ר עִמּ֑וֹ הָסִ֜רוּ אֶת־אֱלֹהֵ֤י הַנֵּכָר֙ אֲשֶׁ֣ר בְּתֹכְכֶ֔ם וְהִֽטַּהֲר֔וּ וְהַחֲלִ֖יפוּ שִׂמְלֹתֵיכֶֽם׃

> So Jacob said to his household and to all who were with him, “Put away the foreign gods which are among you, and purify yourselves and change your garments;

**35:3** וְנָק֥וּמָה וְנַעֲלֶ֖ה בֵּֽית־אֵ֑ל וְאֶֽעֱשֶׂה־שָּׁ֣ם מִזְבֵּ֗חַ לָאֵ֞ל הָעֹנֶ֤ה אֹתִי֙ בְּי֣וֹם צָֽרָתִ֔י וַיְהִי֙ עִמָּדִ֔י בַּדֶּ֖רֶךְ אֲשֶׁ֥ר הָלָֽכְתִּי׃

> and let us arise and go up to Bethel, and I will make an altar there to God, who answered me in the day of my distress and has been with me wherever I have gone.”

**35:4** וַיִּתְּנ֣וּ אֶֽל־יַעֲקֹ֗ב אֵ֣ת כָּל־אֱלֹהֵ֤י הַנֵּכָר֙ אֲשֶׁ֣ר בְּיָדָ֔ם וְאֶת־הַנְּזָמִ֖ים אֲשֶׁ֣ר בְּאָזְנֵיהֶ֑ם וַיִּטְמֹ֤ן אֹתָם֙ יַעֲקֹ֔ב תַּ֥חַת הָאֵלָ֖ה אֲשֶׁ֥ר עִם־שְׁכֶֽם׃

> So they gave to Jacob all the foreign gods which they had and the rings which were in their ears, and Jacob hid them under the oak which was near Shechem.

**35:5** וַיִּסָּ֑עוּ וַיְהִ֣י ׀ חִתַּ֣ת אֱלֹהִ֗ים עַל־הֶֽעָרִים֙ אֲשֶׁר֙ סְבִיבֹ֣תֵיהֶ֔ם וְלֹ֣א רָֽדְפ֔וּ אַחֲרֵ֖י בְּנֵ֥י יַעֲקֹֽב׃

> As they journeyed, there was a great terror upon the cities which were around them, and they did not pursue the sons of Jacob.

**35:6** וַיָּבֹ֨א יַעֲקֹ֜ב ל֗וּזָה אֲשֶׁר֙ בְּאֶ֣רֶץ כְּנַ֔עַן הִ֖וא בֵּֽית־אֵ֑ל ה֖וּא וְכָל־הָעָ֥ם אֲשֶׁר־עִמּֽוֹ׃

> So Jacob came to Luz (that is, Bethel), which is in the land of Canaan, he and all the people who were with him.

**35:7** וַיִּ֤בֶן שָׁם֙ מִזְבֵּ֔חַ וַיִּקְרָא֙ לַמָּק֔וֹם אֵ֖ל בֵּֽית־אֵ֑ל כִּ֣י שָׁ֗ם נִגְל֤וּ אֵלָיו֙ הָֽאֱלֹהִ֔ים בְּבָרְח֖וֹ מִפְּנֵ֥י אָחִֽיו׃

> He built an altar there, and called the place El-bethel, because there God had revealed Himself to him when he fled from his brother.

**35:8** וַתָּ֤מָת דְּבֹרָה֙ מֵינֶ֣קֶת רִבְקָ֔ה וַתִּקָּבֵ֛ר מִתַּ֥חַת לְבֵֽית־אֵ֖ל תַּ֣חַת הָֽאַלּ֑וֹן וַיִּקְרָ֥א שְׁמ֖וֹ אַלּ֥וֹן בָּכֽוּת׃ פ

> Now Deborah, Rebekah’s nurse, died, and she was buried below Bethel under the oak; it was named Allon-bacuth.

**35:9** וַיֵּרָ֨א אֱלֹהִ֤ים אֶֽל־יַעֲקֹב֙ ע֔וֹד בְּבֹא֖וֹ מִפַּדַּ֣ן אֲרָ֑ם וַיְבָ֖רֶךְ אֹתֽוֹ׃

> Then God appeared to Jacob again when he came from Paddan-aram, and He blessed him.

**35:10** וַיֹּֽאמֶר־ל֥וֹ אֱלֹהִ֖ים שִׁמְךָ֣ יַעֲקֹ֑ב לֹֽא־יִקָּרֵא֩ שִׁמְךָ֨ ע֜וֹד יַעֲקֹ֗ב כִּ֤י אִם־יִשְׂרָאֵל֙ יִהְיֶ֣ה שְׁמֶ֔ךָ וַיִּקְרָ֥א אֶת־שְׁמ֖וֹ יִשְׂרָאֵֽל׃

> God said to him,

**35:11** וַיֹּאמֶר֩ ל֨וֹ אֱלֹהִ֜ים אֲנִ֨י אֵ֤ל שַׁדַּי֙ פְּרֵ֣ה וּרְבֵ֔ה גּ֛וֹי וּקְהַ֥ל גּוֹיִ֖ם יִהְיֶ֣ה מִמֶּ֑ךָּ וּמְלָכִ֖ים מֵחֲלָצֶ֥יךָ יֵצֵֽאוּ׃

> God also said to him,

**35:12** וְאֶת־הָאָ֗רֶץ אֲשֶׁ֥ר נָתַ֛תִּי לְאַבְרָהָ֥ם וּלְיִצְחָ֖ק לְךָ֣ אֶתְּנֶ֑נָּה וּֽלְזַרְעֲךָ֥ אַחֲרֶ֖יךָ אֶתֵּ֥ן אֶת־הָאָֽרֶץ׃

> “The land which I gave to Abraham and Isaac,

**35:13** וַיַּ֥עַל מֵעָלָ֖יו אֱלֹהִ֑ים בַּמָּק֖וֹם אֲשֶׁר־דִּבֶּ֥ר אִתּֽוֹ׃

> Then God went up from him in the place where He had spoken with him.

**35:14** וַיַּצֵּ֨ב יַעֲקֹ֜ב מַצֵּבָ֗ה בַּמָּק֛וֹם אֲשֶׁר־דִּבֶּ֥ר אִתּ֖וֹ מַצֶּ֣בֶת אָ֑בֶן וַיַּסֵּ֤ךְ עָלֶ֨יהָ֙ נֶ֔סֶךְ וַיִּצֹ֥ק עָלֶ֖יהָ שָֽׁמֶן׃

> Jacob set up a pillar in the place where He had spoken with him, a pillar of stone, and he poured out a drink offering on it; he also poured oil on it.

**35:15** וַיִּקְרָ֨א יַעֲקֹ֜ב אֶת־שֵׁ֣ם הַמָּק֗וֹם אֲשֶׁר֩ דִּבֶּ֨ר אִתּ֥וֹ שָׁ֛ם אֱלֹהִ֖ים בֵּֽית־אֵֽל׃

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims, each surfaced by more than one tool and each resting on verified counts:

1. **The reconciliation is told in the language of offering and acceptance, and the face is its leading word.** Jacob plans: "I will cover his face (אֲכַפְּרָה פָנָיו, 'I will appease him') with the gift … afterward I will see his face; perhaps he will lift up my face (יִשָּׂא פָנָי, 'accept me')" (32:21 [Eng 32:20]) — פָּנִים ("face") four times in one verse and sixteen times in 32:2–33:20. At the Jabbok he says "I have seen God face to face (פָּנִים אֶל־פָּנִים) and my life was delivered" (32:31 [Eng 32:30]); the phrase occurs elsewhere only of Moses (Exod 33:11; Deut 34:10), of Gideon (Judg 6:22) and in Ezek 20:35. To Esau he says "I have seen your face as one sees the face of God, and you have received me (וַתִּרְצֵנִי, 'you received me favourably')" (33:10). Three of the words are cultic: מִנְחָה ("gift, offering", 32:14, 19, 21, 22; 33:10); כִּפֶּר ("cover, appease, atone"), whose first piel in the canon is 32:21; and רָצָה ("accept"), in Genesis only at 33:10 and next at Lev 1:4, of an offering accepted. Jacob's "perhaps I may atone" — אוּלַי ("perhaps") with אֲכַפְּרָה ("I may atone") — is said in that form again only by Moses after the calf (Exod 32:30). In Genesis מִנְחָה ("gift, offering") is used only of Cain's and Abel's offerings (4:3–5), of Jacob's gift to Esau, and of the brothers' gift to Joseph (43:11–26): **three brother-conflicts, each with a gift and a face**. `[S: audit 2]` *Surfaced by:* Tools 7, 10, 11, Original Languages, Schnittjer step 4; claim audit 2 (G14, G23). *High on the words; the three-member chain moderate.* `[T]` / `[I]` **v1.0 read 32–33 as a reversal of Cain and Abel in particular; the audit found the two passages no closer than chance at block level, with Gen 43 an equal partner — that reading is withdrawn (low–moderate).** `[S: audit 2]`

2. **Jacob gives back the blessing he stole.** "Take my blessing (בִּרְכָתִי, 'my blessing')", Jacob says to Esau (33:11). The noun with that suffix occurs in Genesis only twice — here, and in Esau's cry "now he has taken my blessing" (27:36). Around it, the stolen blessing of 27:29 — "be lord over your brothers, and may your mother's sons bow down to you" — is reversed point by point: Jacob bows to the ground seven times (33:3), his wives and children bow (33:6–7), and he calls Esau אֲדֹנִי ("my lord") eight times and himself "your servant" five times (32:5, 6, 19; 33:5, 8, 13, 14, 15). The night before, the man at the Jabbok "blessed him there" (32:30) after Jacob refused to let him go without it (32:27). He receives a blessing from God, and gives the stolen one back to his brother. *Surfaced by:* Tools 7, 10, 11, 8 (the NASB95 levels "blessing" to "gift"). *High.* `[T]`

3. **The prayer is answered in its own words, and the vow's condition is confessed met.** "Deliver me (הַצִּילֵנִי, 'deliver me'), please, from the hand of my brother" (32:12 [Eng 32:11]) — "I have seen God face to face, and my life was delivered (וַתִּנָּצֵל, 'was delivered')" (32:31). The covenant name occurs **once** in the 112 verses, in that prayer (32:10 [Eng 32:9]). At Bethel Jacob calls God "the God who answered me in the day of my distress and was with me in the way that I went" (35:3), the words of his own conditional vow — "if God will be with me … in this way that I go" (28:20, a phrase found only there); and Ps 20:2 [Eng 20:1] prays "may the LORD answer you in the day of distress; may the name of the God of Jacob set you securely on high" — "answer" + "day" + "distress" in one verse only Gen 35:3; Ps 20:2; 86:7, and in the Greek ἐπακούω ("answer") + ἡμέρα θλίψεως ("day of distress") only Gen 35:3 and Ps 19:2 [Heb 20:2]. `[S: audit 2]` *Surfaced by:* Tools 2, 7, 10, 11; claim audit 2 (G21). *High (32:12 → 32:31; 28:20 → 35:3); moderate–high (Ps 20).* `[T]`

4. **Shechem is the chapter without God, and it brings the language of Israel's later scandals into the canon.** Genesis 34 is the only chapter of 25–35 in which no divine name occurs (31 verses). The purity vocabulary enters the Bible here: טָמֵא ("defile") first at 34:5 (34:5, 13, 27), and טָהֵר ("purify") first at 35:2. The covenant sign of ch. 17 is used as a ruse; the brothers answer "with deceit" (בְּמִרְמָה, 34:13; cf. 27:35). The rest of the vocabulary is that of later outrages: "folly in Israel" (נְבָלָה … בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל, 34:7; Deut 22:21; Josh 7:15; Judg 20:6); "spoke to her heart" (34:3; Judg 19:3, the Levite and his concubine); "you have brought trouble on me" (עֲכַרְתֶּם, 34:30 — in Genesis only here; Josh 7:25, Achan). And 35:2's "put away the foreign gods that are among you" is said in exactly that form again only at Shechem by Joshua (Josh 24:23) and by Samuel (1 Sam 7:3). The narrator gives the brothers the last word (34:31) and defers the verdict to 49:5–7. **How far the later texts draw on Gen 34 differs by text:** Gen 34 is the closest Genesis partner of the virginity laws (Deut 22:13–29) and of Achan (Josh 7); for the Levite's concubine (Judg 19–20) the closer Genesis source is Sodom (Gen 19), and for Amnon and Tamar (2 Sam 13) it is Judah and Tamar (Gen 37–38), so there Gen 34 is one source among several. Add "such a thing is not done" (only Gen 29:26; 34:7; 2 Sam 13:12) and מֹהַר ("bride-price", only Gen 34:12; Exod 22:16; 1 Sam 18:25). `[S: audit 2]` ⟨synthetic⟩ *Surfaced by:* Tools 6, 7, 10, 11, Difficult Verses; claim audit 2 (G16, G26). *High on the words; moderate on the design.* `[T]`

5. **The *toledot* of Isaac closes with the house completed, the name confirmed and the promise re-issued — and with a road to Bethlehem.** God at Bethel: "Your name shall no longer be called Jacob… Israel shall be your name" (35:10), then "I am El Shaddai; be fruitful and multiply (פְּרֵה וּרְבֵה, singular)" (35:11) — the only singular imperative of that pair in the Hebrew Bible — with "kings shall come from your loins" (cf. 17:6, 16). Jacob pours the canon's first drink offering, נֶסֶךְ ("drink offering", 35:14). Rachel dies giving birth "on the way to Ephrath, that is, Bethlehem" (35:19), and Israel pitches his tent beyond מִגְדַּל־עֵדֶר ("the tower of the flock", 35:21) — a name found elsewhere only at Mic 4:8, which goes on to a woman in labour (4:9–10), "Bethlehem Ephrathah… from you one will go forth for Me to be ruler in Israel" (5:1 [Eng 5:2]) and "until she who is in labour has given birth" (5:2) — the text Matthew quotes at 2:6. The two scenes share a reversed frame rather than one sequence: the tower closes Genesis' scene and opens Micah's; and the combination of tower, labour and Bethlehem Ephrathah occurs only in these two texts. `[S: audit 2]` Isaac is buried by "Esau and Jacob his sons" (35:29), as Abraham was by "Isaac and Ishmael his sons" (25:9). ⟨synthetic⟩ *Surfaced by:* Tools 7, 11, Christological Reading, Schnittjer step 6; claim audit 2 (G15). *High on the words; moderate–high on the Micah link.* `[T]` / `[I]`

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book level.** Genesis asks whether the blessing can outrun the curse and through whom, and answers by narrowing to one seed kept alive through the family's own failures. `[S: overview]` This unit is where the seed-bearer is renamed and the house completed: the twelve are listed for the first time (35:22b–26), and the generation of Isaac ends (35:29).

**Passage level.** The unit does four things:

- **It reconciles the brothers** (32:4–33:17) — the debt of 27 is paid back in bows, gifts and the word "blessing". `[T]`
- **It renames the man** (32:23–33; 35:9–15) — the grasper is blessed by God after being wounded by him, and the new name is given twice. `[T]`
- **It shows what the renamed family is like** (34) — rape, deceit, massacre, plunder, and a father who speaks of his reputation. `[T]`
- **It closes the generation** (35) — Bethel revisited, the foreign gods buried, the promise renewed, Rachel and Isaac dead, the twelve counted. `[T]`

Put together: **God makes Jacob Israel, and Israel is still Jacob.** The name is a gift given at the point of greatest weakness, and the family that bears it is not yet fit to bear it. The author's purpose is to show God keeping his Bethel word — "I will be with you … I will bring you back to this land" (28:15) — and to show that the keeping is entirely his. `[I]`

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement.** 28:15 promised presence and return; 28:20–22 made Jacob's loyalty conditional on both. 31:3 commanded the return; 31:13 recalled the vow; 31:44–32:1 settled Laban. What remains outstanding is **Esau** (27:41–45: "I will kill my brother Jacob") and **the vow** ("this stone … shall be God's house", 28:22). `[T]`

**Necessity answer.** This unit exists here because the two outstanding debts must be met before Jacob can be called home: the brother he cheated, and the God he bargained with. It meets Esau first (32–33), and only then Bethel (35:1–15) — and it puts the night with God *between* the message to Esau and the meeting with him. The order is the argument: Jacob cannot face Esau until he has faced God, and the face of Esau looks to him like the face of God (33:10). `[T]` / `[I]`

**Implication.** The Jabbok is not a free-standing spiritual episode. It is set — in Leningrad without even a paragraph break — inside the approach to Esau, and its meaning is measured by what follows: a man who has seen God's face can bow seven times to his brother. Chapter 34 then shows that the renaming did not remake the household, and 35 brings the whole family to Bethel, purified, to pay the vow.

**Revisit after Tool 11.** Move 4 sharpens it further: the brothers' story began in 4:3–8 (offering, face, brother, field) and is answered here (offering, face, brother, embrace); and the debt-language of 27 — "my blessing", "lord", "bow" — is turned back in 33. The unit exists to close the two oldest wounds in the book.

#### Immediate context

**Immediately before.** Laban's farewell blessing and departure (32:1 [Eng 31:55]). **Immediately after.** "These are the generations of Esau" (36:1) — the brother's book, honestly recorded and closed off, as the non-elect line always is before the elect line resumes (37:2). `[S: overview, T]`

**Section.** The second half of the *toledot* of Isaac (25:19–35:29); the sweep's §12b. The overview divides at 33:20 and runs 34:1–36:43 together; this run follows the *toledot* seam at 36:1 and Leningrad's *petuchah* after 35:29. `[S]`

**What Jacob fails to see.** At the Jabbok Jacob asks the man's name and is refused (32:30). He understands only at daybreak what has happened ("I have seen God", 32:31). At Shechem he "kept silent" (הֶחֱרִישׁ, 34:5) and, when he speaks, speaks of his reputation, not of his daughter (34:30). The content of the blindness is constant: Jacob measures events by his own safety. `[T]` / `[I]`

### 3. Structure

Derived from the Hebrew before any secondary source was opened:

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **A** 32:2–3 [Eng 32:1–2] | The angels of God meet him; Mahanaim, "two camps" | The frame from 28:12; God's camp |
| **B** 32:4–22 [Eng 32:3–21] | Messengers, fear, two camps, prayer, gifts | Jacob's strategy, and a prayer inside it |
| **C** 32:23–33 [Eng 32:22–32] | The Jabbok: wrestling, blessing, the new name, Peniel, the limp | The turn |
| **B′** 33:1–17 | The meeting: bows, embrace, "take my blessing", parting | Strategy overtaken by grace |
| **D** 33:18–34:31 | Shechem: a plot bought, an altar; Dinah, deceit and massacre | The renamed family in the land |
| **E** 35:1–15 | Bethel: foreign gods buried, altar, name confirmed, promise, pillar | The vow paid |
| **F** 35:16–29 | Rachel's death; Reuben; the twelve; Isaac's death | The generation closed |

**What carries the shape.** `[T]` throughout:
- **Faces** cluster in B–C–B′: sixteen occurrences of פָּנִים ("face") in 32:2–33:20, with Peniel/Penuel at the centre.
- **Camps.** מַחֲנֶה ("camp") seven times in six verses (32:3, 8, 9 ×2, 11, 22; 33:8). The angels' camp (32:3) frames Jacob's two camps (32:8, 11).
- **Crossing.** עָבַר ("cross, pass over") nine times in eight verses (32:11, 17, 22, 23, 24 ×2, 32; 33:3, 14), with the place-name יַבֹּק ("Jabbok") and the hapax אָבַק ("wrestle") at the centre.
- **Altars and pillars.** An altar at Shechem (33:20), an altar at Bethel (35:7), a pillar at Bethel (35:14), a pillar on Rachel's grave (35:20).
- **Two namings by God.** The name *Israel* is given at the Jabbok (32:29) and again at Bethel (35:10).

**The Leningrad paragraphing** (Extent note) agrees with the run on two points and differs on one: it closes a paragraph after 33:17 and after 34:31, as the table does, but it does **not** set off the Jabbok — 32:4–33:17 is one paragraph. The night with God is, in that witness, an episode *within* the approach to Esau. `[unchecked — apparatus spread]` *Moderate.*

**Checking stage.** The sweep's §12b calls 32–35 "a chiasm of encounters" with the Jabbok "at the turn". `[S: sweep]` The run's table agrees that the Jabbok is the turn of 32–33, but does not find a chiasm across 32–35: chapters 34 and 35 have no counterpart before the Jabbok. A difference of description, not a correction.

### 4. Linking Words

- **כִּי ("for, because") in Jacob's prayer** (32:10–13): "*for* with my staff only I crossed this Jordan" (32:11); "*for* I fear him" (32:12). Then the adversative וְאַתָּה ("but you", 32:13): "*but you* said, 'I will surely do you good'". The prayer turns from Jacob's fear to God's word. `[T]`
- **כִּי אִם ("unless", 32:27)**: "I will not let you go *unless* you bless me." And **כִּי אִם ("but rather", 32:29)**: "not Jacob, *but* Israel". The same two words give the demand and the gift. `[T]`
- **כִּי עַל־כֵּן ("for therefore", 33:10)**: "take my gift … *for therefore* I have seen your face as one sees the face of God". The logic is that seeing Esau's face is like seeing God's — so the gift is an offering. `[T]` / `[I]`
- **עַל־כֵּן ("therefore") in the narrator's voice** (32:33): "*therefore*, to this day, the sons of Israel do not eat the sinew of the hip" — one of the book's narratorial "to this day" asides. `[T]`
- **וְ ("and, but") + subject at 34:5, 7**: "*now* Jacob heard … *but* the sons of Jacob came in from the field" — two responses set side by side: silence and anger. `[T]`

### 5. Parallels

Prose narrative. Two embedded verse-like units:
- **32:29** — the naming: "Not Jacob shall your name be called any more / but Israel; / for you have striven with God and with men / and have prevailed." `[T]`
- **35:11–12** — the promise in parallel members: "be fruitful and multiply; / a nation and a company of nations shall come from you, / and kings shall come forth from your loins; / and the land which I gave to Abraham and Isaac, / I will give it to you, / and to your seed after you I will give the land." `[T]`

**The pattern.** The narrator is at his most restrained at the two moral catastrophes (34; 35:22) and most expansive at the theophanies (32:25–33; 35:9–15). He calls the man *Israel* for the first time in his own voice only at 35:21–22 — in the sentence that reports Reuben's act. `[T]` (Lemma check: יִשְׂרָאֵל ("Israel") in the unit at 32:29, 33; 33:20; 34:7; 35:10 ×2, 21, 22 ×2; the narrator's own use of it as the man's name begins at 35:21.) *High on the data; moderate on the reading.*

### 8. Translations

**NASB95 against the ESV and the KJV, where they diverge in a way that bears on a finding.**
- **בִּרְכָתִי (33:11).** NASB95 "take my **gift**"; ESV "accept my **blessing**"; KJV "take … my **blessing**". The NASB95 loses the only other "my blessing" in Genesis (27:36); a hearer of the ESV can hear Headline 2. *High.*
- **וַתִּנָּצֵל (32:31 [Eng 32:30]).** NASB95 "my life has been **preserved**"; ESV "my life has been **delivered**"; KJV "preserved". With the NASB95's and the ESV's "deliver me" at 32:12 [Eng 32:11], only the ESV lets the answer to the prayer be heard (Headline 3). *High.*
- **אֲכַפְּרָה פָנָיו … יִשָּׂא פָנָי (32:21 [Eng 32:20]).** All three: "appease him … accept me". The four "faces" of the verse are reduced to one in every English version ("see his face"). *High (translation loss).*
- **שָׂרִיתָ (32:29 [Eng 32:28]).** NASB95, ESV "striven"; KJV "as a prince hast thou power" — an older guess linking שָׂרָה ("strive") to שַׂר ("prince"). *Moderate.*
- **וַיְעַנֶּהָ (34:2).** NASB95 "lay with her **by force**"; ESV "lay with her and **humiliated** her"; KJV "**defiled** her". The verb is the one used of the 400 years (15:13), of Sarai's treatment of Hagar (16:6) and of Laban's condition (31:50, NASB95 "mistreat"). The NASB95 folds the verb into an adverb; the ESV keeps it as a verb; none lets the hearer catch Laban's "if you afflict my daughters". *Moderate.*
- **בַּדֶּרֶךְ אֲשֶׁר הָלָכְתִּי (35:3).** NASB95 and ESV "wherever I have gone". At 28:20 the NASB95 has "on this journey that I take", the ESV "in this way that I go". Both versions flatten the echo of the vow in 35:3; the Hebrew repeats דֶּרֶךְ ("way") and הָלַךְ ("go"). *Moderate–high (translation loss).*
- **נְבָלָה (34:7).** NASB95 "a disgraceful thing"; ESV "an outrageous thing"; KJV "folly". The KJV keeps the word that recurs in Deut 22:21; Josh 7:15; Judg 20:6; 2 Sam 13:12. *Moderate.*
- **35:11 פְּרֵה וּרְבֵה ("be fruitful and multiply").** All three, "be fruitful and multiply", lose the singular. *Low (translation loss).*

### 9. Tone and Feel

**Dread, then wonder, then relief; then horror; then grief and order.** Chapter 32 runs on fear — "greatly afraid and distressed" (32:8), the prayer (32:10–13), the elaborate gifts arranged in droves with spaces between (32:15–22). The night is mystery: an unnamed man, a struggle until dawn, a wound, a demand, a blessing, a refusal to give a name. The meeting is unexpectedly tender: Esau *runs*, embraces, falls on his neck, kisses, and they weep (33:4). Chapter 34 is the darkest chapter in the Jacob cycle and its tone is cold — the narration of the massacre and plunder is a list (34:25–29). Chapter 35 is ritual and funeral: purification, altar, pillar, oil, a nurse's grave, a mother dying in labour, a father's death "old and full of days". *Soundtrack:* a single drum through the night; strings at the embrace; silence for Shechem; a slow march to Hebron.

**For the preacher.** The joy of 33 must not be allowed to drown 34. The text puts them side by side.

#### The reverse table — where this passage is heard downstream

**Hos 12:4–5 [Eng 12:3–4] — the prophets' reading** *(high)*
*Source context:* Hosea's indictment of Ephraim: "In the womb he took his brother by the heel, and in his strength he strove (שָׂרָה) with God; he strove with the angel and prevailed (וַיֻּכָל); he wept and sought his favour (וַיִּתְחַנֶּן־לוֹ); at Bethel he found Him, and there He spoke with us." `[T]`
*Book usage:* שָׂרָה ("strive") occurs only at Gen 32:29 and Hos 12:4. `[T]`
*What it adds:* Hosea calls the man an angel, puts weeping and pleading into Jacob's mouth (Genesis gives the weeping to both brothers at 33:4 and the plea for favour to Jacob before Esau), and joins the Jabbok to Bethel (35:1–15) as one story. The prophet reads 32–35 as a single movement. `[I]`

**Exod 33:11, 20; Deut 34:10; Judg 6:22 — face to face** *(high)*
"Face to face" (פָּנִים אֶל־פָּנִים) is said of Moses (Exod 33:11; Deut 34:10) and by Gideon, who fears he will die (Judg 6:22); and God tells Moses "no man can see Me and live" (Exod 33:20). Jacob saw and lived (32:31). `[T]` To a reader with Exodus, Peniel is the exception the law of the face allows only to Moses. `[I]`

**Lev 1:4; 2:1 — acceptance and oil** *(moderate–high)*
רָצָה ("accept") next occurs at Lev 1:4 ("it shall be accepted for him"); "he poured oil on it" (וַיִּצֹק עָלֶיהָ שֶׁמֶן, 35:14) is next said of the grain offering, the מִנְחָה (Lev 2:1). `[T]` The patriarch's acts at Peniel, before Esau and at Bethel are written in the vocabulary of the later sacrificial system. `[I]`

**Deut 7:1–3; Josh 9 — the Hivites and intermarriage** *(moderate–high)*
Hamor proposes "give your daughters to us and take our daughters for yourselves" (34:9). Deuteronomy forbids exactly this with the nations of the land, the Hivites named among them: "you shall not intermarry with them; you shall not give your daughter to his son, nor take his daughter for your son" (Deut 7:1–3). In Joshua the Hivites of Gibeon deceive Israel into a covenant (Josh 9:7); here Israel's sons deceive the Hivites of Shechem. `[T]` / `[I]`

**Josh 7; Judg 19–20; 2 Sam 13 — the language of scandal** *(high on the words)*
"Folly in Israel" (נְבָלָה בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל): Gen 34:7; Deut 22:21; Josh 7:15; Judg 20:6; Jer 29:23. "You have brought trouble on me" (עָכַר, "trouble", 34:30) — Josh 7:25, of Achan, whose "folly in Israel" (7:15) is the other half of the pair. "Spoke to her heart" — Judg 19:3, the Levite to his concubine, whose rape is called "folly in Israel" (20:6). And Tamar to Amnon: "do not afflict me (אַל־תְּעַנֵּנִי, 'do not humble me'), for such a thing is not done in Israel; do not do this folly" (2 Sam 13:12) — the verb of 34:2, the clause of 34:7 ("such a thing ought not to be done") and the noun נְבָלָה ("folly") in one sentence. `[T]` The Dinah chapter is the first of Israel's sexual scandals, and the later ones are told in its words. `[I]`

**Josh 24:23, 26, 32; 1 Sam 7:3 — Shechem and the foreign gods** *(high)*
"Put away the foreign gods that are among you" (35:2) — Josh 24:23 (at Shechem) and 1 Sam 7:3 only. Joshua sets up a stone "under the oak" at Shechem (Josh 24:26; cf. 35:4, "under the oak which was near Shechem"). Joseph's bones are buried "in the piece of ground which Jacob bought from the sons of Hamor the father of Shechem for a hundred *qəśîṭāh*" (Josh 24:32 = Gen 33:19). `[T]` The covenant renewal at the end of the conquest happens where Jacob buried the gods. `[I]`

**Ps 20:2 [Eng 20:1] — the God of Jacob answers** *(moderate–high)*
"May the LORD answer you in the day of distress; may the name of the God of Jacob set you securely on high." עָנָה ("answer") + יוֹם ("day") + צָרָה ("distress") in one verse: Gen 35:3; Deut 31:21; Ps 20:2; 86:7. `[T]` The royal psalm prays for the king in Jacob's own words at Bethel. `[I]`

**Mic 4:8–5:2 [Eng 4:8–5:3] → Matt 2:6, 18 — the tower of the flock and Bethlehem** *(high on the words; moderate–high on the design)*
*Source context:* "And you, tower of the flock (מִגְדַּל־עֵדֶר), hill of the daughter of Zion, to you it will come — the former dominion will come" (Mic 4:8); "agony has gripped you like a woman in childbirth … writhe and labour" (4:9–10); "But as for you, Bethlehem Ephrathah, too little to be among the clans of Judah, from you One will go forth for Me to be ruler in Israel" (5:1 [Eng 5:2]); "until the time when she who is in labour has borne a child" (5:2 [Eng 5:3]). `[T]`
*Book usage:* מִגְדַּל־עֵדֶר ("tower of the flock") occurs only at Gen 35:21 and Mic 4:8; Ephrathah with Bethlehem at Gen 35:19; 48:7; Ruth 4:11; Mic 5:1. `[T]`
*OT-to-OT:* Rachel's labour (35:16–17), her death "on the way to Ephrath, that is, Bethlehem" (35:19), and the tower of the flock (35:21) stand in Genesis in the same order as Micah's tower, labour and Bethlehem. Micah's ruler is "from Bethlehem Ephrathah"; Matthew quotes Mic 5:1 at 2:6, and Jer 31:15's Rachel at 2:18. `[T]` / `[I]`
*What it adds:* the road on which Rachel dies giving birth becomes, in the prophets, the road to the ruler's birth — and in Matthew both are read at the birth of Jesus. **Caution:** the LXX of Genesis moves the tower before 35:16 (Tool 8), so the sequence is a fact about the Hebrew. `[I]`

**Luke 15:20 — the running father** *(moderate–high)*
SBLGNT: δραμὼν ἐπέπεσεν ἐπὶ τὸν τράχηλον αὐτοῦ καὶ κατεφίλησεν αὐτόν ("running, he fell on his neck and kissed him"). **John 1:47 — an Israelite without deceit** *(moderate–high)*

**Ruth 4:11; Jer 31:15** — see the 29:1–31:55 dig. Rachel's grave on the Bethlehem road (35:19–20) is where both texts stand.

### 12. Genre

Hebrew patriarchal narrative, with embedded forms: a **prayer** (32:10–13 — a petition that pleads God's own earlier words back to him: "You who said to me", 32:10, and "You said", 32:13), a **theophany with naming** (32:25–31; 35:9–15), an **etiology** of a food custom (32:33) and of place-names (Mahanaim, Peniel, Succoth, El-Bethel, Allon-bacuth), a **genealogical list** (35:22b–26) and a **death notice** (35:28–29). The night struggle has no stock type-scene in the canon; it is unique. **Read as history told for theology.** Avoid reading 32:25–31 as allegory of the inner life alone; the text insists on a limp and a dietary custom (32:32–33).

### 14. Bible Timeline

**Where the passage sits.** The return to the land; the renaming of the patriarch; the first notice of the twelve as twelve (35:22b). `[T]`

**Where we are.** After the exodus Hosea joins to this story, after the conquest that renewed the covenant at Shechem, after Bethlehem and the birth that Micah promised, after the cross. `[I]`

**Canonical position.** Torah, first book; Genesis presupposes nothing. `[S: overview]` Every later echo traced here — Hosea, Joshua, Judges, Samuel, Micah, Psalms, Luke, John — reads Genesis; none is read by it.

**Between then and now.** The man who saw God face to face and lived (32:31) points to the one who has "made Him known" (John 1:18); the angels of God at Mahanaim (32:2–3), as at Bethel (28:12), to the angels ascending and descending on the Son of Man (John 1:51); the twelve sons to the twelve apostles (Matt 19:28; Rev 21:12–14). `[I]`

### 15. Who Am I?

- **Jacob** — not the model wrestler but the man who is wounded into blessing. We identify with him as the one who receives a new name he did not earn and still limps. `[I]`
- **Esau** — the non-elect brother who forgives first. A reader is allowed to admire him; Hebrews' judgement of Esau (Heb 12:16–17) concerns the birthright, not this embrace. `[I]` / `[S]`
- **The man at the Jabbok** — God (32:31; Hos 12:5 "the angel"); see Christological Reading.
- **Dinah** — a victim, silent throughout; never quoted. `[T]` Not a cautionary tale for women who "go out" (Pitfalls).
- **Simeon and Levi** — zealous, violent, deceitful; Jacob's curse (49:5–7) is the verdict. Negative examples. `[T]`
- **Rachel** — the mother who dies giving the son she asked for. `[T]`
- **Isaac** — dies "old and full of days", reconciled sons at his grave. `[T]`

### 16. So What?

**Stage 1 — the author's intended response.** To trust the God who keeps his word to the undeserving — who meets the fearful man in the night, wounds and blesses him, names him anew, protects his family when it disgraces him, and brings him back to the place of promise — and, having been met by God, to go and bow to the brother one has wronged. `[I]`

**Stage 2.**
- *Worldview:* reconciliation with God and reconciliation with the brother are told as one story; the face of the brother can look like the face of God (33:10). And renaming does not mean instant sanctification (34).
- *Stop:* managing God and people by gifts and strategies alone (32:14–22); speaking of a wrong done to another only in terms of one's own reputation (34:30); clinging to household gods (35:2).
- *Start:* praying as Jacob prayed (32:10–13) — "I am unworthy of all the steadfast love and faithfulness You have shown"; returning what one took ("take my blessing"); purifying before worship (35:2).
- *Motive:* "God has dealt graciously with me, and I have everything" (33:11).

**Four audiences.**
- *For me:* what have I taken that I should give back?
- *For a Christian friend:* the estranged sibling; the person afraid of a meeting they have long avoided.
- *For the church:* a congregation that has been renamed and still behaves like Shechem; purification and honesty before God together.
- *For an unbeliever:* the God of the Bible meets a liar in the dark and blesses him.

**Prayer.** *Lord God of Abraham and Isaac, we are unworthy of all your steadfast love and faithfulness. Deliver us from the fear of our brothers, and from our own deceit. Meet us in the night; give us the name you choose; and send us, limping, to give back what we have taken.*

### Textual Variants

**33:4 — the dotted kiss.**
- *The issue:* Leningrad prints dots over every letter of וַיִּשָּׁקֵהוּ ("and he kissed him") — one of the fifteen *puncta extraordinaria* of the Masoretic tradition. The same verse has a *qere/ketiv*: צַוָּארָו ("his neck") is written with the plural suffix, read as singular ("his neck").
- *Evidence:* BHS's note on the dots, "perhaps to be deleted, cf. the extraordinary points". The Greek has ἐφίλησεν ("kissed") without hesitation. `[S: BHS apparatus; Rahlfs export]` Rabbinic interpretation read the dots as a comment on Esau's sincerity — either that he kissed with his whole heart or that he did not. `[S — recalled, unverified]`
- *Position:* the kiss is in every witness; the dots mark a scribal doubt of some kind, whose meaning is not recoverable from the manuscript. *Moderate.*
- *Preaching implication:* do not build on the dots. The text as it stands — Esau ran, embraced, fell on his neck, kissed, wept — is the reconciliation.

**35:22 — Reuben, the gap and the Greek.**
- *The issue:* Leningrad has a *petuchah* in the middle of the verse, after "and Israel heard", and the accents of 35:22 are given in two systems (BHS prints the double accentuation, so that the verse can be read as one or as two). Rahlfs adds καὶ πονηρὸν ἐφάνη ἐναντίον αὐτοῦ ("and it appeared evil before him").
- *Evidence:* BHS notes the Greek plus with a reference to the gap in the MT. `[S: BHS apparatus]` The mid-verse *petuchah* is visible in the corpus; its spread across manuscripts is not. `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`
- *Position:* the MT's silence is the harder text, and the Greek reads as a filling of the gap. The Masoretic layout marks the silence rather than hiding it. *Moderate.*
- *Preaching implication:* Jacob's response is deferred to 49:3–4, as at 34 it is deferred to 49:5–7. That is the narrator's pattern in this unit, in every witness.

**35:7 — "God revealed himself" (plural).**
- *The issue:* MT נִגְלוּ אֵלָיו הָאֱלֹהִים ("God [lit. "they"] had been revealed to him"), a plural verb with Elohim; the Samaritan, LXX, Syriac, Targum and Vulgate have the singular.
- *Evidence:* BHS notes the versions. `[S: BHS apparatus]` The same construction occurs at 20:13 ("when God caused me to wander", plural). `[T]`
- *Position:* the plural is the harder reading and very probably original; it may reflect the angels of 28:12 or simply the plural form. *Moderate–high.*
- *Preaching implication:* none for the sense.

**35:4 — the gods destroyed.**
- *The issue:* Rahlfs adds καὶ ἀπώλεσεν αὐτὰ ἕως τῆς σήμερον ἡμέρας ("and destroyed them to this day") after the burial under the oak.
- *Position:* a Greek expansion; the MT's burial is the shorter text. *Moderate–high.*

**35:11 — "I am El Shaddai".**
- *The issue:* Rahlfs ἐγὼ ὁ θεός σου ("I am your God") for אֲנִי אֵל שַׁדַּי ("I am El Shaddai"), as elsewhere in Genesis the Greek renders Shaddai variously.
- *Position:* the MT stands; the link to 17:1 and 28:3 is a fact about the Hebrew. *High.*

**33:18 — "safely" or "Salem".** See Tool 8. *Moderate.*

**33:20 — "altar".** BHS proposes מַצֵּבָה ("pillar") for מִזְבֵּחַ ("altar"), on the ground that one "sets up" (הִצִּיב) a pillar rather than an altar. `[S: BHS apparatus]` No witness reads "pillar". The MT stands. *Moderate–high.*

**35:16, 21 — the tower's position.** See Tool 8. *Moderate.*

**Divine names.** The single יְהוָה ("the LORD") of the unit (32:10) and the absence of any divine name in 34 are facts about Leningrad. *High (the data); the Greek and Samaritan distribution not checked here.*

**Paragraphing (32:4–33:17).** Leningrad's single paragraph across the Jabbok. `[unchecked — apparatus spread]` (Tool 3; Open Questions.)

### Historical and Cultural Background

#### Setting

- **Geography.** Gilead and the Jabbok (Nahr ez-Zarqa) east of the Jordan; Mahanaim, Peniel/Penuel and Succoth are all on or near the Jabbok and reappear as Israelite towns (Josh 13:26–27; Judg 8:5–17; 2 Sam 2:8; 17:24). Shechem in the pass between Ebal and Gerizim; Bethel some thirty kilometres south; Ephrath/Bethlehem; Hebron. `[S — standard geography, unverified here]`
- **The gift.** Five hundred and fifty animals in five droves (32:15–16), with gaps between them so that each servant gives the same speech (32:17–21). A ruler's tribute in size. `[T]` / `[I]`
- **The *qəśîṭāh*.** An unknown unit of value, probably a weight of silver; it occurs only here, Josh 24:32 and Job 42:11. `[T]` / `[S]`

#### What the original audience knew

1. **The places were Israelite places.** Penuel's tower (Judg 8:17), Succoth's elders (Judg 8:14–16), Mahanaim as Ish-bosheth's and David's refuge (2 Sam 2:8; 17:24), Shechem as the place of covenant renewal and of the kingdom's division (Josh 24; 1 Kgs 12:1), Bethel as Jeroboam's sanctuary (1 Kgs 12:29). Every place in the unit had a later history the hearer knew. `[T]` / `[I]`
2. **Edom was a real neighbour and often an enemy** (Num 20:14–21; Deut 2:4–8; 23:7, "you shall not detest an Edomite, for he is your brother"). The reconciliation of 33 stands behind Deuteronomy's command. `[T]` / `[I]`
3. **The law on intermarriage, circumcision and purity** (Deut 7:1–3; Exod 12:48; Lev 11–15). An Israelite heard ch. 34 with the law of the covenant sign and of the nations in mind. `[I]`
4. **Simeon and Levi's later histories.** Simeon is absent from the blessing of Moses (Deut 33) and absorbed into Judah (Josh 19:1–9); Levi is scattered among the tribes (Josh 21) but given the priesthood after another act of zeal (Exod 32:26–29; Num 25:7–13). Jacob's "I will scatter them in Israel" (49:7) was fulfilled in two opposite ways. `[T]` / `[I]`

#### What this changes about how we read

The unit reads like the founding narrative of a land: the places the tribes would hold are named in it, and the scandals of later Israel are foreshadowed in it. The Torah's first audience, about to enter the land at Shechem (Deut 11:29–30; 27:4–13), heard of the first Israelite to enter it there.

### Original Audience Reception

#### Canonical audience

Israel in the plains of Moab, renamed and still fractious, about to cross the Jordan and face the peoples of the land (Deut 1–11). `[S: overview]`

#### The first hearers

They heard that their father crossed the Jordan with a staff (32:11) and came back two camps; that he met God at a river crossing on his way into the land; that he was afraid of a brother whose land they had just been forbidden to take (Deut 2:4–5); that his sons made a treaty with Hivites by deceit and broke it with violence; and that God protected them anyway with a "terror" on the cities (35:5; cf. Exod 23:27, "I will send my terror before you"). `[T]` / `[I]`

#### Where the passage fits their world

- **The crossing.** Jacob crosses the Jabbok; Israel will cross the Jordan (Josh 3–4), set up stones, and be circumcised at Gilgal (Josh 5:2–9) — circumcision before conquest, where in Gen 34 it was a weapon. ⟨synthetic⟩ `[I]`
- **The idols.** "Put away the foreign gods" (35:2) is the demand of Josh 24:14, 23 at the same place. `[T]`
- **The face.** Israel had heard the LORD speak "face to face" at Horeb (Deut 5:4) and lived (5:24–26). `[T]`

#### Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances

- **The surprise:** that Esau, the ancestor of the Edomites, is the gracious party.
- **The shock:** the massacre at Shechem done with the covenant sign; and that the narrator does not condemn it there.
- **The comfort:** the God who "answered me in the day of my distress" (35:3).
- **The disturbance:** that the bearer of the name *Israel* is wounded by God, and that his family's first act after the renaming is the outrage of 34.

#### What we bring that they didn't

- A tendency to read the Jabbok as a private spiritual experience — "wrestling in prayer" — detached from Esau and from the land.
- Embarrassment about the violence of 34, and a desire to resolve it quickly; the narrator does not.
- The NT's reading of the twelve and of Bethlehem.

**Grounding:** 32:8–9, 14–22 (Jacob's strategy); 32:26–30 (the blessing given, not extracted); 34:13, 30 (deceit and self-concern).

#### Implication for the sermon

Preach the Jabbok inside the approach to Esau, as Leningrad sets it: a man meets God in order to meet his brother. And do not stop at 33.

### Biblical-Theological Themes

#### 1. Face to face — seeing God and living

- **Where it starts:** the face hidden from Cain (4:14), "his face fell" (4:5).
- **How it develops:** Peniel (32:31); Esau's face "as the face of God" (33:10); Moses "face to face" (Exod 33:11) yet unable to see God's face and live (33:20); the priestly blessing, "the LORD make his face shine on you … lift up his face to you" (Num 6:25–26).
- **What this passage adds:** a sinner sees God's face and lives, and the sight is joined to the brother's face.
- **What comes after:** "the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Christ" (2 Cor 4:6); "they will see his face" (Rev 22:4). `[I]`
- **What it contributes:** reconciliation with God and with the brother is one theme.

#### 2. The offering accepted

- **Where it starts:** Cain's מִנְחָה ("offering") rejected (4:5).
- **How it develops:** Jacob's מִנְחָה ("gift, offering") accepted (33:10–11); the brothers' מִנְחָה ("gift") to Joseph (43); the offering "accepted" for the worshipper (Lev 1:4).
- **What this passage adds:** the gift that covers the face (32:21) is the first piel of כִּפֶּר ("cover, atone").
- **What comes after:** reconciliation with the brother before the gift at the altar (Matt 5:23–24). `[I]`

#### 3. Holiness in the land

- **Where it starts:** the land given (12:7; 28:13); the nations' iniquity "not yet complete" (15:16, with שָׁלֵם, "complete").
- **How it develops:** defilement first named (34:5); purification first named (35:2); gods buried (35:4); a terror of God on the cities (35:5).
- **What this passage adds:** the first Israelite community in the land must be purified to meet God.
- **What comes after:** "you shall be holy, for I am holy" (Lev 11:44); Josh 24.
- **What it contributes:** the land is a place where God is met, and so must be clean.

**Confluence.** *Climactic convergence:* at Bethel (35:1–15) the three meet — the God who answered in distress is approached by a purified household with altar and drink offering, and speaks the promise face to face (35:13, "God went up from him in the place where he had spoken with him"). `[I]`

### Schnittjer Pass

**Skeleton:** Genesis · the *toledot* of Isaac (25:19–35:29) · the seed who fled is renamed, reconciled and returned; the house is completed; the generation closes.

**Findings.**

1. *(Step 1 — selectivity and time.)* The narrator brakes hard on one day and one night: 32:4–33:17 (47 verses) covers about twenty-four hours. He then accelerates: Succoth (one verse, an unknown period — Jacob "built himself a house", 33:17), Shechem (Dinah is a child at 30:21 and now marriageable, so years have passed). Ch. 35 moves fast through years and deaths. *High.* `[T]`
2. *(Step 2 — space.)* From the angels' camp at the border (32:2–3) to the river crossing (32:23), to the meeting place, to Succoth east of the Jordan, then "into the land of Canaan" at Shechem (33:18), Bethel (35:6), Ephrath (35:16), Migdal-eder (35:21) and Hebron (35:27). The route of the patriarch is the route of Abram (12:6–8: Shechem, Bethel) and of the tribes. *High.* `[T]` / `[I]`
3. *(Step 3 — shape.)* The Jabbok is the turn between the plan (32) and the meeting (33) (Tool 3). In Leningrad it does not stand alone. *Moderate.* `[T]` / `[unchecked — apparatus spread]`
4. *(Step 4 — repetition and leading word.)* Leading word פָּנִים ("face"), with מִנְחָה ("gift"), מַחֲנֶה ("camp") and עָבַר ("cross"). *High.* `[T]`
5. *(Step 5 — first versus second reading.)* First reading: the man at the Jabbok is an assailant. Second reading (32:31; Hos 12:5): God. First reading: Jacob's "I will come to my lord at Seir" (33:14) is a promise. Second reading (33:17): a polite evasion. First reading: Shechem's love for Dinah (34:3) softens his act. Second reading (34:26): she was being held in his house. *High.* `[T]` / `[I]`
6. *(Step 6 — polyacoustic intertextuality.)* Multiple imaging: Cain and Abel (4) heard behind Jacob and Esau; Abraham's route heard behind Jacob's. Progressive imaging forward: the crossing, the purification and the covenant at Shechem (Josh 3–5; 24); Hosea's Jacob; Micah's tower of the flock. *Moderate–high.* `[I]`
7. *(Step 7 — irreducibility.)* The theology is the story: a man must be wounded by God before he can bow to his brother; and the name *Israel* is borne by a family who are not yet holy. A proposition ("God reconciles") loses the limp and loses Dinah. `[I]`

**Echoes and signals.** Leading word פָּנִים ("face"); the first piel of כִּפֶּר ("cover, atone"); the Cain–Abel reversal; the purity vocabulary's first occurrences; Shechem as a place of covenant (Josh 24); Migdal-eder (Mic 4:8).

### Christological Reading

#### Type of Christological connection

- **Christophany (moderate–high):** the "man" (אִישׁ, 32:25) whom Jacob identifies as God (32:31), whom Hosea calls "the angel" (Hos 12:5), and who refuses his name (32:30; cf. Judg 13:18). The pattern of the angel who speaks as God (16:7–13; 22:11–16; 31:11–13; 48:15–16) runs through the unit. `[T]` / `[S: christophany reading]`
- **Trajectory (high):** the twelve sons (35:22b–26) → the twelve apostles (Matt 19:28; Rev 21:12–14); "kings shall come from your loins" (35:11) → the king from Judah (49:10); Bethlehem Ephrath (35:19) → Mic 5:1 → Matt 2:6.
- **Contrast (moderate–high):** Jacob covers his brother's face with a gift (32:21); Christ is the offering that reconciles (Rom 5:10–11; Eph 2:16). Jacob is a deceiver renamed; Nathanael is "an Israelite in whom there is no deceit" (John 1:47).
- **Typology (moderate):** Jacob blessed in weakness, wounded by God and "prevailing" by clinging. *Tests:* category (passes in part); NT precedent (none explicit — 2 Cor 12:9–10 is thematic, not typological); escalation (passes); authorial pattern (the limp is stressed, 32:32–33 — passes). Two to three of four — *moderate.*

#### How the passage points to Christ

1. **The face of God seen, and life.** Jacob saw God face to face and lived (32:31); no one has seen God, but the only Son has made him known (John 1:18), and the glory of God is seen "in the face of Jesus Christ" (2 Cor 4:6). *Moderate–high.*
2. **The brother who runs.** The wronged brother runs to embrace the one who cheated him (33:4); in Jesus' parable it is the father who runs (Luke 15:20), in the same Greek. The God who receives sinners is shown through the gesture of Esau. *Moderate–high.*
3. **The Bethlehem road.** Rachel dies giving birth on the Bethlehem road, and the tower of the flock stands beyond (35:16–21); Micah promises a ruler from Bethlehem Ephrathah "when she who is in labour has given birth" (Mic 5:1–2); Matthew quotes Micah and Jeremiah's Rachel at Jesus' birth (Matt 2:6, 18). *Moderate–high.*
4. **The offering accepted.** The gift that covers the face (32:21) is accepted (33:10). The once-for-all offering is accepted, and the face of God is turned toward his people (Heb 10:10–22). *Moderate — canonical reflection.* `[I]`

#### Trajectory

- **What the passage longs for:** a reconciled brother, a blessing that is not stolen, a house that is holy, a ruler from the line.
- **What Christ fulfils:** the reconciliation of brothers in one body (Eph 2:14–16); the blessing of Abraham given in him (Gal 3:14); the holy temple (Eph 2:21); the ruler from Bethlehem.
- **What still remains:** Edom and Israel apart (36); Simeon and Levi under a curse (49:5–7); Dinah unheard.

#### Moralism check

- **The "be like X" temptation:** "wrestle with God until he blesses you"; "be persistent in prayer like Jacob".
- **The gospel grounding:** the blessing is given by the one who wounds (32:26, 30); Jacob prevails by clinging, not by strength.
- **Christ as hero:** the God who meets the sinner, blesses him and sends him to his brother.

#### Confidence

*High* on the twelve, the kings and the Bethlehem trajectory; *moderate–high* on the Christophany, John 1:47 and Luke 15:20; *moderate* on the typology of weakness and the offering link.

### Difficult / Contested Verses

#### 32:25–33 — who is the man?

- **Category:** doctrinal.
- **The difficulty:** an אִישׁ ("man") wrestles with Jacob, cannot prevail, wounds him with a touch, and blesses him; Jacob says he has seen God.
- **Interpretive options:** (a) God in human form — a Christophany (Hos 12:4–5 "with God … with the angel"); (b) an angel; (c) a river spirit or demon, in pre-Israelite tradition behind the text `[S]`. The text itself gives (a), with Hosea glossing "angel".
- **Handling:** preach the text's identification (32:31) and Hosea's gloss; do not speculate beyond.

#### 32:26 — "he could not prevail against him"

- **Category:** doctrinal.
- **The difficulty:** God cannot overcome a man?
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the man disables Jacob with a touch (32:26b); the "not prevailing" is God's chosen restraint. The blessing is given (32:30), not won. *Moderate–high.* `[T]` / `[I]`
- **Handling:** address briefly.

#### 34 — Dinah: rape, love, and the narrator's silence

- **Category:** ethical; pastoral.
- **The difficulty:** the act (34:2) is followed by love (34:3), a proposal (34:4–12), deceit (34:13), massacre (34:25) and the brothers' question (34:31). Dinah never speaks. The narrator does not condemn the brothers in ch. 34.
- **Common mishandlings:** (a) blaming Dinah for "going out" (34:1); (b) treating Shechem's later love as mitigating; (c) justifying the brothers because they defended their sister; (d) avoiding the chapter.
- **Rhetorical function:** the renamed household in the land; the first naming of defilement; the deferred verdict.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the verb at 34:2 is עָנָה ("afflict, violate"), used in the law of rape (Deut 22:24, 29) and at 2 Sam 13:12, 14. Dinah is held in Shechem's house (34:26). The brothers use the covenant sign as a trap and plunder "all that was in the houses" (34:29). Jacob's later verdict condemns "their anger" and "their wrath" (49:5–7). `[T]`
- **Handling:** **there will be survivors of sexual violence in the room.** Name the act as the text names it; do not ask what Dinah did; do not speak of "seduction"; say that the text is silent on her pain and that its silence is not God's indifference. Say also that vengeance that uses God's sign is condemned.

#### 33:14–17 — Jacob's broken promise to come to Seir

- **Category:** ethical.
- **The difficulty:** Jacob promises to follow Esau to Seir and goes the other way.
- **Handling:** note it; the text does not comment. The reconciliation is real and the trust is not complete.

#### 35:22 — Reuben and Bilhah

- **Category:** ethical.
- **The difficulty:** the act; and the silence after "Israel heard".
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the deferred verdict (49:3–4); 1 Chr 5:1 explains the loss of the birthright by it. Taking a father's concubine is a claim to his place (2 Sam 16:21–22; 1 Kgs 2:22). `[T]` / `[I]`
- **Handling:** mention it; set it with 49:3–4.

#### 35:16–20 — Rachel's tomb

- **Category:** historical-geographical.
- **The difficulty:** Genesis places it "on the way to Ephrath, that is, Bethlehem" (35:19; 48:7); 1 Sam 10:2 places it "in the territory of Benjamin at Zelzah"; Jer 31:15 hears Rachel at Ramah.
- **Interpretive options:** (a) a site north of Jerusalem on the Benjamin border, with "that is, Bethlehem" a later gloss `[S]`; (b) the traditional site near Bethlehem; (c) two Ephraths. `[S]`
- **Handling:** for the preacher, the canon's own reading (Ruth 4:11; Mic 5:1; Matt 2:18) is what matters.

#### 35:26 — "born to him in Paddan-aram"

- **Category:** textual/literary.
- **The difficulty:** the list includes Benjamin, born near Bethlehem (35:18).
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the summary statement covers the twelve as a group; the same kind of summary occurs at 46:15 and 46:27. `[T]` / `[I]`
- **Handling:** note if asked.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: "Wrestle with God until he blesses you"

- **What it looks like:** a sermon on persistence in prayer, with Jacob as the model wrestler.
- **Why it's wrong:** the man wounds Jacob with a touch (32:26); the blessing is given (32:30), not extracted; and the scene is unique (Copycat). Hosea's gloss is "he wept and sought his favour" (Hos 12:5), not "he overpowered God".
- **The corrective:** preach the God who meets the fearful man, disables him and blesses him — and who sends him on, limping, to his brother.

### Pitfall: The Jabbok without Esau

- **What it looks like:** 32:23–33 preached alone as the inner spiritual crisis of a believer.
- **Why it's wrong:** the text sets the night between the message to Esau and the meeting (in Leningrad, in one paragraph); its meaning is the bowing of 33:3 and the "take my blessing" of 33:11.
- **The corrective:** preach 32–33 together, or at least end the Jabbok sermon at 33:10.

### Pitfall: Dinah's fault

- **What it looks like:** "She went out to see the daughters of the land" (34:1) as the cause of her rape; a warning to young women.
- **Why it's wrong:** the text puts the act on Shechem (34:2) and calls it folly and defilement (34:5, 7). It does not blame Dinah.
- **The corrective:** name the act as the text names it; preach the men's sins — Shechem's, the brothers', and Jacob's silence.

### Pitfall: The brothers as heroes

- **What it looks like:** Simeon and Levi praised for defending their sister's honour; 34:31 taken as the moral.
- **Why it's wrong:** deceit with the covenant sign, killing every male, plundering wives and children (34:27–29); the verdict of 49:5–7.
- **The corrective:** let 34:31 stand as the question it is, and read 49:5–7 alongside.

### Pitfall: The new name as instant transformation

- **What it looks like:** "From Jacob to Israel — a new man."
- **Why it's wrong:** the narrator goes on calling him Jacob (32:32; 33:1 …; 34; 35:1–20) and calls him Israel first at 35:21, in the sentence about Reuben. The name is renewed at 35:10. The family's next act is 34.
- **The corrective:** the name is God's gift and promise; its realisation is slow and is God's work.

### Pitfall: The gift as manipulation only

- **What it looks like:** Jacob's gifts as cynical bribery.
- **Why it's wrong:** the gift is wrapped in a prayer (32:10–13) and becomes, in Jacob's mouth, "my blessing" returned (33:11).
- **The corrective:** read it as the text does — fear, strategy and restitution together.
