## The Passage

The Hebrew is the WLC as it stands in `_texts/hebrew-wlc/01-Torah/01-Genesis.txt`; the English beneath each verse is the NASB95 from `_texts/logos-exports/`. **One word in the passage is printed unpointed, and that is not a corpus fault:** בגד at 30:11 is a *ketiv* (the written text), whose *qere* (the reading the Masoretes direct) is בָּא גָד ("fortune has come"); see Textual Variants. The section headings are the run's own division (Tool 3), not the NASB95's.

### 29:1–14 · At the well

**29:1** וַיִּשָּׂ֥א יַעֲקֹ֖ב רַגְלָ֑יו וַיֵּ֖לֶךְ אַ֥רְצָה בְנֵי־קֶֽדֶם׃

> Then Jacob went on his journey, and came to the land of the sons of the east.

**29:2** וַיַּ֞רְא וְהִנֵּ֧ה בְאֵ֣ר בַּשָּׂדֶ֗ה וְהִנֵּה־שָׁ֞ם שְׁלֹשָׁ֤ה עֶדְרֵי־צֹאן֙ רֹבְצִ֣ים עָלֶ֔יהָ כִּ֚י מִן־הַבְּאֵ֣ר הַהִ֔וא יַשְׁק֖וּ הָעֲדָרִ֑ים וְהָאֶ֥בֶן גְּדֹלָ֖ה עַל־פִּ֥י הַבְּאֵֽר׃

> He looked, and saw a well in the field, and behold, three flocks of sheep were lying there beside it, for from that well they watered the flocks. Now the stone on the mouth of the well was large.

**29:3** וְנֶאֶסְפוּ־שָׁ֣מָּה כָל־הָעֲדָרִ֗ים וְגָלֲל֤וּ אֶת־הָאֶ֨בֶן֙ מֵעַל֙ פִּ֣י הַבְּאֵ֔ר וְהִשְׁק֖וּ אֶת־הַצֹּ֑אן וְהֵשִׁ֧יבוּ אֶת־הָאֶ֛בֶן עַל־פִּ֥י הַבְּאֵ֖ר לִמְקֹמָֽהּ׃

> When all the flocks were gathered there, they would then roll the stone from the mouth of the well and water the sheep, and put the stone back in its place on the mouth of the well.

**29:4** וַיֹּ֤אמֶר לָהֶם֙ יַעֲקֹ֔ב אַחַ֖י מֵאַ֣יִן אַתֶּ֑ם וַיֹּ֣אמְר֔וּ מֵחָרָ֖ן אֲנָֽחְנוּ׃

> Jacob said to them, “My brothers, where are you from?” And they said, “We are from Haran.”

**29:5** וַיֹּ֣אמֶר לָהֶ֔ם הַיְדַעְתֶּ֖ם אֶת־לָבָ֣ן בֶּן־נָח֑וֹר וַיֹּאמְר֖וּ יָדָֽעְנוּ׃

> He said to them, “Do you know Laban the son of Nahor?” And they said, “We know him.”

**29:6** וַיֹּ֥אמֶר לָהֶ֖ם הֲשָׁל֣וֹם ל֑וֹ וַיֹּאמְר֣וּ שָׁל֔וֹם וְהִנֵּה֙ רָחֵ֣ל בִּתּ֔וֹ בָּאָ֖ה עִם־הַצֹּֽאן׃

> And he said to them, “Is it well with him?” And they said, “It is well, and here is Rachel his daughter coming with the sheep.”

**29:7** וַיֹּ֗אמֶר הֵ֥ן עוֹד֙ הַיּ֣וֹם גָּד֔וֹל לֹא־עֵ֖ת הֵאָסֵ֣ף הַמִּקְנֶ֑ה הַשְׁק֥וּ הַצֹּ֖אן וּלְכ֥וּ רְעֽוּ׃

> He said, “Behold, it is still high day; it is not time for the livestock to be gathered. Water the sheep, and go, pasture them.”

**29:8** וַיֹּאמְרוּ֮ לֹ֣א נוּכַל֒ עַ֣ד אֲשֶׁ֤ר יֵאָֽסְפוּ֙ כָּל־הָ֣עֲדָרִ֔ים וְגָֽלֲלוּ֙ אֶת־הָאֶ֔בֶן מֵעַ֖ל פִּ֣י הַבְּאֵ֑ר וְהִשְׁקִ֖ינוּ הַצֹּֽאן׃

> But they said, “We cannot, until all the flocks are gathered, and they roll the stone from the mouth of the well; then we water the sheep.”

**29:9** עוֹדֶ֖נּוּ מְדַבֵּ֣ר עִמָּ֑ם וְרָחֵ֣ל ׀ בָּ֗אָה עִם־הַצֹּאן֙ אֲשֶׁ֣ר לְאָבִ֔יהָ כִּ֥י רֹעָ֖ה הִֽוא׃

> While he was still speaking with them, Rachel came with her father’s sheep, for she was a shepherdess.

**29:10** וַיְהִ֡י כַּאֲשֶׁר֩ רָאָ֨ה יַעֲקֹ֜ב אֶת־רָחֵ֗ל בַּת־לָבָן֙ אֲחִ֣י אִמּ֔וֹ וְאֶת־צֹ֥אן לָבָ֖ן אֲחִ֣י אִמּ֑וֹ וַיִּגַּ֣שׁ יַעֲקֹ֗ב וַיָּ֤גֶל אֶת־הָאֶ֨בֶן֙ מֵעַל֙ פִּ֣י הַבְּאֵ֔ר וַיַּ֕שְׁקְ אֶת־צֹ֥אן לָבָ֖ן אֲחִ֥י אִמּֽוֹ׃

> When Jacob saw Rachel the daughter of Laban his mother’s brother, and the sheep of Laban his mother’s brother, Jacob went up and rolled the stone from the mouth of the well and watered the flock of Laban his mother’s brother.

**29:11** וַיִּשַּׁ֥ק יַעֲקֹ֖ב לְרָחֵ֑ל וַיִּשָּׂ֥א אֶת־קֹל֖וֹ וַיֵּֽבְךְּ׃

> Then Jacob kissed Rachel, and lifted his voice and wept.

**29:12** וַיַּגֵּ֨ד יַעֲקֹ֜ב לְרָחֵ֗ל כִּ֣י אֲחִ֤י אָבִ֨יהָ֙ ה֔וּא וְכִ֥י בֶן־רִבְקָ֖ה ה֑וּא וַתָּ֖רָץ וַתַּגֵּ֥ד לְאָבִֽיהָ׃

> Jacob told Rachel that he was a relative of her father and that he was Rebekah’s son, and she ran and told her father.

**29:13** וַיְהִי֩ כִשְׁמֹ֨עַ לָבָ֜ן אֶת־שֵׁ֣מַע ׀ יַעֲקֹ֣ב בֶּן־אֲחֹת֗וֹ וַיָּ֤רָץ לִקְרָאתוֹ֙ וַיְחַבֶּק־לוֹ֙ וַיְנַשֶּׁק־ל֔וֹ וַיְבִיאֵ֖הוּ אֶל־בֵּית֑וֹ וַיְסַפֵּ֣ר לְלָבָ֔ן אֵ֥ת כָּל־הַדְּבָרִ֖ים הָאֵֽלֶּה׃

> So when Laban heard the news of Jacob his sister’s son, he ran to meet him, and embraced him and kissed him and brought him to his house. Then he related to Laban all these things.

**29:14** וַיֹּ֤אמֶר לוֹ֙ לָבָ֔ן אַ֛ךְ עַצְמִ֥י וּבְשָׂרִ֖י אָ֑תָּה וַיֵּ֥שֶׁב עִמּ֖וֹ חֹ֥דֶשׁ יָמִֽים׃

> Laban said to him, “Surely you are my bone and my flesh.” And he stayed with him a month.

### 29:15–30 · Seven years, and seven more

**29:15** וַיֹּ֤אמֶר לָבָן֙ לְיַעֲקֹ֔ב הֲכִי־אָחִ֣י אַ֔תָּה וַעֲבַדְתַּ֖נִי חִנָּ֑ם הַגִּ֥ידָה לִּ֖י מַה־מַּשְׂכֻּרְתֶּֽךָ׃

> Then Laban said to Jacob, “Because you are my relative, should you therefore serve me for nothing? Tell me, what shall your wages be?”

**29:16** וּלְלָבָ֖ן שְׁתֵּ֣י בָנ֑וֹת שֵׁ֤ם הַגְּדֹלָה֙ לֵאָ֔ה וְשֵׁ֥ם הַקְּטַנָּ֖ה רָחֵֽל׃

> Now Laban had two daughters; the name of the older was Leah, and the name of the younger was Rachel.

**29:17** וְעֵינֵ֥י לֵאָ֖ה רַכּ֑וֹת וְרָחֵל֙ הָֽיְתָ֔ה יְפַת־תֹּ֖אַר וִיפַ֥ת מַרְאֶֽה׃

> And Leah’s eyes were weak, but Rachel was beautiful of form and face.

**29:18** וַיֶּאֱהַ֥ב יַעֲקֹ֖ב אֶת־רָחֵ֑ל וַיֹּ֗אמֶר אֶֽעֱבָדְךָ֙ שֶׁ֣בַע שָׁנִ֔ים בְּרָחֵ֥ל בִּתְּךָ֖ הַקְּטַנָּֽה׃

**29:19** וַיֹּ֣אמֶר לָבָ֗ן ט֚וֹב תִּתִּ֣י אֹתָ֣הּ לָ֔ךְ מִתִּתִּ֥י אֹתָ֖הּ לְאִ֣ישׁ אַחֵ֑ר שְׁבָ֖ה עִמָּדִֽי׃

> Laban said, “It is better that I give her to you than to give her to another man; stay with me.”

**29:20** וַיַּעֲבֹ֧ד יַעֲקֹ֛ב בְּרָחֵ֖ל שֶׁ֣בַע שָׁנִ֑ים וַיִּהְי֤וּ בְעֵינָיו֙ כְּיָמִ֣ים אֲחָדִ֔ים בְּאַהֲבָת֖וֹ אֹתָֽהּ׃

> So Jacob served seven years for Rachel and they seemed to him but a few days because of his love for her.

**29:21** וַיֹּ֨אמֶר יַעֲקֹ֤ב אֶל־לָבָן֙ הָבָ֣ה אֶת־אִשְׁתִּ֔י כִּ֥י מָלְא֖וּ יָמָ֑י וְאָב֖וֹאָה אֵלֶֽיהָ׃

> Then Jacob said to Laban, “Give me my wife, for my time is completed, that I may go in to her.”

**29:22** וַיֶּאֱסֹ֥ף לָבָ֛ן אֶת־כָּל־אַנְשֵׁ֥י הַמָּק֖וֹם וַיַּ֥עַשׂ מִשְׁתֶּֽה׃

> Laban gathered all the men of the place and made a feast.

**29:23** וַיְהִ֣י בָעֶ֔רֶב וַיִּקַּח֙ אֶת־לֵאָ֣ה בִתּ֔וֹ וַיָּבֵ֥א אֹתָ֖הּ אֵלָ֑יו וַיָּבֹ֖א אֵלֶֽיהָ׃

> So Jacob went in to Rachel also, and indeed he loved Rachel more than Leah, and he served with Laban for another seven years.

### 29:31–30:24 · The births

**29:31** וַיַּ֤רְא יְהוָה֙ כִּֽי־שְׂנוּאָ֣ה לֵאָ֔ה וַיִּפְתַּ֖ח אֶת־רַחְמָ֑הּ וְרָחֵ֖ל עֲקָרָֽה׃

> Now the LORD saw that Leah was unloved, and He opened her womb, but Rachel was barren.

**29:32** וַתַּ֤הַר לֵאָה֙ וַתֵּ֣לֶד בֵּ֔ן וַתִּקְרָ֥א שְׁמ֖וֹ רְאוּבֵ֑ן כִּ֣י אָֽמְרָ֗ה כִּֽי־רָאָ֤ה יְהוָה֙ בְּעָנְיִ֔י כִּ֥י עַתָּ֖ה יֶאֱהָבַ֥נִי אִישִֽׁי׃

> Leah conceived and bore a son and named him Reuben, for she said, “Because the LORD has seen my affliction; surely now my husband will love me.”

**29:33** וַתַּ֣הַר עוֹד֮ וַתֵּ֣לֶד בֵּן֒ וַתֹּ֗אמֶר כִּֽי־שָׁמַ֤ע יְהוָה֙ כִּֽי־שְׂנוּאָ֣ה אָנֹ֔כִי וַיִּתֶּן־לִ֖י גַּם־אֶת־זֶ֑ה וַתִּקְרָ֥א שְׁמ֖וֹ שִׁמְעֽוֹן׃

> Then she conceived again and bore a son and said, “Because the LORD has heard that I am unloved, He has therefore given me this son also.” So she named him Simeon.

**29:34** וַתַּ֣הַר עוֹד֮ וַתֵּ֣לֶד בֵּן֒ וַתֹּ֗אמֶר עַתָּ֤ה הַפַּ֨עַם֙ יִלָּוֶ֤ה אִישִׁי֙ אֵלַ֔י כִּֽי־יָלַ֥דְתִּי ל֖וֹ שְׁלֹשָׁ֣ה בָנִ֑ים עַל־כֵּ֥ן קָרָֽא־שְׁמ֖וֹ לֵוִֽי׃

> She conceived again and bore a son and said, “Now this time my husband will become attached to me, because I have borne him three sons.” Therefore he was named Levi.

**29:35** וַתַּ֨הַר ע֜וֹד וַתֵּ֣לֶד בֵּ֗ן וַתֹּ֨אמֶר֙ הַפַּ֨עַם֙ אוֹדֶ֣ה אֶת־יְהוָ֔ה עַל־כֵּ֛ן קָרְאָ֥ה שְׁמ֖וֹ יְהוּדָ֑ה וַֽתַּעֲמֹ֖ד מִלֶּֽדֶת׃

> And she conceived again and bore a son and said, “This time I will praise the LORD.” Therefore she named him Judah. Then she stopped bearing.

**30:1** וַתֵּ֣רֶא רָחֵ֗ל כִּ֣י לֹ֤א יָֽלְדָה֙ לְיַעֲקֹ֔ב וַתְּקַנֵּ֥א רָחֵ֖ל בַּאֲחֹתָ֑הּ וַתֹּ֤אמֶר אֶֽל־יַעֲקֹב֙ הָֽבָה־לִּ֣י בָנִ֔ים וְאִם־אַ֖יִן מֵתָ֥ה אָנֹֽכִי׃

> Now when Rachel saw that she bore Jacob no children, she became jealous of her sister; and she said to Jacob, “Give me children, or else I die.”

**30:2** וַיִּֽחַר־אַ֥ף יַעֲקֹ֖ב בְּרָחֵ֑ל וַיֹּ֗אמֶר הֲתַ֤חַת אֱלֹהִים֙ אָנֹ֔כִי אֲשֶׁר־מָנַ֥ע מִמֵּ֖ךְ פְּרִי־בָֽטֶן׃

> Then Jacob’s anger burned against Rachel, and he said, “Am I in the place of God, who has withheld from you the fruit of the womb?”

**30:3** וַתֹּ֕אמֶר הִנֵּ֛ה אֲמָתִ֥י בִלְהָ֖ה בֹּ֣א אֵלֶ֑יהָ וְתֵלֵד֙ עַל־בִּרְכַּ֔י וְאִבָּנֶ֥ה גַם־אָנֹכִ֖י מִמֶּֽנָּה׃

> She said, “Here is my maid Bilhah, go in to her that she may bear on my knees, that through her I too may have children.”

**30:4** וַתִּתֶּן־ל֛וֹ אֶת־בִּלְהָ֥ה שִׁפְחָתָ֖הּ לְאִשָּׁ֑ה וַיָּבֹ֥א אֵלֶ֖יהָ יַעֲקֹֽב׃

> So she gave him her maid Bilhah as a wife, and Jacob went in to her.

**30:5** וַתַּ֣הַר בִּלְהָ֔ה וַתֵּ֥לֶד לְיַעֲקֹ֖ב בֵּֽן׃

> Bilhah conceived and bore Jacob a son.

**30:6** וַתֹּ֤אמֶר רָחֵל֙ דָּנַ֣נִּי אֱלֹהִ֔ים וְגַם֙ שָׁמַ֣ע בְּקֹלִ֔י וַיִּתֶּן־לִ֖י בֵּ֑ן עַל־כֵּ֛ן קָרְאָ֥ה שְׁמ֖וֹ דָּֽן׃

> Then Rachel said, “God has vindicated me, and has indeed heard my voice and has given me a son.” Therefore she named him Dan.

**30:7** וַתַּ֣הַר ע֔וֹד וַתֵּ֕לֶד בִּלְהָ֖ה שִׁפְחַ֣ת רָחֵ֑ל בֵּ֥ן שֵׁנִ֖י לְיַעֲקֹֽב׃

> Rachel’s maid Bilhah conceived again and bore Jacob a second son.

**30:8** וַתֹּ֣אמֶר רָחֵ֗ל נַפְתּוּלֵ֨י אֱלֹהִ֧ים ׀ נִפְתַּ֛לְתִּי עִם־אֲחֹתִ֖י גַּם־יָכֹ֑לְתִּי וַתִּקְרָ֥א שְׁמ֖וֹ נַפְתָּלִֽי׃

> So Rachel said, “With mighty wrestlings I have wrestled with my sister, and I have indeed prevailed.” And she named him Naphtali.

**30:9** וַתֵּ֣רֶא לֵאָ֔ה כִּ֥י עָמְדָ֖ה מִלֶּ֑דֶת וַתִּקַּח֙ אֶת־זִלְפָּ֣ה שִׁפְחָתָ֔הּ וַתִּתֵּ֥ן אֹתָ֛הּ לְיַעֲקֹ֖ב לְאִשָּֽׁה׃

> When Leah saw that she had stopped bearing, she took her maid Zilpah and gave her to Jacob as a wife.

**30:10** וַתֵּ֗לֶד זִלְפָּ֛ה שִׁפְחַ֥ת לֵאָ֖ה לְיַעֲקֹ֥ב בֵּֽן׃

> Leah’s maid Zilpah bore Jacob a son.

**30:11** וַתֹּ֥אמֶר לֵאָ֖ה בגד וַתִּקְרָ֥א אֶת־שְׁמ֖וֹ גָּֽד׃

> Then Leah said, “How fortunate!” So she named him Gad.

**30:12** וַתֵּ֗לֶד זִלְפָּה֙ שִׁפְחַ֣ת לֵאָ֔ה בֵּ֥ן שֵׁנִ֖י לְיַעֲקֹֽב׃

> Leah’s maid Zilpah bore Jacob a second son.

**30:13** וַתֹּ֣אמֶר לֵאָ֔ה בְּאָשְׁרִ֕י כִּ֥י אִשְּׁר֖וּנִי בָּנ֑וֹת וַתִּקְרָ֥א אֶת־שְׁמ֖וֹ אָשֵֽׁר׃

> Then Leah said, “Happy am I! For women will call me happy.” So she named him Asher.

**30:14** וַיֵּ֨לֶךְ רְאוּבֵ֜ן בִּימֵ֣י קְצִיר־חִטִּ֗ים וַיִּמְצָ֤א דֽוּדָאִים֙ בַּשָּׂדֶ֔ה וַיָּבֵ֣א אֹתָ֔ם אֶל־לֵאָ֖ה אִמּ֑וֹ וַתֹּ֤אמֶר רָחֵל֙ אֶל־לֵאָ֔ה תְּנִי־נָ֣א לִ֔י מִדּוּדָאֵ֖י בְּנֵֽךְ׃

> Now in the days of wheat harvest Reuben went and found mandrakes in the field, and brought them to his mother Leah. Then Rachel said to Leah, “Please give me some of your son’s mandrakes.”

**30:15** וַתֹּ֣אמֶר לָ֗הּ הַמְעַט֙ קַחְתֵּ֣ךְ אֶת־אִישִׁ֔י וְלָקַ֕חַת גַּ֥ם אֶת־דּוּדָאֵ֖י בְּנִ֑י וַתֹּ֣אמֶר רָחֵ֗ל לָכֵן֙ יִשְׁכַּ֤ב עִמָּךְ֙ הַלַּ֔יְלָה תַּ֖חַת דּוּדָאֵ֥י בְנֵֽךְ׃

> But she said to her, “Is it a small matter for you to take my husband? And would you take my son’s mandrakes also?” So Rachel said, “Therefore he may lie with you tonight in return for your son’s mandrakes.”

**30:16** וַיָּבֹ֨א יַעֲקֹ֣ב מִן־הַשָּׂדֶה֮ בָּעֶרֶב֒ וַתֵּצֵ֨א לֵאָ֜ה לִקְרָאת֗וֹ וַתֹּ֨אמֶר֙ אֵלַ֣י תָּב֔וֹא כִּ֚י שָׂכֹ֣ר שְׂכַרְתִּ֔יךָ בְּדוּדָאֵ֖י בְּנִ֑י וַיִּשְׁכַּ֥ב עִמָּ֖הּ בַּלַּ֥יְלָה הֽוּא׃

> When Jacob came in from the field in the evening, then Leah went out to meet him and said, “You must come in to me, for I have surely hired you with my son’s mandrakes.” So he lay with her that night.

**30:17** וַיִּשְׁמַ֥ע אֱלֹהִ֖ים אֶל־לֵאָ֑ה וַתַּ֛הַר וַתֵּ֥לֶד לְיַעֲקֹ֖ב בֵּ֥ן חֲמִישִֽׁי׃

> God gave heed to Leah, and she conceived and bore Jacob a fifth son.

**30:18** וַתֹּ֣אמֶר לֵאָ֗ה נָתַ֤ן אֱלֹהִים֙ שְׂכָרִ֔י אֲשֶׁר־נָתַ֥תִּי שִׁפְחָתִ֖י לְאִישִׁ֑י וַתִּקְרָ֥א שְׁמ֖וֹ יִשָּׂשכָֽר׃

> Then Leah said, “God has given me my wages because I gave my maid to my husband.” So she named him Issachar.

**30:19** וַתַּ֤הַר עוֹד֙ לֵאָ֔ה וַתֵּ֥לֶד בֵּן־שִׁשִּׁ֖י לְּיַעֲקֹֽב׃

> Leah conceived again and bore a sixth son to Jacob.

**30:20** וַתֹּ֣אמֶר לֵאָ֗ה זְבָדַ֨נִי אֱלֹהִ֥ים ׀ אֹתִי֮ זֵ֣בֶד טוֹב֒ הַפַּ֨עַם֙ יִזְבְּלֵ֣נִי אִישִׁ֔י כִּֽי־יָלַ֥דְתִּי ל֖וֹ שִׁשָּׁ֣ה בָנִ֑ים וַתִּקְרָ֥א אֶת־שְׁמ֖וֹ זְבֻלֽוּן׃

> Then Leah said, “God has endowed me with a good gift; now my husband will dwell with me, because I have borne him six sons.” So she named him Zebulun.

**30:21** וְאַחַ֖ר יָ֣לְדָה בַּ֑ת וַתִּקְרָ֥א אֶת־שְׁמָ֖הּ דִּינָֽה׃

> Afterward she bore a daughter and named her Dinah.

**30:22** וַיִּזְכֹּ֥ר אֱלֹהִ֖ים אֶת־רָחֵ֑ל וַיִּשְׁמַ֤ע אֵלֶ֨יהָ֙ אֱלֹהִ֔ים וַיִּפְתַּ֖ח אֶת־רַחְמָֽהּ׃

> Then God remembered Rachel, and God gave heed to her and opened her womb.

**30:23** וַתַּ֖הַר וַתֵּ֣לֶד בֵּ֑ן וַתֹּ֕אמֶר אָסַ֥ף אֱלֹהִ֖ים אֶת־חֶרְפָּתִֽי׃

> So she conceived and bore a son and said, “God has taken away my reproach.”

**30:24** וַתִּקְרָ֧א אֶת־שְׁמ֛וֹ יוֹסֵ֖ף לֵאמֹ֑ר יֹסֵ֧ף יְהוָ֛ה לִ֖י בֵּ֥ן אַחֵֽר׃

> She named him Joseph, saying, “May the LORD give me another son.”

### 30:25–43 · Wages, rods and flocks

**30:25** וַיְהִ֕י כַּאֲשֶׁ֛ר יָלְדָ֥ה רָחֵ֖ל אֶת־יוֹסֵ֑ף וַיֹּ֤אמֶר יַעֲקֹב֙ אֶל־לָבָ֔ן שַׁלְּחֵ֨נִי֙ וְאֵ֣לְכָ֔ה אֶל־מְקוֹמִ֖י וּלְאַרְצִֽי׃

> Now it came about when Rachel had borne Joseph, that Jacob said to Laban, “Send me away, that I may go to my own place and to my own country.

**30:26** תְּנָ֞ה אֶת־נָשַׁ֣י וְאֶת־יְלָדַ֗י אֲשֶׁ֨ר עָבַ֧דְתִּי אֹֽתְךָ֛ בָּהֵ֖ן וְאֵלֵ֑כָה כִּ֚י אַתָּ֣ה יָדַ֔עְתָּ אֶת־עֲבֹדָתִ֖י אֲשֶׁ֥ר עֲבַדְתִּֽיךָ׃

> “Give me my wives and my children for whom I have served you, and let me depart; for you yourself know my service which I have rendered you.”

**30:27** וַיֹּ֤אמֶר אֵלָיו֙ לָבָ֔ן אִם־נָ֛א מָצָ֥אתִי חֵ֖ן בְּעֵינֶ֑יךָ נִחַ֕שְׁתִּי וַיְבָרֲכֵ֥נִי יְהוָ֖ה בִּגְלָלֶֽךָ׃

> But Laban said to him, “If now it pleases you, stay with me; I have divined that the LORD has blessed me on your account.”

**30:28** וַיֹּאמַ֑ר נָקְבָ֧ה שְׂכָרְךָ֛ עָלַ֖י וְאֶתֵּֽנָה׃

> He continued, “Name me your wages, and I will give it.”

**30:29** וַיֹּ֣אמֶר אֵלָ֔יו אַתָּ֣ה יָדַ֔עְתָּ אֵ֖ת אֲשֶׁ֣ר עֲבַדְתִּ֑יךָ וְאֵ֛ת אֲשֶׁר־הָיָ֥ה מִקְנְךָ֖ אִתִּֽי׃

> But he said to him, “You yourself know how I have served you and how your cattle have fared with me.

**30:30** כִּ֡י מְעַט֩ אֲשֶׁר־הָיָ֨ה לְךָ֤ לְפָנַי֙ וַיִּפְרֹ֣ץ לָרֹ֔ב וַיְבָ֧רֶךְ יְהוָ֛ה אֹתְךָ֖ לְרַגְלִ֑י וְעַתָּ֗ה מָתַ֛י אֶֽעֱשֶׂ֥ה גַם־אָנֹכִ֖י לְבֵיתִֽי׃

> “For you had little before I came and it has increased to a multitude, and the LORD has blessed you wherever I turned. But now, when shall I provide for my own household also?”

**30:31** וַיֹּ֖אמֶר מָ֣ה אֶתֶּן־לָ֑ךְ וַיֹּ֤אמֶר יַעֲקֹב֙ לֹא־תִתֶּן־לִ֣י מְא֔וּמָה אִם־תַּֽעֲשֶׂה־לִּי֙ הַדָּבָ֣ר הַזֶּ֔ה אָשׁ֛וּבָה אֶרְעֶ֥ה צֹֽאנְךָ֖ אֶשְׁמֹֽר׃

> So he said, “What shall I give you?” And Jacob said, “You shall not give me anything. If you will do this one thing for me, I will again pasture and keep your flock:

**30:32** אֶֽעֱבֹ֨ר בְּכָל־צֹֽאנְךָ֜ הַיּ֗וֹם הָסֵ֨ר מִשָּׁ֜ם כָּל־שֶׂ֣ה ׀ נָקֹ֣ד וְטָל֗וּא וְכָל־שֶׂה־חוּם֙ בַּכְּשָׂבִ֔ים וְטָל֥וּא וְנָקֹ֖ד בָּעִזִּ֑ים וְהָיָ֖ה שְׂכָרִֽי׃

> let me pass through your entire flock today, removing from there every speckled and spotted sheep and every black one among the lambs and the spotted and speckled among the goats; and such shall be my wages.

**30:33** וְעָֽנְתָה־בִּ֤י צִדְקָתִי֙ בְּי֣וֹם מָחָ֔ר כִּֽי־תָב֥וֹא עַל־שְׂכָרִ֖י לְפָנֶ֑יךָ כֹּ֣ל אֲשֶׁר־אֵינֶנּוּ֩ נָקֹ֨ד וְטָל֜וּא בָּֽעִזִּ֗ים וְחוּם֙ בַּכְּשָׂבִ֔ים גָּנ֥וּב ה֖וּא אִתִּֽי׃

> “So my honesty will answer for me later, when you come concerning my wages. Every one that is not speckled and spotted among the goats and black among the lambs, if found with me, will be considered stolen.”

**30:34** וַיֹּ֥אמֶר לָבָ֖ן הֵ֑ן ל֖וּ יְהִ֥י כִדְבָרֶֽךָ׃

> Laban said, “Good, let it be according to your word.”

**30:35** וַיָּ֣סַר בַּיּוֹם֩ הַה֨וּא אֶת־הַתְּיָשִׁ֜ים הָֽעֲקֻדִּ֣ים וְהַטְּלֻאִ֗ים וְאֵ֤ת כָּל־הָֽעִזִּים֙ הַנְּקֻדּ֣וֹת וְהַטְּלֻאֹ֔ת כֹּ֤ל אֲשֶׁר־לָבָן֙ בּ֔וֹ וְכָל־ח֖וּם בַּכְּשָׂבִ֑ים וַיִּתֵּ֖ן בְּיַד־בָּנָֽיו׃

> So he removed on that day the striped and spotted male goats and all the speckled and spotted female goats, every one with white in it, and all the black ones among the sheep, and gave them into the care of his sons.

**30:36** וַיָּ֗שֶׂם דֶּ֚רֶךְ שְׁלֹ֣שֶׁת יָמִ֔ים בֵּינ֖וֹ וּבֵ֣ין יַעֲקֹ֑ב וְיַעֲקֹ֗ב רֹעֶ֛ה אֶת־צֹ֥אן לָבָ֖ן הַנּוֹתָרֹֽת׃

> And he put a distance of three days’ journey between himself and Jacob, and Jacob fed the rest of Laban’s flocks.

**30:37** וַיִּֽקַּֽח־ל֣וֹ יַעֲקֹ֗ב מַקַּ֥ל לִבְנֶ֛ה לַ֖ח וְל֣וּז וְעֶרְמ֑וֹן וַיְפַצֵּ֤ל בָּהֵן֙ פְּצָל֣וֹת לְבָנ֔וֹת מַחְשֹׂף֙ הַלָּבָ֔ן אֲשֶׁ֖ר עַל־הַמַּקְלֽוֹת׃

> Then Jacob took fresh rods of poplar and almond and plane trees, and peeled white stripes in them, exposing the white which was in the rods.

**30:38** וַיַּצֵּ֗ג אֶת־הַמַּקְלוֹת֙ אֲשֶׁ֣ר פִּצֵּ֔ל בָּרֳהָטִ֖ים בְּשִֽׁקֲת֣וֹת הַמָּ֑יִם אֲשֶׁר֩ תָּבֹ֨אןָ הַצֹּ֤אן לִשְׁתּוֹת֙ לְנֹ֣כַח הַצֹּ֔אן וַיֵּחַ֖מְנָה בְּבֹאָ֥ן לִשְׁתּֽוֹת׃

> He set the rods which he had peeled in front of the flocks in the gutters, even in the watering troughs, where the flocks came to drink; and they mated when they came to drink.

**30:39** וַיֶּחֱמ֥וּ הַצֹּ֖אן אֶל־הַמַּקְל֑וֹת וַתֵּלַ֣דְןָ הַצֹּ֔אן עֲקֻדִּ֥ים נְקֻדִּ֖ים וּטְלֻאִֽים׃

> So the flocks mated by the rods, and the flocks brought forth striped, speckled, and spotted.

**30:40** וְהַכְּשָׂבִים֮ הִפְרִ֣יד יַעֲקֹב֒ וַ֠יִּתֵּן פְּנֵ֨י הַצֹּ֧אן אֶל־עָקֹ֛ד וְכָל־ח֖וּם בְּצֹ֣אן לָבָ֑ן וַיָּֽשֶׁת־ל֤וֹ עֲדָרִים֙ לְבַדּ֔וֹ וְלֹ֥א שָׁתָ֖ם עַל־צֹ֥אן לָבָֽן׃

> Jacob separated the lambs, and made the flocks face toward the striped and all the black in the flock of Laban; and he put his own herds apart, and did not put them with Laban’s flock.

**30:41** וְהָיָ֗ה בְּכָל־יַחֵם֮ הַצֹּ֣אן הַמְקֻשָּׁרוֹת֒ וְשָׂ֨ם יַעֲקֹ֧ב אֶת־הַמַּקְל֛וֹת לְעֵינֵ֥י הַצֹּ֖אן בָּרֳהָטִ֑ים לְיַחְמֵ֖נָּה בַּמַּקְלֽוֹת׃

> Moreover, whenever the stronger of the flock were mating, Jacob would place the rods in the sight of the flock in the gutters, so that they might mate by the rods;

**30:42** וּבְהַעֲטִ֥יף הַצֹּ֖אן לֹ֣א יָשִׂ֑ים וְהָיָ֤ה הָעֲטֻפִים֙ לְלָבָ֔ן וְהַקְּשֻׁרִ֖ים לְיַעֲקֹֽב׃

> but when the flock was feeble, he did not put them in; so the feebler were Laban’s and the stronger Jacob’s.

**30:43** וַיִּפְרֹ֥ץ הָאִ֖ישׁ מְאֹ֣ד מְאֹ֑ד וַֽיְהִי־לוֹ֙ צֹ֣אן רַבּ֔וֹת וּשְׁפָחוֹת֙ וַעֲבָדִ֔ים וּגְמַלִּ֖ים וַחֲמֹרִֽים׃

> So the man became exceedingly prosperous, and had large flocks and female and male servants and camels and donkeys.

### 31:1–21 · The flight

**31:1** וַיִּשְׁמַ֗ע אֶת־דִּבְרֵ֤י בְנֵֽי־לָבָן֙ לֵאמֹ֔ר לָקַ֣ח יַעֲקֹ֔ב אֵ֖ת כָּל־אֲשֶׁ֣ר לְאָבִ֑ינוּ וּמֵאֲשֶׁ֣ר לְאָבִ֔ינוּ עָשָׂ֕ה אֵ֥ת כָּל־הַכָּבֹ֖ד הַזֶּֽה׃

> Now Jacob heard the words of Laban’s sons, saying, “Jacob has taken away all that was our father’s, and from what belonged to our father he has made all this wealth.”

**31:2** וַיַּ֥רְא יַעֲקֹ֖ב אֶת־פְּנֵ֣י לָבָ֑ן וְהִנֵּ֥ה אֵינֶ֛נּוּ עִמּ֖וֹ כִּתְמ֥וֹל שִׁלְשֽׁוֹם׃

> Jacob saw the attitude of Laban, and behold, it was not friendly toward him as formerly.

**31:3** וַיֹּ֤אמֶר יְהוָה֙ אֶֽל־יַעֲקֹ֔ב שׁ֛וּב אֶל־אֶ֥רֶץ אֲבוֹתֶ֖יךָ וּלְמוֹלַדְתֶּ֑ךָ וְאֶֽהְיֶ֖ה עִמָּֽךְ׃

> Then the LORD said to Jacob, “Return to the land of your fathers and to your relatives, and I will be with you.”

**31:4** וַיִּשְׁלַ֣ח יַעֲקֹ֔ב וַיִּקְרָ֖א לְרָחֵ֣ל וּלְלֵאָ֑ה הַשָּׂדֶ֖ה אֶל־צֹאנֽוֹ׃

> So Jacob sent and called Rachel and Leah to his flock in the field,

**31:5** וַיֹּ֣אמֶר לָהֶ֗ן רֹאֶ֤ה אָנֹכִי֙ אֶת־פְּנֵ֣י אֲבִיכֶ֔ן כִּֽי־אֵינֶ֥נּוּ אֵלַ֖י כִּתְמֹ֣ל שִׁלְשֹׁ֑ם וֵֽאלֹהֵ֣י אָבִ֔י הָיָ֖ה עִמָּדִֽי׃

> and said to them, “I see your father’s attitude, that it is not friendly toward me as formerly, but the God of my father has been with me.

**31:6** וְאַתֵּ֖נָה יְדַעְתֶּ֑ן כִּ֚י בְּכָל־כֹּחִ֔י עָבַ֖דְתִּי אֶת־אֲבִיכֶֽן׃

> “You know that I have served your father with all my strength.

**31:7** וַאֲבִיכֶן֙ הֵ֣תֶל בִּ֔י וְהֶחֱלִ֥ף אֶת־מַשְׂכֻּרְתִּ֖י עֲשֶׂ֣רֶת מֹנִ֑ים וְלֹֽא־נְתָנ֣וֹ אֱלֹהִ֔ים לְהָרַ֖ע עִמָּדִֽי׃

> “Yet your father has cheated me and changed my wages ten times; however, God did not allow him to hurt me.

**31:8** אִם־כֹּ֣ה יֹאמַ֗ר נְקֻדִּים֙ יִהְיֶ֣ה שְׂכָרֶ֔ךָ וְיָלְד֥וּ כָל־הַצֹּ֖אן נְקֻדִּ֑ים וְאִם־כֹּ֣ה יֹאמַ֗ר עֲקֻדִּים֙ יִהְיֶ֣ה שְׂכָרֶ֔ךָ וְיָלְד֥וּ כָל־הַצֹּ֖אן עֲקֻדִּֽים׃

> “If he spoke thus, ‘The speckled shall be your wages,’ then all the flock brought forth speckled; and if he spoke thus, ‘The striped shall be your wages,’ then all the flock brought forth striped.

**31:9** וַיַּצֵּ֧ל אֱלֹהִ֛ים אֶת־מִקְנֵ֥ה אֲבִיכֶ֖ם וַיִּתֶּן־לִֽי׃

> “Thus God has taken away your father’s livestock and given them to me.

**31:10** וַיְהִ֗י בְּעֵת֙ יַחֵ֣ם הַצֹּ֔אן וָאֶשָּׂ֥א עֵינַ֛י וָאֵ֖רֶא בַּחֲל֑וֹם וְהִנֵּ֤ה הָֽעַתֻּדִים֙ הָעֹלִ֣ים עַל־הַצֹּ֔אן עֲקֻדִּ֥ים נְקֻדִּ֖ים וּבְרֻדִּֽים׃

> “And it came about at the time when the flock were mating that I lifted up my eyes and saw in a dream, and behold, the male goats which were mating were striped, speckled, and mottled.

**31:11** וַיֹּ֨אמֶר אֵלַ֜י מַלְאַ֧ךְ הָאֱלֹהִ֛ים בַּחֲל֖וֹם יַֽעֲקֹ֑ב וָאֹמַ֖ר הִנֵּֽנִי׃

> “Then the angel of God said to me in the dream, ‘Jacob,’ and I said, ‘Here I am.’

**31:12** וַיֹּ֗אמֶר שָׂא־נָ֨א עֵינֶ֤יךָ וּרְאֵה֙ כָּל־הָֽעַתֻּדִים֙ הָעֹלִ֣ים עַל־הַצֹּ֔אן עֲקֻדִּ֥ים נְקֻדִּ֖ים וּבְרֻדִּ֑ים כִּ֣י רָאִ֔יתִי אֵ֛ת כָּל־אֲשֶׁ֥ר לָבָ֖ן עֹ֥שֶׂה לָּֽךְ׃

> “He said, ‘Lift up now your eyes and see that all the male goats which are mating are striped, speckled, and mottled; for I have seen all that Laban has been doing to you.

**31:13** אָנֹכִ֤י הָאֵל֙ בֵּֽית־אֵ֔ל אֲשֶׁ֨ר מָשַׁ֤חְתָּ שָּׁם֙ מַצֵּבָ֔ה אֲשֶׁ֨ר נָדַ֥רְתָּ לִּ֛י שָׁ֖ם נֶ֑דֶר עַתָּ֗ה ק֥וּם צֵא֙ מִן־הָאָ֣רֶץ הַזֹּ֔את וְשׁ֖וּב אֶל־אֶ֥רֶץ מוֹלַדְתֶּֽךָ׃

> ‘I am the God of Bethel, where you anointed a pillar, where you made a vow to Me; now arise, leave this land, and return to the land of your birth.’ ”

**31:14** וַתַּ֤עַן רָחֵל֙ וְלֵאָ֔ה וַתֹּאמַ֖רְנָה ל֑וֹ הַע֥וֹד לָ֛נוּ חֵ֥לֶק וְנַחֲלָ֖ה בְּבֵ֥ית אָבִֽינוּ׃

> Rachel and Leah said to him, “Do we still have any portion or inheritance in our father’s house?

**31:15** הֲל֧וֹא נָכְרִיּ֛וֹת נֶחְשַׁ֥בְנוּ ל֖וֹ כִּ֣י מְכָרָ֑נוּ וַיֹּ֥אכַל גַּם־אָכ֖וֹל אֶת־כַּסְפֵּֽנוּ׃

> “Are we not reckoned by him as foreigners? For he has sold us, and has also entirely consumed our purchase price.

**31:16** כִּ֣י כָל־הָעֹ֗שֶׁר אֲשֶׁ֨ר הִצִּ֤יל אֱלֹהִים֙ מֵֽאָבִ֔ינוּ לָ֥נוּ ה֖וּא וּלְבָנֵ֑ינוּ וְעַתָּ֗ה כֹּל֩ אֲשֶׁ֨ר אָמַ֧ר אֱלֹהִ֛ים אֵלֶ֖יךָ עֲשֵֽׂה׃

> “Surely all the wealth which God has taken away from our father belongs to us and our children; now then, do whatever God has said to you.”

**31:17** וַיָּ֖קָם יַעֲקֹ֑ב וַיִּשָּׂ֛א אֶת־בָּנָ֥יו וְאֶת־נָשָׁ֖יו עַל־הַגְּמַלִּֽים׃

> Then Jacob arose and put his children and his wives upon camels;

**31:18** וַיִּנְהַ֣ג אֶת־כָּל־מִקְנֵ֗הוּ וְאֶת־כָּל־רְכֻשׁוֹ֙ אֲשֶׁ֣ר רָכָ֔שׁ מִקְנֵה֙ קִנְיָנ֔וֹ אֲשֶׁ֥ר רָכַ֖שׁ בְּפַדַּ֣ן אֲרָ֑ם לָב֛וֹא אֶל־יִצְחָ֥ק אָבִ֖יו אַ֥רְצָה כְּנָֽעַן׃

> and he drove away all his livestock and all his property which he had gathered, his acquired livestock which he had gathered in Paddan-aram, to go to the land of Canaan to his father Isaac.

**31:19** וְלָבָ֣ן הָלַ֔ךְ לִגְזֹ֖ז אֶת־צֹאנ֑וֹ וַתִּגְנֹ֣ב רָחֵ֔ל אֶת־הַתְּרָפִ֖ים אֲשֶׁ֥ר לְאָבִֽיהָ׃

> When Laban had gone to shear his flock, then Rachel stole the household idols that were her father’s.

**31:20** וַיִּגְנֹ֣ב יַעֲקֹ֔ב אֶת־לֵ֥ב לָבָ֖ן הָאֲרַמִּ֑י עַל־בְּלִי֙ הִגִּ֣יד ל֔וֹ כִּ֥י בֹרֵ֖חַ הֽוּא׃

> And Jacob deceived Laban the Aramean by not telling him that he was fleeing.

**31:21** וַיִּבְרַ֥ח הוּא֙ וְכָל־אֲשֶׁר־ל֔וֹ וַיָּ֖קָם וַיַּעֲבֹ֣ר אֶת־הַנָּהָ֑ר וַיָּ֥שֶׂם אֶת־פָּנָ֖יו הַ֥ר הַגִּלְעָֽד׃

> So he fled with all that he had; and he arose and crossed the Euphrates River, and set his face toward the hill country of Gilead.

### 31:22–42 · Pursuit, search and protest

**31:22** וַיֻּגַּ֥ד לְלָבָ֖ן בַּיּ֣וֹם הַשְּׁלִישִׁ֑י כִּ֥י בָרַ֖ח יַעֲקֹֽב׃

> When it was told Laban on the third day that Jacob had fled,

**31:23** וַיִּקַּ֤ח אֶת־אֶחָיו֙ עִמּ֔וֹ וַיִּרְדֹּ֣ף אַחֲרָ֔יו דֶּ֖רֶךְ שִׁבְעַ֣ת יָמִ֑ים וַיַּדְבֵּ֥ק אֹת֖וֹ בְּהַ֥ר הַגִּלְעָֽד׃

> then he took his kinsmen with him and pursued him a distance of seven days’ journey, and he overtook him in the hill country of Gilead.

**31:24** וַיָּבֹ֧א אֱלֹהִ֛ים אֶל־לָבָ֥ן הָאֲרַמִּ֖י בַּחֲלֹ֣ם הַלָּ֑יְלָה וַיֹּ֣אמֶר ל֗וֹ הִשָּׁ֧מֶר לְךָ֛ פֶּן־תְּדַבֵּ֥ר עִֽם־יַעֲקֹ֖ב מִטּ֥וֹב עַד־רָֽע׃

> God came to Laban the Aramean in a dream of the night and said to him, “Be careful that you do not speak to Jacob either good or bad.”

**31:25** וַיַּשֵּׂ֥ג לָבָ֖ן אֶֽת־יַעֲקֹ֑ב וְיַעֲקֹ֗ב תָּקַ֤ע אֶֽת־אָהֳלוֹ֙ בָּהָ֔ר וְלָבָ֛ן תָּקַ֥ע אֶת־אֶחָ֖יו בְּהַ֥ר הַגִּלְעָֽד׃

> Laban caught up with Jacob. Now Jacob had pitched his tent in the hill country, and Laban with his kinsmen camped in the hill country of Gilead.

**31:26** וַיֹּ֤אמֶר לָבָן֙ לְיַעֲקֹ֔ב מֶ֣ה עָשִׂ֔יתָ וַתִּגְנֹ֖ב אֶת־לְבָבִ֑י וַתְּנַהֵג֙ אֶת־בְּנֹתַ֔י כִּשְׁבֻי֖וֹת חָֽרֶב׃

> Then Laban said to Jacob, “What have you done by deceiving me and carrying away my daughters like captives of the sword?

**31:27** לָ֤מָּה נַחְבֵּ֨אתָ֙ לִבְרֹ֔חַ וַתִּגְנֹ֖ב אֹתִ֑י וְלֹא־הִגַּ֣דְתָּ לִּ֔י וָֽאֲשַׁלֵּחֲךָ֛ בְּשִׂמְחָ֥ה וּבְשִׁרִ֖ים בְּתֹ֥ף וּבְכִנּֽוֹר׃

> “Why did you flee secretly and deceive me, and did not tell me so that I might have sent you away with joy and with songs, with timbrel and with lyre;

**31:28** וְלֹ֣א נְטַשְׁתַּ֔נִי לְנַשֵּׁ֥ק לְבָנַ֖י וְלִבְנֹתָ֑י עַתָּ֖ה הִסְכַּ֥לְתָּֽ עֲשֽׂוֹ׃

> and did not allow me to kiss my sons and my daughters? Now you have done foolishly.

## Headline Findings

The exegetically strongest claims, each surfaced by more than one tool and each resting on verified counts:

1. **The unit is framed by God *seeing affliction*, and Jacob's service is told in the vocabulary of the release law.** Leah opens it: "the LORD has seen my affliction", רָאָה יְהוָה בְּעָנְיִי ("the LORD has seen my affliction", 29:32); Jacob closes it: "my affliction and the toil of my hands God has seen", אֶת־עָנְיִי וְאֶת־יְגִיעַ כַּפַּי רָאָה אֱלֹהִים (31:42). Between the two Jacob *serves* — עָבַד ("serve", 11× in 10 verses, against 23× in the whole book) — and is told he should not serve חִנָּם ("for nothing", 29:15; its only occurrence in Genesis; in Exodus only in the slave law, 21:2, 11). At the end he says "I served you … six years for your flock" (31:41): עָבַד ("serve") + "six" + "year" in one verse occurs elsewhere **only in the release laws** (Exod 21:2; Deut 15:12, 18; Jer 34:14), and with a wage word only at Deut 15:18. He would have been "sent away empty" (שִׁלַּחְתָּנִי רֵיקָם, 31:42) — send away + empty-handed in the Torah only here and Deut 15:13. And his wives complain "are we not reckoned foreigners (נָכְרִיּוֹת) by him? for he has sold us (מְכָרָנוּ)" (31:15): "foreign" + "sell" in one verse only Gen 31:15; Exod 21:8 (the daughter sold as a maidservant); Deut 14:21. Tested against every Genesis block of the same length, **Gen 29–31 is the closest in Genesis to Exod 21:1–11 and Deut 15:12–18 at every setting**. `[S: audit 2]` *High.* `[T]`

   **The exodus colour is real but thinner.** רָאָה ("see") with עֳנִי ("affliction") in one verse stands in the Torah only at Gen 29:32; 31:42; Exod 3:7; 4:31; Deut 26:7; and "it was told … that he had fled" (וַיֻּגַּד … כִּי בָרַח, 31:22) occurs elsewhere only at Exod 14:5 and 1 Sam 27:4. But at block level Gen 29–31 is no closer to Exod 3 or 14 than chance: the exodus layer rests on these two phrases. Hosea reads the chapters together with the exodus — "Israel served for a wife, and for a wife he kept (שָׁמָר)… by a prophet he was kept (נִשְׁמָר)" (Hos 12:13–14 [Eng 12:12–13]; there is no "sheep" in the Hebrew). `[S: audit 2]` *Moderate (exodus); high (Hosea).* `[T]` / `[I]` *Surfaced by:* Tools 3, 7, 10, 11 (Move 4 and the reverse table), Original Audience, Schnittjer step 6; claim audit 2 (G1, G2).

2. **Jacob says, in this unit, the two sentences with which Genesis ends.** "Am I in the place of God?" — הֲתַחַת אֱלֹהִים אָנֹכִי ("am I in the place of God?", 30:2), Jacob to the barren Rachel — occurs **once more in the whole Hebrew Bible**, at 50:19, where Rachel's son says it (הֲתַחַת אֱלֹהִים אָנִי, "am I in the place of God?") to the brothers who sold him. And "What is my transgression? What is my sin?" — פִּשְׁעִי ("my transgression") with חַטָּאתִי ("my sin"), 31:36 — is **the first occurrence of פֶּשַׁע ("transgression") in the canon**, and the pair פֶּשַׁע ("transgression") + חַטָּאת ("sin") stands in Genesis only here and at 50:17, where the brothers ask Joseph to forgive "the transgression of your brothers and their sin". Genesis closes (50:17–19) on Jacob's words from the house of Laban. *Surfaced by:* Tools 7, 10, 11 (Move 4), the checking stage (the claim audit had found 50:19's link to 22:13 and 44:33 and missed 30:2). *High.* `[T]`

3. **The narrator names God only at wombs and in speech; everywhere else God is the claim of the characters.** No divine name occurs in 29:1–30 — thirty verses of arrival, love, contract and the wedding-night substitution, set against nineteen occurrences of the covenant name in the parallel well-and-bride chapter (Gen 24). The narrator's own voice names God six times in 133 verses: at 29:31 (the LORD *saw* and *opened her womb*), 30:17 (*heard* Leah), 30:22 (*remembered*, *heard*, *opened her womb*), 31:3 (the LORD *said* to Jacob) and 31:24 (God *came* to Laban). "Opened her womb" occurs in the Hebrew Bible only at 29:31 and 30:22, and they bracket the births. The prosperity of 30:37–43 is narrated with no God at all — though its last verb, פָּרַץ ("break out", 30:43), is the verb of God's promise at Bethel (28:14), and occurs in Genesis only in those verses, 30:30 and 38:29. Jacob attributes the prosperity to God (31:5–12) and Laban to the LORD (30:27). *Surfaced by:* Tools 3, 6, 10, Schnittjer steps 1 and 5. *High (counts); moderate on what the restraint means.* `[T]` / `[I]`

4. **The search for Laban's stolen gods is planted for the search for Joseph's cup.** Theft (גָּנַב, "steal", 9× in 8 verses here, 10 of Genesis's 12), pursuit and overtaking (רָדַף + נָשַׂג, "pursue" + "overtake": 31:23, 25; then 44:4), a death-oath on the culprit by a man who does not know the culprit is his own (31:32; 44:9), a tent-by-tent or sack-by-sack search — חָפַשׂ ("search") occurs in Genesis only at **31:35 and 44:12** — ending with Rachel (31:33, a household order: Jacob, Leah, the maids, Rachel last) or with Rachel's son (44:12, eldest to youngest), the object in the possession of Rachel (31:34) or of Rachel's son. In 31 it is never found; in 44 it is, and Judah offers himself. Steal + search + find within a few verses occurs **nowhere else in the canon**, and the Greek tightens the link ("with whom it is found", 31:32 ↔ 44:9, nearly verbatim). `[S: audit 2]` *Surfaced by:* Tools 10, 11 (Move 4), Schnittjer step 5; claim audit 2 (G12, Confirmed). *High (lexical); high (pattern).* ⟨synthetic⟩ `[T]`

5. **The deceiver is deceived in a younger-for-elder substitution, and Laban's sentence is the comment the narrator withholds.** "It is not done so in our place, to give the younger before the firstborn" (29:26): הַבְּכִירָה ("the firstborn [daughter]") occurs in the Hebrew Bible only of Lot's daughters (19:31–37), here, and of Saul's (1 Sam 14:49); הַצְּעִירָה ("the younger") answers 25:23's צָעִיר ("the younger" son). Jacob's protest — מַה־זֹּאת עָשִׂיתָ לִּי ("what is this you have done to me?", 29:25) — is the question God asked the woman (3:13) and the deceived kings asked Abram (12:18) and Isaac (26:10); for the first time in Genesis the patriarch is the one asking it. His "why have you deceived me?" uses רִמָּה ("deceive"), the root of Isaac's verdict on him, "your brother came בְּמִרְמָה" ("with deceit", 27:35). *Surfaced by:* Tools 6, 7, 10, 11 (Move 4), Copycat. *High.* `[T]`

## The Sixteen Tools

### 1. Author's Purpose

**Book level (from the overview, Phase 0.5).** Genesis exists under one pressure — can the blessing outrun the curse, and through whom? — and answers it by narrowing to one seed, kept alive "through barrenness, deceit, famine and fratricide". `[S: overview]` This unit is where the narrowing *widens* again for the first time since 25:23: the one heir becomes eleven sons and a daughter, and the family that will be "Israel" is born.

**Passage level.** The unit does three things at once, and they are not separable.

- **It builds the house** (29:31–30:24). Every birth is named, and the names are speeches; the twelve tribes' founders are nearly all here, born of rivalry. `[T]`
- **It repays the deceiver** (29:15–30). The man who put on his brother's clothes in his blind father's presence is given the elder sister in the dark. The narrator draws no moral; the repetition does. `[T]`
- **It brings him out** (30:25–31:55). Twenty years of service end in a flight that is narrated as a release — God seeing, God taking the master's goods, God forbidding the master's pursuit. `[T]` / `[I]`

Put together: **God keeps the Bethel promise ("I will be with you… and will bring you back", 28:15) through a household in which nobody is blameless and nobody is in control — and he does it while staying almost entirely out of the narrator's sight.** The author's purpose is to show the promise surviving the promised family, and the God of the promise as the one who sees the unloved, opens wombs and judges between men. `[I]`

### 2. Context

#### Positional Necessity Check

**Preceding movement.** 25:19–28:22 ends with a promise and a vow. God at Bethel: "I am with you and will keep you wherever you go, and will bring you back to this land; for I will not leave you until I have done what I have promised" (28:15). Jacob: "*If* God will be with me and will keep me on this journey… and I return to my father's house in safety, then the LORD will be my God" (28:20–21). The previous dig's finding was that the vow is the promise "shrunk clause by clause and made conditional". `[S: 25:19–28:22 dig]` The movement has also set up a debt: 27:36 names Jacob the supplanter, and 27:44 sends him to Laban for "a few days".

**Necessity answer.** This unit is the test of both speeches. It exists here because 28:15 and 28:20–22 have made a claim that only twenty years can verify — and the unit reports, in Jacob's own mouth, that the first condition of his vow has been met: "the God of my father has been with me", הָיָה עִמָּדִי ("has been with me", 31:5), the very words of the vow's "if God will be with me" (יִהְיֶה אֱלֹהִים עִמָּדִי, 28:20 — that phrase only there). God then recalls the vow himself: "I am the God of Bethel, where you anointed a pillar, where you vowed a vow to me" (31:13). The unit is the hinge between promise and return. `[T]`

**Implication.** The unit is not a family saga between two theological chapters. It is the outworking of 28:15 in an Aramean household, and its last words before Laban goes home are "and he blessed them" (32:1 [Eng 31:55]) — the blessing reaching the household from which the chosen line was called out. The positional answer is sharpened after Tool 11: the vocabulary of release (Headline 1) means that Jacob's return is narrated as an exodus in miniature, before there has been an exodus.

**Revisit after Tool 11.** Move 2 has nothing to track — Genesis quotes no earlier text — but Move 4 sharpens the positional answer. 27:44's "a few days" (יָמִים אֲחָדִים, "a few days") recurs at 29:20 (and elsewhere only Dan 11:20): Rebekah's "few days" become seven years that *seemed* a few days, then twenty. The unit exists here because 27 sent him and 28 promised to bring him back; 29–31 is the "few days" that became a lifetime's middle.

#### Immediate context

**Immediately before.** 28:10–22, the ladder and the vow at Bethel — stones, a pillar, oil, and a promise that God will be "with" him. **Immediately after.** 32:2–3 [Eng 32:1–2]: "the angels of God met him" — מַלְאֲכֵי אֱלֹהִים ("angels of God") occurs in Genesis only at 28:12 and 32:2 [Eng 32:1] — and he names the place Mahanaim, "two camps". `[S: 25:19–28:22 dig, verified]` Then 32:4 [Eng 32:3] sends messengers to Esau. The unit is enclosed by angels, and the brother whose threat sent Jacob away (27:41–45) is waiting at the far end.

**Section.** The *toledot* of Isaac (25:19–35:29). The whole-book sweep splits it at 29:1 and 32:1; the overview makes 29:1–31:55 one unit and 32:1–33:20 the next. `[S]`

**The content of what Jacob does not see.** The Context tool asks, where the passage turns on a blindness, for its content. Jacob has been the one who sees and the others blind (27:1 Isaac's dim eyes). In this unit Jacob is the one who does not see: he does not see it is Leah until morning (29:25), and "Jacob did not know that Rachel had stolen them" (31:32). What he should have understood — from 27 — is that a younger child dressed to pass for an elder one, in front of a man who cannot see, is exactly what he did. `[T]` / `[I]`

### 3. Structure

The run divided the unit from the Hebrew before opening any secondary source. Scene changes, wage-talk and the two "what have you done?" exchanges give a symmetrical shape:

| Verses | Section | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **A** 29:1–14 | At the well: arrival, kiss, "my bone and my flesh" | Jacob enters Laban's house; kinship claimed |
| **B** 29:15–30 | "What shall your wages be?" — seven years, Leah in the dark, "what is this you have done to me?" | Laban outwits Jacob; the first contract |
| **C** 29:31–30:24 | The births: the LORD opens Leah's womb … God opens Rachel's womb | The house of Israel built; God's acts concentrated |
| **B′** 30:25–43 | "Name your wages" — flocks, rods, the white and the striped | Jacob outwits Laban; the second contract |
| **A′** 31:1–32:1 [Eng 31:55] | Flight, pursuit, "what have you done?", search, protest, heap and pillar, kiss and blessing | Jacob leaves Laban's house; kinship ended at a boundary |

**What carries the shape.** `[T]` throughout:

- **Wages** open both contract sections: מַשְׂכֻּרְתֶּךָ ("your wages", 29:15) and שְׂכָרְךָ ("your wages", 30:28).
- **"What is this you have done?"** — Jacob to Laban, מַה־זֹּאת עָשִׂיתָ לִּי ("what is this you have done to me?", 29:25); Laban to Jacob, מֶה עָשִׂיתָ ("what have you done?", 31:26).
- **The kiss.** נָשַׁק ("kiss") at 29:11, 13 (arrival) and 31:28; 32:1 [Eng 31:55] (departure) — four of its eleven occurrences in Genesis.
- **Stones.** Jacob rolls (גָּלַל, "roll", 29:3, 8, 10) the great stone from the well at the beginning; at the end he raises a stone as a pillar and the brothers pile a heap, גַּל ("heap", 31:46–52), from the same root.
- **The centre** is framed by the only two occurrences in the Hebrew Bible of "opened her womb" (29:31; 30:22), and its last birth triggers the outer movement: "when Rachel had borne Joseph, Jacob said to Laban, 'Send me away'" (30:25).

**The centre is not automatically the point** (Schnittjer's guardrail), but here the centre is where the narrator's own divine verbs cluster: four of his six namings of God are in 29:31–30:24. The outer sections are human contracts; the inner one is gift. That contrast is the shape's contribution. `[I]` *Moderate–high.*

**Checking stage.** Fishbane's well-known chiasm of the Jacob cycle (*Text and Texture*, 1979) places the births at the centre of the *whole* cycle, 25:19–35:22. `[S — recalled, not checked in the library]` The run's derivation was made blind, on the unit alone, and agrees on the centre; it does not depend on the larger chiasm. The whole-book sweep's statement that §12a is "framed by two deceptions of Jacob by Laban (the wives, 29:23–25; the wages, 31:7)" is compatible with this table but describes a frame, not the five-part symmetry. `[S: sweep]`

**The paragraphing.** In Leningrad the whole of 28:10–32:3 [Eng 28:10–32:2] is written without a single *petuchah* or *setumah* — 148 verses, from Bethel to Mahanaim, the angels of God at both ends. The marker detection works (it returns the *setumah* after 28:9 and the *petuchah* after 32:3), so the absence is real in this witness. A Jewish reading tradition holds that the weekly portion *Vayetze* (28:10–32:3) is the one portion in the Torah written without an internal break. `[S — recalled, not verified]` **This is an apparatus feature of one manuscript, not authorial, and its spread across manuscripts has not been checked.** `[unchecked — apparatus spread]` *Moderate at most; not for the overview until a library pass has been run.*

### 4. Linking Words

Narrative; the significant connectors only.

- **כִּי ("for, because, that") in the naming speeches.** Leah's first three names are grounded with כִּי ("for, because"): "*because* the LORD has seen… *for* now my husband will love me" (29:32); "*because* the LORD heard that I am unloved" (29:33); "*because* I have borne him three sons" (29:34). At the fourth the grounding drops and the speech turns: "This time I will praise the LORD" (29:35), עַל־כֵּן ("therefore") naming him Judah. The logic moves from the husband to God. `[T]`
- **הַפַּעַם ("this time")** — 29:34, 35; 30:20. It runs Leah's hope like a refrain: "this time my husband will be attached to me" (29:34); "this time I will praise the LORD" (29:35); and, after her sixth son, "this time my husband will dwell with me" (30:20). She returns to the hope she had given up. `[T]`
- **וְאִם־אַיִן ("and if not")** — Rachel's "give me children, *or else* I die" (30:1). The condition is answered with a death in childbirth at 35:16–19, after the second son she asked for at 30:24. `[I]`
- **לוּלֵי ("if not, unless")** — the counterfactual of 31:42: "*if* the God of my father … had not been for me, surely now you would have sent me away empty-handed." Jacob's whole case rests on a conditional that names God as the only reason he leaves with anything. `[T]`
- **אִם … וְאִם ("if … and if")** — Jacob's rehearsal of Laban's wage changes (31:8) and Laban's covenant conditions (31:50, 52). The two men end bound by conditions, not by trust. `[T]`

### 5. Parallels

Prose narrative; no sustained poetic parallelism. Three prose parallels bear on meaning:

- **31:43**, Laban's four-fold possession: הַבָּנוֹת בְּנֹתַי וְהַבָּנִים בָּנַי וְהַצֹּאן צֹאנִי וְכֹל … לִי־הוּא ("the daughters are my daughters, and the children are my children, and the flocks are my flocks, and all … is mine"). A staircase of possessives that answers his first words, "surely you are my bone and my flesh" (29:14). Kinship as ownership, from the first speech to the last. `[T]`
- **31:40**, Jacob's merism: בַיּוֹם … חֹרֶב וְקֶרַח בַּלָּיְלָה ("by day the heat … and the frost by night"), with 31:39's "stolen by day and stolen by night" — total exposure. `[T]`
- **31:52**, the mirrored oath: "I will not pass this heap to you, and you will not pass this heap and this pillar to me". `[T]`

### 6. Narrator's Comment

Every authorial intrusion, in order:

| Verse | Comment | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 29:17 | "Leah's eyes were weak/tender, but Rachel was beautiful of form and face" | Sets up the love and the substitution; the word for Leah's eyes is disputed (Tool 8) |
| 29:20 | "they seemed to him but a few days because of his love for her" | Inside information; picks up Rebekah's "a few days" (27:44) |
| 29:30 | "he loved Rachel more than Leah" | The comparative that 37:3 will repeat of Joseph |
| **29:31** | "**The LORD saw** that Leah was unloved, and He opened her womb; but Rachel was barren" | The first divine name of the unit, and a theological verdict |
| **30:17** | "**God heard** Leah" | |
| **30:22** | "**God remembered** Rachel, and God heard her and opened her womb" | "God remembered X" is used before of Noah (8:1) and Abraham (19:29), next of the covenant (Exod 2:24) |
| 30:43 | "the man became exceedingly prosperous" | No God named — contrast Jacob's own account at 31:9 — but the verb is פָּרַץ ("break out"), the verb of the Bethel promise (28:14) |
| **31:3** | "**the LORD said** to Jacob, 'Return…'" | The only direct divine speech to Jacob in the narrator's voice |
| 31:19–20 | "Rachel stole the teraphim… Jacob stole the heart of Laban" | The leading verb, applied to both |
| **31:24** | "**God came** to Laban the Aramean in a dream of the night" | God speaks directly to the non-elect man as often as to the elect one |
| 31:32 | "**Jacob did not know** that Rachel had stolen them" | Dramatic irony, and the second time Jacob "did not know" (cf. 28:16) |
| 31:47 | "Laban called it Jegar-sahadutha, but Jacob called it Galeed" | Two languages, one heap: the family has become two peoples |

**The pattern.** Outside the births and the two night-speeches, the narrator never says what God did. The rods (30:37–43) are told as Jacob's technique; it is Jacob who says "God has taken away your father's livestock and given them to me" (31:9), and only in his report do we hear of a dream (31:10–12). The narrator neither confirms nor corrects him — and then, in 31:24, has God speak to Laban, which confirms that God has been watching. `[T]` / `[I]` *High on the data; moderate on the reading.*

### 7. Vocabulary

**Proper-noun inventory** (Tool 7c, run before triage). *Persons:* Jacob, Laban, Nahor, Rachel, Rebekah, Leah, Zilpah, Bilhah, Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Dan, Naphtali, Gad, Asher, Issachar, Zebulun, Dinah, Joseph, Isaac, Abraham. *Peoples:* "sons of the east", the Aramean. *Places:* the land of the sons of the east, Haran, Paddan-aram, the River, the hill country of Gilead, Bethel, Canaan, Jegar-sahadutha, Galeed, Mizpah. *Divine titles:* the LORD, God, the God of Bethel (הָאֵל בֵּית־אֵל), the God of my father, the God of Abraham, the Fear of Isaac, the God of Nahor. *Numbers:* three (flocks, 29:2; days, 30:36; 31:22), seven (years ×4; days of pursuit, 31:23), fourteen, six, ten (times), twenty (years). *Routine:* Haran, Nahor, Rebekah, Isaac, Canaan, Paddan-aram. *Candidates worked below:* Laban, Rachel, the sons' names, Bethel, Gilead/Galeed, Mizpah, the Fear of Isaac, ten, twenty.

**(a) Big Bible words, at their first or defining use.**

- **פֶּשַׁע ("transgression") — 31:36.** Its first occurrence in the canon; next at 50:17, then Exod 22:8 [Eng 22:9], the case law about disputed animals and goods "for every matter of transgression". Jacob uses the word of a property dispute, and the Torah's first law using it is about exactly that. ⟨verified: lemma list, whole canon⟩ *High.* `[T]`
- **כָּבוֹד ("glory, wealth") — 31:1.** Its first occurrence in the canon: Laban's sons say Jacob "has made all this כָּבוֹד ("wealth, glory")" out of their father's goods. The Bible's first "glory" is livestock in the mouths of resentful brothers-in-law. Next in Genesis at 45:13 ("all my glory in Egypt") and 49:6. *High (count); the theological weight is the reader's.* `[T]` / `[I]`
- **מָשַׁח ("anoint") — 31:13.** Its first occurrence in the canon: "where you anointed a pillar". At Bethel the narrator said only that Jacob "poured oil" (28:18, יָצַק, "pour"); it is God who calls the act anointing. The next occurrence is the anointing of Aaron's sons (Exod 28:41). *High.* `[T]` — trajectory under Christological Reading.
- **חִנָּם ("for nothing, gratis") — 29:15.** Only here in Genesis; in Exodus only 21:2, 11, where the Hebrew slave and the slave-wife "go out free, for nothing". *High (count); see Tool 11.* `[T]`

**(b) Familiar words used in a Bible-specific way.**

- **עֳנִי ("affliction") — 29:32; 31:42.** Not "unhappiness". In Genesis it is the word of 16:11 (Hagar, "the LORD has heard your affliction"), these two verses, and 41:52 (Ephraim: "God has made me fruitful in the land of my affliction"). Each time it is the affliction of someone the household has ill-treated — a slave-wife, an unloved wife, a cheated nephew, a sold brother — and in three of the four God is said to have heard or seen it. `[T]`
- **שְׂנוּאָה ("unloved", literally "hated") — 29:31, 33.** The legal term of Deut 21:15 for the less-favoured of two wives. The NASB95's "unloved" is right for the sense and loses the word's later recurrence: the brothers "hated" Joseph (37:4, 5, 8). שָׂנֵא ("hate") in Genesis: 24:60; 26:27; 29:31, 33; 37:4, 5, 8. The unloved wife's sons hate the loved wife's son. `[T]`
- **עִמָּדִי / עִמָּךְ ("with me / with you").** The presence formula: "I will be with you" (31:3), "the God of my father has been with me" (31:5), "God did not allow him to hurt me" (31:7). The claim audit counted the formula at sixteen places in Genesis, 31:3 and 31:5 among them. `[S: audit]`

**(c) Names.** Transliteration is given where the sound is the point.

- **Laban, לָבָן (*Lāḇān*) — "white".** The common adjective לָבָן ("white") occurs in Genesis at 30:35, 30:37 (twice) and 49:12, and לִבְנֶה ("poplar", "white tree") only at 30:37 and Hos 4:13. In one verse Jacob peels rods of *libneh*, makes *ləḇānôṯ* ("white streaks") and exposes *hallāḇān* ("the white") (30:37), to outbreed a man called *Lāḇān*. Laban, in his turn, has just "removed every one with white in it" (30:35). And "Jacob stole the heart of Laban", וַיִּגְנֹב יַעֲקֹב אֶת־לֵב לָבָן (*lēḇ Lāḇān*, 31:20) — the heart and the man sound alike. *High (the words are there); moderate–high (the play).* `[T]` / `[I]`
- **Rachel, רָחֵל (*Rāḥēl*) — "ewe".** The common noun רָחֵל ("ewe") occurs four times in the Hebrew Bible: Gen 31:38; 32:15 [Eng 32:14]; Isa 53:7; Song 6:6. Jacob, defending his shepherding, says "your ewes (רְחֵלֶיךָ) and your female goats have not miscarried" (31:38) — the plural of his wife's name, in the chapter in which she has hidden her father's gods. She was introduced as a shepherdess (29:9). *High (lexical); moderate (the play).* `[T]` / `[I]`
- **Leah, לֵאָה (*Lēʾāh*).** Etymology uncertain ("cow" from Akkadian, or "weary" from the verb לָאָה, "be weary"); the text makes no play on it. *Uncertain — consider a lexicon.* `[S]`
- **The sons.** Every name except Dinah's receives a spoken explanation, and every explanation is a pun: *Rəʾûḇēn* on רָאָה ("see", 29:32); *Šimʿôn* on שָׁמַע ("hear", 29:33); *Lēwî* on יִלָּוֶה ("will be attached", 29:34); *Yəhûḏāh* on אוֹדֶה ("I will praise", 29:35); *Dān* on דָּנַנִּי ("has vindicated me", 30:6); *Naptālî* on נַפְתּוּלֵי אֱלֹהִים נִפְתַּלְתִּי ("wrestlings of God I have wrestled", 30:8); *Gāḏ* on "fortune" (30:11, the ketiv/qere verse); *ʾĀšēr* on בְּאָשְׁרִי ("happy am I", 30:13); *Yiśśāśḵār* on שְׂכָרִי ("my wages", 30:18); *Zəḇulûn* on זְבָדַנִי … יִזְבְּלֵנִי ("has endowed me… will honour/dwell with me", 30:20); *Yôsēp* twice, on אָסַף ("has taken away", 30:23) and יֹסֵף ("may He add", 30:24). **Dinah alone has none** (30:21) — the silence that 34 will fill. *High.* `[T]`
- **Judah.** The root of יְהוּדָה ("Judah"), יָדָה ("praise"), occurs in Genesis only at 29:35 and 49:8: "Judah, your brothers shall praise you". The fourth son of the unloved wife receives the sceptre. *High.* `[T]`
- **Issachar and the wage root.** שָׂכָר ("wages, reward") occurs seven times in six verses of Genesis; apart from 15:1 ("your reward shall be very great", God to Abram) every one is in this unit (30:18, 28, 32, 33; 31:8 ×2). The cognate מַשְׂכֹּרֶת ("wages") is in Genesis only at 29:15; 31:7, 41; the verb שָׂכַר ("hire") only at 30:16 (twice: "I have surely hired you"). Jacob bargains twice for wages; he is himself hired for a night by his wife with mandrakes; and the son born of that night is named "wages". `[T]` *High.*
- **Gilead / Galeed.** גִּלְעָד ("Gilead") is named three times as "the hill country of Gilead" (31:21, 23, 25) before Jacob names the heap גַּלְעֵד ("heap of witness", 31:47–48), which occurs only there. The narrator lets the place's later name stand before the event that explains it. *High.* `[T]`
- **Mizpah.** הַמִּצְפָּה ("the watchtower") with יִצֶף ("may He watch", 31:49) — a spoken pun. *High.* `[T]`
- **The Fear of Isaac**, פַּחַד יִצְחָק — only 31:42 and 31:53 (the second as "the Fear of his father Isaac"). A divine title found nowhere else in the canon. *High.* `[T]`

**Canonical history (Tool 7c triggers).**

- **Bethel.** First canonical occurrence at 12:8, not 28:19. `[S: 25:19–28:22 dig, verified]` Here God names himself by it — "I am the God of Bethel" — and recalls the anointed pillar and the vow (31:13). *Outstanding word:* the vow (28:20–22) is left hanging at 31:13 and paid only at 35:1–7. The unit activates it without discharging it. *High.* `[T]`
- **"Ten times" — עֲשֶׂרֶת מֹנִים (31:7, 41).** מֹנִים ("times") occurs in the Hebrew Bible only in these two verses. Jacob at Bethel promised a tenth (28:22, עַשֵּׂר אֲעַשְּׂרֶנּוּ, "I will surely give a tenth"); Laban changed his wages "ten times". The sound and number are shared; the roots are the same (עשׂר, "ten"). *Moderate (the play is the reader's).* `[I]`
- **Twenty years** (31:38, 41). No first-occurrence freight; it is the measure of the "few days" (27:44). `[T]`

**Character dossier — Laban** (load-the-story rule; 24:29–60 re-read). First seen running to the well "when he saw the ring and the bracelets on his sister's wrists" (24:29–30). He speaks the covenant name — "come in, blessed of the LORD" (24:31) — and with Bethuel says "the matter comes from the LORD; we cannot speak to you bad or good", רַע אוֹ־טוֹב ("bad or good", 24:50 — that phrase only there). He tries to keep the bride "a few days, at least ten", יָמִים אוֹ עָשׂוֹר ("days, or ten", 24:55). In this unit: he runs again (29:13); he speaks the LORD's name again (30:27; 31:49); and God commands him not to "speak … from good to bad", מִטּוֹב עַד־רָע ("from good to bad", 31:24, 29 — that phrase only in these two verses). The man who once said *we cannot speak bad or good, the thing is from the LORD* now has to be told so by God. *High (verbal); the irony `[I]`.*

### 8. Translations

**NASB95 against the ESV and the KJV, where they diverge in a way that bears on a finding.**

- **שְׂנוּאָה ("unloved", 29:31, 33).** NASB95 "unloved"; ESV "hated"; KJV "hated". The ESV and KJV keep the word that returns at 37:4, 5, 8 ("they hated him"), so a hearer of the ESV hears the chain; a reader of the NASB95 does not. The NASB95 is the better gloss of the sense and the weaker witness to the chain. *High.*
- **גָּנַב לֵב ("steal the heart", 31:20, 26) and גָּנַב ("steal", 31:27).** NASB95 "deceived"; ESV "tricked"; KJV "stole away unawares" (31:20), "stolen away unawares" (31:26). Both modern versions level the leading verb at three of its nine occurrences, so the English "steal" chain begins only at 31:19 and resumes at 31:30. The KJV keeps it. *High.*
- **אָח ("brother", 29:15 and the "kinsmen" of 31).** "Because you are my brother" (29:15) — NASB95 "relative", ESV "kinsman", KJV "brother". In 31:23, 25, 32, 37, 46, 54 both modern versions give "kinsmen". The word occurs 13 times in 10 verses of the unit, and only the KJV lets it be heard; the loss matters for the Deut 15:12 echo ("your brother, a Hebrew", Tool 11). *High.*
- **פֶּשַׁע ("transgression") / חַטָּאת ("sin") (31:36).** NASB95 "transgression … sin" here and at 50:17; ESV "offense … sin" here, "transgression … sin" at 50:17. The NASB95 carries Headline 2's second echo; the ESV breaks it. *High.*
- **הֲתַחַת אֱלֹהִים ("in the place of God?", 30:2; 50:19).** ESV "Am I in the place of God?" at both; NASB95 "Am I in the place of God?" (30:2) and "am I in God's place?" (50:19). The ESV carries the identity exactly. *High.*
- **עֵינֵי לֵאָה רַכּוֹת ("Leah's eyes were weak/tender", 29:17).** NASB95, ESV "weak"; KJV "tender eyed". רַךְ ("tender, soft") occurs in Genesis three times: the calf "tender and good" (18:7), Leah's eyes (29:17), the children who are "frail" (33:13). Nothing in the word is pejorative; the contrast with Rachel ("*but* Rachel was beautiful") is what makes "weak" the common rendering. Both readings are defensible; the preacher should not build on either. *Moderate.*
- **רָאָה בְּעָנְיִי ("has seen my affliction", 29:32) / עָנְיִי … רָאָה ("my affliction … has seen", 31:42).** NASB95 "has seen my affliction" / "God has seen my affliction" — the frame is audible. ESV "has looked upon my affliction" / "God saw my affliction" — slightly varied. *Moderate.*

**Ancient versions check** (the trigger fires: the moderns agree at several weight-bearing places). Each divergence is classified under the three-way triage.

- **31:20 — category 1, translation loss.** Rahlfs ἔκρυψεν δὲ Ιακωβ Λαβαν ("Jacob hid from Laban") for "stole the heart of Laban". The Greek cannot carry the *lēḇ / Lāḇān* sound-play or the leading verb here; it keeps κλέπτω ("steal") at 31:19, 30, 32 and κλέμματα ("thefts") at 31:39.
- **30:2 / 50:19 — category 1, a translator's decision that breaks an echo.** Rahlfs Μὴ ἀντὶ θεοῦ ἐγώ εἰμι ("Am I in the place of God?", 30:2) but τοῦ γὰρ θεοῦ εἰμι ἐγώ ("for I am God's", 50:19). The Hebrew of the two is identical but for the pronoun form; the Greek makes Joseph say something else. Headline 2 is a fact about the Hebrew. *High (both checked in the Rahlfs export).*
- **31:47 — category 1.** Βουνὸς τῆς μαρτυρίας / Βουνὸς μάρτυς ("heap of the witness" / "heap, witness") renders both names in Greek; the Aramaic–Hebrew pair cannot survive translation.
- **31:7, 41 — category 1, a rare word guessed.** τῶν δέκα ἀμνῶν / δέκα ἀμνάσιν ("of the ten lambs", "with ten lambs") for עֲשֶׂרֶת מֹנִים ("ten times"). The translator met a word that occurs nowhere else and read it as a kind of animal. No different Hebrew text need be supposed. *Moderate.*
- **30:27 — category 1/2.** εὐλόγησεν γάρ με ὁ θεὸς τῇ σῇ εἰσόδῳ ("for God blessed me at your coming") for "the LORD has blessed me because of you" (בִּגְלָלֶךָ, "on your account"). The Greek loses the בִּגְלַל ("on account of") link to 12:13 and 39:5 (Tool 11) and has θεός for the covenant name.
- **Divine names — category 2, minor.** Rahlfs has θεός for MT יְהוָה ("the LORD") at 30:24, 30:27 and 31:49; the Samaritan Pentateuch, by BHS's apparatus, has יהוה ("the LORD") for MT אֱלֹהִים ("God") at 31:7, 9, 16. - **31:13, 31:44–52, 31:53, 30:11** — category 2; see Textual Variants.

### 9. Tone and Feel

The register is **comedy with a bitter edge**, and it darkens. The well scene is romantic and slightly absurd — a man who rolls alone the stone that needs every shepherd, kisses a woman he has just met and weeps aloud (29:10–11). The wedding night is farce turned cruel: "in the morning, behold, it was Leah" (29:25) is one of the most compressed punchlines in Scripture, and it lands on a woman who did not ask to be the joke. `[I]`

The births are the emotional centre and the most painful part: a wife naming son after son in the hope of being loved (29:32–34), a sister saying "give me children or I die" (30:1), a husband shouting "Am I in the place of God?" (30:2), and a night sold for a handful of love-apples (30:14–16). Leah's sequence from "now my husband will love me" to "this time I will praise the LORD" (29:32–35) is the only movement towards rest in the unit — and she slips back from it at 30:20. `[T]`

The flocks section is a trickster tale (striped, speckled, spotted; peeled white rods; the strong to Jacob, the feeble to Laban). The flight is suspense, and the search of the tents is the second farce: a father groping through a saddle a daughter is sitting on, while she apologises that "the way of women is upon me" (31:35). Then the register changes abruptly to a lawsuit (31:36–42) — the most impassioned speech Jacob makes anywhere in the unit — and closes in a wary treaty and a cool farewell. *Soundtrack:* a folk fiddle, with a cello underneath.

**For the preacher.** Do not preach it either as romance or as moral fable. The text is funny, and the joke is on people who are hurting. Let both be heard.

#### The reverse table — where this passage is heard downstream

Genesis plants; these texts harvest. The triad is run on each from the downstream side, Move 1 being the downstream text's own context.

**Exod 3:7; 4:31; Deut 26:5–7 — God sees affliction** *(high on the words; moderate–high on the design)*
*Source context:* Exod 3:7 is the burning bush: "I have surely seen the affliction of My people … I have heard their cry … I know their sufferings". Deut 26:5–7 is the firstfruits creed: "An Aramean, perishing, was my father … the Egyptians … afflicted us … we cried to the LORD … and the LORD heard our voice and saw our affliction and our toil and our oppression." `[T]`
*Book usage:* within the Torah, רָאָה ("see") + עֳנִי ("affliction") stands at Gen 29:32; 31:42; Exod 3:7; 4:31; Deut 26:7 — and no other place. `[T]`
*OT-to-OT:* the creed's opening word, אֲרַמִּי ("an Aramean"), is the gentilic used of Laban at 31:20, 24. אֲרַמִּי ("Aramean") with אָבַד ("perish") stands only at Deut 26:5. Whether the "Aramean" of the creed is Jacob (the usual modern reading) or Laban (the reading that the Targum and the Passover Haggadah give, "an Aramean sought to destroy my father") is disputed. `[S — the Targumic reading recalled, not checked]`
*What it adds:* the creed that Israel recited at the harvest names an Aramean, an affliction and a God who saw — the three things Genesis 29–31 opens and closes with. Jacob's twenty years are Israel's first rehearsal of its own story. `[I]`

**Exod 21:2–6; Deut 15:12–18 — the release of a Hebrew servant** *(high on the words; moderate–high on the design)*
*Source context:* the first law of the Covenant Code: "If you buy a Hebrew slave, he shall serve six years; and in the seventh he shall go out free, for nothing (חִנָּם)… If his master gives him a wife and she bears him sons or daughters, the wife and her children shall belong to her master" (Exod 21:2–4). Deuteronomy's version: "If your brother, a Hebrew, is sold to you, he shall serve you six years … When you set him free, you shall not send him away empty-handed (רֵיקָם). You shall furnish him liberally from your flock … as the LORD your God has blessed you … for he has given you six years with double the service of a hired man (שָׂכִיר)" (Deut 15:12–14, 18). `[T]`
*Book usage:* the shared vocabulary, each item verified in the WLC — אָח ("brother", 29:15 / Deut 15:12); עָבַד ("serve"); חִנָּם ("for nothing", 29:15 / Exod 21:2); שֵׁשׁ שָׁנִים ("six years", 31:41 / Exod 21:2; Deut 15:12, 18); שָׂכָר ("wages", hire); שִׁלַּח ("send away", 30:25; 31:27, 42 / Deut 15:12–13, 18); רֵיקָם ("empty-handed", 31:42 / Deut 15:13); צֹאן ("flock", 30:31–43 / Deut 15:14); בֵּרַךְ ("bless", 30:27, 30 / Deut 15:14, 18). And Laban's "the daughters are my daughters, the children are my children" (31:43) is the master's claim in Exod 21:4. `[T]`
*OT-to-OT:* Exod 21 and Deut 15 are themselves the Torah's two tellings of the release law, the second explicitly grounded in the exodus ("remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt", Deut 15:15). `[T]`
*What it adds:* Laban plays the master who keeps the wife and children and would send the servant away with nothing; God plays the part the law will give the master — he furnishes the servant from the flock and sends him out blessed. The release law and the exodus are both, in Genesis, already happening to Jacob. `[I]` **Direction:** Genesis is canonically first; for the Torah's first readers the two texts were present together (Original Audience). The pattern is not a claim that Genesis "quotes" the law.

**Exod 14:5–9 — the report of flight and the pursuit** *(high on the phrase)*
*Source context:* "When the king of Egypt was told that the people had fled … Pharaoh and his servants … said, 'What is this we have done, that we have let Israel go from serving us?' … the Egyptians pursued them … and overtook them" (14:5, 9). `[T]`
*Book usage:* וַיֻּגַּד לְ… כִּי בָרַח ("it was told to … that he/they had fled") — Gen 31:22; Exod 14:5; 1 Sam 27:4 only; with מַה־זֹּאת עָשִׂינוּ ("what is this we have done?"), שִׁלַּח ("let go") and עָבַד ("serve") in Exod 14:5, and רָדַף + נָשַׂג ("pursue" + "overtake") in Exod 14:9 (Gen 31:23, 25). `[T]`
*What it adds:* Laban is narrated as Pharaoh before there is a Pharaoh of the oppression; and he is stopped not by the sea but by a dream. `[I]`

**Exod 22:8–12 [Eng 22:9–13] — transgression, the stolen and the torn** *(moderate–high)*
*Source context:* the deposit and herding laws: "for every matter of transgression (פֶּשַׁע) — for ox, for donkey, for sheep …" (22:8); "if it is actually stolen from him, he shall make restitution to its owner; if it is all torn to pieces, let him bring it as evidence; he shall not make restitution for what has been torn" (22:11–12 [Eng 22:12–13]). `[T]`
*Book usage:* פֶּשַׁע ("transgression") first at Gen 31:36, next at 50:17, then Exod 22:8. Jacob: "that which was torn (טְרֵפָה) I did not bring to you; I bore the loss of it myself; you required it of my hand, whether stolen (גְּנֻבְתִי) by day or stolen by night" (31:39). `[T]`
*What it adds:* Jacob claims to have borne more than the Torah's own law would require of a herdsman — no evidence-bringing, restitution for the torn. Laban demanded what the law will forbid. `[I]` **And a forward echo inside Genesis:** טָרַף ("tear") next appears at 37:33, "Joseph has surely been torn to pieces", spoken when the brothers *bring* the evidence to their father. *Moderate.*

**Lev 15:19–20; Lev 18:18; Deut 21:15–17 — the law reads the household** *(moderate–high each)*
- *Lev 15:20:* "everything on which she sits (תֵּשֵׁב עָלָיו) shall be unclean" — of a menstruating woman. Rachel "sat on them (וַתֵּשֶׁב עֲלֵיהֶם)" and claims "the way of women is upon me" (31:34–35). To a reader with Leviticus, Laban's gods have been made unclean, whether or not she was telling the truth. `[I]`
- *Lev 18:18:* "you shall not take a woman as a rival to her sister … while she is alive". `[T]` The narrative shows the rivalry the law forbids (30:1, 8, 15). `[I]`
- *Deut 21:15–17:* "If a man has two wives, the one loved and the other unloved (שְׂנוּאָה), and both … have borne him sons, and the firstborn son belongs to the unloved, … he cannot make the son of the loved the firstborn before the son of the unloved." `[T]` That is Jacob's household exactly, and 37:3 ("Israel loved Joseph more") is the temptation the law names. Reuben's loss of the firstborn's place is given its own reason in Genesis (35:22; 49:3–4), and 1 Chr 5:1–2 explains the transfer to Joseph. `[I]` / `[T]`

**Num 18:20; Deut 10:9; 12:12; 14:27, 29; 18:1; Deut 32:9 — "portion and inheritance"** *(moderate)*
חֵלֶק ("portion") with נַחֲלָה ("inheritance") in one verse stands in the Torah at Gen 31:14 (Rachel and Leah: "Have we still any portion or inheritance in our father's house?"), at Num 18:20 and Deut 10:9; 12:12; 14:27, 29; 18:1 (Aaron and Levi have none — "the LORD is his inheritance"), and at Deut 32:9 ("the LORD's portion is His people; Jacob is the allotment of His inheritance"). The daughters, disinherited by their father, use the formula the Torah keeps for those whose inheritance is God; and the Song of Moses gives the answer: Jacob is God's. `[T]` / `[I]`

**Hos 12:13–14 [Eng 12:12–13] — the prophets' reading** *(high)*
"Jacob fled (בָּרַח) to the field of Aram, and Israel served (עָבַד) for a wife, and for a wife he kept (שָׁמַר) [sheep]. By a prophet the LORD brought Israel up from Egypt, and by a prophet he was kept (נִשְׁמָר)." `[T]` Hosea sets Gen 29–31 and the exodus side by side, with שָׁמַר ("keep") in both verses: Jacob kept sheep; Israel was kept. This is the canon's own warrant for Headline 1. (The Greek of Hos 12:13 in Swete is a different sentence; the observation is a fact about the Hebrew.)

**1 Sam 14:49; 18:17–20; 19:11–17 — Michal replays Rachel** *(high on the words; moderate–high on the design)*
*Source context:* "the names of his two daughters: the name of the firstborn (הַבְּכִירָה) Merab and the name of the younger (וְשֵׁם הַקְּטַנָּה) Michal" (1 Sam 14:49). Saul offers the elder to David for service (18:17), gives her to another (18:19), then gives the younger, who loves David (18:20), for a bride-price. When David flees (בָּרַח, 19:12), Michal takes the teraphim (וַתִּקַּח … אֶת־הַתְּרָפִים, 19:13), covers them with goats' hair and a garment, and deceives her father's men; Saul: "Why have you deceived me thus (רִמִּיתִנִי)?" (19:17). `[T]`
*Book usage:* וְשֵׁם הַקְּטַנָּה ("and the name of the younger") occurs in the Hebrew Bible only at Gen 29:16 and 1 Sam 14:49; הַבְּכִירָה ("the firstborn [daughter]") only at Gen 19:31–37; 29:26; 1 Sam 14:49; the teraphim in the Torah only at Gen 31:19, 34, 35. `[T]`
*What it adds:* the Former Prophets tell David's escape from Saul in the vocabulary of Jacob's escape from Laban — two daughters, elder offered first, the younger loving, service for a bride, flight, teraphim, a daughter deceiving her father, and Laban's own verb in Saul's mouth. A later author has read Gen 29–31 closely enough to write with it, and the goats' hair and the garment come from 27 as well. `[I]`

**Ruth 4:11 — Rachel and Leah who built the house** *(high on the words)*
"May the LORD make the woman … like Rachel and Leah, both of whom built (בָּנוּ) the house of Israel" (Ruth 4:11), in Ephrathah and Bethlehem, where Rachel is buried (35:19). `[T]` The verb is Rachel's at 30:3 (אִבָּנֶה, "I shall be built"). Rachel stands first; the Ketuvim remember the two rivals as co-builders. `[I]`

**Jer 31:15 → Matt 2:18 — Rachel weeping for her children** *(high on the citation; moderate on its link to this unit)*
*Source context:* Jer 31:15, "Rachel weeping for her children; she refuses to be comforted for her children, because they are no more" — set in the book of consolation (Jer 30–33), followed at once by "your children will return" (31:16–17). `[T]`
*Book usage:* Matthew quotes it of the slaughter at Bethlehem (Matt 2:18; the NA28 export agrees with the SBLGNT on the wording of the line about Rachel; NA28's apparatus records θρῆνος καί, "lamentation and", before κλαυθμός as a later addition taken from the Greek of Jeremiah). Rachel is the only one of the unit's women named in the NT; Leah and Laban are not named there at all (SBLGNT lemma search; control: Rachel returned at Matt 2:18). `[T]`
*OT-to-OT:* Jeremiah's Rachel is the mother who said "give me children, or else I die" (Gen 30:1) and died bearing her second son (35:16–19). `[I]`
*What it adds:* the woman whose whole speech in this unit is a demand for sons becomes, in the canon, the mother who weeps for them — and Matthew sets her weeping at the birth of the one in whom her children are given back. `[I]`

**John 4:5–14 — the well and Jacob** *(moderate)*
The Samaritan woman's "our father Jacob, who gave us the well" (John 4:12) refers to another well, at Sychar. `[T]` But the scene is a well, a stranger, a woman who has had several husbands, and a man who offers water that a stone does not cover; and the question "Are you greater than our father Jacob?" puts this unit's Jacob, who rolled the stone and watered the flock (29:10), beside Jesus. `[I]` *Moderate; the betrothal type-scene is a scholarly category* `[S]`.

### 12. Genre

Hebrew narrative of the patriarchal cycle, with three embedded forms: **birth-naming speeches** (29:31–30:24), a **legal dispute** (31:36–42, with the lawsuit vocabulary רִיב ("contend") and יָכַח ("decide") and the courtroom appeal to "our kinsmen"), and a **treaty** with witness-heap, oath and meal (31:44–54). The well-meeting at 29:1–14 is an instance of what Robert Alter called the betrothal type-scene (Gen 24; Exod 2:15–21) `[S]`; the departures from the type here — no prayer, no gifts, no God — are noted under Tool 13 and in Headline 3. **Read it as history told for theology:** it happened, and it is selected to say something. Avoid reading the rods (30:37–42) either as Bronze Age science or as a lesson in technique (see Pitfalls).

### 14. Bible Timeline

**Where the passage sits.** Patriarchal era; the middle of the Jacob cycle; the twenty years between Bethel and Peniel. Israel's twelve tribes have their founders born here — eleven of the twelve, with Benjamin to come (35:18) — so this is the birth of the people who will be enslaved, rescued and given the law. `[T]`

**Where we are.** After the exodus Genesis anticipates, after the law that reads Laban's household, after the cross. Read from here, the sons born of rivalry include Levi, from whom the priesthood comes, and Judah, from whom the king comes, and from whom Christ comes (Matt 1:2–3). `[T]`

**Canonical position.** Torah, first book; Genesis presupposes nothing. `[S: overview]` For this unit that has one sharp consequence: every later echo — Exodus, the release laws, Deuteronomy's creed, Hosea, Samuel, Ruth, Jeremiah — is reading Genesis, not the other way round. The question of how Genesis came to be written is outside this report. `[I]`

**Between then and now.** The God who "saw" Leah's and Jacob's affliction is the God of the burning bush and of the cross; the "Fear of Isaac" is the God whose fear is the beginning of wisdom; the promised presence ("I will be with you", 31:3) is Immanuel (Matt 1:23; 28:20). `[I]`

### 15. Who Am I?

- **Jacob** is not the model and not simply the villain. He is the bearer of the promise who is being repaid in his own coin and kept by a God he does not see working. He is a *recipient*: the reader identifies with him as one who is kept despite himself. Not "be like Jacob". `[I]`
- **Leah** is the representative of the unloved, and the text gives her the first divine name in the unit and the one movement towards rest (29:35). A hearer may legitimately identify with her — *as one seen by God*, not as a moral exemplar. `[T]` / `[I]`
- **Rachel** — loved, barren, envious, a thief of gods, and remembered by God. Representative of the person who has what others envy and wants what she cannot get. `[I]`
- **Laban** is the foil: the man who uses the covenant name (30:27; 31:49), is blessed because of the chosen one, and cannot let go of anything. Also *representative*: the non-elect man whom God addresses (31:24) and who blesses at the last (32:1). `[I]`
- **The sons** are the people of God in embryo; we are, in Christ, among their heirs (Gal 3:29). `[I]`
- **God** is the protagonist, and mostly off-stage. The temptation to make Jacob the hero, or Leah's patience the lesson, is the temptation to make the passage about us. `[I]`

### 16. So What?

**Stage 1 — the author's intended response.** To trust the God who keeps his promise through a family that keeps nothing — who sees the unloved, opens the closed womb, protects the cheated, restrains the powerful and brings the servant out with more than he came with — and to stop treating his gifts as wages to be negotiated. `[I]`

**Stage 2.**
- *Worldview:* God is present where he is not named. The narrator's restraint teaches the reader to look for God at the points where he says God acted — the womb, the night, the word — and not to read success as proof of blessing (30:43 names no God) or suffering as proof of absence (31:42).
- *Stop:* bargaining with God as with Laban — "what shall your wages be?" — and treating children, love or prosperity as earnings. Stop building rival claims inside a family on who is loved more (29:30 → 37:3).
- *Start:* Leah's "this time I will praise the LORD" (29:35) — praise grounded not in a changed husband but in a God who has seen. And Jacob's honest accounting before God (31:36–42), which names affliction and toil without pretending.
- *Motive:* God has seen affliction before he is asked (29:31) and has been "with" Jacob before Jacob admits it (31:5). The gospel grounding is not "work like Jacob", but "the God who brought Jacob out brought Israel out, and has brought us out in Christ".

**Four audiences.**
- *For me:* where do I bargain for what God gives?
- *For a Christian friend:* the unloved wife; the woman who cannot conceive (handle with care — Difficult Verses); the worker cheated "ten times".
- *For the church:* a congregation of rivals is still the house God is building (Ruth 4:11's "both of whom built").
- *For an unbeliever:* the Bible's founding family is not a family of saints; God's faithfulness is the only thing in it that holds.

**Prayer.** *Lord, you saw Leah when she was unloved and Jacob when he was cheated; see us. Forgive us for bargaining for what you give, and for loving some of your children more than others. You were with Jacob when he did not know it; teach us to praise you "this time", and to trust you for the way home.*

#### Grammatical and syntactical features

- **The disjunctive clauses that turn the plot.** וְרָחֵל עֲקָרָה ("but Rachel was barren", 29:31); וְלָבָן הָלַךְ לִגְזֹז ("now Laban had gone to shear", 31:19); וְרָחֵל לָקְחָה אֶת־הַתְּרָפִים ("now Rachel had taken the teraphim", 31:34). Each subject-first clause stops the sequence and hands the reader something the characters do not know. *High.* `[T]`
- **הִנֵּה ("behold") at the punchline.** וַיְהִי בַבֹּקֶר וְהִנֵּה־הִוא לֵאָה ("and in the morning, behold, it was Leah", 29:25) — the reader is put in Jacob's eyes at the moment of discovery. *High.* `[T]`
- **Infinitive absolute.** אָכוֹל ("entirely consumed", 31:15), הָלֹךְ הָלַכְתָּ … נִכְסֹף נִכְסַפְתָּה ("you have surely gone … you longed greatly", 31:30), שָׂכֹר שְׂכַרְתִּיךָ ("I have surely hired you", 30:16). The emphatic doubling clusters where people are claiming rights over one another. *Moderate–high.* `[T]`
- **The plural verb of 31:53.** "The God of Abraham and the God of Nahor … judge (יִשְׁפְּטוּ, plural) between us." Laban's grammar treats them as two. The Samaritan and the LXX have the singular (Textual Variants). Jacob swears by one, the Fear of Isaac. *Moderate.* `[T]` / `[I]`
- **Aramaic in the Torah.** יְגַר שָׂהֲדוּתָא ("heap of witness", 31:47) are the only two words in the whole Torah that the WLC morphology tags as Aramaic (control: the same tag is returned throughout Dan 2:4–7:28). Laban names the heap in Aramaic, Jacob in Hebrew. The family has become two peoples with two languages. *High.* `[T]`

### Textual Variants

**30:11 — ketiv בגד ("with fortune"), qere בָּא גָד ("fortune has come").**
- *The issue:* Leningrad writes one unpointed word, בגד ("with fortune"), and directs it to be read as two, בָּא גָד ("fortune has come").
- *Evidence:* BHS's apparatus: the Syriac and the Targums read as the qere; "probably read with the ketiv, with the LXX and the Vulgate, בְּגָד" ("with fortune", "how fortunate"). Rahlfs has Ἐν τύχῃ ("in good fortune") — the ketiv read as one word. `[S: BHS apparatus; Rahlfs export]`
- *Position:* the ketiv as one word ("with fortune!") has the older Greek witness and BHS's preference; the qere gives the same sense with a verb. Either way the name is a pun on גָּד ("fortune"). *Moderate.*
- *Preaching implication:* none of substance; the NASB95's "How fortunate!" follows the ketiv, the ESV's "Good fortune has come!" the qere.

**31:13 — "I am the God of Bethel".**
- *The issue:* MT הָאֵל בֵּית־אֵל ("the God of Bethel", literally "the El of Bethel") — an article on a construct, which is irregular. Rahlfs: ἐγώ εἰμι ὁ θεὸς ὁ ὀφθείς σοι ἐν τόπῳ θεοῦ ("I am the God who appeared to you in the place of God"), and adds καὶ ἔσομαι μετὰ σοῦ ("and I will be with you") at the end.
- *Evidence:* BHS: the LXX (with Targums Jonathan and the Palestinian Targum) has "who appeared to you in", and BHS suggests "probably insert" it. `[S: BHS apparatus]`
- *Position:* the Greek reading explains the grammar and could have fallen out by haplography; the MT is the harder reading and is matched by El-bethel as a name at 35:7. No default for either. The Greek's "and I will be with you" is very probably a harmonisation with 31:3. *Moderate; unsettled.*
- *Preaching implication:* none for the sense — the God of Bethel recalls the pillar and the vow in both.

**31:44–52 — the arrangement of the covenant.**
- *The issue:* MT 31:44 "let us cut a covenant, I and you, and let it be (וְהָיָה) a witness" has no clear antecedent for "it". Rahlfs rearranges: 31:44 adds "Behold, no one is with us; see, God is witness between me and you" (MT 31:50b); 31:46 adds Laban's "This heap bears witness" (MT 31:48a); 31:48 combines MT 31:48 and 31:51–52; there is no Greek 31:51; 31:49 renders Mizpah as Ἡ ὅρασις ("the sight"). The Greek also adds "and drank" at 31:46 and 31:54.
- *Evidence:* BHS proposes inserting "let us make a heap" at 31:44, or reading the clause of 31:50b there "with the LXX". `[S: BHS apparatus]`
- *Position:* the MT's repetitions (heap and pillar; Galeed and Mizpah; two witness speeches) are the harder text, and the Greek looks like a translator's smoothing of them. Source criticism has long treated the MT here as composite. `[S]` The run takes no position on composition and preaches the MT as it stands. *Moderate.*
- *Preaching implication:* the covenant's suspicion — two witnesses, two names, two languages, two conditions — is in every witness. Nothing hangs on the variant.

**31:53 — "judge between us".**
- *The issue:* MT plural יִשְׁפְּטוּ ("may they judge"); the Samaritan and the LXX (κρινεῖ) singular; the LXX* and two manuscripts lack "the God of their father". `[S: BHS apparatus]`
- *Position:* the plural is the harder reading and very probably original; the singular is a theological correction. *Moderate–high.*
- *Preaching implication:* Laban's two gods and Jacob's one (Original Language Observations).

**Divine names (29:31–31:49).** The Samaritan has יהוה ("the LORD") for MT אֱלֹהִים ("God") at 31:7, 9, 16; Rahlfs has θεός for MT יְהוָה ("the LORD") at 30:24, 27 and 31:49. Any count or distribution of the divine names is a fact about Leningrad. *High (the data); low (the impact on preaching).*

**Paragraphing (28:10–32:3).** Not a variant in the usual sense, but an apparatus feature: Leningrad has no paragraph marker inside the 148 verses. `[unchecked — apparatus spread]` (Tool 3; Open Questions.)

### Historical and Cultural Background

#### Setting

- **Time period:** the patriarchal era as Genesis presents it; no external date is given or needed. `[T]`
- **Location:** "the land of the sons of the east" (29:1); Haran (29:4); Paddan-aram (31:18); the River — the Euphrates in the NASB95's rendering (31:21); the hill country of Gilead, east of the Jordan (31:21–25). `[T]` Haran lies in upper Mesopotamia, some 450–500 miles north-east of Beersheba. `[S]`
- **Political context:** clan society, no state. Laban's authority is a household head's; the covenant is between two household heads with their "brothers" as witnesses. `[T]`

#### What the original audience knew

1. **Bride-price and service.** A groom's family paid the bride's family; Jacob, with nothing (cf. 32:11 [Eng 32:10], "with only my staff I crossed this Jordan"), pays in labour. Rachel and Leah's complaint — "he has sold us, and has also entirely consumed our money" (31:15) — implies that the bride-price should have come back to them in some form, and did not. `[S]` *Moderate.*
2. **Two sisters to one man.** Practised; forbidden later by Lev 18:18. `[T]`
3. **Maidservants as surrogate mothers.** Attested in second-millennium contracts (Nuzi, Old Assyrian) `[S]`; already in Genesis at 16:1–4. Children born "on the knees" (30:3) of the wife are hers: cf. 50:23. `[T]` / `[S]`
4. **Mandrakes** (דּוּדָאִים, "love-apples", 30:14–16; Song 7:14 [Eng 7:13]). The plant was held to aid fertility. `[S]` The narrative undercuts it: Rachel gets the mandrakes and Leah conceives. `[T]`
5. **Teraphim.** Household figurines of some kind — small enough to hide in a camel saddle (31:34), large enough to pass for a man in a bed under a garment (1 Sam 19:13). Their function is debated: ancestor figures, divination, or — on an older reading from the Nuzi tablets — a title to the inheritance. **That last reading is widely doubted since the 1960s** (Greenberg, *JBL* 81, 1962). `[S — recalled, not checked in the library]` The text calls them Laban's "gods" (31:30) and has them put away at 35:2–4. *Uncertain on function.*
6. **Herding contracts.** A shepherd bore losses from theft beyond a set rate; losses to predators could be cleared by showing the carcass (cf. Exod 22:12 [Eng 22:13]; Code of Hammurabi §§261–267). `[S]` Jacob's "I bore the loss of it myself" claims more than custom required.
7. **Menstrual impurity.** The Torah's reader knew Lev 15:19–20. `[T]`
8. **Folk belief about breeding.** That what a mating animal sees marks its young was widespread in antiquity. `[S]` The narrative reports Jacob acting on it and then has God claim the result (31:12).

#### What this changes about how we read

- The *rods* are not a disclosure of animal genetics and not a revelation of a technique; they are what Jacob thought would work, and the dream says what did (31:10–12). `[I]`
- The sisters' "he has sold us" (31:15) turns 29:15–30 from a romance into a transaction in which the women were the goods. `[I]`

### Original Audience Reception

#### Canonical audience

- **Canonical section:** Torah, first book. For the Torah the compositional and the canonical audience coincide in wilderness Israel, with the tabernacle in the camp and the law in hand (Original Audience extension). `[S: method]`
- **What the canonical reader knows:** Genesis 1–28 — the curse and the blessing, Abraham's call, Isaac's preservation, the theft of the blessing, Bethel. And, because the Torah is read as one work, the reader *also* knows the exodus, the release laws and the creed of Deut 26. `[I]`
- **Canonical register:** Genesis and Exodus–Deuteronomy illuminate one another in both directions (Tool 11's reverse table).

#### The first hearers

- **Who they were:** a people whose own story is slavery in a foreign land, a God who saw their affliction, a hasty departure with the master's goods, and pursuit. `[T: Exodus]`
- **Their situation:** living under law that forbids what this household did (two sisters; preferring the loved wife's son) and commands what God did (release with goods; restitution rules).
- **What they brought:** the names of their own tribes. Every hearer belonged to one of these sons — Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Dan, Naphtali, Gad, Asher, Issachar, Zebulun, Joseph (and Benjamin). `[I]`

#### Where the passage fits their world

1. **Their tribal names are born of rivalry and grief.** A Levite hearing that Levi was named "now my husband will be attached to me" (29:34); a Judahite hearing "this time I will praise the LORD" (29:35); a Danite hearing Rachel's "God has vindicated me" (30:6). The tribes are not founded on virtue. `[I]`
2. **Their exodus has happened before.** Jacob served, was cheated, cried out (in effect), was seen, was told to go, left with the master's goods, was pursued and was protected by God at night. `[I]`
3. **Their law reads the story.** Lev 18:18 and Deut 21:15–17 judge the household; Exod 21:2–4 and Deut 15:12–18 judge Laban. `[I]`

#### Surprises, shocks, comforts, disturbances

- **Shocking:** the wedding-night substitution; the sale of a night for mandrakes; a daughter sitting on her father's gods and claiming menstrual impurity.
- **Surprising:** that God speaks to Laban (31:24) and that the LORD blesses Laban because of Jacob (30:27); that the first divine name in the unit is given to the unloved wife (29:31).
- **Comforting:** God sees affliction (29:32; 31:42); God remembers (30:22); God forbids the oppressor to harm (31:7, 24, 29).
- **Disturbing:** the founding family is a household of rivals, thieves and liars, and the God of the promise works through it without endorsing it.

#### What we bring that they didn't

- A romantic idea of marriage, which makes 29:18–20 a love story and hides the transaction (31:15).
- An individualism that hears "Jacob" and not "Israel"; for them the story was about the nation's fathers and mothers.
- A tendency to read prosperity as blessing and blessing as reward.

#### Implication for the sermon

Recover the transaction beneath the romance, and the exodus beneath the family quarrel. Name the God who sees, from the text's own two framing verses.

### Biblical-Theological Themes

#### 1. God sees affliction (exodus)

- **Where this passage sits:** the first two of five Torah verses joining רָאָה ("see") and עֳנִי ("affliction") (29:32; 31:42), before Exod 3:7; 4:31; Deut 26:7. `[T]`
- **What comes before:** Hagar, "the LORD has heard your affliction" (16:11), and El-roi, "the God who sees" (16:13). `[T]`
- **What comes after:** the exodus; the creed; the Psalms ("You have seen my affliction", Ps 31:8 [Eng 31:7]); the cross, where the Servant is "afflicted" (Isa 53:4, 7) and the Father sees and vindicates. `[I]`
- **What it contributes:** the God who rescues Israel from Egypt has already rescued Israel's father from Aram, on the same terms. *High (the words); moderate–high (the theme).*

#### 2. The blessing reaching the nations

- **Where this passage sits:** the first time Genesis narrates an outsider *saying* he has been blessed because of the chosen man (30:27), within days of the promise "in you and in your seed all the families of the earth shall be blessed" (28:14). Laban's last act is to bless (32:1 [Eng 31:55]). `[T]`
- **What comes before:** 12:3; 18:18; 22:18; 26:4; 28:14. `[S: overview]`
- **What comes after:** 39:5 (Potiphar's house blessed "on account of Joseph"); Gal 3:8, 14 ("the blessing of Abraham might come to the Gentiles in Christ Jesus"). `[T]`
- **What it contributes:** the promise is not suspended while the heir behaves badly. *High.*

#### 3. The house of Israel built

- **Where this passage sits:** the building of the house — אִבָּנֶה ("I shall be built", 30:3) and Ruth 4:11 ("Rachel and Leah, both of whom built the house of Israel"). `[T]`
- **What comes after:** the twelve tribes (49), the twelve stones of the high priest (Exod 28:21), the twelve apostles and the twelve gates (Rev 21:12). `[I]`
- **What it contributes:** the people of God is built by God out of rivalry, not by the rivals.

**Confluence.** *Climactic convergence:* the house is built (3) while the father is afflicted and brought out (1), and the outsiders are blessed through him (2). The three meet at 31:42–32:1: "God has seen my affliction … and Laban … blessed them". `[I]`

### Schnittjer Pass

**Skeleton:** Genesis · the *toledot* of Isaac (25:19–35:29) · the seed narrowed to one man is widened to a house, and the promise of 28:15 is kept outside the land.

**Findings.**

1. *(Step 1 — selectivity and time.)* Twenty years pass; the narrator brakes at the well (14 verses for one day), the wedding night (29:21–25), the births (each named and explained), the rods (30:37–42, a method told in six verses) and the search (31:33–35). He accelerates through the second seven years (29:30, one clause) and the six years for the flock (30:43, one verse). The weight falls on the moments of exchange. *High.* `[T]`
2. *(Step 2 — space.)* The unit begins with Jacob "lifting his feet" — וַיִּשָּׂא יַעֲקֹב רַגְלָיו ("Jacob lifted his feet", 29:1; the phrase only here) — eastward, and ends with him crossing the River and setting his face to Gilead (31:21), then the heap that is a border: "I will not pass over this heap to you" (31:52). Out of the land and back to its edge; east of the Jordan is where Moses' Israel will also stand. *Moderate–high.* `[T]` / `[I]`
3. *(Step 3 — shape.)* A five-part symmetry with the births at the centre (Tool 3); the centre is gift, the frame is contract. The well type-scene with its expected elements missing — no prayer, no gift, no God (contrast Gen 24) — is a pattern-break absence with a cited pattern (24:12–27, nineteen occurrences of the covenant name). *Moderate–high.* `[T]` / `[S: type-scene category]`
4. *(Step 4 — repetition and leading word.)* Leading words: עָבַד ("serve") in 29–30, גָּנַב ("steal") in 31, שָׂכָר ("wages") across both; the five-verse frame of "see … affliction". *High.* `[T]`
5. *(Step 5 — first versus second reading.)* First reading: the rods worked. Second reading, after 31:10–12: God showed Jacob what he was doing. First reading: Jacob's oath at 31:32 is bravado; second reading, after 35:16–19 and 44:9–12, it is ominous. First reading: Rachel is the heroine of the love story; second reading: she is the one who carries her father's gods into the family. *High.* `[T]` / `[I]`
6. *(Step 6 — polyacoustic intertextuality.)* Progressive imaging forward: Jacob's twenty years are narrated so that Israel's exodus will sound like them (Tool 11's reverse table). Multiple imaging: the servant who serves for a bride and brings her home (Hos 12:13), the shepherd who bears the losses (31:38–40), the rejected wife who bears Judah — echoes that build toward the Messiah without any one of them being "Jesus". *Moderate–high.* `[I]`
7. *(Step 7 — irreducibility.)* The theology is the story: God keeps the promise through people who do not keep faith. A proposition-list ("God is faithful; sin has consequences") loses the joke on Jacob, the grief of Leah, and the silence of the narrator. `[I]`

**Echoes and signals.** Leading words עָבַד ("serve") / גָּנַב ("steal") / שָׂכָר ("wages"); structural symmetry with a gift-centre; forward imaging of the exodus and of the release law; the 30:2 / 31:36 → 50:17–19 plant.

**Nil returns.** None material.

### Christological Reading

#### Type of Christological connection

- **Trajectory (high):** the line of Judah (29:35 → 49:8–10 → Matt 1:2–3); the blessing to the nations through the seed (30:27 → Gal 3:8, 14).
- **Contrast (high):** "Am I in the place of God?" (30:2) — the man who cannot give life, against the Son who is.
- **Typology (moderate):** the servant who serves for his bride and brings her out; the shepherd who bears the losses. *Tests:* category (servant / shepherd — passes); NT precedent (the shepherd: John 10:11; the servant who wins his bride: Eph 5:25 — passes in kind); escalation (passes — Jacob bears the loss of sheep, Christ lays down his life); authorial pattern (the text makes much of Jacob's shepherding and service — passes in part). Three to four of four — *moderate–high* for the shepherd, *moderate* for the servant-bridegroom.
- **Christophany (moderate):** "the angel of God" who says "I am the God of Bethel" (31:11–13) — the messenger who speaks as God, as at 16:7–13 and 22:11–16, and whom Jacob will later name "the angel who has redeemed me from all evil" (48:16). `[T]` / `[S: christophany reading]`

#### How the passage points to Christ

1. **The God who sees.** 29:32 and 31:42 name the God of the exodus; the exodus is the pattern of Christ's work (Luke 9:31, ἔξοδος). The God who saw Leah's affliction is the God who sent his Son into it. `[I]`
2. **The shepherd who bore the losses.** "That which was torn of beasts I did not bring to you; I bore the loss of it myself" (31:39); "by day the heat consumed me and the frost by night" (31:40). Jacob's own words describe a shepherd who absorbs the cost of the flock; and Jacob ends his life naming God as his shepherd (48:15; 49:24). The Good Shepherd lays down his life for the sheep (John 10:11). *Moderate–high.*
3. **"Am I in the place of God?"** Jacob's honest refusal (30:2) and Joseph's (50:19) mark the limit of every human mediator. The one who *is* in the place of God, and who opens the closed womb of the grave, is Christ. *Contrast; moderate.*
4. **The anointing.** The first מָשַׁח ("anoint") in the canon is a stone at the house of God (31:13). The anointed one (מָשִׁיחַ, "anointed") is named from this root; and the stone, the house of God and the anointed come together in the one whom the builders rejected (Ps 118:22; 1 Pet 2:4–7). *Trajectory; moderate — the link is canonical reflection, not this text's argument.* `[I]`
5. **Rachel weeping** (Jer 31:15 → Matt 2:18) — the mother who demanded sons weeps for them at the birth of the Son. *Moderate.*

#### Trajectory

- **What the passage longs for:** a husband who loves (Leah), a child (Rachel), a fair master (Jacob), a release (Jacob), a home (28:21).
- **What Christ fulfils:** the God who sees the unloved; the giver of life; the master who frees and furnishes; the way home.
- **What still remains:** the house is built but not at peace (37); the boundary heap (31:52) is a truce, not reconciliation.

#### Moralism check

- **The "be like X" temptation:** "be patient like Jacob", "work hard and God will bless", "be like Leah, who learned to praise".
- **The gospel grounding:** the house is built by God's opening of wombs; the release is God's doing (31:7, 9, 24, 42).
- **Christ as hero:** not Jacob's hard work but the faithful Shepherd and Servant who bears the loss and brings his people out.

#### Confidence

*High* on the Judah trajectory and the 30:27 → Gal 3 trajectory; *moderate–high* on the shepherd; *moderate* on the servant-bridegroom, the anointing and Rachel-weeping links.

### Difficult / Contested Verses

#### 29:21–30 — the substituted bride; polygamy; two sisters

- **Category:** ethical; pastoral.
- **The difficulty:** a bride is substituted without her consent being mentioned; a man marries two sisters and their maids.
- **Common mishandlings:** treating it as comedy only; or making Laban the only villain.
- **Rhetorical function:** the repayment of 27, in kind (Headline 5).
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the narrator judges by consequence, not comment; the later law forbids two sisters (Lev 18:18). `[T]`
- **Handling:** acknowledge; do not moralise.

#### 30:1–24 — infertility, surrogacy, rivalry

- **Category:** pastoral.
- **The difficulty:** "Give me children, or else I die" (30:1); Jacob's anger (30:2); maidservants as surrogates.
- **Rhetorical function:** the turn from human demand to divine gift; God opens and remembers.
- **Handling:** **there will be people in the room for whom 30:1 is their own sentence.** Do not use Rachel's eventual son as a promise that every prayer for a child will be answered with one; do let 30:2 stand as honest — no human being, husband or doctor or preacher, is in the place of God. Name the pain before naming the providence.

#### 30:37–43 — the rods

- **Category:** apologetic.
- **The difficulty:** the method rests on a folk belief.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the narrator reports it without endorsement; Jacob attributes the result to God, and the dream (31:10–12) shows the outcome determined before the rods. The text is not teaching biology. `[T]` / `[I]`
- **Handling:** address briefly.

#### 31:19, 34–35 — Rachel's theft and her claim

- **Category:** ethical.
- **The difficulty:** the matriarch steals her father's gods and lies (or perhaps does not) about her period.
- **Interpretive options:** (a) she wanted the gods for protection or worship; (b) for inheritance (the Nuzi reading, now doubted); (c) to deprive her father of divination (cf. 30:27). `[S]` The text gives no motive. Whether 31:35 is true is not said.
- **Handling:** say that the text does not tell us why; say what happens to the gods (35:2–4).

#### 31:32 — Jacob's rash oath

- **Category:** doctrinal/pastoral.
- **The difficulty:** Jacob pronounces death on whoever has the gods, and Rachel dies in childbirth at 35:16–19.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** the text never connects the two; a reader may. `[I]`
- **Handling:** do not preach the connection as cause; note the shadow if at all.

#### 31:49 — the "Mizpah benediction"

- **Category:** commonly misused.
- **The misuse:** "The LORD watch between me and thee, when we are absent one from another" — engraved on lockets and read at partings as a blessing of affection.
- **What an honest exegesis surfaces:** it is the opening clause of a threat between two men who do not trust each other, followed by "if you mistreat my daughters … God is witness" (31:50) and a boundary neither may cross "for harm" (31:52). `[T]`
- **Handling:** correct it plainly and gently.

## Preaching Pitfalls

### Pitfall: "The deceiver deceived" as a karma sermon

- **What it looks like:** "Jacob got what he deserved; what goes around comes around."
- **Why it's wrong:** the repayment is real (Headline 5) but the narrator never calls it punishment, and the unit's main movement is God *keeping* Jacob despite it (31:5, 7, 42). Measure-for-measure is a pattern of the story, not its gospel.
- **The corrective:** preach the repayment as the mirror in which Jacob — and we — see what we have done, and then preach the God who brings the cheat home.

### Pitfall: Hard work pays off

- **What it looks like:** "Jacob worked faithfully for twenty years and God prospered him — be a diligent employee."
- **Why it's wrong:** the prosperity is credited by the text to God (31:9, 12; the 28:14 verb at 30:43), the method is Jacob's scheme, and the employer is a cheat. Copycat and timeline errors together.
- **The corrective:** preach the God who sees the cheated worker and brings him out — and, from the law, the God who forbids sending the servant away empty (Deut 15:13).

### Pitfall: The rods as a lesson in technique

- **What it looks like:** a sermon on "spiritual visualisation", or an apology for Bronze Age biology.
- **Why it's wrong:** the dream (31:10–12) relocates the cause.
- **The corrective:** one sentence, then move on.

### Pitfall: Leah the plain girl, Rachel the pretty one

- **What it looks like:** "God looks on the heart, not on appearances", with Leah as the unattractive heroine.
- **Why it's wrong:** רַכּוֹת (29:17) is not clearly pejorative; the text's point is not her looks but her standing — שְׂנוּאָה ("unloved") — and God's seeing. And Leah slips back into rivalry (30:9–20).
- **The corrective:** preach the God who saw her affliction (29:31–32) and the praise she reached (29:35), not her appearance.

### Pitfall: The Mizpah blessing

- **What it looks like:** 31:49 read as a tender farewell.
- **Why it's wrong:** it is a mutual threat (31:50–52).
- **The corrective:** say so.

### Pitfall: The births as a model

- **What it looks like:** surrogacy, polygamy or rivalry presented as "God's way of building a family".
- **Why it's wrong:** descriptive narrative; the law later forbids two sisters (Lev 18:18) and favouring the loved wife's son (Deut 21:15–17).
- **The corrective:** God builds his house *through* this family's sins, not by endorsing them.

## Book-Overview Tensions

Surfaced, not resolved.

1. **Early↔Late Echo Table — six candidate rows**, each verified in the WLC: **30:2 → 50:19** (הֲתַחַת אֱלֹהִים, "in the place of God?", the only two in the Hebrew Bible); **31:36 → 50:17** (פֶּשַׁע ("transgression") + חַטָּאת ("sin"), the only two in Genesis); **31:19–35 → 44:1–12** (steal, pursue/overtake, death-oath, חָפַשׂ ("search") only 31:35 and 44:12); **3:13 → 12:18 → 26:10 → 29:25** ("what is this you have done?"); **28:14 → 30:30, 43** (פָּרַץ, "break out"); **12:13 → 30:27 → 39:5** (בִּגְלַל, "on account of"). 2. **The *nākar* row.** The overview's chain lists 31:32 but its echo-table row runs 27:23 → 37:32 and says "the same imperative twice". The imperative הַכֶּר ("recognise") occurs three times (31:32; 37:32; 38:25), and the first is Jacob's.
3. **The *taḥat* triad** (claim audit, 7 Sep 2026: 22:13 → 44:33 → 50:19). 50:19's sentence is verbatim at 30:2, which the audit did not record; and its statement that three of Genesis's thirty *taḥat* "carry the substitutionary sense" is low — 4:25, 30:2, 30:15, 36:33–39 and 44:4 also use the word in the sense "instead of, in exchange for". 4. **The reverse table** (where Genesis is used downstream) could gain: Exod 3:7; 4:31; Deut 26:5–7 (see + affliction); Exod 14:5–9 (told he had fled; pursued and overtook); Exod 21:2–4 and Deut 15:12–18 (the release law); Exod 22:8–12 (transgression, stolen, torn); Hos 12:13–14; 1 Sam 14:49; 18:17–19:17 (Michal); Ruth 4:11; Jer 31:15 → Matt 2:18. ⟨synthetic; constituents verified; the design claims are unaudited⟩

6. **Weight.** The overview rates the unit Standard. On this run it carries the centre of the Jacob cycle (the births), plants both of Genesis's last sentences (Headline 2), founds Levi and Judah, and is the Torah's first exodus pattern. A case for ⭐ HIGH. *For Patrick to decide.*
7. **Extent.** The unit's natural seam in Leningrad is 32:3 [Eng 32:2]; the overview's 31:55 [Heb 32:1] leaves the closing angels of God (32:2 [Eng 32:1]) to the next unit, though they answer 28:12. *For Patrick to decide.*
