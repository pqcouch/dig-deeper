## How to read this document

This is a *sweep*, not sixteen solo runs. Each of the sixteen preaching units below receives proportional depth: a Positional Necessity Check (Phase 0.6, always), the tools that carry the most weight for that unit, the Schnittjer Pass (all of Genesis is Torah), the live extensions, and two or three Headline Findings. Briefer tools are folded in; genuinely inapplicable tools get a one-line N/A. Warrant tags — **[T]** derivable from the text, **[I]** a reasonable inference from it, **[S]** supplied by a secondary source and held provisionally — are used throughout, and confidence is flagged (*high / moderate / uncertain*) on every lexical, textual, and intertextual claim.

For any ⭐ HIGH-weight unit (flagged in the header), a full solo dig-deeper should still be run before pulpit preparation; sweep depth is planning-grade, not preaching-grade for those. A **Cross-Passage Convergent Findings** capstone closes the document — the arguments no single unit establishes but the sixteen jointly demonstrate.

## Book-Level Threads (Phase 0.5 — held in peripheral vision throughout)

Four threads from the overview orient the whole sweep. They inform where "deeply worked" lies; they do not drive the exegesis.

**Christological trajectory.** Genesis manufactures categories the NT fills: *seed / zeraʿ* (3:15 → 12:3 → 22:17 → 49:10 → Gal 3:16); a *substitution* grammar built by diction (the ram *taḥat* "instead of" Isaac, 22:13 → Judah *taḥat* Benjamin, 44:33); *presence* ("the LORD was with X" → Immanuel, Matt 1:23); *image / second Adam* (1:26–28 → Col 1:15; Rom 5); *king from Judah* (49:8–12 → Rev 5:5); *suffering-and-exaltation* (Joseph → Acts 7). The Book's Ache: a longing for a true human who fulfils the mandate, is righteous, blesses the nations, and dwells in God's presence — every character comes close and fails; the book ends in a coffin.

**Intertextual map.** Live external sources are thin (Genesis is mostly *alluded to* rather than *alluding*); the dense intertextuality is **internal** (early↔late echoes) and **forward** (the NT reading Genesis). Key internal welds: 3:5 "like God" ↔ 50:19 "Am I in the place of God?"; 1:28 ↔ 47:27 (near-verbatim); Table of 70 nations (10) ↔ 70 descendants (46:8–27); Babel's grasped name (11:4) ↔ Abram's given name (12:2); *tēbâ* (ark, 6–9 / Moses' basket) and *ʾārôn* (Joseph's coffin, 50:26). The verbal welds proving Gen 38's placement: *haker-nāʾ* (37:32 ↔ 38:25) and *ʿārēv* (38:17–18 ↔ 43:9/44:32).

**Preaching traps (book-level).** (1) Hurrying 1–11 as mere prologue; (2) "days-and-dates" apologetics smothering Genesis 1's functional-temple theology; (3) moralism — "be like Abraham / Joseph," "don't be like Jacob"; (4) Moses-is-me self-insertion where Christ is the type; (5) resolving the coffin the book deliberately leaves open; (6) the "onanism" misreading of 38:9 (the sin is refusing levirate duty).

**Presenting situation.** Wilderness Israel, redeemed from Egypt, camped between Egypt and a land of giants — frightened, prone to return. Genesis is *pastoral literature before it is history*: it strengthens faith. It runs a polemic against ANE cosmologies (the moon is a lamp, 1:16; the sea is gathered water). Moses writes looking back; the tabernacle stands among the first hearers, re-opening the question of presence Eden closed. Dominant emotional register: **faithful waiting under threat**.

## §1 — Genesis 1:1–2:3 · Creation ⭐ HIGH

**Load-bearer:** 1:26–2:3 — image, dominion, blessing, climaxing in rest as the goal. **Decision:** Focus.

### Positional Necessity Check
**Preceding movement:** none — this is the book's (and canon's) opening. The check runs *forward*: what does this passage set up that the rest of Genesis requires? **[T]**
**Necessity answer:** Everything downstream presupposes 1:1–2:3. The fall (§3) is only intelligible as the disordering of *this* order; the flood (§5) is the un-making of *these* separations; the mandate (1:28) is what Israel's multiplication (Exod 1:7) fulfils; rest (2:1–3) is the goal the whole Bible reaches for (Heb 4). **[T]**
**Implication:** Preach Genesis 1 as the establishing of categories, not as a science lecture. Its polemical edge (against Enuma Elish, Egyptian cosmogony) is the reason for its shape. *Confirms overview trap #2.*

### Structure (Tool 3)
The seven-day frame is the structure: days 1–3 form *realms* (light, sky/sea, land), days 4–6 fill them with *rulers/occupants* (luminaries, birds/fish, animals/humanity) — a forming/filling symmetry answering the twin problem of 1:2, *tōhû* (formless) and *bōhû* (empty). **[T]** Day 7 stands outside the symmetry as the goal. This is deliberate architecture, not chronology-for-its-own-sake.

### Vocabulary (Tool 7)
- *bārāʾ* ("create," 1:1) — subject is always God; no raw material named. *High confidence.* **[T]**
- *tōhû wābōhû* (1:2) — "formless and void"; non-order, not a rival power. The sea and darkness are *creatures/conditions*, not deities. *High.* **[T]**
- *ṣelem / dĕmût* ("image / likeness," 1:26) — royal-representative language; humanity as God's regents. *High.* **[T]**
- *bārak* ("bless," 1:22, 28) — dynamic empowerment for fruitfulness and rule, not static favour (overview trap #3). *High.* **[T]**

**The seven-structure [S, overview]:** 1:1 = seven Hebrew words; 1:2 = fourteen; "it was good" ×7; God speaks ten times; 2:1–3 = 35 words. Numerical completeness is the text's own signal. Flagged for the preacher; *moderate* on the exact counts without re-counting.

### Genre (Tool 12)
The "days" are genuinely contested — literal 24-hour vs ordered-functional structure. Name the options; note that the passage's *own* signals (the forming/filling frame, the functional "for signs and seasons," the temple-cosmos shape) push toward a functional-theological reading without requiring a verdict on chronology. Don't let the contested question crowd out the theology. **[I]**

### Repetition & Tone (Tools 10, 9)
"And God said… and it was so… and God saw that it was good" — a liturgical drumbeat building to "very good" (1:31). Tone: ordered, unhurried, regal. The soundtrack is a measured processional, not a battle. Contrast the *Chaoskampf* of Israel's neighbours: here creation is *speech*, not combat. **[T]**

### Schnittjer Pass
**Skeleton:** Genesis · primeval history · establishes creation/image/blessing that particularisation will narrow. **Findings:** (1) *Narrative space* — the cosmos is built like a sanctuary (realms then occupants then rest), foreshadowing tabernacle/temple **[I]** *(moderate; cf. Morales, overview)*; (2) *shape* — day 7 is the janus/goal, not an appendix **[T]**; (3) *first vs second reading* — the reader who knows the fall reads 1:31's "very good" as poignant, soon lost **[I]**. **Nil:** type-scene (n/a to cosmic prologue).

### Christological Reading
**Type:** trajectory + contrast. Creation → new creation in Christ (2 Cor 4:6 echoes 1:3; Col 1:15–20; John 1:1–3 rewrites 1:1). Image-of-God trajectory runs to Christ *the* image (Col 1:15) and the last Adam (1 Cor 15:45). Passes category + NT-precedent + escalation tests → *high confidence* trajectory. **Moralism check:** the temptation is "steward creation well" as bare ethics; ground it instead in Christ the true image who does the mandate we botched.

### Headline Findings
1. **Genesis 1 answers "what is the world *for*," not merely "how was it made."** — Surfaced by: Structure (forming/filling), Vocabulary (*ṣelem*, *bārak*), Genre, Tone. The functional-temple shape is the text's own emphasis. **[T]**
2. **Order by word, not by combat — a polemic against rival cosmologies.** — Surfaced by: Repetition, Tone, Historical background. The sea and luminaries are demoted to creatures. *High.* **[T]**
3. **Rest (2:1–3) is the goal of creation, not its afterthought.** — Surfaced by: Structure (day 7 outside the frame), Positional check (Heb 4 trajectory). **[T]**

## §2 — Genesis 2:4–25 · The Human Story Begins

**Load-bearer:** 2:15–25 — vocation, word/test, and the gift of the partner. **Decision:** Focus.

### Positional Necessity Check
**Preceding movement:** 1:1–2:3 established humanity cosmically (image, mandate, blessing). **[T]**
**Necessity answer:** 2:4–25 *zooms in* — the first *tôlĕdôt* (2:4) shifts from cosmos to the human pair: where they are placed (garden-sanctuary), what they are given (work, word/command, each other). It supplies the specific probation (2:16–17) that §3's fall requires to be intelligible. Without ch. 2, ch. 3 has no command to break. **[T]**
**Implication:** Read 2 as the setup for 3, and as the positive vision (vocation + relationship + word) that the fall disorders.

### Structure & Linking Words (Tools 3, 4)
Movement: formation of the man (2:4–7) → garden and command (2:8–17) → the woman (2:18–25). The hinge is 2:18 — "*It is not good* that the man should be alone," the single "not good" in a "very good" creation, driving the search that climaxes in the woman. **[T]**

### Vocabulary (Tool 7)
- *ʿābad / šāmar* ("work / keep," 2:15) — the same pair later describes priestly service of the tabernacle (Num 3:7–8); Eden is a sanctuary and the man a priest-gardener. *Moderate→high* **[I]** *(cross-report to Tool 11)*.
- *ʿēzer kĕnegdô* ("a helper fit for him," 2:18) — *ʿēzer* is frequently used of God himself (Ps 33:20); it denotes strong aid, not subordinate assistance. *High.* **[T]**
- The naming/wordplay *ʾîš / ʾiššâ* (2:23) — the woman "taken out of man." *High.* **[T]**

### Narrator's Comment (Tool 6)
2:24 ("Therefore a man shall leave… and hold fast to his wife") is the narrator stepping out of Eden to address the reader's present — an authoritative aside grounding marriage in creation, cited by Jesus (Matt 19:4–6) and Paul (Eph 5:31). **[T]**

### Schnittjer Pass
**Skeleton:** Genesis · primeval · the garden-sanctuary probation. **Findings:** (1) *space* — Eden is elevated, well-watered, east-facing; a holy place one is later expelled *from* (3:24) **[I]**; (2) *leading words* — *ʿābad/šāmar* bind work to worship **[I]**; (3) *foreshadowing* — the tree of knowledge plants the fuse for §3 **[T]**. **Nil:** chiasm.

### Christological Reading
**Type:** contrast + trajectory. Adam in the garden, given a word to keep, prefigures by contrast Christ in the wilderness keeping the Father's word (Matt 4). Marriage-covenant (2:24) → Christ and the church (Eph 5:31–32). *High* on the Eph 5 link (NT-authorised). **Moralism check:** not "have good marriages / work hard," but the true Adam who keeps the word the first Adam broke.

### Headline Findings
1. **Eden is a sanctuary and the man a priest-gardener (ʿābad/šāmar).** — Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Schnittjer space, Positional check. *Moderate→high.* **[I]**
2. **"Not good to be alone" is the engine of 2:18–25 — the partner is strong aid, God-like help.** — Surfaced by: Structure (the lone "not good"), Vocabulary (*ʿēzer*). **[T]**
3. **2:24 is narrator-to-reader: marriage grounded in creation, not culture.** — Surfaced by: Narrator's Comment, NT citation. **[T]**

## §3 — Genesis 3:1–24 · The Fall ⭐ HIGH

**Load-bearer:** 3:14–19, esp. 3:15 — the protoevangelium inside the curse. **Decision:** Focus (enter through 3:15).

### Positional Necessity Check
**Preceding movement:** ch. 2 gave the command (2:16–17) and the ordered relationships (God, self, partner, ground). **[T]**
**Necessity answer:** ch. 3 exists to narrate the breaking of *that* command and the disordering of *those* relationships — and, decisively, to plant 3:15, the seed-promise the rest of Genesis (and the canon) tracks. This is the hermeneutical spine (overview trap #7): every later threat to the seed is a threat to *this* promise. **[T]**
**Implication:** Enter through 3:15; the curses are the frame the gospel is embedded in.

### Structure (Tool 3)
A tight chiastic movement of approach/withdrawal: temptation (3:1–7) → hiding/interrogation (3:8–13) → sentencing (3:14–19) → expulsion (3:20–24). God's questions ("Where are you?" 3:9; "What is this?" 3:13) mirror the serpent's question (3:1). **[T]**

### Vocabulary & Wordplay (Tool 7)
- *ʿārûm* ("crafty," 3:1) puns on *ʿărûmmîm* ("naked," 2:25) — the shrewd serpent and the exposed pair, same sound. *High.* **[T]**
- *nāḥāš* ("serpent") — the N-Ḥ-Š cluster resonates across the canon (bronze serpent, Num 21). *Moderate.* **[I]**
- "*you shall surely die*" (2:17) vs the serpent's "*you shall not surely die*" (3:4) — verbatim contradiction; the temptation is calling God a liar. *High.* **[T]**
- 3:16's *tĕšûqâ* ("desire") reappears at 4:7 (sin's "desire" for Cain) — same rare word, linking the fall's logic to Cain. *High.* **[T]**

### Narrator's Comment & Linking Words (Tools 6, 4)
"And they knew that they were naked" (3:7) is the narrator reporting the *result* — the promised "eyes opened" (3:5) delivers shame, not divinity. The "therefore" of 3:23 ("*therefore* the LORD God sent him out") links sin to exile. **[T]**

### Repetition (Tool 10)
The god-complex claim "you will be *like God*" (3:5) is the seed of the book's pastoral spine, answered 47 chapters later by Joseph's "*Am I in the place of God?*" (50:19). Flag it forward: `Internal: [planted — resolved at 50:19]`. *High* on the verbal/thematic weld. **[T]**

### Schnittjer Pass
**Skeleton:** Genesis · primeval · the disordering probation, protoevangelium planted. **Findings:** (1) *first vs second reading* — 3:15 is opaque to a first reader, luminous on the re-read from 49:10 / the NT **[T]**; (2) *space* — expulsion is *eastward* (3:24), the first of Genesis's downward/eastward exiles (Cain east of Eden 4:16; Babel in Shinar 11:2) **[I]**; (3) *leading word* — *tĕšûqâ* welds 3:16↔4:7 **[T]**. **Nil:** type-scene.

### Christological Reading
**Type:** direct trajectory / proto-prophecy. 3:15 — the seed of the woman crushes the serpent's head; the long "seed" line runs to Christ (Rom 16:20 "the God of peace will soon crush Satan under your feet" is the NT's own echo). *High* (NT-authorised trajectory). The "God made garments of skins" (3:21) — first death to cover shame; *moderate* as a covering/atonement gesture, not a hard type. **Moralism check:** not "resist temptation like… " (there is no positive human model here) but the promised seed who reverses what Adam lost.

### Difficult / Contested Verses
3:16 ("your desire shall be for your husband, and he shall rule over you") — is this prescriptive or a description of fallen disorder? The 4:7 parallel (*tĕšûqâ* = sin's hostile desire *to master*) supports reading 3:16 as *disorder announced, not order prescribed*. Flag for pastoral care; don't preach it as a marriage blueprint. **[T]** *moderate→high.*

### Headline Findings
1. **3:15 is the book's spine — the first gospel word, embedded inside the curse.** — Surfaced by: Positional check, Christological reading, Structure (it sits at the sentencing centre). *High.* **[T]**
2. **The temptation is calling God a liar ("you shall not surely die," verbatim reversal of 2:17).** — Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Repetition. **[T]**
3. **"You will be like God" (3:5) plants the god-complex thread resolved at 50:19.** — Surfaced by: Repetition, internal-echo check. *High.* **[T]**

## §4 — Genesis 4:1–6:8 · Sin's Logic and Escalation

**Load-bearer:** 6:5–8 — "every intention only evil"; the LORD grieved; Noah found favour. **Decision:** Focus.

### Positional Necessity Check
**Preceding movement:** the fall (§3) disordered the relationships and planted 3:15. **[T]**
**Necessity answer:** this unit shows sin's *logic* — how the fall metastasises: fratricide (Cain), vendetta (Lamech), diverging lines (Cain's vs Seth's), cosmic breach (6:1–4), reaching the verdict (6:5). It exists to make the flood morally necessary and to keep the seed-question alive (Seth "another offspring/*zeraʿ*," 4:25). **[T]**
**Implication:** the load-bearer is the *verdict* (6:5–8), toward which the escalation drives; preach the escalation as setup, not as a set of separate morality tales.

### Structure & Repetition (Tools 3, 10)
Sandwich of genealogies around narrative: Cain's line (4:17–24, ending in Lamech's boast) against Seth's line (4:25–5:32, ending in Noah). The refrain "and he died" hammers through ch. 5 — death reigns (Rom 5:14) despite the long lifespans; the one break is Enoch, who "walked with God, and he was not, for God took him" (5:24). **[T]**

### Vocabulary & Wordplay (Tool 7)
- *tĕšûqâ / māšal* (4:7) — sin "desires" to have Cain, but he must "rule over" it; the 3:16 vocabulary, now moral. *High.* **[T]**
- Cain/Abel: *hevel* ("Abel") means "vapour/breath" (Ecclesiastes' keyword) — the righteous victim's name is fleeting-breath. *Moderate.* **[I]**
- 6:5 *raq raʿ kol-hayyôm* ("only evil all the day") — total, unrelieved. *High.* **[T]**
- 6:6 *wayyinnāḥem… wayyitʿaṣṣēv* ("the LORD regretted / was grieved to his heart") — anthropopathic; God's grief, not caprice. *High.* **[T]**

### Narrator's Comment (Tool 6)
"So Cain… was very angry" (4:5); "sin is crouching at the door" (4:7, God's word); and above all 6:5–6, the narrator's theological turning-point verdict. Don't rush past it (overview: God's grief is the hinge). **[T]**

### Schnittjer Pass
**Skeleton:** Genesis · primeval · escalation to the verdict. **Findings:** (1) *comparison/interchange* — Cain-line vs Seth-line, culminating Lamech (violence) vs Noah (rest) **[T]**; (2) *leading word* — "call on the name of the LORD" (4:26) marks the faithful remnant **[T]**; (3) *ellipsis/first-reading* — the "sons of God" (6:1–4) is deliberately compressed and enigmatic; the narrator wants scale (cosmic breach), not a puzzle solved **[I]** *(moderate)*. **Nil:** none material.

### Christological Reading
**Type:** contrast + trajectory. Abel, righteous and slain, whose blood "speaks" (Heb 11:4; 12:24 — Christ's blood "speaks better than Abel's"). Seth as the seed "instead of" Abel (4:25) keeps 3:15 alive. Enoch's walking-with-God and translation is a *moderate* resurrection-hope pattern (Heb 11:5). *High* on Abel (NT-authorised). **Moralism check:** not "offer God your best like Abel" but the righteous blood that pleads for us.

### Difficult Verses
6:1–4 (sons of God / Nephilim): name the three readings (angelic, Sethite, dynastic-royal) without resolving; the *point* is boundary-transgression at cosmic scale, feeding 6:5. Flag as *uncertain*; route detail to Open Questions. **[T]** on the function; *uncertain* on identity.

### Headline Findings
1. **The unit drives to a verdict (6:5–8), not a gallery of sinners — sin's logic escalates to "only evil."** — Surfaced by: Positional check, Structure, Narrator's Comment. **[T]**
2. **4:7 re-uses 3:16's rare vocabulary (tĕšûqâ) — the fall's logic is now moral combat within.** — Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Repetition. *High.* **[T]**
3. **God's grief (6:6) is the theological hinge — judgment flows from a wounded heart, and "Noah found favour" (6:8) keeps the seed alive.** — Surfaced by: Narrator's Comment, Christological reading. **[T]**

## §5 — Genesis 6:9–9:29 · The Flood

**Load-bearer:** 9:1–17 — re-creation and the Noahic covenant; 8:1 is the pivot. **Decision:** Focus (9:18–29 as the sobering coda).

### Positional Necessity Check
**Preceding movement:** 6:5–8 delivered the verdict and named Noah as the one who found favour. **[T]**
**Necessity answer:** the flood exists to enact *that* verdict and to test whether judgment cancels God's creation purpose. The answer: God de-creates and re-creates, renewing the mandate (9:1, 7 ≈ 1:28) and swearing preservation (9:11) — yet the coda (9:20–27) shows the human problem is not solved. It is positioned to prove judgment is real *and* purpose endures, setting up the need for a solution deeper than a flood. **[T]**
**Implication:** preach it as de-creation/re-creation (overview trap #8), not as a survival adventure.

### Structure (Tool 3)
A grand chiasm centred on 8:1, "**God remembered Noah**." Waters rise (7) → *God remembered* (8:1) → waters recede (8). The turning point is not Noah's competence but God's remembering — covenant faithfulness, not tide tables. *High.* **[T]**

### Repetition & Allusion (Tools 10, 11)
De-creation language reverses Genesis 1: "the fountains of the great deep… and the windows of the heavens" (7:11) undo the day-2 separation of waters; the dove and the emerging dry land (8:8–11) re-run day 3. Then 9:1, 7 re-issue 1:28 almost verbatim — Noah as a new Adam. *High* on the 1↔9 mandate echo (near-verbatim). **[T]** New-creation/judgment trajectory to 2 Pet 3:5–7; the ark→baptism link (1 Pet 3:20–21) is NT-authorised. **[S/NT]**

### Vocabulary (Tool 7)
- *tēbâ* ("ark") — used only here and of Moses' basket (Exod 2:3); both are pitch-covered vessels of rescue through water. *High* — a deliberate lexical weld across the two salvations. **[T]** *(cross-report Tool 11; and note *ʾārôn*, the *other* box, closes the book at 50:26.)*
- *nûaḥ* ("rest") puns on *Nōaḥ* ("Noah," cf. 5:29, "he shall bring us relief"). *High.* **[T]**
- 9:6 grounds capital punishment in the *image of God* — the image survives the fall (echo of 1:26; 5:1). *High.* **[T]**

### Narrator's Comment & Copycat (Tools 6, 13)
9:20–29 (Noah's drunkenness, Ham's offence, the curse on Canaan): descriptive, not prescriptive; a sobering coda showing sin re-emerges in the "new Adam." The curse falls on *Canaan* — theologically loaded for a wilderness audience about to enter Canaan. **[T]**

### Schnittjer Pass
**Skeleton:** Genesis · primeval · de-creation / re-creation; the human problem persists. **Findings:** (1) *shape* — 8:1 chiastic centre = *God remembered* **[T]**; (2) *leading word* — *tēbâ* welds Noah↔Moses **[T]**; (3) *first vs second reading* — the rainbow (*qešet*, also "war-bow") reads as God hanging up his bow **[I]** *(moderate)*. **Nil:** type-scene.

### Christological Reading
**Type:** typology (event) + contrast. Flood-judgment-and-remnant → final judgment and rescue (2 Pet 3; 1 Pet 3:20–21, baptism). Noah the new-Adam who still fails → the true last Adam who does not. *High* (NT-authorised). **Moralism check:** not "be righteous like Noah in a wicked generation" as bare exhortation, but the God who remembers and the ark he provides.

### Headline Findings
1. **8:1 "God remembered Noah" is the structural and theological pivot — the flood is a God-centred story.** — Surfaced by: Structure (chiasm), Tone, Positional check. *High.* **[T]**
2. **Deliberate de-creation/re-creation: the flood unmakes Genesis 1 and the mandate is re-issued (9:1,7 ≈ 1:28).** — Surfaced by: Repetition, Allusion, Vocabulary. *High.* **[T]**
3. **tēbâ welds Noah's ark to Moses' basket — one rescue-through-water vocabulary running to baptism.** — Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Christological reading. **[T]**

## §6 — Genesis 10:1–11:26 · Nations and Narrowing

**Load-bearer:** 11:1–9 — Babel, the turning point of the primeval history. **Decision:** Focus (Table = frame; Shem genealogy = answer).

### Positional Necessity Check
**Preceding movement:** the flood re-created the world but not the heart; humanity is told (again) to fill the earth (9:1). **[T]**
**Necessity answer:** Babel exists to show humanity refusing that scattering-to-fill mandate — grasping a name and a tower "with its top in the heavens" (11:4) instead. It is the climactic primeval failure; the Shem genealogy (11:10–26) then narrows from all nations to *one family*, Terah→Abram, setting up the pivot at 12:1. The Table of 70 nations (ch. 10) frames Babel and pre-loads the "all families of the earth" of 12:3. **[T]**
**Implication:** preach Babel *into* Genesis 12 without a break — the book's own argument runs across the seam.

### Structure & Repetition (Tools 3, 10)
Babel is a miniature chiasm turning on the LORD's "coming down" (11:5): humanity builds *up* / the LORD comes *down*; "let us make a name" (11:4) / "let us go down and confuse" (11:7). The irony is structural — the tower reaching heaven is so small God must descend to see it. Repetition of *šām / šēm / šāmayim* (there / name / heavens) and the *nilbĕnâ / nāvēlâ* sound-play ("let us make bricks" / "let us confuse") drive it. *High* on the wordplay pattern; *moderate* on precise reconstruction. **[T]/[I]**

### Vocabulary (Tool 7)
- *Bābel* (11:9) — the narrator puns it on *bālal* ("confuse"); Babylon's own etymology ("gate of god") is polemically overwritten as "confusion." *High.* **[T]**
- "make a *name* (*šēm*) for ourselves" (11:4) ↔ "I will make your *name* great" (12:2) — the same word, grasped vs given. *High* internal weld. **[T]**

### Internal Echo (Tool 11, Move 4)
`Internal:` Babel's grasped name (11:4) is *answered* by Abram's given name (12:2) — the very next unit; and the scattering (11:8) is *reversed in principle* by "all families of the earth blessed" (12:3). Direction: planted-and-immediately-resolved. *High.* **[T]** Forward to Acts 2 (Pentecost as Babel reversed) is a *moderate*, widely-held trajectory, not a verbal citation. **[I]**

### Genre (Tool 12)
Genealogy (10; 11:10–26) is theological architecture, not filler — note the 70 nations (the number matters: 70 ↔ Jacob's 70, 46:27). The Table maps the *world* Abram is sent to bless. **[T]**

### Schnittjer Pass

## §7 — Genesis 11:27–13:18 · The Call of Abraham ⭐ HIGH

**Load-bearer:** 12:1–3 — the founding promise. **Decision:** Focus.

### Positional Necessity Check
**Preceding movement:** 1–11 established a world disordered by sin, scattered at Babel, narrowed to Terah's line. **[T]**
**Necessity answer:** 12:1–3 is *the pivot of the book* and the answer to 1–11: God's response to universal catastrophe is one called family. The promises (land, seed, great name, blessing-to-all-families) directly counter Babel (name given not grasped; blessing not confusion) and reach back to reverse 3:15's curse-world. This unit exists *here* because everything before it is the problem it answers (overview: the primary pivot at 11:27/12:1). **[T]**
**Implication:** do not rush to Abram's obedience; the initiative is entirely God's (five first-person promises). Preach the God who speaks.

### Structure & Linking Words (Tools 3, 4)
Call and journey (11:27–12:9) → famine and Egypt-failure (12:10–20) → separation from Lot (13). The imperative "Go" (*lek-lĕkā*, 12:1) governs a cascade of cohortatives ("*I will* make… bless… make great"). ### Vocabulary (Tool 7)
- *lek-lĕkā* (12:1) — "go, for yourself" / "go by yourself"; the same emphatic form recurs only at 22:2 (the Akedah) — an intentional bracket around the Abraham cycle. *High.* **[T]** *(cross-report §9.)*
- *bĕrākâ / bārak* — five-fold blessing vocabulary in 12:2–3, the reversal of the primeval curse-run. *High.* **[T]**
- 12:3 *wĕnivrĕkû* ("shall be blessed"; niphal/possibly hithpael) — passive "be blessed" or reflexive "bless themselves"; Paul reads the passive (Gal 3:8). *Moderate* on the stem; *high* on Paul's use. **[T]/[S: NT]**

### Copycat (Tool 13)
12:10–20 (the wife-sister lie in Egypt): *descriptive and negative* — Abram endangers the promise-bearer out of fear; God protects the promise despite him. Do not preach Abram's deception as shrewd faith. This is a proto-exodus (go down to Egypt, plagues on Pharaoh, come out enriched, 12:20) — the pattern the nation will live. *Moderate→high* on the proto-exodus shape. **[I]**

### Internal Echo (Tool 11, Move 4)
`Internal:` 12:2 "great name" *answers* Babel (11:4); the Egypt descent-and-return (12:10–20) *plants* the exodus template picked up at 46–50 and in Exodus. *High* on Babel; *moderate* on the proto-exodus plant. **[T]/[I]**

### Christological Reading
**Type:** trajectory (NT-authorised). The seed-and-blessing promise is "the gospel preached beforehand to Abraham" (Gal 3:8); the singular seed is Christ (Gal 3:16). *High.* **Moralism check:** not "step out in faith like Abraham" as a technique (overview trap), but the God who calls and promises, to whom faith is the response.

### Headline Findings
1. **12:1–3 is the hinge of Genesis — God's particular answer to the universal catastrophe of 1–11.** — Surfaced by: Positional check, Structure, Internal echo (reverses Babel). *High.* **[T]**
2. **The cycle's pattern is set at once: divine promise → human failure (12:10–20) → sustaining grace.** — Surfaced by: Structure, Copycat. **[T]**

## §8 — Genesis 14:1–17:27 · Covenant Cut and Sealed ⭐ HIGH · ★ SPLIT

**Load-bearers (two):** 15:6 *and* ch. 17. **Decision:** Split. §8a (14:1–15:21) — *On what basis does God bind himself to us?* §8b (16:1–17:27) — *What do we do while the promise tarries?* **Seam note:** the text's own hard chronological seam is 16:16→17:1 (a 13-year silence); the thematic 14–15 | 16–17 split cuts across it — take the thematic split eyes-open, or split 14–16 | 17 to honour the chronology. **[S: §8 audit, confirmed text-internally]**

### Positional Necessity Check
**Preceding movement:** 12–13 gave the promise and showed Abram's fear-failures; the promise is made but unsecured. **[T]**
**Necessity answer:** this unit exists to *ground* and *seal* the promise. 15 answers "on what basis?" — faith credited as righteousness (15:6), God alone passing between the pieces (15:17). 17 answers "sealed how?" — circumcision in the flesh, the covenant named "everlasting." 16 (Hagar) sits between as the human shortcut when the promise tarries. ### Copycat (Tool 13)

## §9 — Genesis 18:1–22:19 · Justice and the Akedah ⭐ HIGH · ★ SPLIT

**Load-bearers (two):** 18:16–33/19 (the Judge of all the earth doing right) *and* 22:1–19 (the supreme test and the sworn oath). **Decision:** Split. §9a (18:1–19:38) — *Will the Judge of all the earth do right?* §9b (21:1–22:19) — *Will God's command ever contradict his promise?* **Seam note:** the softest of the three live splits — ch. 20 (Abimelech, a wife-sister doublet) is the orphan whichever way the seam falls. **[S: §9 audit]**

### Positional Necessity Check
**Preceding movement:** the covenant is cut and sealed (§8); the promise is now secured but not yet delivered — Isaac is promised, not born (until 21). **[T]**
**Necessity answer:** these chapters test the *character* of the promise-keeping God on two fronts. 18–19 asks whether he is *just* (Sodom); 22 asks whether his command can *cancel* his promise (the Akedah). The two peaks are the poles of the gospel — God's justice (Sodom: "Shall not the Judge of all the earth do what is just?" 18:25) and God's provision/substitution (Akedah: "God will provide… the lamb," 22:8) — the two truths that meet at the cross. **[S→T]**
**Implication:** the Akedah is the Abraham cycle's *resolution point* (arc-map: maximum tension then absolute oath); it must be its own sermon.

### Structure (Tool 3)
§9a: intercession (18:16–33, Abraham's bargaining down from 50 to 10) framed against the descent of judgment (19). §9b: Isaac given (21) then Isaac bound (22) — gift and test back to back. The Akedah itself is a tight ascent: the *lek-lĕkā* command (22:2, bracketing 12:1), the three-day journey, the exchange with Isaac ("God will provide," 22:8), the stayed hand, the ram, the oath. **[T]**

### Vocabulary (Tool 7)
- *lek-lĕkā* (22:2) — recurs only from 12:1; the cycle's opening imperative returns at its climax. *High.* **[T]**
- *yirʾeh* / *YHWH yirʾeh* (22:8, 14) — "God will *see/provide*"; the mountain named "The LORD will provide." *High.* **[T]**
- *taḥat* (22:13) — the ram offered "*instead of*" his son; the first term in the book's substitution grammar (→ 44:33). *High.* **[T]** *(cross-report §15, Cross-Passage capstone.)*
- 22:17 singular "offspring… **his** enemies" — the seed hook Paul reads as singular (Gal 3:16). *Moderate→high* on the singular; *high* on Paul's use. **[S: §9 audit; T on the text]**

### Quotation/Allusion & the "did not spare" (Tool 11)
22:16 (LXX *ouk epheisō*, "you did not spare your beloved son") is echoed by Paul at Rom 8:32 ("he who did not spare his own Son"). *High* (widely recognised LXX-dependence). **[S: NT]** `Internal:` the Akedah *answers* the whole barrenness-and-promise tension of 12–21 and *plants* the substitution/provision vocabulary the book later welds to Judah. *High.* **[T]**

### Copycat & Difficult Verses (Tools 13; difficult)
The Akedah is *unique salvation-history*, not a template ("sacrifice your Isaac" is a moralistic misreading — overview). The ethical difficulty (a command to child-sacrifice) is answered by Hebrews 11:17–19: Abraham reasoned God could raise Isaac — resurrection faith before the resurrection. Sodom (19): flag the pastoral/interpretive landmines; the sin-of-Sodom debate should be handled from 18:20/Ezek 16:49 breadth without erasing 19's inhospitable-violence horizon. **[T]** *high* on the Hebrews frame.

### Schnittjer Pass
**Skeleton:** Genesis · patriarchal · justice and provision, the cycle's climax. **Findings:** (1) *shape/climax* — 22 is the peak of the Abraham arc; the oath (22:16–18) is the most absolute commitment in Genesis **[T]**; (2) *leading word* — *rāʾâ* ("see/provide") threads 22 **[T]**; (3) *route/type-scene* — the three-day ascent to a mountain God shows **[I]**. **Nil:** none material.

### Christological Reading
**Type:** typology + trajectory (multiple, NT-authorised). Provided ram "instead of" the son → John 1:29 ("the Lamb of God"); "did not spare" → Rom 8:32; singular seed → Gal 3:16; Isaac received back "as from the dead" → Heb 11:19. Passes all four typology tests → *high*. **Moralism check:** not "trust and obey like Abraham," but the Father who did not spare his Son and himself provides the Lamb.

### Headline Findings
1. **The split gives the two poles of the gospel: God's justice (Sodom, 18:25) and God's provision/substitution (Akedah, 22:8,13).** — Surfaced by: Positional check, Structure. *High.* **[S→T]**
2. **22:13's ram "instead of" (taḥat) opens the book's substitution grammar; 22:16 "did not spare" is echoed at Rom 8:32.** — Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Allusion, Christological reading. *High.* **[T/S: NT]**
3. **The Akedah is resurrection faith (Heb 11:19), not a call to imitate — and its oath (22:16) is Genesis's most absolute commitment.** — Surfaced by: Difficult Verses, Schnittjer climax. *High.* **[T]**

## §10 — Genesis 22:20–25:18 · Death and Succession

**Load-bearer:** ch. 24 — providence securing the succession (23 + 25 frame it). **Decision:** Focus.

### Positional Necessity Check
**Preceding movement:** the covenant is sworn with an absolute oath (22:16–18); Sarah and Abraham are old; the promise now needs a *next generation*. **[T]**
**Necessity answer:** this unit exists to carry the promise past the death of its bearers. Sarah dies and Abraham buys Machpelah (23) — the first owned *foothold* in the land. A bride is found for Isaac (24) — the seed-line secured by providence. Abraham dies (25:1–11); Ishmael's line is closed off (25:12–18). It answers: *will the promise survive the promise-bearer's death?* **[T]**
**Implication:** ch. 24's engine is *providence* — the servant's prayer answered before he finishes (24:15, 45); preach God's hidden hand.

### Structure & Repetition (Tools 3, 10)
Ch. 24 is the longest chapter in Genesis — the narrator brakes hard (Schnittjer: braking = weight). The servant's mission is *told twice* (once by the narrator, 24:1–27; once in the servant's own retelling to Laban, 24:34–49) — repetition marking theological weight: the whole point is that God *guided* (24:27, 48). **[T]**

### Vocabulary (Tool 7)
- *ḥesed* (24:12, 27, 49) — covenant-loyal love; the servant asks God to "show *ḥesed* to my master." *High.* **[T]**
- 23: the land *purchase* (400 shekels, the negotiation with the Hittites) — a legal, tangible down-payment on the land promise. *High.* **[T]**
- 24:7 cites the promise back to God ("the LORD… who *swore* to me") — the oath of 22:16 already functioning as ground for prayer. *High.* **[T]**

### Copycat (Tool 13)
24 is often mined for "how to find a spouse / God's guidance." Handle with care: the servant's method (a specific prayer-sign) is *descriptive* providence, not a prescribed technique (cf. Gideon's fleece caution). The prescriptive core is trusting the God who guides, not copying the sign. **[T]**

### Schnittjer Pass
**Skeleton:** Genesis · patriarchal · succession secured by providence. **Findings:** (1) *narrative braking* — ch. 24's length and doubled telling foreground divine guidance **[T]**; (2) *type-scene* — the betrothal-at-the-well (24; recurs Jacob/Rachel 29, Moses/Zipporah Exod 2) begins **[T]**; (3) *space* — Machpelah, the first owned ground, anchors the land hope **[T]**. **Nil:** none material.

### Christological Reading
**Type:** trajectory + faint typology. The land down-payment (23) → the "better country" (Heb 11:16), the patriarchs owning only graves yet dying in faith. The bride sought for the son by the father's servant is a *moderate/uncertain* type of the church sought for the Son (attractive but not NT-authorised — flag as illustration, not type). *Uncertain* on the bride-type; *moderate* on the land-trajectory. **Moralism check:** not "pray specifically like the servant," but the providential God who keeps the seed alive across the grave.

### Headline Findings
1. **Providence is the theme: ch. 24's doubled telling exists to say "the LORD guided" (24:27, 48).** — Surfaced by: Structure (braking, repetition), Vocabulary (*ḥesed*). **[T]**
2. **Machpelah (23) is the first owned foothold — a tangible, grave-shaped down-payment on the land promise (→ Heb 11:16).** — Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Schnittjer space, Christological trajectory. **[T]**
3. **The oath of 22:16 already grounds prayer (24:7) — the sworn promise is now load-bearing.** — Surfaced by: Positional check, Vocabulary. **[T]**

## §11 — Genesis 25:19–28:22 · Jacob the Grasper ⭐ HIGH

**Load-bearer:** 28:10–22 — grace to the fleeing deceiver at Bethel. **Decision:** Focus (grasping 25–27 makes Bethel land).

### Positional Necessity Check
**Preceding movement:** the promise passed to Isaac by providence (§10); now it must pass to the *next* generation — and the oracle says "the elder shall serve the younger" (25:23), *before* the twins have done anything. **[T]**
**Necessity answer:** this unit exists to show election by *grace against merit and primogeniture*: Jacob grasps the birthright (25:29–34) and steals the blessing (27) — and God confirms the Abrahamic promise to him *unconditionally* at Bethel (28:13–15) while he flees as a deceiver. It answers: *does the promise run on human deserving?* No. **[T]**
**Implication:** the load-bearer is Bethel precisely because 25–27 have shown Jacob at his worst; grace lands hardest on the fugitive.

### Structure & Repetition (Tools 3, 10)
Bracketed by two encounters with God (25:23 oracle; 28:12–15 Bethel). The birthright (*bĕkōrâ*) and blessing (*bĕrākâ*) — near-homophones — are the paired objects Jacob seizes. - *bĕkōrâ / bĕrākâ* wordplay (birthright/blessing). *High.* **[T]**
- 28:16–17 *mah-nôrāʾ* ("How awesome…!"); *bêt-ʾĕlōhîm / šaʿar haššāmayim* ("house of God / gate of heaven") — *Bethel* named. *High.* **[T]**

### Internal Echo (Tool 11, Move 4)
`Internal:` Bethel (28:12, the ladder/stairway with God above and angels ascending/descending) is *planted* and activated canonically at John 1:51 (Jesus as the true Bethel — "you will see heaven opened and the angels… on the Son of Man"). *High* (NT-authorised). ### Copycat (Tool 13)
Jacob's deception (27) is *descriptive and negative* — not shrewd faith to admire; yet the narrative insists the promise runs through the deceiver by grace, not despite the reader's discomfort. The measure-for-measure justice (Jacob deceives → Laban deceives him, elder-for-younger, 29:25) is set up here. **[T]**

### Schnittjer Pass

**Type:** trajectory + Christophany-adjacent. Bethel (heaven opened, God's house) → John 1:51, Christ the true meeting-point of heaven and earth (*high*, NT-authorised). The God who commits unconditionally to a deceiver trajectors to justifying grace. **Moralism check:** not "don't deceive like Jacob" (overview trap) and not "be a go-getter like Jacob," but the God who binds himself by grace to the undeserving.

### Headline Findings
1. **Election runs by grace against merit and birth order — the oracle (25:23) precedes the twins' deeds, and Bethel's promise (28:13–15) is unconditional to a fugitive deceiver.** — Surfaced by: Positional check, Structure, Copycat. *High.* **[T]**
2. **Bethel opens the presence programme ("I am with you," 28:15) and is the true meeting-point fulfilled in Christ (John 1:51).** — Surfaced by: Internal echo, Christological reading. *High.* **[T/S: NT]**

## §12 — Genesis 29:1–35:29 · Jacob in Exile ⭐ HIGH · ★ SPLIT (strongest in the book)

**Load-bearers (two):** the twelve sons born (29:31–30:24, the nation forming) *and* the Jabbok (32:22–32, the grasper broken and renamed). **Decision:** Split. §12a (29:1–31:55) — *How does God build his promise through twenty years of family dysfunction?* §12b (32:1–35:29) — *What must God break in us before he can bless us?* This is the cleanest, strongest split; if the calendar allows only one, take this one. **[S: §12 audit]**

### Positional Necessity Check
**Preceding movement:** Bethel committed God unconditionally to the fleeing Jacob (§11); the promise now needs *offspring* (the nation) and the grasper needs *transforming*. **[T]**
**Necessity answer:** the two load-bearers answer the two needs. 29–31: the twelve sons are born amid rivalry — the nation forms while Jacob is himself deceived (elder-for-younger, 29:25 — measure-for-measure). 32: at the Jabbok the grasper is wounded and renamed *Israel* — the man is transformed. Development (the nation) and climax (the man) together answer the cycle question. **[T]**
**Implication:** two distinct load-bearing arguments; subordinating either collapses it (hence the split).

### Structure & Repetition (Tools 3, 10)
§12a is framed by two deceptions of Jacob by Laban (the wives, 29:23–25; the wages, 31:7). The sons' birth-namings (29:32–30:24) are a dense cluster of folk-etymologies encoding the rivalry. §12b is a chiasm of encounters: angels at Mahanaim (32:1–2) → Jabbok (32:22–32) → Esau (33) → Shechem (34) → Bethel/deaths (35), with the Jabbok at the turn. **[T]**

### Vocabulary & Wordplay (Tool 7)
- The Jabbok triple pun: *Yaʿăqōv* (Jacob) / *Yabbōq* (Jabbok) / *wayyēʾābēq* ("he wrestled," 32:24) — three near-identical sounds naming man, place, and act. *High.* **[T]**
- *Yiśrāʾēl* ("Israel," 32:28) — "he strives with God" / "God strives"; the new name for the transformed grasper. *High.* **[T]**
- *Pĕnîʾēl* ("Peniel," 32:30) — "I have seen God face to face, and yet my life is delivered." *High.* **[T]**
- 32:20 *ʾăkappĕrâ pānāyw* ("I may appease/cover his face") — *kāpar* (atonement vocabulary) around the Esau reconciliation. *Moderate.* **[I]**

### Narrator's Comment (Tool 6)
34 (Dinah) is told with pointed narratorial restraint; the sons' deceit-and-slaughter (using circumcision, the covenant sign, as a weapon) is *not* endorsed — 49:5–7 delivers Jacob's verdict on Simeon and Levi, the narrator's deferred comment. Reuben's defilement (35:22) is likewise held for judgment at 49:3–4. **[T]** These disqualifications clear the way to Judah.

### Internal Echo (Tool 11, Move 4)
`Internal:` Peniel ("seen God face to face," 32:30) → 2 Cor 4:6 trajectory (the light of God's face in Christ) *moderate*; the elder-for-younger deception (29:25) *answers* Jacob's own theft of the elder's blessing (27) — measure-for-measure *high* **[T]**. Reuben (35:22) and Simeon/Levi (34) *plant* the disqualifications resolved at 49 — handing kingship to Judah. *High.* **[T]**

### Schnittjer Pass
**Skeleton:** Genesis · patriarchal · the nation formed, the man transformed. **Findings:** (1) *shape* — the Jabbok is the chiastic turn of the whole Jacob cycle **[T]**; (2) *wordplay* — the *Yaʿăqōv/Yabbōq/ʾāvaq* triple pun is load-bearing **[T]**; (3) *measure-for-measure* — the deceiver deceived (29:25) **[T]**. **Nil:** none material.

### Christological Reading
**Type:** contrast + trajectory. The man who must be *broken* (wounded, renamed) to be blessed contrasts with the Son who is faithful; the "face of God" survived (Peniel) trajectors to seeing God's glory in Christ's face (2 Cor 4:6, *moderate*). ### Difficult Verses

### Headline Findings
1. **The split is text-driven: the nation forms (29–31) and the man is transformed (32) — two distinct load-bearing arguments.** — Surfaced by: Positional check, Structure. *High.* **[T]**
2. **The Jabbok triple pun (Yaʿăqōv/Yabbōq/wayyēʾābēq) and the new name Israel make the wound-to-bless the cycle's turning point.** — Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Schnittjer shape. *High.* **[T]**
3. **The elder brothers disqualify themselves here (34; 35:22 → 49:3–7), clearing the line to Judah — and Jacob is deceived in the coin he dealt (29:25).** — Surfaced by: Narrator's Comment, Internal echo (measure-for-measure). *High.* **[T]**

## §13 — Genesis 36:1–38:30 · Two Futures: Esau and Judah ⭐ HIGH

**Load-bearers (two):** 37 *and* 38. **Decision:** Focus with a live split option (§13a 36–37 / §13b 38). **Cost of splitting flagged by §13:** isolating 38 severs the 37↔38 weld — if split, preserve the binding by explicit cross-reference. **[S: §13 audit]**

### Positional Necessity Check
**Preceding movement:** the elder sons disqualified themselves (§12); the Joseph narrative opens (37:2, Jacob's *tôlĕdôt*). **[T]**
**Necessity answer:** ch. 38 is *not* an interruption (overview trap #11). It exists exactly here because 37 has just shown Judah proposing the sale of Joseph (37:26–27); 38 shows Judah's further collapse *and* the first turn ("she is more righteous than I," 38:26), and secures the Perez line (→ David, Ruth 4:18–22; → Christ, Matt 1:3). Esau's genealogy (36) shows the non-elect line prospering *immediately* — the foil to the slow, threatened elect line. **[T]**
**Implication:** the placement of 38 is the argument; preach 37–38 as one bound movement.

### Vocabulary / the verbal welds (Tool 7, cross-report Tool 11)
- *haker-nāʾ* ("identify, please") — the brothers demand Jacob "identify" Joseph's bloodied robe (37:32); Tamar demands Judah "identify" his own pledge (38:25). The *same distinctive imperative* — the deceiver exposed by his own seal. *High.* **[T]** This is the diction-weld that proves 38's placement (§13 audit; text-first confirmable).
- The *goat-kid* deception motif: Jacob deceived Isaac with a goat (27:9,16); the brothers dip Joseph's robe in *goat's blood* (37:31); Judah sends a *goat* to Tamar (38:17). *High.* **[T]**
- *yārad* ("go down") — Judah "went down from his brothers" (38:1); Joseph is "brought down to Egypt" (39:1) — parallel descents opening the two threads. *Moderate→high.* **[T]**
- *ʿārēv / ʿērābôn* ("pledge/surety," 38:17–18) — the pledge Judah gives Tamar; the same root as Judah's later "I will be *surety*" for Benjamin (43:9; 44:32). *High.* **[T]** *(cross-report §15.)*

### Narrator's Comment (Tool 6)
38:26 (Judah: "She is more righteous than I") is the pivot verdict — the patriarch out-righteoused by a Canaanite widow. 38:7, 10 (the LORD "put to death" Er and Onan) are rare direct-divine-action narrator notes. **[T]**

### Copycat & Difficult Verses (Tools 13; difficult)
Onan (38:9): the sin is the *refusal of levirate duty* — defrauding his dead brother and Tamar of an heir — **not** the physical act later tradition ("onanism") made of it. Defuse that misreading explicitly from the pulpit (overview). Tamar's deception is *descriptive*, and the narrator's verdict (38:26) sides with her *justice-claim*, not her method. **[T]** *high.*

### Schnittjer Pass
**Skeleton:** Genesis · patriarchal · the two futures diverge; Judah's line secured. **Findings:** (1) *diction-weld* — *haker-nāʾ* (37:32↔38:25) proves placement **[T]**; (2) *foreshadowing/goat motif* — 27→37→38 **[T]**; (3) *comparison* — Esau's instant kings (36) vs Judah's threatened line (38) **[T]**. **Nil:** none material.

### Christological Reading
**Type:** trajectory (genealogical) + contrast. Perez (38:29) → David → Christ (Matt 1:3); grace works the messianic line through a Gentile widow's justice-claim and a patriarch's failure. Judah's turn (38:26) begins the arc that ends in substitution (44:33). *High* on the genealogy (NT-authorised). **Moralism check:** not "be righteous like Tamar" or "learn from Judah's mistakes," but the God who secures the seed of the king through moral wreckage and grace.

### Headline Findings
1. **Genesis 38 is structurally essential, not an interruption — the haker-nāʾ weld (37:32↔38:25) proves it belongs exactly here.** — Surfaced by: Positional check, Vocabulary/welds, Schnittjer. *High.* **[T]**
2. **38:26 ("she is more righteous than I") begins Judah's transformation and secures the Perez→David→Christ line.** — Surfaced by: Narrator's Comment, Christological trajectory. *High.* **[T]**
3. **Onan's sin is refusing levirate duty — correct the "onanism" misreading from the pulpit.** — Surfaced by: Copycat/Difficult Verses. *High.* **[T]**

## §14 — Genesis 39:1–41:57 · Joseph: Pit to Pinnacle

**Load-bearer:** 41 — exaltation; but 39's "the LORD was with Joseph" is the theological key. **Decision:** Focus (enter through 39:2,21 → 41).

### Positional Necessity Check
**Preceding movement:** Joseph has been sold down to Egypt (37); Judah's parallel descent has begun (38–39:1). **[T]**
**Necessity answer:** these chapters exist to preserve the seed by placing Joseph where he can "keep many people alive" (later named, 50:20) — and to insist, against appearances, that *the LORD was with him* through pit and prison. God's visibility is at its lowest here (arc-map: "largely hidden"), so the refrain (39:2, 21, 23) carries the theology. **[T]**
**Implication:** enter through the presence-refrain, not through Joseph's virtue; the unit is about providence, not personal development (overview trap #6).

### Structure & Repetition (Tools 3, 10)
Two descents-then-rises: Potiphar's house (rise → false accusation → prison, 39) and prison (interpreting dreams → forgotten → remembered → Pharaoh's court, 40–41). The refrain *wayĕhî YHWH ʾet-Yôsēp* ("the LORD was with Joseph," 39:2, 21, 23) brackets the reversals. Dreams come in pairs throughout (butler/baker; Pharaoh's two dreams) — doubling = "the thing is fixed by God" (41:32, stated). **[T]**

### Vocabulary (Tool 7)
- "the LORD was *with* him" (39:2, 21) — the **presence** vocabulary (cf. 26:24; 28:15; 46:4), the book's second originated programme, running to Immanuel (Matt 1:23). *High.* **[T]**
- *ṣālēaḥ* ("succeed/prosper," 39:2–3, 23) — Joseph prospers because the LORD makes him prosper, not by self-help. *High.* **[T]**
- Joseph's Egyptian name *Ṣāpĕnat-Panēaḥ* and marriage (41:45) — full elevation into Egypt. *Moderate* on etymology. **[I]**

### Copycat (Tool 13)
39 (Potiphar's wife) is *commonly* preached as a purity how-to. The text's own reason for Joseph's refusal is theological — "how then can I do this great wickedness and *sin against God*?" (39:9). Descriptive-with-a-commended-virtue, but the frame is God-ward fidelity, not technique. Joseph's dream-interpreting is *unique* (a God-given office), not a template. **[T]**

### Internal Echo (Tool 11, Move 4)
`Internal:` the two prisoners — one restored, one hanged (40:21–22) — is echoed, at *conceptual/patristic* level only, by the two criminals at the cross (Luke 23:39–43); flag as trajectory-**B**, not a verbal type (overview downgrades this). *Uncertain→moderate.* **[S: §14 audit]** The presence-refrain *plants* 45:5–8 and 50:20 (providence named). *High.* **[T]**

### Schnittjer Pass
**Skeleton:** Genesis · patriarchal · providence hidden in the pit. **Findings:** (1) *leading word* — "the LORD was with Joseph" is the interpretive key **[T]**; (2) *narrative selectivity* — God is off-stage in the action, present only in the refrain; the technique *is* the theology (hiddenness) **[I]**; (3) *doubling* — paired dreams signal divine fixity (41:32) **[T]**. **Nil:** type-scene.

### Christological Reading
**Type:** typology (person: rejected-then-exalted deliverer) + trajectory. Joseph sold, suffering, exalted to save (Acts 7:9–16, Stephen's own typological reading) → Christ. Passes category (deliverer/saviour) + NT-precedent (Acts 7) + escalation → *moderate→high* typology; keep it Christ-as-goal, not Joseph-as-model. **Moralism check:** not "keep your integrity / resist temptation like Joseph," but the God who is with his servant in the pit and works the pit for salvation.

### Headline Findings
1. **"The LORD was with Joseph" (39:2,21,23) is the theological key — the unit is about hidden providence, not personal development.** — Surfaced by: Repetition, Positional check, Schnittjer. *High.* **[T]**
2. **Joseph's refusal is God-ward ("sin against God," 39:9), not a purity technique.** — Surfaced by: Copycat, Vocabulary. **[T]**
3. **Paired dreams signal divine fixity (41:32) — Joseph's exaltation is God's doing, positioning the seed to be preserved.** — Surfaced by: Structure, Repetition. **[T]**

## §15 — Genesis 42:1–45:28 · The Great Reveal ⭐ HIGH

**Load-bearer:** 44:18–45:15 — Judah's substitute offer and the reveal (44:33 is the load-bearer). **Decision:** Focus.

### Positional Necessity Check
**Preceding movement:** Joseph is exalted (41); the famine drives the brothers to Egypt; Judah's arc has run from betrayal (37:26) through nadir (38:26) to surety (43:9). **[T]**
**Necessity answer:** this unit exists to bring Judah's transformation to its climax and to name the book's providence-logic. Joseph tests the brothers to see whether they are changed; Judah proves it by offering himself "instead of the boy" (44:33) — the man who sold one brother now offers his own freedom for another. The reveal (45) reframes the whole catastrophe: "God sent me before you to preserve life" (45:5). **[T]**
**Implication:** 44:33 is the moral-spiritual climax of the patriarchal narratives (overview trap #13); do not preach the reconciliation without naming Judah's substitution.

### Structure & Repetition (Tools 3, 10)
Two journeys (42; 43–44) building to Judah's speech (44:18–34, the longest speech in Genesis — narrative braking = weight) and the reveal (45:1–15). "God sent me" recurs three times in 45:5–8 — the interpretive drumbeat. **[T]**

### Vocabulary / the substitution weld (Tool 7, cross-report Tool 11)
- *taḥat* — Judah: "let your servant remain *instead of* the boy" (44:33); the same "instead of" as the ram *instead of* Isaac (22:13). *High* — the substitution grammar completing its climb. **[T]**
- *ʿārēv* ("surety," 43:9; 44:32) — "I will be a *pledge* for him"; the same root as Judah's pledge to Tamar (38:17–18) — the *ʿārēv* thread welding 38 to 44. *High.* **[T]**
- 45:5, 7, 8 *šālaḥ / śîm* ("God *sent* me… God *made* me") — the compatibilist providence vocabulary. *High.* **[T]**

### Narrator's Comment & Copycat (Tools 6, 13)
Joseph's own theology (45:5–8) functions as authoritative interpretation embedded in speech — "it was not you… but God." Judah's self-offer is *descriptive* but commended by the whole trajectory; it is the closest personal, voluntary substitution in Genesis (a guilty man for an innocent) — read as trajectory to Christ, not "be a self-sacrificer like Judah." **[T]**

### Internal Echo (Tool 11, Move 4)
`Internal:` 44:33 *answers* 37:26 (Judah sells a brother → Judah offers himself for a brother) — the arc's two poles; *high* **[T]**. The *ʿārēv* thread *answers* 38:17–18; *high* **[T]**. 45:5–8 *plants* 50:20 (providence named at the book's end). *High.* **[T]**

### Schnittjer Pass
**Skeleton:** Genesis · patriarchal · the reveal; providence named, Judah's climax. **Findings:** (1) *narrative braking* — Judah's speech is the longest, marking the moral peak **[T]**; (2) *ʿārēv weld* — 38→43/44 binds the units **[T]**; (3) *repetition* — "God sent me" ×3 (45:5–8) **[T]**. **Nil:** type-scene.

### Christological Reading
**Type:** typology/trajectory (substitution). Judah *taḥat* Benjamin (44:33) → Christ bearing what the guilty deserve; from the line that produces the king; the offer that unmasks the hidden lord. *Moderate→high* (the superlative "clearest pre-sacrificial type" is refined per §15: the ram of 22:13, Moses at Exod 32:32, the Passover lamb, and the scapegoat are competing types). **[S: §15 audit]** **Moralism check:** not "sacrifice yourself for others like Judah," but the true Judahite who gives himself for the guilty.

### Headline Findings
1. **44:33 — Judah offering himself "instead of" Benjamin — is the moral-spiritual climax and completes the substitution grammar begun at 22:13 (taḥat).** — Surfaced by: Vocabulary/weld, Internal echo, Christological reading. *High.* **[T]**
2. **The ʿārēv (surety) thread welds Judah's pledge to Tamar (38) to his pledge for Benjamin (43:9;44:32) — one designed arc.** — Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Schnittjer. *High.* **[T]**
3. **"God sent me… you meant it, but God" (45:5–8) is providence named — the distributed key that lands fully at 50:20.** — Surfaced by: Narrator's Comment, Repetition, Internal echo. *High.* **[T]**

## §16 — Genesis 46:1–50:26 · Israel in Egypt ⭐ HIGH

**Load-bearers (two):** Judah's sceptre (49:8–12) *and* 50:20 + the coffin. **Decision:** Focus with a live split option (§16a 46–48 / §16b 49–50).

### Positional Necessity Check
**Preceding movement:** the family is reconciled and providence named (§15); Jacob must now go down to Egypt with the whole seed. **[T]**
**Necessity answer:** this unit exists to close the book by *gathering every thread and resolving none prematurely*. Jacob descends with divine assurance (46:3–4, "I will go down with you… and I will bring you up again"); the 70 descendants (46:8–27) form a new Table of Nations; Jacob blesses Pharaoh (47:7–10) — blessing flowing to the nations in embryo; the sons are blessed and Judah given the sceptre and "the obedience of the peoples" (49:10, "the last days"); Joseph names the providence-key (50:20) and dies in a coffin in Egypt (50:26). It answers: *where does the promise stand?* Alive, narrowed to a king from Judah — but in Egypt, land unpossessed, in a box. **[T]**
**Implication:** preach the unresolved ending as a leaning-forward pause; do not resolve the coffin (overview trap #12).

### Structure & the book's bookends (Tools 3, 10, 11 Move 4)
The unit is engineered as the book's *closing bracket*, echoing the opening: `Internal:` **[all high, all [T]]**
- 1:28 "be fruitful and multiply" ↔ 47:27 "were fruitful and multiplied greatly" (near-verbatim) — the mandate fulfilled, but in Egypt.
- Table of 70 nations (10) ↔ 70 descendants (46:8–27) — a new humanity.
- 3:5 / 11:4 "be like God / make a name" ↔ 50:19 "Am I in the place of God?" — the god-complex thread *resolved*: what Adam and Babel grasped, Joseph refuses.
- *bĕrēʾšît* ("in the beginning," 1:1) ↔ *ʾaḥărît hayyāmîm* ("the last days," 49:1) — the book spans beginning to eschatology.
- *tēbâ* (the ark of rescue, 6–9) ↔ *ʾārôn* (Joseph's coffin, 50:26) — the book's first and last "boxes," rescue and death-awaiting-rescue.

### Vocabulary (Tool 7)
- *šēveṭ / mĕḥōqēq* (49:10, "sceptre / ruler's staff") and the contested *šîlōh* ("until Shiloh comes" / "until that which is his / tribute to him comes") — messianic weight, textually and translationally debated. *High* on the messianic thrust; *moderate/uncertain* on *šîlōh*'s parsing. **[T]/[I]**
- *pāqōd yipqōd* (50:24, "God will surely visit you") — the verb that opens Exodus (Exod 3:16; 4:31); Joseph's dying word points past the coffin to the exodus. *High.* **[T]**
- *ʾārôn* (50:26, "coffin") — the book's last word-image; death, but with bones commanded upward (50:25). *High.* **[T]**

### Translations / Ancient versions (Tool 8)
49:10's *šîlōh*: ESV "until tribute comes to him" (footnote "until Shiloh comes"); the ancient tradition is messianically loaded — the LXX renders roughly "until the things stored up for him come / until he comes to whom it belongs," and Targum Onkelos explicitly reads it of the Messiah. **Translation-tradition witness:** the ancient versions confirm an early messianic reading of 49:10. *Moderate→high* on the LXX/Targum direction; recommend NETS/commentary check for exact wording. **[S]**

### Christological Reading
**Type:** direct trajectory/prophecy + typology. 49:8–12 — the lion of Judah holding the sceptre → Rev 5:5 (the Lion of the tribe of Judah). *High* (NT-authorised). 50:20 (compatibilist providence, "you meant evil… God meant it for good") → Acts 2:23 (the cross as the supreme instance). *High.* The coffin awaiting "God will surely visit" → the true seed who rises from a tomb Egypt (death) could not hold. **Moralism check:** not "forgive like Joseph," but the God who works evil for good, and the king from Judah who is coming.

### Difficult Verses
49 (the tribal blessings): handle Reuben's, Simeon's, and Levi's *demotions* (49:3–7) as the deferred verdicts on §12's sins — they explain *why* Judah receives the kingship. 49:10 *šîlōh*: name the options; preach the messianic thrust the whole book has been building. **[T]**

### Headline Findings
1. **The unit is the book's closing bracket — 1:28↔47:27, 70↔70, 3:5/11:4↔50:19, bĕrēʾšît↔ʾaḥărît, tēbâ↔ʾārôn — resolving the god-complex thread (50:19) and fulfilling the mandate, but in Egypt.** — Surfaced by: Internal echo (Move 4), Structure, Repetition. *High.* **[T]**
2. **49:8–12 gives the king from Judah the sceptre (→ Rev 5:5); the elder brothers' demotions (49:3–7) explain the choice.** — Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Difficult Verses, Christological reading. *High.* **[T]**
3. **The coffin is not an ending: "God will surely visit you" (pāqōd, 50:24) is the verb that opens Exodus — the book leans forward.** — Surfaced by: Vocabulary, Positional check, Christological trajectory. *High.* **[T]**

## Cross-Passage Convergent Findings

*What becomes visible only when the sixteen units are read as one sequence — the connective tissue of the series. These are the arguments no single unit establishes but the sweep jointly demonstrates. Confidence: NT-authorised = high; converging text-first diction reached across units = strong; looser trajectory = moderate.*

**1. Genesis argues by diction — the "verbal weld." [strong]** The book proves a unit *placed*, not inserted, by a rare word repeated across a seam: *haker-nāʾ* (37:32 ↔ 38:25, §13); *ʿārēv* (38:17–18 ↔ 43:9/44:32, §13→§15); *taḥat* "instead of" (22:13 ↔ 44:33, §9→§15); *pāqōd* (50:24 → Exod 3:16, §16); the Jabbok pun *Yaʿăqōv/Yabbōq/wayyēʾābēq* (§12); *tēbâ/ʾārôn* (§5↔§16). The load-bearing findings of this whole sweep are Hebrew *diction* reached text-first — which is itself the vindication of the method (macro-arg 10). **[T]**

**2. The Judah arc is the patriarchal narrative's moral spine. [strong]** Fall (37:26, §13) → nadir (38:26, §13) → surety (43:9, §15) → substitution (44:33, §15) → throne (49:8–12, §16). The two welds (*haker-nāʾ*, *ʿārēv*) prove it is one designed arc. Judah, not Joseph, carries the king's line — by grace, through moral wreckage. A series that reaches ch. 44 without naming this has missed the moral heart of the book. **[T]**

**3. A substitution vocabulary climbs toward the cross. [high]** Ram *taḥat* Isaac (22:13, §9) → Judah *taḥat* Benjamin (44:33, §15); "did not spare" (LXX 22:16) → Rom 8:32; "God will provide the lamb" (22:8) → John 1:29; singular seed (22:17) → Gal 3:16. The grammar of substitution is built incrementally and detonated in the NT. **[T/S: NT]**

**4. "You meant evil… God meant good" is the book's distributed key. [strong]** Not a one-off at 50:20 but a logic seeded from the Egypt proto-exodus (12:10–20, §7), through "the LORD was with Joseph" (39, §14), to 45:5–8 (§15), to 50:20 (§16) — compatibilist (human intention *and* divine intention, both real) → Acts 2:23. **[T]**

**5. Presence is the book's second originated programme, running to Immanuel. [strong→high]** "The LORD was with X" (26:24; 28:15, §11; 39:2,21, §14; 46:4, §16) → Exod 3:12 → Matt 1:23; Bethel (28, §11 → John 1:51); Peniel (32:30, §12 → 2 Cor 4:6). The trajectory: Eden's lost presence (3:24) → tabernacle → incarnation → new creation. **[T/S: NT]**

**6. The patriarchal narrative is the conscious answer to 1–11. [strong]** The call reverses Babel (12:2 name *given* vs 11:4 name *grasped*; 12:3 "all families" = the scattered 70, §6→§7); the §16 bookends (*bĕrēʾšît/ʾaḥărît*; 1:28/47:27; 70/70; 3:5,11:4/50:19; *tēbâ/ʾārôn*) close the bracket. Genesis is one cumulative case, not sixteen lessons. **[T]**

**7. Election by grace, against primogeniture. [strong→high]** Isaac-over-Ishmael (§8–9) → Jacob-over-Esau (25:23, *before works* → Rom 9, §11) → Judah-over-Reuben (§12→§16). To a barren couple (11:30) and, later, an idolater's family (Josh 24:2). The elder sons disqualify themselves (35:22→49:3–4; 34→49:5–7), handing the kingship to Judah. **[T/S: NT]**

**8. Measure-for-measure justice is built into the structure. [strong]** The deceiver is repaid in his own coin: Jacob's theft (27, §11) → Laban's elder-for-younger (29:25, §12); Judah's *haker-nāʾ* (37→38, §13); the goat-kid motif (27→37→38); the brothers' selling (37) → their testing (42–44, §15). **[T]**

**9. A uniform Christological method: type-first, moralism-resisted, God-as-hero. [high]** Every ⭐ HIGH unit refuses a "be like X" trap (be like Abraham; sacrifice your Isaac; wrestle a blessing; forgive like Joseph) and grounds application in God-as-hero / Christ-as-goal. Preacher-constructed typology is flagged or refined at the checking stage (Joseph's two prisoners → conceptual-B; Judah's substitution superlative refined against the ram, the Passover lamb, the scapegoat). **[T/S: audit]**

**10. The book's own end interprets its whole: the coffin is a promise held. [strong]** By §16 the *ʾārôn* in Egypt (50:26) is not an ending but "God will surely visit you" (*pāqōd*, 50:24) — the verb that opens Exodus and the hope that opens the gospel. The two microscripts pair across the series: *the seed survives* (weeks 1–15) and *a king is coming* (week 16). **[T]**

## Preaching Pitfalls (book-level)

*Common ways the series as a whole gets mishandled. Passage-level pitfalls sit in each unit above; these run across the book.*

### Pitfall: Treating Genesis 1–11 as prologue to hurry through
- **What it looks like:** three quick weeks on "creation, fall, flood, Babel" before the "real" series starts at Abraham.
- **Why it's wrong:** 12–50 is unintelligible without the categories 1–11 establishes (image, blessing/curse, seed, the disordered world the promise answers). The Abrahamic promises then look arbitrary rather than cosmic.
- **The corrective:** make the congregation *feel the weight* of the disordered world of 1–11 before ch. 12 can land. Preach Babel straight into 12:2.

### Pitfall: Moralising the patriarchs ("be like / don't be like")
- **What it looks like:** Abraham as a faith-technique motivational talk; Joseph as a purity-and-forgiveness self-help arc; "don't deceive like Jacob."
- **Why it's wrong:** the characters are instruments of providence and mirrors of failure, not heroes to emulate; the pattern is God-initiates → human-responds → God-responds. A moralised sermon on Joseph never preaches Christ.
- **The corrective:** ask of every unit, "what does this tell us about *this God*, and how does faith respond?" Make God the hero.

### Pitfall: "Days-and-dates" apologetics swallowing Genesis 1
- **What it looks like:** the whole first sermon spent adjudicating young-earth vs old-earth.
- **Why it's wrong:** the chapter's own signals (forming/filling frame, functional-temple shape, ANE polemic) are about *what the world is for*, not the mechanism.
- **The corrective:** name the genre question honestly, then preach the theology the text emphasises.

### Pitfall: Resolving the coffin the book leaves open
- **What it looks like:** a triumphant series finale — "and they all lived happily in the promised land."
- **Why it's wrong:** Genesis ends with the people in Egypt, the land unpossessed, the seed in a box (50:26). The unresolved ending is deliberate — a cliff-hanger into Exodus and, ultimately, into the not-yet of our own waiting.
- **The corrective:** let the final sermon be a leaning-forward pause — full of faith, not soothed past the lack.

### Pitfall: The "onanism" misreading and other pastoral landmines (38; 19; 34)
- **What it looks like:** Genesis 38 preached as a warning against a physical act; Sodom (19) flattened to a single debate; the Dinah massacre (34) endorsed or moralised.
- **Why it's wrong:** Onan's sin is refusing levirate duty (38:9); 34's violence is under the narrator's restraint and Jacob's verdict (49:5–7); 19's horizon is broader than any single reading.
- **The corrective:** defuse the misreadings explicitly; preach the narrator's own verdicts.

## Open Questions / Uncertainties

- **Genesis 1 "days" (§1):** genre remains contested; the report reads the functional-temple signals without adjudicating chronology. Flag as an interpretive choice, not a settled fact.
